Fred VanVleet Opts Into $25 Million With Houston — A $25M Bet on His Own Comeback

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | August 7, 2026

NBA Contracts / Houston Rockets / Fred VanVleet

In September of 2025, Fred VanVleet tore his ACL. He missed every game of the 2025-26 season — the practices, the road trips, the playoff race, all of it. When the opt-in deadline came for his $25 million player option with the Houston Rockets, he had two choices: walk away from a contract that came with enormous uncertainty and test the open market with question marks about his health, or bet on himself.

VanVleet opted in.

The Man Who Bets on Himself

This is, in some ways, Fred VanVleet's defining characteristic. He went undrafted in 2016 out of Wichita State — a point guard that nobody in the NBA wanted badly enough to spend a pick on. He signed a two-way contract with Toronto, outperformed every expectation, and eventually became a starter, then a key contributor, then a central figure on the 2019 Raptors championship team that shocked the Golden State Warriors.

He left Toronto as a free agent and signed with Houston for real money — a commitment to rebuild a franchise around his leadership. Then the ACL happened. One of the most brutal setbacks in sports: not a slow decline, not a transition, but a sudden, complete interruption. Gone for a full season.

And when the option came up, he chose to stay.

What This Means for Houston

The Rockets' calculus is straightforward: if VanVleet returns at or near his previous level, they have a proven, championship-tested point guard capable of running an offense and making big shots in clutch situations. That kind of floor general is genuinely difficult to find, and the $25 million price tag is reasonable for a player of his track record if healthy.

The risk is equally clear. Returning from a torn ACL at age 32 is not the same as returning from a torn ACL at 26. The modern rehabilitation timelines are better than they've ever been, and VanVleet's profile — a guard who relies on instincts, timing, and basketball intelligence rather than elite athleticism — gives him a plausible path to near-full recovery. But the outcome is genuinely uncertain until he's competing at game speed.

Training Camp Is the Answer

The Rockets, per reports, believe VanVleet's recovery is on track for training camp. That camp opens in late September — roughly 12 months after the injury, which aligns with standard ACL return timelines for guards.

Houston's young core is building toward something real. A fully healthy VanVleet — experienced, steady, championship-tested — could be the piece that makes them a legitimate postseason threat in the loaded Western Conference.

He's betting $25 million on that. So is Houston.

What's Next

Training camp in late September will be the first real look at VanVleet's recovery. Watch for reporting on his participation level once camp opens — specifically whether he's fully active or on a minutes restriction heading into preseason. The Rockets' preseason schedule will offer early indicators before the 2026-27 regular season tips off in late October.
