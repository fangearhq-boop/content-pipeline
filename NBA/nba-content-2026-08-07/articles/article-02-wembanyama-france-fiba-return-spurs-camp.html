Wembanyama Returns to France: FIBA World Cup Qualifying, Spurs Camp, and Three Words — "I Miss FIBA"

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | August 7, 2026

NBA Offseason / San Antonio Spurs / Victor Wembanyama / France Basketball

After a third NBA season that included Defensive Player of the Year, All-NBA honors, and the Western Conference Finals MVP, Victor Wembanyama returned to France this summer and said something that told you everything about how he sees the game.

"I miss FIBA."

Three words. And they landed exactly as he meant them to.

Back With Les Bleus

Wembanyama has been named to France's 15-man roster for the 2027 FIBA World Cup qualifying window — his first stint with Les Bleus since the 2024 Paris Olympics, where France won the gold medal on home soil. The memory of that tournament, played in front of his own country, has stayed with him.

France's schedule for the August qualifying window: matches against Serbia on August 20 and August 23, followed by World Cup qualifying games against Slovenia on August 27 and Sweden on August 30. For Wembanyama, this isn't workload management or obligation. It's a reunion with a version of basketball he genuinely loves.

Building Summer Chemistry in Spurs Territory

Before the FIBA window, Wembanyama has been hosting Spurs teammates for informal workouts in France. ESPN confirmed the group includes De'Aaron Fox, Stephon Castle, Dylan Harper, and others from San Antonio's core. This kind of voluntary, player-organized off-season chemistry work doesn't show up in any box score — but teams that do it consistently tend to look different when training camp opens.

It's worth noting what Wembanyama is choosing to do with his summer: host teammates, compete for his country, and sharpen his game. Not rest. Not disengage. Compete.

The Commitment Behind the Numbers

Wembanyama closed his third NBA season having won Defensive Player of the Year, earned All-NBA recognition, and led San Antonio to the Western Conference Finals — where he won the MVP of that series. He is, at 22, already the consensus best two-way player on the planet.

His contract extension reflects a level of organizational buy-in that goes beyond numbers. When Wembanyama signed a five-year, $252 million extension with San Antonio, he deliberately chose the 25% maximum salary tier rather than the 30% supermax option available to him. That decision left approximately $50 million on the table — money he voluntarily passed on so the Spurs could maintain financial flexibility around him.

It is an act of long-term team building that most players in his position would never consider. Wembanyama considered it, and then signed it.

What's Next

Watch France's FIBA World Cup qualifying window closely — Wembanyama's performances against Slovenia and Sweden in late August will be his last competitive action before San Antonio's training camp opens in September. For Spurs fans, these games offer an early preview of where his game stands heading into what could be San Antonio's most important season in years. The NBA's most compelling figure heads into 2026-27 with a gold medal, a contract extension, and three words still echoing: "I miss FIBA."
