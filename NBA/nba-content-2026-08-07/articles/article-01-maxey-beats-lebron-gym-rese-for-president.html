Tyrese Maxey Beat LeBron to the Gym — and LeBron Called Him "Rese for President"

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | August 7, 2026

NBA Offseason / Philadelphia 76ers / Tyrese Maxey / LeBron James

Training camp doesn't open until late September. The preseason doesn't begin until October. And yet the Philadelphia 76ers are already competing — at 6:15 in the morning.

Earlier this week, Tyrese Maxey arrived at the gym before LeBron James and made sure everyone knew it, posting a public callout. LeBron's response was swift and unambiguous: he posted a workout photo captioned "Rese for President."

For a franchise that has spent years trying to build culture alongside talent, this moment matters more than the scoreboard of any preseason drill.

What the Moment Means

It would be easy to file this under "fun offseason content" and move on. But there is something genuine happening between LeBron James and Tyrese Maxey that goes beyond social media optics.

The two share the same agent in Rich Paul, which means they've orbited the same world for years — summer workouts, business conversations, mutual relationships. LeBron knew exactly who Maxey was before the 76ers became the plan. Maxey knew exactly what LeBron's arrival would mean for him.

The caption "Rese for President" is not LeBron undermining his own arrival. It's LeBron doing exactly what championship-level veteran leadership looks like: publicly endorsing the franchise's long-term anchor, making clear that Maxey is the heartbeat of this team, and sending a message to every opponent that the 76ers' internal culture is healthy.

The Team They're Building

Philadelphia's projected starting lineup heading into training camp reads like a fantasy roster: LeBron James, Joel Embiid, Jaylen Brown, Tyrese Maxey, and Jared Edgecombe. That group collectively holds five NBA MVP awards. Embiid, whose health had been the one lingering uncertainty, has been confirmed ready by the 76ers' coaching staff.

LeBron officially joined the organization on July 24, choosing Philadelphia over several other suitors in a decision reportedly shaped by the presence of Jaylen Brown — a two-time All-Star and championship-caliber wing who arrived from Boston in a blockbuster deal. The Sixers had assembled the talent. Now they are assembling the culture.

The preseason unofficially begins October 8 when Philadelphia is expected to visit Brooklyn's Barclays Center. But the real prep work, as these early workouts demonstrate, is already underway.

The Stakes of This Moment

Context matters here: LeBron James is 41 years old. He does not have unlimited windows left. His choice of Philadelphia was a statement — this is where he believes a championship is possible, and he is willing to grind at 6:15 AM in August to earn it.

Maxey, for his part, understands the weight of this. He has led this franchise through years of health uncertainty around Embiid, roster turnover, and the pressure of playing in a city that expects championships. Having LeBron in his corner — publicly, loudly, before camp even opens — changes the equation for how the league views him.

The Knicks won the championship last season. The Celtics remain a factor. The East is not going to hand Philadelphia anything.

But at 6:15 in the morning, in a gym, before any of that begins — Tyrese Maxey is already showing up first.

What's Next

Training camp for the 76ers opens in late September, with the preseason tipping off October 8 at Brooklyn. The full regular-season schedule is expected Thursday, August 13 — that's when Philadelphia will learn its opening night opponent and get its first look at the marquee matchups that will define this historic season. Watch for LeBron's first public comments about his new city as the preseason approaches.
