LeBron James Is the Only Player in NBA History With 42K+ Points, 11K+ Rebounds, and 11K+ Assists

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | August 7, 2026

NBA / LeBron James / Career Milestones

By now, most basketball fans know LeBron James is the all-time leading scorer in NBA history. What the scoreboard misses is the full picture — a statistical profile so singular that no player in the history of the game comes close to replicating it.

LeBron closed the 2025-26 regular season with 43,440 career points. He is the only player in NBA history to simultaneously accumulate more than 42,000 points, 11,000 rebounds, and 11,000 assists. That triple threshold has never been achieved by anyone else. Not Kareem Abdul-Jabbar. Not Karl Malone. Not Michael Jordan. Not Magic Johnson. Not Wilt Chamberlain.

Just LeBron.

Understanding the Scale

The points record alone is extraordinary. Kevin Durant, one of the greatest scorers of his generation, passed Michael Jordan for No. 5 on the all-time scoring list during the 2025-26 season — a milestone that would define almost any career. Durant's career total sits at approximately 30,000 points. LeBron's total is more than 13,000 higher.

Stephen Curry, meanwhile, has been climbing the all-time scoring list himself — passing Kevin Garnett, John Havlicek, and Paul Pierce during 2025-26, with Tim Duncan's 26,496 as his next target. Duncan's total, a benchmark most players will never approach, is a number LeBron surpassed years ago.

The rebounds and assists numbers are what elevate this from scoring record to something categorically different. LeBron is not a center who accumulated rebounds by proximity to the basket. He is not a point guard who accumulated assists by design. He played small forward and shooting guard and power forward, across four different teams, in four different decades, and somehow accumulated more than 11,000 of both.

What the Triple Means

The triple-threshold matters because it reflects something different from any single statistical category: volume of quality impact across every dimension of the game. Most players who pile up points do so at the expense of playmaking or rebounding. Most players who dominate rebounds sacrifice scoring. Most players who lead in assists are not primary scorers.

LeBron has never made those trade-offs. He has been elite at scoring, elite at playmaking, and elite at rebounding, simultaneously, for more than two decades. The 42K/11K/11K combination is not a coincidence — it is the output of a player who has refused to specialize in ways that would make the numbers easier to accumulate.

The Chapter That Continues in Philadelphia

At 41, LeBron is not done. He joined the Philadelphia 76ers on July 24 in a move that suggests he has not arrived to accumulate statistics — he has arrived because he believes a championship is within reach. The 76ers' projected starting lineup of LeBron, Joel Embiid, Jaylen Brown, Tyrese Maxey, and Jared Edgecombe is perhaps the most talented single-team assembly the Eastern Conference has seen in years.

Whatever happens this season, the historical ledger is already settled. No one has ever played the game of basketball the way LeBron James has played it — at this volume, across this many dimensions, for this long.
