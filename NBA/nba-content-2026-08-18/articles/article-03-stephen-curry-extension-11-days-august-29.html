11 Days: Stephen Curry's Extension Window Opens August 29 With $136.7M on the Table

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | August 18, 2026

Eleven days. That is how long Stephen Curry has until he can sign the most consequential contract in Golden State Warriors history since Kevin Durant left for Brooklyn in 2019. The extension window opens August 29. The Warriors can offer two years and $136.7 million. GM Mike Dunleavy Jr. says he is confident it gets done.

The question is not whether Curry wants to be a Warrior. He does. The question is whether the Warriors can give him something worth staying for — a roster capable of competing for a championship — and whether that belief is enough to sign away the only leverage Curry has ever had in his professional career.

What's on the Table

The Warriors can offer Curry a two-year maximum extension worth up to $136.7 million beginning with the 2027-28 season. Without the extension, Curry's current contract expires at the end of next season and he becomes an unrestricted free agent in the summer of 2027 — for the first time in his professional life.

Dunleavy has been consistent and direct in his public messaging. "There's two things I'm confident about with Steph," the GM said. "He really, really wants to win and he wants to finish his career as a Warrior." ESPN's Anthony Slater, who covers the Warriors as closely as any reporter in the league, has confirmed that mutual interest in the extension remains strong from both sides.

What Makes This Complicated

The Warriors went 37-45 last season, missing the playoffs entirely. The front office aggressively pursued LeBron James in free agency this summer — only to watch the four-time MVP choose Philadelphia. The roster entering 2026-27 is largely the same as the one that fell short last year. Draymond Green is back on a one-year, $27.7 million deal. There have been no transformative additions.

For a player who has won four championships in Golden State and defined an era of basketball, the ask is significant: accept a deal that commits the next three years of your career to a franchise that just had its worst season in over a decade, at age 38.

Brian Windhorst of ESPN has raised questions about how the extension fits into the Warriors' long-term salary flexibility picture. Those concerns are real, even if Dunleavy's confidence remains on the public record.

Why Curry Is Still the Story

Because he is still extraordinary. Shooting 42% from three-point range at 38 years old is not nostalgia — it is elite basketball. Curry remains one of the most dangerous offensive players in the league. The shooting mechanics have not declined. The gravity he creates on every possession has not diminished.

The Christmas Day schedule already has Nuggets vs. Warriors on the card (10:30 PM ET, December 25). The league is scheduling around Curry because it still believes in Curry. The question is whether the Warriors are building something around him worth believing in.

What's Next

August 29 is the date. If both sides want this done, the paperwork can follow quickly once the window opens. If talks stall or take longer than expected, the narrative around the extension will grow louder every day through training camp. Curry is expected to be part of Golden State's preseason and regular-season plans regardless. But the contract question will follow him until it is answered — or until 2027 free agency rewrites the whole story.
