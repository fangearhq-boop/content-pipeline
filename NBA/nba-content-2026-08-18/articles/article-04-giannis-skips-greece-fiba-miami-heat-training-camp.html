Giannis Skips Greece FIBA Qualifiers to Prioritize Miami Heat Training Camp

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | August 18, 2026

Giannis Antetokounmpo has made his priority clear: Miami comes first. The two-time NBA MVP has informed the Greek national basketball federation that he will not participate in this summer's FIBA World Cup qualifiers, choosing instead to remain stateside and build chemistry with the Miami Heat ahead of his first training camp in South Beach.

The decision was not made lightly. Giannis has always been vocal about his pride in representing Greece at the international level, and national team basketball has been an important part of his identity throughout his NBA career. But the circumstances are different this summer. This is his first offseason with a new organization after 13 years in Milwaukee, and both he and the Heat organization have determined that the transition deserves full attention.

The Heat's Request

According to reports, the Miami Heat formally requested that Giannis opt out of the FIBA qualifiers to focus on his new team. The organization's position is straightforward: chemistry matters, and the earlier Giannis begins building it with Bam Adebayo, Andrew Wiggins, and the rest of Miami's roster, the more prepared the Heat will be for Opening Night on October 20.

Giannis agreed without apparent hesitation. He has already been inside the Heat facility and on the floor, documenting his introduction to the organization in a YouTube video titled "My First Day With The Miami HEAT." The video captured his first meetings with head coach Erik Spoelstra and team president Pat Riley — a meeting of a two-time MVP and two of the most respected figures in NBA history.

A New Era Begins

The blockbuster trade that sent Giannis from Milwaukee to Miami earlier this offseason was one of the most shocking moves in recent NBA history. The Bucks received Tyler Herro, Haywood Highsmith, Jaime Jaquez Jr., and four first-round picks in return. Herro's immediate reaction — captured on video and going viral — became the emotional snapshot of a stunned league.

Giannis himself has set the tone for what Miami fans should expect. He told NBA Europe earlier this summer: "I will dominate." The 31-year-old, a 10-time All-Star and former Defensive Player of the Year, is not arriving in Miami to settle. He has made clear that winning a championship with the Heat is the explicit goal — not simply playing out his career at a warm destination.

Head coach Erik Spoelstra, who has built a culture around adaptation and sacrifice, is viewed by many as the ideal fit for a player as competitive as Giannis. The Heat don't expect everything to click immediately. They have communicated patience. But early signals from camp suggest both sides are aligned on what a championship version of this team looks like.

The Supermax Clock

The business side is also in motion. Giannis becomes eligible for a $275 million supermax extension on January 6, 2027. The Heat's willingness to offer it — and the environment they create for Giannis this season — will shape whether he commits to Miami long-term or keeps options open.

Christmas Day (December 25, Heat vs. Celtics, 2:30 PM ET) provides the season's first genuine showcase moment. A nationally televised matchup against one of the Eastern Conference's best teams, with Giannis in a Heat uniform for the first time on the biggest stage of the regular season, will tell the league a great deal about what Miami has built.

What's Next

Most NBA training camps open September 29. For Giannis, the real work has already begun — learning Miami's system, developing trust with teammates, and embedding himself in the culture that Spoelstra and Riley have built over two decades. Preseason games follow in October. Then, on Opening Night, the entire basketball world will be watching to see what Giannis Antetokounmpo looks like in a Miami Heat uniform for the first time. His first game back in Milwaukee — whenever the schedule puts them there — will be appointment television of the highest order.
