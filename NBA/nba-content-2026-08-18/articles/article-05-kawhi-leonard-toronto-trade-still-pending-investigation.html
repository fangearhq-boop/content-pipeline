Kawhi Leonard Trade to Toronto Still Pending: NBA Investigation Covers Two Alleged Secret Deals

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | August 18, 2026

Seven weeks after Kawhi Leonard's trade back to Toronto was first agreed upon, the deal remains officially on hold. The NBA's investigation into alleged salary cap circumvention by the Los Angeles Clippers continues to block the transaction, and the stakes — for Leonard, for the Raptors, and for the Clippers organization — grow with each passing day.

The original allegation: the Clippers funneled money to Leonard beyond his official salary through a now-bankrupt company called Aspiration, reportedly totaling $28 million. A second deal has since come under scrutiny — one involving Daktronics, the scoreboard and jumbotron manufacturer that supplied equipment to the Clippers' own Intuit Dome, with investigators examining whether funds were routed through Daktronics to Leonard as an additional off-the-books payment.

The Agreed Trade

The deal agreed upon June 29-30, 2026 would send Leonard to the Toronto Raptors — the franchise he helped win a championship in 2019 — in exchange for Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, two unprotected first-round picks in 2031 and 2033, a 2027 first-round swap, and two second-round selections. It is a substantial package, reflecting both the Raptors' belief in Leonard's ability to return to elite form and the franchise's desire to reunite with the player who delivered their only title.

None of it is official until the NBA clears the trade. And the NBA has not cleared the trade.

Investigation Timeline and Resolution

The NBA retained an outside law firm to conduct the fact-finding inquiry. That review was reportedly completed around August 10. A resolution is expected roughly six weeks after that point — putting the estimated target date at approximately September 22, 2026.

Toronto's training camp is scheduled for September 28-29 in Quebec City. The math is tight. If the investigation runs past its expected window, Leonard could conceivably miss the start of camp with his new team. Leonard himself has stated he expects the trade to clear before camp opens. The Raptors are operating on that same assumption.

What Resolution Unlocks

Once the trade is official, several things happen quickly. Leonard becomes eligible for a two-year contract extension worth up to approximately $123.7 million — extension talks have been described as ready to begin immediately upon trade completion. The Clippers, depending on the severity of any sanctions, face consequences that could include draft pick penalties or a financial settlement.

For Leonard, the resolution also unlocks his return to Southern California. His first game back in Los Angeles — as a visiting player against his former employer — is penciled in for November 2, 2026. That game, whenever it happens officially, will be one of the most charged atmospheres of the 2026-27 season.

The Clippers' Position

The Clippers have maintained that they did not deliberately circumvent the salary cap. The Daktronics allegation adds a layer of complexity that makes the organization's situation significantly more difficult to defend. Both the original Aspiration arrangement and the Daktronics scrutiny suggest a pattern of behavior that the NBA's investigation team is examining carefully before issuing any ruling.

CBA rules limit what sanctions the league can impose — player suspensions are not among the available penalties in this circumstance. Draft picks and fines represent the most likely consequences for the organization.

What's Next

The next significant marker is approximately September 22 — the estimated resolution date. If the NBA delivers its findings on schedule, the trade could be processed within days, giving Leonard just enough time to arrive in Toronto before training camp opens September 28-29. The Raptors are building their offseason plans around that timeline. Any delay pushes the uncertainty deeper into the preseason. Watch for NBA communications in the final week of September — that is when this story reaches its conclusion.
