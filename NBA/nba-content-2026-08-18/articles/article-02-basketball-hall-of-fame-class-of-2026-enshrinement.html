Basketball Hall of Fame Class of 2026 Enshrined: Candace Parker, Amar'e Stoudemire, Doc Rivers Lead Ceremony

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | August 18, 2026

Saturday night in Springfield, Massachusetts, basketball paused to honor itself. The Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame Class of 2026 was officially enshrined at Symphony Hall on August 15 in a ceremony that covered every dimension of the sport — from WNBA champions to NBA title-winning coaches to the offensive mind who changed how the game is played.

Candace Parker. Amar'e Stoudemire. Doc Rivers. Elena Delle Donne. Chamique Holdsclaw. Mike D'Antoni. Joey Crawford. Mark Few. And the 1996 U.S. Women's Olympic Basketball Team. Nine honorees — eight individuals and one transformative team — who collectively redefined what basketball looks like at its highest levels.

Two WNBA Icons Take Center Stage

Parker and Delle Donne's inclusion in the same class makes this one of the most significant moments in WNBA history. Candace Parker, a two-time WNBA champion and two-time MVP, spent 15 seasons transforming the conversation around women's basketball — first in Los Angeles, then in Chicago. Her combination of skill, basketball IQ, and commercial appeal helped the WNBA reach audiences it had never reached before.

Elena Delle Donne, another two-time champion and the 2015 WNBA MVP, played through one of the most remarkable injury stories in recent sports history. Her decision to continue competing despite a serious chronic condition became as much a part of her legacy as the championships themselves.

Together, Parker and Delle Donne gave Saturday night a weight the ceremony has rarely carried.

Amar'e Stoudemire: "Seven Seconds or Less" Gets Its Due

For Phoenix Suns fans, Amar'e Stoudemire's enshrinement has been a long time coming. The six-time All-Star was the hammer in Mike D'Antoni's anvil — the physical force that gave the Suns' breathtaking uptempo attack its teeth in the mid-2000s. Averaging 26 points and 9 rebounds per game during his Phoenix peak, Stoudemire was simply one of the most devastating offensive players the league had seen.

That the enshrinement reunites him with D'Antoni — also enshrined in the Class of 2026 — is a fitting tribute to the "Seven Seconds or Less" era that made the Suns appointment television across two memorable seasons and permanently influenced how the modern NBA is played.

Doc Rivers and Mike D'Antoni: Coaches Who Changed Everything

Doc Rivers spent over two decades as one of the NBA's most respected head coaches, capping his career with a Boston Celtics championship in 2008. His ability to manage egos, build culture, and compete in the Eastern Conference made him one of the defining coaching voices of his generation.

D'Antoni's influence runs deeper than any single title. The architecture of the modern NBA — positionless, pace-first, three-point heavy — traces a direct line through D'Antoni's Suns teams. Every analytics-driven front office, every small-ball lineup, every pick-and-roll explosion owes something to what he built in Phoenix. His enshrinement is less a recognition of the past than an acknowledgment that his ideas won.

Chamique Holdsclaw, Joey Crawford, Mark Few, and the 1996 USA Women's Team

Chamique Holdsclaw, one of the most decorated players in the history of women's basketball, joins the Hall after a career that featured six WNBA All-Star selections and a legacy of excellence stretching from Tennessee to the professional ranks. Joey Crawford, who refereed NBA games for 39 seasons, is recognized as one of the sport's most important — and most memorable — officiating figures. Gonzaga head coach Mark Few, who turned a small Spokane program into one of college basketball's perennial powers, completes the group.

The 1996 U.S. Women's Olympic Basketball Team — which won gold in Atlanta and set the standard for American women's basketball dominance — received a collective enshrinement befitting a group that changed the trajectory of the sport.

What's Next

The Class of 2026 will now take their place among basketball's immortals, with their plaques joining legends from across every era of the sport inside the Hall of Fame museum in Springfield. The next class of nominees will begin the selection process in the months ahead, with announcement of the Class of 2027 finalists expected early next year. For now, the sport takes a moment — and honors the people who made it what it is.
