Bucks (2-17) at Pistons (11-6): Milwaukee's Historic Losing Pace Raises Alarms

By Jake Torres

November 27, 2026

The Milwaukee Bucks visit Little Caesars Arena on Friday night carrying one of the most troubling records in recent NBA history. At 2-17, Milwaukee is on a pace that would rival the worst single-season performances in franchise history. For a team that contended deep into the playoffs just two seasons ago, the collapse has been stunning in its speed and totality.

The 7:30 PM ET matchup against the Detroit Pistons (11-6) highlights the contrast between a franchise in rebuild-mode crisis and one quietly ascending toward playoff relevance.

The Bucks' Freefall

Milwaukee ended a 13-game losing streak just three days ago with a narrow win over Toronto, giving the organization a brief moment to exhale. That exhale lasted roughly 36 hours. On Wednesday, the Nets beat the Bucks 104-97, extending Milwaukee's misery. The team is back to the same pattern: a brief win followed by a loss that makes the win feel anomalous.

Damian Lillard has been limited by injury throughout this stretch, and his absence has exposed how much the Bucks' offense depends on his ability to generate in isolation and off the ball screen. When Lillard cannot play at full capacity, there is no second option capable of carrying the scoring load. The roster construction, built around Lillard's offensive profile, leaves Milwaukee one-dimensional in ways that playoff-caliber teams exploit immediately.

The coaching staff has attempted different lineup combinations, different defensive schemes, and different rotations. None of it has produced sustainable results. At some point, the conversation shifts from adjustments to larger organizational questions about how this team got here and where it goes from a 2-17 hole.

Detroit's Opposite Trajectory

The Pistons at 11-6 represent everything the Bucks are not right now: cohesive, rising, and playing with collective purpose. Cade Cunningham continues to develop as one of the league's better point guards, and Detroit's depth allows head coach to rotate freely without significant drop-off in production.

The Pistons missed the playoffs last season but have used the offseason to build something more credible. Their 11-6 start suggests a team that has internalized the lessons of recent disappointments and channeled them into consistent early-season effort.

Playing the Bucks tonight offers Detroit a winnable game and an opportunity to push further up the Eastern Conference standings. It also serves as a reminder of how quickly a franchise can deteriorate when the supporting cast around a star player is improperly constructed.

What's Next

If Milwaukee loses tonight, the season record falls to 2-18, and the pressure on the organization's decision-makers will intensify further. The Bucks have a difficult December schedule ahead. For Detroit, a win keeps them in the upper half of the East's playoff picture and builds momentum heading toward a stretch where the standings begin to solidify. Friday night is just one game, but for Milwaukee, every game is now carrying existential weight.
