Wembanyama vs. Doncic: Spurs and Lakers Square Off in NBA Cup West Finale

By Marcus Cole

November 27, 2026

When the NBA Cup's Black Friday slate was constructed, the league's scheduling team could not have drawn up a better nightcap. The San Antonio Spurs (15-4) hosting the Los Angeles Lakers (15-3) with the West Group C title on the line — and Victor Wembanyama and Luka Doncic as the primary headliners — is exactly the kind of marquee matchup the tournament was built to generate.

The 9:30 PM ET tip-off on Prime Video will close out the Cup's group-play chapter on a stage worthy of the moment. This is not a mid-November regular-season game that half the league's fan base will ignore. This is a test of which Western Conference powerhouse owns the best Cup path through December.

Wembanyama's Evolution

Victor Wembanyama has quietly become the most difficult player in the NBA to game-plan against. His 7-foot-4 frame and 8-foot wingspan make him the most disruptive rim protector in the league, while his perimeter skill set — step-back threes, face-up jumpers, creative playmaking — expands his offensive impact in ways no center in league history has quite replicated.

San Antonio's 15-4 record reflects how dangerous this team has become when Wembanyama is healthy and engaged. The Spurs' depth, pace, and versatile wing play complement their star perfectly. Wednesday's loss at Portland — 116-111 — was their first defeat in a stretch that had pushed them to the top of the Western Conference standings.

That loss does not unnerve a Spurs team that understands how good it is. But it does provide the Lakers an opening. San Antonio enters Friday's game knowing a loss to Los Angeles would hand the group title to their opponent.

Doncic and the Lakers' Case

Luka Doncic has been exactly what the Los Angeles Lakers envisioned when they constructed a roster around him. The 15-3 record entering Friday is the Lakers' best 18-game start in several years, and Doncic's command of the offense is the primary reason. His ability to generate high-quality looks for himself and teammates — whether through pick-and-roll mastery, euro-step finishes, or spot-up creation — makes the Lakers difficult to defend at any pace.

Los Angeles heads to San Antonio knowing a road victory clinches West Group C. The Lakers have been tested on the road this season, and the AT&T Center will provide a challenging environment. But the Lakers' record and Doncic's form suggest a team that is not easily rattled by hostile arenas.

The Tactical Battle

The key tactical question is how Los Angeles defends Wembanyama. The Spurs have learned to run actions designed to get their star into advantageous positions — catch-and-shoot threes, post-ups against smaller defenders, and transition opportunities where his length is unguardable.

Defensively, Wembanyama will be tested by Doncic's repertoire of hesitation dribbles and step-back shots. No defender can completely stop Doncic, but Wembanyama's presence at the rim discourages drives and forces the Slovenian star to operate further from the basket.

Both coaches understand the other's system well. This is a chess match with November Cup stakes wearing a December playoff mask.

What's Next

The winner claims West Group C and receives a seeding advantage in the knockout bracket, which will be revealed after Friday's final games. Both teams are likely Cup quarterfinal participants regardless — the Spurs as group winner, and the Lakers as either a group winner or a West wild card if they fall tonight. The real prize of a Cup run is the $500,000 bonus per player that comes with reaching the final. That is motivation enough for two rosters that have championship aspirations far bigger than a trophy in November.
