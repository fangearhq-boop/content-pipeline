Knicks Enter Black Friday on Six-Game Streak in Must-Win NBA Cup Battle vs. Heat

By Jake Torres

November 27, 2026

The New York Knicks have been playing their best basketball of the season over the past two weeks, winning six consecutive games to build one of the more imposing streaks in the Eastern Conference. All of that momentum arrives at Madison Square Garden on Black Friday night for a moment that could define their entire NBA Cup run.

The Knicks host the Miami Heat at 7:00 PM ET in an East Group B finale that doubles as a knockout scenario for both teams. Miami advances to the Cup quarterfinals with a victory. New York's Cup campaign ends with a loss. The stakes are identical to a playoff elimination game, and the Garden — already energized by a holiday crowd — will be at full volume from the opening tip.

Towns Has Been the Difference

Karl-Anthony Towns has been the central driver of New York's winning streak. The All-Star center has averaged 28.4 points and 11.8 rebounds per game during the run, asserting himself as a matchup problem no team has solved. His combination of interior finishing and three-point range creates defensive dilemmas that have stretched opposing defenses thin enough for Jalen Brunson and the perimeter players to operate in space.

Brunson himself has been characteristically steady, managing games with the poise that has made him one of the Eastern Conference's most reliable primary ball-handlers. Even in his less efficient shooting nights, Brunson keeps the offense organized and finds his teammates in advantageous spots.

Mikal Bridges, who posted a season-best 19 points in Wednesday's win over Cleveland, has emerged as a consistent secondary option. The wing's ability to create off movement and hit mid-range jumpers takes pressure off Towns in the post and prevents defenses from collapsing on the star center.

Giannis Is the Obstacle

Miami's path runs through one of the most dominant physical forces in the sport. Giannis Antetokounmpo scored 32 points and grabbed 14 rebounds in the Heat's loss to Philadelphia on Wednesday, displaying the kind of near-unstoppable force that reminds the league why he won back-to-back MVP awards. The challenge for the Knicks will be keeping Giannis contained while also accounting for the Heat's spacing and ball movement.

Towns and Giannis have faced each other in several memorable matchups, and Friday's game adds Cup advancement stakes to the rivalry. If Giannis can overpower the Knicks' interior defense early, Miami has the talent to build a lead that makes catching up on a short-attention-span holiday crowd quite difficult.

The Heat (10-7) enter having won three of their last four games before the Philadelphia setback. Miami is not in a slump — they are a team on a mission to lock up a knockout round spot before the bracket is set.

Cup Context

The Knicks entered Cup group play poorly, going 0-2 in their first two group games. Wednesday's 121-102 dismantling of Cleveland was their third Cup win, lifting them to a 2-2 Cup record — good enough to stay alive heading into the finale. A seventh consecutive win tonight would be the Knicks' biggest Cup result since reaching the knockout rounds last season.

The NBA Cup has become a genuinely compelling midseason tournament that generates tangible stakes in otherwise routine November games. Black Friday adds a cultural dimension: the league is building a holiday basketball tradition, and the Cup finale is the ideal vehicle for it.

What's Next

If the Knicks win, they advance to the Cup knockout quarterfinals in December and will learn their opponent when the bracket is revealed Friday night. A loss sends Miami to the quarterfinals and ends New York's Cup run, though the Knicks will continue their regular-season tear regardless. The win streak is real either way — but tonight's game is about more than momentum.
