Giannis Skipped FIBA to Join Heat's California Minicamp. That Tells You Everything.

By Jake Torres | September 13, 2026

The Miami Heat's new-look roster is already putting in work — golf, karaoke, Thompson's boat, and basketball — weeks before official training camp opens.

Before the Miami Heat even open official training camp, they may have already answered the most important question about their season: Does this team actually want to play together?

Heat players, led by Giannis Antetokounmpo, organized an unofficial minicamp in California this week — gathering the new-look roster for a combination of basketball work and team bonding that covered everything from organized practices to golf outings, karaoke sessions, and a day on Klay Thompson's boat. The minicamp has been documented through the week, with activity reported as recently as September 12.

The headline detail: Giannis opted out of Greece's FIBA World Cup qualifying games to attend. That is not a small sacrifice. FIBA commitments are a point of national pride, and declining to represent your country's national team draws attention. Doing so to build chemistry with new NBA teammates weeks before camp opens is a statement of priorities.

The statement: Miami comes first.

A New Roster Needs Reps

The Heat assembled one of the most dramatic roster transformations in recent NBA history this offseason. The trade that brought Giannis Antetokounmpo and Bobby Portis from Milwaukee sent Tyler Herro, Kel'el Ware, Jaime Jaquez Jr., Kasparas Jakucionis, three first-round picks, and additional assets to the Bucks. The organization also added Klay Thompson and Tim Hardaway Jr. to complete the new core.

Four new key pieces arriving simultaneously creates a genuine chemistry challenge. Offensive sets, defensive rotations, communication in pick-and-roll coverage — none of it happens automatically, even with elite talent. The minicamp provides an informal setting for those reps to start accumulating before the pressure of official camp and preseason games begins.

The combination of Giannis at power forward, Bam Adebayo at center, and a guard rotation built around Tyrese Haliburton, Thompson, and Hardaway Jr. gives Miami a lineup that will require significant coordination. The work done this week in California accelerates that process.

The Contract Timeline

Giannis becomes eligible for a contract extension on October 1 — the same date the Jalen Duren qualifying offer deadline arrives. The expectation around the league is that Antetokounmpo will wait until January 6 before signing a four-year, $275 million maximum extension.

The delay is procedural: waiting until January 6 gives Miami more flexibility in what they can attach to the deal. But it also means spending the first three months of the season on a superstar playing without a long-term commitment locked in — a situation the unofficial minicamp's clear signals of buy-in help manage narratively.

The Heat open official training camp on September 29 at Kaseya Center in Miami. Their first preseason game is October 3 against the Toronto Raptors in Quebec City — a Kawhi Leonard storyline layered directly on top of their opener.

What's Next

Official camp opens in 16 days. All eyes will be on how quickly this new group develops on-court cohesion — and whether Giannis's commitment to the minicamp translates into the kind of early-season performance that cements Miami as a legitimate Eastern Conference title contender. The odds have already moved significantly: Miami went from 30-to-1 to 18-to-1 after the Giannis acquisition. By the time camp closes, those numbers may shift again.
