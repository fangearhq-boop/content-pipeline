Jalen Duren Is Reportedly Open to the $9.6M Qualifying Offer. The Pistons Have 18 Days to Respond.

By Damon Pierce | September 13, 2026

With the October 1 deadline approaching and no deal in sight, the Detroit center's camp is increasingly willing to bet on restricted free agency — a move that would reshape the Pistons' future.

The staring contest is entering its final weeks.

The Detroit Pistons and center Jalen Duren remain stuck in a contract standoff, and with the October 1 qualifying offer deadline now 18 days away, the situation is crystallizing around a stark choice. The Pistons are offering a five-year extension in the range of $190 million. Duren's camp wants $200 million or more. According to multiple reports, the 22-year-old All-NBA center is increasingly open to accepting the $9.6 million qualifying offer and entering restricted free agency next summer rather than close the gap on the Pistons' terms.

ESPN described the standoff simply: "This is just a staring contest."

Understanding the Qualifying Offer

The qualifying offer is a one-year tender worth $9.6 million. If Duren accepts it, he plays the 2026-27 season at that salary and enters restricted free agency in 2027 — meaning the Pistons would have the right to match any offer sheet he receives from another team.

The strategic logic for Duren: prove his value again over a full season, then attract a maximum offer sheet from a competing team, forcing Detroit to either match it or let him walk. The risk: injury, reduced performance, or a market that values him lower than expected in a new free agency class.

The strategic logic for the Pistons: sign him now at $190 million and remove all uncertainty. Losing Duren would create a massive hole in the middle after their No. 1 Eastern Conference seed finish last season.

What Makes Duren Worth the Standoff

Duren was elected to the All-NBA Third Team last season — a designation that made him eligible for a designated rookie max extension worth far more than what the Pistons are offering. His maximum contract value is approximately $287.1 million over five years. Detroit's offer at $190 million represents roughly two-thirds of that ceiling.

At 22, Duren has the argument that his best years are still ahead of him. His defensive anchor role — a rim protector in a league shifting back toward interior defense — is increasingly valuable. A player of his age and caliber settling for $38 million per year when the max is $57 million annually would mean leaving significant money on the table.

Pistons training camp opens September 29. If no deal is reached by October 1, the qualifying offer becomes the only mechanism keeping Duren under Detroit's control in any form.

The Franchise Stakes

For the Pistons, this is not a comfortable situation. General manager Troy Weaver's team is built around Duren's interior dominance. Adding another lottery pick to the rebuild or finding a stopgap center in 2027 free agency would set the franchise back years.

The $10 million gap separating the two sides — if current reporting is accurate — seems small in the context of a five-year franchise relationship. Whether both sides see it that way is the core of the stalemate.

What's Next

The October 1 deadline is firm. After that date, the Pistons can no longer offer Duren a rookie-scale extension — he would enter the 2026-27 season either on the qualifying offer or on a new contract. Watch for any last-minute reporting between now and September 28, when most teams open camp, suggesting whether talks have accelerated or stalled entirely. Either way, the answer is coming soon.
