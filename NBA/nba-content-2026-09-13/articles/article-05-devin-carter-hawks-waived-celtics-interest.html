The Hawks Just Waived the No. 13 Pick From 2024. The Celtics Are Reportedly Watching.

By Marcus Cole | September 13, 2026

Devin Carter's unusual journey from lottery pick to waiver wire has Boston interested — and the 24-year-old may be exactly the depth piece a defending champion needs.

Lottery picks don't often hit the waiver wire in September. When one does, contenders pay attention.

The Atlanta Hawks officially waived Devin Carter, the No. 13 overall pick from the 2024 NBA Draft, on September 11. Carter, 24, cleared waivers and is now a free agent — and per NBA insider Jake Fischer, the Boston Celtics have already expressed interest in signing him.

Carter's path to free agency was winding. Selected by the Sacramento Kings in 2024, he spent two seasons working his way into the rotation before being traded to Atlanta this past summer as part of a cap-clearing exercise designed to help the Hawks escape the league's second apron luxury tax threshold. The Hawks, committed to their own rebuild, had no long-term use for a developing guard on a low-cost deal — so they waived him rather than carrying a roster spot through a condensed training camp period.

What Carter Brings

Carter's production in Sacramento last season — 8.9 points, 3.3 rebounds, and 2.7 assists in 18.4 minutes per game across 38 appearances — won't generate headlines on its own. But the numbers understate his profile.

He is a 24-year-old former lottery pick with legitimate two-way potential. At Providence College, he was known for his defensive instincts and athleticism before being drafted in the lottery for his upside. Two seasons of professional development haven't diminished that potential — they've simply clarified that he needs the right system and opportunity to maximize it.

The Celtics would offer exactly that. Boston's rotation-building philosophy under their coaching staff prioritizes two-way players who accept defined roles, defend multiple positions, and make quick decisions off the ball. Carter's profile fits those criteria.

Why This Matters for Boston

The Celtics are defending champions with most of their core intact. Their offseason focus has been on depth and health rather than stars — filling the gaps behind a starting five that already has championship DNA.

At minimum wage or a veterans' minimum contract, Carter would cost virtually nothing while providing the organization a legitimate developmental asset. If he doesn't work out in the rotation, he's a training camp cut. If he clicks — and his lottery pedigree suggests the tools are there — he becomes genuine playoff depth for a team built to compete deep into May.

Roster construction at this level is often about collecting options. Carter, available in September at age 24, is exactly the kind of option contenders covet.

What's Next

Carter must clear the 48-hour waiver period before becoming a free agent eligible to sign anywhere. Once clear, expect the Celtics to move quickly if interest is genuine. Boston's training camp opens in late September, and they will want their full roster assembled for early practices. Watch for a reported signing in the next several days — or, if Boston passes, for another contender to pounce on a former lottery pick with upside still untapped at 24.
