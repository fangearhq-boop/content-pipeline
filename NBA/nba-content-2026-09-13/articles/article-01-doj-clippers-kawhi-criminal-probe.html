DOJ Opens Criminal Probe Into Clippers-Kawhi Leonard Scoreboard Deal — Here's What It Means

By Jake Torres | September 13, 2026

Federal prosecutors in Brooklyn have issued subpoenas in the criminal investigation into the Los Angeles Clippers' alleged kickback scheme with Kawhi Leonard — escalating the scandal to a new level entirely.

The NBA's biggest offseason scandal just went federal.

The U.S. Department of Justice has opened a criminal investigation into the Los Angeles Clippers and their financial dealings with Kawhi Leonard, with prosecutors from the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Eastern District of New York leading the probe. At least one subpoena has already been issued, according to multiple reports — a sign that the investigation has moved beyond preliminary inquiry into active evidence gathering.

The core allegation: the Clippers used a massive scoreboard contract for Intuit Dome as a vehicle to funnel money to Leonard through no-show endorsement deals, effectively paying the star forward beyond his NBA contract in violation of the league's salary cap rules. League investigators said team officials directed kickbacks from the scoreboard deal and even set the terms of endorsement arrangements on Leonard's behalf.

What the NBA Already Did

The NBA dropped significant penalties earlier this month. Owner Steve Ballmer is suspended for one year from all team and league activities. General manager Lawrence Frank received a six-month suspension. The Clippers surrendered five first-round draft picks — 2029 through 2033. Gillian Zucker was also suspended for one year.

On the player side, Kawhi Leonard was fined $700,000. His business manager, Dennis Robertson, was banned from conducting NBA business for five years. The league determined Leonard benefited from the arrangement, though investigators did not characterize him as the architect of it.

What the DOJ Probe Changes

Everything — and nothing, at least for now.

Federal criminal investigations are in a different category than league discipline. If charges ultimately materialize, the individuals involved could face consequences far beyond fines and suspensions. However, investigators described the probe as being in its "earliest stages," and the scope of who might face scrutiny remains unclear.

The Department of Justice has also reportedly been in contact with Daktronics, the scoreboard manufacturer, according to earlier reporting — suggesting prosecutors are building out a fuller picture of the financial relationships involved.

What Happens to Kawhi Leonard

For now, Leonard's trade to the Toronto Raptors proceeds unimpeded. He is not suspended and faces no criminal scrutiny that has been made public. The trade — which brought Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, unprotected first-round picks in 2031 and 2033, and additional picks and swaps to the Clippers — was ratified by the NBA alongside the announcement of penalties.

Raptors training camp opens September 28 in Quebec City. Leonard, who helped Toronto win its only NBA championship in 2019 before departing in free agency, is set to return to the city where he became a legend.

The reception will be complicated. Raptors fans may not care much about what happens to the franchise Leonard left. But the cloud of a federal investigation — however tangential to Leonard himself — will follow this story into the season.

What's Next

The investigation's trajectory is impossible to predict at this stage. The DOJ typically does not comment on ongoing investigations, meaning updates will likely come through reporting rather than official channels. Whether the probe results in criminal charges — and who might be charged — remains entirely open.

For the Clippers, the immediate future looks grim. Without five first-round picks and with Ballmer sidelined, the franchise faces years of rebuilding under extraordinary scrutiny. The city that just opened Intuit Dome is watching one of the most unusual chapters in NBA ownership history unfold in federal court.
