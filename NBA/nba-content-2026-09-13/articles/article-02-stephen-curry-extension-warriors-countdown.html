15 Days to Warriors Media Day: Will Stephen Curry Sign His Extension?

By Marcus Cole | September 13, 2026

With training camp approaching and ESPN insiders signaling a max deal is likely, the only question left about Curry's future in Golden State may be the timing of the announcement.

The most important contract decision in Golden State Warriors history is drawing closer to its deadline.

Stephen Curry became eligible for a contract extension on August 29, and with Warriors media day scheduled for September 28 — just 15 days away — the window for an announcement before camp has never been narrower. The two-year maximum extension would be worth approximately $136.7 million total, covering Curry's age-38 and 39 seasons.

Multiple reports confirm mutual interest between Curry and the organization. And ESPN Warriors insider Anthony Slater said this week that he "wouldn't be surprised" to see Curry take the maximum on offer.

Three Paths, One Expected Outcome

According to reports, the Warriors presented Curry with three options at the start of the extension window: a one-year maximum deal, the full two-year maximum, or the option to enter free agency for the first time in his career. The first two keep him in Golden State; the third would open his services to the entire league.

Curry has been publicly clear about his preference. He has stated his desire to finish his career as a Warrior — a statement that carries real weight given his history with the organization and his four championship rings won in the Bay Area.

The two-year maximum would put Curry under contract through the 2027-28 season, when he would be 40 years old. For a franchise that built its dynasty around him, it would represent a dignified closing chapter rather than an abrupt, uncertain goodbye.

The Hawaii Camp and What Comes After

The Warriors open their training camp on September 29 at BYU-Hawaii — one day after media day. The proximity of the two dates creates a natural pressure point. Teams historically prefer to resolve major contract questions before camp opens so that player-media dynamics are settled from day one.

The organization also recently added veteran big man Al Horford, who declined a $5.97 million player option before re-signing on a two-year, $14 million deal with a second-year player option. The move signals Golden State's intent to remain competitive while managing cap flexibility — a balancing act that Curry's extension will also inform.

The Legacy Dimension

Curry is, by any reasonable measure, one of the five greatest players in NBA history. His transformation of the game — the three-pointer as offensive weapon, the pull-up from 35 feet, the 73-win season — reshaped how basketball is played at every level. He is the Warriors' all-time leader in virtually every offensive category.

Whether he plays one more year or two, the extension signing will be a moment. Curry has spent his entire NBA career in the same uniform. For that to continue through the end requires nothing more than a signature — and by all accounts, both sides want exactly that.

What's Next

Media day on September 28 will be the clearest signal point. If Curry takes the podium with an extension already signed, the question is settled. If he attends without a deal, expect reporting on the timeline to accelerate rapidly. The two-year maximum remains the likeliest outcome; the only uncertainty is whether the announcement comes before or after camp opens on September 29.
