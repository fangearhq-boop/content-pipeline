### STORY 1: Kawhi Leonard — DOJ Criminal Investigation

**Post 1A** | Window: 9:00 AM | Tier 1 | Long-Form

The Kawhi Leonard scandal just got a lot bigger.

Federal prosecutors in Brooklyn — the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Eastern District of New York — have opened a criminal investigation into the Los Angeles Clippers and their dealings with Leonard. Subpoenas have already been issued, according to multiple reports.

The allegation is serious: the Clippers allegedly used a massive scoreboard contract for Intuit Dome as a vehicle to funnel kickbacks to Leonard in the form of no-show endorsement deals — a way to pay him beyond his NBA contract and circumvent the league's salary cap rules.

The NBA already dropped significant penalties: the Clippers surrendered five first-round draft picks (2029 through 2033), owner Steve Ballmer is suspended for one year, and Lawrence Frank was banned for six months. Leonard himself was fined $700,000, and his business manager Dennis Robertson is banned from NBA business for five years.

But the criminal probe is a different magnitude entirely. Whether charges ever materialize remains to be seen — the investigation is described as being in its earliest stages. What is clear: Kawhi Leonard's trade to the Toronto Raptors is proceeding, and he is not suspended.

The franchise he's leaving, though, is facing unprecedented legal scrutiny.

### STORY 2: Stephen Curry Extension — Warriors Media Day Clock

**Post 2A** | Window: 1:00 PM | Tier 1 | Long-Form

Fifteen days. That's how long until the Golden State Warriors hold their media day on September 28 — and possibly learn where Stephen Curry stands on his contract extension.

Curry became eligible for a two-year maximum extension on August 29. The deal would be worth roughly $136.7 million total — north of $60 million per season through his age-38 and 39 campaigns. The Warriors have reportedly given him three paths: a one-year max, a two-year max, or free agency.

ESPN's Anthony Slater, who covers the Warriors closely, said this week he "wouldn't be surprised" to see Curry take the maximum on offer. League sources confirm there is mutual interest between Curry and Golden State. The veteran guard has been clear: he wants to retire a Warrior.

The Hawaii training camp opens September 29, one day after media day. The expectation around the league is that we'll have a clearer picture — or perhaps a signed deal — by then.

For a franchise that has already said goodbye to so much of its dynasty core, keeping Curry through the end of his playing days would be a meaningful close to the greatest chapter in Warriors history.

### STORY 3: Jalen Duren / Pistons Stalemate

**Post 3A** | Window: 11:00 AM | Tier 2 | Long-Form

The Jalen Duren standoff is entering its most critical phase.

Detroit is offering Duren a five-year extension in the range of $190 million. Duren's camp wants $200 million or more. The gap seems small — but the October 1 qualifying offer deadline is now just 18 days away, and both sides appear to be dug in.

The wildcard: Duren, 22 and coming off an All-NBA Third Team season, is reportedly increasingly willing to accept the $9.6 million qualifying offer and test restricted free agency next summer. That's a massive gamble — he'd be betting on himself to stay healthy and perform at the same level, then secure a bigger deal in 2027.

For the Pistons, losing their franchise center after an Eastern Conference No. 1 seed season would be a crushing blow. For Duren, turning down $190 million guaranteed is a high-stakes bet on his own future value.

ESPN described the situation simply: "This is just a staring contest."

Someone is going to blink. The question is who — and when.

### STORY 4: Miami Heat — Giannis Minicamp

**Post 4A** | Window: 9:00 AM | Tier 2 | Long-Form

The Miami Heat's new era is already underway — and official training camp hasn't even opened yet.

Heat players, led by Giannis Antetokounmpo, organized an unofficial minicamp in California this week. The new additions — Giannis, Bobby Portis, Klay Thompson, and Tim Hardaway Jr. — joined the returning core for a session that mixed basketball work with serious team bonding: golf outings, karaoke sessions, and a day out on Klay Thompson's boat.

Official training camp opens September 29 at Kaseya Center. The Heat open their preseason schedule on October 3 against the Toronto Raptors in Quebec City.

With Giannis extension-eligible on October 1, and the expectation being that he waits until January 6 to sign a four-year, $275 million max, the Heat are operating on a foundation of trust right now.

### STORY 5: Devin Carter — Hawks Waive

**Post 5A** | Window: 1:00 PM | Tier 2 | Long-Form

A 2024 lottery pick just hit the waiver wire — and the Boston Celtics are paying attention.

The Atlanta Hawks officially waived Devin Carter, the No. 13 overall pick from the 2024 NBA Draft, according to multiple reports. Carter was originally drafted by the Sacramento Kings before being traded to Atlanta this past summer as part of a cap-clearing move.

The 24-year-old Providence College product showed promise in limited minutes last season, averaging 8.9 points, 3.3 rebounds, and 2.7 assists in 18.4 minutes per game across 38 appearances for Sacramento.

Insider Jake Fischer reports that the Boston Celtics are interested. For a defending champion looking to add depth at a minimum cost, Carter's combination of youth, two-way potential, and former lottery pedigree makes him an intriguing target.

Carter must clear waivers before becoming a free agent. The window is now — and it sounds like Boston may be ready to move.
