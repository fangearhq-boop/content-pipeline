Embiid's First Surgery-Free Summer Convinced LeBron to Choose Philly

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | July 27, 2026

The recruiting pitch that landed LeBron James in Philadelphia did not happen at a press conference or through back-channel negotiations between agents. It happened in a private two-hour meeting in Los Angeles, about one month before the announcement, when Joel Embiid gave LeBron the one answer that mattered.

James, entering his 24th NBA season at age 41, had one overriding concern about the 76ers: Embiid's health. The center had spent the better part of a decade battling injuries that kept him off the floor during critical stretches. LeBron pressed him directly about it.

Embiid told him this had been his first surgery-free summer in memory. No procedures, no rehabilitation setbacks, no lengthy recovery timelines. Just training. The 7-footer said he felt completely healthy and was committed to the work ahead. That declaration, according to reports, is what convinced LeBron that the 76ers partnership could actually work.

The Deal That Surprised Everyone

LeBron James formally signed a 2-year, $8 million contract with the Philadelphia 76ers, with a player option. The financial terms tell only part of the story — James is the lowest-paid of the three stars the 76ers have now assembled. Jaylen Brown, acquired from Boston, and Joel Embiid, the franchise cornerstone, are the primary contracts.

In his announcement, James was unambiguous about his motivation: "I still want to compete, to win and to have a chance at the feeling of winning another championship." He also noted: "I believe I can help make the Philadelphia 76ers a championship team and I am so excited to energize a new fan base and start this incredible journey one last time."

Rich Paul, LeBron's agent, confirmed the contract terms to ESPN.

The Big Three on Paper

The 76ers now have three players who, combined, have won five MVP awards and earned 32 All-Star selections. LeBron James is a four-time MVP and four-time champion. Joel Embiid won the MVP award and is one of the league's most dominant offensive forces when healthy. Jaylen Brown, acquired from the Celtics, is an All-Star wing who has shown he can carry a team.

The addition moved Philadelphia's championship odds dramatically. Before the signing, the 76ers were 20-1 to win the 2026-27 NBA title. Within hours of LeBron's announcement, those odds had shifted to 10-1, making them one of the clear frontrunners. Philadelphia has not won an NBA championship since 1983.

Why Philly Over the Other Contenders

LeBron spent weeks — ultimately more than a month — weighing his options. The Miami Heat and Golden State Warriors were considered serious contenders to land him. The Cleveland Cavaliers, where James won a championship in 2016, were also in the mix. The 76ers, despite having Brown and Embiid on the roster, were viewed by many observers as a long shot to win the recruiting battle.

What ultimately separated Philadelphia was the combination of two things: the Embiid health conversation and the quality of the co-stars already in place. James did not need to be the best player on this team — a role that might have been uncomfortable elsewhere. With Embiid and Brown, he could slot into the third-best player position and still give the team a significant edge.

What's Next

Philadelphia is expected to hold an introductory press conference for LeBron James in the coming days. The 76ers will also need to finalize the rest of their roster around the Big Three, identifying role players who complement their style of play. Eyes will be on the Eastern Conference, where title odds have already been reshuffled. If Embiid's surgery-free summer translates to a full, healthy season, the 76ers could be exactly as dangerous as the odds suggest.
