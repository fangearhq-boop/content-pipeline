Warriors Strike Out on LeBron and DeRozan: What Curry's Trio Does Now

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | July 27, 2026

The Golden State Warriors spent this offseason chasing a championship reset. They cleared cap space, made their recruiting pitches, and watched every major target choose somewhere else. LeBron James signed with the 76ers. DeMar DeRozan chose the Bulls over Draymond Green's personal appeal.

The result is that Golden State enters the 2026-27 season with essentially the same core it started the offseason with, minus the possibility of a transcendent free agent addition. The framework — Curry, Kristaps Porzingis, and Draymond — is still competitive. Whether it's championship-competitive is the question nobody can answer yet.

The Core That Remains

Stephen Curry remains the best shooter in NBA history and one of the game's most impactful offensive players. Even at his current age, defenses still must plan their entire scheme around where he is on the floor. His gravity creates open looks for teammates and opens the paint for drivers. As long as Curry is healthy and elite, the Warriors are a threat.

Kristaps Porzingis re-signed on a 2-year, $40 million deal, opting to stay in Golden State rather than test the open market. The 7-foot-3 Latvian provides the Warriors with legitimate scoring from the perimeter, post scoring, and shot-blocking — a combination of skills that is genuinely rare. His durability has been the consistent knock on him throughout his career, but when healthy, he elevates any lineup he is in.

Draymond Green declined his $27.7 million player option earlier this month before signing a 4-year, $100 million extension to remain the heart of the Warriors' defense and connective tissue. Draymond makes the team more coherent in ways that do not appear in traditional box scores. His impact on both communication and scheme execution is difficult to quantify but easy to see.

The Gap Between Competitive and Championship Caliber

General manager Mike Dunleavy acknowledged before the free agency period that the Warriors needed "everything." That candid assessment suggested the front office understood the gap between where the roster was and where it needed to be. The pursuit of LeBron and DeRozan were attempts to close that gap in one stroke. Both attempts failed.

The Eastern Conference has shifted dramatically. The 76ers now have LeBron, Jaylen Brown, and Joel Embiid. The Miami Heat still have Giannis Antetokounmpo. The Boston Celtics have Paul George. The landscape for a Western Conference team like Golden State is challenging: they would need to navigate a gauntlet of Western playoff series and then face one of those Eastern juggernauts in the Finals.

A Curry-led Warriors team can win playoff series. But winning four rounds in this league against this level of competition requires more depth than what they currently have.

What Comes Next

The Warriors still have tools available. The trade market could unlock a second star at a cost of young assets or future picks. The mid-level exception and other contract mechanisms could allow them to add veteran scoring. There may also be surprising name that emerges — someone the league-wide conversation has not fully focused on — who fits what Golden State needs.

The more difficult question is whether Curry's remaining prime years will align with the right moment for the Warriors to build another championship contender. He has been central to four titles in Golden State. The organization owes him a legitimate chance to compete for a fifth.

How the Warriors use the rest of this summer and the upcoming trade deadline will say everything about whether this front office is still capable of building around their franchise legend before time runs out.

What's Next

Golden State will now turn its attention to the trade market and remaining free agents. Training camp opens in late September. The 2026-27 NBA schedule is expected to be released within days, now that LeBron's destination is settled — and Warriors fans will want to circle the date when Curry faces the Philadelphia 76ers Big Three for the first time.
