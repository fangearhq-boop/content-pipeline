DeMar DeRozan Returns to Chicago: Chose Bulls Over Warriors Despite Draymond's Pitch

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | July 27, 2026

Draymond Green made the personal call. He pitched DeMar DeRozan on joining the Golden State Warriors, offering the 6x All-Star guard the chance to chase a championship ring alongside Stephen Curry in the final chapter of one of the NBA's most storied franchises. DeRozan listened — and turned it down.

Instead, DeRozan is returning to Chicago, signing with the Bulls and heading back to the city where his NBA career found its second life. The decision leaves the Warriors empty-handed in a free agency period where they missed on both LeBron James and their secondary target in the same summer.

Still Producing at 36

DeRozan is not a player in decline. The Sacramento Kings released him as a cap casualty, not a performance one. In his final season with Sacramento, he averaged 18.4 points per game on nearly 50 percent shooting — numbers that would represent a quality starter's season for any player in the league. At 36 years old, turning 37 in August, DeRozan remains one of the most efficient mid-range scorers the game has ever produced.

His decision was not between competing offers from teams at the same stage of their rebuild. The Warriors, with Stephen Curry, Kristaps Porzingis, and Draymond Green, had legitimate championship aspirations. The Bulls are beginning a new chapter under first-year head coach Tiago Splitter. These are not equivalent destinations from a ring-chasing perspective, which makes DeRozan's choice all the more striking.

What the Bulls Are Getting

Chicago is getting one of the most reliable offensive players on the market. DeRozan's mid-range game — the floaters, the pull-up jumpers, the footwork in the post — has always been his foundation, and it ages better than athleticism-dependent skills. He has been a clutch performer throughout his career, consistently making shots when the game is on the line.

Tiago Splitter inherits DeRozan as the centerpiece of his first NBA head coaching job. Splitter's background includes time as an assistant with the San Antonio Spurs, where the culture of player development and systematic basketball produced multiple deep playoff runs. His ability to integrate DeRozan's scoring with a developing roster will be one of the more interesting storylines of the 2026-27 season.

What This Means for the Warriors

Golden State entered this offseason with a clear two-target plan: LeBron first, DeRozan as the backup. Both said no. The Warriors are left with their current core — Curry, Porzingis, and Draymond — a group that represents real playoff capability but does not project as a championship frontrunner in a loaded Western Conference.

The front office now faces a different kind of question heading into the rest of the summer. Do they make a trade to upgrade the roster? Do they pursue remaining free agents at a lower tier? Or do they commit to the current group and trust Curry and Draymond to lead a playoff run that could turn into something more?

Those are difficult questions, made more difficult by the fact that Curry's championship window is narrowing with every passing season.

What's Next

DeRozan's arrival changes Chicago's immediate ceiling. The Bulls will open the season with veteran scoring leadership in place and a new coach trying to build something lasting. For the Warriors, the pivot to whatever comes next begins now — whether that means trades, mid-level exception signings, or reassessing the roster as is. Both teams have real decisions ahead before training camp opens in September.
