Kawhi Leonard Investigation: Fact-Finding Done — NBA Now Decides Raptors Trade Fate

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | July 27, 2026

The outside law firm hired by the NBA to investigate the Kawhi Leonard and Clippers case has completed its fact-finding work, according to reports from The Athletic. The development moves the months-long saga into its most critical stage yet — the NBA must now review the findings and determine whether Leonard, the Los Angeles Clippers, or owner Steve Ballmer face discipline.

The answer will determine whether a blockbuster trade with the Toronto Raptors goes through, gets renegotiated, or is voided entirely.

What the Investigation Has Been Examining

At the center of the probe is whether the Clippers used an endorsement deal to improperly funnel money to Kawhi Leonard, circumventing the NBA's salary cap. The company involved — Aspiration, a green banking startup — paid Leonard approximately $28 million in endorsement fees while simultaneously holding a $300 million, 23-year arena naming rights agreement with the Clippers. Ballmer made a personal $60 million investment in Aspiration as well.

Aspiration has since gone bankrupt, and one of the company's executives, Michael Sanberg, was convicted and sentenced to 14 years in prison in an unrelated fraud case. The scope of the NBA's investigation expanded earlier this month to include a second, previously undisclosed endorsement deal connected to Leonard.

With the law firm's work now complete, the league is no longer in the information-gathering phase. The focus shifts to what, if anything, the evidence shows — and what the NBA chooses to do about it.

What Happens Next

The NBA has several potential outcomes at its disposal. A finding of no wrongdoing represents the cleanest path forward and would clear the way for the Raptors-Clippers trade to be completed immediately. A suspension of Leonard — even a brief one — could complicate the terms of the trade since Toronto would be acquiring a player who cannot participate in training camp or early games.

More significant consequences, such as draft pick penalties for the Clippers, could force both teams back to the negotiating table to rework the terms of the agreed deal. The original agreement would send Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, two unprotected first-round picks in 2031 and 2033, a 2027 first-round pick swap, and two second-round picks to Los Angeles — with Kawhi Leonard returning to Toronto.

Commissioner Adam Silver said earlier this month that the investigation must be resolved before the start of the 2026-27 season in October. With the law firm done, that October deadline is now very much achievable.

A Long Wait for Toronto

The Raptors have been in limbo since June 30, when the trade was announced. Toronto has been unable to finalize the rest of its roster around the assumption that Leonard is incoming — any major roster moves had to account for the uncertainty about whether the deal would actually close. That uncertainty has been uniquely frustrating for a franchise that has been methodically rebuilding since the LeBron/Giannis blockbuster era reshaped the Eastern Conference.

If the league clears the path for the trade to be completed, Kawhi Leonard would return to the city where he delivered Toronto's only NBA championship in 2019. He was the best player on that team and remains one of the most talented two-way players in the game when healthy — although his own injury history is a legitimate concern for the Raptors.

What's Next

The NBA is expected to announce its findings within weeks. The Raptors and Clippers are watching closely, as are the broader league and fan bases who have followed this case for nearly a year. If the ruling is swift and clean, training camp preparation for both franchises could begin in earnest before September.
