If Embiid's answer holds up when the games count — if he stays on the floor — this Big Three could be everything the city has waited four decades to see.

### STORY 2: Kawhi Leonard Investigation — Fact-Finding Complete

#### Long-Form Post — 11:00 AM ET

Today, that may finally be changing.

The outside law firm hired by the NBA to investigate the Kawhi Leonard–Aspiration case has reportedly completed its fact-finding work. The investigation has been probing whether the Clippers improperly funneled money to Leonard through a $28 million endorsement deal with Aspiration — a green banking company that has since gone bankrupt.

The Clippers also had a $300 million, 23-year arena naming rights deal with Aspiration, and owner Steve Ballmer made a $60 million personal investment in the company. The scope of the probe expanded earlier this month to include a second, previously undisclosed endorsement deal.

Now the NBA must review the law firm's findings and decide what — if anything — to do. The outcomes range from no wrongdoing (cleanest path to completing the Raptors trade) to suspensions, fines, or draft pick penalties (which could force both teams back to the negotiating table).

The agreed trade would send Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, two unprotected first-round picks, a pick swap, and two seconds to the Clippers — with Kawhi returning to Toronto.

Commissioner Adam Silver has said the investigation must be resolved before the season starts in October. With the law firm done, that deadline is now in play.

Toronto has been patient. Resolution may finally be coming.
