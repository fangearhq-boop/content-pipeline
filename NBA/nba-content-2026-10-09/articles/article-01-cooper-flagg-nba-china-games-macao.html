Cooper Flagg Steps Onto the World Stage: NBA China Games 2026 Preview

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | October 9, 2026

Cooper Flagg has played one professional season. He has already been the most talked-about young player in basketball. Tonight, in Macao, China, he becomes something else entirely: a global NBA ambassador on the biggest international stage the preseason offers.

The NBA China Games 2026 tip off tonight at The Venetian Arena in Macao, with Flagg's Dallas Mavericks facing the Houston Rockets in the first of two preseason games. Game 1 is set for 7:30 p.m. CST, with Game 2 following on October 11. This is the 16th edition of the NBA China Games — and its most anticipated in years.

Why Flagg Is the Story

The NBA selected these two franchises for a reason. Dallas brings the 2025 No. 1 overall pick — a 19-year-old from Maine who dominated college basketball at Duke and immediately became one of the most talked-about rookies in league history. Flagg joins a Mavericks squad that also includes Kyrie Irving, giving Dallas one of the more intriguing pairings of youth and experience in the Western Conference.

For Flagg, this is a new kind of test. He has handled the American spotlight well. He dominated summer league. He looked composed and ready in early preseason appearances domestically. But stepping off a plane in Macao and playing in front of a crowd that has followed NBA basketball across a 14-hour time difference — that's a different energy entirely.

The question isn't whether Flagg is good enough. It's whether the stage grows or shrinks in his first international assignment.

Houston's Star Power

The Rockets aren't here to be a backdrop. Houston brings Kevin Durant, Alperen Sengun and Amen Thompson to Macao — a trio that represents the league's present and its future simultaneously. Durant, playing his first full season with Houston after signing last summer, gives the Rockets a legitimate superstar alongside one of the most technically skilled big men in basketball in Sengun.

Amen Thompson — the younger half of the Thompson twins, who each signed contracts worth more than $100M this past offseason — adds athleticism and defensive intensity. The Rockets have built a legitimate contender, and they're here to remind Asia's basketball fans of that.

The Event Around the Game

The NBA China Games have grown into a full cultural week, not just a basketball game. The 2026 edition includes NBA House, NBA Fan Day on October 10, community youth clinics, a Community Impact Week and halftime performances from Asian artists. Tickets sold through Cotai Ticketing, Taobao 88VIP, Trip.com and MPay. The games will be broadcast in more than 200 countries and territories.

The venue itself carries a layer of local significance: The Venetian Arena in Macao is operated by Sands China, whose parent company is connected to Mavericks governor Patrick Dumont's family. The connection between the franchise and the venue adds a personal touch to what is already a marquee event.

What's Next

Following the two Macao games — tonight and October 11 — both franchises return stateside to finish their preseason preparations. Opening night rosters must be set by October 19, with the regular season tipping off October 20.

For Cooper Flagg, tonight is not about points or statistics. It's about carrying himself like a franchise player on the world's most watched stage. That's a skill that takes time to develop. But if the last 12 months are any guide, Flagg learns fast.
