Hukporti's Torn Achilles Exposes the Sixers' Hidden Vulnerability

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | October 9, 2026

The Philadelphia 76ers built a super-team this offseason. LeBron James. Joel Embiid. Jaylen Brown. Tyrese Maxey. Klay Thompson. On paper, it is the most star-studded Eastern Conference roster assembled in years.

But super-teams are only as strong as their depth — and before the preseason was even a week old, that depth took a hit the franchise never saw coming.

Ariel Hukporti, the 24-year-old center signed in July to be Embiid's primary backup, ruptured his right Achilles tendon in the first quarter of Philadelphia's preseason opener against the Knicks on October 5. The injury occurred without contact; Hukporti fell to the floor while defending Karl-Anthony Towns. An MRI confirmed the diagnosis on October 6. Surgery was performed successfully the same day. His season is over.

The Second Achilles

What makes this injury more than just a roster setback is the personal history behind it. Hukporti tore his left Achilles in 2022 while playing for Melbourne United in Australia's National Basketball League — a devastating injury at the start of a young career that requires extraordinary physical and mental recovery. He fought back, earned an NBA contract, won an NBA championship with the Knicks last season, and parlayed that into a one-year, $3.4M deal with Philadelphia in July 2026.

Now, at 24, he faces his second Achilles tear — this time on the other leg. The prognosis for recovery from a second Achilles rupture is more complicated than the first. Hukporti's career path just got harder again.

The Cap Crunch That Follows

Beyond the human element, the Sixers face a practical problem: replacing Hukporti is not simple. Philadelphia is operating only $1.8 million under the first apron — a hard salary cap threshold that restricts the team's ability to add payroll without waiving a player or engineering a small trade.

Adem Bona moves into the primary backup role behind Embiid. Bona is a capable defender with good energy and solid length, but he lacks the combination of size, championship-level experience and defensive versatility that Hukporti brought. If Embiid faces foul trouble or misses any time — always a concern given his history — the Sixers' interior depth will be tested early and often.

Does It Change the Outlook?

The honest answer is: not dramatically for the regular season, but meaningfully for April and beyond.

Philadelphia's top five remains elite. LeBron James, at 41, showed in the preseason opener — 22 points for Jaylen Brown without him — that this team's ceiling does not depend on Hukporti. The Sixers can win a title even with Bona as the primary backup center. The roster still has more star power than any team in the East.

But roster construction matters in the playoffs, where a foul-plagued Embiid can make a backup center the difference between a series win and a loss. The Sixers entered October believing they had adequately addressed that concern. As of October 6, they do not.

What's Next

Philadelphia can explore waiver wire options, a cheap trade or converting a two-way contract player to a full roster spot. None of those solutions are as clean as what they had with Hukporti, but the front office will work through the options before October 19, when opening-night rosters must be set.

For Hukporti, the road ahead is long and familiar in the worst way. But it's worth remembering that he has already walked this path once — and came out the other side as an NBA champion. That kind of resilience doesn't disappear with one more setback.
