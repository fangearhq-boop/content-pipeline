Curry Takes a Discount to Stay a Warrior: Inside the 2-Year, $116M Extension

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | October 9, 2026

Stephen Curry has never worn another NBA jersey. He has never played for another city, another coaching staff, another front office. In an era when player movement defines the league and no franchise loyalty is ever truly certain, that fact alone makes Curry a singular figure.

He just made it even more singular. Curry agreed to a 2-year, $116M contract extension with the Golden State Warriors, running through the 2028-29 season with a player option in the final year. By the time the deal expires, Curry will have spent roughly 20 seasons with one franchise.

He had options. He chose this.

The Discount He Chose

Under NBA salary rules, Curry was eligible for up to $136.7M over two years — a figure that would have set a record for his age bracket and maximized his earning potential in what will likely be the final major contract of his career. He left more than $20M on the table.

According to ESPN, Curry ultimately decided that security and legacy mattered more than earning the maximum. He believes in the Warriors' organization, the continuity they've built, and the opportunity to finish his career playing in a system perfectly designed for his skills. That belief translated into a concrete financial decision.

It's easy to say "loyalty over money" when you've already earned hundreds of millions of dollars. But the choice still carries weight. At this stage of his career, another organization would have paid him more. He said no.

What Golden State Gets

The Warriors are betting that Curry can perform at an elite level deep into his late 30s, and recent history supports that bet. In the preseason against the Los Angeles Lakers — a game Golden State won 124-98 — Curry scored 16 points in under 15 minutes before resting for the night. The sharpness is still there. The shooting touch, the off-ball movement, the gravity that opens the entire floor for Golden State's system — none of that has diminished.

The player option in the second year (2028-29) gives Curry flexibility if circumstances change, and gives the Warriors some planning room as they manage a roster being built around the future. But the expectation from both sides is that this deal carries him through the end of his career in Golden State.

A Legacy Sealed

Curry changed basketball. The three-point revolution that now defines how every team at every level plays the game traces back to him. Four championships. Two MVPs. Multiple All-Star selections. The greatest shooter in the history of the sport, by almost any reasonable measure.

And he did it all in one place.

When the Hall of Fame comes calling — which it will, the moment he's eligible — Curry will go in wearing a Warriors cap, without any debate or ambiguity about which franchise to honor. That's a kind of career clarity that almost no player in the modern NBA achieves.

What's Next

With the extension signed, Curry's focus shifts entirely to the 2026-27 season. Golden State has a competitive roster, and the Warriors' regular-season opener is approaching. Curry's preseason work has already shown he's ready. The question now isn't whether Steph Curry is still capable of playing elite basketball — the question is how many more championships he can add to a legacy that's already cemented.

Warrior for life. That phrase isn't just a marketing slogan anymore. It's a signed contract.
