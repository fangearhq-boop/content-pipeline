Down 24, Still Won: Pelicans Give Jamahl Mosley the Perfect Welcome

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | October 9, 2026

There are first wins as a head coach, and then there are first wins like this.

Jamahl Mosley made his debut as head coach of the New Orleans Pelicans on Wednesday night at the BOK Center in Tulsa, and the circumstances couldn't have been more dramatic. The Pelicans fell behind by 24 points in the first half against the Oklahoma City Thunder. They came back. They won 116-110.

Welcome to the NBA, Coach Mosley.

How They Did It

Oklahoma City's lead grew to 24 in the opening half, fueled by an aggressive start from their backup and developmental roster — the Thunder sat Shai Gilgeous-Alexander and most of their regular contributors. That decision would come back to haunt them.

New Orleans turned the tide in the third quarter. The Pelicans outscored the Thunder 38-26 in the third period, slicing Oklahoma City's lead to three points heading into the fourth. It wasn't until the two-minute mark of the third frame that New Orleans finally cut the deficit into single digits — but once they did, momentum shifted completely.

The Pelicans finished the job in the fourth, outscoring OKC 26-17 to close out the 116-110 victory. Final difference: two quarters of elite offensive and defensive execution after a first half to forget.

Mathurin Sets the Tone

Bennedict Mathurin led New Orleans with 13 points off the bench, providing the energy and scoring punch that turned the momentum. Mathurin's ability to generate offense in transition and off movement — without needing the ball in his hands to be effective — was exactly the kind of contribution Mosley needed from a reserve piece.

For Oklahoma City, Bennett Stirtz was the most productive player on the floor, finishing with 15 points, 6 assists and 5 rebounds. But the Thunder were playing with second-unit personnel for much of the night, and the result needs to be framed accordingly.

What It Means for Mosley

Winning a preseason opener under those conditions — down 24, with a new system, new personnel and a new staff — sends a message before the regular season even begins. That message isn't "this team will win 50 games." It's something more valuable: this team won't quit.

Mosley stepped into a difficult situation in New Orleans. The Pelicans have a talented roster, but they've also dealt with injury disruptions, roster turnover and playoff disappointments in recent seasons. The new coaching staff's job is to establish a culture of toughness and consistency from day one.

Wednesday's comeback was day one. It didn't come easily, but it came.

What's Next

The Pelicans continue preseason preparations before the regular season tips off October 20. For Mosley and his staff, the work now shifts to refining rotations, building chemistry between starters and reserves, and preparing for what should be a highly competitive Southwest Division.

The comeback against the Thunder won't appear in any playoff box score. But team identity is built in moments exactly like this — moments where everyone on the floor had a choice between folding and fighting, and they chose to fight.
