### Long-Form Post The Philadelphia 76ers entered this preseason with the most star-studded roster in the Eastern Conference. LeBron James. Joel Embiid. Jaylen Brown. Tyrese Maxey. And in a single non-contact play in their first preseason game, the entire enterprise got more fragile. Ariel Hukporti — the 24-year-old center the Sixers signed in July to be Embiid's primary backup — ruptured his right Achilles tendon in the first quarter against the Knicks on October 5. An MRI confirmed the diagnosis. Surgery was performed successfully on October 6. His season is over. What makes this particularly troubling for Philadelphia: the Sixers are only $1.8 million under the first apron. That cap position severely limits their ability to sign a replacement without waiving a player or making a small trade. For now, Adem Bona steps in as the primary backup behind Embiid. Bona is a capable defender, but Hukporti's combination of size, mobility and championship pedigree (he won a title with the Knicks last season) is hard to replicate cheaply. And if that weren't enough, this is the second major Achilles injury of Hukporti's career. He tore his LEFT Achilles in 2022 while playing for Melbourne United in Australia's NBL. Nobody wants to see a young player go through this twice. The Sixers will find a way forward — they're still the favorites in the East — but this is a story that deserved more than a footnote. ### Image Post Caption Prayers for Ariel Hukporti. ### Long-Form Post Down 24 points. In a new coach's first game. On a neutral court. The New Orleans Pelicans just gave Jamahl Mosley the most emphatic possible introduction to the NBA head coaching ranks. New Orleans trailed by 24 in the first half against Oklahoma City at the BOK Center in Tulsa on Wednesday. The Thunder — without Shai Gilgeous-Alexander and most of their starters — were supposed to be the perfect opponent to build early confidence. Instead, they jumped out to a massive lead and the Pelicans had to earn every inch of the comeback. They did. New Orleans outscored OKC 38-26 in the third quarter, slicing the deficit to three points heading into the final period. Then they pulled it out, outscoring the Thunder 26-17 in the fourth to win 116-110. Bennedict Mathurin led the Pelicans with 13 points off the bench. Bennett Stirtz paced OKC with 15 points, 6 assists and 5 rebounds. Was this the most important game of the preseason? No. But in sports, momentum and belief matter — and this team just chose both, loudly. Welcome to the NBA, Coach Mosley. ### Image Post Caption Down 24. Still won. Stephen Curry Signs 2-Year, $116M Extension — Stays a Warrior for Life
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Stephen Curry has never played for any team other than the Golden State Warriors. In a league defined by player movement, free agency chaos and superteam-building, that's a remarkable statement of loyalty. Now he's extended it further. Curry agreed to a 2-year, $116M contract extension with Golden State, running through the 2028-29 season with a player option in the final year. By the time the deal expires, Curry will have spent roughly 20 seasons as a Warrior. Here's the part worth noting: he didn't have to take that deal. Curry was eligible for up to $136.7M over two years. He discounted himself by more than $20M — a significant amount at any level, and a choice that few players of his stature would make. Why the discount? According to ESPN, Curry ultimately chose security and the continuation of his legacy over maximizing his earning potential. He believes in the Golden State organization. He wants to finish his career there. And yet, this is still a massive commitment. $116M is life-altering money by any measure, and the Warriors are betting that Curry can continue performing at an elite level into his late 30s. Based on the preseason — where he scored 16 points in under 15 minutes against the Lakers — there's no reason to doubt that. Steph Curry, Warrior for life. Some things in the NBA remain sacred. ### Image Post Caption Steph passed on $20M to stay a Warrior. Loyalty still exists in the NBA. 🏆 #Warriors
