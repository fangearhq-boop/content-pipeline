Breaking Down the 76ers' 2026-27 Roster: LeBron, Embiid, Brown and the Health Question

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | September 8, 2026

Philadelphia's new-look roster has championship-caliber talent from top to bottom. The starting five is set. The supporting cast is in place. The one variable nobody can overlook: Joel Embiid has played 57 games in two seasons.

The Philadelphia 76ers rebuilt this offseason in a way that would have seemed implausible a year ago. After being swept by the Knicks in the 2026 second round, the organization didn't tinker around the edges. They overhauled the roster entirely — trading for Jaylen Brown, signing LeBron James, and surrounding one of basketball's most talented — if fragile — centers with the kind of talent Embiid has rarely had around him.

Here is how the 2026-27 76ers are built.

The Starting Five

Tyrese Maxey returns as the primary ball-handler. The 25-year-old All-Star has developed into one of the better point guards in the Eastern Conference — quick, relentless in transition, and improved as a pick-and-roll partner for Embiid. Maxey is the engine. Everything else runs through him.

Jaylen Brown comes over in a trade and brings exactly what Philadelphia lacked last season: a two-way wing who can initiate offense, defend multiple positions, and score in a variety of ways. Brown had his best seasons in Boston and brings championship experience — he won in 2024 — to a Sixers team hungry for their first title since 1983.

LeBron James is the connector. At the power forward spot, James brings IQ, playmaking, and the ability to guard multiple players. He won't play 36 minutes a night anymore, but deployed intelligently alongside Maxey, Brown, and Embiid, he is still one of the most impactful players in the game in the right situations.

The fourth starting spot remains a decision for head coach. The options include Anfernee Simons, Kentavious Caldwell-Pope, or a smaller lineup that slides James to the four more traditionally.

Joel Embiid anchors the center position. The three-time scoring champion and former MVP is the most talented player on the roster when healthy — a dominant post scorer, an elite passer from the high post, and a genuine defensive force. When Embiid plays, the Sixers are a different team. The challenge is keeping him on the floor.

The Supporting Cast

Philadelphia surrounded its stars with a supporting cast that addresses the team's weaknesses from last season. Kentavious Caldwell-Pope, a championship-winning role player, adds perimeter defense and corner shooting. Anfernee Simons provides scoring punch and ball-handling depth off the bench. Ariel Hukporti is a physical interior presence. Dean Wade brings versatile, switchable defense. Labaron Philon Jr. is a young developmental piece at the guard position.

It is a deeper, more functional group than the one that was swept out of the playoffs three months ago.

The Embiid Variable

All of it — the LeBron signing, the Brown trade, the organizational transformation — hinges on one thing: Joel Embiid's health.

Over the past two seasons, Embiid has played a total of 57 games. The injuries have ranged from knee issues to recovery complications, and while he has been one of the most dominant players in basketball when on the floor, the Sixers' playoff fate has repeatedly been derailed by his absence at critical moments.

The 2026-27 season will be a test of whether Philadelphia can keep their franchise center healthy through the full arc of a campaign. The front office built the roster to play without him at times — LeBron and Brown are capable of carrying offensive stretches — but the Sixers are only a true championship contender when Embiid is in the starting lineup and healthy.

That is the deal with this team. Extraordinary upside when healthy. Significant risk when not.
