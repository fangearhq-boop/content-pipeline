Kawhi Leonard Trade to Toronto Still Not Official as NBA Investigation Continues

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | September 8, 2026

The Kawhi Leonard trade from the LA Clippers to the Toronto Raptors remains unratified three weeks after it was first reported, as the NBA's investigation into an alleged undisclosed sponsorship deal drags on with training camp 20 days away.

Kawhi Leonard still isn't a Toronto Raptor — at least not officially.

The trade sending Leonard from the Los Angeles Clippers to the Toronto Raptors has not been formally ratified by the NBA, and as of September 8, the league's investigation into an alleged undisclosed sponsorship agreement between Leonard and Aspiration — a Clippers corporate partner — remains unresolved.

Raptors training camp is scheduled to open September 28 in Quebec City. That is 20 days away. And while all indications suggest the investigation is unlikely to block the trade entirely, every day without a formal clearing adds tension to Toronto's offseason.

What the NBA Is Investigating

The core of the investigation centers on whether Leonard and the Clippers entered into a sponsorship arrangement that was not disclosed to the league — a potential violation of the NBA's rules around player-team financial relationships. Aspiration, an environmentally focused financial company, had an existing partnership with the Clippers. The specific nature of Leonard's alleged relationship with the company is what the league is working to clarify.

NBA investigations of this kind can conclude quickly or extend for weeks. In this case, the league cleared an initial threshold on September 2, allowing the trade to be reported as agreed in principle. But "agreed in principle" is not the same as "finalized," and until the league officially ratifies the deal, Leonard cannot practice, attend official team functions, or be listed on the Raptors roster.

The Trade Terms

The deal, when it eventually clears, will send Kawhi Leonard to Toronto in exchange for Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, two unprotected first-round picks (2031 and 2033), a 2027 first-round swap, and two second-round picks heading to Los Angeles.

For the Raptors, the return of Kawhi Leonard represents the most significant offseason move in franchise history since acquiring him before the 2018-19 championship season. Leonard, who won the title with Toronto and then signed with the Clippers in free agency, is coming back to the place where he reached the top of the sport.

For the Clippers, the haul is a rebuild package — two unprotected first-round picks from a team in transition, plus young rotation players. It is the kind of deal that makes sense for an LA franchise looking to retool after falling short of contention with Leonard and Paul George.

What It Means for Toronto's Preparation

The timing creates a genuine headache for the Raptors' coaching staff and front office. Training camp preparation in the modern NBA involves complex film study, lineup integration, and system installation — all of which are harder to execute when you don't know definitively who your best player will be.

Leonard has reportedly told the organization he intends to report to Quebec City when camp opens. The question is whether the league will have cleared the deal by then. If not, Toronto faces the awkward scenario of preparing systems around a player who technically remains a Clipper until the paperwork is finalized.

This is not unique in NBA history — investigations have delayed trades before — but the timeline is tighter than most, and the stakes for Toronto are significant. The Raptors are expected to be an Eastern Conference contender with Leonard, and the uncertainty around his status creates a ripple effect on everything from lineup planning to contract conversations with existing players.

What's Next

The NBA is expected to conclude its investigation and either ratify or raise additional concerns about the trade within the coming days. Toronto's training camp opens September 28 in Quebec City. If the deal clears before then, the Raptors can hit the ground running. If it doesn't, the organization will face some uncomfortable decisions about how to proceed.
