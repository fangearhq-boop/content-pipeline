Lillard Is Back, Morant Is Here: Portland Trail Blazers Built Something Unexpected

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | September 8, 2026

Damian Lillard is cleared from his Achilles injury. Ja Morant arrived via trade. The Portland Trail Blazers enter training camp with one of the most electrifying backcourts in the Western Conference.

Nobody put Portland on their contender watch list this summer. They probably should have.

The Trail Blazers enter the 2026-27 season with a backcourt that will make every opponent uncomfortable: Damian Lillard, cleared from his Achilles injury and ready to return after missing most of last season, paired with Ja Morant, the two-time All-Star who arrived in Portland via trade. Add role depth with Branden Carlson on a guaranteed minimum-salary deal and Micah Potter claimed off waivers from Indiana, and the Blazers look meaningfully improved.

Are they a championship contender? Not realistically. But they are going to be one of the most compelling teams to follow from the moment training camp opens September 29.

Lillard's Return

Damian Lillard missed most of the 2025-26 season with an Achilles tendon injury, one of the most serious ailments a basketball player can face. The recovery timeline for Achilles injuries has shortened considerably in the modern era, but the return to pre-injury form remains a deeply individual process, and Lillard's health entering camp is the variable that determines everything else about Portland's ceiling.

The news from the Blazers' medical staff is encouraging: Lillard has been cleared for full basketball activities and is expected to participate in camp without restrictions. At 35, he is no longer the player who could carry a team for 38 minutes a night, but the late-game scoring instincts and pull-up ability that made him one of the great guards of his generation do not disappear with an Achilles.

The key will be workload management. Portland will almost certainly monitor his minutes through training camp and the preseason, building toward a regular-season load that keeps him healthy for the stretch run and — if the Blazers get there — the playoffs.

What Morant Brings

Ja Morant is 26 years old and entering what should be the peak years of his career. The two-time All-Star is one of the most explosive offensive players in the NBA — a slasher and finisher who generates impossible angles at the rim and plays with an edge that makes him genuinely difficult to guard.

His fit alongside Lillard is not immediately obvious on paper: two ball-dominant guards sharing the backcourt is a familiar recipe for friction. But the Blazers' coaching staff has an obvious solution — stagger their minutes, build two distinct offensive units around each player, and use the combination sparingly in clutch situations where both are on the floor.

Morant's arrival gives Portland something it hasn't had in years: a player whose ceiling is All-NBA and whose best basketball might still be ahead of him. That alone changes the conversation around the franchise.

Supporting Cast and Camp Outlook

The Blazers added practical depth alongside the star pieces. Branden Carlson, a 7-footer, fills out the frontcourt on a veteran minimum deal. Micah Potter, claimed off waivers from Indiana after the Pacers cut him, adds another frontcourt option. Neither is a difference-maker, but both provide the roster depth a team needs to survive the grind of an 82-game schedule.

Training camp opens September 29. The preseason schedule includes what could become a historic night: October 12, when the Blazers face BC London Lions — the first-ever contest between an NBA team and a British club. Before any of that, Portland will put in the work to figure out how two of the more dynamic guards of their generation can coexist at the highest level.

The answer to that question will determine whether Portland is a dark-horse playoff team or just a wildly entertaining miss.

What's Next

The Trail Blazers open training camp September 29. Preseason begins October 7 with a four-game slate running through October 16. Their regular-season opener is October 20. Lillard's health and the Morant integration will be the two storylines to track through camp.
