Giannis and Bam Are Building Something in Miami — and a $275M Decision Is Coming in January

By Damon Pierce | August 12, 2026 | Hoop Heroes

Six weeks after one of the biggest trades in NBA history, the Miami Heat are settling into their new reality. Giannis Antetokounmpo — two-time MVP, Finals champion, six-time All-Star — is a member of the Miami Heat. He arrived July 6. He and Bam Adebayo are spending the summer together. And January 6, 2027 brings the moment both sides have been building toward: a four-year, $275 million supermax extension.

The trade itself was seismic. Milwaukee sent Giannis and Bobby Portis to Miami. In return, the Bucks received Tyler Herro, Kel'el Ware, Jaime Jaquez Jr., Kasparas Jakucionis, two unprotected first-round picks (2031 and 2033), the No. 13 pick, a 2030 swap, and a 2033 second-rounder. The Bucks, clearly in rebuild mode, accepted an enormous collection of assets. The Heat, clearly in championship mode, accepted the risk that comes with a supermax commitment to a 31-year-old.

The Partnership Being Built

Giannis and Adebayo are one of the more fascinating frontcourt combinations the league has seen — two players who can each guard multiple positions, initiate offense, and affect the game without the ball. Adebayo, who signed his own max extension to stay in Miami, anchors a defense that was already one of the league's best. Adding a player of Giannis's physical profile and defensive IQ raises that ceiling considerably.

The question surrounding any Giannis partnership has been shooting. The Heat, who have spent years developing shooters around Jimmy Butler and Adebayo, are presumably asking the same question in a different context. Tyrese Maxey and LeBron James will provide that for the 76ers. The Heat's answer to the spacing question will define how real their title contention is when October arrives.

FIBA Decision Signals Seriousness

One notable development in the weeks since the trade: the Heat opted to keep Giannis out of the late-August FIBA window. He will not play for Greece in the upcoming international games. The franchise is managing his workload deliberately, protecting their asset with training camp roughly six weeks away.

The contrast with Victor Wembanyama — who is actively returning to the French national team for games beginning August 20 — is worth noting. Different players, different situations. But the Heat's decision makes clear where their priorities are: maximizing Giannis's readiness and health for October 20.

What's Next

The immediate future is straightforward: training camp in late September, preseason games in October, and Opening Night on October 20. Christmas Day brings a nationally televised Heat-Celtics matchup. By the time January 6 arrives, the Heat and Giannis will have played approximately 40 games together — enough to know exactly what they have and whether that $275 million makes sense for both sides.

The expectation from everyone connected to the situation is that the extension gets done. Miami would not have traded what they traded if they were uncertain about keeping Giannis long term. Giannis would not have forced the situation in Milwaukee if he was unsure about where he wanted to commit. The January extension is a formality that represents the real starting line of the Miami Heat's championship window.
