Wembanyama Is Heading to Belgrade — and His Return to France Says Everything About Who He Is

By Marcus Cole | August 12, 2026 | Hoop Heroes

Victor Wembanyama could be anywhere this summer. He just finished his third NBA season with the San Antonio Spurs. He could be training privately, resting, preparing for what figures to be another year of individual brilliance. Instead, he is heading to Belgrade on August 20 to play for France.

He said it plainly when the French Basketball Federation named him to its 15-man FIBA roster in July: "Coming back here does my heart a lot of good." And: "I do not miss the NBA right now. I miss FIBA basketball."

For a player who has been analyzed, dissected, and celebrated as the most extraordinary physical specimen the sport has produced in decades, those quotes are a reminder that before he was a Spur, before the hype and the draft and the media circus, he was a French kid who loved playing for his country.

The August Schedule

France has a busy four-game window over eleven days. The squad plays exhibition games against Serbia on August 20 in Belgrade and August 23 in Orleans, France. Then come the stakes: FIBA Basketball World Cup qualifiers, with France hosting Slovenia on August 27 before traveling to Sweden on August 30.

France enters the qualifier window sitting 5-1 in Group L of the European first round — a strong position that this window could improve further. A deep run in the 2027 World Cup qualifiers is the goal. Wembanyama is the centerpiece around which that goal is built.

He will be joined by Rudy Gobert, who has played alongside Wembanyama in the French national team program for years, and Evan Fournier. The roster puts three of France's most experienced international players together for the first time since the 2024 Paris Olympics, where France won silver — losing the gold medal game to Team USA.

A New Coach, a New Chapter

One element that makes this window notable: the French national team is operating under a new head coach. Frederic Fauthoux replaced Vincent Collet after the Paris Games, and these August games will be the first time he has coached Wembanyama. The partnership between coach and player is new, untested, and starts under the pressure of competitive qualifying games.

Wembanyama's own comments suggest he is not approaching this as a burden. The enthusiasm reads as genuine. He has described FIBA basketball as a different, more personal kind of competition — team-oriented in ways that resonate with him even at his level of individual stardom.

The Contrast With Giannis

The decision is not without notable context. Giannis Antetokounmpo — freshly traded to the Miami Heat — will not play for Greece in the same late-August FIBA window. The Heat made the call: they are protecting their investment heading into training camp. It is a reasonable organizational decision.

Wembanyama is making the opposite one. San Antonio has allowed it. He has embraced it. The comparison is not a criticism of either player or either organization — it simply underscores that Wembanyama's relationship with international basketball is something he actively chooses, not something he tolerates.

What's Next

The first game is August 20. France vs. Serbia in Belgrade. For Wembanyama, it is the beginning of a stretch that carries him through August 30 before attention turns fully back to the NBA. Training camps open in late September. The Spurs will have him back for a fourth season that carries the same questions: Can they build around him fast enough? Can he carry the load in another transitional year?

For now, those questions belong to October. August belongs to France.
