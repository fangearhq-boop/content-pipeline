Stephen Curry's Extension Window Opens August 29 — And the Warriors Have 17 Days to Figure Out Their Future

By Jake Torres | August 12, 2026 | Hoop Heroes

Seventeen days from now, Stephen Curry becomes eligible to sign a two-year, $136.7 million extension with the Golden State Warriors. The expectation, from virtually every source covering the situation, is that he will sign it — likely in September. But the extension window arriving marks something larger than a contract signing. It is the moment the Warriors find out what chapter they are actually in.

The existing deal already breaks ground: Curry will earn $62.6 million in the 2026-27 season, making him the first NBA player in history to surpass $60 million in a single year. The proposed extension would pay him approximately $68 million per season through 2028-29. That number reflects the market, and it reflects the reality that Curry, at 38, remains one of the five most marketable and impactful players in the sport.

Mutual Interest, Lingering Questions

Golden State GM Mike Dunleavy Jr. has been unambiguous about the organization's intentions. "I'm confident he wants to win and I'm confident he wants to finish his career as a Warrior," Dunleavy said. He described an aggressive offseason approach — "super aggressive," in his words — noting that the cold calls he made to explore roster additions would surprise people who saw the Warriors' relatively quiet summer.

That quiet offseason, honestly, is the context that makes the extension conversation more interesting than a rubber-stamp signing. Draymond Green returned on a one-year deal worth $27.7 million. Gary Payton II came back on $3.9 million. Beyond those moves, Golden State did not dramatically improve the roster around Curry. The Warriors finished 37-45 last season. The question of whether Curry is extending into a legitimate title contender or into more transition years has no clean answer right now.

ESPN's Brian Windhorst floated a concept in recent weeks: Curry accepting below the maximum in the extension as a recruiting mechanism — creating cap room to attract a difference-making partner. Curry was described as "absolutely" open to that kind of sacrifice. Whether that concept translates into action is a separate question. The Warriors have not confirmed any such framework publicly.

The Alternative Is Stark

If Curry does not sign the extension on or before the deadline, he would complete the 2026-27 season and then hit unrestricted free agency for the first time in his career — at age 39. That scenario would be unprecedented territory for all involved. No NBA player of Curry's caliber has reached free agency that late in his career in the modern era. The Warriors would face a summer in which the greatest player in franchise history could walk.

That scenario is almost universally described as one neither side wants. The extension is the expected outcome. But the fact that the window is arriving without a more dramatically retooled roster around Curry is the undercurrent that makes this situation worth watching beyond the contract announcement.

What's Next

The window opens August 29. A signing announcement could come anytime between that date and the start of training camp in late September. Once the extension is signed, attention shifts to whether the Warriors use any remaining cap flexibility to add pieces before camp opens — and whether the Dunleavy "cold calls" he alluded to produce any results before the roster locks in for the year.

Christmas Day brings the Warriors to Denver to face the Nuggets. By then, Curry's future in Golden State should be resolved. The real work of deciding what that future looks like — roster-wise — may just be beginning.
