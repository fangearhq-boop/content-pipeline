New Daktronics Deal Deepens Clippers Investigation: Source Says Kawhi Arrangement Was '1,000%' Cap Circumvention

By Marcus Cole | August 12, 2026 | Hoop Heroes

The NBA investigation into the Los Angeles Clippers and Kawhi Leonard expanded in early August with a striking new revelation: a second alleged endorsement deal — this one with Daktronics, the publicly traded company that manufactured the halo scoreboard at the Clippers' own Intuit Dome — is now under scrutiny alongside the previously reported Aspiration agreement.

A source with knowledge of the arrangement told journalist Pablo Torre that the Daktronics deal was "1,000% a way to circumvent the salary cap" and described the money as "funneling from the Clippers through Daktronics back to Kawhi." Leonard reportedly performed no public-facing work for the company. Daktronics' public relations firm, when contacted, confirmed that the company does not currently have a deal with Kawhi Leonard.

Two Deals, One Investigation

The inquiry began with the Aspiration agreement, involving a financial technology company that had ties to the Clippers organization. After the trade was agreed upon in late June — Leonard returning to Toronto, where he won a title in 2019, in exchange for Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, and future draft picks — the NBA launched a formal investigation. Nine days after the deal was publicly announced, both teams stated the trade was on hold.

The Wachtell Lipton Rosen & Katz law firm is conducting the investigation on the league's behalf. The Daktronics angle, reported in the first week of August, adds a second line of inquiry to what was already a complex case. The scoreboard company's connection to the Clippers' own arena infrastructure makes the alleged arrangement uniquely brazen, per the source's characterization.

Owner Steve Ballmer has denied the cap circumvention allegations. Kawhi Leonard has said through representatives that he strongly believes the Toronto trade will ultimately go through.

NBPA Arbitration and the Timeline

The players' union has made clear that it will pursue neutral arbitration if the league seeks to penalize Leonard without evidence it deems sufficient. Under the collective bargaining agreement, that arbitration process would be handled by a jointly appointed arbitrator — and could theoretically push a final resolution into 2027.

League sources cited by HoopsHype in early August described belief in league circles that a resolution — including possible sanctions against the Clippers and clearance to complete the trade — could arrive within approximately six weeks. That timeline would put a conclusion in late September, roughly concurrent with training camp.

The Raptors are not proceeding with any trade logistics while a potential suspension of Leonard hangs over the situation. Leonard is owed $50.3 million in 2026-27 and has maintained that he expects to be in Toronto when the season begins.

What's Next

The investigation is now in its 13th month with two distinct endorsement deals under scrutiny. If the league moves toward sanctions against the Clippers, the NBPA's arbitration threat becomes the next inflection point. If the parties reach a negotiated settlement — which ESPN and CBS Sports have identified as the most likely outcome — the trade would be cleared and Leonard would report to Toronto.

Training camps open in late September. Whether Kawhi Leonard walks into one wearing a Clippers jersey or a Raptors jersey depends entirely on how quickly Wachtell Lipton wraps its work and whether both sides can agree on consequences. The clock is running.
