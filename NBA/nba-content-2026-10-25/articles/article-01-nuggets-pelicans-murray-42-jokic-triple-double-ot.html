Murray 42, Jokic 35/15/10: Nuggets Stun Pelicans 141-135 in OT After 23-Point Hole

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | October 25, 2026

Down 23 points and playing at home in only their second game of the 2026-27 season, the Denver Nuggets looked cooked. The New Orleans Pelicans were rolling, methodically picking apart Denver's defense and building a commanding 73-57 halftime lead that felt even more comfortable by the third quarter. What followed was one of the most remarkable comebacks of the young NBA season.

Jamal Murray put on a shooting clinic unlike anything Ball Arena had seen in years. He finished with 42 points — going 12-of-25 from the field and a scorching 8-of-16 from three-point range — while adding six assists, three rebounds, a block, and a steal across 43 minutes. His free-throw shooting down the stretch was automatic: 10-of-13 from the line. Murray had no turnovers.

Nikola Jokic, as reliable as ever, produced the 199th triple-double of his regular-season career: 35 points, 15 rebounds, and 10 assists. Combined, Murray and Jokic accounted for 77 of the Nuggets' 141 points in what became a 141-135 overtime victory.

The Comeback

New Orleans led by as many as 23 points during the second half, and entering the fourth quarter the Pelicans' advantage stood at 19. Then the Nuggets began to find their rhythm. Murray started heating up from beyond the arc, Jokic converted big baskets on the interior, and Aaron Gordon provided the exclamation point with a thundering reverse dunk that helped swing the momentum.

Denver clawed back, tied the game late, and forced overtime. In the extra period, it was rookie forward Trevon Brazile who stepped up in his home debut. Brazile scored 7 of his 17 points in overtime, providing crucial contributions off the bench that gave Denver enough cushion to hold on.

Jokic's 199th

The triple-double was yet another milestone in an already Hall of Fame career. Jokic's 199th regular-season triple-double puts him in elite company, and the pursuit of No. 200 will be one of the storylines Denver fans follow closely through the rest of October. The Serbian big man continues to be the most complete player in basketball, doing everything the Nuggets need on any given night.

He and Murray form one of the league's most devastating pick-and-roll combinations, and Saturday night was a perfect example of why. Murray creates so much gravity as a shooter that Jokic had open driving lanes all night long. When the defense finally collapsed on the roll man, Murray stepped into threes — and drilled them at a historic rate.

What's Next

The Nuggets head into Sunday's games with momentum restored after a comeback win that will be remembered long after this week. Denver's ability to fight through adversity — as they have so many times over the past several seasons — is the hallmark of a championship-caliber team. If Murray can sustain this kind of shooting and Jokic keeps filling up the stat sheet, the Nuggets will be a genuine contender in the Western Conference once again.

For the Pelicans, blowing a 23-point lead will sting. They played well for three quarters but could not put Denver away. New Orleans will need to shore up its second-half defensive resolve if it wants to compete with the West's best teams over the full 82-game schedule.
