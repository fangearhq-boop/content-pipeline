History in Philadelphia: LeBron James Climbs to No. 3 on NBA's Career Assists List

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | November 16, 2026

LeBron James moved into third place on the NBA's all-time assists list on Sunday, passing Jason Kidd to reach 12,092 career assists as the Philadelphia 76ers beat the Indiana Pacers 131-124.

In the fourth quarter of a Sunday night win over Indiana, LeBron James found VJ Edgecombe with a pass that most players would not have seen. It was not a highlight reel moment. It looked, as so many of LeBron's assists do, entirely routine. It was also the pass that moved him past Jason Kidd and into third place on the NBA's all-time assists list.

His career total now stands at 12,092. Only John Stockton (15,806) and Chris Paul (12,552) have handed out more in the history of the league. James is approximately 460 behind Paul, which at his current rate and health means the conversation about reaching second place is at least theoretically worth having.

The Night He Did It

James finished Sunday's 131-124 win with 15 points, 11 assists, and six rebounds. The performance was not a spectacle. It was, characteristically, a complete one — a veteran point-forward managing tempo, finding the right reads, and delivering a milestone while simultaneously helping his team win a game that mattered in the Eastern Conference standings.

He had missed Thursday's game against Toronto to manage sciatica in his back. Coming back for a Sunday game and passing a Hall of Famer on the all-time list was simply the return he provided.

What the Number Means

There is a tendency to treat LeBron's statistical milestones as afterthoughts by this stage of his career — another record attached to the catalog. The assists list is worth pausing on, though, because passing distribution at this level is not an accident of longevity. It requires decision-making, court vision, and consistent positioning over thousands of games against the best defenders in the world.

Jason Kidd was a two-time All-Star as a facilitator, one of the most celebrated passers in league history. LeBron James has now passed more assists in his career than Kidd did in his. That is not a coincidence or a rounding error. It is a reflection of a player who, in transitioning from scorer to point-forward across his career, never stopped making everyone around him better.

At 12,092, the gap between LeBron and Chris Paul at No. 2 (12,552) is achievable. Whether James's body cooperates over the remaining seasons of his contract will determine whether the conversation reaches that far. For now, third place is his.

VJ Edgecombe: The Recipient of History

The young guard who caught the record-breaking pass will have a story to tell. VJ Edgecombe, one of the 76ers' most promising developmental pieces, became the latest player to find himself on the receiving end of a moment that gets replayed in highlight packages years from now. For a player still building his NBA résumé, sharing a frame with LeBron on a milestone play is a career footnote with real meaning.

What's Next

The 76ers at 131-124 over Indiana improved their standing in the Eastern Conference, though the full extent of their record entering Monday was not confirmed from available sources. With LeBron managing his back carefully and the team building around his playmaking, Philadelphia's ceiling will depend on whether this supporting cast is capable of doing enough when defenses scheme primarily for James.

As for the record itself: John Stockton at 15,806 is a number that belongs to a different era and a different style of play. No active player is realistically approaching it. But for LeBron James, the chase for No. 2 on that list — a chase against a player (Chris Paul) who understood his own place in the same record book — has a pleasing symmetry that the basketball world has not fully processed yet.
