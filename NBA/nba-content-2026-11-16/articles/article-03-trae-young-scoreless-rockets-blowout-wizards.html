Trae Young Scoreless for First Time as Rockets Blast Wizards 147-119, Improve to 11-3

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | November 16, 2026

The Houston Rockets routed the Washington Wizards 147-119 on Sunday, with Amen Thompson leading the blitz with 26 points while Trae Young was held scoreless for the first time in his NBA career.

It was a night that produced two distinct stories. The first was about the Houston Rockets — a team that is quickly becoming one of the scariest offensive units in the NBA. The second was about Trae Young — a player who, for the first time in his professional career, finished a game without a single point to his name.

The Rockets won 147-119, improved to 11-3, and did it with a collective elegance that has become their calling card. Forty assists. Fifty-seven-point-eight percent from the field. Three players with 25 or more points. This was not a performance that needed Trae Young's misfortune to tell a story. But the two things happening in the same game made it impossible to separate.

Thompson, Durant, and Eason: 76 Points Between Them

Amen Thompson led the way with 26 points on 9-of-11 shooting, adding seven rebounds and seven assists for one of the most efficient all-around lines of his career. Kevin Durant added 25 points, working his spots in the mid-range with the precision that has defined his game across two decades. And then Tari Eason came off the bench to contribute another 25 points, punctuating a depth chart that gives Houston options no opponent has fully answered.

The Rockets' 40 assists were the story beneath the story. When a team has that kind of ball movement — when Durant, Thompson, and Eason are not just scoring but creating for one another — the defense has no good answer. Washington had no answer. Houston led by 37 at the peak, shot over 57 percent, and never once looked like a team that needed to try harder than it was trying.

Trae Young's Career First

Trae Young entered Sunday having never been held scoreless in an NBA game. It is a remarkable streak for a player of his offensive gifts — a scorer and playmaker whose career has been defined by his ability to create points even against the most suffocating schemes. The streak ended in Washington against Houston's defense.

He went 0-of-5 from the field and finished with zero points. The nature of the performance — not a one-off struggle but a systematic shutout — speaks to what this Rockets defense can do when it is locked in. Young may well bounce back to 30 points in his next game. That is the nature of elite scorers. But the fact that Houston took him entirely out of the game in this moment is a data point worth remembering.

Washington had bright spots. Alex Sarr posted 25 points and 9 rebounds — a reminder that the Wizards' long-term building blocks are real. AJ Dybantsa contributed 16 points on a difficult shooting night at 6-of-18. The youth is developing. The 4-10 record reflects the gap between developing and competing.

Houston at 11-3: A Western Conference Force

The Rockets have won four straight and are playing the kind of basketball that contenders play when everything is clicking. At 11-3, they sit near the top of the Western Conference and have done it with balance rather than reliance on any single player.

The Durant acquisition gave Houston a closer. Amen Thompson's growth gave them a force. The depth — Eason headlining a bench that consistently produces — gives them resilience. This team is built for a long run, and the evidence is mounting that it will make one.

What's Next

Washington at 4-10 faces a Monday home game against Memphis after absorbing this loss. The Grizzlies are no easy opponent, and the Wizards will need to find their defensive composure before it compounds into a streak. Trae Young responding to this performance — in terms of shot selection and assertiveness — will be one of the storylines worth monitoring in the days ahead.

For Houston, the question is not whether they are good. It is whether they can maintain this efficiency through the grind of a full season. At 11-3 in mid-November, they have earned the right to have that question asked about them. That is the best thing that can be said about any team at this stage of the year.
