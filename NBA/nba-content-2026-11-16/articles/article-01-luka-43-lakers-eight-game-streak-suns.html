Luka Erupts for 43, Lakers Survive Suns Fightback for 8th Straight Win

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | November 16, 2026

Luka Dončić delivered 43 points, nine assists, and five three-pointers as the Los Angeles Lakers held off a Phoenix Suns comeback to win 131-123 on Sunday, extending their winning streak to eight games.

The Los Angeles Lakers led by 31 points in the third quarter at Footprint Center. Then Phoenix came alive — and for a stretch, it looked like Luka Dončić might not be enough. He always was. By the final buzzer, the Lakers had their 131-123 victory and their eighth consecutive win, the best current streak in the NBA.

Dončić finished with 43 points, nine assists, and five three-pointers. He went 12-of-14 from the free-throw line in the game's decisive moments. It was his sixth 40-point performance of the season — a pace that has the MVP conversation fully open in November.

Luka Builds the Lead, Then Closes It Out

The first three quarters were a statement. Los Angeles moved the ball, attacked the paint, and pushed the lead to as many as 31 points. The kind of lead that should produce a comfortable win. But the Suns had different plans in the fourth.

Phoenix, led by a surge from Collin Gillespie, chipped away relentlessly. The 31-point lead shrank to five at one point, and suddenly the building had the energy of a game that wasn't decided. Dončić, who had made the fourth quarter look routine all season, made it look routine again. Free throws, isolation possessions, decision-making under pressure — he delivered each time the Suns needed to be turned away.

His 12-of-14 showing at the charity stripe was not incidental; it was the difference. Twelve points from the line in a game won by eight is about as direct a statistical connection as the box score allows.

Reaves Out, No Problem

Austin Reaves sat out Sunday with a heel injury, removing one of the team's most reliable secondary options. In previous seasons, the absence of a co-star would have shown up in the final score. On this current streak, it barely showed in the game plan.

Quentin Grimes stepped up with five three-pointers of his own, providing the perimeter support that allowed the Lakers' offense to function even without Reaves threading the backcourt. The depth that general manager Rob Pelinka built around Dončić is passing its first real tests.

The Streak in Context

Eight consecutive wins in mid-November is not a championship. It is, however, a signal. The Western Conference is crowded — Oklahoma City, Houston at 11-3, and San Antonio's extended run all present legitimate competition. The Lakers winning eight straight through injury, travel, and playoff-caliber opponents suggests this is not a mirage.

Dončić's shooting efficiency — 43 points on what would have been a sustainable line by most players' standards — combined with the team's collective defense in the fourth quarter paints a picture of a franchise that has found something real. The Suns, who pushed hard in the final 12 minutes, could not have done more. Los Angeles was simply better when it mattered.

What's Next

The Lakers will need to monitor Reaves's heel injury as the schedule tightens through November. If he misses extended time, more weight falls on Dončić and the perimeter depth around him. Based on Sunday, that group has shown it can carry the load.

For Phoenix, the fourth-quarter rally is evidence that this team is not prepared to fold. They pulled within five against the NBA's hottest team on the road. The question for the Suns is whether that fight translates into wins when the moments come against opponents less capable of withstanding the pressure than Luka Dončić.
