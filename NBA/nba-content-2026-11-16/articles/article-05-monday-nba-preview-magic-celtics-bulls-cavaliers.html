Monday NBA Preview: Magic at Celtics Leads Three-Game Slate With Cup Implications

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | November 16, 2026

Monday's NBA schedule features three games at 7:00 PM ET — the Orlando Magic at the Boston Celtics, the Chicago Bulls at the Cleveland Cavaliers, and the Memphis Grizzlies at the Washington Wizards — with NBA Cup group play adding weight to every result.

Sunday's NBA action delivered milestones, blowouts, and a career first. Monday offers something different: three games, all tipping at 7:00 PM ET, all carrying implications in the NBA Cup group stage that runs through November 27. The marquee matchup sends the Orlando Magic into Boston, where the Celtics are fresh off a 37-point demolition of Dallas.

Magic at Celtics (7:00 PM ET)

The Boston Celtics set a franchise record for their first-quarter lead just 24 hours ago. Now they come home to host a Magic team that has constructed its identity around defense, length, and making every game slow and uncomfortable. That contrast — Boston's offense against Orlando's defensive system — is the central tension of Monday's most watchable game.

Jayson Tatum at 32 points on Sunday will not automatically carry forward in this form. Orlando, anchored by Franz Wagner and Paolo Banchero, has the size to challenge Tatum's driving lanes and the awareness to make life difficult for Boston's shooters. The question is whether the Magic can generate enough offense to keep it competitive, or whether the Celtics' depth proves too much once again.

The NBA Cup standings give this game a second layer of relevance. Boston has been among the more consistent teams in group play, and a home win against Orlando would solidify their position heading toward the knockout rounds in December.

Bulls at Cavaliers (7:00 PM ET)

Chicago visits Cleveland in what projects as a competitive Eastern Conference matchup. The Cavaliers have used their size and defensive structure to stay competitive through early-season turbulence, while the Bulls have leaned on a balanced offensive approach that makes them difficult to game plan against in a single night.

Cleveland at home has the advantage of a crowd and familiarity. The Bulls, playing on the road, will need their guards to generate early momentum if they want to avoid falling behind in a building that can get loud quickly. This is one of those games where the first six minutes tell you whether it will be close.

Grizzlies at Wizards (7:00 PM ET)

Washington absorbed a 147-119 loss to Houston on Sunday. Twenty-eight points is a significant margin; following it with a Monday home game against Memphis will test the Wizards' resilience. Trae Young being held scoreless for the first time in his career adds a psychological layer to the question of how Washington responds.

Memphis, meanwhile, brings a physical brand of basketball that plays to their strengths on the road. Ja Morant and the Grizzlies have the athleticism and defensive edge to extend Washington's difficult stretch. But if the Wizards get Alex Sarr — who showed 25 points and 9 rebounds on Sunday — going early, the building has a reason to believe.

What's Next

The NBA Cup group stage wraps up November 27, with quarterfinals on December 4 and 5, semifinals on December 8 and 9, and the championship game on December 11. Teams currently in group play position that win on Monday move closer to securing knockout round berths. Teams outside that position face an increasingly narrow path.

Monday's three-game slate is not the heaviest night of the week, but the Cup context makes each result consequential. The Celtics hosting the Magic is the game worth clearing your schedule for — the collision of Boston's offensive ceiling and Orlando's defensive identity is exactly the kind of stylistic clash the NBA in November does best.
