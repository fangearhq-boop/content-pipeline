Shannon Jr. Sets Career High with 34 Points, Gobert Dominates as Timberwolves Top Blazers 106-98

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | November 17, 2026

MINNEAPOLIS — Terrence Shannon Jr. announced his arrival as one of the league's most dangerous young guards with a career-high 34-point performance Monday night, leading the Minnesota Timberwolves to a 106-98 victory over the Portland Trail Blazers.

The explosive second-year guard topped his previous career best of 33 points — set against the Orlando Magic in April 2026 — shattering that mark in a complete offensive display that had Target Center buzzing. Rudy Gobert was equally impressive in his own way, posting 18 points and 15 rebounds for another dominant two-way performance.

Shannon Jr. Takes Over

Shannon Jr. has been building toward this kind of performance all season. The former Illinois standout possesses an explosive first step, the ability to finish at the rim through contact, and an improving three-point shot that is forcing opposing teams to guard him all the way to the arc.

Against Portland, Shannon Jr. was relentless. He attacked mismatches, got to the free-throw line repeatedly, and hit shots when the Blazers dared to give him space. The result was a performance that served notice to the rest of the league: Terrence Shannon Jr. is becoming a consistent scoring force.

Minnesota head coach clearly trusts Shannon Jr. in big moments, and the second-year guard is rewarding that confidence. As the Timberwolves continue to build around Anthony Edwards and their core, Shannon Jr.'s emergence as a legitimate secondary scorer gives Minnesota another dynamic offensive weapon.

Gobert Anchors Both Ends

While Shannon Jr. made headlines with his scoring explosion, Rudy Gobert's 18-point, 15-rebound night was arguably just as impactful. The veteran center remains one of the most dominant two-way forces in the NBA, and his ability to protect the rim on defense while scoring efficiently in the post on offense is a matchup nightmare for most teams.

The combination of Shannon Jr.'s speed and skill from the perimeter and Gobert's dominance in the paint gives Minnesota a devastating inside-outside balance. Portland had no answer for either, and the Timberwolves controlled most of the second half to secure the victory.

Portland's Youth Movement Faces More Growing Pains

The Trail Blazers continue to work through their rebuild. Facing a Timberwolves team at full strength, with both Shannon Jr. and Gobert operating at a high level, was always going to be a difficult test. Portland showed fight — 98 points is not an embarrassing total — but the gap between these two franchises is currently significant.

What's Next

Minnesota's win keeps them on track as a Western Conference playoff contender. Shannon Jr.'s career-high performance is likely just the beginning — a 34-point game at this stage of his development suggests his ceiling is considerably higher. For the Timberwolves, the ongoing question is how high their star guard can climb when given expanded opportunities. If this performance is any indication, Shannon Jr. could have a breakout season that reshapes how the entire league views Minnesota's offensive identity. The Blazers return home to focus on their developmental goals, knowing their young core will need more nights like this to eventually close the gap on teams like Minnesota.
