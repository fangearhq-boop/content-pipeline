Jeremiah Fears Delivers First Career Triple-Double as Pelicans Rout Warriors 115-94

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | November 17, 2026

NEW ORLEANS — Jeremiah Fears made history on Monday night, posting the first triple-double of his career as the New Orleans Pelicans snapped a six-game losing streak with a dominant 115-94 victory over the Golden State Warriors.

Fears, the electrifying young guard who has drawn comparisons to some of the NBA's elite playmakers, delivered a complete performance that had the Smoothie King Center crowd on its feet for most of the second half. Saddiq Bey was equally impressive, connecting on five three-pointers on his way to a team-high 23 points, while Zion Williamson added 16 points in a supporting role.

Fears Arrives on the Big Stage

The triple-double is a statistical milestone that separates the great playmakers from everyone else, and Fears showed all the tools that made him such a highly anticipated prospect. His ability to score, distribute, and rebound at the highest level in the same game signals a new chapter for his young career.

For a Pelicans team that entered the game having dropped six consecutive contests, Fears' performance was exactly the spark they needed. New Orleans controlled the game from the opening tip, turning what could have been another frustrating night into a statement win against a Warriors team that has struggled to find consistency this season.

Bey's Shooting Provides Critical Spacing

Bey's five made three-pointers were instrumental in keeping the Warriors' defense honest. When defenses collapse on Zion Williamson or Fears drives to the basket, Bey's ability to punish them from distance opens up the entire offense. His 23-point performance was a reminder that the Pelicans' supporting cast is capable of stepping up on any given night.

Williamson's 16 points came efficiently, as the former No. 1 overall pick continues to develop his game alongside an increasingly dangerous supporting cast. With Fears running the show and Bey providing floor spacing, Williamson finds himself with more room to operate than he has in recent seasons.

Warriors Struggle to Contain New Orleans Attack

Golden State had no answers for the Pelicans' multiple offensive weapons. The 21-point margin of victory — 115-94 — reflects how thoroughly New Orleans controlled the contest. The Warriors, who have shown flashes of their old form but lack the consistency of championship-era teams, could not establish any rhythm against New Orleans' energized defense.

The blowout also allowed Pelicans head coach to manage playing time and get the entire roster meaningful minutes in a game that was well in hand by the third quarter.

What's Next

The Pelicans will look to build on this momentum as the NBA season continues. Fears' first career triple-double is not just a personal milestone — it is a signal to the rest of the league that New Orleans is capable of competing with anyone when their young core is healthy and focused. If Fears, Williamson, and Bey can find this kind of rhythm together, the Pelicans could become one of the more dangerous teams in the Western Conference. Golden State, meanwhile, will need to regroup and address their defensive breakdowns before facing stronger competition on their upcoming schedule.
