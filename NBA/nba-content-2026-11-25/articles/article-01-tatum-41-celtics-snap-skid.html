Tatum Erupts for Season-High 41 as Celtics End 4-Game Slide with 131-116 Rout of Wizards

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | November 25, 2026

Jayson Tatum was overdue for a game like this. After four consecutive losses had Boston fans questioning the direction of the season, the Celtics' star delivered the loudest possible answer on Tuesday night — a season-high 41 points in a 131-116 NBA Cup victory over the Washington Wizards.

Tatum was relentless from the opening tip. He poured in 28 of his 41 points before halftime, putting the Celtics up 80-49 at the break. At one point, it seemed less like a game and more like a coronation. Boston's offense was moving with the kind of rhythm that had been absent in recent weeks, and Tatum was at the center of all of it — attacking the rim, draining pull-up threes, and refusing to let the Wizards settle.

Washington had other ideas in the fourth quarter. Anthony Davis, who finished with 33 points and 13 rebounds, refused to let his team go quietly. The Wizards clawed back to within 117-112 as the fourth quarter wore on, and suddenly a comfortable blowout had a new narrative: could Boston hold on?

Scheierman Steps Up in the Clutch

The answer came from an unlikely source. Baylor Scheierman, who had been scoreless through the first 45 minutes of the game, erupted for eight consecutive points in the final stretch to put the game out of reach for good. His timely contributions underscored one of the more encouraging themes for the Celtics this season: depth matters, and Boston has it.

Paul George added 28 points and nine assists in a performance that might have been the talking point on any other night. Neemias Queta chipped in 21 points off the bench, providing Boston with a consistent interior presence that the Wizards could never adequately answer.

Davis and the Wizards showed fight, but Boston's defense found enough answers when it mattered most. Washington is now mathematically eliminated from the NBA Cup tournament.

Celtics Find Their Footing at the Right Time

The win improved Boston to 12-6 overall and, more critically, to 3-0 in NBA Cup East Group C play. After four straight losses had cast doubt over the Celtics' early-season identity, Tuesday's performance provided clarity: when Tatum is locked in and the supporting cast contributes, this team is as dangerous as anyone in the Eastern Conference.

Head coach Joe Mazzulla will appreciate not just the result but the manner of it. Tatum's season-high erased any questions about his form, and the supporting cast — particularly Scheierman and Queta — answered the call when the Wizards threatened in the fourth.

Boston scored 80 first-half points, the highest single-half total for any team in the NBA this season to date. That kind of offensive output, combined with the resilience shown when Washington pulled to within five, suggests a team that is finding its rhythm at the right moment.

What's Next

The Celtics return home to host the Atlanta Hawks on Friday to complete their NBA Cup East Group C schedule. A win would clinch the group outright and potentially position Boston as one of the top seeds heading into the knockout quarterfinals. With Tatum playing at his peak and the roster showing renewed depth, the Celtics look like a Cup contender that no team will want to face in December.

Washington, now eliminated from the Cup, will look to regroup in the regular-season standings where their young core led by Davis has shown flashes of genuine potential. The long view favors the Wizards, even as Tuesday's loss stings in the moment.

For Boston, the message was clear: Tatum is back, the depth is real, and the Celtics are coming for the Cup.
