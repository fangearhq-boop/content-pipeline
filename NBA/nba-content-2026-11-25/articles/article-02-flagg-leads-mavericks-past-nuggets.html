Flagg's 24-Point, 12-Assist Masterclass Fuels Mavericks' 4th Straight Win Over Faltering Nuggets

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | November 25, 2026

If there were still questions about Cooper Flagg's ability to carry a game on both ends of the floor, Tuesday's performance in Denver answered all of them. Flagg posted 24 points and 12 assists as the Dallas Mavericks rolled past the Denver Nuggets 120-101 in Ball Arena, extending their winning streak to four games and moving to 3-0 in NBA Cup West Group A play.

It was not just the numbers that impressed — it was the way Flagg controlled every phase of the game. When Denver threatened to make it interesting, Flagg made the right pass. When Dallas needed a bucket, he created one. The Mavericks' second-year star is averaging 24.6 points per game on the season, and nights like Tuesday explain why the league is starting to pay close attention.

Dallas finished with a 35-7 edge in fast break points, a margin that tells the story of the game as well as any stat in the box score. The Mavericks turned Denver turnovers into transition opportunities relentlessly, and their energy on those possessions never wavered for four quarters.

Nuggets Stage a Scare, But Dallas Closes the Door

Denver deserves credit for the third quarter. The Nuggets, who trailed by 21 at their worst, went on a 38-26 run in the period to cut the deficit to 84-77 entering the fourth. For a moment, the comeback narrative was alive, and the Ball Arena crowd sensed it.

Dallas shut it down immediately. The Mavericks outscored Denver 36-24 in the fourth quarter, putting any doubt to rest and extending the final margin to 19 points. It was the kind of fourth-quarter response that separates teams with genuine playoff potential from those merely going through the motions.

Jamal Murray was the bright spot for Denver, finishing with 20 points, but the Nuggets shot just 40.5 percent from the field. Their offense, which can be devastating when functioning at full capacity, never found the rhythm that makes Denver dangerous. Dallas's length and switching ability made life difficult for every Nuggets ball-handler.

Dallas Emerging as a Western Conference Force

The Mavericks sit at 11-7 overall, a record that understates how well they are playing right now. Four consecutive wins, three of them in Cup play, have announced Dallas as a legitimate threat heading into December. The combination of Flagg's two-way dominance and a roster built to run has them playing some of the fastest, most efficient basketball in the Western Conference.

Denver, meanwhile, falls to 10-6. For a franchise with Nikola Jokic and the talent they have on paper, that record — combined with Tuesday's flat performance — raises real questions about consistency. The Nuggets have tools; they are not yet using them reliably.

Dallas's Cup position is excellent. As the leader of West Group A at 3-0, the Mavericks are well-positioned to advance to the quarterfinals when group play concludes November 27. If Flagg keeps performing at this level, teams across the West will need to plan specifically for how to stop him — and right now, nobody has figured it out.

What's Next

Dallas has one more NBA Cup group-stage game before the quarterfinals and will carry the momentum of a four-game winning streak into the knockout round. For a team that has found its identity through pace, defense, and Flagg's versatility, the Cup represents a genuine opportunity to announce themselves on a national stage.

Denver needs a response. With Jokic capable of producing a virtuoso performance on any given night, the Nuggets can flip the script quickly. But Tuesday was a reminder that talent alone does not win basketball games in November — effort, pace, and execution matter just as much. The Mavericks brought all three. The Nuggets did not.

The NBA Cup is heating up. If Flagg keeps rolling, Dallas might be the story of the entire tournament.
