Isaiah Joe Delivers 21 Points as Pistons Beat Magic 104-86 Without Cade Cunningham

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | November 25, 2026

Thirty minutes before tip-off at Kia Center on Tuesday night, the Detroit Pistons learned they would be playing without Cade Cunningham. Right knee soreness had sidelined their star guard, leaving a lineup void that needed filling against an Orlando Magic team that had been rolling in NBA Cup play.

Isaiah Joe filled it. The Detroit guard came off the bench — well, stepped into the starting five — and delivered 21 points, the best individual performance on the floor for the Pistons in a 104-86 win that handed Orlando its first NBA Cup loss of the season and derailed the Magic's bid for an early quarterfinal clinch.

Joe's performance was the kind of response a team's culture either produces or it doesn't. With no time to prepare and no rehearsal available, the Pistons absorbed the late news and went out and executed. That is not an accident. Detroit is building something real in 2026.

An 18-0 Run Changes Everything

The game was decided in the second quarter. Detroit went on an 18-0 run and outscored Orlando 32-14 in the period, turning a competitive game into something resembling a rout before halftime. The Pistons' defense was suffocating during that stretch, holding Franz Wagner — one of Orlando's best offensive weapons — to just two points in the entire first half.

Daniss Jenkins complemented Joe's scoring with 15 points and 10 assists, running the offense efficiently in Cunningham's place. Jenkins's ability to push the pace and make quick decisions kept Detroit's offense humming and kept Orlando's defense scrambling all game.

When the Pistons make it this hard defensively — forcing turnovers, stifling the Magic's ball movement in the half court — they are a genuinely difficult team to beat. Orlando discovered that the hard way on Tuesday.

Magic's Struggles Mount

Desmond Bane salvaged a measure of individual pride with 21 points for Orlando, but the team's night was largely undermined by its own mistakes. The Magic shot just 41 percent from the field and committed 20 turnovers — a staggering number for a team that prides itself on taking care of the basketball.

Orlando falls to 7-10 overall and 3-1 in NBA Cup East Group A play. The loss was their first of the in-season tournament and cost them the chance to clinch a quarterfinal spot ahead of schedule. While the Magic are still well-positioned in the group, Tuesday was a reminder that nothing in this league is automatic.

Wagner and Paolo Banchero will need to be better. Orlando's starting unit has the talent to be one of the Eastern Conference's most dangerous teams, but 20 turnovers against a Detroit defense missing its primary creator suggests execution issues that go beyond one bad game.

What's Next

The Cunningham situation bears monitoring. Right knee soreness can be a minor inconvenience or the beginning of something more serious — the Pistons' medical staff will need to evaluate carefully. Detroit is 11-6 and playing well enough that they can sustain a brief absence from their star, as Tuesday proved. But Cunningham's health matters for their ceiling.

Orlando hosts Washington this weekend with an opportunity to right the ship. The Magic will not miss the Cup quarterfinals over one loss, but their 7-10 overall record suggests there are real problems beyond the in-season tournament that need addressing.

For Detroit, Tuesday was simply another entry in a season that continues to exceed expectations. The Pistons are building their identity one performance at a time, and on Tuesday, that identity looked very much like a team that finds a way — with or without its best player on the floor.
