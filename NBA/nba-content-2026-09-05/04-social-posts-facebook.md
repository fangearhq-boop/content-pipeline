### STORY 1: Kawhi Leonard Trade Officially Complete

**FB Long-Form Post**
Window: 9:00 AM ET

Seven years ago, Kawhi Leonard hit the most iconic shot in Toronto Raptors history — a four-bounce buzzer beater against Philadelphia that sent the city into a frenzy. He went on to win the NBA Finals MVP, deliver the Raptors their first and only championship, and then quietly walk out the door to Los Angeles.

Now, after one of the most drawn-out and dramatic trade sagas in recent NBA memory, Kawhi Leonard is coming home.

The trade between the LA Clippers and Toronto Raptors is officially complete, cleared after the NBA concluded its salary-cap circumvention investigation on September 2. The league declined to void Kawhi's contract or suspend him — removing the final barrier to the deal.

Here's what the Raptors gave up: Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, two unprotected first-round picks in 2031 and 2033, a 2027 first-round swap, and two second-round picks. It's a steep price. But when you're trading for a two-time Finals MVP who already knows what winning in Toronto feels like, steep might be exactly what it takes.

The trade was originally agreed June 30. It was frozen July 9 when the NBA warned the Raptors they'd inherit the risk of any Clippers penalties. It sat in limbo for nearly two months while the investigation concluded. Now, 66 days later, it's done.

Kawhi expects to report to training camp on September 28 in Quebec City. The North didn't wait this long to waste it.

### STORY 2: James Harden Signs 3-Year, $97M Deal with Cavaliers

**FB Long-Form Post**
Window: 11:00 AM ET

James Harden bet on himself — and it paid off.

The 37-year-old guard declined his $42.3 million player option for the 2026-27 season, then turned around and agreed to a new 3-year, $97 million deal to remain with the Cleveland Cavaliers. The contract includes a player option in Year 3, meaning Harden has even more security and control over his future.

Think about what that number means in context. Harden's career earnings now sit at $511 million — making him the fourth player in NBA history to surpass $500 million, joining LeBron James, Kevin Durant, and Stephen Curry. At 37, he also becomes the second player in league history to sign for over $90 million in guaranteed money at that age. The first? LeBron, of course.

Critics love to point out Harden's age. But in 37 games last season with Cleveland, he was still a legitimate playmaker — orchestrating the Cavaliers' offense and providing championship-level IQ alongside a younger core.

Cleveland isn't rebuilding. They're building toward something. With Harden locked in, the Cavaliers retain one of their most important veterans heading into a 2026-27 season where the East has never been more competitive.

Spain training camp. $511 million man. The Cavaliers mean business.

### STORY 4: Cavaliers Training Camp in Spain

**FB Long-Form Post**
Window: 7:00 PM ET

The Cleveland Cavaliers are heading to Spain.

Following Media Day on September 28 at Rocket Arena in Cleveland, the Cavaliers will travel to Andalusia for their training camp at The Embassy — a world-class sports facility that was partly designed by none other than Jose Calderon, the former Cavalier and Spanish basketball legend.

Camp runs September 30 through October 5 before the team returns stateside for preseason action.

It's part of a growing trend: the Golden State Warriors are holding camp in Hawaii at BYU-Hawaii's campus starting September 29. Teams are increasingly going overseas or to destination cities to build chemistry and create unique early-season memories for their rosters.

For the Cavaliers — coming off a season in which Harden just re-upped for $97M — this is about arriving at training camp focused, hungry, and unified. There's something about getting away from the routine that can accelerate team-building.

Calderon, who played for Cleveland during the 2014-15 championship season, has deep ties to the city. Bringing the Cavaliers to a facility he helped create is a story within the story.

### STORY 5: Rockets Trade Finney-Smith to Charlotte

**FB Long-Form Post**
Window: 1:00 PM ET

The Houston Rockets aren't done reshaping their roster.

The team has officially traded forward Dorian Finney-Smith to the Charlotte Hornets, along with three second-round picks — a 2027 pick (via Memphis), a 2028 pick, and a 2033 pick. In exchange, Houston receives a $13 million trade exception, a valuable financial tool that gives them flexibility to pursue another deal without adding salary.

For the Rockets, this is a chess move. They already signed Marcus Smart and Bogdan Bogdanovic this offseason. Finney-Smith played 37 games for Houston last season, averaging just 3.3 points per game — a bit player in a core rotation that had outgrown his role.

Now comes the trade exception: $13 million is a meaningful number in today's NBA cap environment. It can be used to absorb a contract in a future trade without sending matching salary back. It's the kind of asset that doesn't show up on a stat sheet, but general managers know what it's worth.

For Charlotte, Finney-Smith is a known defensive commodity — a capable wing who can guard multiple positions. Only his 2026-27 salary ($13.3M) is fully guaranteed, giving the Hornets a clear exit ramp next summer if things don't work out.

Houston is playing the long game. Watch what that trade exception becomes.
