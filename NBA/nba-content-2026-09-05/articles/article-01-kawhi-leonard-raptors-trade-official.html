Kawhi Leonard Is Officially a Toronto Raptor Again — And the Trade Is Done

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | September 5, 2026

After 66 days in limbo, one of the most dramatic trade sagas in recent NBA history is finally over. Kawhi Leonard is officially a Toronto Raptor.

The NBA confirmed the completion of the Kawhi Leonard–Toronto Raptors trade following the conclusion of its salary-cap circumvention investigation into the Los Angeles Clippers on September 2. The league declined to void Leonard's contract or suspend him, clearing the final barrier to a deal that had been frozen since July 9.

The Trade Details

The Raptors are sending a significant package to Los Angeles in exchange for the two-time Finals MVP. The deal: Leonard goes to Toronto for Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, two unprotected first-round picks in 2031 and 2033, a 2027 first-round pick swap, and two second-round picks.

It is a steep price. But for a franchise that has been rebuilding since its 2019 championship, acquiring a proven star with Finals experience and deep ties to the city changes the calculus entirely.

Leonard himself is expected to report to training camp on September 28 in Quebec City — the Raptors' scheduled camp location for the 2026-27 season opener.

How It Almost Didn't Happen

The trade was originally agreed on June 30. Then, on July 9, everything screeched to a halt. The NBA informed the Raptors that they would assume the risk of any punishments handed down from the ongoing Clippers investigation that directly affected Leonard. Toronto put the deal on hold.

For nearly two months, the trade sat in suspense. The Raptors operated as if the deal would eventually happen — Leonard received no indication his contract would be voided — but the uncertainty was real. The Clippers' investigation, conducted by law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz, eventually confirmed that the team had funneled improper endorsements through business partners to benefit Leonard in violation of salary-cap rules.

The penalties were severe: five consecutive first-round picks forfeited starting in 2029, a $30 million fine, suspensions for owner Steve Ballmer, President of Basketball Operations Lawrence Frank, and General Manager Michael Winger, and a lifetime ban from NBA business for consultant Dennis Robertson. Leonard paid $700,000 and avoided a suspension.

With the investigation concluded and Leonard cleared, there was nothing left standing in the way.

What It Means for the Raptors

Toronto hasn't been to the NBA Finals since 2019, when Leonard led them past the Golden State Warriors to claim the franchise's only championship. His famous four-bounce buzzer-beater against Philadelphia in the second round remains one of the most celebrated shots in league history.

Now 35, Leonard brings championship pedigree back to a Raptors team that has been methodically building toward contention. Paired with the assets already in place — including the picks and players already on the roster — his arrival signals a clear transition from rebuild to championship mode.

Whether Leonard can stay healthy over a full season remains the central question. His history of load management and injuries is well-documented. But for a fan base that has waited seven years for this moment, that's a risk Toronto was clearly willing to take — and now, officially, has.

What's Next

Leonard will report to training camp in Quebec City on September 28. The Raptors' preseason schedule is set, and the regular season tips off on October 20. Toronto's front office, coaches, and fans spent the summer waiting for this moment — the waiting is done. The real work starts now.
