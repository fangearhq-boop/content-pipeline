James Harden Signs 3-Year, $97M Deal to Stay with Cavaliers — Career Earnings Hit $511M

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | September 5, 2026

James Harden turned down $42.3 million. Then he signed for $97 million. And in doing so, he joined the most exclusive financial club in NBA history.

Harden has agreed to a new three-year, $97 million contract to remain with the Cleveland Cavaliers, declining his existing player option for the 2026-27 season in favor of a more lucrative long-term deal. The new contract includes a player option in Year 3, giving the 11-time All-Star flexibility heading into his late career.

The Numbers That Tell the Story

With this deal, Harden's career earnings reach $511 million — making him just the fourth player in NBA history to surpass $500 million in total earnings. He joins LeBron James, Kevin Durant, and Stephen Curry in that elite company.

At 37 years old, Harden also becomes the second player in league history to sign for more than $90 million in guaranteed money at that age or older. The first was LeBron James, whose own longevity continues to reshape what we consider possible at the end of an NBA career.

These aren't just numbers. They are a statement about how much the Cleveland Cavaliers value Harden's presence — and how much the market continues to value his production.

Why He Opted Out — And Why Cleveland Kept Him

Harden's decision to decline a $42.3 million player option surprised some observers. Walking away from guaranteed money at his age carries risk. But the calculus was clear: by entering free agency, he gave himself the opportunity to secure more total guaranteed dollars over a longer term. That bet paid off.

For Cleveland, keeping Harden was not just about sentiment. In 37 games last season, Harden remained a capable playmaker — someone whose court vision and experience anchor a team still figuring out how its pieces fit together at championship level. The Cavaliers have built a roster with championship ambitions, and losing their veteran facilitator to free agency was not part of the plan.

What It Means for Cleveland's Title Window

The Cavaliers now head into 2026-27 with a clear identity: experienced, battle-tested, and stable. Harden's new deal keeps the roster intact as Cleveland navigates an Eastern Conference that has grown increasingly competitive with the Philadelphia 76ers assembling LeBron James, Joel Embiid, and Jaylen Brown into a genuine super team.

Adding to the team-building narrative, the Cavaliers announced their training camp will be held in Andalusia, Spain at The Embassy — a world-class facility partly designed by former Cavalier Jose Calderon. Camp runs September 30 through October 5.

A $511 million man, heading to Spain, preparing to chase a ring. That's James Harden's 2026 offseason.

What's Next

Harden's contract becomes official when the new league year begins. Cleveland opens training camp with Media Day on September 28 at Rocket Arena before departing for Spain. The Cavaliers' regular season begins on October 20, and with Harden locked in for three more years, their championship window remains open — and they know it.
