Rockets Trade Dorian Finney-Smith to Charlotte, Create $13M Trade Exception

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | September 5, 2026

The Houston Rockets are playing financial chess heading into the 2026-27 season — and their latest move just gave them a powerful new piece.

Houston has officially traded forward Dorian Finney-Smith and three second-round picks to the Charlotte Hornets, according to multiple reports. In return, the Rockets receive a $13 million trade exception — a valuable asset that gives them flexibility to absorb future contracts without adding matching salary.

The Trade Details

The three second-round picks heading to Charlotte are: a 2027 selection (via Memphis), a 2028 selection (via Houston), and a 2033 selection (via Houston). Charlotte receives Finney-Smith and the picks without sending salary back — they used part of their non-tax mid-level exception to execute the trade structure.

For Finney-Smith, the deal comes with some nuance. His contract covers three more years at $13.3 million per season, but only his 2026-27 salary is fully guaranteed in Charlotte. That means the Hornets retain the option to part ways next summer if the fit doesn't work out — a flexible arrangement that gives both sides an exit.

Finney-Smith played 37 games with Houston last season, averaging 3.3 points, 2.5 rebounds, and 1.0 assist per game. In a Rockets lineup that had evolved past his role, the writing was on the wall.

Why Houston Made This Move

The Rockets have been active reshaping their roster around their young core all offseason. This summer, they signed guards Marcus Smart and Bogdan Bogdanovic, adding veteran playmaking depth to complement their developing stars. With those additions, Finney-Smith's spot in the rotation had become redundant.

More importantly, the $13 million trade exception created by this transaction is a legitimate asset. Unlike a cap space number or a draft pick, a trade exception allows a team to absorb a player's salary from another team in a future deal without sending matching salary back — within a specific time window. It is the kind of flexibility that can enable Houston to pivot quickly if the right opportunity presents itself.

What Charlotte Gets

For the Hornets, Finney-Smith represents a known defensive commodity. He is an experienced wing capable of guarding multiple positions — the kind of veteran glue piece a young team can use around its developing core.

Charlotte is in a different phase of its roster construction than Houston. Adding a player like Finney-Smith at a manageable guaranteed commitment gives them defensive versatility without significant long-term risk. If he contributes, great. If not, they can move on after one year.

What's Next

Houston now enters training camp with a leaner roster, a significant trade exception, and cap flexibility to act before or during the 2026-27 season. The Rockets' front office has made clear this offseason that they are building with purpose — not just filling spots. The Finney-Smith trade is the latest confirmation that they are playing the long game. What they do with that $13 million exception is the next question to answer.
