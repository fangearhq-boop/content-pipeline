Cleveland Cavaliers Take Training Camp to Spain — And There's a Jose Calderon Connection

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | September 5, 2026

The Cleveland Cavaliers are packing their bags — and they're heading to Andalusia.

Following Media Day on September 28 at Rocket Arena in Cleveland, the Cavaliers will travel to Spain for their 2026-27 training camp at The Embassy in Andalusia. Camp runs September 30 through October 5, making Cleveland one of several NBA teams going international before the regular season begins.

The Calderon Connection

There is a meaningful story within the story: The Embassy in Andalusia was partly designed by Jose Calderon, a former Cleveland Cavalier and one of the greatest Spanish basketball players of all time.

Calderon joined the Cavaliers during the 2014-15 championship season — the year LeBron James returned to Cleveland and the city began its march toward the 2016 NBA title. Though his stint in Cleveland was brief, his connection to the franchise endures. Now his fingerprints are on the very facility where the Cavaliers will build their championship preparation for 2026-27.

It is the kind of detail that turns a team news item into a human story — and it gives the Cavaliers something to talk about as they prepare to spend a week together in southern Spain.

NBA's Growing International Camp Trend

The Cavaliers are not alone in going global this preseason. The Golden State Warriors are holding their training camp on the campus of BYU-Hawaii, opening September 29, in what the organization is billing as an opportunity to build culture away from home.

International and destination training camps have grown increasingly popular across the NBA over the past several seasons. The thinking is straightforward: remove players from their home environments, put them in a new setting, and accelerate the team chemistry that comes from shared experience. For a team with championship ambitions, the investment in that kind of environment is considered worthwhile.

A Team With Something to Prove

Cleveland heads into camp with significant momentum. James Harden just agreed to a 3-year, $97 million extension — declining a $42.3 million player option to secure more long-term guaranteed money and signaling he believes in what this team is building. His career earnings now stand at $511 million, placing him among the four highest-earning players in NBA history.

The Cavaliers have assembled a veteran-heavy, experience-rich roster that has its sights set on championship contention in a brutally competitive Eastern Conference. A week in Spain isn't just a travel story — it's a culture statement from an organization that is taking 2026-27 seriously from day one.

What's Next

After camp wraps in Spain on October 5, the Cavaliers return to the United States for preseason action before the regular season tips off on October 20. The home opener at Xfinity Mobile Arena comes October 22 against the Philadelphia 76ers — LeBron James' first regular-season visit to Cleveland since joining the Sixers. The NBA's September storylines have a way of connecting in October.
