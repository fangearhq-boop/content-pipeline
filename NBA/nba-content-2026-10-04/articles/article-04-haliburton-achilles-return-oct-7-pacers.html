Tyrese Haliburton Eyes Oct. 7 Return — 16 Months After Achilles Tear in the Finals

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | October 4, 2026

There are injuries that happen in moments that make them harder to forget. For Tyrese Haliburton, that moment came in Game 7 of the 2025 NBA Finals — the biggest stage in basketball, the highest-pressure point of his career — when he went down with a torn Achilles and did not return. The Indiana Pacers went on without him. The season ended. And Haliburton spent the following 16 months fighting his way back.

On October 7, three days from now, he is scheduled to return to the court in Pacers preseason action. For Indiana fans, that date has been circled since the moment he was cleared to resume full-contact practice.

The Achilles Comeback Trail

Achilles tears are among the most feared injuries in professional basketball. Recovery is long, demanding, and psychologically taxing in ways that most injuries are not. The tendon that generates push-off power for nearly every athletic movement in the sport must be completely rebuilt and retrained — a process that typically takes a full year and often longer for a player to feel fully like themselves again.

Haliburton was 25 years old when he tore his Achilles. He is 26 now, entering what should be the prime stretch of his career. The medical staff in Indiana has been deliberate about his recovery progression, and the choice to bring him back for the tail end of the preseason — rather than waiting until regular-season play — reflects a careful approach to managing his first competitive minutes.

The Pacers' decision to give him a preseason target rather than an opening-night target is telling. Indiana wants to see him in game conditions before the real stakes arrive. They want to monitor how his body responds to the physical demands of live basketball after 16 months of absence. October 7 is not a finish line. It is a checkpoint.

What the Pacers Get Back

Haliburton, when healthy, is one of the most complete point guards in the NBA. He sees the game at a rare level — his passing angles, his shot timing, his instinct for pace — and he runs an offense with an intelligence that is difficult to coach and nearly impossible to replicate. Indiana's 2025 Finals run was built around his ability to control a game without dominating the ball, creating advantages through movement and decision-making that extended to every player around him.

The Pacers built their roster in his absence with his eventual return in mind. The supporting cast has been maintained and refined. The system is built for him. October 7 begins the process of confirming that what made him so effective translates intact from before his injury to after it — a question only competitive minutes can truly answer.

What's Next

Indiana will monitor Haliburton's minute load carefully through the preseason. The goal is to give him enough competitive reps to build confidence and rhythm while avoiding the kind of aggressive usage that could create setbacks at the most critical time.

If October 7 goes smoothly — if Haliburton looks like Haliburton — the Indiana Pacers will enter the regular season with one of the most compelling comeback storylines in recent NBA memory fully intact. And the league will have another reason to take them seriously when the real games begin on October 20.
