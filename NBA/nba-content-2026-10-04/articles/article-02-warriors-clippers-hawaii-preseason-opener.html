Warriors and Clippers Open Preseason in Honolulu With a Glimpse of the New NBA West

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | October 4, 2026

Two familiar Western Conference rivals met in an unfamiliar place Sunday afternoon, with the Golden State Warriors hosting the Los Angeles Clippers at Stan Sheriff Center in Honolulu, Hawaii. The game capped the Warriors' Hawaii training camp with a preseason opener that served as the first official look at two significantly remade rosters — and a natural showcase for the biggest roster overhaul either franchise has attempted in years.

The backdrop was paradise. The stakes, in preseason terms, were modest. But the basketball was real enough to raise genuine questions about how both teams fit together, and Sunday offered the first competitive answers.

The Clippers Come to Honolulu With a New Identity

Los Angeles arrived in Hawaii having made some of the most significant roster changes in the Western Conference this offseason. Brandon Ingram and Gradey Dick came over from Toronto. Rui Hachimura signed from the Lakers. Max Strus joined from Cleveland. The Clippers have pivoted away from the era defined by Kawhi Leonard and Paul George and toward a younger, more versatile core.

Brandon Ingram's arrival is the centerpiece of the rebuild. The 6-foot-7 forward averaged more than 23 points per game in his final season with Toronto, and his combination of length, footwork, and creation ability fits the modern NBA's demands at the wing. Gradey Dick, still just 22, gives the Clippers a shooter who stretches the floor next to Ingram's drives. It is a pairing with legitimate upside, and Sunday's preseason game gave Clippers fans and analysts their first extended glimpse at how it looks in game settings.

Warriors Navigating Injury Questions Early

Golden State entered the game without Kristaps Porzingis, who is targeting a mid-October return from an undisclosed injury. The 7-foot-3 Latvian center was a key acquisition for the Warriors and his absence creates a noticeable hole in the frontcourt until he returns. Moses Moody is also sidelined with a knee issue, further shortening Golden State's rotation in the early preseason.

For Stephen Curry, the preseason is about rhythm more than proving anything. At 38, Curry enters the 2026-27 season as one of the game's enduring stars, still capable of the shooting that has defined his era. The Hawaii training camp gave Curry and his teammates a chance to bond in an environment removed from the Bay Area's familiar pressures — a reset before the real work begins.

Draymond Green, now in his 14th NBA season, brings championship DNA and defensive intelligence that no amount of roster turnover can replace. Whatever the final result looked like Sunday in Honolulu, Green's presence as an anchor gives Golden State a foundation to build around as the season's first weeks unfold.

What's Next

Both franchises have more preseason games ahead before the regular season opens on October 20 and 21 respectively. For the Clippers, Sunday was a statement — a new roster introducing itself to the league with confidence. For the Warriors, it was the first of several preseason tune-ups before their own opener on October 21.

Hawaii provided a beautiful and uncommon stage for the first chapter of both teams' seasons. The basketball world will be watching to see how the story develops — and whether the Clippers' new identity proves to be as formidable on a real NBA night as it looked in paradise.
