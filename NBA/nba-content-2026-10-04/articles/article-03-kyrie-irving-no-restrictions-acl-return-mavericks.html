Kyrie Irving Says "No Restrictions" as Mavericks Preseason Begins — The Return Is Real

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | October 4, 2026

When Kyrie Irving tore his ACL with the Dallas Mavericks in March 2025, the question was not just when he would return — it was whether the player who came back would be the same one who left. ACL tears in the late thirties are the kind of injuries that end careers, or at minimum redefine them. But heading into the 2026-27 preseason, Irving's message has been consistent and unequivocal: he is back, fully, and ready.

"No restrictions," Irving told reporters heading into training camp — a declaration that Mavericks fans have been waiting over 18 months to hear. He missed the entire 2025-26 season recovering from the injury. He spent the summer in pickup games, received praise from players around the league for showing his characteristic footwork and finishing ability, and arrived at camp medically cleared with no limitations on his activity.

The Long Road Back

ACL recovery timelines in the NBA typically run 9 to 12 months for a return to play. Irving's path has been more conservative, and deliberately so. The Mavericks and Irving's medical team chose patience over speed, ensuring that every milestone in his recovery — the first practice cuts, the first full-speed work, the return to competitive pickup games — was reached on the body's terms rather than the calendar's.

That caution appears to have paid off. Reports from Irving's summer pickup appearances described a player who still moves with the kind of lateral fluidity and positional awareness that makes him one of basketball's most unique presences. The spin moves were there. The hesitation dribble was there. The touch around the basket — always a signature — was there.

Irving is 34 years old. He enters this season aware that the margin for physical setbacks has narrowed with time. But he also arrives having done the work to put himself in the best possible position to recapture what made him special.

What Kyrie Means to Dallas

The Mavericks without Kyrie Irving in 2025-26 were a team operating below their ceiling. His injury forced Dallas into a rebuilding posture — exploring lineup combinations that could approximate his production while making long-term roster decisions around his eventual return. The entire franchise calculus changes when Irving is healthy and functional at the highest level.

Irving's partnership with the Mavericks' supporting cast — rebuilt and retooled over the past 18 months with his return in mind — gives Dallas a genuine Western Conference contender profile if everything holds together. The roster around him has been shaped to support his style: floor spacing, transition opportunities, and late-clock isolation situations where his individual skill set is maximally deployed.

What's Next

Irving is set to suit up for Mavericks preseason action in the coming days. The preseason slate gives him — and Dallas — a chance to ease him back into game speed gradually, managing minutes carefully while confirming that everything he has done in the summer translates to live competition.

The NBA world will be watching. An Irving who is truly "no restrictions" changes the Western Conference conversation. The Mavericks have been patient. Now comes the proof.
