The Kawhi Leonard Investigation: What 'Significant Punishment' Actually Means

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | July 29, 2026

The NBA hasn't issued its ruling yet. But the full scope of possible consequences is now known — and they range from a fine to voiding Leonard's contract entirely. The Clippers are bracing. The Raptors are waiting. The league is deciding.

The word "imminent" has hovered over the Kawhi Leonard investigation for weeks. A ruling was expected after Las Vegas Summer League. Summer League ended July 21. The ruling hasn't come.

What has come is a clearer picture of just how serious the consequences could be.

According to reports from multiple outlets, the Los Angeles Clippers are bracing for significant punishment — not the kind of fine that teams quietly absorb and move on from. Under the collective bargaining agreement, the worst-case scenarios in a case of this type include voiding Leonard's contract entirely, a suspension of up to 40 games, or a fine of up to $7.5 million combined with forfeiture of draft picks. Any combination of these outcomes is theoretically on the table.

What Started All of This

The investigation centers on a $28 million endorsement deal between Kawhi Leonard and Aspiration Fund Adviser LLC — a financial services company that has since filed for bankruptcy. Aspiration co-founder Joseph Sandberg was convicted of wire fraud and sentenced to 14 years in federal prison.

The Clippers have maintained throughout the investigation that they were victims of Sandberg's fraud — that the organization did not knowingly participate in any CBA violations and that the arrangement was presented to them as legitimate. The NBA has been gathering evidence through an outside law firm since the probe began nearly a year ago.

That fact-finding phase is now complete. The league is internally reviewing the findings before determining whether to issue discipline against Leonard, the Clippers, and/or owner Steve Ballmer.

The Process After the Ruling

Even after the NBA announces its findings, the matter doesn't resolve immediately. Under the CBA, any proposed punishment must go through an assistant arbitrator process. The National Basketball Players Association retains the right to appeal. Multiple steps remain between the league's announcement and any final, binding outcome.

That's why the pending trade between the Clippers and the Toronto Raptors — which would send Leonard to Toronto in exchange for Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, and future draft compensation — remains completely frozen. Toronto has declined to commit in writing to assuming the risk of any penalty that might attach to Leonard's contract. Until the league acts and the CBA process runs its course, the Raptors cannot finalize their roster plans.

Commissioner Adam Silver has said the probe must conclude before the 2026-27 season begins. That deadline is roughly eight weeks away.

What's Next

The ruling itself could come any day. When it does, the language the league uses will matter enormously. If the NBA finds wrongdoing by the Clippers as an organization, the punishment could be severe. If the league determines the Clippers were genuinely victims of fraud, the consequences may fall more heavily on Leonard as an individual — or the penalties could be less than the worst-case scenarios suggest.

Either way, the Raptors will finally have an answer. Toronto's offseason — already disrupted by nearly a year of frozen planning — will reach its first moment of clarity when the NBA speaks. The Eastern Conference could look different within hours of that announcement.
