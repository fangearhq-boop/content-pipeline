Ja Morant's Fresh Start in Portland: Facility Tours, WNBA Support, and a Different Kind of Energy

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | July 29, 2026

Morant held court at Summer League on July 11. He toured the Portland practice facility. He showed up for the city's new WNBA team. Six weeks in, the fresh start in the Pacific Northwest looks real.

The trade to Portland happened. The arrival happened. What's taken a few weeks to see clearly is what Ja Morant looks like on the other side of Memphis — and the early evidence suggests the reset is genuine.

On July 11, Morant sat for his first Portland Trail Blazers media session at the NBA Summer League in Las Vegas. He spoke openly about lifestyle changes: fishing, kayaking, biking. He described the trade as a fresh start and talked about the clarity that comes from stepping away from the weight of everything that accumulated in Memphis — the injuries, the suspensions, the noise. He was composed and thoughtful in a way that was noticeably different from his public appearances during the difficult final seasons with the Grizzlies.

On July 24, the Trail Blazers posted a practice facility tour video on their social channels. The caption read: "Feels like home." The video showed Morant moving through the Portland facility with the relaxed energy of someone who had already made the mental transition.

The Community Gesture

Morant has also made a point of showing up for Portland beyond the basketball court. The city's new WNBA expansion team, the Portland Fire, debuted this summer — and Morant recorded a brief public message of support: "What's up Blazers fans, Ja here. I'm in the city, here to support the Fire, let's get it."

It's a small gesture, but it reads as intentional. Players who've rebuilt their relationships with a city tend to find ways to connect beyond the sport — and Morant, arriving in Portland needing to establish credibility after a difficult few years, appears to be doing exactly that.

The Team Around Him

The roster Portland has assembled alongside Morant is one of the more intriguing collections of talent in the Western Conference. Damian Lillard is back on a three-year deal, returning to the city and fan base that made him a legend after a difficult stretch in Milwaukee. Deni Avdija emerged as one of the West's most versatile young wings last season. New head coach Micah Nori, who comes from the Minnesota Timberwolves staff, brings a system built around movement and defensive principles.

The question the team's entire offseason is built around: Can Morant stay on the floor? He played just 79 games across the past three seasons in Memphis — a stretch defined by injuries and suspensions that made his talent easy to forget between appearances.

When he's healthy and focused, there is no more electrifying point guard to watch. The Portland Trail Blazers are betting a significant amount of their short-term future on the version of Morant that was healthy and focused being the real one — the one who shows up on October 20 and plays through the schedule without interruption.

What's Next

Training camp opens in late September. Morant's first preseason games in a Portland uniform will be the next genuine test of where he is physically and mentally. If the summer has gone the way the early signs suggest, the Pacific Northwest could be watching something special. The fresh start, six weeks in, looks exactly like what both sides needed.
