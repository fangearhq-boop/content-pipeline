The 76ers Aren't Done Building — KCP Signs, Broome Moved in Busy Roster Day

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | July 29, 2026

Philadelphia assembled the Big 4. Now they're building the team around it. Kentavious Caldwell-Pope brings two championships and LeBron experience to the bench. Marcus Broome heads to Los Angeles in a cap-clearing move. The 76ers are operating with precision.

The Philadelphia 76ers announced their most important acquisition of the offseason weeks ago when LeBron James signed a two-year, $3.9 million deal — then followed it up by finalizing the Jaylen Brown trade, locking in the Big 4 with Joel Embiid and Tyrese Maxey. That was the headline. What comes after the headline is where championships are built or lost.

On Tuesday, Philadelphia added Kentavious Caldwell-Pope on a one-year, $3.9 million deal after the guard received a buyout from the Memphis Grizzlies. KCP is a two-time champion — part of the Los Angeles Lakers' 2020 NBA title team and the Denver Nuggets' 2023 championship roster. He also spent three-plus seasons as LeBron's teammate in Los Angeles, a shared history that Sixers executives view as immediate chemistry upside.

KCP averaged 8.4 points, 2.7 assists, and 2.5 rebounds in 21.3 minutes per game before a right-pinkie injury ended his Grizzlies season in February. At 32, he's not the same athletically explosive player he was in his Lakers prime — but he shoots threes at an above-average clip, plays disciplined defense, and knows how to play alongside stars without needing the ball.

The Broome Move

On the same day, Philadelphia moved Marcus Broome and a second-round pick to the Los Angeles Clippers in exchange for cash considerations. It's the kind of transaction that doesn't make back-page news but signals front-office competence: the 76ers are actively managing their cap and roster depth, not treating the roster as finished after the Big 4 was assembled.

Broome, who appeared in 46 games for Philadelphia last season, was an expendable contract in a year when every dollar and every roster spot matters. The cash Philadelphia receives in return can be applied toward future operational flexibility. The second-round pick sweetens the deal for a Clippers organization rebuilding in the post-Kawhi era.

A Bench Built for Winning

The projected starting lineup — Maxey, VJ Edgecombe, Brown, LeBron, Embiid — doesn't need a scorer off the bench. It needs shooters, defenders, and players who understand their role. KCP checks every box.

He will not be asked to create his own offense in Philadelphia. He will be asked to catch and shoot, guard the opponent's third-best wing, and not turn the ball over. He spent four years in Los Angeles doing exactly that alongside LeBron James. The transition to a new city, new system, and new supporting cast requires zero adjustment period on the most important dimensions.

The 76ers will continue filling the roster in the coming weeks. The 2026-27 schedule releases August 13-14. The regular season opens October 20. In the meantime, Philadelphia's front office is using every available day to get the supporting cast right.

What's Next

The Sixers have remaining roster spots to fill before training camp opens. Expect more veteran minimum signings in the coming weeks, with an emphasis on players who fit the defensive profile the team will need to compete in a revamped Eastern Conference.

KCP signing is a statement: Philadelphia is not coasting. The Big 4 was the foundation. What gets built on top of it is the difference between contending and winning. The front office appears to understand that distinction — and it's moving accordingly.
