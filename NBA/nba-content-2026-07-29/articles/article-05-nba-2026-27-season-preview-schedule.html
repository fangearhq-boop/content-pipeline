The 2026-27 NBA Season Is Almost Here — Schedule Drops Aug 13-14, Opening Night Oct 20

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | July 29, 2026

After one of the most active offseasons in NBA history, the basketball calendar has a start date. What you're getting may be the most anticipated NBA season in a decade.

The waiting is almost over.

The 2026-27 NBA schedule releases August 13-14. The regular season opens October 20. Between now and then: training camp. Preseason games. Roster moves. Six new head coaches getting their systems installed. And the slow build of anticipation that accompanies a season that looks, from every angle, unlike anything the league has produced in years.

What changed this offseason is not one thing. It's everything at once.

The Eastern Conference Has Been Rebuilt

LeBron James is in Philadelphia. At 41 years old, he signed with the 76ers on a two-year, $8 million veteran-minimum deal and joined Joel Embiid, Jaylen Brown, and Tyrese Maxey to form a Big 4 that rearranged the entire Eastern Conference power structure. The 76ers went from 20-to-1 championship longshots to 10-to-1 contenders overnight.

Giannis Antetokounmpo is in Miami. The Bucks traded the two-time MVP to the Heat, where he joins Bam Adebayo in a frontcourt that creates matchup problems for every team in the conference. The Milwaukee Bucks, who hired Taylor Jenkins as their new head coach, are now in rebuild mode. The Eastern Conference title fight runs through Philadelphia and Miami — with the Boston Celtics, Cleveland Cavaliers, and New York Knicks all pushing to remain relevant.

Six New Coaches Debut Simultaneously

One-fifth of the NBA enters 2026-27 under new head coaches — and the collection of storylines attached to those hires rivals any coaching class in recent memory.

Dusty May left a national championship at the University of Michigan to coach No. 1 overall pick Cooper Flagg in Dallas. May is the highest-profile college-to-NBA jump in years. Taylor Jenkins gets his second chance, this time in Milwaukee. Tiago Splitter takes over a Chicago Bulls team that just reportedly re-signed DeMar DeRozan. Sean Sweeney goes from Spurs Finals assistant to head coach in Orlando. Jamahl Mosley heads to New Orleans. Micah Nori — fresh off working with the Minnesota Timberwolves — inherits a Portland squad with Damian Lillard, Deni Avdija, and Ja Morant.

Six systems opening simultaneously. Six sets of roster adjustments, lineup experiments, and early-season storylines. The first month of the NBA calendar is going to require multiple screens.

The Western Conference Is Wide Open

Victor Wembanyama signed a five-year, $252 million deal with the San Antonio Spurs — a commitment that locks up the franchise's cornerstone through at least 2031. The Spurs, who won the 2025-26 title with Wemby, are positioned as long-term Western Conference contenders.

But the West is deep and loaded with uncertainty. The Golden State Warriors enter the season without LeBron or DeRozan after missing on both. The Oklahoma City Thunder, Denver Nuggets, and Minnesota Timberwolves all have legitimate ambitions. Anthony Edwards and the Timberwolves are pushing toward their first serious championship window.

What's Next

The schedule drops in two weeks. The dates that matter most: When do the 76ers first play the Heat? Is Christmas Day as loaded as it looks from here? Which new coach opens on the road, and which gets a home opener?

October 20 is the start. Between now and then, the league keeps moving. Roster spots fill, coaches finalize systems, and players get into shape for the most anticipated NBA season in years. The offseason has given fans more story lines than they can track. The season will start sorting them out.
