DeMar DeRozan's Chicago Bulls Return Appears Close — The Dollar Figure Is Still Contested

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | July 29, 2026

Yahoo Sports, Bleacher Report, and NBC Sports all have DeRozan headed back to Chicago. The contract terms haven't been confirmed by the league's top reporters. A homecoming is happening — but cautious fans should wait for the official word.

DeMar DeRozan is going back to Chicago.

That's the conclusion multiple major outlets are pointing toward. Yahoo Sports, Bleacher Report, and NBC Sports have all reported on the Chicago Bulls and DeRozan moving toward an agreement, after the Sacramento Kings waived the veteran guard on July 6. The specific contract terms remain unconfirmed by the league's most reliable NBA reporters, but the direction is consistent across enough credible sources to treat as likely.

What we don't have: confirmation from Shams Charania or Adrian Wojnarowski, the two reporters whose word would effectively make this official. The dollar figures in circulation range widely — from a significant multi-year deal worth $40 million per year to a much lower figure that appeared only on unverified social media accounts. Until one of the top reporters drops the confirmation, the specific terms should be treated as unknown.

The Story Behind the Story

DeRozan spent three years with the Chicago Bulls from 2021 to 2024 and turned them into the best years of his career. In the 2021-22 season, he averaged 26.2 points per game and made the All-Star Game as a starter for the first time in years. He led Chicago to the playoffs and gave the franchise an identity it hadn't had since the Derrick Rose era.

He was traded to Sacramento in 2024 as part of a broader rebuild conversation. The Kings, unable to establish a clear direction of their own, waived him this summer after failing to find a trade partner. At 37, DeRozan is still capable of being a significant offensive contributor. His midrange game — one of the most reliable in the NBA — ages better than most skill sets.

The Bulls, under new head coach Tiago Splitter, spent the early part of the offseason talking about building around their young players. A DeRozan signing pushes the team away from that narrative and toward something different: a team that wants to win now, with one of the game's most proven isolation scorers at the center of the offense.

What the Bulls Are Getting

Splitter's system at his previous coaching stops emphasized ball movement and player development. DeRozan is not a traditional ball-movement player — he's one of the league's great one-on-one scorers, a player who creates his own shot and draws free throws at a rate most wings cannot match.

That creates an interesting early tension in Chicago. How does Splitter deploy one of the NBA's most isolation-heavy offensive players in a system that typically favors flowing basketball? The answer will say a lot about what kind of team the Bulls want to be in 2026-27.

What's Next

The confirmation — when it comes — will set off a wave of secondary stories: how does DeRozan's return affect the Bulls' roster decisions, who plays alongside him in the starting lineup, and what does his presence mean for the team's direction under Splitter. For now, multiple credible outlets point to a homecoming. The official word is the last piece missing.
