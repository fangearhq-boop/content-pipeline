Hawks Center Henri Veesaar Tears ACL, Will Miss Entire 2026-27 Season

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | October 3, 2026

The Atlanta Hawks received devastating news before the 2026-27 season even reaches its first training camp practice: center Henri Veesaar suffered a torn ACL during a Sept. 15 workout and will require surgery this month, sidelining him for the entire upcoming season.

It is the cruelest kind of injury — one that arrives before competition has even begun, stripping an athlete of a full year at a critical point in their development. For Veesaar, who had been building toward a role in the Hawks' frontcourt rotation, the setback resets an important timeline.

A Blow to Atlanta's Frontcourt Depth

The Hawks entered training camp with questions about their frontcourt depth, and Veesaar's absence deepens those concerns. He had been expected to compete for meaningful rotation minutes alongside Atlanta's starting group, offering size and mobility that the team values as it continues its rebuild toward postseason contention.

Without him, Atlanta's options in the frontcourt are thinner. The coaching staff will need to adjust rotation plans and potentially lean more heavily on younger players or rely on the organization's development depth for coverage. Roster flexibility may also prompt additional moves before the season begins.

The Hawks have faced a run of injury-related setbacks in recent years that have disrupted their attempts to build consistency. Losing a key frontcourt piece before a single preseason game is precisely the kind of obstacle that organizations must overcome if long-term growth is the goal.

The Road Back

ACL recovery timelines have improved significantly with advances in surgical technique and rehabilitation, but a full season is still typically required before a player returns to competitive action. For Veesaar, that means roughly nine to twelve months of focused rehabilitation — a process that will carry him through the 2026-27 season entirely and position him for a potential return at the start of the 2027-28 campaign.

Young players who return well from ACL tears often report improved physical conditioning and a renewed mental approach to the game. The injury is a setback, but it does not have to be the defining moment of Veesaar's trajectory in Atlanta.

What's Next

The Hawks will need to address the frontcourt gap through internal development or external acquisitions. With training camp beginning in earnest, Atlanta's coaching staff has limited time to reconfigure their plans before preseason games begin. Veesaar himself faces the immediate challenge of surgery and the longer challenge of staying connected to the team through a season he will watch from the sideline.

The Hawks and their fan base will be watching Veesaar's recovery with genuine support. He remains part of Atlanta's long-term picture — just on a delayed timeline than anyone hoped when camp opened this fall.
