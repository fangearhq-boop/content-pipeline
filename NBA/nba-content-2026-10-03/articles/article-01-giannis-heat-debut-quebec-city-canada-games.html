Giannis Antetokounmpo Makes Heat Preseason Debut in Historic Quebec City Game

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | October 3, 2026

The moment Miami Heat fans have been waiting for since the blockbuster offseason trade finally arrived Saturday night — Giannis Antetokounmpo stepped onto the court in a Heat uniform for the first time, facing the Toronto Raptors at Videotron Centre in Quebec City as part of the NBA Canada Games 2026.

It was historic before tip-off. The game marked the first NBA contest — preseason or regular season — ever played in Quebec City, a long-anticipated milestone for Canadian basketball fans who have watched the sport explode in popularity over the past decade. A sold-out Videotron Centre roared as Giannis was introduced, even as the road team, a testament to the two-time MVP's global following.

A New Frontcourt Takes Shape

The most scrutinized subplot entering training camp has been how Giannis and All-Star center Bam Adebayo would fit alongside each other. Two dominant, physical, versatile forwards sharing a frontcourt is not a new concept in the NBA, but the specifics of this pairing — Giannis' unique skill set as a ball-handler and Bam's elite defensive awareness — gave coaches and analysts plenty to theorize about through the summer.

Saturday night's preseason opener provided the first real data points. Coaches will study the tape carefully for spacing, pick-and-roll mechanics, and transition coordination. For Heat Nation, the simple act of watching those two jerseys on the same floor was enough to generate genuine excitement about what this season might hold.

Canadian forward Andrew Wiggins, who joined the Heat in the offseason, added a particular edge to the Quebec City setting. Playing in front of fans from his home country, Wiggins represented a compelling storyline beyond even the Giannis headline.

A Season That Started Early

The Heat's offseason was defined by the Giannis acquisition, which reshaped the Eastern Conference landscape. Miami went from a perennial playoff contender to an immediate championship favorite. With Bam Adebayo entering the prime years of his career and head coach Erik Spoelstra bringing one of the NBA's most sophisticated systems, the expectation is nothing short of a Finals appearance.

Training camp in early September gave the roster its first chance to learn the adjustments Spoelstra had engineered to accommodate Giannis' offensive role. The early reports were encouraging — Giannis arrived in shape, engaged in the system, and drew immediate praise from teammates. But practice is one thing. Live competition is another.

The Heat did receive some concerning news earlier this week when guard Dru Smith suffered a right calf strain during training camp and is expected to miss several weeks. The injury is not considered serious long term but underscores how quickly a preseason can deliver unwanted surprises.

What's Next

The Heat will continue their preseason schedule over the coming weeks before the regular season opens on Oct. 20. For Giannis, every game between now and then is a chance to build chemistry with Adebayo, sharpen his understanding of Miami's offensive sets, and silence any remaining doubters about whether the pairing works. Saturday night was a beginning — and a historic one at that.

The Raptors, meanwhile, head to Vancouver for the second NBA Canada Game on Oct. 10 against the LA Clippers, extending what has become a rich tradition of international basketball in Canada. Saturday was Quebec City's inaugural chapter — and based on the reception, it almost certainly won't be the last.
