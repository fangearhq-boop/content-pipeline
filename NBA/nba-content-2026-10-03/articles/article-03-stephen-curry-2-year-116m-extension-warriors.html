Stephen Curry Signs 2-Year $116M Extension: A Warrior for Life Chases Ring No. 5

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | October 3, 2026

Stephen Curry has never been a player who chased headlines. He let his game speak, his championships accumulate, and his loyalty define his legacy. Now, with his latest contract decision, Curry has added another chapter to a story that could only have been written in Golden State.

The Golden State Warriors and their franchise cornerstone have agreed to a two-year, $116 million contract extension, sources confirmed this week. The deal includes a player option for the 2028-29 season, giving the 38-year-old the ability to play through the end of this decade in the organization where he has spent his entire career.

Home Is Not Just a Word

At Warriors media day, Curry summed up his decision in characteristically grounded terms. "This is home," he said. "This is what we've built. This is a community that I've played in front of my entire career. Being able to finish the story the right way is a big part of what I can control."

There was genuine intrigue this offseason about whether Curry might explore options elsewhere for one final championship push. He is 38 — healthy, still one of the most dangerous shooters alive, and with no shortage of contenders who would have reorganized their roster to land him. Ultimately, the pull of San Francisco and the belief that Golden State's retooled roster can compete proved stronger than any external option.

The Warriors' new additions include Kristaps Porzingis, the 7-foot-3 stretch center who provides spacing Curry has always thrived alongside, and Jimmy Butler III, a versatile wing who brings playoff experience and defensive intensity to a team that lacked both in recent seasons.

The Hunt for Ring No. 5

Curry already has four championships — 2015, 2017, 2018, and 2022. Those banners hang in Chase Center as permanent evidence of what he built with Klay Thompson, Draymond Green, and a system engineered by Steve Kerr. This version of the Warriors is different, but Curry's presence ensures the ceiling remains championship-caliber.

With training camp underway at BYU-Hawaii — the Warriors' annual departure from the Bay Area that has become a team-bonding tradition — the new roster is beginning to find its rhythms. The preseason opener against the LA Clippers, also in Hawaii, comes Sunday. It will be the first live look at Curry alongside his new supporting cast.

The chemistry question is real. It takes time to build the kind of trust required to win in April and May. But Curry has done this before, integrating new pieces and making them better simply by virtue of the attention defenses pay to him from opening tip to final buzzer.

What's Next

The Warriors open the regular season Oct. 21 and project as a top-four Western Conference seed based on the offseason's moves. Curry's extension removes any distraction that a contract year might have created and gives Golden State's front office the stability to build around him with purpose. The fifth ring remains a question — but it is no longer an unrealistic one.

For the NBA landscape as a whole, Curry's loyalty to Golden State is a reminder that not every star is available, not every era ends with a departure, and not every story needs a Hollywood twist. Sometimes the best ending is simply going home and finishing what you started.
