LaMelo Ball Is a Timberwolf — But DiVincenzo and Gobert Situations Cloud Camp

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | September 22, 2026

The Minnesota Timberwolves made one of the most exciting moves of the offseason when they acquired LaMelo Ball from the Charlotte Hornets in a deal headlined by former Sixth Man of the Year Naz Reid. The projected result is a starting lineup featuring Anthony Edwards, LaMelo Ball, Jonathan Kuminga, Jaden McDaniels, and Rudy Gobert — arguably the most dangerous collection of talent the franchise has ever assembled.

And yet, as training camp approaches on September 29, roster questions are accumulating faster than the excitement around LaMelo's debut.

The LaMelo Arrival

LaMelo Ball is 25 years old, a multi-time All-Star, and one of the most creative passers and scorers in the modern NBA. His arrival in Minnesota alongside Anthony Edwards creates the kind of backcourt pairing that keeps opposing coaches awake at night. Edwards is already one of the league's premier scorers. LaMelo is one of its premier playmakers. Together, they project as the centerpiece of a Western Conference contender.

Jonathan Kuminga, signed this offseason, adds a versatile, physical wing presence that fills a need Minnesota had been trying to address for years. McDaniels provides length and defensive capability. Gobert anchors the interior with one of the most durable rim-protecting presences in the conference.

On paper, this lineup looks like a legitimate title contender in the Western Conference.

The DiVincenzo Complication

Donte DiVincenzo is among the hottest trade candidates entering camp, according to multiple reports published this week. The situation is complicated: DiVincenzo faces the possibility of missing the entire 2026-27 season, and if Minnesota trades him, their path back to him is limited — they could only re-sign him via the taxpayer mid-level exception, a number that almost certainly cannot compete with what other teams could offer.

That dynamic creates pressure to make a decision before camp starts, or risk either losing him for diminished value or carrying a player whose long-term fit is uncertain. Neither outcome is ideal for a team trying to build cohesion around a newly acquired star.

The Gobert Question

Rudy Gobert has two years and $75 million remaining on his contract, including a player option. The Timberwolves turned away trade interest in Gobert this offseason — but the conversation is not over. His contract size and the cap complications it creates will continue to surface as Minnesota navigates the financial realities of building around Edwards, LaMelo, and Kuminga simultaneously.

Gobert remains one of the best rim protectors in basketball. His defensive impact is measurable and consistent. But at $37.5 million per year in a league where cap space drives roster construction, his contract creates constraints that Minnesota will have to manage carefully. The question entering camp is not whether Gobert is a good player. It is whether he is the best use of $37.5 million for a team with these specific championship aspirations.

The Big Picture

The Timberwolves enter 2026-27 with genuine Western Conference championship ambitions and genuine roster uncertainty at the same time. That combination is not unusual for contenders in the modern NBA — building championship teams almost always involves navigating financial complexity and personnel decisions that do not have clean answers. What Minnesota has that most teams do not is a generational talent in Anthony Edwards and an equally gifted backcourt partner now in LaMelo Ball.

The DiVincenzo and Gobert situations will resolve themselves — through trades, through camp performance, through decisions that front offices make when the roster has to finalize. What will not resolve itself is the excitement of watching Edwards and LaMelo run the floor together for the first time.

What's Next

Timberwolves training camp opens September 29. Watch for any movement on the DiVincenzo front before that date — a trade before camp would signal clarity about the direction of the roster. Gobert's situation is a longer play. LaMelo Ball's first game in a Minnesota uniform, whenever it comes, will be the moment the franchise's new identity becomes visible. Until then, camp is where the questions start to get answers.
