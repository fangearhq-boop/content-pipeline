Knicks Banner Night Tickets Now $2,127 to Get In — and Demand Is Still Rising

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | September 22, 2026

The market has officially validated what every basketball fan already knew: the October 20 Knicks banner night is not just a game. It is an event. And you are going to pay for it.

The get-in price for the New York Knicks' season opener against the Philadelphia 76ers at Madison Square Garden has now surpassed $2,127 on Gametime, according to reports published this week. For context on what that number represents: the Boston Celtics at Detroit Pistons on that same opening night has a get-in price of $195. The Oklahoma City Thunder at San Antonio Spurs is $450.

The Knicks banner night is operating in a different market entirely. And with 28 days until the first tip, the price appears to have no ceiling.

What Drives the Demand

The factors stacking up for October 20 at MSG are almost too many to count. The New York Knicks will raise their first championship banner since 1973 — a 53-year wait that defines one of sport's longest droughts finally ending in front of a sold-out crowd at the most famous arena in the world. That alone would sell this game out.

Then there is the opponent. LeBron James — 42 years old, signed with the Philadelphia 76ers on a two-year veteran-minimum contract this offseason — will walk into Madison Square Garden in a Sixers jersey for his first regular-season game as a member of that franchise. LeBron's first regular-season game in any new uniform is automatically a television event. His first regular-season game as the enemy in the enemy's house on their banner night is something beyond that.

Jaylen Brown, acquired from the Celtics in the summer in a trade that sent Paul George and four draft picks to Boston, makes his own Sixers debut that night. And this is not just any opponent visiting the Garden: the Sixers are the same team the Knicks swept 4-0 in last season's playoffs, holding them to 102 points per game while averaging 124.3 on the other end.

Revenge. History. Legacy. They are all converging on October 20 in Midtown Manhattan.

The Most Anticipated Opening Night in Years

NBC and Peacock will broadcast the game nationally, which means the sellout crowd inside MSG will be accompanied by a television audience that almost certainly makes it the most-watched regular-season opener in recent memory. Jalen Brunson, the Finals MVP who led New York to its first title in more than five decades, will be on the floor for the ceremony. The banner will rise.

And then the game will begin, and the Sixers — LeBron, Brown, Joel Embiid, Tyrese Maxey — will try to ruin it.

That narrative writes itself. The defending champion celebrating at home. The galactic new roster trying to assert itself. The revenge subplot built in from the first tip. There are opening nights, and then there is this.

The Secondary Market Reality

At $2,127 to get in the door — before fees, before any premium on better seats — October 20 has priced out most casual fans from attending in person. The crowd at MSG will be among the wealthiest and most invested in Knicks history. Scalpers are holding inventory. Prices will almost certainly continue to rise as the date approaches.

For the fans who cannot afford a ticket, the NBC/Peacock broadcast begins at 7 PM ET, with the banner ceremony scheduled for 6:30. It will be one of the most-watched events in basketball this year — not just the most-watched opener, but the most-watched single game, period, until the playoffs begin.

What's Next

The Knicks and 76ers hold their media days on September 28. Official training camps open September 29. Preseason games begin October 3. October 20 is the date every basketball fan has circled, and the secondary market has already told you everything you need to know about how history is going to treat this night.
