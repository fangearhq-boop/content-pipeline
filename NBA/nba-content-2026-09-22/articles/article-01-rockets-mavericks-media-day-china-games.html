The NBA Season Starts Today — For the Rockets and Mavericks, at Least

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | September 22, 2026

While the rest of the NBA spends one more week in its offseason, two franchises are already doing the real work. The Houston Rockets and Dallas Mavericks hold the first media day of the 2026-27 season on September 22 — a full seven days before the remaining 28 teams follow suit — as both clubs prepare for a trip to Macao and the NBA China Games on October 9 and 11.

For the league, this is a logistics story. For basketball fans, it is the first real look at a matchup that will be talked about all season: Cooper Flagg versus Kevin Durant.

Why These Two Teams?

The NBA China Games have been a fixture of the preseason calendar for years, pairing teams willing to absorb the travel disruption in exchange for the marketing exposure that comes with games in Asia. This year, the Rockets and Mavericks were selected. Their media days were moved to September 22, with Rockets training camp officially opening September 23, so both organizations have the preparation time they need before making the trans-Pacific trip.

The Venetian Arena in Macao will host both games — October 9 and October 11 — and the NBA will surround the event with fan programming including NBA House, Fan Day events, youth basketball clinics, and NBA Cares community initiatives. It is the league's global presence made tangible, and it gives two of the NBA's most compelling storylines a worldwide audience before the regular season even begins.

The Flagg-Durant Storyline

Cooper Flagg is 20 years old. He won the NBA Rookie of the Year award in 2025-26, averaging 21 points, 6.7 rebounds, and 4.5 assists across his debut season in Dallas. He entered the league with enormous expectations — and he met them.

Kevin Durant has been doing this for nearly two decades. He is the most technically complete offensive player of his era, a seven-time scoring champion whose footwork and release point remain the standard by which every tall scorer is measured. He is now with the Houston Rockets, and he has already weighed in on his upcoming opponent.

"He will be considered one of the best of all time," Durant said of Flagg, in comments that circulated widely this fall. That is not typical preseason praise. That is a veteran acknowledging a peer-in-progress — and it sets the table for two genuinely compelling matchups in Macao before the regular season matters.

Mavericks head coach Anthony Davis has already said he is confident Flagg is ready to defend Durant. That assessment may be tested early and often at The Venetian Arena.

The Bigger Picture

For Dallas, the China Games are a chance to build on what Flagg started as a rookie. His sophomore campaign begins with eyes on him from the entire basketball world — and matching up against Durant twice before the regular season gives him a benchmark that very few young players get. The experience of competing against the best, on an international stage, in front of a global audience, is not incidental preparation. It is exactly the kind of accelerant that separates good players from great ones.

For Houston, the optics are equally appealing. Durant is at the center of a Rockets team that has been building toward contention for years. The China Games are a showcase. His relationship with Flagg — whether adversarial or mentorship-flavored on the court — is the story the basketball world wants to watch.

What's Next

The rest of the NBA holds media day on September 28. Training camps around the league open September 29. Preseason games begin October 3. The regular season tips off October 20. But today, in Dallas and Houston, the 2026-27 NBA season is already underway. The China Games in Macao on October 9 and 11 will be the league's first meaningful action — and Cooper Flagg versus Kevin Durant will be the first must-watch matchup of the year.
