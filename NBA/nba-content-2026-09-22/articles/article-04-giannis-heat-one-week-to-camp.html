One Week Until Giannis and the Heat Take the Floor — The Work Is Already Done

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | September 22, 2026

On September 29, the Miami Heat will open official training camp at Kaseya Center. For the rest of the NBA, that date marks the start of work. For Giannis Antetokounmpo and his new teammates, it is simply the next phase of a process that began weeks ago.

Giannis has been in California for an unofficial minicamp with Heat players — organized by the players themselves, without coaching staff, before the doors at Kaseya open for business. He skipped Greece's FIBA qualifier invitation to be there. He showed up before anyone could reasonably expect him to. He has been learning every aspect of Erik Spoelstra's offensive and defensive systems through voluntary work that most stars would have taken the summer off to avoid.

That approach is not subtle. It is a declaration: Giannis Antetokounmpo came to Miami to win a championship, and he is not waiting for September 29 to start pursuing it.

What Miami Has Built

The trade that brought Giannis to South Beach from Milwaukee was finalized on July 6, 2026, ending his 13-season run with the Bucks. He is a two-time NBA MVP. He is arguably the most physically dominant player in the league. And he arrived in a city that had already shown it knew how to build championship teams around superstars.

Miami did not stop there. The Heat also added Klay Thompson this offseason — a four-time champion with championship DNA and still one of the most reliable catch-and-shoot threats in basketball. Bobby Portis brings energy and rebounding off the bench. Tim Hardaway Jr. adds depth on the wing. The roster Spoelstra is inheriting for 2026-27 is the most talented group Miami has fielded in years.

Giannis met Spoelstra and president Pat Riley during the unofficial minicamp period. The relationship is already forming before the first whistle blows at an official session.

The Spoelstra Factor

Erik Spoelstra has coached two different superstars to championships in Miami: LeBron James and Dwyane Wade in the Heat's back-to-back title run, and a culture-first, team-oriented group built without marquee names. He knows how to integrate stars without losing the defensive identity and collective accountability that has defined Heat basketball for two decades.

Giannis chose Miami in part because of Spoelstra. The early minicamp work — the system installation, the film sessions, the informal on-court conversations — reflects a player who wants to know the playbook before the test. For a head coach known for his preparation and attention to detail, having the franchise player arrive already familiar with the scheme is the best possible starting point.

The Championship Window

Miami missed the playoffs last season. The Giannis trade reset everything. With Klay Thompson alongside Giannis and Spoelstra running the operation, the Heat are not a dark horse anymore. They are a genuine Eastern Conference contender in a conference that also features the Knicks, the 76ers, and the Celtics.

The first preseason game is October 3. The regular season opens October 20. But for the Heat, the preparation advantage they have built through weeks of unofficial work before camp even opens could be the difference between a team that looks good on paper and one that looks dangerous from the first game.

What's Next

Official Heat training camp opens September 29 at Kaseya Center in Miami. The first preseason game is October 3. Giannis's first official game in a Heat uniform is the moment South Beach has been waiting for since July 6 — but the preparation for that moment has been underway for weeks. One more week of unofficial work, then the real season begins.
