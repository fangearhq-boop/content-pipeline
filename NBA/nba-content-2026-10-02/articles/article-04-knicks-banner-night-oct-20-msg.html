Knicks Banner Night Is 18 Days Away: 53 Years Compressed Into One Ceremony

By Jake Torres | October 2, 2026

On October 20, Madison Square Garden will raise its first championship banner since 1973, with the Philadelphia 76ers — and LeBron James — waiting courtside for the party to end.

In 18 days, the New York Knicks will raise a banner at Madison Square Garden for the first time in 53 years. The ceremony begins at 6:30 PM ET on October 20, with the regular season opener tipping off at 7:00 PM on NBC and Peacock. The opponent? The Philadelphia 76ers — featuring LeBron James in his Sixers debut and Jaylen Brown in what could be an instantly iconic rivalry opener.

If you want to be in the building, budget accordingly. The average secondary market ticket is sitting at $2,560, with get-in prices still above $1,000 — the most expensive NBA game of the week.

The Wait: 53 Years in Context

The New York Knicks won the 1973 NBA Championship under coach Red Holzman, with Willis Reed, Walt Frazier, and Dave DeBusschere as the franchise's last title heroes. Since then, New York has made the Finals twice — in 1994 and 1999 — and lost both times. Generations of fans have grown old waiting.

The Knicks swept the Philadelphia 76ers four games to zero in the 2025-26 playoffs before claiming the championship. The banner that goes up October 20 represents the culmination of what began as a patient rebuild and ended in the franchise's greatest modern achievement.

Why the Sixers Are the Perfect Guests

The Knicks swept the Sixers 4-0 on their way to the title last season. Now the Sixers are back — with LeBron James (41) and Jaylen Brown added to the roster — walking into MSG for the most emotionally charged home opener imaginable.

For the Knicks, winning Banner Night against the team they eliminated in the playoffs would be a statement. For the Sixers, stealing it on national television would make a statement of their own. There are few better opening-night matchups in recent NBA history.

The Spectacle

MSG's banner ceremonies are elaborate, emotional affairs even when the wait isn't 53 years. For this one, expect the arena to be filled beyond capacity with energy before LeBron even takes his warmups. The ceremony at 6:30 PM allows time for the full production before the 7 PM tip, broadcast nationally on NBC.

Tickets in the get-in range at over $1,000 represent more than a basketball game — they represent a piece of New York sports history most of the Garden's regulars thought they might never see in their lifetimes.

What's Next

The Knicks play two preseason games before the opener. Watch how they handle the media circus and the hype cycle of the next 18 days — championship defenses that get distracted early can stumble. For the fans, October 20 cannot come fast enough. For the team, the goal is to arrive that night focused, sharp, and ready to defend the banner they're about to raise.
