Jalen Duren Signs $200M Extension: Pistons Holdout Is Over

By Jake Torres | October 2, 2026

The 22-year-old All-Star center ends a prolonged contract standoff, agreeing to five years and $200 million fully guaranteed to remain the cornerstone of Detroit's rebuild.

The waiting is over. Jalen Duren and the Detroit Pistons have agreed on a five-year, $200 million fully guaranteed contract extension, ending a holdout that cast a shadow over the start of the 2026-27 training camp.

Duren, 22, missed media day and the opening days of camp as the two sides remained deadlocked on terms. The deal, averaging $40 million annually, keeps one of the league's most dominant young big men in Detroit for the foreseeable future.

What Duren Gave Up — And Why He Took the Deal

The math on Duren's decision is striking. Coming off an All-Star, All-NBA Third Team season, he was eligible for a maximum contract worth approximately $287 million over five years — roughly $57.4 million per year. By signing for $200 million, he left an estimated $87 million on the table.

For many players, full guarantees at $200 million over the length of a deal represent certainty over upside. Duren, who reportedly felt "perceived disrespect" during the negotiating process, ultimately chose security — and a franchise that has steadily built around him.

The Season That Made This Necessary

Duren's leverage was real. In the 2025-26 season, he averaged 19.5 points and 10.5 rebounds while shooting an extraordinary 65 percent from the floor — by any measure a dominant campaign for a 22-year-old center. He earned his first All-Star selection and finished third in Defensive Player of the Year voting while earning All-NBA Third Team recognition.

For context, Duren's twin brother Amen Thompson signed a $155 million extension with Houston this offseason. Together, the twins have now signed deals totaling more than $355 million — first brothers to each sign $100 million-plus NBA contracts.

What This Means for the Pistons

Detroit can now move forward. The Pistons have been steadily building since bottoming out earlier this decade, and Duren's extension — paired with Ausar Thompson's recent commitment — gives them foundational pieces locked in through the late 2020s. Head coach J.B. Bickerstaff can now run a full training camp with his entire roster.

The Pistons open their regular season on October 22. With Duren officially back in the fold, the mood inside the organization has shifted considerably.

What's Next

Duren returns to training camp and the Pistons resume their preseason preparations. The big question entering the 2026-27 season is whether Detroit can translate individual talent into playoff contention — Duren's contract buys them the time to find out. Watch for him in the Pistons' Oct. 4 preseason opener as he makes his return to the floor after the holdout.
