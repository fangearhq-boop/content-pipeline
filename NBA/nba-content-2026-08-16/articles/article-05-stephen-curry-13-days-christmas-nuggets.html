Thirteen Days to Stephen Curry's Extension Window — and Christmas Day Just Told You Everything

By Marcus Cole | August 16, 2026

The window to sign Curry to a two-year, $136.7 million extension opens August 29. The Warriors say they're "super aggressive." The schedule puts Golden State vs. Denver on Christmas Day. The league has made its vote. Thirteen days until Curry makes his.

The 2026-27 NBA schedule dropped on August 13, and somewhere in the five-game Christmas Day slate was a data point more revealing than any contract report: Nuggets vs. Warriors on December 25, on ESPN or ABC.

The league doesn't schedule teams on Christmas Day if it doesn't believe the matchup delivers. And Golden State vs. Denver — Stephen Curry vs. Nikola Jokic, two of the most watchable players in the sport — is a compelling Christmas game only if Curry is there. The schedule is a statement of intent. The league expects Stephen Curry to be a Warrior in 2026-27. The extension window opens August 29, thirteen days from now.

The Terms on the Table

The reported offer: two years, $136.7 million. That's approximately $68 million per season, and it would carry Curry through his age-40 season. Warriors GM Mike Dunleavy Jr. has described the team as "super aggressive" in its efforts to retain him. ESPN's Anthony Slater — the most closely sourced Warriors reporter working — has confirmed mutual interest in getting the deal done as soon as the extension window opens on August 29.

The counterargument, raised by ESPN's Brian Windhorst, is that a shorter deal might give Golden State more roster flexibility. It's a reasonable structural question. The Warriors finished 37-45 in 2025-26, and the salary structure around a $68 million Curry carries real constraints.

But Curry's leverage is the alternative: without a signed extension, he becomes an unrestricted free agent next summer — for the first time in his entire career. He has never tested the open market. He has spent 17 seasons in Golden State. The possibility that he leaves is low by any credible read of the situation. But it's no longer zero, and that changes the negotiation dynamics for both sides.

Why Curry Still Matters at 38

Curry shot 42 percent from three-point range in 2025-26 at age 38, on a Warriors team that finished below .500. He remains one of the most efficient offensive players in basketball — a shooter whose gravity shapes defenses regardless of whether the ball ends up in his hands. The Christmas Day inclusion is a recognition of that continued relevance.

A two-year extension at the reported figure is also an acknowledgment that the Warriors believe the version of Curry who can move the needle for a franchise is not gone. It's a bet on another two years that look like 2025-26 or better — not a bet on a decline that's already arrived.

What's Next

August 29 is the date to watch. If the extension is signed that day or within the first few days after the window opens, the conversation moves immediately to what Golden State builds around Curry for one or two more championship windows. If there's a delay — if Windhorst's shorter-deal framing becomes a negotiating sticking point — a countdown begins that everyone in the NBA will be watching. The Christmas Day schedule implies the league isn't worried about that scenario. Thirteen days will tell us if the league is right.
