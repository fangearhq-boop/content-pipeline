ESPN Warns the Kawhi Leonard Investigation Could Push Into 2027 — And No One Has a Deadline

By Marcus Cole | August 16, 2026

The outside law firm hired by the NBA has finished its fact-finding phase. Sanctions against the Clippers are expected. But a new ESPN report introduced the possibility that this 11-month probe might not resolve before the calendar year ends. Here's what that means for Toronto, Kawhi, and the league.

On June 30, the Kawhi Leonard trade was agreed. On July 9, it was paused. Eleven months and counting, the deal that would send Leonard to the Toronto Raptors for Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, and a package of picks remains in limbo — and the exit ramp is no longer guaranteed to arrive before training camp.

The latest reporting: The outside law firm hired by the NBA to investigate the Clippers' alleged cap circumvention has completed its fact-finding phase, per HoopsHype around August 10. That's news. Sources around the league believe sanctions against Los Angeles are coming. The trade — widely expected to ultimately be cleared — has moved a step closer to resolution.

But ESPN, in a late July report, added a warning: if complications arise, the investigation could push past the end of 2026 and into 2027. That framing resets the expectations that had been building around a September finish line.

The September Target Isn't Official

The "six-week" resolution window that sources mentioned to HoopsHype puts a potential finish line around September 22. Toronto's training camp is scheduled for Quebec City around September 28-29. The math lines up: finish the investigation before camp, clear the trade, bring Kawhi to Quebec City.

The problem is that this timeline is internal league belief, not an official deadline. The NBA has not committed to any specific date. The September 22 window is a projection, not a promise. And ESPN's warning is a reminder that projections in an investigation this complicated can slip.

Neither the Raptors nor the Clippers have a mechanism to force resolution. They can negotiate. They can cooperate fully with the investigation. But the pace is determined by the league and its outside counsel, and the league has not telegraphed an end date publicly.

What Kawhi Can and Cannot Lose

The circumvention allegation centers on a four-year, $28 million endorsement deal between Leonard and Aspiration, a company that held a major Clippers sponsorship during the relevant period. The claim is that the endorsement deal functioned as undisclosed compensation — a way to funnel money to a player above the salary cap.

Under the current collective bargaining agreement, cap circumvention by a team does not result in player suspension. Leonard is not at risk of missing games because of this investigation. His contract could theoretically be voided, but in practice that would be an unusual outcome that creates its own complications. Leonard's public posture has reflected his understanding of this: he has said he expects the trade to go through before training camp.

That confidence may be justified. The expected outcome remains that the Clippers face financial penalties, draft pick forfeitures, or both — and the trade gets cleared. But "expected" and "guaranteed" are not the same thing. Not anymore.

What's Next

The watch dates are September 22 (the projected internal resolution target), September 28-29 (Toronto camp in Quebec City), and — if ESPN's warning proves correct — some indeterminate point in 2027. Leonard's statement that he expects the trade to happen before camp carries weight, but it also carries a note of uncertainty neither he nor anyone else has fully resolved. The investigation has no official finish line. That's the state of play heading into Dutch GP week with NBA training camps less than six weeks away.
