LeBron James and the 76ers Walk Into MSG on Opening Night — Against the Defending Champions

By Damon Pierce | August 16, 2026

The 2026-27 schedule handed Philadelphia exactly what the franchise needed: a statement opener against the New York Knicks at Madison Square Garden on October 20. LeBron James called this his last decision. Joel Embiid is reportedly the healthiest he has been in years. This is the game every NBA fan circled the moment the schedule dropped.

When the NBA released the 2026-27 schedule on August 13, the first thing fans did was find Opening Night. And when they found it — Philadelphia 76ers at the New York Knicks, Madison Square Garden, October 20 — the reaction was immediate and unanimous: this is exactly right.

LeBron James and Joel Embiid walking into MSG against the defending champions on the first night of the season is not a coincidence of scheduling math. It's the league's statement that the 76ers are one of the stories it wants to tell in 2026-27, and that the Knicks' championship is going to be contested immediately and publicly.

The Last Decision

LeBron James called his signing with the 76ers his "last decision." In the context of a career that includes stops in Cleveland, Miami, Cleveland again, and Los Angeles, the phrasing carries a specific weight. This is not a two-year deal structured around optionality and market positioning. It's an ending — an attempt to add a final chapter to a story that already includes four championships and four MVP awards.

The projected starting five is staggering: Tyrese Maxey, VJ Edgecombe, Jaylen Brown, LeBron James, Joel Embiid. That unit carries five combined MVP awards onto the floor. Even by the standards of NBA superteam construction over the past 20 years, that projection is unusual.

Philadelphia is already appearing on preseason Finals favorite lists. The schedule structure helps: the 2026-27 season features the fewest back-to-backs in NBA history, averaging 14.2 per team. That number matters specifically for Embiid, whose biggest obstacle in previous seasons has been managing wear across a long schedule.

The Health Update

The most significant development heading into the season is this: Joel Embiid has been described by team personnel as "looking healthier than he has in a long time," per Yahoo Sports. That kind of report from inside the organization — not a media spin, but an on-record assessment from someone who sees him work — changes the calculus on the 76ers' championship ceiling.

Embiid has been Philadelphia's most talented player for years. He's also been the league's most expensive health risk. Back problems, knee surgeries, orbital fractures, and the accumulated impact of a 7-foot frame absorbing NBA contact over nine years have made his availability the single biggest variable in any 76ers projection.

If he plays close to a full season at or near his peak — and the health update suggests that's at least plausible — this team is a legitimate title contender. If not, it's a collection of expensive injuries at the end of their windows.

What's Next

Opening Night on October 20 is the first public test. The Knicks are defending champions walking into their own building with a banner they haven't raised yet. LeBron and Embiid are arriving with something to prove. The rest of the schedule will tell the longer story. But the league has made its intentions clear by giving the 76ers the most high-profile opener possible. The question October 20 will begin to answer: is this the team that wins the NBA's next championship, or the team that spent most to come close?
