Stephen Curry's Extension Window Opens August 29 — What the Warriors Need to Figure Out First

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | August 6, 2026

NBA Contracts / Golden State Warriors / Stephen Curry

There has been some confusion this week about the timeline for Stephen Curry's contract extension with the Golden State Warriors. To be clear: Curry does not become eligible to sign an extension until August 29, 2026. Nothing has happened yet. But when that date arrives, one of the most consequential contract decisions of the offseason will be at hand.

The expected deal: two years, $136.7 million — roughly $68.35 million per year — which would carry Curry through his age-40 season. ESPN's Anthony Slater has reported "mutual interest" from both sides, and those close to Curry have consistently said he wants to finish his career in Golden State. The structure of the deal is widely expected to be straightforward.

But the context around the deal has shifted since the question first emerged this summer.

The Roster Question

Curry is entering the final year of his current contract at $62.6 million for 2026-27. He is 38 years old. He shot 42% from three-point range last season — still elite production at any age, let alone his. He has told people close to him he has no interest in requesting a trade or leaving the Warriors. He wants to retire where he became great.

But wanting to stay and being convinced the Warriors can compete are two different things. Curry has reportedly made clear to those in his circle that he wants a roster capable of challenging for a title. He will sign the extension. The question is whether he'll sign it with conviction or with reservation.

This summer complicated the calculus. LeBron James, once briefly connected to Golden State in trade and free agency conversations, chose Philadelphia. The Warriors retained Draymond Green on a one-year, $27.7 million deal and brought back Gary Payton II at $3.9 million. But the roster is not the same kind of "Curry deserves this" roster that surrounded him during the dynasty years.

The Brian Windhorst Question

ESPN's Brian Windhorst has floated a different structural idea: that Curry might opt for a one-year deal rather than the full two, retaining flexibility for the offseason following 2026-27. Windhorst characterized the idea as "bold" and "risky" — and it has not been confirmed as the Warriors' official offer or Curry's preference. But it reflects the genuine uncertainty around where the franchise is headed beyond this season.

If Curry signs a two-year deal, he's committed through age 40 in a Warriors uniform. If he signs a one-year deal, he keeps his options open. Both outcomes are possible. The two-year deal is still considered the more likely outcome.

What August 29 Will Tell Us

The extension signing — when it happens — will likely be paired with some kind of statement from both Curry and the Warriors organization about the direction of the franchise. Whether that statement is bullish ("we have a plan to compete") or cautious ("we want to keep the door open") will reveal more about Golden State's 2027 plans than any reported move this offseason.

Curry is not done playing great basketball. He's also not going to play out his twilight years on a team that isn't trying to win.

What's Next

The August 29 eligibility window is three weeks away. Between now and then, watch for any reports of informal discussions between Curry's camp and the Warriors front office about deal structure. Once the extension is signed, attention will shift immediately to what moves Golden State makes before training camp to build around their foundational star for what may be the final chapter of one of basketball's greatest careers.
