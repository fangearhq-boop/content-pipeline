5 AM. Every Morning. LeBron James Is Already Building the 76ers' Culture Before Training Camp

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | August 6, 2026

NBA Offseason / Philadelphia 76ers / LeBron James / Tyrese Maxey

Training camp doesn't open until late September. The Philadelphia 76ers' first preseason game is projected for October 8. But LeBron James isn't waiting.

The 41-year-old forward — who officially joined the 76ers on July 26 on a two-year, $7.94 million deal — has been in the gym before most of the country wakes up, working alongside Tyrese Maxey in pre-dawn sessions that speak volumes about how seriously this team is taking its championship window.

"Every morning, 5 a.m., we're in the gym, 5:30, whatever in the gym," LeBron said of the routine, "and you just see his dedication."

A Veteran and a Star on the Rise

The pairing of James and Maxey was one of the more intriguing subplots of Philadelphia's blockbuster offseason. When the 76ers added LeBron, they weren't just adding star power — they were adding someone who has a specific reputation for shaping the work ethic and habits of teammates around him.

Maxey, 25, is one of the NBA's elite point guards after two consecutive seasons averaging more than 25 points and 7 assists. But even at his level, voluntary pre-dawn workouts with the greatest player of his era represent a different kind of off-season investment.

That LeBron is impressed by what he sees — specifically calling out Maxey's "dedication" — is its own kind of endorsement.

The Roster Around Them

The 76ers' projected starting five now carries five combined MVP awards: LeBron James (four), Joel Embiid (one), Jaylen Brown, Tyrese Maxey, and Jared Edgecombe. It's a lineup that draws comparisons to the superteam era — and with good reason.

Brown arrived from Boston in a trade that sent Paul George to the Celtics. Edgecombe represents youth and athleticism. And Embiid — the one variable that has always defined whether Philadelphia can make a deep playoff run — has cleared the first hurdle.

The 76ers' coaching staff has publicly confirmed that Joel Embiid is healthy heading into the 2026-27 season. No injury flags, no conditioning concerns. For a team whose ceiling has historically been limited by Embiid's availability, that news matters as much as any offseason transaction.

The Clock Is Ticking — In Multiple Ways

LeBron turns 42 during what would be a 2027 playoff run if Philadelphia makes it that far. His two-year contract takes him through his 24th season — a territory no player in modern NBA history has covered at his level.

The 5 AM sessions aren't just chemistry-building. They're a statement: that James is not coasting on reputation, and that his arrival in Philadelphia comes with the same relentless preparation that has defined his career across four franchises.

He's 41 and still first in the gym. His teammates are taking notes.

What's Next

The 76ers' unofficial preseason opener is currently projected for October 8, when Philadelphia is expected to visit the Brooklyn Nets at Barclays Center. Training camp opens in late September. The 2026-27 NBA schedule — expected to be released on Thursday, August 13 — will confirm opening night assignments and marquee match-ups for the season.

The question isn't whether this 76ers team has the talent to contend. It does. The question has always been health. Between these early workout sessions and Embiid's cleared bill of health, the early indicators are pointing the right direction.
