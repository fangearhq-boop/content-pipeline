Kawhi Leonard Investigation Could Extend Into 2027 — And the NBA Has No Easy Off-Ramp

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | August 6, 2026

NBA Investigation / Los Angeles Clippers / Toronto Raptors

The NBA's investigation into Kawhi Leonard and the Los Angeles Clippers was supposed to be a summer story. It may become a full-season saga.

According to ESPN, the probe — which centers on allegations that the Clippers funneled approximately $28 million to Leonard through an environmental company called Aspiration — could now extend into 2027. Commissioner Adam Silver had publicly stated his desire to resolve the matter before the 2026-27 season begins. But that resolution is no longer guaranteed, and the road to one is longer than it appeared even a month ago.

How We Got Here

The investigation's core allegation is straightforward: Aspiration, a company in which Clippers owner Steve Ballmer had personally invested $50 million, paid Leonard approximately $28 million in an endorsement deal that authorities believe was actually a disguised salary — a mechanism to pay Leonard above the NBA's salary cap without formal league approval.

Paul Sanberg, a prominent figure connected to the arrangement, has already been sentenced to 14 years in prison on related charges. That conviction gave the investigation added gravity and complicated any quick settlement path.

Before this offseason, the Clippers had reached an agreement in principle to trade Leonard back to the Toronto Raptors, where he won an NBA championship in 2019. But the moment the NBA formally told Toronto that it would assume full financial and basketball liability for any punishment handed to Leonard upon completing the trade, both organizations placed the transaction on hold. Toronto was unwilling to absorb an open-ended penalty — and that decision effectively froze the market for Leonard.

The 2027 Scenario

If the Clippers and the NBA cannot reach a settlement, the case proceeds to a neutral arbitrator under the Collective Bargaining Agreement — and that arbitrator operates with no hard deadline. It's a process that could easily run past the start of the 2026-27 season and, in a worst-case scenario, well into calendar year 2027.

The NBA Players Association has signaled its own willingness to escalate. The NBPA has made clear it will initiate arbitration proceedings if the league attempts to discipline Leonard without what it considers sufficient evidence. That's not a bluff — it's a procedural right the union has used before, and it adds another layer of delay to an already complicated timeline.

The Penalties on the Table

If found in violation of CBA rules, the penalties the league can impose are severe. Under the league's framework, the Clippers could face fines up to $7.5 million, the forfeiture of draft picks, and, in the most extreme outcome, the voiding of Leonard's $50.3 million contract for the 2026-27 season. Leonard himself could face a suspension of up to one year.

The Clippers have denied all wrongdoing throughout the process. They maintain that the Aspiration relationship was a legitimate business arrangement and that there was no intent to circumvent league salary rules.

The Raptors' Dilemma

Toronto is caught in a uniquely uncomfortable position. The organization built a trade around Leonard's return — a storyline that would have brought the franchise's most beloved recent champion back to the city where he became a legend. Instead, the Raptors are watching from the sidelines while the investigation plays out, unable to finalize a transaction they've agreed to in principle.

With training camp approaching and the season set to open in October, the window for resolving this in time to allow Toronto any meaningful roster-building around Leonard is shrinking rapidly.

What's Next

The most likely near-term development is a push toward settlement — a negotiated outcome between the league, the Clippers, and Leonard's representatives that avoids the arbitration path. But settlements require agreement on facts and penalties, and neither side has shown urgency toward that outcome.

Watch for updates in the coming weeks as the pre-training camp period approaches. Silver's "resolve before the season" mandate may prove aspirational rather than binding. The NBA has set a deadline the investigation appears unwilling to respect.
