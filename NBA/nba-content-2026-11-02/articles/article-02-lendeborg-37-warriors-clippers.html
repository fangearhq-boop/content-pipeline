Lendeborg Goes Off for 37 Without Curry: Warriors 124, Clippers 112

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | November 2, 2026

When Stephen Curry is sidelined, the Golden State Warriors typically lean on their system, their veterans, and their championship DNA to grind out results. On Sunday night against the Los Angeles Clippers, they had all of that — and a 21-year-old Dominican Republic native named Yaxel Lendeborg who took the opportunity and ran straight to a 37-point, six-three-pointer, six-assist masterpiece in a 124-112 Warriors victory.

Lendeborg, Golden State's 2026 first-round pick, was sensational from start to finish. He finished 13-of-19 from the field, including six three-pointers, and added six assists without a turnover. He led all scorers in the game. He filled every category on the box score with the kind of easy efficiency that signals not just potential but arrival.

The Kid Who "Used to Hate Curry"

The backstory on Lendeborg makes Sunday's performance even more compelling. At the 2026 NBA Draft, when his name was called by Golden State, Lendeborg caused a minor stir by openly admitting that he had grown up as a Kyrie Irving fan and had "used to hate Steph Curry." The comment went viral immediately, creating a fascinating dynamic between the veteran legend and his incoming rookie.

Curry, typically unflappable, reportedly joked with Lendeborg about the remark and took the young player under his wing. In the months since, Lendeborg has spoken frequently about the influence Curry has had on his development, crediting his three-point improvement in particular to hours of work with the greatest shooter in NBA history.

On Sunday, with Curry watching from the bench, Lendeborg channeled everything he has learned and put on a show that his mentor would have been proud of. Six three-pointers. Thirteen of nineteen from the field. The kind of performance that shifts how a fanbase thinks about its future.

Warriors Depth on Display

The victory underlined something important about this Golden State roster: they are not a one-man team. While Curry's brilliance defines the franchise brand, the Warriors have built a roster capable of competing at a high level when their star sits. The coaching staff's system — ball movement, open three-point looks, defensive rotations — functions regardless of who is running it, and Sunday was proof.

The Clippers, for their part, played a competitive game but had no answer for Lendeborg's combination of length, shooting, and playmaking. Golden State's 12-point margin of victory was not flattering to Los Angeles, but it reflected the reality of the evening: one player dominated, and the Warriors won going away.

What's Next

The Warriors host the Phoenix Suns on Monday night at 10:00 PM ET. The question is whether Curry returns to action or whether Lendeborg gets another extended run in the spotlight. Either way, Golden State's fan base has something exciting to watch. A rookie with a 37-point game in his portfolio has a tendency to become very popular very quickly. Yaxel Lendeborg has officially introduced himself to the NBA.
