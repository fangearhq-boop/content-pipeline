Luka Dončić Makes NBA History: 45-15-6-5-8 Leads Lakers Past Raptors 131-125

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | November 2, 2026

Luka Dončić has been delivering legendary performances since his NBA debut, but Sunday night at Crypto.com Arena, he did something no player has done in the 80-year history of professional basketball. The Los Angeles Lakers star became the first player ever to record at least 45 points, 15 rebounds, five assists, five steals, and five three-pointers made in a single game, leading Los Angeles to a 131-125 victory over the Toronto Raptors.

The final stat line — 45 points (12-of-23 from the field, 8-of-12 from three), 15 rebounds, six assists, and five steals — reads like a video game simulation gone haywire. But it was real, and it happened in a competitive game against a Toronto team that refused to fold. The Raptors hung around all night, making the historic performance even more meaningful because it was delivered under pressure, not in a blowout garbage-time explosion.

A Performance for the Ages

The numbers alone don't capture how dominant Dončić was. He was equally dangerous in every phase of the game. From three, he connected on eight of 12 attempts — a mark that would be impressive for a specialist, let alone a 6-foot-7 primary ball-handler. He swiped five steals while also drawing three charges, a testament to the defensive improvement that has defined his 2026-27 campaign.

Dončić was asked about the historic nature of the performance after the game and responded with characteristic understatement, touting his improved defensive effort and team execution. ESPN reported his comments focused on the win and his teammates more than any personal milestone.

Austin Reaves contributed a vital 19 points off the bench, providing spacing and secondary playmaking that kept the Raptors' defense from collapsing entirely on Luka. Walker Kessler quietly put together a monster performance of his own: 16 points, 15 rebounds, and three blocks, making him one of the rare cases of a player recording a 15-rebound game in the same contest as a 45-point performance from a teammate.

Toronto Gave Everything It Had

The Raptors made a game of it. Scottie Barnes was brilliant with 29 points and showed why he remains one of the East's most dynamic forwards. RJ Barrett added 22 points of his own, and Toronto's collective effort kept the margin close through three quarters. But every time the Raptors threatened to take the lead or close within single digits in the final period, Dončić had an answer — a pull-up three, a crafty floater, or another ill-timed steal that ended a promising Toronto possession.

The Raptors were held to 125 points and still lost by six. That is what a historic individual performance looks like from the other side: you play well enough to win most nights, and you still come up short.

Lakers Sit at 5-2

With the victory, Los Angeles improved to 5-2, firmly among the Western Conference's elite early-season performers. The Lakers were counted out by many analysts heading into this season after a series of roster upheavals, but Dončić's arrival via the trade from Dallas last spring has transformed this franchise. The combination of his scoring and playmaking with Kessler's rim protection and Reaves' off-ball shooting has proven to be a genuinely dangerous NBA roster construction.

Sunday's performance was Luka's clearest statement yet that the Lakers are not pretenders. They're contenders, and their No. 1 player just posted the most complete individual game the league has ever seen.

What's Next

The Lakers return to action Monday night with the Brooklyn Nets, then face a stretch of road games that will test the depth of this roster. For Dončić personally, the question after a historic night is always the same: can he sustain it? Based on his early-season form, the answer appears to be yes. The rest of the Western Conference had better hope otherwise.
