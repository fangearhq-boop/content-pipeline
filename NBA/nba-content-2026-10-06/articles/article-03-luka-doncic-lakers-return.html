Luka Doncic Returns: 31 Points in His First Lakers Game in Over 200 Days

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | October 6, 2026

After 200-plus days away from a basketball court, Luka Doncic stepped back onto the floor in a Los Angeles Lakers uniform on Monday night — and immediately looked like he belonged at the very top of the league.

Doncic finished with 31 points, 9 assists, and 5 rebounds as the Lakers beat the Sacramento Kings 127-103 in their preseason opener. The performance validated months of confident reports from Lakers staff that Doncic had made a full recovery from the hamstring injury that derailed his first season in Los Angeles before it truly began.

The Long Road Back

Doncic's time off the court stretched across the spring, summer, and into fall. The hamstring injury he suffered early in the 2025-26 season cost him virtually the entire year, leaving his new franchise — and a fanbase eager to see him in gold and purple — waiting for a version of him that never came.

The wait, judging by Monday's preseason debut, was worth it. Doncic showed his full repertoire within the first few minutes — mid-range pull-ups, step-back threes, skip passes to the corner, pick-and-roll reads that left Sacramento defenders a step behind. The rust, if there was any, burned off quickly.

Doncic said after the game that he had spent the offseason working extensively on strengthening the muscles around the injury site. "I feel good," he said simply. "Like myself."

The New Lakers Identity

This is a Lakers team that looks different from the one Doncic signed with. LeBron James departed for Philadelphia, the most seismic free agency move of the summer. In his place, the Lakers added Walker Kessler, Quentin Grimes, Collin Sexton, and Matisse Thybulle — pieces designed to support a Doncic-led offense rather than a LeBron-centered one.

The organization traveled to Slovenia in August for a chemistry-building trip centered around Doncic's homeland — a gesture that illustrated where the franchise's priorities now stand. He is the undisputed face of the franchise, the centerpiece around whom everything is being constructed.

Monday was the first on-court evidence that the construction is heading somewhere promising. Austin Reaves was sharp in 32 minutes. Rui Hachimura contributed 18 points. And Doncic orchestrated everything, finishing with nine assists without a single turnover.

The Franchise Stakes

The Lakers haven't won a championship since 2020, and LeBron's departure closed one of the most scrutinized chapters in franchise history. Doncic's arrival opened another. At 26 years old, he is entering what should be the prime of his basketball life — fully healthy, fully motivated, and leading a team being built specifically around his skill set.

The Western Conference is as competitive as it has been in years. The Oklahoma City Thunder, Dallas Mavericks, and Golden State Warriors all present serious obstacles. But if Monday's performance previews anything, the Lakers under Doncic will not be easy to beat.

What's Next

The Lakers play their second preseason game Tuesday night at Chase Center in San Francisco against the Warriors. The regular season opens October 21 — also against Golden State. Doncic's full availability heading into that opener is now, for the first time in over a year, a certainty rather than a hope.

The Lakers are back. Luka is back. And 31 points in a preseason game is just the opening act.
