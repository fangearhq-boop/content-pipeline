Knicks Banner Night Is 14 Days Away — and Demand Shows No Signs of Stopping

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | October 6, 2026

There are exactly 14 days until the New York Knicks raise their NBA championship banner at Madison Square Garden. The secondary market hasn't gotten the memo that the excitement is supposed to cool down. Average ticket price is still $2,560. Get-in remains above $1,000. It is, by a significant margin, the most expensive NBA game of the week, the month, and arguably the entire regular season.

This is what 53 years of waiting does to a fanbase.

The Night Itself

The ceremony is scheduled for 6:30 PM ET on October 20. The Knicks' championship banner — their first since 1973 — will be raised to the MSG rafters in front of a sold-out crowd that has been waiting for this moment through decades of disappointment, near-misses, and rebuilds that never quite came together.

Tip-off follows at 7:00 PM ET on NBC and Peacock. The opponent is the Philadelphia 76ers.

That detail is not incidental. The 76ers are the team the Knicks swept 4-0 en route to their title run last spring. And those 76ers no longer look like the version that got swept. They've added LeBron James and Jaylen Brown to a roster that already featured Joel Embiid and Tyrese Maxey. If there's a team designed to crash a championship party, Philadelphia is it.

LeBron's Opening Night Debut

The Knicks' banner ceremony will also serve as LeBron James's regular-season debut as a Sixer. He didn't play in Philly's preseason opener against New York on Monday — a fact that only heightens the drama heading into October 20.

James, who turns 42 in December, has made no secret of his motivation in joining Philadelphia. He said at media day that he "didn't leave his family to lose in the second round." His first regular-season appearance will be on the opponent's side of the floor as New York celebrates what Philadelphia failed to win.

The narrative writes itself. Ticket prices confirm that fans understand exactly what they're going to see.

A Historical Backdrop

The 1973 championship was the last time the Knicks stood atop the NBA. Walt Frazier, Willis Reed, Dave DeBusschere — names from a different era of basketball, honored in retired numbers and franchise mythology for five decades while the trophy stayed away.

Last season's championship run was celebrated across the city in ways that hadn't been seen in a generation. The parade. The block parties. The ticker tape. Now the moment of formal recognition arrives — the banner going up, a permanent fixture in the building, visible from any seat in the house.

The price of a ticket reflects what the market believes October 20 will deliver: history, ceremony, and the opening salvo of a rivalry that could define the Eastern Conference for the next several years.

What's Next

The Knicks continue their preseason schedule this week, with the starting unit expected to see its first major minutes over the coming days. The full-strength 76ers likely won't be on the floor until opening night itself — meaning the October 20 matchup could be the first time both teams are fully operational.

Two weeks from tonight, Madison Square Garden will be the loudest building in professional sports. The championship banner goes up. The rival walks in. And 53 years of history gets its proper send-off.
