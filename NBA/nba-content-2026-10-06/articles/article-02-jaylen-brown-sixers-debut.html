Jaylen Brown's 76ers Debut: 22 Points in 17 Minutes Is a Warning to the NBA

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | October 6, 2026

Jaylen Brown took the floor at the Xfinity Mobile Arena on Monday night, pulled on a Sixers uniform for the first time in a real game, and immediately looked like he had been playing in Philadelphia for years.

The result: 22 points, 2 rebounds, 2 assists, and 1 block in 17 minutes. Brown shot 8-of-13 from the field and knocked down two of his three three-point attempts. The 76ers beat the Knicks 120-97 in their preseason opener. And the headliners — LeBron James, Joel Embiid, and Tyrese Maxey — never left the bench.

It was a statement performance. It was also just a preseason game. But it felt like the beginning of something dangerous.

Ready on Day One

Brown arrived in Philadelphia via a sign-and-trade that restructured the Sixers' entire roster around their new Big Three. The expectation was always that he would fit — the question was how long it would take. Based on Monday's showing, the answer might be: not long at all.

Brown played with the second unit for most of his minutes and immediately asserted himself as the group's most dangerous offensive option. He attacked closeouts off ball screens, got into the paint at will, and showed the precise, disciplined shooting mechanics that made him one of the most sought-after wing players in free agency.

Sixers coach Nick Nurse called Brown's performance "exactly what we hoped for," noting that Brown had worked extensively with the team's staff throughout training camp to learn the offensive scheme.

The Stars Sat — and Philly Still Dominated

Perhaps the more telling detail from Monday's win was not what Brown did, but what the Sixers accomplished without three-fifths of their projected starting lineup. LeBron James, Joel Embiid, and Tyrese Maxey all sat out the preseason opener — a routine precaution for veteran players in meaningless October games.

And yet the Sixers still beat the Knicks — the reigning NBA champions — by 23 points.

This is a team with depth that most contenders can only dream about. Even without its three biggest stars, Philadelphia has enough talent, size, and shooting to handle a quality opponent. When LeBron, Embiid, and Maxey are on the floor together with Brown and a healthy supporting cast, the ceiling is almost impossible to project.

The Knicks Connection

There's a specific irony in Brown's dominant debut coming against New York. The Knicks won the championship last season by sweeping the 76ers 4-0 in the second round. That memory is fresh. Brown wasn't on the team for that sweep — but he was aware of it, and every player in the Sixers locker room has heard about it repeatedly since July.

The rematch, for real, comes on October 20 at Madison Square Garden — the Knicks' championship banner ceremony opener. LeBron James will be there. Embiid will be there. And Brown will be there, having already provided a preview of what the Sixers are capable of doing to a team that thought last season's sweep told the whole story.

What's Next

The 76ers play next in Brooklyn as part of their preseason slate. LeBron is reportedly targeting next week for his first preseason appearance. The full starting five together on the floor is something the NBA will be watching closely — not just for chemistry, but for the sheer spectacle of it.

Brown made one thing clear on Monday: he's already a weapon. When you add him to the two best players on the planet and one of the best point guards in the league, you have something that the rest of the league should genuinely fear. The debut is over. The warning has been issued.
