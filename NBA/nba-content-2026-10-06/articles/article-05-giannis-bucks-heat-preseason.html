Giannis Faces His Old Team: Bucks Win 103-93 as Heat's New Era Gets Its First Test

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | October 6, 2026

It was always going to be strange. Giannis Antetokounmpo walked into the Fiserv Forum on Tuesday night not as the franchise cornerstone who had defined a decade of Milwaukee basketball, but as the visitor. The player wearing a Miami Heat uniform. The man whose departure this summer shocked the league and reshaped two franchises simultaneously.

The Bucks won 103-93 in the NBA preseason. The score, in October, doesn't matter much. The scene did.

A Complicated Welcome

The crowd's reaction to Giannis was layered, which is perhaps the most accurate word for what 13 seasons together can produce when they end. There were cheers — genuine ones, from fans who remember what he meant to Milwaukee, the two MVPs, the 2021 championship, the unbreakable commitment to a market that most stars of his caliber would have fled years earlier.

There were also boos. Also genuine. Because it still stings to watch a legend walk through your building in the wrong colors.

Giannis took it in stride. He has been through more dramatic moments than a preseason game in his former home. He's a 13-year NBA veteran with two MVP awards, a championship ring, and a global platform. He handled the noise the way you'd expect: professionally, warmly when his former teammates greeted him, and then with complete focus once the ball was in the air.

Miami's New Roster Under the Lights

The Heat are a dramatically different team than the one that spent the last several seasons as a solid playoff contender. Alongside Giannis, Miami added Klay Thompson, Bobby Portis, and Tim Hardaway Jr. in the offseason — surrounding their new superstar with shooters, energy, and veterans who have won at the highest level.

Erik Spoelstra, already one of the most respected coaches in the league, now has his first true franchise cornerstone. The challenge is installing Giannis into the Heat's famously complex offensive system in time for opening night. The connections weren't fully formed on Tuesday — some hesitation on cuts, a few offensive possessions where reads didn't quite translate — but those are exactly the kinds of rough edges preseason games are designed to smooth out.

The talent is not in question. The chemistry is still being built.

What This Game Was Really About

Preseason results mean very little. The Bucks winning 103-93 on Tuesday says almost nothing about where either team will be in April. What the game provided was a first look at the new realities — Giannis in Heat red against familiar faces, Damian Lillard and Bobby Portis playing alongside different teammates, a Milwaukee roster that has to figure out its identity without the player who defined it for over a decade.

Both teams have questions to answer. The Heat need time to integrate Giannis into the Spoelstra system. The Bucks need to prove they can remain a legitimate contender after losing their franchise player. Neither question has a preseason answer.

What's Next

The Heat and Bucks will meet again when the regular season begins in mid-October. When they do, the stakes will be real, the rotations will be tighter, and the crowd will be louder. Tuesday night was the prologue — emotional, historic in its own small way, but ultimately just the first chapter of a much longer story.

Giannis smiled when he walked off the floor. Whatever he said to his old teammates in those brief post-game exchanges, it looked friendly. Milwaukee and Miami may be rivals now, but the warmth of 13 seasons doesn't vanish overnight. The basketball relationship is just beginning a new chapter.
