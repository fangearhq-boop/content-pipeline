Wembanyama Said He Doesn't Miss the NBA. He Misses FIBA. In 9 Days, He Returns.

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | August 11, 2026

NBA Offseason / San Antonio Spurs / Victor Wembanyama / France FIBA

Victor Wembanyama summed it up more directly than most professional athletes would dare. Asked about his time away from basketball this summer: "I don't miss the NBA at the moment, but I do miss FIBA."

In nine days, he gets what he's been missing. France opens against Serbia in Belgrade on August 20 — the first time Wembanyama has played for Les Bleus since the 2024 Paris Olympics, where France took silver in front of a home crowd. Two years of waiting, nine days to go.

The August Schedule

France's window is a four-game sprint:

August 20 in Belgrade: an exhibition against Serbia on their home court. August 23 in Orleans: a rematch, this time in France. Then two matches that carry real stakes — a 2027 FIBA World Cup qualifier against Slovenia at home on August 27, and a road qualifier at Sweden on August 30.

France enters this window 5-1 in Group L of World Cup qualifying. The qualifiers matter. The exhibitions are a warm-up. Wembanyama is ready for both.

A New Partnership at the Top

This August window is also the first time France's new head coach, Frederic Fauthoux, will coach Wembanyama in an official setting. Fauthoux has been in the role since September 2024 — but has not had Wembanyama available until now. It is a relationship that the French national program has been waiting to build.

The roster around Wemby includes Rudy Gobert and Evan Fournier — the same core that made France a genuine threat at the Paris Olympics. A generation of French basketball talent, reunited, with the best player in the world leading them.

What Spurs Fans Are Watching

From San Antonio's perspective, this window is a balance of excitement and caution. Wembanyama is the franchise cornerstone — the player the Spurs have built their entire future around. Seeing him play four competitive games before NBA training camp opens carries some workload risk, and Spurs fans will be paying attention to every minute he plays.

But Wembanyama himself seems energized by it. His quote about missing FIBA is not a slight against the NBA — it's the honest reaction of a player who grew up in European basketball culture, who remembers what it felt like to play in those environments. Paris was the biggest stage he'd ever been on. Belgrade will not be that. But it's basketball. And Wemby is hungry for it.

What's Next

Victor Wembanyama and France open their FIBA August window on August 20 in Belgrade against Serbia. A rematch in Orleans follows August 23. World Cup qualifying matches come on August 27 (vs. Slovenia, home) and August 30 (at Sweden). France is 5-1 in Group L. Head coach Frederic Fauthoux and Wembanyama will meet on the court together for the first time. For a player who says he misses FIBA, the wait is almost over.
