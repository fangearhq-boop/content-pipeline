18 Days, $136.7 Million, and a Discount Debate: What Steph Curry's Extension Decision Really Means

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | August 11, 2026

NBA Contracts / Golden State Warriors / Stephen Curry

The extension window opens in 18 days. The expected number is two years, $136.7 million — roughly $68 million per season, taking Stephen Curry through his age-40 year in 2028-29. The mutual interest is confirmed. The Warriors are ready. Curry is ready. On the surface, this looks like a formality.

Underneath it, something more interesting is forming.

The Milestone That Would Be Made

If Curry signs the full two-year maximum extension, his 2026-27 earnings would make him the first NBA player in history to surpass $60 million in a single season. No player has ever crossed that threshold. It is a financial milestone that, in the context of where Curry sits in the history of the game, feels entirely appropriate.

Warriors GM Mike Dunleavy Jr. has made the team's stance clear: whatever structure Curry wants — one year, two years, the full max — Golden State is ready to have that conversation. "If he wants a one-year extension at big money, if he wants a two-year extension, the Warriors are very open to that conversation."

The Discount Debate

ESPN's Brian Windhorst has raised the possibility of a shorter-term deal structured to give Golden State more roster flexibility to bring in another star. A Western Conference executive told reporters that Curry is smart enough to at least explore a below-max structure: "He'll want to know the options and the plan."

Curry's own words add texture to that framing. He has said he is "absolutely" open to sacrifice — language that, from most players, would be dismissed as polite deflection. From Curry, who built four championship teams in part on a famously below-market deal, it carries real weight. He has done this before. He knows what it unlocks.

The context matters. LeBron James chose Philadelphia over Golden State this summer. The Warriors finished last season at 37-45. Draymond Green is back on a one-year deal. Gary Payton II returned on a one-year deal. The roster around Curry, while improving, still needs another piece to contend. The structure of this extension — its length, its dollar figure — sets the ceiling on what Golden State can build around him.

The History

Curry's relationship with contract structure has always been more complicated than the headline number. His famous below-market deal in the early 2010s gave the Warriors the cap space to build a dynasty. He eventually signed a supermax extension worth $215 million — but by then, the team was already built. This extension is different. The team is not yet built around him for one final championship run.

Whether Curry signs the full $136.7 million or takes something more creative, the window opens August 29. A September signing is widely expected. What that signing says about Curry's intentions — both financial and competitive — is the real story of the next 18 days.

What's Next

Stephen Curry's extension window opens August 29, 18 days from today. The expected deal is two years, $136.7 million — with the possibility of a shorter or below-max structure to give Golden State roster flexibility. A formal signing is expected in September. Once it's done, the Warriors' 2026-27 roster picture will come into sharper focus — and the question of whether this team can compete for a fifth championship banner will move from speculation to analysis.
