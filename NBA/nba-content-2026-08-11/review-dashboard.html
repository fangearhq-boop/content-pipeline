NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post ⚖️ The most complicated trade in NBA history may be approaching the finish line. HoopsHype reported Monday that there is a belief in some circles of the league that the NBA's investigation into Kawhi Leonard and the Los Angeles Clippers could resolve within the **next six weeks** — putting a resolution squarely before training camp opens in late September. And for the first time in this saga, the signal isn't coming just from league observers. Kawhi Leonard himself believes the trade to the Toronto Raptors will go through before camp. Commissioner Adam Silver has made his position clear publicly: "This needs to be wrapped up before the beginning of next season." Here's where the investigation stands: **The probe:** The NBA has been examining whether the Clippers circumvented the salary cap through a $28 million endorsement deal between Kawhi Leonard and Aspiration (a company that also held a $300M, 23-year sponsorship deal with the Clippers). A second previously undisclosed deal with Daktronics is also under review. The fact-finding phase is now complete. The NBA is in resolution mode. **The trade (still frozen):** Kawhi Leonard goes to Toronto for Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, unprotected 2031 and 2033 first-round picks, a 2027 first-round pick swap, and two second-round picks. Toronto was told it would bear the risk of any penalties by finalizing the trade before the probe ends — so the Raptors chose to wait. **What happens next:** If the Clippers are found guilty of willful circumvention, potential penalties include fines up to $7.5 million, draft-pick forfeiture, and suspensions of up to one year for personnel involved. But even with sanctions, the expectation is the trade clears. Kawhi Leonard is owed $50.3 million for the 2026-27 season. He currently has no team to play for. The light at the end of the tunnel is visible. ⏰ ---

Stephen Curry — 18 Days Until the Extension Window Opens
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post 🏀 Stephen Curry's extension window opens in 18 days. And a question is starting to simmer: will he actually take the full max? The expected number is two years, $136.7 million — roughly $68 million per year, taking Curry through his age-40 season. If signed, it would make him the first NBA player in history to earn more than $60 million in a single season. The Warriors want it done. Curry wants to be in Golden State. Mutual interest is confirmed. But ESPN's Brian Windhorst has floated a different idea — a shorter-term structure that would give Golden State more roster flexibility to pursue a complementary star. And a Western Conference executive said to expect Curry to at least explore a below-max structure: "He's a smart guy — he'll want to know the options and the plan." Curry himself has said he's "absolutely" open to sacrifice. That word has weight. The context: LeBron James chose Philadelphia over Golden State this summer. The Warriors finished 37-45 last season. Draymond Green is back (1yr/$27.7M). Gary Payton II is back (1yr/$3.88M). But the roster around Curry needs reinforcement, and how much money Curry takes — and for how long — determines what comes next. The window opens August 29. A September signing is widely expected. The question is whether the number ends up being exactly $136.7 million. 🤔 ---

That's a different kind of motivation than anything he's had before. **What's next.** LeBron and Maxey are already working out together under trainer Chris Johnson. Kentavious Caldwell-Pope signed a 1-year deal (Aug 5) to add veteran depth. Training camp opens in late September. If Embiid is truly healthy for 60+ games, this team is the favorite.
