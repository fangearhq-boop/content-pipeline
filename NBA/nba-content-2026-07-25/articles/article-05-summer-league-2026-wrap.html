Warriors Win the 2026 NBA Summer League — Lendeborg Shines, Dybantsa and Peterson Deliver on the Hype

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes

July 25, 2026

The 2026 NBA Summer League in Las Vegas has ended, and the Golden State Warriors are holding the championship trophy. What came before that July 19 title game — two weeks of basketball featuring the top picks in the draft, surprising performances from unknowns, and a tournament that set new attendance and merchandise records — gave the league a genuine reason to be excited about what comes next.

Warriors Take the Title

Golden State defeated the Memphis Grizzlies in the championship game, with first-round pick Yaxel Lendeborg leading the way with 21 points. Lendeborg's performance in the title game was the exclamation point on a Summer League run that gave Warriors fans an early look at what their next wave of talent might bring.

For Golden State, a franchise that has spent recent seasons in transition after their dynasty years, the Summer League championship provides modest but meaningful momentum heading into the fall. A first-round pick who can score 21 points in a championship game is, at minimum, a player worth watching in training camp.

The Showdown Everyone Wanted: Dybantsa vs. Peterson

The headline storyline of the 2026 Summer League was the matchup between the top two picks in the 2026 Draft. No. 1 overall AJ Dybantsa, selected by the Washington Wizards, faced No. 2 Darryn Peterson, selected by the Utah Jazz, in what became the most anticipated early look at two of the NBA's most talked-about prospects.

Both players came into Las Vegas with enormous expectations built from their pre-draft performances, and both delivered enough to justify the buzz. Dybantsa, the Wizards' crown jewel of their rebuild, showed the offensive versatility that made him the consensus top pick. Peterson, Utah's investment in their youth movement, demonstrated the two-way intensity that separated him from the rest of the class.

Their paths will cross again in regular-season play. July was only the preview.

Record-Setting Event

The NBA reported that the 2026 Summer League set new attendance and merchandise records. The combination of an off-season filled with major transactions — including LeBron James's free agency deliberation and multiple blockbuster trades — elevated interest in the league to a level that translated directly into fan engagement in Las Vegas.

Each team played four games during the group stage, with the top four teams advancing to a single-elimination playoff beginning July 18. The format rewarded consistency and gave fans a genuine bracket to follow through to the final.

What's Next

Summer League is the preview; training camp is the test. Lendeborg's championship-game performance will be filed away and revisited when Golden State's full roster assembles in late September. Dybantsa and Peterson will spend the summer refining their games before their first regular-season tip-offs in October.

For the NBA, the 2026 Summer League closed one of the most watched offseason periods in recent memory. LeBron chose Philadelphia. Wembanyama and Fox committed to San Antonio. Kawhi went home to Toronto. Morant moved to Portland. The summer delivered stories that will define the 2026-27 season before it even tips off. And in Las Vegas, the Warriors got to hoist one more trophy — however small — to welcome their newest pieces to the fold.
