Kawhi Leonard Returns to Toronto — The Franchise That Gave Him Everything Is Getting Him Back

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes

July 25, 2026

Kawhi Leonard is coming home to Toronto.

After six seasons with the Los Angeles Clippers — a tenure defined as much by injuries and investigation as by basketball — Leonard is returning to the Raptors franchise as a free agent. It is the kind of full-circle moment that the NBA only occasionally delivers, and Toronto fans have been waiting seven years for it.

What Toronto Meant to Kawhi

The 2019 NBA Championship remains the defining moment in Toronto Raptors history. Leonard's series-winning performance — including the four-bounce buzzer-beater off the glass against Philadelphia that became one of the most replayed clips in NBA history — turned him from an elite two-way wing into a legend. He averaged 28.5 points per game in the 2019 Finals against Golden State and was named Finals MVP.

He left that summer in free agency, choosing the Clippers for a return to Southern California and a partnership with Paul George. The Raptors moved forward without him, making the Eastern Conference Semifinals the following season and eventually reshaping their roster around a younger core.

Now, at 35, Leonard is choosing Toronto again.

The Long Road Back

The path back to Toronto was complicated. Leonard's time with the Clippers included significant knee injuries that cost him entire seasons, and more recently the franchise became embroiled in an NBA investigation that froze his roster status entirely. Commissioner Adam Silver had mandated that the investigation — which expanded beyond its original scope to cover a second endorsement deal and additional personal expenses — must conclude before the start of the 2026-27 season.

The Raptors had been sitting in roster limbo for months, unable to finalize moves around a player they were hoping to receive via trade. That uncertainty is now resolved. Toronto has its centerpiece. The front office can build around Leonard with clarity.

What Leonard Brings at 35

The honest assessment of what Kawhi Leonard offers at 35 comes with appropriate caveats. His injury history is real, and the expectation of a full 82-game season from him is not grounded in recent evidence. But when Leonard is healthy and engaged — which has been true in stretches even during his Clippers years — he remains one of the most effective two-way players in the league. His footwork in the post, his defensive instincts, and his ability to create shots in the half-court translate across age.

Toronto does not need him to replicate 2019. They need him to be the identity and the veteran anchor around which their current players can grow.

What's Next

The Raptors will now build out their roster around Leonard with the salary cap certainty they lacked all summer. Training camp opens in late September, and the question of how to surround a 35-year-old Leonard with the right supporting cast will occupy Toronto's front office through the preseason.

For the fanbase, the math is simpler. The player who gave them their greatest basketball moment is coming back. The saga is over. Kawhi Leonard is a Raptor again, and Toronto is ready to celebrate.
