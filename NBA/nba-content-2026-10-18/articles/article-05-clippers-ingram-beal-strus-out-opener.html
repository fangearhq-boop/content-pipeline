Clippers Opener Is a Mess: Ingram, Beal, Strus, and Konan All Confirmed Out

By Marcus Cole October 18, 2026

Tyronn Lue Confirms the Bad News

Head coach Tyronn Lue made it official: the Los Angeles Clippers will open their season on October 21 against the Sacramento Kings without four rotation players. Brandon Ingram, Bradley Beal, Max Strus, and rookie Yanic Konan Niederhauser are all unavailable for the opener. Lue confirmed every designation in his media availability, giving no indication that any of the four are close to returning.

Of the four names, Beal's is the most concerning. The veteran shooting guard was signed this summer as a major contributor to the Clippers' plans. He is dealing with knee inflammation and has no timeline for return. That phrase — "no timeline" — is the kind of phrasing NBA teams use when they genuinely don't know how long a situation will last. At 33 years old, Beal's availability for any sustained stretch this season is now a real question.

Who Fills the Gaps

Cameron Christie, who played on a two-way contract last season, is expected to see meaningful rotation minutes from the jump. Christie is 22 years old and showed enough in limited opportunities that the Clippers kept him around. He will need to deliver in a more prominent role earlier than anyone anticipated. Babi Miller is expected to start at center, an assignment that comes by necessity rather than matchup preference against Sacramento's frontcourt.

Konan Niederhauser, the 7-foot-1 rookie from Switzerland, suffers a foot injury that keeps him sidelined while he was already expected to ease into NBA life at his own pace. The Ingram situation is a continuation of health concerns that have limited him in previous seasons. Strus is the most straightforward roster loss, a floor-spacing wing whose absence reduces the Clippers' perimeter shooting depth.

Sacramento's Opportunity

The Kings get the best possible draw to start their season. Sacramento has been a middle-of-the-pack Western Conference team for the past several years, competitive enough to make the play-in but not deep enough to advance in the playoffs. Opening at home against a severely depleted Clippers team is exactly the kind of result that can set a positive tone for a long year.

For Los Angeles, the next few weeks will be about treading water until the injury list clears. The Clippers have enough talent in the building, even accounting for these absences, to be competitive over a full season. But first impressions matter, and opening the year 0-3 because key players are unavailable is not the foundation they were hoping to build on.

What's Next

Clippers-Kings tips off October 21 in Sacramento. Watch for updates on Beal's knee inflammation over the next week. The Clippers need him healthy, and his "no timeline" designation is the situation to monitor as the season gets underway.
