MRI Confirmed: Strawther Out Six Weeks as Nuggets Open Season Shorthanded

By Damon Pierce October 18, 2026

The Results Are In

Julian Strawther will not be part of Denver's Opening Night rotation. The MRI taken after he was injured in the third quarter of an October 14 preseason game against the Los Angeles Clippers confirmed a left knee sprain. No structural damage, no torn ligament. But the timeline is still painful: at least six weeks, with a re-evaluation expected around Thanksgiving in late November.

Strawther is 24 years old and had been developing into a reliable perimeter shooter for the Nuggets since being drafted in the second round in 2023. Over his first three seasons in Denver he established himself as a capable spot-up weapon, the kind of wing who makes life easier for Nikola Jokic and the pick-and-roll offense by occupying corner three-point territory. Losing him for five-plus weeks means those corner minutes go to someone else, and that someone else will not shoot at the same level.

How the Nuggets Adjust

The most likely candidates to absorb Strawther's wing minutes are Christian Braun and Peyton Watson. Braun, 24, has been with Denver since the 2022 draft and has experience in the rotation during playoff runs. Watson, 22, gives head coach David Adelman a longer, more athletically versatile option on the perimeter but is a work in progress offensively. Neither replicates what Strawther does from the corner, which means Denver's spacing takes a small hit during this stretch.

The broader context for Denver's season is that Jokic, Jamal Murray, and Aaron Gordon are all healthy and available for Opening Night. Losing Strawther is a real setback but not an existential one for a franchise that has learned to manage its roster through tight spots. The Nuggets have been playing catch-up with injury management for years under this core. They know how to redistribute minutes without panicking.

The Extension Question

There is a business layer to this situation that has not been fully resolved. Strawther was eligible for a rookie contract extension through October 19, the day after this report was published. It is unclear whether Denver and Strawther reached an agreement before the injury news became public. If they did not, the extension window closes Monday, and Strawther becomes a restricted free agent at the end of this season. An injury that sidelines him for the first six weeks of the year could complicate any remaining negotiations.

What's Next
