32 Lead Changes, Denver Wins: Nuggets Outlast Rockets 133-124 in Wild Thriller

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | November 21, 2026

Thursday night's matchup between the Denver Nuggets and Houston Rockets was the kind of game that basketball fans will be talking about for weeks. With 32 lead changes throughout the contest, the game swung back and forth like few regular-season games do, before Denver finally pulled away for a 133-124 victory. It was chaos, and it was magnificent.

What 32 Lead Changes Looks Like

Thirty-two lead changes is an extraordinary number. For context, most NBA games see the lead change hands a handful of times — perhaps eight to twelve times in a close contest. When a game reaches 32 lead changes, it means neither team could build a sustainable advantage for more than a few possessions at a time.

That's exactly what happened Thursday. Houston would go on a run, Denver would answer. Denver would push ahead, Houston would come back. Back and forth, back and forth, for more than three quarters of basketball, before the Nuggets finally found enough separation to close the game out.

Nikola Jokic was at the center of Denver's ability to manage the chaos. The three-time MVP doesn't panic in high-paced, high-scoring environments — he processes them. His vision and decision-making in transition, his ability to find open teammates when the defense collapses on him, and his efficient scoring make him the ideal fulcrum for a team that needed to stay composed when the game kept tilting back toward Houston.

Houston Shows Its Upside

The Rockets will be disappointed to lose, but Thursday's performance revealed something important about who they can be: a team dangerous enough to push Denver to the limit on the road. Houston's youth and athleticism gave Denver real problems throughout the night, and the fact that the Rockets stayed in this game — through 32 lead changes, on the road against a Jokic-led team — is a genuine statement about their ceiling.

The Rockets are not yet at Denver's level, but they demonstrated Thursday that they're capable of competing with Western Conference contenders in the kind of fast-paced game that suits their personnel. That's a foundation to build on.

Denver Digs In

For the Nuggets, this win was a stress test they passed. Coming out of a game that could have gone either way on 20 different possessions with a W is a measure of a team's resilience, and Denver showed they have it. Jokic's fingerprints were all over the decisive moments, as they usually are.

Denver remains in strong position in the Western Conference, and wins like this — ugly, earned, over a scrappy opponent on a night when nothing came easy — are often the ones that define playoff runs.

What's Next

The Nuggets will keep building on a win that showed their ability to close out games in chaotic environments. For Houston, the question is how they respond after coming so close: 124 points on the road in a 32-lead-change game against a Jokic team is not a failure. It is a proof of concept. The Rockets are closer to competing with the West's best than their record might suggest — and nights like Thursday are the evidence.
