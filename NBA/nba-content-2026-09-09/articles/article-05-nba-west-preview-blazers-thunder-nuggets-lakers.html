The West Has Its Own Storylines — and Portland's New Backcourt Is the Most Intriguing of All

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | September 9, 2026

While the East dominates the offseason narrative, the Western Conference is quietly building toward one of its most unpredictable seasons in years. Lillard and Morant headline a crowded, wide-open field.

The East is getting all the attention. LeBron in Philadelphia. Kawhi in Miami workouts. Giannis in a new uniform. The narrative gravity of the Eastern Conference has been impossible to escape all summer.

Meanwhile, the Western Conference has been quietly building one of the most unpredictable fields it has assembled in years. And the story that will define it — at least for the first two months of the season — is Portland.

The Experiment: Lillard and Morant

The Portland Trail Blazers spent the summer building something no one has seen before: Damian Lillard and Ja Morant in the same backcourt.

Lillard is back after missing most of the 2025-26 season with an Achilles injury. The team's medical staff has cleared him for full return, and his recovery timeline has reportedly gone smoothly through the summer. Morant arrived via trade — a two-time All-Star, one of the most explosive attacking guards in basketball when healthy and engaged.

Whether two perimeter-dominant guards can share the floor effectively at the highest level is the basketball question. The aesthetic question is answered: this backcourt is going to be electric to watch. Portland opens camp September 29. Their first international showcase — a game against BC London Lions on October 12, the first time an NBA team has played a British club — puts the new-look Blazers in front of a global audience within weeks of camp opening.

The Thunder's Continuing Rise

The Oklahoma City Thunder have been the West's young dynasty-in-waiting for two years. Shai Gilgeous-Alexander is one of the best players in basketball. The Thunder's core is deep, young, and getting a year older together — which in this case means more dangerous.

OKC has not yet won a championship with this group, but they have been a consistent playoff force and are annually the team Western Conference rivals circle on their schedule. Another year of experience for a team that has been this good this young is not a minor thing.

Jokic and Denver

Nikola Jokic changes every calculation on a basketball floor. The Denver Nuggets remain anchored by the most analytically dominant center in NBA history — a player whose combination of passing, scoring, and rebounding produces outcomes that don't look like traditional basketball. Denver won the 2023 NBA championship with this core and has remained a Western Conference fixture.

The question for Denver is whether the surrounding pieces have enough left to carry Jokic one more deep run — and whether Jokic himself, at this stage of his career, continues to operate at the historically anomalous level that has defined his prime.

The Lakers Without LeBron

For the first time since the 2017-18 season, the Los Angeles Lakers will open a training camp without LeBron James on their roster. He is in Philadelphia now. The franchise that defined itself around James' second Lakers tenure is figuring out what comes next.

The Lakers have young pieces and draft capital that gives them a path forward. But the transition from a franchise defined by an all-time great to whatever comes after is never seamless, and this organization has gone through that transition more than most. The 2026-27 season will tell the Lakers — and the West — what they are without him.

What's Next

Western Conference training camps open September 22 (overseas programs) and September 29 (all others). The West's defining early story — Portland's Lillard/Morant backcourt — gets its first real test in camp and preseason games beginning October 3.
