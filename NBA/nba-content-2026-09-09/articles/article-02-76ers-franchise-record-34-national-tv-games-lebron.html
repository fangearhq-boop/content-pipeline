The 76ers Will Play 34 National TV Games This Season — and That's the Best Proof of What LeBron James Still Means

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | September 9, 2026

Philadelphia will appear in a franchise-record 34 nationally televised games in 2026-27. One signing — at age 41 — turned the Sixers into the NBA's prime-time centerpiece.

You want to understand what LeBron James still represents to the NBA — not to his team, not to his fans, but to the league itself?

The Philadelphia 76ers will play in a franchise-record 34 nationally televised games in the 2026-27 season.

That number — 34 — tells you more about LeBron James at age 41 than anything else in this offseason. It is the league's official, televised, publicly scheduled bet that James, Joel Embiid, Jaylen Brown, and Tyrese Maxey are the most watchable basketball team in the country. Not a contender. Not a story. The show.

What Changed

Before LeBron James signed with Philadelphia this summer, the Sixers were a team that earned national television time when their opponents were interesting. Embiid versus Jokic drew eyeballs. Maxey in a playoff game was compelling. But the team itself was not a primary draw — it was the fourth option in a league that sells stars first, rosters second.

That calculus flipped the moment James signed. Not because he is the best player on the team — Joel Embiid, at full health, might still hold that distinction. Not because he is the youngest or most athletically dominant. But because LeBron James has been the most commercially significant player in the NBA since the mid-2000s, and at 41, in his 24th season, that does not appear to be changing.

The league knows what it has. Thirty-four games, nationally televised. Every major market will see the Sixers multiple times. Every network partnership benefits. The schedule was built for this team.

The Schedule Itself

The Sixers' schedule already carried the league's most attention-grabbing Opening Night: October 20 at Madison Square Garden on ring night for the defending champion New York Knicks. The team that swept Philadelphia in the 2026 second round will be raising its first championship banner in 53 years. LeBron James will be on the floor trying to take the energy out of the building.

Two days later, October 22, Philadelphia hosts the Cleveland Cavaliers at home for the first time — the home opener. Then October 28 at Miami, where Giannis Antetokounmpo waits. Then November games that take the Sixers through every major market in the country over and over, 34 times on national television, for an entire season.

LeBron's first preseason game is October 8 at Brooklyn — fans will get their first look at the new-look Sixers in organized game action before any of those regular-season matchups arrive.

What This Team Needs to Justify It

Thirty-four national TV games is a marketing bet as much as a basketball assessment. The Sixers will need to deliver on what the schedule promises. That means Embiid needs to stay on the floor — the central uncertainty for a team that has watched its franchise center miss significant time in two consecutive seasons. It means LeBron needs to maintain the quality of play that earned him those game minutes in year 23. And it means Brown and Maxey need to function as the reliable, high-level contributors the team built around them to be.

If everything works, the 76ers are the most compelling team in basketball, playing 34 times where the whole country can watch. If Embiid's health slips, the national TV schedule becomes a very public showcase of a team fighting through familiar problems.

The NBA is betting on the former. So, apparently, is LeBron James.
