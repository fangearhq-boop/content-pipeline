NBA East Power Rankings: Where Every Contender Stands 19 Days Before Training Camp

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | September 9, 2026

The Eastern Conference enters the 2026-27 season with five genuine playoff contenders and three legitimate title threats. Here is where every team stands as camps prepare to open.

The Eastern Conference hasn't looked like this in a decade.

Three different franchises — New York, Philadelphia, Miami — have reasonable arguments for championship-level rosters. Toronto is waiting on Kawhi Leonard's trade to officially clear. Cleveland locked in its championship-run group at the last minute. And everyone else is figuring out how to compete in a conference that just got a lot more expensive.

Here is where the East stands with 19 days until training camps open.

No. 1 — New York Knicks

They are the defending champions. They swept the 76ers in the second round of the 2026 playoffs and defeated the team that went to the Finals. Jalen Brunson had the signature season of his career. Karl-Anthony Towns was the most important piece of New York's championship run. The Knicks added Andre Drummond to address the frontcourt depth that became an issue late in the playoffs.

The Knicks retained the core that won the franchise's first championship in 53 years. They are the favorites until someone proves otherwise — and that someone will have to take the crown away from them on a basketball court.

No. 2 — Philadelphia 76ers

If Joel Embiid plays 65 games, the 76ers might be the best team in basketball. That is the qualifier that has applied to Philadelphia for years, and it applies again in 2026-27 — this time with LeBron James, Jaylen Brown, and Tyrese Maxey alongside him.

The Sixers added more firepower than any team in the East this offseason. A franchise-record 34 national TV games reflect what the league thinks of this team. The margin between Philadelphia and New York might come down entirely to Embiid's availability.

No. 3 — Miami Heat

Giannis Antetokounmpo in Miami is the offseason's other blockbuster acquisition. Pat Riley's franchise has a documented history of unlocking star talent — LeBron himself proved that during the Heat's championship years. Giannis provides the elite interior presence Miami has never had in their system, and the Heat's coaching infrastructure is built to maximize players who work hard and compete on every possession.

Whether Giannis in Miami can reach the level he showed with Milwaukee depends on roster construction around him. But Miami is not a team to dismiss.

No. 4 — Toronto Raptors

The Raptors are an incomplete team on paper until Kawhi Leonard's trade is official — but Tuesday's photos of Leonard working out in Miami alongside Raptors teammates, combined with his "I hope so...we gonna win" comment, suggest that the outcome is a matter of when, not if. If the trade clears before September 28, Toronto arrives at training camp as one of the most interesting teams in the East.

The Raptors' young core built around Kawhi's expected arrival. If he is healthy and engaged — and the evidence from Miami suggests he is — this team will push for a top-four seed.

No. 5 — Cleveland Cavaliers

James Harden's contract is official as of Tuesday. Donovan Mitchell, Evan Mobley, and Jarrett Allen are all under contract. The team that reached the 2026 Eastern Conference Finals is back together, with continuity and experience in a conference that got harder.

The Cavs' ceiling depends on how much Mitchell can elevate in a potential second conference finals run, and whether Harden's age-37 season holds up over 82 games. Their floor is a playoff team. Their ceiling is a conference finals re-run with better odds of finishing the job.

The Rest

The Milwaukee Bucks are in transition after Giannis departed — a difficult rebuild for a franchise that built its identity around one of the greatest players of his generation. The Boston Celtics are rebuilding after trading Jaylen Brown to Philadelphia, breaking up a core that reached two NBA Finals. Indiana, Chicago, and Atlanta are building but not yet competing at the top tier.

The gap between the top five and the rest of the East is significant this season. The battle for playoff positioning will be fierce between seeds five through nine, but the conference's identity will be shaped by what happens at the top — and there has never been more talent concentrated in the East's upper tier than right now.
