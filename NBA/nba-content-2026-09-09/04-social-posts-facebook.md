### STORY 1: Kawhi Leonard in Miami

**FB Long-Form**
Window: 9:00 AM ET

Kawhi Leonard is officially putting his intentions on the court — literally.

Photos and video surfaced Tuesday showing Kawhi Leonard working out alongside Toronto Raptors players in Miami at an informal mini-camp. His trade from the LA Clippers hasn't been officially ratified by the NBA yet, which means Leonard is limited to individual workouts rather than participating in full team activities. But he's there. And when a bystander asked him in downtown Miami whether he's coming to Toronto, Kawhi responded with four words that Raptors fans have been waiting all summer to hear:

"I hope so...we gonna win."

The trade — Kawhi for Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, unprotected first-round picks in 2031 and 2033, second-round picks in 2030 and 2033, and a first-round pick swap in 2027 — has been agreed to since June 30. But the NBA's investigation into an alleged undisclosed sponsorship arrangement between Leonard and Clippers partner Aspiration has put the ratification on hold. Both Clippers owner Steve Ballmer and team president Lawrence Frank received suspensions as part of the investigation.

The Raptors open training camp September 28 in Quebec City — 19 days from now. Whether Kawhi is an official Raptor by then remains the open question. But if those photos from Miami tell you anything, it's that Leonard has already made his choice.

### STORY 2: 76ers Franchise-Record 34 National TV Games

**FB Long-Form**
Window: 11:00 AM ET

Here's your single best stat for understanding how much LeBron James changed the Philadelphia 76ers:

The Sixers will play in 34 nationally televised games in the 2026-27 NBA season — a franchise record.

Before this summer, Philadelphia was a team that earned national TV time when their opponents were interesting. Joel Embiid's health was always the asterisk; Tyrese Maxey was compelling but not yet a marquee national draw. The Sixers were appointment TV in Philadelphia, optional TV everywhere else.

That changes now. LeBron James at age 41, entering his 24th professional season, still moves the needle in ways no other player in the history of the sport can match. Add Jaylen Brown — a genuine star who won a championship with Boston before coming to Philly — and the 76ers become the most story-rich team the NBA has produced in years.

The schedule is designed accordingly. The Sixers open October 20 at Madison Square Garden on ring night against the defending champion Knicks — LeBron's 76ers debut against the team that swept them in last year's playoffs. Home opener is October 22 against the Cavaliers. LeBron's first preseason game is October 8 at Brooklyn.

Thirty-four national games. The league is not hiding what it thinks this team represents. Every week, in every market, people are going to have to decide how they feel about LeBron James in a Sixers jersey. And they are going to keep deciding, for an entire season, on national television.

### STORY 3: James Harden Contract Official

**FB Long-Form**
Window: 9:30 PM ET

Nineteen days. That's how long it took the Cleveland Cavaliers to officially close the James Harden deal.

Harden agreed in principle to a 3-year, $97 million contract after declining his $42.3 million player option in late August. Then came the scramble — cap maneuvering, paperwork, and the logistical complexity of signing a player to a near-hundred-million-dollar deal late in the offseason calendar. On Tuesday, it became official: James Harden is a Cleveland Cavalier, under contract, locked in for the next three years.

The deal is approximately $30 million per year with a player option in year three — a reasonable structure for a 37-year-old coming off a full season with Cleveland, where he helped the Cavaliers reach the Eastern Conference Finals before falling to the eventual champion New York Knicks. The Cavs' projected starting five — Harden, Donovan Mitchell, a wing, Evan Mobley, and Jarrett Allen — is one of the more balanced lineups in the East.

Now comes October 22. That's Cleveland's home opener — and it's against LeBron James, Joel Embiid, Jaylen Brown, and the Philadelphia 76ers. The NBA has a way of delivering the storylines without trying. James Harden, officially a Cavalier, officially opening at home versus the man whose arrival changed the Eastern Conference's entire structure.

### STORY 4: East Power Rankings

**FB Long-Form**
Window: 1:00 PM ET

Here's where every Eastern Conference team stands 19 days before training camps open:

**No. 1 — New York Knicks:** The defending champions return largely intact. Jalen Brunson, Karl-Anthony Towns, and a retooled roster that added Andre Drummond. They swept the Sixers in last year's second round. They're the bar everyone else is chasing.

**No. 2 — Philadelphia 76ers:** LeBron James, Joel Embiid, Jaylen Brown, Tyrese Maxey, and a franchise-record 34 national TV games. If Embiid stays healthy, this team is the most talented roster in basketball. If he doesn't — same story as every other year.

**No. 3 — Miami Heat:** Giannis Antetokounmpo joined Pat Riley's franchise in what was the offseason's other blockbuster deal. The Heat have a history of maximizing star talent. Giannis provides the elite interior presence they've never had.

**No. 4 — Toronto Raptors:** They're waiting on Kawhi. Tuesday's photos of him working out with Raptors in Miami suggest the intention is there. When the trade clears, Toronto becomes a serious playoff contender.

**No. 5 — Cleveland Cavaliers:** With Harden's deal officially closed and Donovan Mitchell, Evan Mobley, and Jarrett Allen in place, the Cavs are built for a second run at the conference finals.

The East hasn't looked this deep at the top in a long time. Five teams with legitimate playoff ambitions. Three with genuine title hopes. Twenty-nine days until Opening Night answers start arriving.

### STORY 5: West Preview

**FB Long-Form**
Window: 4:00 PM ET

While every NBA conversation centers on the Eastern Conference this offseason — LeBron, Kawhi, Giannis, Embiid, Brunson — the Western Conference has its own offseason story to tell.

The headline is Portland. The Trail Blazers spent the summer acquiring Ja Morant via trade and clearing Damian Lillard for return from his Achilles injury. Lillard and Morant in the same backcourt is not just an experiment — it is an event. Two of the most electric offensive players in the Western Conference, playing alongside each other for the first time, opening camp September 29. Their first real test is October 12 against BC London Lions in the first NBA-vs.-British-club game in league history.

Behind Portland, the Oklahoma City Thunder continue building around Shai Gilgeous-Alexander. The Thunder have been the West's young dynasty-in-waiting, and another year of experience for their core makes them dangerous. The Denver Nuggets remain anchored by Nikola Jokic, whose ability to change every calculation on the floor hasn't diminished.

Then there are the Los Angeles Lakers — facing their first season without LeBron James since 2018. Whatever the Lakers are now, they are finding out without the man who has defined the franchise's modern identity.

The West won't dominate the conversation this fall. But when basketball starts, it might deliver the season's most compelling subplot: whether Lillard and Morant can turn Portland into a genuine contender.
