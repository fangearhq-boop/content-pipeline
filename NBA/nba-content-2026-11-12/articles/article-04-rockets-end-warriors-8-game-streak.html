Houston Ends Golden State's 8-Game Winning Streak, 115-108

By Jake Torres | November 12, 2026

The Houston Rockets defeated the Golden State Warriors 115-108 on Wednesday night, snapping Golden State's eight-game winning streak behind a 20-point effort from Kevin Durant and a balanced team performance that kept Houston in control for most of the night.

Houston Had an Answer for Everything

Golden State's eight-game winning streak was the longest active run in the NBA entering Wednesday night. The Warriors had been playing beautiful basketball — moving the ball, defending, and finding the right shot at the right moment. Houston was not impressed.

Kevin Durant, in his first full season with the Rockets after the offseason trade, delivered exactly what the organization brought him in to provide: a go-to scorer who can impose his will in the most important stretches of a game. His 20 points came efficiently, and his ability to draw fouls and keep the defense honest opened spacing for teammates throughout the night.

Houston's 115-108 victory was not a fluke. The Rockets controlled the pace, executed their offense, and defended well enough to keep Golden State from ever truly taking over.

What This Means for Golden State

The Warriors' streak is over, but eight consecutive wins in November is a remarkable achievement that should not be overlooked. Golden State has the talent and depth to reload quickly, and one loss to a quality Rockets team is not cause for concern in San Francisco.

What it does mean is that the Western Conference pecking order remains unsettled. The Los Angeles Lakers sit at a league-best 11-2, and the Spurs are on a 10-game winning streak of their own. The Warriors, now one game removed from their streak, will need to find their rhythm again against a loaded set of upcoming opponents.

Houston as a Western Conference Force

The Rockets are building something real. With Durant leading the charge, Houston has the kind of player who can carry a franchise in big moments. Wednesday's win over the previously streaking Warriors was a résumé-building result — the kind that gets circled on future playoff seeding discussions.

Houston's ability to slow the game down, control the tempo, and execute when it mattered most was the story of Wednesday night. These are the habits of teams that do real damage in April and May.

What's Next

Golden State will look to respond quickly, and the Warriors have enough proven talent to bounce back without a prolonged slump. The more interesting narrative may be Houston's trajectory: how far can the Rockets go this season with Durant as their centerpiece?

Wednesday's win over Golden State was a statement. The Western Conference is on notice that the Rockets are not satisfied being a good team — they want to be great. And on nights like Wednesday, they are.
