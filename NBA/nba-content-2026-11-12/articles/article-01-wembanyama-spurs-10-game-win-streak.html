Wembanyama Drops 32 as Spurs Overcome 22-Point Deficit for 10th Straight Win

By Jake Torres | November 12, 2026

Victor Wembanyama scored 32 points and San Antonio rallied from 22 points down to defeat the Chicago Bulls 124-119 on Wednesday night, extending the Spurs' winning streak to 10 games and cementing their status as one of the NBA's most dangerous teams.

A Comeback That Defied the Deficit

Wednesday night at Chicago looked bleak for the San Antonio Spurs. Down 22 points, facing a Bulls team that had played with energy and precision, the easy call would have been to live to fight another night. Victor Wembanyama and the Spurs had a different idea.

Wembanyama took over. His 32-point performance was a mix of dominant interior scoring, mid-range precision, and the kind of defensive anchor work that changes possessions without touching the box score. Piece by piece, San Antonio cut into the deficit, shifted the momentum, and ultimately finished the Bulls 124-119 to run their winning streak to 10 consecutive games.

No other active streak in the NBA comes close.

Wembanyama's October and November Have Been Generational

Context matters here. Wembanyama entered this week having already scored a career-high 52 points against Dallas on November 4 — the game that pushed the Spurs' winning streak to six. Since that night, San Antonio has not lost. And Wembanyama has not looked anything other than elite.

At just 22 years old, the French center has evolved into the kind of player who takes over games not just with offense, but with an intelligent understanding of when to impose himself and when to let teammates operate. Wednesday's 32-point effort was the latest example of that maturity under fire.

San Antonio's coaching staff has been careful not to overload him. That care is paying dividends: Wembanyama is healthy, fresh, and playing his best basketball at the right time of the season.

What 10 Straight Means for the Western Conference

The Spurs entered Wednesday's game at 9-3. With the win, they moved to 10-3 — one of the best records in the entire league. The Western Conference was supposed to be a tightly packed race, and in many ways it still is. But San Antonio has separated themselves with this streak.

Ten straight wins means the Spurs have beaten different kinds of teams, in different circumstances, on different nights. This is not a team hot-shooting their way through a soft schedule. This is a squad that has figured out how to compete, how to close, and how to win ugly when necessary.

Wednesday's comeback was the clearest proof yet. When the Bulls built a 22-point lead, most teams would have absorbed the loss and moved on. The Spurs treated it as a problem to solve.

What's Next

San Antonio will face a Western Conference road test over the coming days, with opponents who will be very aware of the streak and highly motivated to end it. The Spurs, however, have shown this season that they are built for exactly that kind of challenge.

Victor Wembanyama is playing like the best player on the planet right now. If San Antonio can sustain that level of play — and there is every reason to believe they can — the Western Conference is going to hear a great deal more from this team between now and the spring.
