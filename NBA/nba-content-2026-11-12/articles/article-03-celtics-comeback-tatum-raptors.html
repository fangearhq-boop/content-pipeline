Tatum's Clutch Free Throws Seal 22-Point Comeback as Celtics Down Raptors 98-93

By Damon Pierce | November 12, 2026

Jayson Tatum hit the tying and go-ahead free throws with 25.2 seconds remaining as the Boston Celtics completed a stunning 22-point comeback to defeat the Toronto Raptors 98-93 on Wednesday, extending their winning streak to five games.

From 22 Down to One More Win

The Boston Celtics had no business winning Wednesday night's game in Toronto. Down 22 points at some point during regulation, facing a Raptors team that was playing with discipline and precision on its home floor, Boston looked beaten. The Celtics, though, have shown a stubborn refusal to accept those circumstances this season.

They chipped away. Quarter by quarter, possession by possession, the deficit narrowed. By the time the final minute arrived, Boston had turned a blowout into a one-possession game. And Jayson Tatum, with 25.2 seconds remaining, stepped to the free-throw line and converted both shots — first the tying basket, then the go-ahead opportunity — to complete one of the most dramatic comebacks of the young NBA season.

Final: Celtics 98, Raptors 93. Five-game winning streak for Boston.

Tatum in the Clutch

Free throws in the closing seconds of a road comeback are a test of composure, and Jayson Tatum passed it. His ability to stay focused in high-pressure moments has long been a defining characteristic of his game, and Wednesday's sequence was a textbook example of why Boston trusts him with the ball when the game is on the line.

Neither free throw went directly in without some drama — the moment itself was intense — but Tatum made both, and that is the only result that matters. Boston needed a player to step up and take ownership of the final minute. He did.

The Comeback That Says Something

Five straight wins is a meaningful stretch of basketball, but the how matters as much as the results. The Celtics have won games this week that less resilient teams would have let slip away. The comeback in Toronto — from 22 points down — is not something that happens by accident. It requires sustained effort, belief, and the defensive intensity to hold an opponent scoreless long enough for the offense to work.

Boston did all of that on Wednesday. The Raptors were not poor — they played a strong game for three-plus quarters. Boston just refused to let that be the final story.

What's Next

The Celtics return to the TD Garden with a five-game winning streak and a growing sense that this team is built for the long haul. The Eastern Conference will not get easier, and Boston will face more difficult tests in the weeks ahead. But Wednesday's comeback in Toronto is exactly the kind of win that builds character, confidence, and belief.

When a team rallies from 22 down on the road and finds a way to win in the final 30 seconds, it tells the rest of the conference something: these Celtics are not going away.
