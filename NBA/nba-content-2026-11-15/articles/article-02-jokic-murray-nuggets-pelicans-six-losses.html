Jokic 26/10, Murray 23/9 Lead Nuggets Past Pelicans as New Orleans' Losing Streak Hits Six

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | November 15, 2026

Nikola Jokic posted 26 points, 10 assists, and 8 rebounds as the Denver Nuggets defeated the New Orleans Pelicans 116-108 on Saturday night, handing the host team its sixth consecutive loss. DeJounte Murray's trade request looms over a Pelicans franchise in visible freefall.

Some nights, the Nuggets win because Nikola Jokic is the best basketball player on the floor. Some nights, they win because Jamal Murray is locked in. On Saturday in New Orleans, they won because both men were operating in concert — 26 points and 10 assists for the three-time MVP, 23 points and 9 assists for his backcourt partner — and the Pelicans simply had no answer for either of them.

The final score was 116-108. New Orleans has now lost six consecutive games. The Pelicans are 4-10. And the disarray surrounding this franchise is extending well beyond the box score.

Jokic and Murray — The NBA's Most Durable Partnership

Jokic's stat line — 26 points, 8 rebounds, 10 assists — lands with the matter-of-fact efficiency that defines everything he does. He produced a triple-double's worth of numbers without ever seeming to force anything. Murray, shooting 9-of-16 from the field and posting a team-high plus-minus of plus-16, was the perfect running mate.

Christian Braun added 17 points, 7 rebounds, and 4 steals off the bench, and DeMar DeRozan's spinning mid-range jumper pushed the Denver lead to 102-87 with 6:36 remaining — the game-sealing moment that ended any drama. Denver built the lead methodically, jumping out 19-11 early and controlling the tempo for the entire second half.

The Nuggets improved to 7-4 and look increasingly like the Western Conference contender many expected them to be when training camp opened.

Chaos in the Crescent City

Zion Williamson led the Pelicans with 24 points, and the effort was visible. But Zion's production only highlights how isolated it is from the team's larger struggles. Saddiq Bey shot 4-of-15 from the field. DeJounte Murray — who was absent with an ankle injury — made headlines Friday by formally requesting a trade from the organization, adding an unmistakable layer of dysfunction to an already-difficult stretch.

When a player of Murray's caliber publicly asks out during a losing streak, it signals that the problems in New Orleans go beyond the injury list. At 4-10 with no apparent momentum, the Pelicans face serious questions about roster construction, coaching, and front-office direction heading into the meat of the November schedule.

What's Next

The Nuggets head home having completed a road trip win, and at 7-4, they are firmly in the Western Conference conversation. For Denver, the goal remains what it always is: stay healthy, stay together, and let Jokic be Jokic. The formula works.

For New Orleans, the immediate need is clarity — on Murray's trade situation, on Zion's supporting cast, on whether this front office believes the current roster can compete. At 4-10 with six straight losses, the window for a mid-season correction is closing. Answers are needed before this season becomes something to simply survive.
