Pelicans Hit 4-10 as Six-Game Skid Deepens — DeJounte Murray's Trade Request Signals Front-Office Crisis

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | November 15, 2026

The New Orleans Pelicans lost their sixth consecutive game Saturday, falling 116-108 to Denver in a defeat compounded by DeJounte Murray's trade request. At 4-10, the franchise faces a crossroads that demands immediate and clear-eyed decision-making.

Saturday's 116-108 loss to the Denver Nuggets was the sixth consecutive defeat for the New Orleans Pelicans. The numbers are stark: 4-10, bottom-third of the league, losing streak now nearly two weeks old. And then, on Friday — before anyone had even tipped off — DeJounte Murray made the loudest off-court statement of the early season by formally requesting a trade.

When a player of Murray's caliber asks out during a losing streak, it is rarely about one game or one month. It is a signal about trust, direction, and institutional confidence in the organization's ability to build something worth fighting for. That signal is not encouraging for New Orleans right now.

Zion Scoring, Team Losing

Zion Williamson led the Pelicans with 24 points on Saturday. The frustrating reality of Zion's situation in New Orleans is that his individual contributions are not the problem. He has shown up, produced, and given this team a genuine star presence on most nights this season. The issue is everything surrounding him.

Saddiq Bey shot 4-of-15 from the field. Murray wasn't available. The bench provided no consistent secondary option to give Denver's defense a second problem to solve. When your supporting cast cannot give 50 percent shooting nights against a team that has absorbed significant losses of its own, the roster construction conversation becomes unavoidable.

The Front Office's Moment

New Orleans faces a fundamental decision about its direction. The core of Zion Williamson, surrounded by a roster that has underperformed consistently, is a template that is not working. Murray's trade request, if granted, would reshape the team's identity entirely and open the door for a rebuild around Zion with new pieces. If denied or delayed, it creates a locker room dynamic that rarely produces winning basketball.

The organization needs to respond decisively. Letting this situation drift into late November and December without clarity would compound the damage already done by six consecutive losses and the public dysfunction of a trade request.

What's Next

For the Pelicans, the immediate task is stopping the losing streak before it becomes a franchise-defining wound. That requires getting Murray's situation resolved — one way or another — and finding the kind of defensive cohesion and offensive consistency that has eluded this team through six weeks of the season.

Zion's potential in New Orleans remains enormous. He is the kind of talent that organizations build around for a decade. The question is whether this front office has the vision, the capital, and the decisiveness to build the right roster around him before his window begins to close. The answers need to come faster than this season has been moving.
