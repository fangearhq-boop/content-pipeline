Tyrese Proctor Delivers First Career Triple-Double as Short-Handed Cavaliers Stun Mavericks 127-117

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | November 15, 2026

Tyrese Proctor posted his first career triple-double — 24 points, 11 rebounds, and 10 assists — to lead a depleted Cleveland Cavaliers squad past the Dallas Mavericks 127-117 on Saturday, with the Cavs missing seven rotation players including Donovan Mitchell and James Harden.

When the injury report came out for Saturday night's game in Cleveland, the Mavericks must have liked what they saw. Donovan Mitchell — out. James Harden — out. Jarrett Allen — out. Peyton Watson — out. Evan Mobley — out. Sam Merrill — out. Nae'Qwan Tomlin — out. Seven rotation players, their starting center, and their two best offensive players, all unavailable.

Tyrese Proctor had something to say about that.

The Cavaliers guard delivered 24 points, 11 rebounds, and 10 assists — his first career triple-double — as Cleveland pulled off a 127-117 victory that left Dallas shaking its head and scrambling for answers. The Cavs are 7-7. They just beat a full-strength Mavericks team on a night they had no business winning.

Proctor's Coming-Out Night

Proctor has shown flashes all season of the playmaking potential that made him an intriguing prospect, but Saturday was different. This was a 48-minute statement — running the offense from the point guard spot, scoring from multiple spots, crashing the glass in ways that guards typically don't, and distributing with the precision of a veteran point guard who has seen the same coverages a hundred times.

The triple-double is the headline number, but the margins tell the deeper story. Cleveland built a lead of 22 points in the first half. Dallas mounted an 11-0 run in the third quarter to cut it to a manageable margin. Then Proctor and his teammates closed the door. The final ten-point margin doesn't fully capture how thoroughly Cleveland controlled this game.

Mario Hezonja scored 22 points. Meleek Thomas added 20. This was not a Proctor solo act — it was an entire second unit stepping into the breach and refusing to fold.

Dallas Can't Find Its Footing

Kyrie Irving's 34-point performance was everything Dallas needed from their star guard — and it still wasn't enough. Cooper Flagg, who had a career-high 43 points earlier this month, went 6-of-19 from the field for 18 points. The good and the bad of Flagg's rookie season compressed into a single game: flashes of brilliance, stretches of inefficiency, the learning curve of an elite prospect figuring out NBA defenses for the first time.

Dallas falls to 7-6. The Mavericks have dropped two straight games. When the most alarming part of your loss is that your opponent beat you while missing seven key players, the warning lights are blinking.

What's Next

For Cleveland, Proctor's triple-double performance raises a genuinely interesting question: when Mitchell and Harden return, does Proctor maintain enough minutes to stay in rhythm? The Cavs have discovered something — a backup guard who can run a lineup and produce at an elite level when given extended opportunities. That is a valuable asset as the schedule deepens.

For Dallas, the conversation shifts to Flagg's consistency and whether the Mavericks can build an offense that doesn't put Kyrie in the position of carrying the entire load. When Kyrie goes for 34 and the team loses, it is the supporting cast that needs to look in the mirror.
