Giannis Drops 34, Klay Returns With 26 as Heat Rout Pistons 137-117 for Fourth Straight Win

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | November 15, 2026

Giannis Antetokounmpo posted 34 points, 14 rebounds, and 8 assists while Klay Thompson returned from a sprained ankle to drain six three-pointers as the Miami Heat dominated the Detroit Pistons 137-117 on Saturday, moving to 7-6 with their fourth consecutive win.

Saturday night at Kaseya Center was a glimpse of everything the Miami Heat envisioned when they reshaped their roster around Giannis Antetokounmpo. The Greek Freak delivered 34 points, 14 rebounds, and 8 assists on a staggering 13-of-17 from the field. Beside him, Klay Thompson shook off a sprained ankle and promptly lit up the Pistons for 26 points and six three-pointers in a 137-117 victory that wasn't particularly close.

Four straight wins. Miami is 7-6 and above .500 for the first time all season.

Giannis Continues to Dominate

The numbers on paper are striking enough — 34-14-8 on 76 percent shooting — but they don't fully capture the effortless authority with which Antetokounmpo controlled this game. Detroit led 39-37 after the first quarter, a moment that could have signaled a competitive night. Miami responded by outscoring the Pistons 31-21 in the second quarter and 35-26 in the third, and by the time the fourth quarter began, the game had long been decided.

Giannis added a steal and three blocks to his line, punctuating a defensive performance that matched his offensive one. For a team that entered the season with questions about whether Giannis could carry a Heat identity built on defensive excellence, those questions are getting answered emphatically.

Klay Thompson's Statement Return

If Giannis was the foundation, Klay Thompson was the exclamation point. His return from a sprained ankle produced six three-pointers and 26 points — the kind of output that reminded everyone exactly what this Heat roster looks like at full capacity. Bam Adebayo added 20 points, and Miami's supporting cast maintained the defensive intensity that has defined this winning streak.

The Heat blocked 14 shots and recorded 10 steals on the night — a season-high in blocks that speaks to a defensive identity that is becoming Miami's calling card in this stretch run. When a team can get this kind of defensive performance alongside a 34-point night from Giannis and a comeback performance from Klay, it is easy to see why the Eastern Conference is paying attention.

Cunningham Fights, But Detroit Falls

None of this takes away from what Cade Cunningham did for Detroit. His 33 points and 8 assists were a reminder of how good the Pistons are becoming as an offense. Kevin Huerter came off the bench for 20 points and pushed the game competitively through three quarters. Detroit is 8-4 — a genuinely impressive record — and Cunningham's continued development makes the Pistons a team to monitor as the season progresses.

Duncan Robinson's departure with a thigh injury adds an injury concern to watch. If significant, it would be a meaningful blow to Detroit's depth over the coming weeks.

What's Next

The Heat at 7-6 are no longer a team treading water — they are a team trending upward with their most dangerous players healthy and in rhythm. The question now is whether Miami can sustain this level through the season's first real test: a stretch of games in which opponents will have film on this starting lineup and will adjust accordingly.

For the Pistons, the message is simpler: Cunningham at 33 on a losing night means Detroit has the offensive firepower. The question is whether the defense, the depth, and an injury-free roster can hold together long enough to build something meaningful in the East.
