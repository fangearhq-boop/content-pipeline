Kyrie's 34 Points Can't Save Dallas: Mavericks Lose to Injury-Ravaged Cavaliers as Flagg Goes 6-for-19

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | November 15, 2026

Kyrie Irving's 34-point effort went to waste Saturday as the Dallas Mavericks dropped a 127-117 decision to a Cleveland Cavaliers team missing seven rotation players — a result that exposes the gap between Dallas's star power and its depth.

At some point, the Mavericks have to reckon with what Saturday night revealed. Kyrie Irving scored 34 points. Dallas still lost — by 10, to a Cleveland team playing without Donovan Mitchell, James Harden, Jarrett Allen, and four other rotation contributors. It is the kind of result that sits uncomfortably in the locker room and demands honest self-assessment from a franchise that entered this season with legitimate championship expectations.

Dallas falls to 7-6. Two straight losses. The warning signs are accumulating.

Flagg's Rough Night Raises Questions

Cooper Flagg went 6-of-19 from the field for 18 points. That follows a November where he had a 43-point career high against one opponent, then struggles like these against another. The inconsistency is expected from a rookie — but the degree of the swing, and the fact that Saturday's poor shooting came against a battered opponent, is cause for concern.

Flagg is not the problem in Dallas. He is a generational talent and his ceiling is enormous. But right now, the Mavericks are asking him to be their No. 2 scorer on most nights, and he is not yet ready to consistently fill that role against NBA-caliber defenses. Cleveland's backup-heavy lineup made no particular adjustment to stop him — Flagg made eight turnovers' worth of decisions with his shot selection on a night when the Mavericks needed him most.

The Kyrie Problem Is Not Kyrie

It is unfair to frame Irving negatively after a 34-point performance. That is what elite guards do. The issue is not Kyrie's production — it is that Dallas has no guaranteed third option to take pressure off him when Flagg struggles. On nights like Saturday, when Flagg goes cold and the bench provides little resistance to a depleted opponent's backup unit, Kyrie's points become noise rather than a foundation.

The Mavericks need to find that third piece — whether through roster moves, through the development of young players who have yet to emerge, or through Flagg's own maturation. Until that piece arrives, Dallas will continue to have nights like this.

What's Next

At 7-6, Dallas is not in crisis. The Western Conference is competitive enough that two losses do not define a season. But the Mavericks need to use this result as motivation for a corrective response. Kyrie Irving and Cooper Flagg have enough combined talent to make Dallas a genuine Western Conference contender. The question is whether the supporting infrastructure will catch up before the season gets away from them.

The answers need to come soon. November's remaining slate will tell a great deal about what this Mavericks team actually is.
