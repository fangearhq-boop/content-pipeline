53-Year Wait Is Over: Knicks Lock In Core for Championship Defense, Banner Goes Up October 20

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | August 1, 2026

The New York Knicks ended 53 years of waiting when they beat the San Antonio Spurs 4-1 in the 2026 NBA Finals — with Jalen Brunson scoring 45 points in a 94-90 Game 5 clincher to win the Bill Russell Finals MVP Award. New York's first championship since 1973 was followed, in the weeks since, by a methodical and strategic offseason focused on one objective: do it again.

The Knicks have now largely completed their offseason, and the core is intact. The five-man foundation of Brunson, Josh Hart, OG Anunoby, Mikal Bridges, and Karl-Anthony Towns is under contract for at least two more seasons. The team re-signed Jordan Clarkson, Jose Alvarado, Landry Shamet, and Mohamed Diawara to keep the depth that carried them through the playoffs. They added center Andre Drummond for rebounding depth.

The one meaningful departure: Mitchell Robinson signed with the Boston Celtics.

Why the Core Works

The 2026 Knicks championship was built on a specific identity — physicality, defense, and Jalen Brunson's late-clock genius. Every roster decision this offseason has been made to preserve and extend that identity rather than chase something different.

Clarkson brings bench scoring depth, having averaged over 15 points per game in his best seasons. Alvarado is one of the best perimeter defenders in the league off the bench. Shamet provides the floor spacing that allows Towns and Embiid-level bigs to operate. Drummond — a career rebounder who brings energy and frontcourt depth — fills the hole Robinson leaves.

The Knicks' front office understood something that championship-winning organizations have historically understood: when you build a system that wins, you do not dismantle it. You reinforce it.

The Eastern Conference Around Them

Keeping the title is harder now than winning it was. The Philadelphia 76ers added LeBron James to a roster already featuring Joel Embiid and Jaylen Brown. The Miami Heat added Giannis Antetokounmpo in a blockbuster trade. The Eastern Conference is legitimately deeper than it was when the Knicks lifted the trophy in June.

But the Knicks were not a surprise champion. They were built deliberately, they were tested in a long playoff run, and their best players — Brunson most of all — rose in the biggest moments. That kind of proven performance is more reliable than projected potential, and New York enters the 2026-27 season as one of the legitimate title contenders despite the landscape getting more competitive around them.

What's Next

On October 20, Madison Square Garden will host the first game of the 2026-27 NBA season with a championship banner raising that New York has waited a long time to see. The 2026-27 schedule releases in mid-August, and when it does, every matchup involving the Knicks will carry the weight of the defending champions.

The Knicks won it once. Repeating — the thing no team has done since the Golden State Warriors in 2018 — is the goal now. Draft picks Kayil and Nickel add depth at the margins. The core drives everything else. So far, the recipe looks right.
