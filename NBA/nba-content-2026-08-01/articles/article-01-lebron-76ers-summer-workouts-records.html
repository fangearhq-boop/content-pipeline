LeBron James Already Working Out With Maxey as 76ers Mini Camps Loom — and History Awaits

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | August 1, 2026

LeBron James hasn't played a single game in a Philadelphia 76ers uniform yet, and he is already putting in work with his new teammates. The 41-year-old forward is in Los Angeles working out with All-Star guard Tyrese Maxey — unprompted, unscheduled, just the kind of self-driven preparation that has defined two-plus decades of greatness.

The 76ers signed LeBron to a two-year, $7.94 million deal on July 26, pairing him with Joel Embiid and Jaylen Brown in what is immediately one of the most intriguing rosters in recent memory. The official announcement came July 27. No introductory press conference was planned — LeBron hasn't held one since his 2010 signing with the Miami Heat, and that tradition is holding in Philadelphia. His first formal Sixers event will be Media Day, roughly eight weeks away.

But the unofficial work has already begun. And when mini camps arrive in August, LeBron will show up having already built a connection with Maxey — the kind of head start that matters when the goal is an NBA championship.

Records Within Reach in Year 24

LeBron James enters his 24th NBA season in one of the most record-rich positions of his career. As of today, he holds 672 career 30-point games — one more than Michael Jordan's 671. A single signature performance in a Sixers jersey puts him alone at the top of that list for good.

Beyond the 30-point game record, LeBron has averaged at least 20 points per game in every NBA season from his rookie year through his 41-year-old campaign last season. A 20-point average in Philadelphia would extend that to 24 consecutive seasons — a mark that would stand alone in league history.

The largest record is also the most context-dependent. If Philadelphia wins the NBA title in 2026-27, LeBron James would become the first player in history to win championships with four different franchises. He already has rings with the Cleveland Cavaliers (2016), Miami Heat (2012, 2013), and Los Angeles Lakers (2020). A fourth with the Sixers would be unprecedented.

LeBron called the decision to come to Philadelphia his "last decision" and said he came close to choosing retirement before committing to another run. That framing makes everything that follows — the mini camps, the first preseason game, the opening night in October — carry extra weight.

The Core Around Him

The projected Philadelphia starting five heading into training camp: LeBron James, Joel Embiid, Jaylen Brown, Tyrese Maxey, and VJ Edgecombe. Brown arrived in early July via a blockbuster trade from Boston — the Celtics received Paul George and two first-round picks. Brown, a 2024 Finals MVP with Boston, brings five All-Star selections and proven championship experience to a roster that didn't need more star power but gained it anyway.

Embiid, for his part, is entering his first offseason without a major surgical procedure in several years. That alone changes the calculus for Philadelphia heading into October. A healthy Embiid alongside LeBron James is a combination the Eastern Conference has not faced before.

Head coach Nick Nurse was the only coach LeBron spoke with during the three-week recruitment process. That level of trust in a coaching staff matters when a player describes a move as his last.

What's Next

76ers mini camps are expected in August. Media Day follows roughly six to eight weeks after LeBron's signing announcement. The 2026-27 NBA schedule is expected to release in mid-August, with opening night set for October 20 — when the New York Knicks will raise their 2026 championship banner at Madison Square Garden.

Philadelphia's first regular-season game, whenever and wherever it lands on that schedule, will be one of the most-watched games of the opening week. LeBron James in a new city, chasing records, chasing history, and — according to the man himself — chasing one final title. The work has already started in Los Angeles.
