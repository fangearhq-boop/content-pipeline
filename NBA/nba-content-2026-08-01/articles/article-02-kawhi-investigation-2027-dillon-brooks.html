Kawhi Leonard Trade Saga Grows Messier: Investigation May Drag to 2027 as Dillon Brooks Says Deal Won't Happen

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | August 1, 2026

The NBA's investigation into Kawhi Leonard and the Los Angeles Clippers is not getting simpler. If anything, it is becoming one of the most legally complicated situations in modern league history — and as of this week, sources told ESPN the inquiry could now stretch well into 2027.

The outside law firm hired by the NBA, Wachtell Lipton, has completed its fact-finding work. The league is now reviewing those findings and determining what, if any, discipline to pursue. But that review process — and the likely resistance from the National Basketball Players Association — is where the timeline begins to unravel.

What the Investigation Is About

At the center of the probe is a $28 million endorsement deal Kawhi Leonard signed with Aspiration, a green-banking company that has since gone bankrupt. The NBA is examining whether the Clippers, whose owner Steve Ballmer invested $50 million in Aspiration, used that deal to compensate Leonard outside his official contract and circumvent salary-cap rules.

The Clippers and Ballmer have denied wrongdoing. Ballmer has said he was "duped" and that his investment in Aspiration was unrelated to any arrangement with Leonard. But an anonymous Aspiration employee told the NBA that the arrangement was designed specifically to get around cap rules, which triggered the formal investigation roughly a year ago.

Potential penalties, if the NBA finds violations, include: a lengthy player suspension, partial or full voiding of Leonard's contract, fines, and loss of draft picks. Leonard's 2026-27 salary sits at $50.3 million — a significant figure both for what it represents and for what it complicates.

The NBPA Will Fight

The National Basketball Players Association has made its position clear: if the league pursues punishment without sufficient evidence, the union will not accept the ruling. It will pursue formal arbitration — a process that could extend the timeline indefinitely and delay any resolution until well into 2027.

That threat carries weight. Arbitration involves independent review, procedural timelines, and the kind of back-and-forth that rarely moves quickly. NBA commissioner Adam Silver said earlier this summer that he was "hopeful" the investigation would resolve before the new season. But hopeful and resolved are different things, and the NBPA's posture suggests the league may not get the clean ending it wants.

The Trade Is Still Frozen

In June, the Clippers and Toronto Raptors agreed to a trade that would send Leonard back to Toronto — where he won a championship in 2019 — in exchange for Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, and a package of future draft picks including unprotected 2031 and 2033 first-round picks.

That deal is on hold. The NBA informed Toronto that completing the trade would require the Raptors to accept in writing full financial and basketball liability for any penalties handed to Leonard. The Raptors declined. Both organizations chose to pause rather than proceed.

Now Clippers center Dillon Brooks has added his voice to the uncertainty. In a conversation with a streamer known as 'Neon,' Brooks said the Kawhi Leonard-to-Toronto trade simply will not happen. Brooks is not in the Clippers' front office, but his confidence in the statement caught attention and quickly circulated through NBA circles on social media.

What's Next

The NBA's Kawhi Leonard situation now has three possible tracks. The league and the NBPA could reach an agreement on findings and penalties before the season, allowing the trade to close and Leonard to begin his Toronto chapter. Alternatively, arbitration proceedings could push the entire matter into 2027, leaving a $50.3 million player in organizational limbo. Or the investigation could produce findings that fundamentally change the nature of the deal — voiding Leonard's contract, restarting trade talks from scratch, or producing outcomes no one has fully modeled.

Opening night is October 20. That deadline is now very much in question for Kawhi Leonard's immediate future.
