Zach LaVine Bets on Himself: Kings Guard Exercises $49M Player Option, Stays in Sacramento

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | August 1, 2026

Zach LaVine is not going anywhere. Agent Rich Paul confirmed that the Sacramento Kings guard has exercised his $49 million player option for the 2026-27 season, locking him in for another year with an organization that just this offseason parted ways with DeMar DeRozan to reshape its roster around the former All-Star.

It is, in its own way, a significant statement of intent. LaVine had the option to test the open market this summer — to see what teams would offer after a 2025-26 campaign that was interrupted by a hand injury. Instead, he is betting on himself to stay healthy, to perform, and to be worth more than $49 million when the time comes to negotiate again.

What LaVine Showed When Healthy

In the 39 games he played last season before the hand injury ended his year, LaVine averaged 19.2 points per game. Those are All-Star-caliber numbers — the kind of production that would earn serious contract offers on the open market if LaVine stayed healthy through a full season.

The problem, as has been the case in recent years, is the injuries. The hand surgery ended his season prematurely and was part of a pattern that has prevented LaVine from stringing together the sustained runs of production that defined his peak years in Chicago, where he made two All-Star teams and established himself as one of the league's most explosive scorers.

But at 31 years old, LaVine still has the athleticism, the shooting mechanics, and the offensive skill set to be a 20-point scorer when healthy. In Sacramento, he is the highest-profile guard on the roster after the departure of DeRozan, and the Kings are counting on him to be a cornerstone of their competitive future in the Western Conference.

Kings' Calculations

The Sacramento Kings have no plans to cut ties with LaVine, and that position makes sense given the alternatives. At $49 million, his contract represents a significant cap commitment, but releasing a healthy 31-year-old scorer in a thin wing market would gain the Kings short-term flexibility while losing them one of their most marketable and talented players.

The Western Conference is deep and competitive. The Kings need a star-caliber scorer to remain relevant in the playoff race, and LaVine, when healthy, provides exactly that. The question for Sacramento heading into training camp is not whether to keep LaVine — that question has been answered — but how to build the right pieces around him to maximize his production in 2026-27.

What's Next

LaVine's player option decision closes one chapter of Sacramento's offseason and opens another. The Kings still need to fill the void left by DeRozan's departure to Chicago and continue addressing roster depth around LaVine and De'Aaron Fox, who remains the franchise's foundational point guard.

Training camp in September will be the first real look at how the pieces fit together under the 2026-27 roster construction. If LaVine can stay healthy and approach 20 points per game again, Sacramento enters the season with a legitimate case for postseason relevance in a Western Conference that has shuffled significantly over the summer.
