DeMar DeRozan Returns to Chicago Bulls on Two-Year $30M Deal — Chose Home Over Multiple Rivals

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | August 1, 2026

DeMar DeRozan is back in Chicago. The six-time All-Star has officially signed a two-year, $30 million deal to return to the Chicago Bulls — the organization where he spent three of his most productive recent seasons before being shipped to Sacramento.

The Sacramento Kings waived DeRozan, making him an unrestricted free agent and kicking off a brief but competitive recruitment process. The Washington Wizards, Miami Heat, Denver Nuggets, and Cleveland Cavaliers all had conversations with DeRozan before he made his choice. He chose Chicago.

Why Chicago Again

DeRozan averaged 18.4 points per game last season with the Kings, but the Sacramento experiment never delivered what anyone hoped. The Kings missed the playoffs in a crowded Western Conference, and DeRozan — despite his individual production — was waived as the organization pivoted.

The return to Chicago represents both a professional reset and, by all accounts, a personal one. DeRozan's time as a Bull was defined by genuine connection — to the city, to the fanbase, and to an era when Chicago was rebuilding its basketball identity. The Bulls were his home, and he chose it again when he had options.

He will now do so under new head coach Torsten Splitter, the former Spurs big man who takes over a Chicago roster with legitimate questions about direction but now has a veteran leader to anchor the offense. DeRozan's mid-range game, his ability to get to the free throw line, and his locker room presence are exactly the kind of experience a young coaching staff wants at its disposal.

The Role He'll Play

DeRozan turned 37 this past August, and the two-year deal reflects both confidence in his durability and reasonable expectations for his ceiling. He is not being brought in to be the franchise centerpiece — that role has evolved in Chicago. He is being brought in to be a veteran scorer, a mentor, and a professional presence who has seen everything the league has to offer.

In his prime Chicago seasons, DeRozan averaged 26.9, 25.0, and 22.8 points per game across three campaigns — numbers that made him a legitimate All-Star vote getter and one of the most consistent mid-range scorers in the game. Those peak numbers may not return, but the skill set and competitive instinct that produced them are still very much present at 18.4 PPG in his most recent season.

What's Next

DeRozan's return gives the Bulls a known commodity around which Splitter can build his opening season in Chicago. Training camp in September will be the first look at how the new coaching staff integrates DeRozan's veteran skill set with the younger pieces on the roster. Opening night is October 20, and for Chicago fans who watched DeRozan thrive in a Bulls uniform, his return will be one of the more welcomed storylines of the early season.

He passed on Heat, Nuggets, Cavs, and Wizards to be here. DeRozan clearly had unfinished business in Chicago.
