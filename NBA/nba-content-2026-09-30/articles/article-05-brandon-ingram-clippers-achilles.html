Brandon Ingram Suffers Partially Torn Achilles: Clippers Dealt Brutal Blow

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | September 30, 2026

The Los Angeles Clippers had a plan heading into the 2026-27 season. Brandon Ingram was a big part of it. That plan now needs revising.

The Clippers announced at training camp media day that doctors discovered a partially torn Achilles tendon in Ingram's foot following recent heel surgery. He will miss the start of the 2026-27 season, the organization said, though no specific timetable was provided.

A Franchise That Knows Injury Pain

For longtime Clippers fans, this kind of news carries a particular weight. Los Angeles has watched talented rosters crumble at the hands of injury year after year. Kawhi Leonard's availability issues defined the franchise's most ambitious era. Paul George dealt with significant injuries during his Clippers tenure as well.

Now, as the organization attempts yet another rebuild toward championship contention, they're losing their newest key acquisition before the first preseason game is played. The Clippers simply cannot catch a break.

Understanding the Injury

A partial Achilles tear differs meaningfully from a complete rupture. A full tear typically requires surgery and a recovery measured in the better part of a year or longer. A partial tear — depending on its location, severity, and the player's body — can sometimes be managed with conservative treatment, meaning no surgery and a rehabilitation period of roughly six to ten weeks.

However, the Achilles tendon is notoriously difficult to predict. Even partial tears carry risk of full rupture if not handled carefully, which explains why the Clippers are being cautious with their initial timeline. Any organization facing this situation would err on the side of protecting its player from further damage.

The phrase "partial tear" discovered during Ingram's heel surgery recovery also suggests the Achilles issue may have been present or developing before the team was fully aware of its extent. That's a common scenario in professional sports, where athletes push through discomfort.

What It Means for Los Angeles

Ingram's absence creates an immediate gap in the Clippers' offensive options. The team will need other players to absorb those responsibilities in the early portion of the season.

The best-case scenario is that Ingram's injury responds well to conservative treatment and he's back before the calendar flips to November. The more concerning scenario is a longer absence that sets a concerning tone for the Clippers' 2026-27 campaign before it truly begins.

Los Angeles will be watching his progress closely throughout the preseason and hoping for a timeline that keeps him healthy for a full stretch run come spring.

What's Next

The Clippers will provide updates on Ingram's recovery as his status evolves. The team's medical staff will oversee his rehabilitation, with the goal of returning him to the floor as safely and quickly as possible.

In the meantime, the organization must pivot its early-season approach and rely on the depth of its roster. Whether that depth is sufficient for a team with championship ambitions remains to be seen.
