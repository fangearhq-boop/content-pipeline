Trail Blazers 2026-27 Preview: Morant, Lillard, and a Backcourt Built to Compete

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | September 6, 2026

The Portland Trail Blazers head into the 2026-27 season with one of the most intriguing backcourts in the Western Conference, a recovering franchise cornerstone, and a significant early-season challenge to manage.

Ja Morant. Damian Lillard. Jrue Holiday. On paper, that trio belongs on a contender. In practice, the Blazers must first answer a series of questions that training camp — opening September 29 — will begin to address.

The Morant-Lillard Combination

Morant arrived in Portland via trade from Memphis this offseason, adding All-Star firepower to a backcourt that already featured a returning Lillard. Lillard's comeback is one of the NBA's most anticipated storylines heading into 2026-27: the six-time All-Star missed all of 2025-26 after suffering an Achilles injury the previous season and enters training camp with a full year of recovery behind him.

If Lillard can return to his pre-injury form, the Blazers have a real argument as one of the West's most dangerous teams. Morant averaged 19.5 points and 8.1 assists in 20 games with Portland last season. Lillard, in 2024-25 before the injury, was a legitimate scoring machine averaging over 25 points per game. Holiday provides veteran defensive stability and playoff-tested IQ alongside them.

The Sharpe Problem

The largest variable heading into the season is Shaedon Sharpe. The 22-year-old wing tore the meniscus in his right knee and faces a recovery timeline of approximately six months, placing his return somewhere around late February or early March 2027. That means Portland will navigate the first four-plus months of the season without one of their most dynamic offensive weapons.

The Blazers' current roster has 12 players on fully guaranteed contracts, with the final two or three spots up for competition in training camp and preseason. Jeremy Sochan is among those fighting for a spot, and Portland is expected to add wing depth before camp opens to cover for Sharpe's absence. Donovan Clingan, Deni Avdija, and Toumani Camara round out the frontcourt core.

What's Next

Portland opens training camp September 29 with a roster that has genuine upside and legitimate concerns in equal measure. Lillard's health will be monitored closely through every preseason game. The team is expected to announce one or two additional signings before camp begins, specifically targeting wings who can cover for Sharpe's absence. The regular season begins October 20, and the Western Conference offers no grace period — Oklahoma City, San Antonio, Denver, and Golden State all figure to be in the playoff race from day one. The Blazers' ceiling is real. The path to it requires things going right that have not always gone right for this franchise.
