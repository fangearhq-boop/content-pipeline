Christian Braun Responds to Nuggets Critics: 'We've Got Everything We Need'

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | September 6, 2026

The Denver Nuggets had a complicated offseason. Michael Porter Jr. was traded to Brooklyn for Cam Johnson. Peyton Watson went to Cleveland. The crown jewel addition was DeMar DeRozan — a six-time All-Star, yes, but also a 37-year-old who signed for $3.9 million. Critics noticed. Predictions came in. Some pundits said the Nuggets' championship window was closing. A few said it was already shut.

Christian Braun is not interested in that narrative.

The Denver guard addressed offseason skeptics directly ahead of training camp, which opens September 29. His message was simple: the outside noise is welcome, because it is motivating him and his teammates. "We've got everything we need to win a championship," Braun told The Denver Post.

A Bounce-Back Season in Focus

Braun's confidence comes with context. In 2025-26, ankle injuries limited the guard to just 44 games — a career low. When he was on the floor, he averaged 12.0 points, 4.8 rebounds, and 2.7 assists per game while starting every game he played. The production was real. The durability was the question.

Now healthy, Braun enters the 2026-27 season as a locked-in starter on one of the league's most experienced teams. With Watson traded to Cleveland, the two-guard spot is his. The projected lineup — Jamal Murray, Christian Braun, Cameron Johnson, Aaron Gordon, Nikola Jokic — is one of the most experienced in the Western Conference, featuring a two-time MVP at center and a healthy Murray running the offense.

Braun knows what critics are pointing to. But he also knows something they might be underestimating: this team has done it before.

The Case for Denver

The Nuggets' offseason moves were criticized primarily through the lens of what they lost. Porter Jr., in his first full season with Brooklyn, posted career highs across the board. Watson, 24 years old, figures to be a defensive cornerstone for Cleveland for years. On paper, the departures stung.

But the additions tell a different story when framed properly. Cameron Johnson, coming to Denver from Brooklyn in the Porter trade, is a 28-year-old shooter averaging north of 18 points who fits perfectly as a floor-spacer alongside Jokic. DeRozan gives the Nuggets a midrange scorer who doesn't need the ball to operate effectively and brings championship-caliber IQ to a bench role. And this roster still centers on Nikola Jokic, who has won two MVPs and one NBA championship and shows no signs of decline.

The Nuggets won in 2023 without Porter Jr. contributing at his peak. They rebuilt and competed. Denver's front office, coaching staff, and players believe they can do it again.

What's Next

Training camp opens September 29 in Denver. The Nuggets will use the preseason to integrate DeRozan into the rotation and confirm Braun's ankle health before the regular season begins October 20. With one of the most demanding Western Conference fields in recent memory — Oklahoma City, San Antonio, Golden State, and the Trail Blazers all legitimate threats — every camp day matters. Braun's message was less a promise than a declaration of intent. Denver is not done competing. And its starting guard wants the league to know it.
