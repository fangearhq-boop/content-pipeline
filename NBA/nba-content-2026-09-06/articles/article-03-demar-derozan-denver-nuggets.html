DeMar DeRozan Signs with Denver Nuggets — Veteran Wing Joins Jokic's Championship Core

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | September 6, 2026

Sometimes the most meaningful offseason move is the one no one is talking about. DeMar DeRozan signing with the Denver Nuggets on a one-year, $3.9 million veteran minimum deal might be exactly that.

The six-time NBA All-Star agreed to join Denver after spending conversations with the Miami Heat, Washington Wizards, and New Orleans Pelicans. He chose the Nuggets — a decision that speaks to where DeRozan sees the best opportunity to compete for a championship in the final chapter of his career.

The Player Arriving in Denver

At 37, DeRozan is not the explosive athlete he was at his peak. He is something arguably more dangerous on a well-constructed roster: a relentless midrange scorer with championship-caliber IQ and no ego about his role. In 2025-26 with the Sacramento Kings, DeRozan averaged 18.4 points on nearly 50% shooting before the Kings waived him to trim salary — only $10 million of his $25.7 million contract was guaranteed.

The Nuggets' pitch was straightforward: show DeRozan how he fits alongside Nikola Jokic and Jamal Murray. With Jokic drawing double-teams on every possession and Murray creating off the dribble, there are open midrange windows every single game. For a player who has built a Hall of Fame case on exactly that shot, Denver's system is tailor-made.

Filling the Role Left by Porter Jr.

When the Nuggets traded Michael Porter Jr. to Brooklyn for Cam Johnson, they absorbed criticism for losing their primary backup scorer. Johnson is a capable starter and three-point threat, but he does not replicate what Porter brought as a pure bucket-getter. DeRozan — signed for the minimum — gives Denver that bucket-getting off the bench at a fraction of the cost.

The roster depth now looks considerably stronger: Jokic and Murray lead the first unit, with Cameron Johnson, Aaron Gordon, and Christian Braun rounding out the starting five. Off the bench, DeRozan provides scoring punch alongside younger contributors. Coach Mike Malone built a championship team around a similar depth model in 2023. The blueprint exists.

What's Next

DeRozan will report to Denver's training camp, which opens September 29. The Nuggets open the 2026-27 regular season on Christmas Day at Golden State, a nationally televised showcase of two of the West's most experienced rosters. For DeRozan, the one-year deal means this is his moment to prove he can contribute on a winner — and potentially parlay it into one more contract before his career ends. The Nuggets believe in him. He clearly believes in them.
