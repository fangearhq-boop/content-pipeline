Austin Reaves Signs $185M Max Deal — The Lakers Are His Team Now

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | August 2, 2026

Five years ago, Austin Reaves went undrafted. Every team in the NBA passed on him twice. He found his way to the Los Angeles Lakers on a two-way contract, earned his spot in the rotation, became a fan favorite, earned his first contract extension, became a starter, and then became the kind of player around whom a franchise builds.

On Saturday, he became the face of that franchise. The Lakers have agreed to a four-year, $185 million maximum contract extension with the 28-year-old guard. LeBron James is gone — signed with the Philadelphia 76ers last week. The new era in Los Angeles has a name, and it is Austin Reaves.

The Career That Earned the Contract

Reaves arrived in Los Angeles undrafted out of Arkansas in 2021, and then spent three seasons quietly becoming indispensable. He brought a shooting touch, a competitive engine, and a game that got bigger in big moments. By the time LeBron James was in his final seasons as a Laker, Reaves had evolved into a genuine running mate — a player who could operate the offense, defend multiple positions, and knock down shots when the defense tried to take away the stars.

His development trajectory — from undrafted to starter to cornerstone — is one of the more compelling in recent NBA history. The Lakers did not have to build a case for paying him $185 million. He built it himself over five seasons.

What the Post-LeBron Lakers Look Like

LeBron James signed with the 76ers on July 26, and the franchise began immediately planning its next chapter. The Lakers used the roster flexibility created by LeBron's departure to acquire center Walker Kessler from the Utah Jazz, then traded DeAndre Ayton to the Washington Wizards in a roster management move that created clarity in the frontcourt.

The result is a Lakers team being constructed around Reaves and Kessler rather than being organized around a superstar. It is a different model from the one Los Angeles has operated under for decades — first Kobe, then LeBron. A Reaves-first franchise is a franchise built on continuity, culture, and development rather than recruitment of marquee free agents.

Whether the Lakers' fanbase accepts that model with the patience it requires is a separate question. The team has answered the question of who they are building around clearly and loudly: $185 million worth of clearly.

What It Means Across the League

Reaves' contract is not just a Lakers story. It is a market signal about what guards with his profile can expect in 2026's NBA economy. Players who combine shooting, shot creation, positional versatility, and playoff performance are commanding maximum contracts. The fact that he went undrafted makes the headline striking. The fact that he earned it makes it appropriate.

For the dozens of undrafted players grinding in the G League and on two-way contracts around the league, Reaves' deal is the clearest proof point in years that the path exists.

What's Next

The Lakers open training camp September 29. Reaves will arrive as the undisputed leader of the locker room — in role, in contract, and in expectation. The Western Conference is still stacked: the Spurs are building their dynasty, the Warriors are retooling, the Nuggets and Thunder remain contenders. Los Angeles will need Reaves to be everything the $185 million says he is.

He has spent five seasons proving doubters wrong one game at a time. The undrafted kid from Newark, Arkansas, who was passed over by every team in the NBA, is now the franchise centerpiece of the Los Angeles Lakers. The next proving ground is October.
