What Is Happening in Milwaukee? Inside the Bucks' 1-10 Crisis

By Marcus Cole | November 11, 2026

The Milwaukee Bucks entered the 2026-27 season with high expectations and a roster capable of competing for a championship. Through eleven games, they sit at 1-10 with seven consecutive losses and no clear answers. What is going wrong, and can they fix it?

The Numbers Tell a Grim Story

Eleven games into the 2026-27 NBA season, the Milwaukee Bucks have won exactly once. Seven of those losses have come in a row, a skid that extends from early November and shows no sign of ending. Tuesday's 117-85 blowout at the hands of the New York Knicks was the latest evidence that something is fundamentally wrong with this team right now.

For a franchise with the talent level Milwaukee possesses, a 1-10 start is not just disappointing — it is alarming. The Bucks are not losing close games. They are being beaten convincingly, outplayed in the fundamentals of basketball that coaches drill into teams from training camp through the first weeks of the season.

Identifying the Problems

No single explanation covers the entirety of Milwaukee's struggles, but a few recurring themes stand out across their losses. Defensive breakdowns have been consistent — the Bucks have allowed opponents to generate easy looks at the rim and open three-pointers with a regularity that cannot be explained away as bad luck. Offensive execution has also been an issue, with the team struggling to create quality looks in the half-court and turning the ball over at inopportune moments.

The roster does not lack talent. Players like Jaime Jaquez Jr. and Ryan Rollins have flashed their ability off the bench, suggesting the depth is there. But individual flashes of quality have not translated into the collective effort required to win NBA games night after night. That is a team-wide problem — and one that falls on the coaching staff and leadership to address.

Historical Context and the Path Forward

NBA history offers occasional reminders that poor starts are not always death sentences. Teams have rebounded from 1-10 starts to make the playoffs. The window for such a turnaround is not unlimited, but it is not closed. The key is identifying the core issues quickly, making adjustments, and stringing together a few consecutive wins to rebuild confidence and momentum.

The Bucks' schedule over the next two weeks will be revealing. If Milwaukee can find ways to compete — even in losses — that is a signal the culture is intact and the turnaround is coming. If the blowouts continue, the front office will face harder questions about the direction of the franchise.

What's Next

For the Milwaukee Bucks, the most urgent task is simple: win a game. Any win at this stage would do more for team chemistry and confidence than any amount of practice or film review. The 2026-27 season is still early enough to salvage, but the Bucks need to demonstrate that they understand what is wrong and have the collective will to fix it.

The NBA will not wait. The Knicks, the Celtics, the Warriors — the best teams in the league are only getting better. Milwaukee's window for action is now.
