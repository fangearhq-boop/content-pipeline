Knicks Roll Bucks 117-85 as Milwaukee Falls to 1-10 With Seventh Straight Loss

By Marcus Cole | November 11, 2026

Jalen Brunson scored 23 points and Karl-Anthony Towns posted 21 points and 16 rebounds as the New York Knicks extended their winning streak to four games with a 117-85 rout of the Milwaukee Bucks on Tuesday. The loss dropped Milwaukee to 1-10 — their seventh consecutive defeat.

Brunson and Towns Make It Look Easy

The New York Knicks looked every bit like a team that has found its rhythm. Jalen Brunson orchestrated the offense with the calm efficiency that has become his trademark, finishing with 23 points while keeping defenses honest with his mid-range precision and playmaking. Karl-Anthony Towns was even more dominant, putting up 21 points and 16 rebounds — a performance that underscored why the Knicks made acquiring the big man a priority.

The 117-85 final score tells the story of a game that was never close. New York led for the overwhelming majority of the contest, pushing the pace when it suited them and grinding the Bucks into submission when they tried to slow things down. This was the Knicks' fourth straight victory, and they look increasingly dangerous as a contender in the Eastern Conference.

Milwaukee Can't Find Solutions

For the Milwaukee Bucks, Tuesday's blowout loss was simply the latest chapter in a deeply troubling start to the season. At 1-10, the Bucks have now lost seven consecutive games, a streak that has taken a team many expected to compete for a conference title and left it near the bottom of the Eastern Conference standings.

Jaime Jaquez Jr. and Ryan Rollins provided energy off the bench — scoring 16 and 14 points respectively — but their efforts were not enough to spark a turnaround. Milwaukee's defensive breakdowns and offensive inconsistencies have been recurring problems, and Tuesday's performance against New York offered little reason for optimism.

The Bucks' Growing Crisis

What makes Milwaukee's start so jarring is the talent on the roster. This is not a team lacking in capable players, yet the wins are simply not coming. The Bucks are being outplayed, outcompeted, and in many games, outcoached. When a team loses by 32 points to a divisional rival, it raises serious questions about more than just the starting lineup.

The question now is whether Milwaukee can course-correct before the losses become too deep to overcome. The NBA season is a marathon, and 1-10 teams do occasionally turn things around — but the window for recovery is not unlimited.

What's Next

The Knicks continue to roll, cementing themselves as one of the Eastern Conference's most dangerous teams after just the first few weeks of the season. Towns' ability to impact games on both ends gives New York a dimension that few rosters can match, and Brunson's floor-general play ensures the offense stays organized and efficient.

The Bucks, meanwhile, face a crossroads moment. Something needs to change — whether in personnel deployment, strategy, or mindset — before the 2026-27 season becomes a lost cause in Milwaukee. The time to fix it is now.
