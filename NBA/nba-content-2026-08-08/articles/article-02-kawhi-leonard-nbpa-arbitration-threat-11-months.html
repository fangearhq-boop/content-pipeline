Kawhi Leonard's Trade Saga Enters Month 11 — and the NBPA Just Raised the Stakes

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | August 8, 2026

NBA Investigation / Los Angeles Clippers / Toronto Raptors / Kawhi Leonard

Eleven months. That is how long the NBA has been investigating the Los Angeles Clippers for alleged salary cap circumvention related to Kawhi Leonard — and as of this week, the most significant new development in the case is not a resolution. It is an escalation.

The National Basketball Players Association has stated publicly that it "wouldn't hesitate" to demand that the matter be referred to a neutral arbitrator if the league pursues penalties without, in the union's view, sufficient evidence. That statement changes the calculus. An arbitration proceeding would carry its own timeline — one without a hard deadline — and could realistically push a final resolution into 2027.

Training camp opens in approximately seven weeks.

What the Investigation Is About

The probe centers on a secret endorsement arrangement between Kawhi Leonard and Daktronics, a scoreboard company. Investigators believe the Los Angeles Clippers allegedly facilitated this deal in a way designed to work around Leonard's salary cap number — effectively providing Leonard compensation outside of his official contract, which would constitute cap circumvention under league rules.

The investigation has been ongoing since before the summer of 2025. It entered its 11th month in August with no settlement announced, no penalty issued, and no resolution timeline confirmed.

The Trade That Is Waiting

On approximately June 30, the Clippers and Raptors agreed to a trade: Leonard would return to Toronto in exchange for Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, and two unprotected first-round picks — the 2031 and 2033 drafts. Both franchises have publicly stated their intention to complete the deal.

The NBA has informed the Raptors, however, that they would assume the risk of any investigation outcome if they choose to proceed before the probe concludes. That warning effectively froze the trade, since Toronto has no interest in inheriting whatever penalty the league ultimately imposes on the Clippers for a deal that predates their involvement.

Leonard himself remains owed $50.3 million in the 2026-27 season. He is, in every legal sense, still a Clipper — with nowhere to play while the investigation drags on.

The Arbitration Threat

The NBPA's statement this week shifts the dynamic. If the league moves to impose penalties, the union has signaled it will force the process into arbitration with a neutral third party. That arbitration ruling would then be subject to appeal before a binding three-person appeals panel, jointly appointed by the NBA and NBPA. There is no hard deadline attached to that process.

Multiple insiders, including reporters at ESPN and CBS Sports, continue to describe the most likely outcome as a negotiated settlement between the NBA and the Clippers. That path avoids arbitration, allows the trade to proceed, and gives both sides a face-saving resolution. But a settlement has been "imminent" for multiple months now without materializing.

What This Means for Both Teams

The Raptors are in a holding pattern. Their offseason reconstruction — built around the assumption that Kawhi Leonard would eventually be wearing a Toronto uniform again — cannot proceed with full clarity until the trade clears. Brandon Ingram and Gradey Dick remain Raptors for now, but their futures hinge on a resolution.

The Clippers, meanwhile, are sitting on a $50.3 million cap number for a player who is attempting to leave. Their ability to restructure the roster around that reality is limited as long as Leonard's status remains in legal limbo.

What's Next

The investigation remains the single most consequential unresolved situation in the NBA — for the Clippers, the Raptors, and the league's credibility in enforcing salary cap rules. A negotiated settlement remains the most likely path forward, but the NBPA's arbitration warning means the clock is now running on two tracks simultaneously: toward a settlement, or toward a process that could outlast the start of the season. Watch for any announcement from the NBA's front office in the next four to six weeks as the league attempts to resolve this before training camps open.
