The Spurs Flew to Paris — Coaches, Staff, and All — to Train With Wembanyama

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | August 8, 2026

NBA Offseason / San Antonio Spurs / Victor Wembanyama / France FIBA

Victor Wembanyama is in Paris. That part is expected — he is French, it is August, and France's national team has a FIBA window starting in 12 days. What is not expected — or at least, what is uncommon in the NBA landscape — is what the San Antonio Spurs did next.

They went with him.

Not just teammates, though a group of approximately eight to ten Spurs players did make the trip. The coaching staff and organizational personnel also traveled to France to train alongside Wembanyama before the national team camp formally begins. It is a significant organizational commitment, one that speaks to how seriously San Antonio is investing in the chemistry and development of its franchise player — and in the group around him.

A New Coach for the French National Team

Wembanyama's return to France comes with a notable variable: he will be working under a new national team head coach for the first time. Frederic Fauthoux took over the French program in September 2024, following the Paris Olympics. He has not yet coached Wembanyama at the senior international level.

The August window gives both player and coach a chance to establish their working relationship before the more consequential qualifying games in 2027. For France — a team that reached the gold medal game at the 2024 Paris Olympics and came within moments of beating the United States — the stakes of the 2027 World Cup qualification cycle are real.

Joining Wembanyama on the 15-man roster are two veterans with deep international experience: Rudy Gobert, who has been a pillar of the French program for more than a decade, and Evan Fournier, making his first appearance with France since the Paris Games. The roster blends championship-level youth with experienced international presence.

The August Schedule

France faces a demanding four-game window before September:

August 20: Exhibition vs. Serbia in Belgrade
August 23: Exhibition vs. Serbia in Orleans
August 27: FIBA World Cup 2027 qualifier vs. Slovenia (home)
August 30: FIBA World Cup 2027 qualifier at Sweden (away)

The Serbia exhibitions serve as preparation for the two qualifying games that matter most. France enters the second-round qualifying window in a strong position in Group L — they went 5-1 in the first round — but the path to the 2027 World Cup requires continued winning in this window.

What Wembanyama Said

Wembanyama's public quote about returning to international play captures something genuine about who he is as a competitor: "I do not miss the NBA right now. I miss FIBA basketball."

That is not a slight against the Spurs or the NBA. It is the statement of someone who genuinely loves the international game — the team structure, the shorter windows, the national pride. Wembanyama played eight years of his youth career at a basketball court in Nanterre, a suburb of Paris, before his professional career began. He attended the reopening of that renovated court earlier this summer. For him, being in France in August is not a detour from basketball. It is a return to where basketball started.

The Spurs understand this — which may be exactly why the coaching staff made the trip.

What's Next

France's first exhibition against Serbia tips off August 20 in Belgrade. The FIBA World Cup 2027 qualifying games — the ones that count — begin August 27 against Slovenia. Wembanyama returns to Spurs training camp in late September, followed by the NBA preseason and the October 20 regular-season opener. Keep an eye on how Fauthoux deploys him through the qualifying window — it will be the first look at France's 2027 World Cup roster taking shape under new leadership.
