The Kawhi Leonard Investigation Has a Target Date: September 22, Quebec City, and a Trade That's Almost Done

By Jake Torres | August 15, 2026

League circles believe the 11-month probe wraps within six weeks — landing exactly one week before Toronto opens training camp. Here's what the resolution looks like, and why Kawhi isn't the one paying the price.

The investigation into Kawhi Leonard's endorsement arrangements has run for 11 months. It involves a law firm, the NBA's front office, a bankrupt company, and two franchises in limbo. But for the first time, there is a date attached to the resolution: approximately September 22.

That is the timeline league circles are currently working with, according to HoopsHype reporting. Six weeks from now. Six days before the Toronto Raptors are scheduled to open Media Day. One week before training camp begins in Quebec City — the location the Raptors chose for their September 28-29 camp window. The precision of that calendar alignment is not accidental.

Where the Investigation Stands

The law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz — retained by the NBA to conduct the independent review — has completed its fact-finding phase. The NBA office is now reviewing the findings. The probe centers on Leonard's four-year, $28 million agreement with Aspiration, the now-bankrupt financial technology company, which the league is examining as potential salary cap circumvention.

The Raptors agreed to acquire Leonard in a trade on June 30, receiving him from the Clippers for Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, and unprotected first-round picks in 2031 and 2033. On July 9, Toronto paused finalizing the trade pending the probe's outcome. Leonard is owed $50.3 million in 2026-27.

What the CBA Actually Says

The detail that gets consistently underreported in coverage of this investigation: the current collective bargaining agreement does not list player suspension as a penalty for salary cap circumvention. The Clippers — not Kawhi Leonard — face the primary sanctions. Leonard's contract could theoretically be voided, but that would be a punishment to the Clippers and a benefit to Leonard, not the other way around.

Leonard himself has been operating under the assumption that the trade to Toronto will be completed. Multiple reports indicate he expects to be in a Raptors uniform when camp opens. The Raptors, for their part, remain committed to the deal — they have simply been waiting for the legal path to clear before finalizing the paperwork.

The Complication

One important caveat: ESPN and HoopsHype both reported in late July that the investigation could extend into 2027 if complications arise. The September 22 expectation is internal league belief — not an official NBA deadline. If additional developments surface or the legal review takes longer than anticipated, the timeline slips. The tension between the six-week estimate and the 2027-risk scenario is the live editorial question heading into camp season.

What's Next

September 22 is the date to watch. If the investigation concludes on schedule, the Raptors can finalize the Leonard trade, bring him to Quebec City for camp, and begin building for the 2026-27 season with their new centerpiece. If the probe drags — or uncovers new complications — the timeline extends and the limbo continues. For now, the calendar is pointing toward a resolution before camp. Six weeks is not a long time in a story that has already taken 11 months.
