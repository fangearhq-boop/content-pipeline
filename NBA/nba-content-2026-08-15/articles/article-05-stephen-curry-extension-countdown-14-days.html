Fourteen Days: Stephen Curry's Extension Window Opens Aug. 29 — What the Warriors Do Next

By Damon Pierce | August 15, 2026

The Warriors say whatever Steph wants. Curry says he's staying either way. Windhorst says don't rush. The $136.7M countdown has officially started.

In 14 days, Stephen Curry can sign a two-year, $136.7 million contract extension with the Golden State Warriors. He is 38 years old. He has spent his entire NBA career in the Bay Area. He has four championships, two MVP awards, and a Hall of Fame career that is ongoing. August 29 is the date everything becomes official — and the conversation around the extension has already started without him needing to say a word.

The Warriors' Position

Golden State has made its stance clear: whatever Curry wants, the Warriors will offer. Full two years through the 2028-29 season — carrying Curry to age 40 — or a shorter arrangement if he prefers flexibility. GM Mike Dunleavy Jr. has aligned publicly with Curry's preferences. ESPN's Anthony Slater confirmed mutual interest between the two sides. Every available data point says this extension gets done.

The posture reflects both business and relationship. Curry is the franchise in Golden State. Four championships, the all-time three-point record, the player who rebuilt the Warriors into a dynasty after the Kobe-era Lakers had dominated the Western Conference. Offering him the maximum is not a difficult decision for the front office — it is the obvious one.

The Windhorst Dissent

The most interesting counter-argument came from ESPN's Brian Windhorst, who argued on-air that the Warriors should tell Curry NOT to sign immediately. Windhorst's logic: committing $65-70 million per year in guaranteed salary for two additional seasons limits what Golden State can add around their star. If the Warriors are rebuilding around Curry's final run, structuring flexibility into the final year of his current deal might create more roster options than an immediate extension would.

It is a minority view. Windhorst acknowledged as much. But it is not unreasonable — and it introduces a legitimate debate about whether the Warriors are optimizing for the next two seasons or managing long-term flexibility.

Curry's Perspective

Curry has been consistent: he is staying in Golden State. The extension deadline does not change his intentions. Whether he signs on August 29 or plays out his current contract year as a future free agent, his destination is the same. The extension is about security and mutual commitment — not about where he plays.

Draymond Green is already back on a one-year, $27.7 million deal. The Warriors' core is intact. The question is whether Curry adds two more guaranteed years or creates a different kind of exit ramp heading into the 2027-28 offseason. All indications point to the extension. Fourteen days will tell us whether the indicators are right.

What's Next

August 29 is the first moment Curry can sign. Nothing prevents the extension from being agreed to in principle before then — teams and players negotiate terms ahead of the eligibility window. Whether the announcement comes on August 29 or shortly after, the expectation across the league is that Curry and Golden State will both sign before training camp opens in late September. At that point, the Warriors will have their franchise cornerstone locked in for two more seasons, and the countdown to Curry's final championship push begins in earnest.
