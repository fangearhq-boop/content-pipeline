Bradley Beal Returns to the Clippers on 2yr/$13.2M — Now Health Has to Hold

By Damon Pierce | August 15, 2026

Six games played in 2025-26. A declined player option. A tour of the market. And then a return to LA on a deal that says both sides believe the hip is behind him.

Bradley Beal is returning to the Los Angeles Clippers, and the terms of the deal tell the whole story. Two years, $13.2 million. A player option in Year 2. Three million dollars guaranteed. For a three-time All-Star who averaged 30 points per game at his peak in Washington, those numbers look modest. But context makes everything: Beal played six games last season before a hip injury ended his year, and at 33, he is attempting a comeback that requires proving health before earning anything more.

The Clippers confirmed the signing Friday morning, capping a negotiation that included Beal declining his $5.6 million player option and reportedly exploring interest from the Miami Heat before ultimately returning to the franchise he has been with since the 2023-24 season.

The Career Trajectory

Beal was one of the premier scorers in the NBA for most of the 2010s and early 2020s. His run in Washington — where he spent his first decade — produced three All-Star selections and years of near-misses in the Eastern Conference. The trade to Phoenix brought a championship shot that never materialized. The move to Los Angeles brought a hip injury that cost him most of a season.

Now, at 33, Beal is in a different position. The Clippers did not need to offer him significant guaranteed money. He accepted a deal built heavily on future optionality — his own and theirs. If he plays well and stays healthy, the player option in Year 2 gives him leverage to capitalize. If the hip doesn't cooperate, the Clippers are not deeply committed.

The Fit in LA

The Clippers built their 2025-26 offense around Darius Garland after acquiring the All-Star point guard from Cleveland. Garland averaged 24.3 points and 8.1 assists — numbers that anchored a team that finished .500 and missed the playoffs. With Rui Hachimura and Bennedict Mathurin rounding out the rotation, adding a healthy Beal creates a meaningful scoring threat off ball.

The operative word is healthy. A fully functional Beal — a player who can create his own shot, shoot off screens, and get to the line — alongside Garland would give the Clippers a top-five offensive backcourt in the Western Conference. The version of Beal who logged six appearances and then disappeared to the training room does not move the needle.

What's Next

Beal is expected to be fully cleared for training camp, which opens in late September. The Clippers will monitor his health carefully through the preseason before making any commitments about his role in the rotation. If the hip cooperates, Beal's return adds a legitimate dimension to a team trying to work its way back to the playoffs in the Western Conference. If the injury recurs, the Clippers are protected by the structure of the deal. The bet is on health — and on a 33-year-old with a track record worth believing in, if the body holds.
