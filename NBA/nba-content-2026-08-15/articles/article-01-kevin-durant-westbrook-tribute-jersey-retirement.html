Kevin Durant's Tribute to Russell Westbrook Reframes a Complicated Legacy — and Now the Thunder Face a Question

By Marcus Cole | August 15, 2026

KD called the career "iconic" and Westbrook "quiet and methodical." Jalen Williams wants the No. 0 in the rafters by Opening Night. The NBA's most dissected partnership just entered its final chapter.

For years, the NBA's shorthand for Russell Westbrook was noise: the emphatic dunks, the visible fury, the triple-double machine who played every possession like it was his last. When he retired this week after 18 seasons, the tributes came quickly. But none carried more weight than the one from Kevin Durant.

Durant posted a tribute calling Westbrook's career "iconic" — and then offered a reframe that stopped people mid-scroll. "A lot of people looked at the emotions on the court and thought Russ was loud," KD wrote. "Nah, to me, he was quiet and methodical." Coming from the man who shared a locker room with Westbrook in Oklahoma City at the peak of his career, that framing lands differently than any columnist's retrospective ever could.

The Partnership Revisited

The KD-Westbrook partnership produced five consecutive 50-win seasons and one NBA Finals appearance in OKC before Durant's departure to Golden State in 2016 — a move that fractured one of the league's most compelling duos and set the stage for years of perceived tension between the two. Durant's public tribute represents the most direct acknowledgment of their complicated history, and the "quiet and methodical" framing is a significant revision of a long-standing public narrative.

Other tributes came in quickly. Donovan Mitchell paid his respects. Trae Young weighed in. Kendrick Perkins recalled Scott Brooks telling him that Westbrook gave the team its "swag." But KD's post generated a reaction wave of its own — CJ McCollum responded, and the tribute became a story within the story.

The Legacy by the Numbers

Russell Westbrook finished with 209 career triple-doubles — the most in NBA history, a record no active player is close to matching. He was a nine-time All-Star. He won the 2016-17 MVP award in one of the most dominant individual seasons in league history, when he averaged a triple-double for the entire year — a feat Oscar Robertson had accomplished in 1961-62. Westbrook did it again in 2019-20 with the Houston Rockets. He played 18 seasons across six franchises, logging over 1,100 career regular-season games.

The retirement video, produced in collaboration with Academy Award-winning actor Michael B. Jordan, signaled the end with the same flair Westbrook brought to every on-court moment.

The Jersey Question

Within hours of the retirement wave, a new conversation began. A fan floated the idea of OKC retiring Westbrook's No. 0 immediately, and Jalen Williams — the current face of the Thunder franchise — responded on social media with "dare I say opening night... 🤓." The Thunder home opener is October 22.

If Oklahoma City acts, Westbrook would join Nick Collison as only the second player in franchise history to have his jersey retired. Collison, who played all 13 of his NBA seasons in OKC, had his number raised in 2022. Westbrook's No. 0 alongside Collison's would make a different kind of statement about what the franchise values — and who it remembers.

No official announcement has been made by the Thunder organization. But the public conversation is moving in one direction, and Williams's post carries more than casual weight coming from OKC's current star.

What's Next

The Thunder are preparing for their 2026-27 season with Jalen Williams leading a young, ascending core. Opening Night on October 22 at home would be a natural moment to honor the franchise's most decorated player. Whether the organization acts that quickly — or takes more time to plan a proper ceremony — will be the next Westbrook story to watch. What's clear is that the conversation has shifted from debate to celebration. Kevin Durant's tribute helped make sure of that.
