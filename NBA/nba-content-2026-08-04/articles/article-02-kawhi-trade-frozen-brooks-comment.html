Dillon Brooks Says Kawhi Trade 'Won't Happen' — And the NBA's Most Frozen Deal Is Running Out of Time

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | August 4, 2026

The most complicated transaction in recent NBA history gained a new voice on July 31 — and it was not a reassuring one.

Dillon Brooks, speaking candidly with streamer Neon, told him plainly that the Kawhi Leonard trade to the Toronto Raptors "won't happen." Brooks spent years alongside Leonard in Los Angeles. He did not elaborate extensively. He did not need to. The comment landed with the weight of someone who has been close to the situation and has drawn his own conclusion about where it is heading.

For two franchises that agreed on a trade more than five weeks ago, that is a damning assessment from inside the building.

How We Got Here

The trade itself was agreed upon on June 30, 2026. The Los Angeles Clippers would send Kawhi Leonard to the Toronto Raptors in exchange for Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, unprotected first-round picks in 2031 and 2033, a 2027 first-round pick swap, and two second-round picks. It was a comprehensive package — everything Toronto could offer short of their entire future.

It has not moved since.

The NBA is investigating whether the Clippers circumvented the salary cap through a $28 million endorsement deal routed to Kawhi Leonard through a company called Aspiration — a green banking startup that also held a $300 million, 23-year naming rights deal with the Clippers. Aspiration has since gone bankrupt. The man central to the arrangement has been convicted on related charges and sentenced to 14 years in federal prison.

The Clippers maintain they were defrauded. The NBA is reviewing the findings from its outside law firm. The Raptors declined to accept liability for any penalties that might stem from the investigation and said they would wait for the league's conclusions before completing the deal. Toronto's position is reasonable. The Clippers' position is also understandable. And yet neither position produces resolution.

The NBPA Complication

The newest dimension of the standoff involves the NBA Players Association. The NBPA has the ability to demand a CBA arbitration hearing, which would send the investigation to a neutral arbiter — an outside party with no obligation to meet the NBA's internal timeline.

Commissioner Adam Silver stated publicly that the investigation must conclude before the 2026-27 regular season begins. That deadline looked attainable when it was first articulated. It looks far less certain now. If the NBPA exercises its arbitration right, the investigation enters a process that could reasonably extend deep into the season itself — potentially running alongside regular-season games while Kawhi's contractual status remains unresolved.

Kawhi Leonard's 2026-27 salary is $50.3 million. His contract, his employer, and his basketball future are all undefined six weeks before training camps open.

What Penalties Could Look Like

The NBA's potential penalties for cap circumvention are significant. They can include: voiding contracts, suspending team executives, stripping draft picks, and imposing financial fines. The specific risk is that if Leonard's contract were to be voided as a result of the investigation, or if the Clippers were stripped of future assets, the trade package Toronto expects to receive in exchange for Leonard would change substantially.

Toronto's refusal to accept that risk is logical. Why trade your best available young assets and draft capital for a player whose existing team may have broken the rules to acquire — and inherit whatever penalties follow?

The Clippers, caught between being cast as victims of fraud and facing league discipline as an institution, have no clean path forward either.

What's Next

There is no imminent resolution publicly on the horizon. The NBPA's next move on arbitration is the key variable — if they file, the timeline extends significantly. If they do not, the NBA's review process remains the controlling factor. Training camps across the league open in approximately five weeks. If the case is not resolved by then, Kawhi Leonard could theoretically report to Clippers camp while the trade officially remains in suspension — an unprecedented scenario for a player with his contract value and status. Commissioner Silver's pre-season deadline, once a firm line in the sand, is now the question everyone in the league office, in Toronto, and in Los Angeles is watching most closely.
