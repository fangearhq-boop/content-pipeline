LeBron James Is One 30-Point Game Away From Passing Michael Jordan — And That's Just the Start

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | August 4, 2026

You already knew LeBron James holds the NBA's career scoring record. You already knew he owns the career assists-by-a-forward mark and the all-time regular-season minutes record. You probably know he is heading into his 24th season, which will break Vince Carter's record for most seasons ever played.

What you might not have fully absorbed is this: LeBron James has played 672 career regular-season games in which he scored 30 or more points. Michael Jordan played 671.

One game. One night. One 30-point performance. That is all that separates LeBron from sole possession of another landmark in a career already overflowing with them.

The Record Nobody Talks About Enough

The 30-point game record is in some ways the purest measure of sustained offensive excellence over time. It is not inflated by three-point era volume, and it is not diminished by longevity alone — you have to actually score 30 to get credit. Jordan averaged 30.1 points per game for his career. LeBron averaged 27.1. That Jordan dominated this list for decades is entirely consistent with his offensive legacy.

That LeBron is now one game away from passing him — at age 41, heading into his 24th season — is something else entirely.

LeBron averaged 20.9 points, 7.2 assists, and 6.1 rebounds in 60 games with the Los Angeles Lakers during the 2025-26 season. He is not the scorer he was at 28. But he does not need to average 30 to reach 30 on any given night, and over the course of an 82-game season with Joel Embiid and Tyrese Maxey drawing defensive attention, there will be more than a few nights where the shots open up and he remembers what that number feels like.

Year 24 Alone Makes History

The 30-point games record is just one of approximately 10 all-time NBA marks within LeBron's reach entering this season. The most significant, from a sheer longevity standpoint, is the seasons-played record.

Vince Carter played 22 NBA seasons before retiring in 2020. No player in league history has played more. LeBron's 24th season will surpass that by two — a number that would have seemed absurd even as recently as five years ago, when most analysts assumed LeBron was approaching the end.

He also owns the record for career regular-season minutes and continues to extend it every time he steps on the court. He has already passed Kareem Abdul-Jabbar on that list. Each game adds to a total that now belongs entirely to him.

The Championship Record Nobody Has Ever Held

If the Philadelphia 76ers win the 2026-27 NBA Championship — not a given, but not a fantasy — LeBron James would become the first player in league history to win titles with four different franchises. He has rings with the Miami Heat (2012, 2013), the Cleveland Cavaliers (2016), and the Los Angeles Lakers (2020). A fourth ring, with a fourth team, would be unprecedented.

The projected 76ers starting lineup of Tyrese Maxey, VJ Edgecombe, Jaylen Brown, LeBron James, and Joel Embiid represents five combined MVP awards. That is not a combination assembled for a one-year show. That is a roster built to compete at the highest level for at least the two years LeBron committed to.

Whether he can maintain the health to chase these records through February and March is the real question. He played 60 games in 2025-26. If he plays 65 to 70 games for the 76ers, he will have ample opportunity to hit the marks.

24 Consecutive Seasons of 20-Point Averages

There is another record that gets less attention but may be the hardest to wrap your mind around: if LeBron averages 20 or more points per game for the 76ers in 2026-27, it will mark 24 consecutive 20-PPG seasons to start his career. No player in NBA history has done that. Not Jordan. Not Kareem. Not Wilt Chamberlain.

He averaged 20.9 in his 23rd year. The 76ers' offensive environment, with Maxey running the pick-and-roll and Embiid commanding every defense's attention in the post, should create enough open looks for LeBron to maintain that threshold without needing to be a first or even second option every night.

What's Next

The 76ers' August mini camps give LeBron his first extended time in a team setting with Embiid and company. The NBA schedule drops on August 13-14, revealing when and where history will first get its chance to happen. Opening night is October 20. If you're looking for a single date that could hold multiple all-time records simultaneously — the night LeBron makes his 76ers regular-season debut and potentially scores his 673rd career 30-point game — that's the one to watch for. The record books are paying attention. The only question is when LeBron decides it's time.
