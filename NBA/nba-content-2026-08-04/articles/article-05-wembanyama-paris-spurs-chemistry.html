Wembanyama Is Organizing a Paris Team Trip for the Spurs — Before Training Camp Even Begins

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | August 4, 2026

Victor Wembanyama is in Paris. He is training with his personal strength coach, Guillaume Alquier, getting his body ready for a fourth NBA season. That part, for anyone following the Spurs, is expected.

What is not expected is what he is doing around the training: organizing a trip to France for 8 to 10 San Antonio Spurs players. His own initiative. His own home country. His own push to build the kind of shared experience that transforms a collection of talented individuals into something that functions like a team — before the season begins, before the official practices start, before any of it becomes mandatory.

Leadership looks like this sometimes. It does not always announce itself with a press conference.

The Foundation He Has Already Built

Victor Wembanyama is 22 years old. He is also the reigning NBA Defensive Player of the Year, a two-time All-Star, a two-time All-Defensive First Team selection, and the holder of the NBA record for blocks in a postseason game. He won Rookie of the Year in his first season, and he has been the organizing principle of the Spurs' rebuild since the night he was drafted No. 1 overall in 2023.

In July, he signed a five-year, $252 million rookie max extension with San Antonio. In doing so, he chose the 25 percent maximum salary rather than the 30 percent supermax he was entitled to. The decision left approximately $50 million on the table — money that San Antonio can now use to extend Stephon Castle and Dylan Harper, the young guards who figure prominently in the Spurs' long-term plans.

He gave back the money. He made the sacrifice public. And now he is doing the thing after the thing — not waiting for September, not waiting for the Spurs' staff to organize team activities, but taking personal responsibility for building a chemistry that the regular season will then test.

Why Paris

This question answers itself. Wembanyama is French. He was raised in France, developed his game in France, and still trains there in the summer with the people who helped shape him into an NBA player. Paris is his home base during the offseason in a way that San Antonio, for all its warmth toward him, cannot replicate.

Bringing teammates to France invites them into his world. It creates a shared experience in a context entirely removed from the weight rooms, practice courts, and locker rooms where the team normally exists together. The bonding that happens when you navigate a city with someone — eating together, exploring, spending time outside of the structure that professional sports imposes — is not something you can manufacture through scheduled team events in September.

Wembanyama knows this. He knows it intuitively, and the trip is the expression of that intuition.

The Spurs' Moment

The San Antonio Spurs have not reached a Western Conference final since the Tim Duncan era. That dynasty, which produced five championships over two decades, was built on a specific culture — one that prioritized team over individual, deferred to veteran leadership, and developed young players with exceptional patience and intention.

Wembanyama is not Tim Duncan. He is different in almost every measurable way — younger, more physically unique, more internationally famous at a younger age, more skilled as a perimeter player. But the qualities that Duncan brought to the culture that produced championships — the willingness to sacrifice, the investment in teammates, the long-term thinking — those qualities are recognizable in what Wembanyama is already doing at 22.

The Spurs have their franchise cornerstone locked in through his late 20s. He is helping lay the culture on which the next run will be built, one summer trip at a time.

What's Next

Wembanyama returns to international competition next month, joining the French national team for FIBA Basketball World Cup 2027 qualifying games — prep contests against Serbia on August 20 and 23, then qualifiers against Slovenia on August 27 and Sweden on August 30. France enters Group L atop the standings at 5-1 with Wembanyama leading the squad alongside Rudy Gobert and Evan Fournier. After the FIBA window closes, attention turns fully to San Antonio and the start of training camp, where the players who came to Paris will carry whatever they built there into the season. The work begins now.
