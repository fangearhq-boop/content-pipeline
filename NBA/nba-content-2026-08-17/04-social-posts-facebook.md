### STORY 1: Kawhi Leonard — Second Endorsement Deal (Daktronics)

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 9:00 AM ET

The NBA's investigation into the Kawhi Leonard trade just got a whole lot more complicated — and a whole lot stranger.

We already knew about the $28 million Aspiration Fund endorsement deal that triggered the league's probe into whether the Clippers were circumventing the salary cap. Now NBC Sports and CBS Sports are reporting a second undisclosed deal has entered the picture: an arrangement between Leonard and Daktronics, the company that manufactures scoreboards and jumbotrons. Specifically, the company that installed the video board at the Clippers' own Intuit Dome arena.

The allegation is blunt: Daktronics was used as a vehicle to funnel additional money from the Clippers organization to Kawhi Leonard — another potential CBA violation stacked on top of the Aspiration Fund deal that started all of this.

Here's where things stand: The NBA's outside law firm completed its fact-finding around August 10, per HoopsHype. The expectation in league circles is that the Clippers will face sanctions — but that the actual trade will eventually be cleared to proceed. Leonard himself is working under the belief the investigation wraps before Toronto's training camp (September 28-29 in Quebec City). One source framed it as "resolution within six weeks" as of August 10.

The wildest detail in all of this? The company at the center of the second deal literally supplied the screens hanging from the ceiling of the Clippers' arena. Steve Ballmer's organization is in deeper trouble than most realized.

This investigation has been running for months. The league has made no promises. But Kawhi is betting it gets resolved. What do you think — does the trade clear in time for Toronto's camp?

### STORY 2: Stephen Curry — 12 Days to the Extension Window

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 1:00 PM ET

Twelve days. That's all that stands between Stephen Curry and one of the most significant contract signings in Golden State Warriors history.

On August 29, Curry officially becomes eligible to sign a two-year, $136.7 million maximum extension with Golden State — a deal that would carry the 38-year-old through his age-40 season. And by all accounts, both sides want to make it happen. Warriors GM Mike Dunleavy Jr. told reporters he's "pretty confident Steph will finish his career" in Golden State. Curry himself has told people close to him that he wants to remain a Warrior — but also made clear the team needs to give him something competitive to come back to.

Here's the context that adds extra weight to what should be a celebratory moment: The Warriors tried to recruit LeBron James this offseason. It didn't work. LeBron chose Philadelphia. That failed pursuit reinforces just how important Curry is to Golden State's identity — without him, the franchise has no argument for relevance in a stacked Western Conference.

Curry is still elite. He shot 42% from three-point range last season at age 38. Draymond Green is back. The core is intact. And the alternative — Curry reaching free agency for the first time in his career next summer — is almost unthinkable.

Almost. The NBA has surprised us before.

August 29 can't get here fast enough. Are you confident this extension gets done?

### STORY 3: Wembanyama — France vs. Serbia Thursday (3 Days Away)

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 10:00 AM ET

Victor Wembanyama plays basketball on Thursday. Real basketball. FIBA basketball.

The world's most dominant big man returns to the French national team in Belgrade on August 20 — his first appearance for France since the 2024 Olympic final in Paris. Nearly two years away from international competition. He hasn't been hiding how much he missed it.

"I do not miss the NBA right now," Wemby told FIBA.basketball. "I miss FIBA basketball."

The roster is stacked: Wembanyama, Rudy Gobert, Evan Fournier — arguably France's most talented core since the gold-medal push at Tokyo. But there's a significant new variable: head coach Frederic Fauthoux, who replaced Vincent Collet after the Paris Olympics. Thursday in Belgrade will be the first time Fauthoux has ever coached Wembanyama at the senior level.

After the Serbia exhibition on August 20 and a rematch in Orleans on August 23, the real stakes arrive: FIBA 2027 World Cup qualifying games against Slovenia (August 27, home) and Sweden (August 30, away). France enters those qualifiers at 5-1 in Group L — well-positioned, but nothing is guaranteed.

One detail Spurs fans will appreciate: San Antonio's coaching staff reportedly traveled to Paris alongside Wemby. The franchise is fully invested in his international development. This isn't just a summer diversion — it's part of his growth.

Thursday is three days away. Will you be watching?
