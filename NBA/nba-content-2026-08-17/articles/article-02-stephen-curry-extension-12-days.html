Stephen Curry's Extension Window Opens in 12 Days — And the Warriors Can't Afford to Lose Him

By Marcus Cole | August 17, 2026 | Hoop Heroes

Twelve days from now, the Golden State Warriors will officially be able to offer Stephen Curry the most important contract extension in franchise history. And according to everyone involved, the outcome should be a formality — but the NBA has a way of making formalities feel like cliffhangers.

Curry's extension window opens August 29. The deal on the table: two years, $136.7 million, carrying the 38-year-old through the 2028-29 season and his age-40 year. Warriors GM Mike Dunleavy Jr. told reporters he is "pretty confident Steph will finish his career" in Golden State, and multiple reports from Yahoo Sports and HoopsRumors confirm the offer will be for the full maximum amount.

The LeBron Context

This offseason provided an unexpected backdrop to Curry's situation. The Warriors reportedly pursued LeBron James during free agency — a pursuit that ultimately ended with LeBron signing a two-year deal with the Philadelphia 76ers alongside Joel Embiid.

The failed recruitment attempt has clarified something important: Stephen Curry is not just the face of the Warriors. He is the Warriors. Without him, there is no identity, no foundation, no argument for relevance in a Western Conference that is growing more competitive by the year.

That pressure, however, is also motivating. Curry has told people close to him that he wants to finish his career as a Warrior — but also made clear he wants the team to give him something worth coming back for. The Warriors' front office needs to show a credible path to contention alongside Curry's extension commitment.

What the Numbers Say

Critics who question whether the Warriors should pay a 38-year-old $136.7 million only need to look at last season's numbers. Curry shot 42% from three-point range in 2025-26, a figure that would be elite for a player a decade younger. His ability to draw defenses, create shots for teammates, and hit impossible shots off the dribble hasn't diminished with age.

Draymond Green is back on a one-year, $27.7 million deal. Gary Payton II re-signed for $3.9 million. The core is intact. The question is whether the supporting cast — and the development of younger players — can get Golden State back above .500 after a 37-45 finish last season.

The Alternative Is Unthinkable

If Curry and the Warriors don't reach an agreement by the extension deadline, the situation doesn't automatically become a crisis — but it gets complicated fast. Without a new deal, Curry becomes an unrestricted free agent after the 2026-27 season. That would be the first time in his entire professional career he has ever reached the open market.

The league would be watching. Every franchise would be calling. The possibility, however remote, of Curry in a different uniform would be one of the great NBA storylines of the decade.

Nobody in Golden State wants that conversation. The expectation is that Curry will sign on August 29 or very shortly after. Swipe Sports reported that "if Steph wants the full $136.7 million through the 2028-29 season, he will get that."

What's Next

August 29 is the date everyone is watching. Expect the formal announcement to come quickly once the window opens — possibly the same day. After the extension is signed, attention will shift to the Warriors' roster decisions heading into training camp and whether they can supplement Curry with enough talent to make a playoff push. Watch this space — 12 days could feel like forever when the most important signing in Warriors history is on the clock.
