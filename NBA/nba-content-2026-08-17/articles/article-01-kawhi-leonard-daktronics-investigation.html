Kawhi Leonard Investigation Expands: Second Endorsement Deal With Scoreboard Maker Surfaces

By Jake Torres | August 17, 2026 | Hoop Heroes

The NBA's investigation into the stalled Kawhi Leonard trade just became significantly more serious — and a lot stranger. A second undisclosed endorsement deal is now under scrutiny, and this one involves the company that manufactured the scoreboard inside the Clippers' own arena.

According to reports from NBC Sports and CBS Sports, investigators are examining a deal between Leonard and Daktronics — the South Dakota-based company that supplied the jumbotron and video boards installed at Intuit Dome, the Clippers' home arena. The allegation is that the Daktronics deal was used as a vehicle to funnel additional compensation from the Los Angeles Clippers to Leonard, potentially in violation of the NBA's salary cap rules and collective bargaining agreement.

A Second Deal Complicates Everything

The investigation began after the NBA opened a probe into a separate $28 million endorsement contract between Leonard and Aspiration Fund Adviser LLC — a company that subsequently filed for bankruptcy. That deal, the original centerpiece of the investigation, raised questions about whether the Clippers were circumventing the salary cap by providing Leonard with additional compensation outside of his playing contract.

The Daktronics revelation suggests the scope of the inquiry is broader than initially understood. Rather than a single suspicious arrangement, investigators are now examining what could be a pattern of transactions designed to direct money from the Clippers organization to Leonard.

Commissioner Adam Silver has publicly acknowledged the league's investigation process. The NBA engaged an outside law firm to conduct fact-finding, which was reported to have concluded around August 10 per HoopsHype.

What Comes Next

Despite the expanding scope, there is cautious optimism in league circles that the trade itself will eventually complete. HoopsHype reported on August 10 that while the Clippers are expected to face some level of sanctions, the NBA is believed to be moving toward clearing the actual trade.

Leonard himself is reportedly operating under the belief that the investigation will conclude before the start of training camp. The Toronto Raptors' training camp is scheduled for September 28-29 in Quebec City.

The trade terms — Leonard to Toronto for Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, unprotected first-round picks in 2031 and 2033, a 2027 swap, and two second-round picks — remain in a holding pattern while the league works through its process.

Crucially, the NBA's collective bargaining agreement does not allow for a player suspension in cases of cap circumvention. Any sanctions would fall on the Clippers organization.

The Stakes for Both Franchises

For the Clippers, the second deal — particularly one involving their own arena's scoreboard manufacturer — raises the stakes considerably. Steve Ballmer's organization now faces the prospect of meaningful league penalties even if the trade ultimately proceeds.

For the Raptors, the wait continues. Toronto agreed to this deal anticipating a franchise-defining moment: a seven-time All-Star arriving to lead the team's next competitive chapter. Each week of delay costs the organization preparation time.

Leonard is owed $50.3 million in the 2026-27 season, making the financial stakes enormous for all parties involved.

What's Next

The coming weeks will be critical. The six-week resolution timeline suggested by HoopsHype on August 10 points to a potential conclusion in mid-to-late September — cutting very close to training camp. Watch for an official NBA announcement regarding sanctions against the Clippers and whether the league simultaneously approves the trade to proceed. The Raptors and Leonard's camp are expected to push hard for clarity before Quebec City in September.
