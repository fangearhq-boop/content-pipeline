West Power Rankings Entering Training Camp: OKC's Dynasty Window Has Officially Opened

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | September 10, 2026

The Western Conference heading into the 2026-27 season is shaped by one clear truth: Oklahoma City and Shai Gilgeous-Alexander are the team everyone else is measuring themselves against. Here is where every major West contender stands before camp opens.

A day after breaking down the Eastern Conference hierarchy heading into training camp, Hoop Heroes turns west — where the picture at the top is surprisingly clear, and the picture behind No. 1 is genuinely fascinating.

The East has three teams with legitimate championship arguments. The West has one dominant frontrunner and a collection of teams that could either challenge for a Finals spot or finish out of the top four depending on health, chemistry, and breakout performances from young players.

Here is the West as it enters camp, with training facilities set to open in the coming weeks.

No. 1: Oklahoma City Thunder

Shai Gilgeous-Alexander won back-to-back Most Valuable Player awards. He is 28 years old. The Thunder have assembled one of the deepest rosters in the conference around him through a deliberate, patient four-year rebuild that is now fully arrived.

OKC went deep in the 2026 playoffs and have lost nothing meaningful from that group while adding depth. The coaching staff under Mark Daigneault is widely regarded as one of the best in the league. The team's defensive identity — physical, switching, disruptive — matches the roster's athleticism. And SGA's individual offensive ceiling remains the highest in the West outside of possibly Nikola Jokic on his best nights.

The Thunder enter camp as the team everyone in the conference is trying to dethrone. That is not a position they are unfamiliar with heading into a third consecutive deep playoff run.

No. 2: Denver Nuggets

Nikola Jokic is, by almost any advanced metric, the most valuable basketball player on the planet. The Nuggets have built a system around his unique combination of size, vision, and offensive creativity that maximizes the entire roster, and their track record in high-leverage playoff moments is built on having the most consistently reliable superstar in either conference.

Denver's challenge is the same it has been for several years: can they withstand a postseason grind against younger, more athletic competition? They answered that question emphatically in their 2023 championship run. They have answered it with varying results since. Jamal Murray's health remains a factor; when healthy and engaged, he is the legitimate No. 2 option who can carry a series on his own. The Nuggets are No. 2 and will challenge for the top spot if Jokic delivers another MVP-caliber regular season, which seems like a near-certainty.

No. 3: Portland Trail Blazers

Here is the wildcard. The Portland Trail Blazers have assembled one of the most intriguing backcourts in recent NBA history: Damian Lillard, fully cleared from his Achilles procedure, paired with Ja Morant, acquired via trade in the offseason. Lillard is a five-time All-Star and one of the great offensive creators of his generation. Morant, when healthy and engaged, is the most explosive drive-and-kick player in the sport.

The questions are obvious. Can Lillard's Achilles hold up for a full season? Can Morant play a responsible two-way game as the secondary star rather than the primary focal point? Do the Blazers have enough frontcourt depth behind them to compete in a seven-game series against OKC or Denver? Training camp will begin answering those questions. The upside of this team is genuinely elite. The floor, if health or chemistry fails, is a comfortable 45-win season that falls short in the first round.

Camp opens September 29 in Portland, and the Blazers have an early highlight on the calendar: a game against BC London Lions on October 12 in London — the first-ever NBA game against a British professional basketball club.

No. 4: Golden State Warriors

The Warriors are in the most interesting position in the West — clearly capable of winning a playoff round or two, clearly no longer the automatic Western Conference Finals threat they once were. The question for Golden State is what kind of team they are at the end of a long season. Steph Curry remains an elite scorer when healthy; the supporting cast has turned over significantly. This is either the beginning of a successful transition to a younger core or a 47-win team that exits in the first or second round.

No. 5: Los Angeles Lakers

The Lakers enter their first season without LeBron James since 2018 without a clear identity. LeBron's departure to Philadelphia restructures everything about how the franchise is evaluated. The young core — Anthony Davis, Austin Reaves, and several developing pieces — has genuine individual talent, but the absence of LeBron James's gravity on every possession is a structural change that has no easy workaround.

LA could be a 40-win team that surprises in the play-in. They could also be a 32-win team that starts over. The range of outcomes is genuinely wide. That uncertainty alone is remarkable for a franchise accustomed to clarity at the top of their roster.

What's Next

Training camps across the West begin opening in the coming weeks. OKC and Portland are the two teams to watch most closely at the outset — the Thunder because SGA's form entering the season sets the tone for the conference's hierarchy, and the Blazers because the Lillard-Morant partnership has never been on an NBA floor together.
