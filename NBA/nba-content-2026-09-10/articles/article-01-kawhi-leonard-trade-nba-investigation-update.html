The Kawhi Leonard Trade Is Still Not Official — and Raptors Camp Is 18 Days Away

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | September 10, 2026

One day after photos showed Kawhi Leonard working out with Toronto Raptors players in Miami, the NBA's investigation into his trade from the Los Angeles Clippers continues without a resolution timeline. Training camp opens September 28.

The photos are out. The quote is on record. "I hope so...we gonna win." And still, as of Thursday morning, Kawhi Leonard is technically a Los Angeles Clipper.

The NBA's investigation into the trade between the Clippers and Toronto Raptors is ongoing, and the league has offered no public timeline for when it expects to conclude its review. That review centers on an alleged undisclosed sponsorship arrangement between Leonard and Aspiration, a financial services company that was a Clippers partner — the kind of arrangement that could constitute a prohibited side agreement affecting player compensation or team cap accounting.

Clippers owner Steve Ballmer and team president Lawrence Frank have already received suspensions as part of the league's findings. But the trade itself — agreed June 30, more than two months ago — has not been ratified. And the clock is ticking in a way that is becoming impossible to ignore.

Eighteen Days to Training Camp

The Toronto Raptors open training camp on September 28 in Quebec City. That is 18 days away. It is the NFL equivalent of a week before kickoff, the moment when squads need to be set, starters confirmed, and the work of building chemistry formally begun.

If the NBA has not ratified the trade by then, the Raptors open camp without Kawhi Leonard on the official roster. He would be restricted from participating in any official team activities — he is currently limited to individual workouts because the deal has not cleared. The informal Miami gathering this week was possible only because it was not a sanctioned team practice.

Raptors president Masai Ujiri has consistently expressed optimism that the trade will clear before camp opens. Multiple league sources have suggested that timeline is realistic. But the NBA investigates at its own pace, and no official statement has backed any specific deadline.

What the Investigation Involves

The core of the NBA's concern is whether the Aspiration deal constituted an undisclosed financial benefit to Leonard — a side arrangement that effectively added value to his contract without being reported to the league as required. The NBA's collective bargaining agreement and its rules on player contracts require that all compensation-related arrangements be disclosed. Anything that bypasses that requirement is treated as a serious violation.

Ballmer and Frank's suspensions indicate the league found sufficient evidence that something improper occurred at the organizational level. The question now is how that finding affects the trade itself. The NBA can approve the trade despite prior misconduct, require modifications to terms, or in extreme circumstances decline to ratify it — though the last outcome would be extraordinary given that both teams have been operating for months under the assumption that the deal will close.

There is no public indication that the NBA is considering blocking the trade outright. The most likely outcome remains ratification, with the investigation's resolution focused on the disciplinary record already established through the Ballmer and Frank suspensions.

Leonard Is Already There in Every Way That Counts

The bureaucratic reality is increasingly at odds with the practical one. Kawhi Leonard flew to Miami to work out alongside Raptors players at an informal team gathering. He spoke to reporters in downtown Miami and told them he hopes to come to Toronto. He is preparing like a Raptor, talking like a Raptor, and apparently has been since the trade was agreed in June.

For Raptors fans who endured an extended stretch after Leonard won the 2019 championship and left in free agency, his apparent eagerness to return is a genuinely meaningful signal. He did not need to be in Miami. He chose to be there. He did not need to answer reporters. He chose to say something that sounded like a promise.

The trade terms, when and if it closes, would send Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, unprotected first-round picks in 2031 and 2033, second-round picks in 2030 and 2033, and a first-round pick swap in 2027 to Los Angeles. Toronto would receive one of the most impactful two-way wings of his generation in his early 30s, now apparently motivated in a way he has not always visibly been.

What's Next

The immediate follow to watch is any NBA statement on the investigation's timeline. Any signal from the league that ratification is imminent — or delayed — will have significant operational consequences for both franchises. Raptors training camp opens September 28 in Quebec City. That is the hard operational deadline. If the trade clears before then, Leonard arrives as an official member of the team for the start of camp. If it does not, the Raptors face an unusual situation in which their most important addition may be practicing informally but not officially on the roster.
