The Knicks Want to Repeat — and Opening Night Against LeBron at MSG Is the Perfect Start

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | September 10, 2026

The New York Knicks are the defending NBA champions. They added Andre Drummond, kept their core intact, and open their title defense on October 20 at Madison Square Garden — against LeBron James and the Philadelphia 76ers, the team they swept in the second round.

When the confetti fell in June at Madison Square Garden, New York had its first NBA championship in over four decades. Four months later, the Knicks are preparing to defend it — and they are doing so with a composure that suggests they understand the rarity of what they have.

General manager Scott Perry chose not to blow it up. He chose not to chase another star. He recognized that the combination of Jalen Brunson, Karl-Anthony Towns, OG Anunoby, Josh Hart, and Mitchell Robinson — the core that delivered a title — was worth protecting. And he added Andre Drummond, a veteran rim protector and rebounder, to give the Knicks additional interior depth behind Towns.

That is the team that will raise a banner on October 20. And on the same night, LeBron James will walk into the building wearing a Philadelphia 76ers uniform, in what is already the most anticipated regular season game on the 2026-27 calendar.

The Drummond Addition

Andre Drummond brings something straightforward and valuable: rebounding and interior defense on a veteran minimum or near-minimum contract. He does not need to be a starter or a focal point. His role is to give Tom Thibodeau depth at the five behind Towns, absorb punishment in back-to-back games, and provide the kind of relentless second-chance energy that Thibodeau has always valued in his big men.

Drummond has played in enough winning environments to understand what a role like this requires. He is not being asked to be the old Drummond who put up 17 points and 15 rebounds. He is being asked to be the version of himself that helps a championship team stay healthy over 82 games.

It is a sound addition to a roster that did not need to be restructured. It is also the kind of move that signals Perry is running this title defense with a steady hand rather than a panicked one.

The Defending Champion's Challenge

Repeating in the modern NBA is one of the most difficult achievements in professional sports. The target is on your back from the first game. Every opponent is motivated. Every roster decision is scrutinized. The emotional peak of winning a championship is hard to sustain through a long regular season.

Thibodeau is aware of this dynamic better than most coaches. He has seen what happens to teams that arrive in camp satisfied with what they accomplished. His practice philosophy — physical, demanding, focused on defensive detail — is designed precisely to prevent complacency from setting in. The Knicks will not arrive at their first practice celebrating last June. They will arrive preparing for next June.

Brunson is the player who sets that tone. His work ethic and competitive personality are the franchise's defining characteristic at this point. When he walks into camp, the message is implicitly delivered: there is more work to do.

October 20: Ring Night at MSG

The NBA's scheduling team did not give New York an easy opening night. LeBron James, Joel Embiid, and Jaylen Brown walking into MSG for the Sixers' debut is the kind of matchup the league prioritizes for maximum impact. It is also, from the Knicks' perspective, the kind of test that clarifies exactly where you stand.

The Knicks swept the Sixers in the 2026 second round. The Sixers built their entire offseason around making sure that does not happen again. October 20 will be the first measuring stick of whether they succeeded. Thibodeau will have his team ready. The crowd at MSG, watching a championship banner rise before tip-off, will be louder than it has been for any regular season game in the building's history.

The Knicks earned that night. Now they have to defend it.
