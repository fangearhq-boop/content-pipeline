Rockets Bounce Back Hard: Houston Tops Thunder 119-98, Improves to 6-1

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | November 1, 2026

One loss does not define a team. The Houston Rockets proved that on Halloween Friday night.

Twenty-four hours after the Dallas Mavericks handed Houston its first defeat of the season in a fourth-quarter romp, the Rockets came out and delivered a statement: a dominant 119-98 NBA Cup win over the Oklahoma City Thunder. Houston is now 6-1 on the year, sitting right behind the unbeaten Memphis Grizzlies atop the Western Conference standings, and looking like the group that will challenge Memphis for supremacy in the West all season long.

A Perfect Response

The nature of how Houston lost to Dallas — a fourth-quarter collapse against Kyrie Irving and Cooper Flagg — could have left a mark. Instead, the Rockets showed the temperament of a mature team. They came to their Halloween matchup against OKC with focus, executed their game plan, and turned what could have been a troubling stretch into a one-game blip.

The 21-point margin against the Thunder tells the story of a team that was never threatened. Houston imposed its will from early on, using its depth, athleticism, and half-court execution to grind OKC down across four quarters. The Thunder, at 4-3, are still a solid team — but on this night they had no answer for Houston's firepower.

The Cup Context

This win was not just a regular-season box check — it counted in the NBA Cup standings, where Houston plays in a wide-open Western Conference group. The Rockets' Cup record now stands at 1-1 after their opening loss to Dallas, and they need to finish the group stage strongly to qualify for the December knockout rounds.

The NBA Cup format rewards consistency across five group-stage games (Tuesdays and Fridays through November 27), and Houston's biggest remaining opponents in Cup play will likely define whether they get a crack at the $1 million per-player prize.

The West Is Two Teams

With Memphis at 6-0 and Houston at 6-1, the Western Conference picture is becoming sharper. Every other team is at least two full games behind the pace set by these two organizations. The Golden State Warriors, Dallas Mavericks, and Denver Nuggets are all capable of making noise, but none have shown the same consistency as Houston and Memphis in the early going.

When these two teams meet later in the season — potentially in the NBA Cup semifinals in December — it will be the defining matchup of the West's first half.

What's Next

Houston continues its November schedule on Tuesday, with another NBA Cup group game on the horizon. Oklahoma City, meanwhile, falls to 4-3 and must regroup quickly in a loaded Western Conference. The Rockets head into November as the West's No. 2, and they show no signs of ceding that position anytime soon.
