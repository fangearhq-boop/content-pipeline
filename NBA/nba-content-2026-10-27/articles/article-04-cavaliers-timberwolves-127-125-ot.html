Cleveland Edges Minnesota 127-125 in OT — Wolves Drop First Game of 2026-27

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | October 27, 2026

The Timberwolves had blown out Toronto by 23 the night before. They pushed Cleveland to overtime on a back-to-back. Their first loss of the season required all of that.

Not many teams lose to the Cleveland Cavaliers by two points in overtime and walk away feeling good about themselves. The Minnesota Timberwolves might be one of them.

Cleveland held off Minnesota 127-125 in overtime on Monday night, handing the Timberwolves their first loss of the 2026-27 season. The context makes it harder to view as a failing: the Wolves had beaten the Toronto Raptors by 23 points the previous night and turned around to play at Cleveland on a back-to-back. They took the game to overtime. They went right at the Cavaliers in the extra period before Cleveland finally closed it out.

That is not the profile of a team with a fatal flaw. That is the profile of a very good team that ran out of legs on the second night of a back-to-back against a solid home team.

Cleveland Grinds It Out

The Cavaliers have built their identity on exactly this kind of win. Cleveland does not always dazzle, and they do not always build insurmountable early leads. What they do is compete for 48 minutes, execute in the fourth quarter, and find ways to win close games. Monday's overtime result was vintage Cavaliers basketball — uncomfortable, tense, and ultimately successful.

Cleveland's ability to win at home against quality opponents has been a hallmark of their recent seasons, and Monday's result adds to that ledger. The Wolves are not an easy out for anyone — they have the size, the defensive capability, and the offensive firepower to challenge every team they face. Holding them off in overtime, on your home floor, while they're running on empty from the previous night, is a legitimate accomplishment.

Minnesota's First Loss in Context

The Timberwolves had looked dominant in their early games, including the lopsided win over Toronto that preceded Monday's trip to Cleveland. A first loss on the road, in overtime, after a back-to-back, is the kind of loss a good team absorbs and moves past quickly.

Minnesota will return home with a schedule that offers them the chance to regroup. The early-season form suggests this Wolves team is serious. The defense has been suffocating when fully engaged, and the offense has enough creation options to stress any opponent. One overtime loss on a back-to-back does not change any of that.

The Eastern Conference — and the rest of the Western Conference — should take note of how hard it was to beat Minnesota on a night when they had every excuse to mail it in. They didn't mail it in. They went to overtime against a very good Cleveland team. That reveals something about the character of this group.

What's Next

Cleveland returns to action later this week as the NBA Cup group stage opens Thursday, October 30. Minnesota heads home to regroup after the back-to-back road trip, with Cup games also on the horizon. For both teams, Monday's thriller offered an early-season blueprint: the Cavaliers are a legitimate home-court threat, and the Timberwolves are a complete team that has no intention of being anyone's easy win.
