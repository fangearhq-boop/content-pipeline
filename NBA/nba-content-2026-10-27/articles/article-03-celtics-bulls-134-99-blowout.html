Defending Champions Mean Business: Celtics Destroy Bulls 134-99 at TD Garden

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | October 27, 2026

Boston issued a 35-point statement to the rest of the NBA on Monday night: the champions have no interest in coasting.

Champions do not typically make apologies for how good they are. The Boston Celtics are not starting now.

In one of the most dominant performances by any team in the early days of the 2026-27 season, the Celtics routed the Chicago Bulls 134-99 at TD Garden on Monday night. The 35-point margin was a statement — the kind that circulates around locker rooms across the league Tuesday morning as a reminder of what the defending champions look like when they're locked in.

There is a familiar narrative that surrounds title defenses: that repeat champions sometimes relax early, lose focus, or require a slow ramp-up before rediscovering the urgency of a championship pursuit. The Celtics are not running that script. Monday's performance was thorough, efficient, and uncompromising from start to finish.

The Championship Blueprint in Action

What makes Boston so difficult to game-plan against is that the same qualities that won them a championship — elite defensive versatility, multiple shot creation options, and a relentless offensive pace — are all present again this season. The system does not depend on any single player overperforming; it depends on everyone doing their job at an exceptionally high level, which the Celtics manage because they have been doing it together for years.

The Bulls, for their part, entered Monday's game understanding the challenge but unable to solve it. Chicago is rebuilding toward something, and games like Monday's are part of that process. Getting blown out by the defending champions at their home court is not a comfortable experience, but it is a useful data point for a young team trying to understand the gap between where they are and where they need to be.

For Boston, the 35-point win had value beyond the result. Rolling through a quality opponent with that kind of margin keeps the rhythm of the championship machine turning. The deeper into October the Celtics go without a concerning result, the more the rest of the conference has reason for concern.

Early-Season Context

Boston is among the early favorites to repeat as NBA champions, and games like Monday's feed that narrative. The Celtics have built their identity around cohesion and intelligent basketball, and they have all the key pieces returning from the championship run. The early schedule has not forced them to face any elite competition yet, but they have not permitted any trap games either — every opponent so far has received the full championship-level effort.

The Eastern Conference will have its challengers this season. Detroit has started strongly. Cleveland is dangerous. New York remains a wildcard. But if the Celtics continue to play like this, the standard they set for the rest of the conference is a formidable one to chase.

What's Next

Boston heads to Brooklyn on Tuesday for a matchup against the Nets at 7:30 PM ET on NBA League Pass. After that, the Celtics are scheduled to host Chicago again in a rematch — but with higher stakes attached, as that game on October 30 at TD Garden is part of the NBA Cup group stage. The Cup format tips off Thursday, and Boston will be among the favorites to advance deep into the tournament. For the Bulls, Tuesday is a rest day as they head home to regroup.
