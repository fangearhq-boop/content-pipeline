Thunder Stay Perfect Despite Losing Williams and Holmgren — OKC Tops Suns 125-114

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | October 27, 2026

The bad news piled up for Oklahoma City on Monday. The scoreboard told a different story.

It started with Jalen Williams being carried off the court Sunday night. It continued Monday when the Oklahoma City Thunder confirmed the worst: Williams will be out for an extended period. Then came word that Chet Holmgren — the team's 7-foot-1 defensive anchor — would also miss Monday's game with right patellar tendinitis.

Two starters. Gone. In the first week of the season.

The Thunder beat the Phoenix Suns 125-114 anyway.

Oklahoma City remains undefeated to start the 2026-27 campaign, a fact that would be stunning in any context but borders on surreal given the roster news that has accompanied it. Shai Gilgeous-Alexander and the broader OKC supporting cast refused to let the injury news define the early season, at least on the scoreboard.

The Injury Toll

The Williams situation is the more alarming of the two. The All-Star guard suffered what appeared to be a serious lower left leg injury in the closing minutes of Sunday's win over the Clippers, leaving the arena unable to walk. On Monday, Oklahoma City confirmed the diagnosis: Williams faces a long absence. No specific timeline was provided, but the framing of "extended period" in the team's official communication carries unmistakable weight.

Williams is one of the two pillars upon which Oklahoma City's championship aspirations rest. His two-way ability — the shot creation, the defensive versatility, the pick-and-roll processing — is not something that can be replicated from the bench. The Thunder will need to find new ways to generate offense and cover defensively in his absence, and that task begins immediately.

Holmgren's situation is less severe in the short term. Patellar tendinitis can be managed with rest and treatment, and the team is likely being cautious with the big man at this early juncture. But combining Holmgren's absence with Williams' long-term injury turns what was a full, deep Oklahoma City lineup into something considerably thinner overnight.

SGA and the Thunder Culture

What Monday's win over Phoenix demonstrated — and what the Thunder have shown throughout the young season — is that Oklahoma City's foundation is stronger than any two-player injury crisis can immediately undermine. SGA's brilliance is the obvious starting point: the reigning All-Star has been as dominant as ever, and his ability to carry scoring loads while the rest of the team fills complementary roles has always been OKC's baseline identity.

But there is something else at work here. The Thunder's culture under head coach Mark Daigneault has produced a roster that genuinely buys into team basketball at every position. The depth players who step in when stars are absent do not suddenly become confused or passive — they execute the system, defend hard, and make winning plays. It is not glamorous, but it is effective.

Whether that culture can sustain an extended absence from Williams — who was arguably OKC's most complete offensive weapon alongside SGA — remains to be seen over a full season. Winning five games against a mixed early schedule with limited rosters is very different from winning 50 games over a full year without a cornerstone player.

What's Next

The Thunder and their medical staff will continue to evaluate Williams' injury and provide updates as the week progresses. Holmgren's status will be assessed on a day-to-day basis. Oklahoma City's schedule gets more demanding as October gives way to November, and the NBA Cup group stage arrives Thursday. The most important question in the Western Conference right now is not whether OKC is still good without Williams and Holmgren — they clearly are, at least over a small sample — but whether they can remain championship contenders over a full season with their two-star core reduced to one. That answer will come slowly, game by game, over the months ahead.
