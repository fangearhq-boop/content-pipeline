Cooper Flagg Ignores Shoulder Sprain, Drops Career-High 29 as Mavericks Top Heat 113-109

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | October 27, 2026

Dallas's rookie franchise cornerstone played through a questionable tag to deliver the best game of his young NBA career, lifting the Mavericks to 3-0.

He was listed as questionable. He showed up anyway — and made everyone remember why Dallas traded up for the No. 1 pick.

Cooper Flagg finished Monday night's game against the Miami Heat with 29 points and 11 assists, both career highs, as the Dallas Mavericks defeated the Heat 113-109 to improve to 3-0 on the young 2026-27 season. Flagg had been listed as a game-time decision with a mild left shoulder sprain heading into the contest — a concern that dissipated the moment he touched the floor.

The Heat had no answer for him. Flagg created off the dribble, found cutters, and knocked down mid-range jumpers with the confidence of a veteran. His 11 assists were a testament to his vision; his 29 points to his finishing ability through contact.

A Statement Performance

Context matters here: Flagg was widely regarded as a once-in-a-generation prospect coming out of Duke, and Dallas moved heaven and earth to land the top pick in the 2026 draft. Early-season performances are inherently small samples, but when a player drops a career-high in Week 1 while playing through an injury, the basketball world pays attention.

The shoulder sprain had raised legitimate concerns heading into Monday's game. There was real discussion about whether Dallas would rest Flagg to preserve him for the long haul — a sensible precaution with 79 games still remaining. That conversation became irrelevant the moment tip-off arrived. Flagg made clear he intended to play, and the result was the best individual performance of the night across the entire NBA slate.

Miami, for their part, threw different bodies at Flagg and tried to take away his driving lanes. Nothing worked. When they gave him space, he shot over the top. When they crowded him, he found his second option before the defense could recover.

Dallas Quietly Building Something

Three games into the season and the Mavericks are undefeated. The organization made a deliberate decision to rebuild around Flagg following a transitional period, and the early returns suggest they got the process right. At 3-0, Dallas has beaten opponents of varying quality, which tells you something about the team's baseline competitiveness.

The shoulder will remain a storyline to monitor. Dallas has every incentive to be cautious with their franchise cornerstone — one week into an 82-game season is not the time to push through a nagging injury if the situation worsens. For now, though, Flagg has played through it and produced, which speaks to both his toughness and his physical resilience.

Miami drops to 1-2 and faces questions of their own. The Heat are a veteran club with a defined identity, but finding no answer for a rookie on a bad shoulder is a damaging result. They will look to regroup quickly with a challenging schedule ahead.

What's Next

Dallas will monitor Flagg's shoulder closely with a busy schedule ahead. The Mavericks return to action later this week, with the NBA Cup group stage opening Thursday, October 30 — and the prospect of their rookie star playing at full health in a Cup game will excite the Dallas fanbase considerably. For now, Cooper Flagg has announced himself: he is healthy enough, good enough, and mentally tough enough to be the player everyone believed he would be.
