### Long-Form Post Window: 9:00 AM ET The clock is ticking. ⏰ Stephen Curry's contract extension window opens August 29 — just six days from now — and the decision he makes will define the next chapter of one of the most celebrated careers in NBA history. The maximum available offer is two years and $136.7 million. Warriors GM Mike Dunleavy said he is "pretty confident" Curry will re-sign, and multiple ESPN reporters confirm mutual interest. Without any extension, Curry becomes an unrestricted free agent for the first time in his career in 2027. That is not a scenario the Warriors want to contemplate. August 29. The greatest shooter in NBA history decides. Golden State is holding its breath.

### Long-Form Post Window: 7:00 PM ET The NBA has made its position clear: the 2026 All-Star Game will stay at Intuit Dome in Inglewood, California — despite the Kawhi Leonard investigation still ongoing against the Los Angeles Clippers. That decision is a signal. The league is not treating the Clippers' arena as the site of an unresolved scandal requiring punitive action. And that tells you something about where this case is heading. The investigation centers on allegations that Clippers owner Steve Ballmer funneled money to Leonard through Aspiration (a company that reportedly owed Leonard around $28 million for consulting work) and through a separate Daktronics scoreboard deal at Intuit Dome. The NBA has narrowed its focus to a "failure to supervise" charge — meaning the Clippers improperly introduced Leonard to corporate partners, rather than directly paying him. HoopsHype reports that Leonard has received no indication his contract will be voided, and that both he and the Toronto Raptors are operating as if the trade to Toronto will complete before training camp. The Board of Governors meets in mid-September. Resolution is expected around September 22. Clippers face sanctions — the form of which is still being determined. Kawhi gets to move on. The show goes on at Intuit Dome. That's the story.
