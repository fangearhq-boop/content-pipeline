### STORY 1: Ja Morant to Portland Trail Blazers

**Long-Form Post**
Window: 11:00 AM ET

The Ja Morant era in Memphis is officially over. 🏀

The Grizzlies have traded the two-time All-Star to the Portland Trail Blazers, closing the book on one of the most thrilling — and complicated — chapters in recent NBA memory.

At his best, Morant was one of the most breathtaking players in the sport: a high-flying point guard with elite court vision, the ability to take over fourth quarters, and a highlight reel that made casual fans tune in every night. The Grizzlies built their identity around him and reached the playoffs behind his leadership. But a series of off-court incidents and suspensions repeatedly pulled the spotlight away from basketball, and both sides needed a clean break.

For Portland, this is a pivot point. The Trail Blazers have spent several seasons accumulating assets and drafting for the future after the Damian Lillard era ended. Landing Morant signals the rebuild is ready to accelerate. When healthy and locked in, he is a top-tier point guard — the kind of player who changes how a team is perceived around the league overnight.

Portland now has a face of the franchise. The Western Conference just got more complicated.

### STORY 2: Stephen Curry Extension — 6 Days

**Long-Form Post**
Window: 9:00 AM ET

The clock is ticking. ⏰

Stephen Curry's contract extension window opens August 29 — just six days from now — and the decision he makes will define the next chapter of one of the most celebrated careers in NBA history.

The maximum available offer is two years and $136.7 million. Warriors GM Mike Dunleavy said he is "pretty confident" Curry will re-sign, and multiple ESPN reporters confirm mutual interest. Curry himself has told people close to him that finishing his career as a Warrior is his preference.

But the backdrop makes this more than a formality. Golden State went 37-45 last season and struck out on LeBron James, Giannis Antetokounmpo, and Anthony Davis in the offseason. Curry is 38, still shooting 42 percent from three, still the best shooter the game has ever seen. Locking in $65 to $70 million per year limits what the Warriors can build around him.

Brian Windhorst has floated a bolder, shorter-term structure as an alternative. Some reports suggest a one-year deal around $62.6 million may be under discussion, giving both sides more flexibility.

Without any extension, Curry becomes an unrestricted free agent for the first time in his career in 2027. That is not a scenario the Warriors want to contemplate.

August 29. The greatest shooter in NBA history decides. Golden State is holding its breath.

### STORY 3: Kawhi Leonard Investigation

**Long-Form Post**
Window: 7:00 PM ET

The NBA has made its position clear: the 2026 All-Star Game will stay at Intuit Dome in Inglewood, California — despite the Kawhi Leonard investigation still ongoing against the Los Angeles Clippers.

That decision is a signal. The league is not treating the Clippers' arena as the site of an unresolved scandal requiring punitive action. And that tells you something about where this case is heading.

The investigation centers on allegations that Clippers owner Steve Ballmer funneled money to Leonard through Aspiration (a company that reportedly owed Leonard around $28 million for consulting work) and through a separate Daktronics scoreboard deal at Intuit Dome. The NBA has narrowed its focus to a "failure to supervise" charge — meaning the Clippers improperly introduced Leonard to corporate partners, rather than directly paying him. HoopsHype reports that Leonard has received no indication his contract will be voided, and that both he and the Toronto Raptors are operating as if the trade to Toronto will complete before training camp.

The Board of Governors meets in mid-September. Resolution is expected around September 22. Clippers face sanctions — the form of which is still being determined. Kawhi gets to move on.

The show goes on at Intuit Dome. That's the story.

### STORY 4: Dillon Brooks Extension

**Long-Form Post**
Window: 1:00 PM ET

Dillon Brooks has earned a long-term contract — and the Phoenix Suns just gave him one. 💪

After averaging a career-high 20.2 points per game last season, Brooks has agreed to a three-year, $73 million extension with Phoenix through the 2029-30 season. It is the biggest financial commitment of his career, and a recognition that his game has evolved well beyond the defensive specialist label he built his reputation on.

Brooks has always been known for his toughness — a physical, relentless wing defender who gets under opponents' skin and makes their lives miserable. But the scoring development he showed last season proved there is significantly more to his game. His combination of shot creation, physicality, and high-pressure toughness made him a perfect fit in Phoenix's system.

The Suns have been active this offseason. They also signed Mark Williams, acquired Miles Bridges and Luke Kennard, and are clearly building toward a harder, more physical team identity. Brooks, locked in at 30 years old, is central to that vision.

From enforcer to scorer to $73 million man. The journey of Dillon Brooks continues in Phoenix — and the next chapter looks promising.

### STORY 5: France vs. Serbia Orleans Rematch

**Long-Form Post**
Window: 4:00 PM ET

Three days ago in Belgrade, Nikola Jokic sent a message. The three-time MVP finished with 20 points, 9 rebounds, and 8 assists as Serbia defeated France 90-87 in a FIBA World Cup 2027 qualifier. Victor Wembanyama, France's newly appointed captain, had just 10 points and 6 turnovers. Today in Orleans, France gets a chance to respond.

Wembanyama has spoken openly about how much he loves representing France internationally — he called this qualifying window a chance to "miss FIBA basketball" in the best way possible. But Belgrade was a reality check. Even generational talents face a steep learning curve against the greatest center of the modern era. Jokic's 20/9/8 performance was vintage: efficient, understated, and completely in control.

Today's rematch is on French soil, which means Wembanyama will have the home crowd behind him. France entered this week's games at 5-1 in Group L qualifying, still well-positioned to advance to the 2027 World Cup. A win today would split the head-to-head with Serbia and restore momentum heading into upcoming qualifiers against Slovenia on August 27 and Sweden on August 30.

Jokic vs. Wemby. The three-time MVP against the next generation. In a gym in France, with World Cup qualifying on the line.

This is the appointment viewing that makes August worth watching. 👀
