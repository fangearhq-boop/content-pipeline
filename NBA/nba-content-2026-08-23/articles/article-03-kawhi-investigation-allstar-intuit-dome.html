NBA Keeps All-Star Game at Intuit Dome as Kawhi Investigation Nears Resolution

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | August 23, 2026

The NBA has confirmed that the 2026 All-Star Game will remain at Intuit Dome in Inglewood, California — a deliberate signal from the league that it expects the Kawhi Leonard investigation to reach a resolution before the marquee midseason event arrives. It is the clearest indication yet that the NBA sees an endpoint to a saga that has dominated the offseason.

The investigation centers on allegations that Los Angeles Clippers owner Steve Ballmer funneled money to Leonard outside of approved channels. According to reports, Ballmer invested approximately $50 million into Aspiration, a company that owed Leonard around $28 million for what critics have described as "no-show" consulting arrangements. A separate Daktronics scoreboard deal — Daktronics supplies the scoreboard system at Intuit Dome — has also been flagged as a potential vehicle for off-books compensation. If proven, this would represent a serious salary cap circumvention violation.

The Clippers have denied the core allegations, with Ballmer's representatives saying he was "duped" and pointing to legitimate carbon credit investments related to Intuit Dome's emissions goals as the purpose of the Aspiration stake. The NBA Players Association and the Clippers have been preparing challenges to any potential sanctions.

Where the Investigation Stands

The league has narrowed its focus to a "failure to supervise" charge — essentially, that the Clippers organization improperly introduced Leonard to corporate partners in violation of NBA rules, rather than the owner directly paying him. That narrowing matters: it suggests the harshest possible outcome for Leonard himself — contract voiding — is not expected.

HoopsHype reported in August that Kawhi Leonard has received no indication from the NBA that his contract will be invalidated. Both Leonard and the Toronto Raptors, who have been waiting to complete a trade for him, are operating as if the deal will go through before training camp begins in late September. The Raptors hold their training camp September 28-29 in Quebec City.

The NBA Board of Governors is expected to discuss the matter in a mid-September meeting, with a resolution anticipated around September 22. The Clippers will face sanctions. The form those sanctions take — fines, draft pick penalties, or stronger measures — will be the final piece of the puzzle.

What's Next

By keeping the All-Star Game at Intuit Dome, the NBA has, in effect, told the public it is not treating the Clippers' arena as the site of an unresolved scandal requiring punitive relocation. That posture is consistent with the reporting that the league expects a clean resolution — sanctions for the organization, clearance for the trade, and a new chapter for Kawhi Leonard in Toronto.

The Board of Governors mid-September meeting is the inflection point. After months of uncertainty, a resolution before training camp is within reach.
