Stephen Curry's Extension Window Opens in 6 Days — And the Warriors Are Holding Their Breath

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | August 23, 2026

Six days from now, on August 29, Stephen Curry will be eligible to sign the most consequential contract extension of his career — a two-year deal worth up to $136.7 million that would keep him in Golden State through his age-40 season. The mutual interest is real. The urgency is even more real.

Warriors GM Mike Dunleavy has been direct about where the organization stands, saying he is "pretty confident" Curry will re-sign. Multiple ESPN reporters have confirmed that both sides want a deal done. Curry himself has told people close to him that finishing his career as a Warrior is his preference. On paper, this looks like a formality.

But the backdrop makes it more complicated than the confident quotes suggest. Golden State finished 37-45 last season. This offseason, the Warriors made serious runs at LeBron James, Giannis Antetokounmpo, and Anthony Davis — and struck out on all three. The team that Curry would be committing to is essentially the same one that missed the playoffs. Locking in $65 to $70 million per year limits what general manager Dunleavy can add around him.

The Numbers and the Stakes

Curry is 38 years old, but he shot 42 percent from three-point range last season — still among the most efficient shooters in the league. Age has not diminished the skills that made him the greatest shooter in NBA history. The question is not about talent; it is about fit, depth, and whether the Warriors can construct a legitimate contender around their cornerstone.

Without the extension, Curry would become an unrestricted free agent for the first time in his career in the summer of 2027. That possibility — Stephen Curry in free agency at 39 — is one the Warriors cannot afford to let materialize. It would signal the end of an era in a way that no one in the organization wants.

ESPN's Brian Windhorst has floated a bolder alternative: a shorter-term or more creatively structured deal that would give Golden State more flexibility. Some reports suggest Curry may prefer a one-year, $62.6 million structure over a full two-year max. If that is the route taken, it would give both sides a shorter commitment while preserving options.

What's Next

August 29 is the date everyone is watching. If Curry signs as expected, Golden State avoids the worst-case scenario and gives themselves a foundation to build from — even if the roster questions remain unsolved. If the window opens without a deal, the conversation about Curry's future will grow louder by the hour.

The greatest shooter the game has ever seen is six days from a decision that will define how his story ends. Golden State, and the NBA, are paying close attention.
