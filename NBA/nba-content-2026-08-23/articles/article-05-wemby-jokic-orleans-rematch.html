Wembanyama Gets Another Shot at Jokic in Orleans Rematch After Serbia's Belgrade Win

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | August 23, 2026

Three days ago in Belgrade, Nikola Jokic sent a message. The three-time MVP finished with 20 points, 9 rebounds, and 8 assists as Serbia defeated France 90-87 in a FIBA Basketball World Cup 2027 European qualifier — with Victor Wembanyama, France's newly appointed captain, held to just 10 points and saddled with 6 turnovers. Today, in Orleans, France gets a chance to respond.

Wembanyama accepted France's captaincy for this qualifying window with the stated mission of leading his country back to the World Cup. His enthusiasm for international play has been genuine — he has spoken openly about missing FIBA basketball — and the Belgrade assignment was supposed to be a showcase. Instead, it was a reminder that even generational talents face a steep learning curve against the greatest center of the modern era.

Jokic at full strength, engaged, and playing for his country is one of the most dominant forces the sport can produce. His Belgium performance was vintage Jokic: efficient, understated, and completely in control. He did not need to dominate in highlight-reel fashion. He just wore France down, made the right pass at the right time, and repeatedly found ways to tilt the game's balance without anyone fully being able to explain how he did it.

What Orleans Means

France entered this qualifying window at 5-1 in Group L, still well-positioned to advance to the 2027 World Cup despite the Belgrade setback. A win today in Orleans would split the head-to-head with Serbia and restore momentum heading into upcoming qualifiers against Slovenia on August 27 and Sweden on August 30. France's supporting cast includes Rudy Gobert and Evan Fournier, two experienced international contributors who will need to do more than they did in Belgrade to take pressure off their captain.

Coach Frederic Fauthoux, in his first major qualifying campaign leading France, faces a tactical challenge: how to deploy Wembanyama in ways that neutralize Jokic's reading of the defense rather than allowing the Serb to dictate the flow of the game from the elbow.

At 22 years old, Wembanyama is still developing every dimension of his game. His summer with France — including this difficult test against Serbia — is exactly the kind of competitive pressure that accelerates that development, even when the individual matchup does not go his way.

What's Next

Today's result in Orleans will shape how this qualifying window is remembered and how Wembanyama's growth is evaluated heading into his third NBA season. Whether France wins or loses, the experience of going head-to-head with Jokic in a competitive setting is invaluable for a player being asked to carry an entire national program.

Jokic made his statement in Belgrade. The world is watching to see what Wembanyama does with the response.
