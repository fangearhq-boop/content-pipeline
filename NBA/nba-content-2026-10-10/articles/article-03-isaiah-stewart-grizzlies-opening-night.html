Isaiah Stewart Out for Opening Night: Memphis's New Big Man Goes Down in Preseason Debut

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | October 10, 2026

It was supposed to be a starting point. Isaiah Stewart arrived in Memphis this offseason as a symbol of the Grizzlies' commitment to building a complete team around their young core — a tough, experienced big man who could defend multiple positions, set physical screens, and provide the grit their front court needed.

Instead, he is already watching from the sideline. Stewart was injured during his Memphis preseason debut and will miss the remainder of the preseason as well as the Grizzlies' regular-season opener on October 21 against the Utah Jazz.

The Injury and Its Timing

Stewart was acquired from the Detroit Pistons in an offseason trade. At 24 years old, with four seasons of NBA experience, he arrived in Memphis as a plug-and-play piece — not a star, but exactly the type of complementary player playoff contenders are built around. His physical style, ability to play center or power forward, and prior playoff experience were the selling points.

Going down before the season even begins is a reminder of how quickly these situations change. The Grizzlies did not provide a detailed diagnosis or timeline beyond the initial announcement — he will miss the opener, with updates expected as the team approaches its regular-season schedule.

Memphis's Bigger Roster Problem

Stewart's injury compounds an already complicated situation in Memphis. The Grizzlies are currently carrying 18 players on standard contracts — three over the maximum of 15 allowed on opening-night rosters. By October 19 at 5 p.m. ET, three players must be waived.

Stewart's roster spot is not in danger — he is a guaranteed contract player with a clear role on the team when healthy. But his absence changes the calculus of what the Grizzlies are building their rotation around in these final ten days of the preseason. The players next to him on the depth chart suddenly matter more, and the cuts the team must make will affect how that rotation looks on opening night.

Memphis opens against Utah on October 21. That game, which was already going to look different from what the Grizzlies projected when they assembled their roster, will now look different again.

What's Next

The Grizzlies will continue their preseason schedule with Stewart sidelined, monitoring his progress toward a return date that remains undefined. The October 19 roster deadline forces the team to make its final decisions next week, with or without clarity on his timeline.

Memphis's front office has shown the ability to adapt quickly. The Stewart situation tests that capacity before the 2026-27 season has played a single regular-season minute. Preseason is supposed to be about fine-tuning. This became something more complicated.
