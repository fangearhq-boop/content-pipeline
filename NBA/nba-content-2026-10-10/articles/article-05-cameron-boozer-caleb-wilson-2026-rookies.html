Boozer's Debut, Wilson's Buckets, and the 2026 Rookie Class Making Its Mark

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | October 10, 2026

Friday night in Chicago, Cameron Boozer stepped onto an NBA floor for the first time as a Memphis Grizzly and did exactly what the Grizzlies hoped he would do: played solid, played smart, and looked like someone who belongs.

Ten points, four rebounds, three assists in Memphis's 104-100 preseason win over the Bulls. Efficient and composed. Boozer — the son of NBA veteran Carlos Boozer — carries himself with a patience uncommon in 19-year-olds, and it showed in the way he moved, set up teammates, and avoided the rookie mistake of trying to do too much in a debut.

On the other sideline, Chicago first-round pick Caleb Wilson was trying to do exactly that — and it was also worth watching.

Wilson Goes for 21 in His First NBA Statement

Wilson put up 21 points on 7-of-17 shooting, including five three-point attempts. He did not shoot efficiently. He also did not hesitate. Five threes attempted by a guard who just entered professional basketball says something about how that player sees himself, and Wilson clearly sees himself as a shooter who needs to be accounted for from the moment he steps on the floor.

For the Bulls, who have been rebuilding for several years, a scoring guard willing to take big shots is exactly what the front office hoped to find in the draft. Wilson's first NBA performance will be debated — 21 points sounds impressive; 7-of-17 sounds like a lot of misses — but in context, for a first preseason game, the aggression is the encouraging part.

Cooper Flagg: 17 Points in One Quarter

Neither Boozer nor Wilson was the most dramatic rookie performance on Friday. In Macao, Cooper Flagg scored 17 of his 19 total preseason points in a single quarter, turning a second period against the Houston Rockets into something the basketball world talked about for the rest of the day.

Flagg is the superstar of the class — the No. 1 overall pick who carries the weight of franchise expectations at 19 years old. His 17-point burst at The Venetian Arena in Macao happened with Kyrie Irving sitting out and the Rockets already comfortably ahead. The stage couldn't have been much bigger. Flagg's performance on that stage was the clearest possible answer to anyone still wondering whether he was ready.

Veterans on a Different Timeline

While the rookies were putting numbers on the board, Kevin Durant played eight minutes for the Rockets in the China Games and scored eight points — one for each minute, with no wasted effort. Houston was up 65-45 at halftime. There was nothing to prove.

The contrast tells the preseason story accurately. Rookies are auditioning. Veterans are maintaining. The class of 2026 is announcing itself with the energy of players who know they have something to prove and the talent to back it up.

What's Next

Cameron Boozer and Memphis will continue building depth before the October 19 roster deadline. Caleb Wilson will attempt to carry his scoring aggression through additional preseason games as the Bulls evaluate their options before opening night.

And Cooper Flagg? He plays Game 2 of the China Games on Sunday in Macao. Seventeen points in a quarter is a start. There are 82 regular-season games still to come.
