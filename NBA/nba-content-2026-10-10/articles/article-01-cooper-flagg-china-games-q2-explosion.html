Cooper Flagg's 17-Point Q2 Wows the World — But Rockets Win 135-117 in Macao

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | October 10, 2026

The moment came 19 minutes into Cooper Flagg's first international NBA assignment. He wasn't supposed to be the headline. Houston had already built a comfortable lead at The Venetian Arena in Macao, the Rockets were running through their sets with precision, and the China Games were shaping up as a comfortable showcase for a well-constructed Houston team.

Then the second quarter started, and Flagg turned it into something else entirely.

The 19-year-old Dallas Mavericks rookie scored 17 points in a single quarter in front of a sold-out crowd in Macao — an explosion that produced the highlight of the night, of the China Games, and arguably of the entire preseason to this point. He finished with 19 total points in 19 minutes before being rested in the second half.

What Flagg Did — and Why It Mattered

Flagg's final line read 19 points, 6 rebounds, and 3 assists on 7-of-14 shooting from the floor, 1-of-5 from three, and a perfect 4-of-4 at the free-throw line. But the statsheet undersells what happened in that second quarter, when he looked like a veteran player who had made this stage his own. Seventeen points in a single period. In a preseason game. On the other side of the world.

This matters because the 2026 NBA China Games are not a random preseason trip. The league chose Dallas and Houston deliberately — Cooper Flagg is the most-watched young player in basketball right now, and his first international game was always going to be scrutinized. He passed that test easily.

The Mavericks did hold him back appropriately. Head coach Jason Kidd kept Flagg's minutes capped at 19, and with the game slipping out of reach by the third quarter, there was no need to chase it. Dallas sat without Kyrie Irving, P.J. Washington, and Dereck Lively II as a precaution — this was never intended as a full-strength test. Game 2 on Sunday offers a different look.

Houston Was the Better Team

Flagg's showcase came at the cost of a 135-117 loss, and that shouldn't be buried. The Rockets were simply better. Tari Eason led all scorers with 20 points. Jabari Smith Jr. added 19. Fred VanVleet, returning from injury, contributed 17 points and looked sharp in his limited minutes. And Alperen Sengun — the Turkish big man who many consider a top-five center in the league — posted a triple-double in a game that was never close after halftime.

Kevin Durant played eight minutes and scored eight points. Houston could afford to pace him, and they did. At halftime, the Rockets led 65-45. The game was effectively over.

That's what a legitimate title contender looks like. You bring Durant, Sengun, and Eason to Macao, you play hard for two and a half quarters, and you win comfortably without taxing your stars. The Rockets looked like a team that knows what it is.

What's Next

Game 2 of the 2026 NBA China Games tips off Sunday, October 11 at 6 a.m. ET on NBA TV. Kyrie Irving is expected to be available for Dallas after sitting Game 1 as a precaution.

Both teams return stateside after Game 2 to complete their preseason schedules. Opening-night rosters must be finalized by October 19. The 2026-27 regular season begins October 20-21.
