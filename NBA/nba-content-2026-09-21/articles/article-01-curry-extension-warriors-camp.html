Steph Curry Heads Into Warriors Camp Without an Extension — What Happens Next?

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | September 21, 2026

Golden State Warriors training camp opens September 29 at BYU-Hawaii, and Stephen Curry — the franchise's four-time champion and the greatest shooter the sport has ever produced — will walk through those doors still unsigned to a contract beyond this season.

That is not necessarily a crisis. But it is a storyline that will define Golden State's offseason and reshape the franchise's competitive window for the next several years.

Curry became eligible for a contract extension in late August 2026. Under current collective bargaining rules — specifically the Over-38 Rule, which limits extensions to two seasons for players who will be 38 or older — he can sign a deal worth as much as $136.7 million over two years. The alternative is finishing out the current campaign, earning $65 million in 2026-27, and entering free agency next summer.

The Warriors' Position

Golden State has been unambiguous on their end: Curry gets whatever he wants. Team sources confirmed the organization expects to engage in extension talks with genuine desire to keep the 38-year-old in a Warriors uniform for the remainder of his career. That kind of organizational alignment rarely exists in professional sports — and Curry knows it.

The question hanging over camp is not whether the Warriors will offer Curry the maximum. It is whether Curry will take it.

Whispers around the league suggest the six-time All-Star is open to taking a below-max deal — potentially sacrificing tens of millions to give Golden State the cap flexibility to add another star. It would be an act of loyalty rare in today's NBA, reminiscent of Tim Duncan's multiple discounts that kept the Spurs competitive deep into his career.

What a Discount Would Mean

If Curry signs below the $136.7 million cap on his extension, the Warriors could use that cap space to pursue a second star — giving the franchise one more legitimate championship window before Curry's career winds down. At 38, playing at the level he played last season, Curry is not walking away from competition. He is walking toward it.

But taking a discount also sets a precedent that few players in NBA history have been willing to accept. LeBron James took a series of discounts in Cleveland to build title-contending rosters. Kevin Durant famously took below-max deals in Golden State. Each time, the decision shaped a legacy.

Curry's legacy is already secure. He is the greatest shooter in history, a four-time champion, and the architect of a dynasty. Whether he takes the max or not changes nothing about what he has accomplished. What it changes is what comes next.

The Timeline

There is no hard deadline forcing a decision before the regular season begins October 20. Extensions can be signed throughout the season. But the longer this goes unsigned, the louder the questions become — and the more the uncertainty hovers over a franchise that has already navigated enough turbulence in recent years.

If no extension is signed, Curry becomes a free agent next summer. At that point, every team with cap space and championship aspirations would pursue him. The possibility is not theoretical. It is something the Warriors cannot afford to treat as such.

What's Next

All eyes will be on Curry when Warriors camp officially opens September 29 at BYU-Hawaii. Whether he takes the podium and announces a deal, or deflects questions and lets the mystery deepen, this is the most important contract situation in the NBA right now. Golden State's competitive future — and one of basketball's all-time careers — hinges on what happens next.
