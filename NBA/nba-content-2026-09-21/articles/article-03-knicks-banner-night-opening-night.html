Knicks Banner Night Is the Greatest Opening Night Storyline in Modern NBA History

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | September 21, 2026

Ticket prices at Madison Square Garden glitched above $1,000 this week, and nobody was surprised. The demand for a seat at Opening Night on October 20 is not a bug. It is a feature of the most stacked single-game storyline the NBA has produced in at least a generation.

Here is what happens on that one night, at that one arena, in those few hours:

The New York Knicks raise their first championship banner since 1973. Fifty-three years. Jalen Brunson, Finals MVP, stands on the court that made him a legend. The crowd that has waited longer than most of its members have been alive watches that banner rise to the Garden rafters.

And then the Philadelphia 76ers walk in for tipoff.

The Visitors Are Not Just Any Team

LeBron James, 41 years old, makes his 76ers debut on Opening Night. He is alongside Jaylen Brown, also appearing in a Philadelphia uniform for the first time after his blockbuster trade from Boston. Joel Embiid and Tyrese Maxey are there too. Four stars, all in Sixers blue, all walking into a building where the crowd will be mid-ceremony celebrating the championship they could not prevent.

Context matters: New York swept the 76ers in four games during last season's playoffs, holding Philly to just 102 points per game while the Knicks averaged 124.3. The Sixers were humiliated at MSG. The rematch, on Opening Night, with a remade roster, while New York hangs its banner — the NBA could not script this better.

The Ceremony Details

The banner ceremony begins at 6:30 PM ET, with tipoff scheduled for 7:00 PM on NBC and Peacock. The game is part of a tripleheader that also includes the Boston Celtics versus the Detroit Pistons at 3:00 PM and the Oklahoma City Thunder versus the San Antonio Spurs to cap the night.

But nothing on the schedule competes with the main event. The Knicks-76ers game is the one the basketball world has circled since the NBA released the schedule. When tickets briefly flooded the secondary market due to a platform glitch this week and prices surged past $1,000, fans still bought. That is how much this night means.

Why This Moment Is Bigger Than Basketball

Madison Square Garden has hosted some of the most iconic moments in sports history. The 1970 and 1973 championships. Patrick Ewing's career. The Linsanity explosion. But raising the first championship banner in 53 years — in a city that has waited since Richard Nixon was president — is in a category of its own.

For an entire generation of Knicks fans, this moment was never supposed to be possible. They were born into losing. They grew up in losing. They watched the franchise stumble through bad signings, bad drafts, and bad decisions for five decades. And then, in the spring of 2026, Jalen Brunson delivered what none of them ever truly expected.

Now it gets raised to the ceiling. On national television. With LeBron James standing in the visiting tunnel watching it happen.

What's Next

October 20 cannot get here fast enough. For Knicks fans, it is the night they have waited 53 years for. For the 76ers, it is their first chance to send a message to the team that swept them. For the rest of the NBA, it is the moment the 2026-27 season truly begins. Tune in at 6:30 PM ET on NBC. You do not want to miss this.
