NBA Thursday Preview: Three Games Tonight, NBA Cup Group Play Opens Friday

By Marcus Cole

October 29, 2026

NBA Preview

Wednesday's NBA slate produced buzzer-to-buzzer drama — a Curry explosion, a 19-point Knicks comeback, a Wembanyama overtime show. Thursday keeps the pace with three more games before the biggest addition to the basketball calendar arrives on Friday: the NBA Cup begins its group stage.

Here is what to watch Thursday night and what to expect as the tournament tips off Friday.

Thursday's Three-Game Slate

The Cleveland Cavaliers visit the Milwaukee Bucks at 8 PM ET in what should be a competitive Eastern Conference matchup between two teams with legitimate playoff aspirations. Evan Mobley has looked sharp through the early season for Cleveland. The Bucks, still building around their core, will look to assert themselves at home.

At the same time, the Minnesota Timberwolves visit the San Antonio Spurs — a game that carries a specific kind of narrative weight. The Spurs are riding a three-game winning streak fueled by Wembanyama's dominance, and Minnesota arrives without Anthony Edwards. It is a favorable matchup for San Antonio at home, and the Spurs will be motivated to extend their streak to four.

The Oklahoma City Thunder visit the Houston Rockets at 8:30 PM ET in a Western Conference bout worth watching. The Thunder have been one of the league's more intriguing young teams, and a road game in Houston is a legitimate test of where they stand.

NBA Cup Kicks Off Friday

The 2026 NBA Cup officially enters group play Friday, October 30, adding an extra layer of stakes to select matchups through December. Every team has been placed into conference-based groups, with the top clubs from each group advancing to the quarterfinals in early December.

Friday's opening slate is headlined by the Raptors-Magic rematch in Toronto — two teams that just played Wednesday, with the Raptors winning by 30. The NBA Cup turns that blowout into a narrative hook: can Orlando respond with tournament points on the line?

The tournament runs through the holiday stretch, with quarterfinals scheduled for December 4-5, semifinals on December 8-9, and the NBA Cup championship on December 11 at Hinkle Field House.

What's Next

The NBA calendar enters one of its most compelling stretches with Cup group play now layered on top of the regular-season race. Thursday's three games — Cavaliers at Bucks, Timberwolves at Spurs, and Thunder at Rockets — all carry potential swing implications for conference standings. Friday's Cup opener will set the tone for how teams approach the tournament over the next several weeks. Bookmark your calendar: there are a lot of meaningful basketball games on the horizon.
