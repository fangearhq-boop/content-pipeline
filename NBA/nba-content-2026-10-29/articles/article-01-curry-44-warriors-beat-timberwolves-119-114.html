Stephen Curry Erupts for Season-High 44 as Warriors Beat Shorthanded Timberwolves 119-114

By Jake Torres

October 29, 2026

Golden State Warriors

Stephen Curry had a message for the rest of the NBA on Wednesday night: age is just a number, and October is no time to coast. The Golden State Warriors guard erupted for a season-high 44 points as Golden State defeated the Minnesota Timberwolves 119-114 — a win made sweeter by the fact that the Wolves were already hurting before tip-off.

Anthony Edwards, who had just dropped 47 points in his final game before undergoing knee surgery, was nowhere near the court. Minnesota is already navigating life without its franchise player. But even on a good night, it is hard to imagine anyone slowing down Curry when he is locked in the way he was Wednesday.

A Season-High Already

Forty-four points in late October is not supposed to happen — not with a full season still ahead and teams still finding their rotations. But Curry's game has never cared much about the calendar. He attacked relentlessly, drained shots from distances that make defenders shake their heads, and kept Golden State's offense humming even when the Wolves briefly threatened to get back into it.

Rookie Yaxel Lendeborg added 26 points in what amounted to a breakout statement game for the young forward. The Warriors' depth has been a question this preseason, and Lendeborg answered some of it in emphatic fashion Wednesday. With Curry creating chaos and Lendeborg finishing, Golden State proved it has more than one dimension to its attack.

Minnesota's Challenge Ahead

The Timberwolves entered this game already carrying the weight of Edwards' absence. Losing their star scorer and best defender to knee surgery is a significant blow for a team with Western Conference aspirations. Without him, Minnesota's offense lacks a closer — the kind of player who can take over when the game is on the line.

The Wolves have enough talent to stay competitive for stretches. But against a team as well-constructed as Golden State, with Curry playing at an elite level and a rookie stepping up behind him, the margin for error disappears quickly. Minnesota managed to stay within range for much of the night, but the deficit proved too large to overcome.

The road ahead for the Timberwolves without Edwards will be defined by how well the supporting cast can compensate. There is no easy answer, but there are capable players. How they respond to this challenge will be one of the early-season storylines worth following in the West.

What's Next

The Golden State Warriors will look to build on Wednesday's win as the schedule moves into November. For the Timberwolves, Thursday night brings a visit to San Antonio — another difficult road game — as the team begins what could be a two-month stretch without Edwards. Golden State improved to one of the better records in the early Western Conference standings, and Curry's performance on Wednesday serves as a reminder that they are not to be taken lightly. Minnesota has no choice but to keep moving forward without their star.
