Wembanyama's 36-Point, 14-Rebound Night Lifts Spurs Past Jazz 117-111 in Overtime

By Jake Torres

October 29, 2026

San Antonio Spurs

Victor Wembanyama delivered one of the early highlights of the 2026-27 season Wednesday night, posting 36 points and 14 rebounds as the San Antonio Spurs outlasted the Utah Jazz 117-111 in overtime. When the game was on the line in the extra period, the Spurs went on a 12-0 run to seal the victory and claim their third consecutive win.

Three straight. The number feels significant for a franchise that spent much of last season searching for consistency. With Wembanyama playing like a force of nature, the Spurs are starting to look like a team that belongs in the Western Conference conversation.

Overtime Dominance

Regulation ended with the teams deadlocked, and Utah seemed to have the momentum heading into the extra period. The Jazz had battled hard all night and refused to let the Spurs pull away. But overtime revealed the difference between the two teams — and that difference has a name.

Wembanyama took over in overtime with the commanding presence that has made him one of the most discussed players in basketball. His combination of size, skill, and basketball IQ makes him almost impossible to guard in one-on-one situations, and he exploited that advantage at the most critical moment. The 12-0 run San Antonio built in overtime was not just about one player, but it started and ended with Wembanyama doing things that most NBA players simply cannot do.

By the time the run was complete, the Jazz had no time to recover. San Antonio closed out the game and walked away with a win that required everything they had.

A Trend Worth Watching

Three wins in a row is notable in any context, but it carries special weight for the Spurs. After a difficult 2025-26 campaign that tested the patience of fans who remembered San Antonio's championship years, a three-game winning streak in the opening weeks of this season is exactly the kind of early signal that changes the conversation around a team.

Wembanyama was always going to be special. The question entering this season was whether the supporting cast and coaching staff had caught up enough to help him win consistently. These first weeks suggest the answer may be yes. The Spurs are not just losing games they should lose while Wembanyama racks up impressive individual stats — they are competing, fighting, and winning games in overtime against teams that pushed them to the limit.

What's Next

The San Antonio Spurs host the Minnesota Timberwolves on Thursday night — a team coming off a loss to Golden State and without Anthony Edwards. It is exactly the kind of game a surging team needs to capitalize on. For Utah, the Jazz drop another close game in a season that will likely require patience as the team develops. Wembanyama's 36-point, 14-rebound performance will be replayed extensively across NBA broadcasts. But what matters most to San Antonio is keeping this winning streak alive — and Thursday presents a significant opportunity to do exactly that.
