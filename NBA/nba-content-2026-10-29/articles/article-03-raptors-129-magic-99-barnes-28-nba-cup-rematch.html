Raptors Crush Magic 129-99, Set Up NBA Cup Rematch Friday in Toronto

By Damon Pierce

October 29, 2026

Toronto Raptors

Scottie Barnes scored 28 points, RJ Barrett added 25, and the Toronto Raptors sent a loud message to the Eastern Conference by blowing out the Orlando Magic 129-99 on Wednesday night. The 30-point margin was emphatic, and the timing could not be better — because these two teams meet again Friday night in an NBA Cup group-stage game at Scotiabank Arena.

For Toronto, Wednesday was a statement. For Orlando, it was a result that demands a response in less than 48 hours.

Barnes and Barrett in Command

The Raptors have built their identity around Barnes and Barrett, and Wednesday's game was a showcase of what that combination looks like at full strength. Barnes was relentless — attacking the basket, hitting mid-range shots, and defending with the intensity that makes him one of the more complete forwards in the Eastern Conference. Barrett complemented him perfectly from the perimeter and off the drive.

When both players are clicking simultaneously, Toronto has an offense that is difficult to contain. The Magic had no answer for the Raptors' pace and physicality, and the lead grew steadily from the opening quarter. By the time it was clear this would be a blowout, Toronto's starters had made their point.

The 30-point margin speaks to how completely the Raptors controlled this game. There was no late magic from Orlando, no quarter where the visitors threatened to make it interesting. This was a dominant performance from wire to wire.

Magic Facing a Quick Turnaround

Orlando has now lost back-to-back games after an earlier victory at Madison Square Garden over the Knicks. The Magic are a talented team capable of competing on any night — their earlier road win showed that. But consecutive losses, including a 30-point blowout, creates urgency.

The challenge now is responding on the road Friday, in the same building where they just absorbed a beating, with NBA Cup points on the line. That is a significant test of this team's mental toughness and ability to regroup quickly.

NBA Cup Rematch Raises the Stakes

The NBA Cup group stage officially begins Friday, October 30, and the Magic-Raptors rematch is one of the first marquee matchups of the tournament. Group-stage results accumulate over several weeks, with the top teams from each group advancing to the quarterfinals in December.

That means Friday's game is not just a chance for Orlando to avenge Wednesday's loss — it is also a game with tangible implications for the tournament standings. Toronto will be motivated to extend their dominance. Orlando will be motivated to prove this week's blowout was an aberration.

What's Next

The Raptors and Magic meet again Friday night at Scotiabank Arena in what is now the opening marquee game of the 2026 NBA Cup group stage. Toronto will carry 30-point momentum into that matchup, while Orlando has less than 48 hours to reset mentally and physically. Barnes and Barrett will look to carry the energy they showed Wednesday into a Cup game that already carries elevated stakes. The rematch should be a sharp contrast to the blowout — expect Orlando to come out with significantly more urgency on Friday.
