Brunson Scores 38, Knicks Rally From 19 Down to Beat Bulls 133-127

By Marcus Cole

October 29, 2026

New York Knicks

It looked like another painful night for New York. The Knicks trailed the Chicago Bulls by 19 points Wednesday, the kind of deficit that can break a team's early-season confidence before it has time to form. Then Jalen Brunson took over, and the story changed entirely.

Brunson finished with 38 points as the Knicks stormed back to beat Chicago 133-127, earning their second consecutive victory and delivering the kind of win that can shift a team's entire trajectory. The Garden — and the Knicks fanbase watching around the country — exhaled.

Brunson's Statement Night

Comebacks of this magnitude do not happen by accident. They require a player willing to carry the weight of the moment, make shot after shot, and drag teammates back into belief. That is exactly what Brunson provided Wednesday.

Thirty-eight points is the kind of output that commands attention across the league. The Bulls built what should have been an insurmountable lead, and Brunson erased it through will and skill. He drove, pulled up from the midrange, and hit the shots that mattered when the game was in the balance. His fourth quarter was a reminder of why the Knicks built around him.

When a team trails by 19, there is a natural temptation to go through the motions and save energy for the next game. What separates a team with a real star from one without is exactly this: the refusal to accept that the game is over. Brunson made sure New York never accepted it.

Two Straight for New York

The Knicks entered the season with elevated expectations and struggled to meet them early. Their first win came against the Pistons. Now they have back-to-back victories, and the confidence that comes with a dramatic road comeback is genuinely hard to manufacture.

Chicago played well enough to win this game for much of the night. Building a 19-point lead is a significant achievement, and the Bulls forced the Knicks to play at an uncomfortably high level to recover. The problem was that Brunson reached that level, and then some.

New York still has work to do as a team, but a win like Wednesday's is worth a dozen smooth victories. Learning to fight back from difficult positions is a skill, and the Knicks proved they have that skill with Brunson running the show.

What's Next

The New York Knicks return home after Wednesday's win and will be among the teams playing in the NBA Cup group stage beginning Friday. Two consecutive wins, including a dramatic comeback, has the Knicks looking like a team that is finding its identity around Brunson. Chicago will need to regroup after blowing a large lead — the Bulls had the win in their hands and let it get away. For New York, the momentum is real, and the Garden faithful will be watching closely to see if the Knicks can keep it rolling.
