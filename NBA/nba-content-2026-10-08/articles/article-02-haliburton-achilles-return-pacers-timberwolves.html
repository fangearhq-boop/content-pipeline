He's Back: Haliburton Returns From Achilles, Pacers Win 123-112

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | October 8, 2026

Tyrese Haliburton did something on Tuesday night that nobody was sure he could do just a year ago: he played basketball again.

The Indiana Pacers beat the Minnesota Timberwolves 123-112 in their preseason opener at Gainbridge Fieldhouse, with Haliburton making his long-awaited return from the torn right Achilles tendon that cost him the entire 2025-26 season. After the game, the All-Star point guard kept the message simple.

"No limitations," he said.

A Year-Long Road Back

Haliburton's Achilles injury was one of the most significant losses in the Eastern Conference last season. In the year before the injury, he had established himself as one of the most dynamic point guards in the league — a floor general with a gift for creating offense and making his teammates better.

The rehab process was long and deliberate. Torn Achilles injuries are among the most difficult recoveries in sports. The concern was never just whether Haliburton could come back — it was whether he could come back as the same explosive player who terrorized defenses before the injury.

Based on what Indiana's medical staff and coaching staff have seen, the answer is yes. Head coach Rick Carlisle has confirmed that the team will monitor Haliburton's workload carefully this preseason, but that caution is procedural — not a sign of lingering concern.

The Pacers' Ceiling Gets Higher

Indiana navigated the 2025-26 season without their franchise player and still remained a competitive team. The front office made smart moves around Haliburton's absence, developing young pieces and maintaining a system that could function without a superstar point guard running every play.

Now he's back. And the projection for what Indiana can do in 2026-27 changes significantly.

With Haliburton healthy, the Pacers have a legitimate argument as a top-four Eastern Conference team. He stretches defenses with his shooting, controls the pace with his court vision, and elevates everyone around him. In a conference that now features the Knicks (defending champions), 76ers, Celtics and Bucks, getting back to full strength matters enormously.

Why This Win Matters

Preseason results rarely predict regular season outcomes. But beating Minnesota 123-112 — with LaMelo Ball, Anthony Edwards and Rudy Gobert on the other side — is a statement. The Wolves have championship aspirations of their own, and the Pacers went into Gainbridge Fieldhouse and handled business.

More than the result, the night was about what it meant. A crowd that showed up to welcome their point guard home. A player who fought through one of the hardest recoveries in the sport and walked out healthy on the other side. A team that never stopped believing in each other.

What's Next

Haliburton and the Pacers open their regular season on October 21 at the New Orleans Pelicans. He was reportedly "shocked" to learn the game won't be nationally televised — which says everything about how much the league and its fans have missed watching him play.

They won't be waiting much longer.
