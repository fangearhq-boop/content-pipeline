LeBron Watch: The Sixers' Full Lineup Has Yet to Take the Floor Together

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | October 8, 2026

The Philadelphia 76ers have played one preseason game. In that game, they beat the New York Knicks 120-97. Jaylen Brown scored 22 points in 17 minutes on 8-of-13 shooting. The Sixers were good.

But LeBron James, Joel Embiid and Tyrese Maxey all sat.

The full lineup — the one Philadelphia assembled over the summer to challenge for an NBA championship — has yet to share the floor in a game setting. Tonight in Brooklyn, that could finally change.

What the Sixers Have Been Building

Philadelphia's roster construction this offseason was aggressive and calculated. LeBron James, at 41, joined the team in search of one more championship. Jaylen Brown arrived from Boston to provide a second scoring option alongside Joel Embiid. Tyrese Maxey — who developed into a genuine star — now has more help than any point guard in the conference.

On paper, it's one of the most talented rosters in the Eastern Conference. Potentially one of the most talented rosters in recent NBA history. But talent on paper doesn't win games. Chemistry does. Systems do. Repetitions do.

And right now, the Sixers are short on all three — because they haven't been able to get everyone on the floor together yet.

Brown's Debut Was Encouraging

What the Sixers did get out of the first preseason game was an encouraging look at Jaylen Brown. In 17 minutes against the Knicks, Brown went 8-for-13 from the field, including 2-of-3 from beyond the arc, with 2 rebounds, 2 assists and a block.

Brown was assertive and confident from the jump. He attacked the rim, hit pull-up jumpers, and showed the defensive awareness that made him a champion in Boston. The transition from Celtic to Sixer appeared seamless — at least in a preseason environment.

The question is how the entire unit functions. With LeBron, Embiid, Brown and Maxey all needing significant usage, how does the offense flow? Who defers in crunch time? Who takes the big shot?

Those answers won't emerge until all four share the floor.

The October 20 Deadline

Opening Night is October 20 at Madison Square Garden — banner night for the defending champion Knicks. The Sixers are arriving as the most anticipated visitors in years, with their full superstar cast expected to be on display.

Whether they play together tonight in Brooklyn, or save the moment for October 20, one thing is certain: the NBA world cannot wait to see what Philadelphia looks like at full strength.

What's Next

Watch tonight's Nets-Sixers game for LeBron James's preseason debut. Whether he plays or sits, the direction of Philadelphia's preseason will become clearer heading into the final week before the regular season. Every preseason minute now is preparation for the moment that actually matters: October 20 at the Garden.
