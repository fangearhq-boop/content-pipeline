LaMelo Ball's Minnesota Debut: What the Numbers Tell Us

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | October 8, 2026

LaMelo Ball's first game in a Minnesota Timberwolves uniform went about as well as anyone in the Twin Cities could have hoped. In 17 minutes off the bench against the Milwaukee Bucks, Ball finished with 11 points, 4 rebounds and 3 assists — quiet by his standards, but purposeful in the way that preseason debuts need to be.

The Wolves won 116-97. The debut was a success. And now the real questions begin.

What LaMelo Did Right

Ball shot 3-of-6 from the field, converting 2 of his 5 three-point attempts, and knocked down all three of his free-throw attempts. He didn't force anything. He ran the pick-and-roll with Rudy Gobert, found open shooters on drive-and-kicks, and showed the kind of playmaking instincts that made him a perennial All-Star in Charlotte.

What stood out was the chemistry with Gobert. The two connected on multiple possessions, using the lob threat to open up pull-up opportunities for Ball in the mid-range. It's a pairing few teams can replicate, and Minnesota executed it smoothly from game one.

Dwyane Wade noticed. The Hall of Famer praised LaMelo's debut on social media, and when one of the greatest point guards to ever play the game gives you his seal of approval, it carries weight.

The Edwards Question

Anthony Edwards was efficient in the Bucks game, finishing with 14 points, 4 rebounds, 2 assists and 2 blocks. But the most important question of the Timberwolves' season isn't whether Edwards can produce — it's whether two ball-dominant, high-usage scorers can coexist as co-creators on the same team.

The early evidence says yes. Ball defers naturally within a system. He doesn't need the ball in his hands every possession to impact the game. And Edwards has matured enough to understand what it means to share the spotlight with a player of LaMelo's caliber.

The Wolves built a 31-20 lead after the first quarter and closed the game with a dominant 35-21 fourth quarter. Structure and discipline — not just talent — drove the win.

How the Trade Looks Now

Minnesota gave up Naz Reid and a package of picks to acquire LaMelo from Charlotte. At the time of the trade, it was debated heavily. Reid was a fan favorite and a legitimate rotation piece. But the Wolves' front office decided that championship contention required a second creator at the highest level.

One preseason game isn't enough to justify or condemn a trade. But the early signs are that LaMelo Ball fits exactly what this team needed: a playmaker who can operate in traffic, shoot from deep, and make the offense genuinely unpredictable.

What's Next

The Timberwolves' preseason continues as they build toward the regular season. The next step is getting LaMelo more time alongside Edwards and seeing how coach Chris Finch structures the offense when both are on the floor at full throttle.

The Anthony Edwards-LaMelo Ball era in Minnesota is just beginning. If their first game together is any indication, Wolves fans have every reason to be excited.
