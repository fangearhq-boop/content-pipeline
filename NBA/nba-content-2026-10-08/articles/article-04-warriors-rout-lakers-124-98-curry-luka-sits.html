Warriors 124, Lakers 98: Curry Shows Out and the Post-LeBron Lakers Have Questions

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | October 8, 2026

Stephen Curry played under 15 minutes, scored 16 points with 5 assists, and left the building before the final whistle. The Golden State Warriors already had it in hand.

The Warriors topped the Los Angeles Lakers 124-98 in preseason action, winning comfortably without needing their star to play more than a quarter's worth of minutes. For Los Angeles, the result raised the question the entire league is asking: what exactly are the post-LeBron Lakers?

Curry Looks Right

There's always a degree of speculation going into a new season about whether Steph Curry is still Steph Curry. The answer, based on this showing, is an emphatic yes.

Curry was crisp and efficient in limited minutes. He got into the paint, found open shooters, and fired off jumpers from his usual impossible range. The Warriors have built their offense around his gravity for over a decade, and the spacing that creates is still among the most effective in the league.

Golden State wins preseason games differently than most teams — not by running up the score, but by executing a system. That system was evident early against the Lakers, and the lead ballooned quickly without needing dramatic moments.

The Lakers Without Luka

Luka Doncic sat out for Los Angeles, and the absence exposed how much the team's offense relies on him. The new Lakers additions — center Walker Kessler, wing Quentin Grimes, guard Collin Sexton and Matisse Thybulle — are quality NBA players. None of them can replace what Luka provides as an offensive engine.

Without Doncic orchestrating, the Lakers struggled to generate clean looks against Golden State's defense. The attack was fragmented — good players in individual moments, but not a cohesive unit.

To be fair, the Lakers are a work in progress. LeBron James departed for Philadelphia in the offseason, and rebuilding an offense from scratch around Luka — a center in the Western Conference — takes time. But a 26-point loss in a game where the opponent played their starters for under 15 minutes each isn't an encouraging look.

The Real Test Is Coming

The Lakers' regular season opener on October 21 is at home against the Warriors — a rematch that will matter far more than this preseason result. When Luka is healthy and the roster has had time to gel, the picture will look different.

But right now, the Warriors appear to be exactly what they were expected to be: a disciplined, well-coached team with one of the greatest shooters in history still pulling the strings.

For the Lakers, the work is just beginning.

What's Next

Both teams have more preseason games before the regular season begins. The bigger storyline for Los Angeles is Luka Doncic's timeline for his preseason debut — and whether the Lakers can find any cohesion before October 21. For Golden State, it's about staying healthy and sharp heading into what promises to be another competitive Western Conference race.
