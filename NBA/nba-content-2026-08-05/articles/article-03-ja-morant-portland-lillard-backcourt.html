Ja Morant Lands in Portland — Trail Blazers Build a Lillard-Morant Backcourt and Swing for Contention

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | August 5, 2026

The Memphis Grizzlies traded Ja Morant to the Portland Trail Blazers this offseason, and Portland wasted no time building around him. Damian Lillard — back on a three-year deal after being waived by the Milwaukee Bucks — is already there. The projected backcourt of Lillard and Morant is now the centerpiece of a rebuilt Blazers roster and one of the most intriguing story lines in the Western Conference heading into 2026-27.

The trade cost Portland Jerami Grant, Kris Murray, and cash. No draft picks. The Grizzlies, in exchange, receive legitimate role players and enter a full rebuild ahead of schedule — an acknowledgment that the Morant era in Memphis did not produce what they hoped.

The Vision in Portland

Damian Lillard spent a decade building his legend in Portland before his departure. His return, at 36, on a three-year deal after Milwaukee waived him, was already a compelling story. Now add Ja Morant, 26, at the height of his physical prime.

The pairing makes theoretical sense. Lillard is a pull-up shooter from forty feet who commands defensive attention on every possession. Morant is one of the most explosive above-the-rim finishers at the point guard position in the modern era, capable of turning any transition into a highlight. They play different ways. They are not redundant. And in theory, the off-ball movement that Lillard's presence creates would give Morant a cleaner path to the paint than he ever had in Memphis.

Chris Haynes reports the projected starting lineup includes Deni Avdija at forward. The Blazers made the playoffs last season for the first time since 2021. With this backcourt, they are targeting something more significant than a first-round appearance.

The Risk Side of This Bet

Morant is 26 years old and has played 79 games over the last three seasons combined. That is the number that gives every scout pause. When he is on the floor and healthy, he is an All-Star-caliber talent with the kind of explosiveness that cannot be coached or developed — it either exists or it does not. In Morant, it absolutely exists.

But 79 games in three years means injuries and availability have been real concerns, not theoretical ones. He is owed the remainder of a five-year, $197 million contract through 2027-28. Portland is absorbing that obligation and betting that a change of scenery, a veteran partner, and a fresh organizational context can bring out the version of Morant that makes that contract look like a bargain.

Memphis made the opposite calculation. The Grizzlies are starting over, getting back a player of Grant's quality and the financial flexibility to rebuild. They are not saying Morant cannot be great. They are saying their window to build around him closed before it fully opened, and it is time to move in a different direction.

What This Means for the West

The Western Conference is growing more complicated by the day. Golden State is running it back with Curry. The Denver Nuggets remain a threat with Nikola Jokic. Oklahoma City is young and dangerous. Now Portland has added a backcourt that — if healthy — is capable of taking any team to seven games in a playoff series.

The best version of Lillard-Morant is one of the most fun offensive combinations in the league. Two guards who can both create from the perimeter and finish at the rim, sharing the ball for a team that has just rediscovered its playoff identity. The risk is real. But so is the upside.

What's Next

The Blazers will round out their roster around Lillard, Morant, and Avdija before training camp. Portland's first real test will come in the preseason, when Morant's health and his comfort in a new system get their first public look. The regular season tips off in October. If Morant can play 65 games and find his rhythm alongside Lillard, the Blazers will be one of the more dangerous teams in the West. That is the bet Portland just made. Whether it pays off depends, as it always has with Ja Morant, on whether he stays on the floor.
