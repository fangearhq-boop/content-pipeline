Raptors Developing Cold Feet as Kawhi Leonard Investigation Drags On — Still Weeks From Resolution

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | August 5, 2026

The trade was agreed June 30. It is now August 5. It still has not happened.

New reporting from Shams Charania reveals that the Toronto Raptors have developed cold feet as the NBA's investigation into the Los Angeles Clippers drags into its second month with no resolution in sight. The two sides are still weeks away from any settlement, according to Charania. Commissioner Adam Silver has expressed that he is "hopeful" for a resolution before the season, but that window is narrowing.

If the NBA and the Clippers cannot reach an agreement, the case gets handed to a neutral arbitrator jointly appointed by the league and the National Basketball Players Association. That process carries no hard deadline. The Kawhi Leonard trade could still be frozen when training camp opens. It could still be frozen in 2027.

Why the Raptors Are Getting Cold Feet

Toronto's concern is not new, but it has intensified. The Raptors have consistently refused to accept liability for whatever penalties the NBA ultimately assigns to the Clippers as part of this investigation. The trade terms — Kawhi Leonard to Toronto for Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, unprotected 2031 and 2033 first-round picks, a 2027 first-round swap, and two second-rounders — were agreed to before the full scope of the investigation was clear.

What is clear now: the NBA is investigating the Clippers for alleged cap circumvention involving Kawhi's $28 million endorsement deal with Aspiration, a company in which Clippers owner Steve Ballmer personally invested $50 million. Aspiration's founder, Andrei Sanberg, was convicted on separate fraud charges and sentenced to 14 years in prison — a development that complicated the Clippers' position that they were victims rather than architects of any scheme.

The penalties the league could impose are significant: suspension, contract voiding, fines, draft pick forfeiture. The Raptors do not want to be on the receiving end of any of those consequences if they come packaged with the player they are acquiring. Their cold feet are rational.

The Arbitration Scenario

If a negotiated settlement cannot be reached, the NBA and NBPA jointly appoint a neutral arbitrator to hear the case. Unlike the NFL's standard arbitration processes with defined timelines, this mechanism has no hard deadline. The NBPA has made clear it is prepared to demand arbitration if necessary — both to protect its own procedural rights and to ensure the process does not disadvantage players, including Kawhi himself.

Kawhi's 2026-27 salary is $50.3 million. He is currently, technically, still a Los Angeles Clipper. Every day the investigation continues without resolution is another day of uncertainty for his career, his salary, and his team situation.

Silver acknowledged earlier this summer that he had hoped the matter would be resolved before the season. "Hopeful" is doing a lot of work in that sentence. Hopeful is not confident. And confident is not where anyone involved in this case appears to be right now.

The Roster Implications

For the Raptors, the frozen trade means they cannot fully build their 2026-27 roster around what they expect to receive. Ingram, Dick, and the pick assets are committed. But without confirmation of the trade, planning around Kawhi as a Raptor carries obvious risk.

For the Clippers, who claim they are victims of fraud, the investigation continues to prevent them from moving forward with their own rebuild or trade activity related to Kawhi. They are stuck with a $50.3 million obligation and no clear answer on when it ends.

Dillon Brooks, a Clippers teammate, told streamer Neon on July 31 that the trade "won't happen." That may reflect genuine insider information, or it may reflect the frustration of a player watching his team remain in limbo. Either way, it added another public layer to what is already one of the most complicated NBA legal situations in recent memory.

What's Next

The two scenarios from here: a negotiated settlement between the NBA and the Clippers that includes agreed-upon penalties and releases the trade to complete, or a transfer to neutral arbitration with no timeline. Commissioner Silver's "summer resolution" window was optimistic when he said it; it looks even more optimistic now. Training camp opens in late September. Whether Kawhi Leonard enters it as a Raptor, a Clipper, or something in between is a question nobody has been able to answer since June 30.
