Stephen Curry's Extension Window Opens — Warriors Expected to Offer 2-Year, $136.7M Max Deal

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | August 5, 2026

The most important piece of Golden State Warriors business this offseason has a date attached to it now. Stephen Curry becomes eligible for a contract extension on August 6, and the Warriors are expected to put a 2-year, $136.7 million maximum offer on the table. That is roughly $68 million per year and would carry Curry through his age-40 season.

ESPN's Anthony Slater has reported mutual interest from both sides. Curry, for his part, will not request a trade. Team sources told HoopsWire that he will suit up for the Warriors in 2026-27 regardless of the extension timeline, but the formal structure is expected to be in place before the month is out.

One more run in San Francisco. On his terms.

The Warriors Are Betting on Continuity

Golden State's offseason has been defined by one consistent theme: keep the core together. Draymond Green re-signed on a one-year, $27.7 million deal. Gary Payton II returned on a one-year, $3.9 million contract. The Warriors missed out on LeBron James, but they did not panic. They leaned into what they already have.

Locking in Curry at two years and $136.7 million is the final, largest piece of that strategy. It signals that the Warriors believe their window has not closed — that the combination of Curry's gravity-bending shooting, Green's defensive orchestration, and the supporting cast around them can compete at the level this franchise is accustomed to competing at.

Curry shot 42% from three-point range last season. At 38, he remains one of the most dangerous offensive forces in the league — not because he is faster or more athletic than at 30, but because decades of movement, preparation, and basketball intelligence have made his off-ball game as efficient as any player alive. Defenses still have to chase him off screens. They still have to commit to his catch-and-shoot spots. His gravity is unchanged.

What $136.7 Million at 38 Actually Means

The contract would run through the 2027-28 season, when Curry would be 40 years old. That is not a typical arrangement for any player. It is, however, a reasonable bet on a player who has shown none of the decline markers that usually precede those kinds of conversations.

Compare it to what LeBron James signed for in Philadelphia — two years at $7.94 million, well below market value, as a cap-friendly arrangement for both sides. Curry is taking a different approach. He is taking the max because he has earned it, because the Warriors want to pay it, and because committing to Golden State at full value sends a message about where he wants to finish his career.

The formal signing is expected around August 29, once the extension window has been open long enough for the process to run its course. The mutual interest has already been established. The structure is agreed upon in all but paperwork.

The Question for 2026-27

The Warriors return a core of Curry, Green, and Payton II, supplemented by a supporting cast that will need to be filled out before training camp opens. They did not make a transformative addition this summer. They chose depth and continuity over a swing at superstardom.

Whether that approach can produce a deep playoff run depends on Curry's continued health and effectiveness, on Green's ability to anchor the defense at 36, and on whether the supporting pieces find the right roles in a system that has worked for most of a decade. The Western Conference has grown more complicated with Lillard and Morant now in Portland, LeBron in Philadelphia, and Giannis reshaping the Eastern Conference with Miami.

But the Warriors have been written off before. They know what their core looks like when it is healthy and locked in. They are betting two years and $136.7 million that they can get there one more time.

What's Next

The extension window opens August 6. The formal signing is expected around August 29. Training camp opens in late September, when Curry will walk in as the signed, fully committed face of a Warriors team that chose loyalty and continuity over a reset. For fans who have watched this run since 2015, there is something satisfying about that. The most transformative shooter in basketball history will finish his career exactly where he built it.
