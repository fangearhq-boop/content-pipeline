Wembanyama's Paris Camp Is Confirmed — ESPN Has the Full Spurs Roster That Made the Trip to France

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | August 5, 2026

Victor Wembanyama organized it. ESPN confirmed it. The players are in France.

The Spurs teammates who made the trip to Nanterre with Wembanyama: De'Aaron Fox, Stephon Castle, Dylan Harper, Tobias Harris, Harrison Barnes, Carter Bryant, and Jordan McLaughlin. Approximately eight Spurs players total — all there because Wembanyama asked them, and all there weeks before training camp begins, giving this young roster an extended window of voluntary preparation that most NBA teams do not get.

This was Wembanyama's initiative. His idea. His leadership. He is 22 years old and he organized a multinational team trip with eight of his teammates to work on basketball and build relationships in his home country. That tells you something about the kind of leader he is becoming.

The Roster Matters Beyond the Headlines

The names on this trip are not random. Fox, acquired in trade this past offseason, is one of the fastest players in the league and one of the better defensive point guards in the Western Conference. Castle and Harper are the young backcourt pieces who represent the Spurs' future. Barnes and Harris provide veteran experience and winning habits.

The chemistry dimension matters here. The Spurs are attempting to build something with a mix of established veterans, young developing players, and a franchise centerpiece who is already one of the two or three most unique players in basketball history. Voluntary trips like this one — where players train together, eat together, and spend time in an environment away from the normal routine — accelerate the bonding process in ways that standard training camp simply cannot replicate.

The Spurs who went to Paris will arrive at training camp in September knowing each other in a different way than the ones who stayed home.

Wembanyama Has No Offseason

While in France, Wembanyama is preparing for an August schedule that would exhaust most players who were supposed to be resting. He is working with his personal coach Guillaume Alquier and building toward France's national team obligations, which run through the end of the month.

The schedule: exhibitions against Serbia on August 20 in Belgrade and August 23 in Orleans, followed by 2027 World Cup qualifying matches against Slovenia on August 27 and Sweden on August 30. That is four competitive international games in eleven days, at the end of what should be an offseason, for a player who just signed a five-year, $252 million extension.

He left roughly $50 million on the table by signing that extension at the 25% max rather than waiting for the 30% designation. He did it because he wanted to commit to San Antonio, not because he had to. Now he is spending his summer building team chemistry and competing for his country. The financial sacrifice and the personal commitment are consistent with the same message: he is not approaching this career as a business transaction.

The Spurs' 2026-27 Ceiling

Through three seasons, Wembanyama has won Defensive Player of the Year, earned two All-Star selections, made two All-Defensive First Teams, and won Rookie of the Year. He has done all of that on a Spurs team that has not yet broken through in the playoff bracket the way this talent level suggests they should.

Fox's arrival changes the dynamic. Castle and Harper are entering the years when young players take genuine leaps. The veterans provide stability. And Wembanyama, now in Year 4 with a max extension locked in and a team of eight players training together in France this August, looks less like a project and more like the cornerstone of something that is about to arrive.

What's Next

Wembanyama and the Spurs contingent return from France before the end of August. His national team obligations run through August 30. Training camp opens in late September. The Spurs have not made a deep playoff run since the Tim Duncan era — but for the first time in a long time, there is a real argument that the pieces and the leadership are coming together at the same time. This Paris trip is a small data point in that larger story, but data points add up. Ask the Spurs when the postseason starts.
