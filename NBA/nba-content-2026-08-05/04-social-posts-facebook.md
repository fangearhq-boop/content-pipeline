### STORY 2: Kawhi Leonard Investigation — Raptors Developing Cold Feet, Still Weeks Away

**Long-Form Post (11:00 AM ET):**
The Kawhi Leonard trade was agreed on June 30. It is now August 5. It is still not done — and it just got more complicated.

New reporting from Shams Charania reveals that the Toronto Raptors have developed cold feet as the NBA investigation drags on. The two sides are still weeks away from any resolution. Commissioner Adam Silver has said he is "hopeful" for a summer resolution, but that window is closing fast.

If the NBA and the Clippers cannot reach a settlement, the case gets handed to a neutral arbitrator jointly appointed by the league and the NBPA. That process has no hard deadline. Resolution could stretch into 2027.

Kawhi Leonard's 2026-27 salary: $50.3 million. His status at training camp: unknown. His trade to Toronto: frozen.

The core of the case: the Clippers are accused of funneling roughly $28M to Kawhi through Aspiration, a company owner Steve Ballmer had invested $50 million into. Aspiration's founder Andrei Sanberg was convicted and sentenced to 14 years in prison on unrelated fraud charges, which complicated the Clippers' "victims of fraud" defense.

The Raptors won't accept penalty liability. The Clippers can't resolve the case alone. And Kawhi sits in the middle of all of it.

### STORY 3: Ja Morant Lands in Portland — Trail Blazers Build Lillard-Morant Backcourt

**Long-Form Post (1:00 PM ET):**
The Memphis Grizzlies traded Ja Morant to the Portland Trail Blazers for Jerami Grant, Kris Murray, and cash.

Portland already had Damian Lillard — back on a 3-year deal after being waived by Milwaukee. Now they've added Morant. The projected backcourt: Lillard and Morant, two of the most electric point guards of their generation on the same court.

On paper, this is one of the most exciting backcourts in the Western Conference. Lillard's pull-up shooting from distance. Morant's above-the-rim athleticism and finishing. Both at their best when given space to create.

The question, as always with Morant, is health. He is 26 years old and has played only 79 games over the last three seasons due to injuries and off-court issues. He is owed the remainder of a five-year, $197 million deal through 2027-28. Portland is betting heavily on his potential — and accepting the risk that comes with it.

Memphis, for its part, gets legitimate building blocks in Grant and Murray and moves toward a full rebuild. This is the Grizzlies' acknowledgment that the Morant window didn't open the way they planned.

Portland made the playoffs last season for the first time since 2021. With this backcourt, they're swinging for something more.
