Knicks Go 0-2: Tatum's 36 Points Send Defending Champions Into Early Crisis

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | October 24, 2026

The New York Knicks are the defending NBA champions. The banner is up. The rings are on fingers. And they are 0-2 to start the 2026-27 season after falling to the Boston Celtics 107-103 Friday night at TD Garden.

Jayson Tatum made sure of it. The Celtics' superstar posted 36 points, 9 rebounds, and 7 assists — and when the game was on the line in the final 90 seconds, he scored seven consecutive points to slam the door on New York's comeback attempt. The Knicks are now the first defending NBA champion to open a season 0-2 in 15 years.

Tatum Was Everywhere When It Mattered

For three quarters, this game lived up to its billing. The Knicks matched Boston shot for shot, the defenses were engaged, and the atmosphere at TD Garden — rocking for the Celtics' first home game — made every possession feel significant.

Then Tatum took over.

With the Knicks within striking distance late in the fourth, Tatum delivered the kind of closing sequence that separates franchise cornerstones from everyone else. Seven points in the game's final 80 seconds — pull-up jumpers, free throws, and one dagger three-pointer — turned a tight game into a comfortable Celtics win. His 36-point total was his best of the early season, but it was the timing of his production that made it historically significant.

Paul George, now in his first full season with Boston after arriving from Milwaukee in the summer blockbuster trade, contributed on both ends. The Celtics are 1-1 and looking increasingly comfortable with their new identity.

Brunson, Towns, and the Knicks' Slow Start

Karl-Anthony Towns worked for 22 points and was the best Knick on the floor for long stretches. But the story of the night for New York was Jalen Brunson, who finished with 19 points on a difficult 7-for-19 shooting night.

Brunson — the engine behind New York's championship run last spring — has looked a step behind in both games this week. Whether that's conditioning, a minor health issue, or the kind of slow start that stars occasionally navigate, the Knicks need him to find his form. When he struggles, New York lacks the secondary scoring creation to compensate.

Orlando Anunoby gave the Knicks a strong game with 20 points and 10 rebounds, showing the kind of two-way effort that will be required if the Knicks are to right the ship. But it wasn't enough against Tatum at his best.

New York's defense, a hallmark of their championship run, hasn't been as suffocating in these first two games. Boston scored 107 — not an unusually high number, but enough to put pressure on an offense that hasn't been clicking consistently.

The Weight of the Championship

Context matters here. Opening 0-2 doesn't end a season. It barely registers in October. The 2011-12 Heat started slowly before winning the championship. Teams find their rhythm, stars get going, and the standings in late October are a snapshot, not a forecast.

But the Knicks' situation carries a particular weight because of what they are. Defending champions absorb the opponent's best shot every night. Every team circles the game against New York. Every building is louder when they come to town. The Knicks lost at home on opening night, and now they've lost at TD Garden to a Boston team that is motivated, talented, and has historical reasons to want to beat New York.

The defending champion pressure is real. And the first 15 years without a 0-2 start is a historical footnote that will follow this team's narrative until they turn things around.

What's Next

The Knicks return to Madison Square Garden for their home schedule, where the crowd will be louder and the urgency will be higher. Brunson needs to find his shot. Tom Thibodeau will have the defense dialed in. New York is too talented, too proven, and too experienced to stay in an early-season hole.

But they need to respond, and they need to respond quickly. Zero wins in the first week isn't a crisis for a roster this good. Zero wins by the end of next week might be.

For Boston and Jayson Tatum, this is an early statement. The Celtics, who added Paul George to chase another championship, are showing they can win tight games on their home floor. And with Tatum operating at this level, they'll be hard to beat when it matters most.
