Hawks 128, Hornets 81: Atlanta Sets Franchise Record With 47-Point Blowout

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | October 24, 2026

You can't take the regular season calendar and turn it into a championship résumé. But you can make the rest of the Eastern Conference take notice.

The Atlanta Hawks did exactly that Friday night, crushing the Charlotte Hornets 128-81 at State Farm Arena in a performance that ESPN reported set a franchise record for margin of victory. The 47-point win was dominant from tip to horn, and it announced that this Atlanta team is not a fringe playoff contender — it's a genuine force.

Jalen Johnson led the Hawks with 19 points, 8 assists, and 7 rebounds in another multidimensional performance from the player who looks increasingly like one of the East's elite forwards.

Jalen Johnson Is Running the Show

At 23 years old, Jalen Johnson is the most compelling young star in the Eastern Conference who doesn't get enough national credit. In Atlanta's early-season games, he's been everything the franchise envisioned when they committed to him — a high-motor, two-way forward capable of carrying an offense and anchoring a defense.

Against Charlotte, Johnson's 19 points came efficiently. But it was the 8 assists and 7 rebounds that made the performance special — the kind of stat line that says this isn't a player just scoring, it's a player orchestrating. Atlanta's pace, their spacing, their ability to make Charlotte uncomfortable all started with Johnson's vision and decision-making.

The Hawks are built around him, and so far, he is delivering on everything the organization hoped he would become.

Charlotte Has No Answers

Naz Reid led the Hornets with 17 points, but this was not a game where Charlotte found any offensive rhythm. The Hornets were held to 81 points — a number that feels impossibly low in the modern NBA era of pace and shooting volume.

The Hornets are 0-2 and being outscored at alarming rates. In their two losses, they've failed to reach 100 points in one of them and came far short in this one. Charlotte's offensive system isn't generating the quality of looks their roster needs, and defensively they gave Atlanta too much open space.

The Hornets have talent. But two games in, they look like a team that hasn't sorted out how to compete with the East's better rosters. The 47-point margin wasn't a fluke — it was a structural problem. When your opponent is up 30 in the third quarter and playing their starters with purpose, the final margin tells a story.

An Atlanta Statement

The Hawks have been a franchise in search of a consistent identity for years. Trae Young's era was brilliant in flashes but never translated into deep playoff runs. Since the roster rebuild, Atlanta has been more cohesive, more defensively committed, and more capable of beating teams without carrying the entire offensive load on one player's shoulders.

Friday's blowout was the kind of performance that cements a team's reputation early in a season. Other teams in the East won't want to play Atlanta when the Hawks are moving this efficiently. Johnson will get All-Star consideration if these performances continue. The depth is real.

What's Next

The Hawks host the Houston Rockets tonight on a back-to-back. It'll be a different test — a Houston team coming off a win, defensively focused, and with something to prove on the second night of consecutive games. Atlanta gets to play at home with their crowd and their momentum.

For Charlotte, the path forward starts with defensive toughness. The margin of last night's loss is the kind of thing that gets watched on film all week. The Hornets need to find an edge — and they need to find it soon.
