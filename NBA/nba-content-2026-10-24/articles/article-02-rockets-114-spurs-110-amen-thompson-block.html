Rockets 114, Spurs 110: Amen Thompson's Game-Saving Block Seals Houston's Escape

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | October 24, 2026

For a few tense minutes in the fourth quarter Friday night, the Houston Rockets appeared to be on the verge of blowing a lead they'd spent the entire game building. The San Antonio Spurs had clawed back to tie the game at 96, the AT&T Center was electric, and the Rockets' early-season confidence was being tested.

Then Amen Thompson made the play of the game.

Thompson timed a crucial block in the closing minutes — arriving at exactly the right moment to swat away what would have been a go-ahead shot for San Antonio. It changed the game's momentum irreversibly. Houston closed it out with composed late-game execution, winning 114-110 in a tight contest that underscored both teams' competitive spirit in the season's opening week.

Thompson's Defensive Identity on Display

Amen Thompson has always been one of the league's premier defensive players. His length, anticipation, and athleticism give the Rockets something few teams can replicate — a wing defender who can protect the rim, switch onto guards, and make the kind of decisive play that changes games.

Friday's block was Thompson at his best: patient, reading the play rather than gambling, and committing at precisely the moment when the Spurs needed a basket most. The Rockets' defensive identity — that grinding, communicative, switch-everything scheme — runs through Thompson, and moments like this are why.

The block wasn't just a play. It was a statement of who the Rockets are when the game is on the line.

A Spurs Team That Won't Go Away

San Antonio deserves credit for what they put together Friday night. The Spurs erased a double-digit deficit in the fourth quarter and tied the game at 96 — a comeback that required focused execution, disciplined shot selection, and the kind of toughness that has been a Spurs organizational staple for decades.

The Spurs are in an interesting position this season. Cooper Flagg, the No. 1 pick in the 2026 draft who went to Dallas, means San Antonio's path forward runs through player development and smart team basketball rather than a star-driven offense. Friday night showed that approach can compete — the Spurs nearly pulled off the upset.

The four-point margin in the final score masks how competitive this was. San Antonio will be fine.

Houston's Winning Formula

The Rockets won't always score 120 and make it easy. That's not who they are. Houston is a team that wins through defensive pressure, second-chance opportunities, and the kind of gritty late-game execution they showed in San Antonio.

They competed on the road in a tough environment, absorbed the Spurs' best counter-punch, and still found a way to win. For a young roster building toward something larger, those wins are as valuable as any blowout. You learn more about yourself when a game gets tight than when it doesn't.

What's Next

The Rockets play again tonight, visiting the Atlanta Hawks on the second night of a back-to-back. Atlanta just set a franchise record with a 47-point blowout win over Charlotte, so Houston will face a team playing with serious confidence. Managing Amen Thompson's minutes and keeping the roster fresh will be the challenge.

The Spurs, meanwhile, travel to Dallas tonight to face a Mavericks team with something to prove. San Antonio's early-season road schedule isn't doing them any favors, but they've shown they can compete with playoff-caliber teams. That matters for a franchise still finding its identity.
