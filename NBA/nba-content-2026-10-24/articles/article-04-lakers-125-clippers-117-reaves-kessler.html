Lakers 125, Clippers 117: Reaves and Kessler Give LA a New Identity in Derby Win

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | October 24, 2026

The Los Angeles Lakers are 2-0, and the two-game snapshot says something meaningful about who this team is going to be this season.

Austin Reaves posted 28 points and 9 assists Friday night. Walker Kessler added 22 points and 6 blocks. Together, they carried the Lakers past the Clippers 125-117 in the LA Derby — and neither stat line belongs to a player whose name is typically mentioned first when discussing Los Angeles's championship prospects.

That depth is the Lakers' edge. And so far, it is very real.

Reaves: The Most Undervalued Star in the West

Austin Reaves has been making this case for years, and at this point the argument needs no further defending. He is one of the most reliable, multifaceted players in the Western Conference — a guard who defends multiple positions, creates for others at a high level, and delivers in situations that test a player's nerve.

Against the Clippers, Reaves was relentless. His 28 points came with efficiency — he got to the line, made open threes, and attacked closeouts with the kind of controlled aggression that can only be executed by someone deeply comfortable in big moments. The 9 assists were the other half of the story: Reaves as orchestrator, finding open shooters and making second-level reads that broke down the Clippers' rotations.

In a city of stars, Reaves has carved out stardom through consistent, essential play. Friday night was one of his best.

Walker Kessler Announces Himself in LA

The lesser-told story of the Lakers' early success is Walker Kessler, whose 22 points and 6 blocks Friday represented the most impactful individual performance in the LA Derby.

Kessler was acquired this offseason as part of the Lakers' plan to add a physical, rim-protecting center who could anchor the defense without needing the ball to be effective offensively. Through two games, he is doing more than that — he is actively scoring, actively dominating the paint, and giving the Lakers a frontcourt presence that opposing teams haven't seen from Los Angeles in years.

Six blocks against the Clippers is an elite number. But the 22 points is what changes the calculus. Kessler isn't just protecting the rim — he's exploiting defensive mismatches, catching and finishing at a high rate, and reading the offensive glass with the kind of consistency that makes him an offensive threat, not just a defensive anchor.

The Lakers' Depth Is Real

Reaves and Kessler performing at this level doesn't mean the Lakers are a top-five team or a championship lock. It's two games in October. But it does mean their supporting structure is functioning, which is what you need to find out early.

Los Angeles entered this season with high expectations built around a core that had already proved its ability to win. The question — the one every playoff contender faces — was whether the pieces around the core could deliver. Two games in, the answer looks encouraging.

What's Next

The Lakers play their next game at home against the Miami Heat, who were beaten by the Detroit Pistons last night in what was an encouraging start for a Pistons team that showed genuine depth. Los Angeles won't be sneaking up on anyone with a 2-0 record and two dominant supporting performances on the court.

For the Clippers, Friday's loss doesn't define them. They've shown in recent years the ability to compete in the West — but the margin of Friday's defeat, particularly in the paint, is a concern that will require adjustments. The Clippers gave up 6 blocks to Kessler alone. That's a scheme and roster problem, and it will need attention.

For the Lakers, the message is clear: don't sleep on Los Angeles just because the star power is distributed differently than in past seasons. This team is going to compete.
