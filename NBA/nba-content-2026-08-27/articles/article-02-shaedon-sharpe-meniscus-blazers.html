Shaedon Sharpe Tears Meniscus: Blazers' Season Outlook Takes a Devastating Hit

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | August 27, 2026

The Portland Trail Blazers built their entire offseason around becoming a contender. They traded for Ja Morant. They brought in pieces designed to put them back on the NBA's radar. And then, during an individual offseason workout, the plan started coming apart.

Shaedon Sharpe has torn the meniscus in his right knee and will miss approximately six months, per ESPN. The timeline — surgery this week, return around late February or early March — means Portland's most important complementary player will be absent for roughly the first half of the 2026-27 season.

For a team that gave up significant assets to build around Morant and Sharpe, this is not just an injury. It is an alarm going off in a building that was supposed to be launching.

What Sharpe Meant to Portland's Plan

Sharpe, 23, came into this offseason as one of the NBA's most exciting young players. He averaged a career-high 20.8 points and 4.3 rebounds per game in 2025-26 — proof that the No. 7 pick in the 2022 NBA Draft had taken the leap teams hope for from their high-ceiling prospects.

The pairing with Morant was supposed to be electric. Two explosive, dynamic players who can create off the bounce, punish defenses in transition, and play with an energy that bleeds into an entire locker room. Portland's front office imagined a Morant-Sharpe duo as the foundation of years of playoff basketball.

Now the foundation has a crack in it before the first game is played.

Morant Faces the First Half Alone

Ja Morant arrived in Portland as a two-time All-Star and one of the most gifted athletes in the sport. He can carry a team — he has done it before in Memphis. But doing it through the grind of a full first half of an NBA season, while the team waits on its missing piece, is a significant ask.

Portland will need its younger players to step forward during Sharpe's absence. The silver lining identified by some around the franchise is that development opportunities will open up — players who might have had reduced roles will now receive extended minutes to prove themselves.

That is the optimistic view. The realistic view is that the Blazers were building toward playoff contention, and six months of Sharpe's absence makes that ceiling considerably harder to reach in 2026-27.

What's Next

Sharpe is scheduled to undergo surgery this week, with the recovery clock starting immediately after. The six-month timeline is an estimate — recoveries can run ahead of or behind schedule depending on how the procedure goes and how the player responds to rehab.

If all goes well, Sharpe could be back in uniform for a playoff push in February or March. Portland's best-case scenario: Sharpe returns healthy, Morant has kept them in the playoff picture, and the team gets its full-strength run for the final quarter of the season.

Worst case: the first half goes sideways without him, and Portland finds itself on the outside looking in when Sharpe finally suits back up.

The Blazers' big offseason bet just got a lot more complicated.
