Three Days: What Happens When Steph Curry's Extension Window Opens Saturday

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | August 26, 2026

Saturday, August 29 is not just another day on the NBA calendar. It is the first moment Stephen Curry can formally agree to a contract extension with the Golden State Warriors — a two-year, $136.7 million maximum deal that would carry him through his age-40 season. The Warriors want it done. Curry hasn't sent any signals to the contrary. But the most important offseason transaction left in the NBA is still 72 hours away.

Warriors general manager Mike Dunleavy has said publicly that he is "pretty confident Steph will finish his career here," and ESPN's Anthony Slater has confirmed the mutual interest from both sides. The extension was never really in doubt — this is one of the cleanest, most sentimental franchise decisions in the league, a 10-time All-Star choosing to end his career where he won four championships. The drama, to the extent there is any, comes from the wrinkles.

The Windhorst Wrinkle

ESPN's Brian Windhorst floated the unconventional take that Curry should not rush to sign on Saturday. His reasoning: if Curry waits until 2027 and becomes an unrestricted free agent for the first time in his career, the market — and the bidding — could be unprecedented. A healthy, still-elite 38-year-old who shot 42% from three last season on a Warriors team that went 37-45 has demonstrated he is not a player in decline. He is a player on a declining team.

The argument has some surface logic. A 1yr/$62.6M bridge deal has also been floated as a middle path — Curry signs short-term, the Warriors regroup, and both sides reassess in a year when the roster picture may be clearer. Flashscore reported at one point that Curry had reportedly agreed to the one-year structure, though that conflicts with the vast majority of reporting suggesting the two-year max is the expected outcome.

More likely than any of the dramatic alternatives: Curry signs Saturday. He has never shown any indication he wants to play anywhere but Golden State. Dunleavy's public confidence is not a bluff. The Warriors have the cap room and the motivation. Saturday's signing ceremony, if it happens, would close one of the league's longest-running extension storylines and give the Warriors' front office something concrete to build around going into the 2026-27 season.

What's Next

Saturday, August 29 — the moment of truth. If Curry signs, the Warriors move forward with their most important piece under contract through the age-40 season, and the Christmas Day game against the Denver Nuggets becomes a statement about what this team can still be. If he doesn't sign immediately, a new chapter in one of basketball's best franchise relationships opens — one with uncertainty it has never had before. Either way, 72 hours will tell us everything.
