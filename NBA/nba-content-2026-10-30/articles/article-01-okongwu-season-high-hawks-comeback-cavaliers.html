Okongwu's Season-High 29 Powers Hawks' 15-Point Comeback Over Cavaliers

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes

October 30, 2026

Onyeka Okongwu delivered the best game of his season Thursday night, posting a career-high 29 points and 12 rebounds to lead the Atlanta Hawks back from a 15-point deficit and past the Cleveland Cavaliers 114-111.

The victory moved Atlanta to 3-3 on the early season while Cleveland slipped to 3-2 after what had appeared to be a comfortable lead slipped away in the second half.

Okongwu Takes Over

The fourth-year big man has long been one of the NBA's most quietly effective players on both ends of the floor. Thursday, he made sure no one could overlook him. Okongwu's 29-point performance was not merely a stat-line curiosity — it was the performance that defined a game and, perhaps, a turning point in Atlanta's season.

His work in the second half was particularly dominant. When Cleveland's lead evaporated, it was largely because Okongwu refused to let the Hawks stay down. Finishing around the rim, knocking down mid-range looks, and crashing the boards with relentless aggression, he provided exactly the interior anchor Atlanta has always hoped he could be.

The 12-rebound total matched his impact on the glass, giving Atlanta a decided advantage on second-chance opportunities during the critical rally.

The Comeback

Down 15 at its worst point, Atlanta mounted a methodical comeback that reflected the character of a team that has been building toward this kind of resilience. The Hawks tightened their defense, started converting at a higher clip on offense, and eventually took a lead they would not relinquish.

The final margin of three points does not fully capture how thoroughly Atlanta controlled the second half once the momentum shifted. Cleveland, to its credit, pushed back and made it a game late — but Atlanta held firm.

Cleveland's Early Stumble

The Cavaliers entered the night at 3-1 following strong performances to start the year. A loss to Atlanta does not derail a season in late October, but it does serve notice that Cleveland's path through the Eastern Conference will require more consistency.

The Cavs had the game in hand and could not close it out. That is the kind of detail that teams in playoff races cannot afford to repeat.

What's Next

For Atlanta, the win provides a confidence boost heading into NBA Cup group play, which tips off Friday. The Hawks will compete in the Eastern Conference bracket, and a stretch of wins like this one sets the tone for their Cup ambitions.

Cleveland will look to bounce back quickly. The Cavaliers' next games come against winnable opponents, and a return to form should be expected from a team with this much talent. But Thursday proved that no lead is safe — especially when a player like Okongwu is on the floor for the other team.
