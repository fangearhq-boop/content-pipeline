Three Days to Opening Night: Injuries Can't Stop the NBA's Most Anticipated Season Opener

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | October 17, 2026

NBA Opening Night | Season Preview | Injuries | New York Knicks | Philadelphia 76ers

The injury reports are piling up. The Clippers are missing three rotation players. The Nuggets are waiting on MRI results. The Thunder lost a key piece to a season-ending Achilles tear. Around the league, the preseason has taken its toll.

None of that matters on Monday night.

Opening Night is three days away, and the games on October 20 — nine hours of basketball across three matchups on NBC — are still as loaded as any season opener in recent memory. The injury chaos swirling around the league has not touched the teams that matter most on Monday, and the storylines heading into the tripleheader are as compelling as anything the sport has served up in years.

The Banner Goes Up, Then the Game Begins

Before a single basket is scored Monday evening, the New York Knicks will raise a championship banner at Madison Square Garden. It will be their first banner since 1973 — a 53-year wait that finally ended when Jalen Brunson poured in 45 points in Game 5 of the Finals to close out the San Antonio Spurs and end the longest title drought in franchise history.

The ceremony happens. The crowd loses its mind. And then the Philadelphia 76ers walk onto the floor.

LeBron James, 41 years old and in his 24th season, will make his regular-season debut in a Sixers uniform at Madison Square Garden — as the visiting team, in a building that has every reason to boo him. He spent the offseason telling anyone who would listen that Philadelphia can win a championship. Monday night is where he has to start proving it.

Jaylen Brown, traded from Boston to complete the blockbuster deal that put him and LeBron on the same team as Joel Embiid and Tyrese Maxey, plays his first game against his former Celtics teammates on October 20. The Garden will not be a comfortable environment for either of them.

The Sixers-Knicks game tips at 7:00 PM ET. Secondary market tickets passed $2,200 within hours of the schedule release. This is the biggest regular-season game in New York in decades.

The Afternoon Opener and the Night Closer

The tripleheader begins at 3:00 PM ET, when Paul George makes his Celtics debut against the Detroit Pistons. George, who came to Boston in an offseason trade that sent Jaylen Brown to Philadelphia, is 36 years old and walking into what he believes is his best remaining chance at a championship. The Pistons, building around Jalen Duren and a young core, open as a legitimate Eastern Conference team worth watching. It is a historically significant afternoon game — a legitimate appetizer for what follows.

At 9:30 PM ET, the night closes with the matchup that has generated the most anticipation since the schedule was released: the Oklahoma City Thunder at the San Antonio Spurs.

The Spurs ended the Thunder's season in Game 7 of the Western Conference Finals last June, winning in Oklahoma City to reach the Finals. Shai Gilgeous-Alexander, the two-time MVP and the overwhelming favorite to win a third straight award, travels to San Antonio knowing that his first chance at revenge comes immediately. Victor Wembanyama, on a five-year, $252 million extension, will be waiting.

This is the best young-player matchup in professional basketball. It closes the biggest night of the year.

The Injury Picture

The NBA's preseason has been hard on rosters around the league. The Clippers will enter their October 21 opener without Brandon Ingram, Max Strus, and Yanic Konan Niederhauser. The Thunder lost Otega Oweh to a season-ending Achilles tear before Monday's game even tips off. The Nuggets are awaiting MRI results on Julian Strawther's left knee.

But the teams at the center of Opening Night's biggest stories — the Knicks, Sixers, Thunder, Spurs, Celtics, and Pistons — all enter Monday in reasonably good health. The chaos is real, but it is hitting teams whose biggest games come later in the week.

The rosters are intact where Monday needs them to be. Three days from now, the league opens its season the right way.

What's Next

Today's non-guaranteed waiver deadline (4 PM CT) will finalize rosters league-wide. The Knicks still need to announce a decision on their final roster spot between James Wiseman and Bruce Brown. Final injury reports will be released Sunday. And then, on Monday, October 20, the NBA season begins with a tripleheader on NBC starting at 3:00 PM ET.

Everything the league has been building toward since June starts on Monday night. Three days.
