Clippers Enter Season in Triage Mode: Ingram Out (Achilles), Strus Out, and Garland Must Carry the Load

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | October 17, 2026

Los Angeles Clippers | Brandon Ingram | Injuries | Opening Night | Darius Garland

The Los Angeles Clippers will open the 2026-27 NBA season on October 21 against the Sacramento Kings without three of their rotation players — and no clear timeline for when the most important of them will return.

Brandon Ingram has not played a game since April 29, the night he left Game 5 of the Clippers' first-round playoff series against Toronto with what was eventually diagnosed as a partially torn Achilles in his right heel. He had surgery on May 8. Coach Ty Lue gave an encouraging update on October 14, describing Ingram's progress as positive — but offered no return date. Ingram will not be available for the season opener.

He will not be alone on the sideline.

The Depth Chart Hit

Max Strus, a key rotation wing, is dealing with a partially torn plantar fascia in his right foot. He will miss the opening night matchup against Sacramento. Yanic Konan Niederhauser, a reserve big the Clippers were counting on to provide energy and rim protection, will also be unavailable for the opener.

That is three players — including the team's highest-paid offensive player — sitting out before the season has played its first minute of real basketball.

Kobe Sanders is optimistically expected to be available for the opener. Darius Garland, acquired this offseason to run the offense, will shoulder the primary scoring load alongside whoever the Clippers can put around him. Rookie Keaton Wagler is also expected to see significant minutes in the early weeks.

The Ingram Problem

The Clippers are, fundamentally, a team built around Brandon Ingram's ability to create offense from multiple positions. He can score off the dribble, pull up from mid-range, get to the free-throw line, and play the pick-and-roll at an elite level. He is the kind of player a franchise offense runs through.

Without him, Darius Garland becomes the first option in ways the Clippers did not plan for at the start of the season. Garland is a skilled ball-handler and shooter — his ability to run the offense and create for others is genuine. But the gap between Ingram on the floor and Ingram on the sideline is substantial.

The good news, such as it is: Lue's October 14 update was described as reassuring. The Achilles repair process takes time, and a partial tear is meaningfully different from a complete rupture. The Clippers are not treating this as a lost season. They are treating it as a waiting game.

What the Clippers Must Do

The early portion of the schedule will test the organization's patience. Garland and whoever steps into expanded roles must keep this team competitive in the Pacific Division — a competitive conference where falling behind early can be difficult to recover from.

Sanders, when healthy enough to contribute, gives the team another creator. The young pieces around the core will need to develop faster than planned. And the entire organization must hold together long enough for Ingram to return.

The season opens October 21 against Sacramento. The Clippers will be without three players who were supposed to be on the floor for Game 1. That is the situation they are managing — and they will need to manage it well before their most important player comes back.

What's Next

The Clippers open the regular season on October 21 at home vs. the Sacramento Kings. Final injury designations will be released Sunday before the opener. Brandon Ingram's timeline will become clearer as the season begins — the team has declined to give a specific return date. Watch for Garland's early performances as the de facto face of the offense.
