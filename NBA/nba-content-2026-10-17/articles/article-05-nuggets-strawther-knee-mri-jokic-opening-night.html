Nuggets' Strawther Awaits MRI Results — Jokic's Supporting Cast Takes Another Preseason Hit

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | October 17, 2026

Denver Nuggets | Julian Strawther | Injuries | Nikola Jokic | Opening Night

The Denver Nuggets have an injury question hanging over their roster heading into the start of the regular season. Julian Strawther suffered a left knee sprain during the team's October 14 preseason game, and the team planned an MRI to assess the extent of the damage. As of Saturday morning, the results had not been publicly reported.

The Nuggets open their 2026-27 season on October 21 — four days from now — which means Denver will need clarity on Strawther's availability in the very near future.

What Strawther Means to Denver

Julian Strawther is not a household name beyond serious NBA followers, but his value to the Nuggets is real. He provides spacing, three-point shooting, and the kind of low-maintenance production that lets Nikola Jokic operate freely. Around a three-time MVP who draws constant attention from opposing defenses, a player like Strawther — someone who can knock down open shots, move without the ball, and stay out of Jokic's way — is more important than his statistics alone would suggest.

Denver's championship window is tied directly to Jokic's health and production. The three-time MVP is the best player on the floor on most nights he steps onto it. But the Nuggets need reliable contributors around him to maximize that window, and losing rotation players to injury before the season even starts makes the early schedule harder than it needs to be.

The League-Wide Injury Context

Strawther's knee sprain is part of a broader pattern this preseason. The Clippers lost Brandon Ingram back in April and are entering the season without three rotation players. The Thunder just lost Otega Oweh to a season-ending Achilles tear. The Knicks had to waive Drew Eubanks after he suffered a torn pectoral muscle. The preseason, played on tired legs at the end of a long offseason, has been particularly hard on rosters this cycle.

For Denver, the hope is that a knee sprain at Strawther's age — still in his mid-20s — is a short-term issue rather than something that threatens his season. The MRI results will determine next steps. If it is a minor sprain, he could be ready for the opener or shortly after. A more significant finding would push his timeline further back.

What's Next

The Nuggets expect to have MRI results before the end of the weekend. Once the findings are in, the team will determine Strawther's availability for the October 21 opener. Nikola Jokic remains healthy and ready for the start of the season. Denver's full roster status — including Strawther — will be clearer by Sunday's final injury reports.
