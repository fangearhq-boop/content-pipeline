16 Days: Stephen Curry's Extension Window Opens August 29, and the Warriors' Future Hangs on What Happens Next

By Marcus Cole | August 13, 2026 | Hoop Heroes

With the full 2026-27 schedule now public — every game, every date, every road trip — the clock ticking down to August 29 suddenly feels more concrete. That is the day Stephen Curry becomes eligible to sign a two-year, $136.7 million extension to remain with the Golden State Warriors. Sixteen days from Thursday.

A September signing is widely expected. The Warriors have made their intentions clear, Curry has given no indication he wants to leave, and the financial framework is already established. But the window is not yet open, and until it is, there is a version of events where the sport's most decorated three-point shooter enters free agency at 39.

The Historic Numbers

What makes this extension negotiation unusual is that Curry is already being paid more than any NBA player in history. His 2026-27 salary of $62.6 million makes him the first player ever to earn more than $60 million in a single season — a threshold that seemed like science fiction when Curry signed his first max contract with Golden State in the early part of his career. The extension, at $136.7 million over two years, would bring his average annual salary to approximately $68 million per year.

To put that in context: when Curry joined the Warriors in 2009, the entire NBA salary cap was $57.7 million. He will earn more than that in a single season before this contract expires.

What the Warriors Built Around Him

Golden State GM Mike Dunleavy Jr. has been emphatic about the organization's commitment to keeping Curry. He described the Warriors' offseason approach as "super aggressive" and said he was "confident" Curry wants to finish his career in the Bay Area. That confidence has been reflected in the roster decisions around the extension: Draymond Green signed a one-year deal worth $27.7 million to return. Gary Payton II came back on a one-year deal worth $3.9 million.

The core is being maintained. The financial commitment to do so is significant. If Curry signs the extension, the Warriors are looking at two more years of building around the league's most decorated shooter, with everything that entails in terms of cap flexibility and long-term planning.

The Alternative No One Wants to Discuss

If Curry does not sign the extension — if August 29 comes and goes without a deal — he becomes an unrestricted free agent after the 2026-27 season at age 39. That is not a desirable scenario for either side. For the Warriors, it means their franchise cornerstone could leave after a single additional season, and recruiting the next wave of talent around an expiring deal becomes exponentially more complicated. For Curry, it means the entire season plays out under a cloud of uncertainty about his future.

The expectation, from everyone who covers this situation, is that this scenario does not materialize. But the window is not yet open. The schedule is now real, the season is 68 days away, and Stephen Curry's status with the Warriors — already a done deal in the minds of most — gets official confirmation in 16 days.
