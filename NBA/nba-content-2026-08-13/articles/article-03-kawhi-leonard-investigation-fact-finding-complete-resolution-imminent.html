Kawhi Leonard Investigation: Fact-Finding Phase Complete, Resolution Expected Within Six Weeks

By Damon Pierce | August 13, 2026 | Hoop Heroes

The NBA's investigation into whether the Los Angeles Clippers used third-party endorsement deals to secretly funnel money to Kawhi Leonard has entered its final phase. According to multiple reports, the fact-finding portion of the probe — conducted by law firm Wachtell Lipton Rosen & Katz — is now complete. What comes next is the verdict, and league insiders expect it within six weeks.

The Clippers-Raptors trade, which has been frozen since last July, remains on hold. Leonard is still technically a Clipper. The resolution of this investigation will determine whether he becomes a Raptor — or something more complicated.

Two Deals, One Investigation

The probe has expanded beyond its original scope. When the investigation began, the central question was whether Leonard's four-year, $28 million endorsement deal with Aspiration — a tech company that has since gone bankrupt and had financial ties to Clippers owner Steve Ballmer — constituted illegal cap circumvention. That question has not gone away.

But a second deal has now been drawn into the investigation. The company is Daktronics, the publicly traded manufacturer that built the halo scoreboard inside the Clippers' own Intuit Dome. A source told journalist Pablo Torre that the arrangement was "1,000% a way to circumvent the salary cap," and that money was effectively flowing from the Clippers, through Daktronics, back to Leonard. Leonard reportedly did no public-facing work for the company. Daktronics has confirmed through its PR firm that there is currently no active deal with Leonard.

Two separate alleged arrangements. The same basic accusation: the Clippers found ways to pay Leonard more than the salary cap allows.

What the Players' Association Is Watching

The NBPA has been a significant variable throughout this investigation. The union has made clear it would "not hesitate" to demand neutral arbitration if the league moved to punish Leonard without what the association considers sufficient evidence. An arbitration ruling, unlike a league decision, would be subject to a binding appeals panel — introducing another layer of complexity and delay.

The most likely outcome, according to league insiders, is a negotiated settlement: the Clippers face sanctions, potentially including draft pick penalties or fines, while Leonard is cleared to complete the trade to Toronto. Whether Ballmer faces additional consequences for what would amount to a multi-deal scheme against the salary cap remains an open question.

The Trade Waiting on the Other Side

When and if the investigation concludes in Leonard's favor, the path is clear: he would complete his trade to the Toronto Raptors in exchange for Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, and future draft picks. Leonard is owed $50.3 million in 2026-27. He has reportedly expressed confidence that the trade will eventually go through.

Training camp for both franchises opens in late September. If the six-week timeline holds — resolution by late September — the Raptors would receive one of the game's most accomplished two-way players with just enough runway to integrate him before the season. If it runs longer, the frozen trade becomes a training camp distraction for two different teams.

The fact-finding is done. Now the waiting begins in earnest.
