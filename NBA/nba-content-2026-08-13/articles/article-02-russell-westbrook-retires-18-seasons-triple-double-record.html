Russell Westbrook Retires After 18 Seasons — The All-Time Triple-Double Record Belongs to Him Forever

By Marcus Cole | August 13, 2026 | Hoop Heroes

Russell Westbrook announced his retirement from the NBA on Thursday in a video narrated by actor Michael B. Jordan — a fitting send-off for a career that was, in many ways, cinematic. Eighteen seasons. Nine All-Star selections. A 2017 Most Valuable Player Award. The all-time triple-double record. A statistical footprint that will be in the record books long after the players who come after him have come and gone.

He is 37 years old. He chose his exit on his own terms, turning down minimum-contract offers from the Sacramento Kings and Washington Wizards to end a career that never compromised on intensity.

The Career That Defined an Era

Westbrook's numbers demand full attention. He finishes fifth all-time in assists, 14th all-time in points. He won the MVP in 2016-17 by averaging a triple-double for an entire season — the first player to accomplish that feat since Oscar Robertson in 1961-62. He would go on to average a triple-double in multiple seasons, building the all-time triple-double record into territory that may genuinely be unreachable.

Nine times he was selected as an All-Star. Nine times he earned All-NBA honors. He played for six different franchises across his 18 seasons, always bringing the same relentless motor — the same commitment to attacking the game at maximum velocity regardless of circumstance, score, or consequence.

The 2017 MVP Season

The peak of Westbrook's individual career came in the year after Kevin Durant left Oklahoma City. Playing without his longtime running partner, Westbrook did not retreat or adjust downward. He averaged 31.6 points, 10.7 rebounds, and 10.4 assists per game across 81 games. He had 42 triple-doubles in a single season. He was named the NBA's Most Valuable Player unanimously by some accounts, and his performance that year stands as one of the most dominating individual statistical seasons in the history of the sport.

Oklahoma City went 47-35. They lost in the first round of the playoffs. In some ways, that tension — the individual magnificence that sometimes strained team results — was always part of the Westbrook story. He was too good to be a secondary option and too singular to always fit neatly into how teams wanted to play. The results were sometimes magnificent and sometimes maddening, and always memorable.

What He Never Won

Westbrook retires without an NBA championship. That absence will follow his legacy, though it says as much about circumstance as it does about him. He was teammates with Kevin Durant during the Oklahoma City years that seemed to promise at least one title. He was part of a Houston team with James Harden that won 65 games and went to Game 7 against Golden State. He chased the ring in multiple stops, including a year with LeBron James in Los Angeles. It never came together.

He was offered minimum contracts at the end of his career by teams willing to take him as a reserve. He declined both. Some players take that bus seat. Westbrook, characteristically, did not.

A First-Ballot Hall of Famer

There is no debate about what happens next. Westbrook is a first-ballot Basketball Hall of Famer — among the most automatic selections the sport has produced in years. The triple-double record alone would make him a Hall of Famer. The MVP, the All-Star appearances, the career scoring and assists totals make him one of the most decorated players of his generation.

Russell Westbrook played 18 seasons of NBA basketball exactly the way he wanted to. That is worth something. The record books will carry him forward whether the conversation catches up or not.
