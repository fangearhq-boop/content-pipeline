James Harden Signs 3-Year, $97M Deal With Cleveland Cavaliers

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | August 21, 2026

James Harden is a Cleveland Cavalier.

The 10-time All-Star has agreed to a three-year, $97 million contract with Cleveland, including a player option for the 2028-29 season — a deal that arrives alongside a busy stretch of roster construction for a franchise that has steadily built toward Eastern Conference contention.

The Harden signing is part of a broader wave of Cleveland moves that also brought Peyton Watson to the Cavaliers via a three-team deal with the Denver Nuggets and Los Angeles Clippers, and Cam Whitmore via a separate trade with the Washington Wizards. In one week, the Cavaliers' roster has changed significantly.

The Full Picture of Cleveland's Moves

The three-team deal that brought Watson to Cleveland worked like this: Watson goes to the Cavaliers, Max Strus goes to the Clippers, and Denver picks up an unprotected 2031 Cavaliers first-round pick plus a 2032 Sacramento Kings second-rounder. It's the kind of multi-team logistics that the NBA offseason specializes in — everyone gets something, the star player (Watson) moves to a team competing for a championship, and the rebuilding team (Clippers) receives a rotation piece and depth.

Separately, Cleveland sent Tre Mann to the Washington Wizards in exchange for Cam Whitmore and a 2027 second-round pick. Whitmore, 21, gives the Cavaliers a young wing who can develop alongside the organization's core. The Mann move allowed Cleveland to absorb the deal without significant cap complications.

What Harden Brings

At this stage of his career, Harden is not the ball-dominant isolation scorer who won the scoring title multiple times. He is, however, one of the more skilled initiators in the league — a player who can create his own shot, find open teammates, and attack the basket in pick-and-roll situations in ways that create real problems for defenses.

His fit alongside Cleveland's core will be the defining question. The Cavaliers have built around multiple ball handlers and shooters; Harden's ability to play off others, combined with his shot creation off the bounce, gives the team a genuine offensive option late in games when plays break down and improvisation becomes necessary.

The $97 million over three years (with player option in Year 3) reflects a market that values Harden's specific skill set at this stage of his career — elite enough to command a significant deal, realistic enough that Cleveland isn't mortgaging its future on a player past his prime. The player option gives Harden leverage without committing Cleveland to a long-term guarantee if his production declines.

The Broader NBA Landscape

With Cleveland actively building, the Eastern Conference's competitive picture grows more complicated. Meanwhile, elsewhere around the league, the Dallas Mavericks currently have 16 players under guaranteed contracts and must shed one before the season — a roster crunch situation that may force them into the trade market. Reports also indicate that Dallas is exploring potential trades of Klay Thompson, who has reportedly expressed openness to a return to the Los Angeles market.

The offseason, despite being deep into August, remains very much alive.

What's Next

The Cavaliers open training camp on September 29. Harden will begin integrating with Cleveland's roster and coaching staff as the team finalizes its pre-season preparations. Cleveland's preseason schedule will provide an early look at how their new acquisitions fit together. The regular season opens October 20, with the Cavaliers and the rest of the Eastern Conference aiming to challenge the league's best when the games count.
