Stephen Curry's $136.7M Extension Is 8 Days Away — And It's Already a Done Deal in Everything But Name

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | August 21, 2026

Eight days from now, Stephen Curry will be eligible to sign a contract extension with the Golden State Warriors. And everything that has been reported since the offseason began points in one direction: he will sign it.

The deal being discussed is a two-year, $136.7 million maximum extension — an offer that would carry the 38-year-old Curry through his age-40 season and cement his status as a Warrior for the foreseeable future. ESPN's Anthony Slater, the reporter closest to Curry and the Warriors organization, has confirmed that mutual interest remains strong. Warriors GM Mike Dunleavy Jr. has been unambiguous: the franchise will offer Curry whatever he wants, up to the maximum.

It isn't signed yet. Until August 29, it can't be. But the posture on both sides is clear.

The Terms Under Discussion

The primary structure being discussed is two years at $136.7 million — the maximum available to the Warriors. This is not a hometown discount. The Warriors aren't asking Curry to take less; they're offering full max, full stop.

A one-year option at roughly $62.6 million has also come up in reporting as a possible alternative structure. That would give Curry more flexibility — the ability to re-evaluate his situation after one more season and, theoretically, enter the market again if circumstances change. The two-year max, though, remains the expected outcome based on available reporting.

Either way, Dunleavy's public posture has been consistent: Curry picks the terms; the Warriors follow his lead. That kind of deference to a player's preferences is rare in NBA negotiations and reflects the franchise's recognition that Curry's legacy and their own are permanently intertwined.

What's at Stake if It Doesn't Happen

The stakes on the unlikely downside are genuinely historic. If no extension is signed — if August 29 passes, then September, then the season, then next summer — Stephen Curry would become an unrestricted free agent in 2027 for the first time in his career. He has never been a free agent. He has never worn another NBA uniform. The idea of Curry testing the open market at 39 years old is not something either side appears to want to contemplate.

Nobody in the basketball establishment expects that to happen. The Warriors are not going to let it happen. Curry is not signaling any desire to force it. But the stakes are real, which is why August 29 functions as a soft deadline — not because the deal might fall apart, but because the business of basketball requires paperwork to be signed on a specific date, and the basketball world will collectively exhale when that day arrives.

What This Means for the Warriors

Golden State finished 37-45 last season — a campaign that reflected the limitations of a roster in transition following various offseason moves. Curry's continued presence as the franchise centerpiece is not just symbolically important; it's the anchor around which the Warriors will build their next competitive run. At 38, playing 42 percent from three-point range, he remains elite.

The Warriors failed in their reported pursuit of LeBron James during the offseason, which adds urgency to their relationship with Curry. He is, now more than ever, the entirety of what Golden State projects to the world. The extension isn't just a contract — it's a statement of direction.

What's Next

The extension window opens August 29 — eight days from today. Expect an announcement on or very shortly after that date. The Warriors and Curry have exchanged enough public signals that the signing will likely be a celebration, not a negotiation. Golden State opens training camp on September 29. Curry's Christmas Day game — Nuggets vs. Warriors — is already circled on the calendar.
