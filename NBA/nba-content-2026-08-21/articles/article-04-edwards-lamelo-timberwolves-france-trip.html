Anthony Edwards Flew the Whole Timberwolves Roster to France. That Tells You Everything.

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | August 21, 2026

Anthony Edwards could have spent August doing what most NBA stars do: workouts at a private facility, some light public-facing content, maybe a few sponsor appearances. Instead, he organized a trip to France for his entire Minnesota Timberwolves roster.

Workouts. Sightseeing. Team bonding. The whole thing.

It's a detail that might seem trivial — rich athletes taking a nice trip — but it reveals something real about how Edwards views his role on this team and what he's trying to build alongside his new backcourt partner, LaMelo Ball.

What the Trade Actually Means

The Timberwolves acquired LaMelo Ball from the Charlotte Hornets earlier this summer in a trade that cost Minnesota significantly: Naz Reid, a 2033 unprotected first-round pick, three first-round pick swaps (2028, 2029, 2030), and three second-round picks (2029, 2032, 2033).

That's a steep price. Josh Green came along in the deal as well, providing some wing depth. But the message of the trade was clear: Minnesota is betting on this pairing of Edwards and Ball, and they're willing to pay now for the chance to win now.

The basketball logic is sound. Edwards finished last season with the most clutch points in the league — 157 — a number that reflects how much his team leaned on him when games were on the line. Ball's playmaking and gravity as a scorer can take pressure off Edwards in those moments and create a system where Edwards operates more effectively as an off-ball weapon, cutting and spotting up from positions where his shooting is lethal.

Edwards in His Own Words

When asked about championship expectations following the Ball acquisition, Edwards gave an answer that was more measured than his usual boldness might suggest: "Right now, I don't think we're thinking about winning the championship. It's step by step. We got LaMelo, a new player, and a bunch of new pieces. So, we are just trying to build it together and I think we're trying to gel it all together."

That's a different Edwards than the one who, just a year ago, declared publicly that the Timberwolves would beat the Oklahoma City Thunder and San Antonio Spurs. He's still confident — this is Anthony Edwards — but the France trip and the measured public statement suggest a player who understands that adding a star and immediately winning a championship requires things to actually work on the court.

Building chemistry takes time. Taking your team to France in August is one way to accelerate it.

The Question Analysts Are Asking

The pairing of Edwards and Ball addresses one problem — offensive creation depth — while creating potential questions about another: wing depth. Analysts have noted that Minnesota's wings behind the two stars may not be sufficient to compete with the deepest teams in the Western Conference, particularly the Oklahoma City Thunder and the San Antonio Spurs, who play a brand of basketball that stresses opponents across every position.

Josh Green provides some versatility, and the front office may not be done making moves. But as currently constituted, the Wolves have invested enormous assets in their top two players and are asking their depth to hold up in the postseason against elite competition.

That's the bet. Edwards and Ball together, at their best, is a bet worth making. Whether the supporting cast is sufficient is the storyline that runs through training camp and into October.

What's Next

Minnesota Timberwolves training camp opens September 29. The Edwards-Ball backcourt will begin formal on-court integration, and the team's coaching staff will work on building the system that best unlocks both players. The France trip is part of the foundation. The basketball will tell the rest of the story.
