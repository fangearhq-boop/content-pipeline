Jokic Outduels Wembanyama: Serbia Edges France 90-87 in Belgrade

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | August 21, 2026

Victor Wembanyama wanted this. He said so publicly — that he doesn't miss the NBA right now, that he misses FIBA basketball. Wednesday night in Belgrade, FIBA basketball gave him a firm lesson in the form of Nikola Jokic.

Serbia defeated France 90-87 in a tightly contested exhibition at the Belgrade Arena, with Jokic delivering one of the more complete individual performances of the international summer: 20 points, 9 rebounds, and 8 assists in just 29 minutes. Wembanyama, making his debut as France's captain in this FIBA World Cup 2027 qualifying window, managed 10 points and 4 rebounds while committing 6 turnovers in 24 minutes.

The result stings for France. The opportunity to respond arrives Saturday in Orleans, when the two sides meet again in the second of their back-to-back exhibitions.

Jokic's Statement Game

Nikola Jokic has three NBA MVP awards and is widely regarded as one of the two or three best players in the world. On Wednesday night, he put that résumé on display against the player most often mentioned alongside him in those conversations.

Jokic's stat line — 20/9/8 in 29 minutes — undersells his influence. He controlled the game's rhythm from the opening tip, moving Serbia's offense with the passing wizardry that makes him so difficult to guard. When France collapsed into the paint to contain him, he found cutters. When France went under screens, he stepped into mid-range jumpers. When they went over, he punished them from deep.

Nikola Milutinov provided Serbia with a reliable secondary option, scoring 15 points on 5 rebounds. Aleksa Avramovic added 13 points, 5 assists, and 3 steals off the bench — giving Serbia consistent production at multiple positions. The Serbs were disciplined, physical, and organized throughout.

A Rough Debut for France's Captain

The moment carried weight. Victor Wembanyama, 22 years old and in his first France appearance since the 2024 Olympic final silver medal, had been named captain for this qualifying window — a statement of confidence from the French basketball federation in their most important player.

It did not unfold the way anyone in France had hoped. Serbia's physicality and help defense contained Wembanyama in a way that few teams manage in the NBA. The 6 turnovers were the most damaging aspect — several came on drives where Serbia's rotations were simply better organized than what Wemby encountered. He produced a spectacular windmill dunk that immediately began circulating on social media, a reminder that even an off night from Wembanyama carries highlights. But on balance, Serbia's game plan succeeded.

Amine Noua was France's most efficient scorer, posting 14 points on 6-of-9 shooting from the field. Sylvain Francisco contributed 13 points. The supporting cast performed; the star did not.

Context and What It Means

A loss in an August exhibition does not define a team or a player. France head coach Frederic Fauthoux is working with Wembanyama for the first time in an official capacity — this window is about building chemistry and testing systems as much as winning basketball games. That context matters.

It also matters that France's schedule still includes games against Slovenia (August 27 at home) and Sweden (August 30 away) — both FIBA World Cup qualifier matches where results count in the standings. The Serbia exhibitions are preparatory; the qualifier results are what move France through the bracket.

And then there is Saturday's rematch in Orleans. Serbia leads the two-game mini-series 1-0. France plays on home soil. Wembanyama will have had 48 hours to review the film from Wednesday and adjust. The basketball world will be watching.

The Bigger Picture

For NBA fans, the Wemby-Jokic matchup in an August exhibition is a gift — the kind of thing that happens only rarely outside the Olympics. Two of the most gifted big men in the history of the sport, competing at full intensity under international rules, in front of a packed Belgrade crowd that was fully invested in every possession.

Wednesday night belonged to Jokic. Saturday gives Wembanyama a chance to answer.

What's Next

France and Serbia meet again Saturday, August 23, in Orleans — the rubber match of their back-to-back exhibitions. France then opens formal World Cup qualifying play at home against Slovenia on August 27, followed by a trip to Sweden on August 30. Wembanyama's NBA season begins when the San Antonio Spurs open training camp on September 29.
