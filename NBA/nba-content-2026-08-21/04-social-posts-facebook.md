### STORY 1: Serbia vs. France — Jokic Outduels Wembanyama

**Long-Form Post**
Window: 11:00 AM ET

Nikola Jokic just put the basketball world on notice.

In Victor Wembanyama's debut as France's captain, Jokic stepped up and delivered one of the great international performances of the summer — 20 points, 9 rebounds, and 8 assists in just 29 minutes as Serbia edged France 90-87 in Belgrade last night.

Wembanyama — who was named captain for this FIBA World Cup qualifying window — struggled with Serbia's physicality, managing only 10 points and 4 rebounds while committing 6 turnovers in 24 minutes. He did produce a spectacular windmill dunk that's already circulating everywhere, but the Spurs star simply wasn't his usual self.

Amine Noua led France's offense with 14 points on 6-of-9 shooting. Sylvain Francisco added 13. For Serbia, Nikola Milutinov gave Jokic excellent support with 15 points and 5 rebounds; Aleksa Avramovic added 13 with 5 assists.

The two sides have a quick turnaround — they meet again Saturday in Orleans, France (August 23). Then France moves to World Cup qualifiers: home vs. Slovenia on August 27 and away at Sweden on August 30.

One thing is certain: Jokic-vs.-Wemby has now become one of the marquee individual matchups in international basketball. The rematch on Saturday feels must-watch.

### STORY 2: Kawhi Leonard — Forbes Salary-Cap Loophole Warning

**Long-Form Post**
Window: 1:00 PM ET

The Kawhi Leonard investigation just got a new wrinkle — and it could affect the entire NBA.

Forbes published a detailed analysis this week warning that however the NBA rules on the Clippers' alleged salary-cap circumvention, the decision could create a precedent — essentially a loophole — that other teams exploit down the road.

Here's where things stand: Outside law firm Wachtell Lipton has completed its fact-finding on two deals under scrutiny — a $28 million agreement with Aspiration (a fund Clippers owner Steve Ballmer personally invested in) and a deal with Daktronics, the scoreboard manufacturer that supplied Intuit Dome's jumbotron. The NBA has already cleared Leonard personally — there's no evidence Ballmer funneled money directly to him.

Sanctions against the Clippers are widely expected. The pending trade — Leonard to the Toronto Raptors for Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, and draft picks — is expected to clear once the investigation concludes. Sources say Commissioner Adam Silver is hoping for resolution before the 2026-27 season begins, though ESPN has raised the possibility the case drags into 2027 if parties dispute the findings.

Toronto Raptors training camp opens September 28 in Quebec City. They are operating as if the trade completes.

### STORY 3: Stephen Curry Extension — 8 Days

**Long-Form Post**
Window: 4:00 PM ET

Eight days.

That's all that stands between Stephen Curry and his next contract as a Golden State Warrior.

Curry becomes eligible to sign a contract extension on August 29. The expected structure: two years, $136.7 million — the maximum the Warriors can offer — which would carry the 38-year-old through his age-40 season. ESPN's Anthony Slater has confirmed mutual interest on both sides, and Warriors GM Mike Dunleavy Jr. has made clear the team will offer Curry whatever he wants, up to the max.

The stakes are real: if no extension is reached, Curry becomes an unrestricted free agent in 2027 — the first time in his career. Nobody truly expects that to happen, but the date matters.

### STORY 4: Edwards and LaMelo — Timberwolves Go to France

**Long-Form Post**
Window: 7:00 PM ET

Anthony Edwards didn't wait for training camp to start building chemistry with LaMelo Ball.

He flew the entire Minnesota Timberwolves roster to France.

Workouts, sightseeing, team bonding — the whole deal. And it's exactly the kind of move that makes you believe in what this team is trying to build. When you add a star-level backcourt partner and your reaction is to immediately invest in togetherness, that's a different kind of team culture.

The trade sent LaMelo Ball and Josh Green from the Charlotte Hornets to Minnesota in exchange for Naz Reid, a 2033 unprotected first-round pick, three first-round pick swaps, and three second-rounders. The cost was steep — but the Wolves clearly believe it's worth it.

Edwards was characteristically measured when asked about championship expectations: "Right now, I don't think we're thinking about winning the championship. It's step by step. We got LaMelo, a new player, and a bunch of new pieces. So, we are just trying to build it together."

Analysts are high on the Edwards/Ball backcourt — pointing to LaMelo's playmaking enabling Edwards to operate more as an off-ball weapon — though questions remain about wing depth behind them.

Training camp opens September 29. The Wolves can't get here fast enough.

### STORY 5: James Harden Signs With Cleveland Cavaliers

**Long-Form Post**
Window: 9:00 AM ET

James Harden is officially a Cleveland Cavalier.

The 10-time All-Star has agreed to a three-year, $97 million contract with the Cavaliers, with a player option for the 2028-29 season. The move is part of a flurry of Cleveland activity that also saw the Cavaliers acquire Peyton Watson in a three-team deal involving the Denver Nuggets and Los Angeles Clippers.

In that deal: Watson goes to Cleveland, Max Strus goes to the Clippers, and Denver picks up an unprotected 2031 Cavaliers first-round pick plus a 2032 Sacramento Kings second-rounder. Separately, the Cavaliers sent Tre Mann to the Washington Wizards for Cam Whitmore and a 2027 second-round pick.

Harden brings high-level shot creation and playmaking experience to a Cavaliers team that has steadily built toward contention. The Cavaliers are making no secret of their ambitions this offseason.

Meanwhile, around the league: the Dallas Mavericks currently have 16 guaranteed contracts and must shed one, and there's buzz that they could explore trading Klay Thompson, who has reportedly expressed openness to a return to Los Angeles.

The NBA's offseason roster chess match is very much still live.
