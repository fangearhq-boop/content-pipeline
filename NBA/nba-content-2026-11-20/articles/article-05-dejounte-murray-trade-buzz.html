Dejounte Murray Wants Out of New Orleans — But His Trade Market Is Lukewarm

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | November 20, 2026

Dejounte Murray has requested a trade from the New Orleans Pelicans. The ask is official. The market, however, is not responding with enthusiasm. Per multiple reports, the Miami Heat and Phoenix Suns have emerged as the teams most loosely connected to Murray's name, but no meaningful conversations have taken place. The word most often used to describe his market right now: lukewarm.

What Murray Brings — and What He Costs

Murray is a legitimate two-way guard. He defends, creates, distributes, and can score. In his prime seasons, he was an All-Star and one of the more complete backcourt players in the NBA. His value is real.

So is his contract. Murray signed a max extension with New Orleans, which means any team acquiring him takes on a significant, long-term salary commitment. That is the wall every interested team runs into. In a league where roster flexibility is prized, absorbing a max deal for a player who is very good (but not transcendent) requires a specific kind of urgency that most teams do not feel in mid-November.

The Heat have been building toward contention on the back of their recent winning streak. Giannis Antetokounmpo and Bam Adebayo give them a foundation. Adding Murray would be additive — but would it be worth the price? The Suns ask the same question from a roster that has been in flux.

Zion in the Background

Murray is not the only Pelican whose future is uncertain. Zion Williamson's situation in New Orleans is equally ambiguous. The organization is managing two of its highest-profile players through trade speculation simultaneously, which is never a comfortable position.

Whether the Pelicans are genuinely trying to build around a core or quietly beginning a teardown is a question the front office has not answered publicly. The answers to those questions will determine what kind of return they pursue for Murray — if and when a deal materializes.

Patience Is the Play

The NBA trade deadline is not until February. Teams rarely pull the trigger on major deals in mid-November — the season is too young, rosters have not been fully evaluated, and the costs are too uncertain. Murray's situation will evolve as the season progresses. If the Pelicans continue to struggle and Murray's discontent becomes more visible, the market will heat up.

For now, it is a story worth monitoring but not one demanding immediate resolution. Murray wants out. The league is listening — just not rushing to the phone.
