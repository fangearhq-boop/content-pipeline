KAT Silences Target Center: Knicks Edge Timberwolves 102-99

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | November 20, 2026

Karl-Anthony Towns walked back into Target Center for the first time since his trade to New York and did exactly what you would expect from a player with something to prove. He scored 22 points, grabbed 12 rebounds, and blocked three shots. The New York Knicks held on to beat the Minnesota Timberwolves 102-99, completing their third straight win.

LaMelo Ball had a chance to tie the game in the final seconds with a triple from the corner. It didn't go. Towns walked off the court with a Knicks win.

The Return

Towns spent nine years in Minnesota. He was the No. 1 overall pick in 2015, grew up in the system, and became one of the franchise's most recognizable faces. His trade to New York last season was seismic — a franchise-altering move for both sides.

Thursday's visit to Minneapolis was his first return trip. The crowd at Target Center had a lot of feelings. Towns appeared to have none. He scored 17 points in the first half, built the lead, and went 1-of-5 in the fourth quarter when things got tight. It didn't matter — his first-half work had already done the damage.

When asked about the moment after the game, Towns was characteristically even-keeled. The win spoke louder than any quote anyway.

Brunson Steadies the Ship

Jalen Brunson scored 21 points and dished 7 assists, providing the steady floor leadership New York needed when the game tightened. OG Anunoby added 15 points, 7 rebounds, and 2 blocks.

The Knicks blew a 19-point lead and were forced to grind out the final minutes. That kind of resilience — the ability to protect a lead that looked comfortable and then survive when it evaporated — is the mark of a mature roster. This New York team has that quality.

Minnesota's Stumble

The Timberwolves entered Thursday at 11-5, one of the stronger records in the West. They didn't play badly — Jonathan Kuminga returned from a two-game absence to score 23 points, and the Wolves battled all the way to the final possession. But LaMelo Ball's shooting woes (6-of-21 from the field) and the missed late triple were the difference.

Minnesota is still a very good team. This was a game decided by one shot, and the wrong player was left holding the ball at the wrong time. At 11-6, they will bounce back quickly.

Knicks at 9-6

Three straight wins. Towns is finding his footing in New York. Brunson remains one of the most reliable closers in the Eastern Conference. The Knicks are not a perfect team — the 19-point blown lead proves that — but they are a winning one right now.

The revenge subplot is closed. The result is what stays. KAT went back to Minnesota, and New York came home with the win.
