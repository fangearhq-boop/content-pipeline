Grizzlies Roll Warriors 126-98 for Fifth Straight Win; Curry Out With Oblique Tear

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | November 20, 2026

The Memphis Grizzlies are 12-4. That sentence deserves to be repeated, because the basketball world has not fully absorbed it yet. On Thursday night, Memphis ran through a Stephen Curry-less Golden State Warriors team to the tune of 126-98, completing a five-game winning streak and cementing their place as one of the most dangerous teams in the Western Conference.

Jaylen Wells led five Grizzlies in double figures with 22 points. Cedric Coward added 19, Cam Boozer and Olivier Maxence-Prosper each had 16, and Javon Small contributed 14. Memphis shot 52.2% from the floor and finished with 34 assists — the hallmarks of a team that plays basketball the right way.

Memphis Without Morant

Ja Morant was traded to Portland in June. For many observers, that trade signaled a rebuild, a step back, a bridge year. Memphis did not receive that memo.

The Grizzlies have constructed a roster built on collective depth rather than a singular star. Wells, Coward, Boozer, Small — none of them is a household name yet, but all of them can play. When a team shoots 52% from the field and distributes the ball like Memphis does, it does not need a transcendent talent to win games. It needs execution, and the Grizzlies have it right now.

At 12-4, they are tied for the third-best record in the NBA, neck-and-neck with the Rockets in the West's upper tier. Five straight wins. A new identity. Memphis Grind City lives on, just with different names on the jerseys.

Golden State in Crisis

Stephen Curry did not play. He is dealing with a right oblique tear — an injury that carries no guaranteed timeline and a track record of lingering longer than expected. The Warriors finished Thursday at 8-8, and the gap between where they expected to be and where they actually are is growing.

Without Curry, Golden State shot 41.3% from the floor and 5-of-29 from three. Those numbers tell the full story. Charles Bassey did his part with 16 points and 13 rebounds, and Brandin Podziemski and Will Richard each had 15. But a team built to thrive when Curry is on the court simply does not function at the same level without him.

The Warriors need clarity on Curry's timeline. At 8-8, they can still right the ship, but falling further back while Curry is sidelined would create a hole that is very difficult to dig out of.

What to Watch

Memphis plays three of their next four on the road. How they handle adversity away from home will define whether this is a true contending run or a hot start in front of a forgiving schedule. If the Grizzlies stay above .700 heading into December, every team in the West will need to adjust their thinking.

For Golden State, the question is simpler: when does Curry come back, and how much ground do they lose before he does?
