Spurs 99, Lakers 96: Castle 26, Wembanyama 25/16 as San Antonio Steals West Wild Card

By Damon Pierce

November 28, 2026

The San Antonio Spurs are in the NBA Cup quarterfinals. Stephon Castle dropped 26 points and Victor Wembanyama added 25 points and 16 rebounds as San Antonio rallied to beat the Los Angeles Lakers 99-96 on Black Friday, clinching the West wild card and ending Los Angeles' Cup run in the process. The win sends the Spurs to Dallas on Dec. 4 to face the only 4-0 team in group play.

Castle was the story from start to finish. The second-year guard delivered in a Cup-clinching moment under national spotlight — the kind of performance that accelerates a career. His 26 points provided the offensive foundation that allowed San Antonio to withstand whatever the Lakers threw back at them in the closing minutes.

Castle Is the Real Deal

Stephon Castle was selected in the lottery just two drafts ago. He already plays like a veteran. The poise he showed in a three-point game against an experienced Lakers roster — a team with championship-level talent and playoff experience — went beyond what most expect from a second-year player in a pressure situation.

Castle did not simply get hot from three or find easy looks. He created his own offense, operated in pick-and-roll situations with the efficiency of a primary ball-handler, and hit the shots when the Spurs needed them most. San Antonio's coaching staff has been accelerating his development, and moments like Friday night's Black Friday Cup closer are the reason.

Playing alongside Wembanyama, Castle also benefits from the floor-spacing and rim-protection advantages that come with having the sport's most unique player as a co-star. The pairing looks increasingly like one of the more frightening young duos in the Western Conference.

Wembanyama Anchored the Defense

Victor Wembanyama's 25-point, 16-rebound performance was not just a statistical achievement — it was a defensive anchor on a night when the Lakers pushed hard in the fourth quarter. His rim presence made the paint inhospitable for Los Angeles' attacking guards, and his rebounding margin helped San Antonio control possessions in a game decided by three points.

When the Lakers made their move in the final minutes, Wembanyama was the deterrent that kept the deficit manageable. He altered shots, closed out on shooters, and finished at the rim efficiently on the other end. At 22 years old, Wembanyama is already one of the most difficult players in the league to game-plan against for a full 48 minutes.

What It Means for the Lakers

Los Angeles needed a win to advance and came up short by three points. The Lakers entered the game knowing their Cup fate was entirely in their own hands — a win sends them through, a loss ends it — and they still could not close out San Antonio in a winnable game. That will sting in ways that a blowout loss might not.

The regular season continues, and the Lakers' overall record and playoff positioning remain significant. Missing the Cup bracket is a disappointment, not a disaster. But for a franchise with championship ambitions, losing a 99-96 three-point game to miss a tournament they had every opportunity to win is a missed chance.

What's Next

The Spurs travel to Dallas for Dec. 4 quarterfinal action on Prime Video. The Mavericks went 4-0 in group play — the only unbeaten side in the entire tournament — and will host the game. It is an enormous challenge for San Antonio, but Castle and Wembanyama have shown they are equipped for exactly this kind of moment. The Lakers pivot to the regular season, where their longer-term playoff ambitions remain very much alive.
