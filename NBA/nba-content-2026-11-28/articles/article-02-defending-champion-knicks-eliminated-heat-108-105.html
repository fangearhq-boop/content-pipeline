Defending Champion Knicks Eliminated: Heat 108, Knicks 105 as Giannis Delivers 22/23 Performance

By Marcus Cole

November 28, 2026

The New York Knicks will not be defending their NBA Cup title in December. The 2025 Cup champions lost 108-105 to Miami on Black Friday, finishing group play at 1-3 and missing the knockout round for the first time since the tournament format was introduced. Giannis Antetokounmpo made sure of it, delivering 22 points and 23 rebounds in what amounted to a dominant individual shutdown of the Knicks' Cup hopes.

The result at Madison Square Garden was equal parts shocking and fitting. Shocking because the Knicks had been playing their best basketball of the season over a six-game win streak entering Friday. Fitting because Cup group play had been unforgiving all month — New York simply did not win enough of the right games at the right times.

Giannis Was Unstoppable

The numbers require a second look: 22 points and 23 rebounds. Giannis Antetokounmpo matched the energy of the moment and then exceeded it. That rebounding total on a holiday crowd at Madison Square Garden, against a Knicks frontcourt that had been playing with confidence, is the kind of performance that defines a Cup run.

The Heat's ability to control the glass proved decisive in a three-point game. Miami won on the margins — rebounding position, second-chance opportunities, and Giannis making contested shots feel routine. The Knicks had no answer for his combination of size and athleticism at the four or five.

Karl-Anthony Towns had been excellent during New York's six-game win streak, but Giannis presented a physical challenge that prevented Towns from operating on his own terms. The matchup dictated the game's texture from the opening minutes.

The Tiebreaker That Added Insult

Miami won the game and still missed the bracket. Philadelphia clinched East Group B on the head-to-head tiebreaker over the Heat, meaning the 76ers advanced despite not playing Friday. The situation produced one of the more unusual Cup-day emotions — a team winning what everyone identified as a must-win game, then finding out the win was not enough.

For New York, there was no such ambiguity. The Knicks needed to win and needed Philadelphia to lose. They lost. The bracket door closed regardless of what happened in the other game.

Finishing group play 1-3 as the defending champions is a difficult result to contextualize. The Knicks were 12-6 in the regular season entering Friday — a legitimately strong team by most measures. Cup group play revealed something different. At 0-2 entering the final stretch, New York needed perfection and got one win instead of two.

What Went Wrong

The Knicks started Cup play poorly and could not recover quickly enough. By the time they found their Cup form — winning their third group game to stay alive — the math had become too steep. A team that depended on other results to advance should not have been in that position, but group play is an unforgiving format and New York's early stumbles defined the entire run.

Jalen Brunson, who has been a consistent offensive anchor throughout the season, could not close a three-point gap in the final minutes. The Knicks had the firepower to win this game on a different night. Friday night belonged to Giannis.

What's Next

The Knicks shift focus entirely to the regular season, where their six-game win streak and 12-7 record keep them firmly in the Eastern Conference playoff picture. The Cup is gone, but December is full of meaningful games. For Miami, the advancement was hollow — the Heat won the right game and still ended up watching the bracket from the outside. Philadelphia, by virtue of a head-to-head tiebreaker, is the team heading to the Dec. 5 quarterfinal against Atlanta.
