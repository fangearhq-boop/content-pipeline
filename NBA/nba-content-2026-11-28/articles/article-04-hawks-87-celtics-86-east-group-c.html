Hawks 87, Celtics 86: Atlanta Wins East Group C in One of the Grittiest Cup Games of Group Play

By Jake Torres

November 28, 2026

Eighty-seven to eighty-six. In a sport that routinely produces 120-point performances, this was an outlier — a defensive war between two teams that both finished group play at 3-1 and needed every possession to matter. The Atlanta Hawks won East Group C by that one-point margin over the Boston Celtics, and both teams will be in the Dec. 4-5 quarterfinals.

Atlanta takes the group title and the No. 3 East seed, drawing a quarterfinal matchup against the Philadelphia 76ers. Boston enters as the East wild card — same 3-1 record as the Hawks, different fate via tiebreaker — and will face Detroit on Dec. 4.

Defense Defined the Game

An 87-86 final score in November 2026 is not normal. This was two teams that refused to let the other breathe — switching defensively, contesting every shot, and making the paint inhospitable on both ends. The game looked like a December playoff push more than a midseason group-play finale.

Atlanta's defensive identity has been a talking point all season. The Hawks can guard. Their athleticism at multiple positions creates switching versatility that allows them to disrupt opponents without committing to a single defensive scheme. Against Boston, that flexibility was the difference between a one-point win and a tiebreaker elimination.

The Celtics, for their part, were not poor defensively — they simply ran into an opponent that matched their level and found a way to come out on the right side of a one-possession game. Boston's Cup campaign continues regardless; winning the wild card rather than the group changes the draw but does not end the run.

What Group Play Said About These Teams

Both Atlanta and Boston finished 3-1. Neither team was dominant every night, but both were consistent enough to advance. The Hawks took the group by winning the game that mattered most on the final day. The Celtics proved their resilience by recovering from whatever loss they absorbed earlier in the group stage and still qualifying.

These are two teams that will not be easy outs in the knockout round. Boston has the star power — Jayson Tatum's ability to create offense in the half-court gives the Celtics a primary option capable of winning any single game on any given night. Atlanta has the depth and defensive structure to grind opponents into uncomfortable situations across a full game.

What's Next

The Hawks face the No. 2 East seed Philadelphia 76ers on Dec. 5 in a quarterfinal matchup that will test Atlanta's defensive identity against one of the Eastern Conference's most talented offensive rosters. Boston travels to Detroit on Dec. 4, where the Pistons hold the No. 1 East seed after finishing 3-1 in East Group A. All games are on Prime Video. The gritty group-play win for Atlanta earned them a spot in the bracket — now they have to prove they belong against tougher competition.
