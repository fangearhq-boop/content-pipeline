Steph Curry's Extension Window Is Open — And the Warriors Aren't Waiting

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | August 29, 2026

August 29, 2026, is a date that matters for the Golden State Warriors, and for anyone who has watched Stephen Curry dismantle defenses for nearly two decades. Today is the first day Curry is eligible to sign a contract extension — and based on everything coming out of both camps, nobody expects him to wait long.

Curry, 38, is entering the final year of his current deal, a contract worth approximately $62.6 million for the 2026-27 season. A two-year max extension valued at $136.7 million has emerged as the widely anticipated framework, one that would carry him through the 2028-29 season and into his age-40 campaign. That would make him a Warrior for the entirety of his professional life — something that has quietly become the north star for both sides of this negotiation.

The GM's Message Has Been Clear

Golden State general manager Mike Dunleavy has not hidden the ball. Throughout the offseason, he has repeatedly and publicly expressed his belief that Curry will finish his career in a Warriors uniform. "Pretty confident" is the phrase that keeps appearing in his public statements, and according to ESPN's Anthony Slater, both sides have been "engaged with a mutual desire to keep Curry with the Warriors for the entirety of his career."

That is about as close to a done deal as you can get without an actual signature on paper. The only variable left is timing — whether the two sides finalize terms today, this weekend, or sometime before the regular season opens on October 20.

What's at Stake

Here is the number that puts this moment in full perspective: if Curry does not sign an extension by the contractual deadline, he will become an unrestricted free agent following the 2026-27 season. That would mark the first time in his entire 18-year NBA career that he ever hits the open market. Free agent Stephen Curry is a concept so foreign that it barely feels real — but it is exactly what the Warriors are motivated to avoid.

There has been some discussion around a shorter-term alternative: a one-year extension that would lock him in through the 2027-28 season only. ESPN's Brian Windhorst has noted that there is no particular urgency to rush into the two-year max deal if both sides want to preserve flexibility. But the dominant reporting points toward the 2yr/$136.7M as the expected outcome, with mutual interest strong on both sides.

Golden State was 37-45 last season, a team clearly in transition beyond the championship era. Curry has been the constant. Signing him to a long-term extension doesn't just keep a franchise icon — it signals to the fanbase, to free agents, and to the rest of the league that the Warriors intend to rebuild around him one final time.

What's Next

Watch for a formal signing announcement in the coming days. Once Curry's extension is official, attention turns to who Golden State surrounds him with for what could be his final two or three seasons. The Warriors have work to do to become a playoff contender again, and securing their franchise cornerstone is Step 1. Step 1 happens today.
