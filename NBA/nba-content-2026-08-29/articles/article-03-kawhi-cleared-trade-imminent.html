The Kawhi Leonard Saga Is Over — His Return to Toronto Is Now Imminent

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | August 29, 2026

The longest offseason story in the NBA finally has an ending. The league has cleared the Los Angeles Clippers of cap circumvention in its investigation into payments made to Kawhi Leonard through the now-bankrupt company Aspiration, removing the final obstacle to Leonard's blockbuster trade to the Toronto Raptors.

The trade, agreed to in principle on June 30 and placed on hold on July 9 when the investigation was formally announced, can now be officially processed and completed. Kawhi Leonard is returning to Toronto — where he won the 2019 NBA championship and earned Finals MVP honors — and the Raptors can finally exhale after two months of agonizing uncertainty.

The Deal, Refreshed

The terms of the trade remain as originally reported: Leonard goes to the Raptors in exchange for Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, two unprotected first-round picks (2031 and 2033), one pick swap, and two second-round picks. For Toronto, it is a significant package to give up, but the return is a proven two-way star with championship pedigree who knows the city, the organization, and what it takes to win there.

Leonard was personally cleared of any wrongdoing throughout the investigation. Whatever liability existed rested with the Clippers organization, not the player. He spent the summer operating under the belief — and presumably the reassurance — that the trade would clear in time. It has.

What the Investigation Found

The NBA's examination centered on whether the Clippers circumvented salary cap rules to compensate Leonard through Aspiration, a company that has since declared bankruptcy. The investigation dragged through July and August, with the Board of Governors meeting in mid-September looming as the originally anticipated deadline. Instead, the league reached its conclusion ahead of that timeline.

The Clippers may still face organizational sanctions as a result of the investigation — that outcome has been reported as likely — but those penalties, whatever form they take, do not block the Leonard trade from completing. The investigation's resolution is what matters for the Raptors, and that resolution has arrived.

Toronto's Timeline

The Raptors open training camp September 28 in Quebec City. Leonard is expected to be there, integrated into a new roster built around his return. The front office spent the summer in a difficult position: they had already committed to giving up Ingram, Dick, and premium draft capital for a player they could not yet call their own. The wait has made this moment sweeter.

Seven years after Leonard's legendary shot bounced four times on the rim before falling through to knock out the Philadelphia 76ers in the 2019 playoffs — a moment that defined an era and sparked a championship run — he comes back to a city that never stopped wanting him. The reunion, long promised, is finally here.

What's Next

Look for the trade to be officially processed by the NBA's transaction center in the coming days, with both the Raptors and Clippers confirming the deal. Then attention turns to what Leonard's Raptors look like on the floor — a team built for a deep Eastern Conference playoff run, with a superstar who has done it before in exactly this market. Training camp starts September 28. The 2026-27 season cannot get here fast enough for Toronto fans.
