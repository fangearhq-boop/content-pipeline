Shaedon Sharpe's Surgery Is Done — Portland's Season Plans Crumble Before They Begin

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | August 29, 2026

The Portland Trail Blazers confirmed what the basketball world feared when news of Shaedon Sharpe's knee injury first broke: surgery was necessary, and the 22-year-old guard is now looking at a recovery timeline that extends deep into the 2026-27 season.

Sharpe underwent a procedure to repair a lateral meniscus tear in his right knee. The surgery was performed by Dr. Kristofer Jones at the UCLA Department of Orthopedic Surgery and Sports Medicine. Portland has set a reevaluation date of approximately six months post-surgery — putting the next checkpoint around late February 2027.

Understanding the Timeline

Six months is not a return date. That distinction matters enormously in how Portland plans its season, and how fans understand what's ahead. Meniscus repairs do not follow a universal recovery clock. Some players come back within the same season; others require eight or nine months of rehabilitation before they are cleared for game action. Sharpe's February evaluation will answer the critical questions about his strength, range of motion, and readiness.

Sharpe was injured during a workout — a particularly cruel twist, as off-court workouts are supposed to be where players build themselves up, not where careers are derailed. He was immediately evaluated, and surgery was determined to be the appropriate course of treatment.

The Void He Leaves

The statistical loss is significant. Sharpe averaged 20.8 points, 4.3 rebounds, and 2.6 assists in 50 games during the 2025-26 season — a breakout campaign that had cemented his reputation as one of the most explosive young scorers in the Western Conference. Selected seventh overall in the 2022 NBA Draft, Sharpe had finally delivered on the enormous promise that surrounded him from draft night forward.

The Blazers moved aggressively this offseason, acquiring Ja Morant from the Memphis Grizzlies in what was billed as the formation of a backcourt capable of turning Portland into a legitimate playoff contender. That plan assumed both players would be available. It no longer does.

Morant now carries Portland alone from Opening Night. He is talented enough to do it — he is a two-time All-Star and one of the most dynamic players in the league — but the margin for error on the Blazers' season just shrank dramatically. Any injury to Morant, any stretch of poor form, and Portland could be looking at a lottery season rather than a playoff push.

What's Next

Portland enters training camp — set to open later in September — with a drastically altered outlook. The Blazers will need to identify who can fill Sharpe's scoring role in the starting lineup and off the bench. More critically, they will watch Morant's workload and health with a level of concern that wasn't there when both stars were healthy and the season felt full of promise. The February evaluation can't come fast enough.
