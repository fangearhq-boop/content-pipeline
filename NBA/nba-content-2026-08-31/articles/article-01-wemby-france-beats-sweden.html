Wemby Drops 18 as France Beats Sweden 84-69 in Record Stockholm Crowd

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | August 31, 2026

FIBA World Cup Qualifying · France National Team · Victor Wembanyama

Victor Wembanyama was a man on a mission in Stockholm. The San Antonio Spurs superstar delivered 18 points, 8 rebounds, 3 blocks, and a steal while shooting 6-of-7 from the field, leading France to an 84-69 victory over Sweden in their FIBA World Cup 2027 qualifier on August 30. The crowd of 14,750 fans packed into Avicii Arena — a record for any basketball game ever played on Swedish soil — watched in disbelief as Wemby took over the fourth quarter and turned a tight contest into a comfortable French victory.

A Tight Game Until Wembanyama Decided It

This was no blowout from the opening tip. The game featured 15 lead changes and seven ties, with the tension building throughout. Sweden — led by Miami Heat guard Pelle Larsson's 23-point outburst — actually held a 65-64 lead with five minutes remaining. For a brief moment, the record Stockholm crowd had a genuine chance of celebrating a historic upset.

Then Wembanyama took over.

France outscored Sweden in the final five minutes to seal the 15-point win. Wembanyama's efficiency was almost unfair — 6-of-7 from the floor, drawing defenders, altering shots on the other end, and doing the things that simply cannot be coached or duplicated. His combination of size, skill, and basketball IQ was on full display in Stockholm.

France Locks Up Top Spot in Group L

The victory improved France's record to 7-1 in FIBA World Cup 2027 qualifying Group L — giving Les Bleus the undisputed top position and putting them firmly on a path to the 2027 World Cup. It was another emphatic statement from a French squad that has played at an elite level throughout this qualifying campaign.

Wembanyama has embraced the role of national team captain with remarkable maturity. After his 22-point, 9-rebound performance against Slovenia earlier in the qualifying window, and a previous 18-point effort against Serbia, the 22-year-old continues to elevate his game on every stage — not just the NBA.

Larsson and Sweden Made Them Work

Credit should be given to Sweden, who gave France everything they could handle. Pelle Larsson — who plays alongside Jimmy Butler and the Heat's backcourt in Miami — was sensational with 23 points, keeping his team competitive for nearly 40 minutes. The 14,750 fans got their money's worth before France's star power prevailed.

The record crowd underscores just how much basketball is growing in Scandinavia, and how much of that growth is connected to the presence of international NBA stars who bring the global game to their home countries during the offseason.

What's Next

France will continue their World Cup qualifying campaign in the coming months as they work toward locking up berth in the 2027 FIBA Basketball World Cup. For Wembanyama, this qualifying window provides both a competitive outlet and an opportunity to sharpen his game ahead of the Spurs' October 20 season opener.

Spurs fans should be thrilled: their franchise cornerstone is in elite form. When NBA training camps open September 29 and the new season begins October 20, Wemby will bring this level of confidence and performance with him. For San Antonio, the future — and the present — looks very bright.
