Anthony Edwards' Bold Take: Timberwolves Will Beat the Thunder and Spurs

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | August 20, 2026

Anthony Edwards doesn't need a season preview to know how he feels about the Timberwolves' chances. With LaMelo Ball now in Minnesota, the confidence is already at midseason levels — and the target is the two best teams in the Western Conference.

"Against those two teams," Edwards told ESPN, referring to the Oklahoma City Thunder and the San Antonio Spurs, "I think we're going to beat them."

There it is. No hedging. No "we'll compete." We're going to beat them.

What Changed: LaMelo Ball Arrives

The trade that brought LaMelo Ball from Charlotte to Minnesota was one of the biggest moves of the 2026 offseason. Minnesota shipped out Naz Reid, a 2033 unprotected first-round pick, three first-round pick swaps in 2028, 2029, and 2030, and three second-round picks. Josh Green came back with Ball.

The cost was steep. The Timberwolves bet heavily on the idea that Ball and Edwards together form something greater than either produces independently — and that the combination unlocks Minnesota as a genuine Western Conference threat.

The early read from analysts suggests the logic holds. Edwards was one of the league's most powerful isolation scorers and clutch performers, but he was also one of the primary ball-handlers and pick-and-roll initiators. That's a lot of cognitive load for one player. With Ball handling creation duties — and Ball is one of the best playmakers in the league when healthy — Edwards can drift off-ball, attack closeouts, and operate in the spaces that make him most efficient.

The Edwards Track Record

Edwards isn't talking boldly without receipts. He led the entire NBA with 157 clutch points during the 2024-25 season and finished third in official Clutch Player of the Year voting. His postseason performances have shown consistent growth over each of his seasons — a young star learning what it takes to win when the games matter most.

He's entering his prime. He has the type of two-way game — elite athleticism, developing defensive instincts, explosive creation off the dribble — that scales as the competition intensifies. Bold as the quote sounds, it comes from a player who has demonstrated he can perform at the highest level when it counts.

The Competition: OKC and San Antonio

Edwards' confidence is bold for a reason — the competition is real. Oklahoma City, built around a young core with one of the league's best coaching staffs, has been one of the West's elite programs. San Antonio, with Victor Wembanyama entering his third NBA season, represents the long-term future of the conference and arguably the whole league.

Beating either team in a playoff series is a formidable task. Beating both in the same postseason would be an enormous statement about what Minnesota has built.

But that's the nature of championship windows. Teams that believe they can win tend to compete like it. The Timberwolves, with this new core, believe.

What's Next

Minnesota opens training camp with the rest of the NBA's non-overseas teams on September 29. The regular season begins October 20. Ball and Edwards will have a full preseason to build chemistry, develop read-and-react habits together, and figure out the late-game possessions that decide the tight games.

Edwards has already set the tone. In the Western Conference this season, everyone is hunting the Spurs and Thunder. The Timberwolves, in their superstar's own words, plan to hunt them down. 🐺
