Wemby vs. Jokic: France Takes on Serbia Tonight in Belgrade

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | August 20, 2026

Victor Wembanyama has said he doesn't miss the NBA right now. Tonight in Belgrade, we'll see why — because FIBA basketball at this level, with this kind of opponent, is something else entirely.

France faces Serbia in the first of two exhibition games Thursday at 2 p.m. ET (8 p.m. local time). The matchup headlines an international window that also includes World Cup qualifier games against Slovenia and Sweden later this month. For Wembanyama, it's his first competitive appearance for the French national team since the 2024 Olympic final — a run that ended with a silver medal and a career-defining moment for the then-20-year-old.

He's 22 now. He's France's captain. And tonight, he faces Nikola Jokic.

A Captain Steps Up

The French basketball federation announced Wembanyama as captain for this qualifying window roughly two weeks ago — a decision that was met with widespread approval across the basketball world. It's a symbolic gesture, yes, but also a practical one. Wembanyama is France's best player and one of the three or four best players on the planet. Leadership comes with the territory.

"I do not miss the NBA right now, but I miss FIBA basketball," Wembanyama said ahead of the window. That quote circulated everywhere, and for good reason — it captures something authentic. The San Antonio Spurs star plays 82 regular season games plus playoffs, but the pace and texture of FIBA ball is different: shorter shot clocks, tighter international defense, the emotion of representing your country.

Wembanyama thrives in it. In the 2024 Olympics, he was one of the most dominant players in the tournament — carrying France past Germany to the gold medal final before the United States prevailed.

Serbia's Counterpunch: Nikola Jokic

Serbia won't make things easy. Nikola Jokic — three-time NBA MVP and arguably the most skillful big man in the history of the game — anchors a Serbian squad that has been one of Europe's most formidable programs for the past decade.

The Wemby-Jokic individual matchup is the kind that basketball purists savor. Wembanyama, at 7-foot-4, brings length and athleticism that nobody in history has combined at his level. Jokic, at 6-foot-11, brings a passing vision and offensive IQ that makes him nearly impossible to scheme against. When they share the court, the game happens at a different intellectual altitude.

Serbia found ways to frustrate Wembanyama in their previous international meeting — mostly through physicality and help defense — but Wembanyama has continued to evolve. At 22, he's in a different place than he was two years ago.

France's Supporting Cast

Wembanyama won't be alone. France's roster includes Rudy Gobert, the Utah Jazz's former franchise center now with the Minnesota Timberwolves, providing veteran presence and defensive anchor support alongside Wemby. Evan Fournier, one of France's most experienced international players, will run the perimeter.

The team is under new head coach Frederic Fauthoux, who is working with Wembanyama for the first time in an official capacity. Chemistry and system familiarity will be storylines to watch, particularly as France shifts from these exhibition games to the formal World Cup qualifiers against Slovenia and Sweden later this month.

Why This Game Matters Beyond the Score

This isn't a regular-season game. The result won't change any standings. But it matters for several reasons. For Wembanyama, competitive basketball — any kind — sharpens the game. August exhibition windows at the highest level are invaluable preparation, especially for a player still developing his leadership identity at the international level.

For NBA fans in the United States, the 2 p.m. ET tip gives a midday window to watch two of the sport's most compelling figures compete. There's a scarcity to it: outside the Olympics and FIBA World Cup, these matchups simply don't happen.

And for the sport itself, a Wemby-Jokic game in a packed Belgrade arena is exactly the kind of moment that grows basketball globally.

What's Next

France and Serbia meet again in Orleans on August 23, before France opens World Cup qualifying play at home against Slovenia on August 27 and travels to Sweden on August 30. Wembanyama's NBA season with the Spurs begins when training camp opens — alongside most teams — on September 29.

Tonight, though, it's Belgrade. It's Wemby and Jokic. And it's a reminder that basketball at its highest level doesn't take the summer off — it just moves continents.
