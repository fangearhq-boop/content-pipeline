NBA Clears Kawhi Leonard Personally — Raptors Trade Moving Closer

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | August 20, 2026

The most prolonged trade saga of the 2026 NBA offseason may finally be approaching its conclusion. The NBA's investigation into the Kawhi Leonard situation has reached a significant milestone: the league found no evidence that Los Angeles Clippers owner Steve Ballmer personally funneled money to Leonard, clearing the star forward of direct violations.

It's a meaningful development — and one that Raptors fans have been waiting for since the investigation began weeks ago.

What the NBA Found — and Didn't Find

The investigation centered on two alleged arrangements: a reported $28 million endorsement deal involving Aspiration Fund, and a separate deal with Daktronics, the scoreboard manufacturer that supplied the jumbotron at Intuit Dome. The allegation, as described by sources with knowledge of the situation, was that these arrangements were used to circumvent the NBA's salary cap — effectively funneling additional compensation to Leonard through corporate sponsors with Clippers connections.

The league found no evidence of direct involvement by Ballmer in routing money to Leonard. That doesn't close the book on the Clippers' organizational conduct — investigations into the Daktronics arrangement in particular are still active — but it removes the most legally exposed piece for the player at the center of it all.

Under the NBA's collective bargaining agreement, players cannot be suspended for salary cap circumvention. Any sanctions that land on the Clippers would come in the form of fines and forfeited draft picks — not on Leonard himself.

The Trade Package and What Toronto Gets

The deal as constructed sends Leonard to the Toronto Raptors for Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, and multiple first-round picks. Toronto gets a proven star with championship experience — the same player who willed the franchise to its only NBA title in 2019 and delivered one of the most iconic shots in league history (the "Kawhi Shot" against Philadelphia in Game 7 of the second round).

Leonard's contract carries $50.3 million for the 2026-27 season. He will be 35 years old when the regular season opens October 20. Questions about his durability, given a history of soft-tissue injuries dating back to his Spurs years, remain a legitimate concern. But if healthy, Leonard is still a legitimate two-way star who can affect games in the moments that matter most.

Training Camp Timeline: The Clock Is Ticking

Toronto's training camp opens September 28 in Quebec City at the Université Laval PEPS athletic complex. The Raptors have not officially changed their plans around the Leonard situation — they are operating as if the trade will complete before camp.

Sources told CBS Sports in August that the investigation is "closer to completion than advertised" and that resolution — some level of Clippers sanctions plus trade clearance — is expected within weeks. If the timeline holds, Leonard could be in a Raptors uniform before September ends.

What This Means for Both Franchises

For the Raptors, getting Leonard is a massive swing on a team that has been rebuilding since his departure in 2019. Brandon Ingram heading to Los Angeles gives the Clippers a younger, cheaper wing to help shape their next chapter. Gradey Dick, a promising perimeter shooter, adds shooting depth and upside.

For the Clippers, there's the shadow of whatever sanctions the NBA ultimately imposes. Lost picks and fines would sting — but the franchise can move forward from this chapter knowing it has at least avoided the worst-case outcome of Leonard being ineligible to play.

For the broader NBA, the case serves as a high-profile reminder that the league monitors player compensation arrangements carefully. Cap circumvention investigations of this scope are rare, and the public attention on this one will influence how teams and agents structure future sponsorship agreements for years to come.

What's Next

The NBA has not announced a timeline for completing its investigation. The six-week estimate from HoopsHype (published around August 10) would put resolution around September 22 — six days before Toronto's training camp opens. That gives the Raptors a narrow window to get Leonard processed, fitted, and ready to practice.

Leonard has said he expects to be in Toronto before camp. The NBA just made that expectation a lot more realistic.
