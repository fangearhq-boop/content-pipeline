### STORY 1: France Beats Serbia 91-90 — Orleans Thriller

#### Long-Form Post — 7:00 PM ET

Victor Wembanyama came to Orleans with a point to prove — and he delivered.

Just three days after Nikola Jokic outdueled him 90-87 in Belgrade, Wembanyama led France to a 91-90 thriller that went down to the final seconds. Jaylen Hoard's fast-break layup with just 11 seconds remaining sealed it, completing one of the most dramatic reversals in recent FIBA history.

Wemby nearly doubled his scoring output from the first meeting, finishing with 18 points — mostly through dunks and free throws — while showing the composure of a player who refuses to let one bad game define him. On the other side, Nikola Jokic was nothing short of spectacular: 34 points, 13 rebounds, and 5 assists in a virtuoso performance that somehow still ended in a loss.

France at one point led by 19 and nearly let it slip away. They didn't.

The split sends both teams forward in FIBA World Cup 2027 qualifying. France (now 6-1 in Group L) faces Slovenia on August 27, then Sweden on August 30. This chapter of Wemby vs. Jokic ends 1-1 — and it's already one of basketball's great rivalries.

### STORY 4: Kawhi Leonard Investigation

#### Long-Form Post — 4:00 PM ET

The Kawhi Leonard saga just entered another week of limbo.

The trade sending Leonard from the Los Angeles Clippers to the Toronto Raptors remains officially on hold while the NBA completes its investigation into two endorsement deals — Aspiration ($28 million) and Daktronics — that allegedly involved the Clippers improperly funneling money to Leonard.

Here is what we know: Leonard has been personally cleared. The league's focus has shifted to a "failure to supervise" charge against the Clippers organization. The NBA has confirmed the 2026 All-Star Game stays at Intuit Dome — a signal that league leadership expects the investigation to resolve without catastrophic consequences.

Resolution is expected around September 22. Toronto training camp opens September 28 in Quebec City.

For Leonard, it's more waiting. Two seasons spent in this investigation's shadow. For the Raptors, it's hope deferred — the trade is all but certain to close, just on its own timeline.

### STORY 5: James Harden / Cavaliers

#### Long-Form Post — 1:00 PM ET

The Cleveland Cavaliers have officially added James Harden — and the Eastern Conference should be paying attention.

Harden agreed to a 3-year, $97 million deal with a player option in the third season, coming to Cleveland through a complex three-team transaction. Peyton Watson heads to the Cavaliers, Max Strus goes to the Clippers, and the Nuggets receive an unprotected 2031 Cavaliers first-round pick plus a 2032 Sacramento second.

For Cleveland, this is a statement. The Cavaliers entered this offseason as a team with serious playoff credentials but lacking the veteran playmaking needed to go the distance. Harden, at 37, brings exactly that: pick-and-roll mastery, late-game shot creation, and championship experience from his Philadelphia, Brooklyn, and Houston years.

The fit questions are real — Harden needs the ball in his hands, and so does Cleveland's existing core. But head coach J.B. Bickerstaff has shown the ability to get the most from multiple creators, and the front office clearly believes this addition pushes Cleveland over a threshold.

Training camp opens September 29. The Cavaliers' preseason schedule will be appointment viewing for East-watchers.
