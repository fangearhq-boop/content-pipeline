Four Days: Stephen Curry's Extension Window Opens August 29 as Warriors Hold Their Breath

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | August 25, 2026

The most significant contract negotiation of this NBA offseason is four days away. Stephen Curry's extension eligibility window opens August 29, and the Golden State Warriors are ready to offer the greatest shooter in NBA history a two-year, $136.7 million maximum contract that would take him through the 2028-29 season — his age-40 year. Every credible league insider expects the deal to get done. The pressure to make sure it does has never been higher.

Warriors general manager Mike Dunleavy has left no ambiguity. He is "pretty confident" that Curry will finish his career as a Warrior. Multiple ESPN reporters have confirmed mutual interest. The extension talks, when they formally begin on the 29th, are expected to move quickly. The Warriors will offer whatever he wants, up to the maximum.

But the context around this contract has changed dramatically over the past twelve months. Golden State finished last season at 37-45, a record that reflects a team in transition. The front office made an aggressive run at LeBron James this summer and was turned down. Pursued Giannis Antetokounmpo — already spoken for by Miami. Had Anthony Davis on their radar — nothing materialized. Every avenue for a superstar co-star closed. The Warriors' entire competitive ceiling for the foreseeable future runs through Curry, and only Curry.

What the Extension Means

Curry signing this extension does more than keep a legend in a Golden State uniform. It sends a message to every young free agent and trade target over the next two years that the Warriors are still a relevant destination. It preserves Curry's legacy as a single-franchise player in an era where staying put is increasingly rare. And it gives the organization the stability to build around their cornerstone without the distraction of impending free agency.

The alternative — an unsigned Curry heading into the final year of his deal in 2026-27 — would be a slow-motion disaster. Every game would become an existential referendum on the franchise. Reporters would track Curry's body language through a losing stretch. Fans would speculate. The front office would be distracted. The Warriors simply cannot afford to play the 2026-27 season under those conditions.

A shorter-term, discounted structure has been floated by ESPN's Brian Windhorst as one possible alternative — a deal that gives Golden State more cap flexibility next summer. Whether Curry opts for that discount or takes the full max, the expectation throughout the league is that he stays.

What's Next

The August 29 window opening will likely be met with immediate reporting from Bay Area insiders as the two sides sit down. If the deal closes on the 29th or shortly after, it will be one of the cleanest and most expected signings of the summer. If there are any complications, the resulting news cycle will be loud.

Warriors training camp opens September 29. Whether Curry arrives with a new deal in hand will set the tone for everything that follows — from the preseason to the opening-night atmosphere at Chase Center. Four days is a long time and no time at all.
