Kawhi Leonard Trade to Toronto Remains on Hold as NBA Investigation Stretches Into September

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | August 25, 2026

The Kawhi Leonard saga that has consumed the NBA's attention for much of the past year entered another week without resolution. The trade sending Leonard from the Los Angeles Clippers to the Toronto Raptors — agreed in principle weeks ago — remains officially on hold while the NBA's investigation into two endorsement deals concludes. Resolution is now expected around September 22, and Toronto's training camp is set to begin September 28 in Quebec City.

The investigation centers on allegations that Clippers owner Steve Ballmer improperly introduced Leonard to two sponsors: Aspiration, an eco-friendly financial services brand, and Daktronics, a scoreboard and display company that works extensively at Intuit Dome. The league's probe, led by outside law firm Wachtell Lipton, has reportedly finished its fact-finding phase and is working toward a formal ruling on sanctions.

Crucially, Leonard has been personally cleared. The NBA found no evidence that the two-time Finals MVP sought out or knowingly participated in impermissible arrangements. The sanctions expected to fall on the Clippers organization — likely framed around a "failure to supervise" charge — are not expected to impact Leonard's eligibility or the trade's ultimate completion.

The Signal in the All-Star Decision

The NBA's confirmation that the 2026 All-Star Game will remain at Intuit Dome in Inglewood was widely read as a tell. Had the league expected this investigation to spiral into catastrophic consequences — a voided contract, ownership sanctions that would functionally damage the Clippers franchise, or a prolonged legal battle — relocating the All-Star Game would have been the prudent call. Keeping it in place signals that league leadership expects a contained resolution that does not permanently scar the Clippers or the building.

That reading aligns with on-the-ground reporting: both Leonard and the Raptors are operating as if the trade will close. Toronto's front office has been planning around Leonard joining the roster. There has been no indication from either side that the deal is in jeopardy — only that it is waiting on the NBA's process to finish.

What's Next

The September 22 resolution target gives both teams just under four weeks of certainty before training camp opens. If the ruling arrives on schedule and the trade processes cleanly through the league office, Leonard could be in a Raptors uniform for the first formal practices in Quebec City.

If there are delays — ESPN has raised the possibility that complications could push a final ruling into 2027, though that scenario is considered unlikely — the situation would shift from background noise to genuine distraction for the Toronto front office. For now, the expectation throughout the league is that this trade closes. The when, not the if, remains the question.
