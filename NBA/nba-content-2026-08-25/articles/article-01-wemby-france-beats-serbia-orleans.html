France Edges Serbia 91-90 in Orleans Thriller — Wembanyama Gets His Revenge

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | August 25, 2026

Victor Wembanyama walked into the Palais des Sports d'Orléans on August 23 with something to prove. Forty-eight hours earlier, Nikola Jokic had outdueled him 90-87 in Belgrade, holding France's captain to just 10 points and 6 turnovers. The rematch delivered the drama both sides deserved — and ended with Jaylen Hoard's fast-break layup sealing a 91-90 victory with just 11 seconds remaining.

Wembanyama nearly doubled his scoring output from the first meeting, finishing with 18 points through dunks, free throws, and mid-range buckets that showed he processed the Belgrade loss and adjusted. He wasn't transcendent — Jokic made sure of that — but he was resilient, and in a game this close, resilience was what France needed.

On the other side, Nikola Jokic was nothing short of extraordinary. The three-time MVP posted 34 points, 13 rebounds, and 5 assists and dragged Serbia to the final seconds. His performance underlined everything basketball fans already know: Jokic remains the game's most complete player, and no rivalry quite captures his unique genius like the matchups against Wembanyama.

A Game That Refused to End Quietly

France built a 19-point lead at one point — exactly the kind of cushion that should have made the final minutes comfortable. It didn't. Serbia's Bogdan Bogdanovic hit back-to-back threes, Jokic converted three consecutive possessions, and suddenly what looked like a comfortable French victory turned into a white-knuckle finish.

Mathias Luwawu-Cabarrot contributed 19 points off the bench to keep France's offense humming, and Bilal Coulibaly Francisco added 20 to give the team enough firepower to weather the storm. When Serbia pulled within one possession in the final minute, the Orléans crowd went silent — before Hoard collected the ball in transition and finished at the rim to send the building into pandemonium.

The 1-1 split in the FIBA World Cup 2027 qualifying series reflects exactly how evenly matched these two nations are. France and Serbia are separated by inches — on the scoreboard and in the generation-defining talent debate at the top of basketball.

What's Next

France, now 6-1 in Group L qualifying, returns to action August 27 against Slovenia before closing out the window August 30 against Sweden. Wembanyama will carry the momentum of this result into the stretch run, knowing he went toe-to-toe with Jokic across two games and came out 1-1.

For Wembanyama personally, the development arc could not be clearer. He was erratic in Belgrade and poised in Orléans. Every FIBA window adds reps, battle-testing, and context that the San Antonio Spurs will benefit from when he returns for the 2026-27 NBA season. The rivalry with Jokic has only just begun — and basketball is better for it.
