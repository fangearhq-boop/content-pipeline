Tyler Herro Scores 32 as Bucks End 13-Game Losing Streak with 116-111 Win Over Raptors

By Damon Pierce | November 24, 2026 | Hoop Heroes

Two nights after setting a career high with 50 points in a loss, Tyler Herro returned Monday with 32 points and a different kind of night — a winning one. The Milwaukee Bucks beat the Toronto Raptors 116-111, snapping a 13-game losing streak that had made this one of the most difficult starts in recent franchise history.

Milwaukee entered the game at 1-14, the worst record in the Eastern Conference and a number that put real pressure on a roster built to compete. The win doesn't erase the hole they've dug themselves into, but it changes the internal narrative — and in a locker room that needed something to hold onto, that matters.

Herro's Response

Herro's ability to bounce back after Saturday underlines why Milwaukee paid to bring him here. The 50-point performance in a losing effort was one of those moments that could have lingered — a great individual night that felt hollow because the team couldn't close. Instead, Herro came back Monday and competed, working within the game rather than trying to carry it alone.

Thirty-two points is still a heavy lift, but the manner mattered. Herro made the right plays, hit shots when the Bucks needed them, and was part of a team effort that managed to hold Toronto off down the stretch. Milwaukee converted key possessions in the fourth quarter and Toronto couldn't find the equalizer.

What 13 Games Without a Win Looks Like

Thirteen-game losing streaks at this stage of the season carry weight that goes beyond the standings. They test cohesion, confidence, and belief. Milwaukee had been living through that — close losses, blowout losses, different reasons for the same result night after night.

What made this stretch particularly unusual is that the talent hasn't been the issue. Herro's scoring has been excellent throughout the skid. The problems were elsewhere — defense, late-game execution, consistency from the supporting cast. Monday's performance against Toronto didn't solve all of those things, but it solved the most important one: the team went home with a win.

What's Next

The Bucks are still a long way from where they want to be in the Eastern Conference standings, but every winning streak has to start somewhere. For Milwaukee, Monday night is the first brick. Whether they can build on it — tighten the defense, improve late-game execution, get more consistent contributions around Herro — will define the next phase of their season. The streak is over. The work continues.
