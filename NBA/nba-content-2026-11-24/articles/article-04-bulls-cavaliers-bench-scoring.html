Bulls Bench Erupts for 63 Points, Chicago Rolls Cavaliers 122-108 Behind Hawkins' Season-High 19

By Damon Pierce | November 24, 2026 | Hoop Heroes

Chicago's bench scored 63 points and the Bulls dismantled the Cleveland Cavaliers 122-108 on Monday, with Jordan Hawkins posting a season-high 19 and Buddy Hield adding 17 off the bench. The Bulls led 72-50 at halftime — a first-half beating that turned a competitive game into a statement before the fourth quarter even began.

Bench scoring at that volume is unusual. Getting 63 points from reserves requires a consistent rotation, players hitting shots when their number is called, and a team that isn't overly dependent on any single contributor. Monday, Chicago had all three.

Hawkins Steps Up

Jordan Hawkins has been developing as a reliable scoring option off the bench, and his 19-point performance against Cleveland was his clearest demonstration of that so far this season. He made shots in a variety of ways — not just catch-and-shoot moments, but created looks too — and his activity level kept the Bulls' offense moving during stretches when other contributors were resting.

Season-high performances early in the year are interesting because they set a floor while hinting at a ceiling. Hawkins has shown what his best game looks like. The question for Chicago is how often he can get there.

Hield's Contribution and the Halftime Blowout

Buddy Hield's 17 points were a perfect complement. Hield has been one of the most reliable three-point shooters in the league throughout his career and his effectiveness off the bench gives Chicago a secondary scoring option who doesn't require the ball to be effective. When both Hawkins and Hield are going simultaneously, the Bulls' bench becomes legitimately difficult to game-plan against.

The 72-50 halftime lead told the full story. Chicago built its advantage steadily in the first quarter, then extended it in the second through the bench contributions. By the break, Cleveland was in a hole that required the kind of third-quarter comeback they simply didn't have in them.

What's Next

The Bulls' bench depth is becoming one of the more interesting storylines of their season. When Chicago gets this kind of production from non-starters, their overall team is genuinely competitive against anyone on the schedule. The challenge is replicating it. Monday was a best-case scenario for how their rotation can function. If they can get close to that output with any consistency, the Bulls will be a difficult team to dismiss.
