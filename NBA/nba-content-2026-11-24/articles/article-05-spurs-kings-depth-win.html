Spurs Beat Kings 123-107 Without Wembanyama, Blast Sacramento by 18 in Second Quarter

By Jake Torres | November 24, 2026 | Hoop Heroes

The San Antonio Spurs beat the Sacramento Kings 123-107 on Monday despite playing without Victor Wembanyama, outscoring their hosts 41-23 in the second quarter to put the game beyond reach early. The win was a demonstration of organizational depth — a team winning not because of its biggest star, but in spite of his absence.

The absence of Wembanyama looms over any San Antonio game. When one of the most unique players in the league is unavailable, teams with less depth simply lose. The Spurs won. That gap in outcome reveals something real about how San Antonio has been built.

The Second Quarter Blitz

Outscoring an opponent 41-23 in a single quarter is a rout in a 12-minute span. For San Antonio to do that without their primary offensive anchor speaks to how well the supporting cast executed. Sacramento, for their part, had no answer for a Spurs team that was generating quality looks from multiple positions and converting them at a high rate.

The 18-point second-quarter advantage essentially decided the game. Sacramento spent the second half trying to make up ground they never should have had to make up. San Antonio played the final two quarters with the advantage of a comfortable lead, managing the clock and protecting the cushion without overextending.

What Depth Wins Say About San Antonio

The Spurs under their current coaching staff have emphasized collective execution over individual brilliance. When Wembanyama is healthy and in the lineup, that philosophy is amplified — his two-way presence makes everyone around him better. But the approach doesn't depend on him being there every night.

Monday was a proof point. San Antonio's rotations covered for the absence, role players stepped into expanded minutes and made the most of them, and the defensive structure held. These are organizational wins as much as individual ones.

What's Next

The Spurs will be watching Wembanyama's availability closely as the season progresses, but nights like Monday provide reassurance that the team can tread water without him. Sacramento, meanwhile, needs to respond from this result — letting a shorthanded opponent get away from them in the second quarter is the kind of lapse that can undermine a season if it becomes a pattern. Both teams have more to show before the picture gets clearer.
