### STORY 2: Russell Westbrook Retires

**Facebook Long-Form** | Window: 10:00 AM ET

Russell Westbrook has retired from the NBA after 18 seasons.

He announced the decision via a video narrated by actor Michael B. Jordan — fitting for a player who always approached the game with a cinematic intensity that no one could quite replicate.

The numbers alone barely capture what he meant to the game: 209 career triple-doubles, the all-time NBA record. The 2016-17 MVP season where he averaged a triple-double for an entire year — something Oscar Robertson hadn't done since 1962. Nine All-Star appearances. Nine All-NBA selections. A career scoring average that places him 14th all-time with 27,176 points, and 10,351 assists — fifth-best in NBA history.

He played 18 seasons for seven teams: the Thunder, Rockets, Wizards, Lakers, Clippers, Nuggets, and Kings. He was offered minimum contracts by the Kings and Wizards this offseason, but after weighing his options, he decided this chapter was over. He chose to walk away rather than grind out a 19th season on a minimum deal. That decision — refusing to diminish his legacy for a roster spot — says everything about how he viewed himself.

His partnership with Kevin Durant in Oklahoma City was one of the most electrifying duos in modern NBA history. The three seasons they shared, and the Finals run in 2012, produced moments that basketball fans still talk about today.

Russell Westbrook played the game on his terms. He retired on his terms. That's the only way it could have ended.

Thank you, Brodie. 🫡

### STORY 3: Kawhi Leonard Investigation

**Facebook Long-Form** | Window: 1:00 PM ET

The Kawhi Leonard saga finally has a finish line in sight.

According to HoopsHype, the NBA's investigation into the Clippers-Aspiration cap circumvention case could be wrapped up within the next six weeks. That window — counting from the Aug. 10 report — puts a potential resolution around Sept. 22, just six days before the Toronto Raptors' Media Day and one week before training camp begins in Quebec City.

Leonard himself reportedly expects to be with the Raptors. The belief among league sources is that the investigation will result in some level of sanctions against the Clippers, but ultimately clearance for the trade — sending Kawhi to Toronto in exchange for Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, and two unprotected first-round picks — to be completed.

The Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz law firm has spent 11 months investigating the matter. The trade has been on hold since July 9, when the Raptors paused the deal pending the outcome.

For Toronto fans, the timeline is tight but manageable. The Raptors are rebuilding around a 35-year-old Leonard who brings proven championship experience — his Raptors title in 2019 remains one of the great underdog runs in NBA history. Getting clarity before camp opens is the deadline they cannot miss.

### STORY 4: Stephen Curry Extension

**Facebook Long-Form** | Window: 4:00 PM ET

Stephen Curry's extension window opens in 15 days.

On Aug. 29, the Golden State Warriors will be able to offer their franchise cornerstone a two-year, $136.7 million max extension — and Warriors GM Mike Dunleavy Jr. made it clear this week that he's not worried about the outcome.

"There's two things I'm confident about with Steph," Dunleavy told reporters. "He really, really wants to win and he wants to finish his career as a Warrior. I don't think that changes tomorrow, August 29th, start of the season, middle of the season, end of next season."

The Warriors are coming off a 37-45 season and missed the playoffs, but Curry — who turns 39 this season — has shown no signs of slowing down. ESPN's Anthony Slater reports mutual interest remains strong. A September signing is widely expected.

Without the extension, Curry would become an unrestricted free agent next summer for the first time in his career. That scenario is apparently the last thing either side wants. Draymond Green (1yr/$27.7M) and Gary Payton II (1yr/$3.9M) are already back in the fold.

The dynasty core may be aging, but it's not done yet.

### STORY 5: Wembanyama / France FIBA

**Facebook Long-Form** | Window: 7:00 PM ET

Victor Wembanyama is six days away from suiting up for France — and he's been counting the days.

"I do not miss the NBA right now, I miss FIBA basketball," Wemby told reporters ahead of the upcoming window, capturing exactly the enthusiasm France's national team program needs heading into a critical stretch.

On Aug. 20, France faces Serbia in Belgrade in an exhibition game, followed by another exhibition in Orleans on Aug. 23. Then the real stakes kick in: FIBA World Cup qualifiers vs. Slovenia on Aug. 27 (home) and Sweden on Aug. 30 (away). France enters the window 5-1 in Group L.

The roster is loaded: Wembanyama + Rudy Gobert + Evan Fournier headline a squad making its first appearance since the 2024 Paris Olympics gold run. New head coach Frédéric Fauthoux will be working with Wemby for the first time in a competitive setting — a fascinating debut for a team that genuinely believes it can win another major international title.

For Spurs fans watching from across the Atlantic: Wemby looks healthy, engaged, and motivated. Training camp isn't far away.
