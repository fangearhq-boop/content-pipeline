Stephen Curry's Extension Window Opens in 15 Days — Warriors GM Confident, $136.7M Max on the Table

By Damon Pierce | August 14, 2026

Mike Dunleavy Jr. went public this week with his confidence that Curry will remain a Warrior for life. On Aug. 29, the paperwork can begin.

The Golden State Warriors will be able to formally offer Stephen Curry a two-year, $136.7 million max extension in 15 days, when his contract window opens on Aug. 29. And according to Warriors general manager Mike Dunleavy Jr., the outcome is about as certain as anything in professional sports ever gets.

"There's two things I'm confident about with Steph," Dunleavy told reporters this week. "He really, really wants to win and he wants to finish his career as a Warrior. I don't think that changes tomorrow, August 29th, start of the season, middle of the season, end of next season."

Mutual Interest, Confirmed

ESPN's Anthony Slater has reported that mutual interest in the extension remains strong on both sides. Curry's existing contract pays him $62.5 million for the 2026-27 season — already one of the largest annual salaries in the league. The extension would add up to two guaranteed years beyond that, locking him into Golden State through his late 30s and making a departure during his playing career essentially off the table.

Without the extension, Curry would become an unrestricted free agent for the first time in his career after the 2026-27 season. That scenario — Curry as a true free agent, fielding calls from championship contenders — is the only leverage point worth noting. But given the public alignment between player and franchise, a September signing appears likely before training camp begins.

The Warriors' Context

Golden State finished 37-45 last season and missed the playoffs — a difficult result for a franchise that grew accustomed to June basketball. Draymond Green is back on a one-year, $27.7 million deal. Gary Payton II returned on a one-year deal. The roster is built around retaining Curry while giving the front office flexibility to rebuild around him.

ESPN's Brian Windhorst floated an alternative structure — a shorter-term arrangement that might give the Warriors more roster flexibility — but that idea hasn't gained traction publicly. The most straightforward path: two years, $136.7M, done before October.

What the Extension Would Mean

A completed extension would effectively end the Stephen Curry free agency conversation for two more seasons and allow the Warriors to focus entirely on roster construction around their franchise player. Curry will turn 39 during the 2026-27 season. The question is not whether he can still play at an elite level — he has answered that repeatedly — but whether the supporting cast around him can compete for a championship in the final chapter of his career.

Dunleavy's confidence suggests the Warriors' front office believes they can build a contender around Curry one more time. The extension is step one of that project.

What's Next

Aug. 29 is the starting gun. Both sides have indicated they want this done efficiently, and a September signing announcement appears to be the expected timeline. Once the extension is finalized, attention will shift to Golden State's opening-night matchup and whether the revamped Warriors can make a run back toward the postseason in 2026-27. Fifteen days from now, one of the offseason's last remaining major storylines should have its resolution.
