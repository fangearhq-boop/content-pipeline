Wembanyama Returns to FIBA: France vs. Serbia in Belgrade Six Days Away — "I Miss FIBA Basketball"

By Jake Torres | August 14, 2026

Victor Wembanyama is six days from his first competitive action since the 2024 Paris Olympics, and he's not hiding his excitement. France has legitimate title aspirations with a loaded roster and a critical qualification window ahead.

Victor Wembanyama has been spending the offseason in Paris, working out with Spurs staff who made the trip with him, biding his time before he can do what he says he's been genuinely missing. In six days, he gets to do it: put on a France jersey, walk into Štark Arena in Belgrade, and play basketball in the FIBA context that — by his own admission — he's been craving since the Paris Olympics ended.

"I do not miss the NBA right now, I miss FIBA basketball," Wembanyama told reporters, a quote that captured exactly why the upcoming window matters beyond just qualification points. This is a 22-year-old superstar at the height of his excitement for the international game.

A Loaded Roster for a Critical Window

France head coach Frédéric Fauthoux has assembled a formidable squad for the August window. Alongside Wembanyama, the roster features Rudy Gobert — one of the premier defensive centers in NBA history — and Evan Fournier, who rounds out one of the most experienced frontlines in international basketball. It will mark the first time this trio has suited up together since the 2024 Paris Olympics, where France won gold on home soil in front of a euphoric crowd.

Fauthoux, who is coaching Wembanyama for the first time in a competitive setting, inherits a program at an interesting inflection point: established enough to contend, young enough in Wemby that its ceiling is not yet defined. The Aug. 20 Belgrade exhibition against Serbia offers an early look at how his vision for the team translates onto the court.

The August Schedule

France's August schedule breaks down as follows: an exhibition in Belgrade against Serbia on Aug. 20, followed by a second exhibition in Orleans on Aug. 23, then the real thing — FIBA World Cup qualifier road game vs. Slovenia on Aug. 27 and away vs. Sweden on Aug. 30. France enters the window 5-1 in Group L and in a strong position to continue its qualification campaign. A solid window could effectively secure their spot in the next round.

The qualifiers carry more weight than any exhibition, but the Serbia game serves a purpose: competitive sharpening for a squad that hasn't played together since last summer. Wemby needs live action. Fauthoux needs to see how his rotations and systems hold up against a quality opponent. Serbia — with its own NBA talent pipeline — provides exactly the right caliber of test.

What This Means for the Spurs

For San Antonio, Wembanyama's FIBA participation is both an obligation and a positive sign. The fact that he's engaged, healthy, and playing through the August window before training camp opens Sept. 22 for overseas-game teams suggests the Spurs will have a fully prepared Wemby when camp begins. The team staff traveling to Paris with him — coaches and support personnel — underscores how closely the Spurs are monitoring his development and conditioning even during the offseason.

What's Next

The Belgrade exhibition on Aug. 20 will be the first chance to assess the new-look France roster under Fauthoux, and Wembanyama's performance will be watched closely by scouts and fans worldwide. After the qualifiers Aug. 27 and 30, France will have a clearer picture of its standing in Group L. Wemby returns to the Spurs with FIBA minutes under his belt and Spurs training camp just around the corner. For a franchise that has built its future around him, every healthy appearance is a good sign. Six days and counting.
