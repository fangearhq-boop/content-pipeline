Captain Wemby and Unbeatable France Take on Sweden in FIBA World Cup Qualifier

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | August 30, 2026

Victor Wembanyama · France National Team · FIBA Basketball · San Antonio Spurs

Victor Wembanyama does not miss the NBA right now. He said so himself — and if you have watched France play in the 2027 FIBA Basketball World Cup European Qualifiers over the past two weeks, you understand exactly what he means. Today, with France 6-1 in Group L and riding a wave of dominant basketball, Captain Wemby leads his team into another qualifying match against Sweden.

A Dominant Window for France

France enters today's game against Sweden with the best record in Group L at 6-1, and the performances have been nothing short of spectacular. Wembanyama, named France's captain for this qualifying window, has been the engine driving the charge.

In France's most recent game, a 92-67 demolition of Slovenia, Wembanyama put up 22 points and nine rebounds on 60 percent shooting from the field — the kind of performance that makes even the most devoted NBA fans grateful for these international windows. He is not just present; he is dominant.

Before that, France survived a genuine thriller against Serbia — the same Nikola Jokic-led team that has tormented French squads in recent years. Final score: France 91, Serbia 90. Jokic posted 34 points, 13 rebounds, and five assists in a performance that would have won almost any other game. It did not win this one. Wembanyama contributed 18 points and the resilience to match Jokic's brilliance with a team-wide effort that held the lead late.

Wemby's Own Words

"I do not miss the NBA right now, I miss FIBA basketball," Wembanyama said during the qualifying window — a quote that has circulated widely and captured something genuine about how the 22-year-old approaches international play.

For a player who has become one of the NBA's most scrutinized and celebrated stars, the FIBA stage offers something different: a chance to represent his country, play alongside childhood friends and national team veterans like Rudy Gobert and Evan Fournier, and compete in arenas filled with fans who have watched him grow from a teenager playing in the French Pro A league into a generational talent.

France vs. Sweden — What to Expect

Sweden enters this matchup as a significantly weaker opponent on paper. France's depth — led by Wembanyama, Gobert, and Fournier — represents a considerable talent advantage, and the momentum from consecutive strong performances should carry over.

The game tips off at 3:30 PM UTC today. For American fans, that is 11:30 AM ET — a rare midday international window slot that should allow for live viewership before the afternoon NBA news cycle takes over.

A win would push France even further up Group L and strengthen their position toward World Cup qualification for the 2027 FIBA Basketball World Cup. The French program is not merely participating in these qualifiers — they are one of the favorites to make a deep run in the tournament itself, provided Wembanyama continues to develop at this trajectory.

The Bigger Picture for Wemby

For NBA watchers, these FIBA windows serve a secondary purpose: they provide an unfiltered look at Wembanyama in a different context. He won the NBA Defensive Player of the Year award last season, earned All-NBA honors, and led the San Antonio Spurs to the Western Conference Finals — performances that already secured his $252 million, five-year extension before age 22.

What's Next: After today's match against Sweden, France will conclude this qualifying window and return to the national league schedule. Wembanyama will then shift focus toward the San Antonio Spurs' training camp, which opens September 29 alongside the rest of the NBA. The 2026-27 season begins October 20 with Opening Night on NBC — and Wemby's second chapter is just getting started.
