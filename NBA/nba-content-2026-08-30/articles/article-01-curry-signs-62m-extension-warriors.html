Stephen Curry Signs 1-Year, $62.6M Extension — Staying a Warrior for One More Historic Season

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | August 30, 2026

Golden State Warriors · NBA Contracts · Stephen Curry

The moment the calendar flipped to August 29, Stephen Curry's extension window opened — and he didn't hesitate. Within hours, the Golden State Warriors and their franchise cornerstone agreed to a one-year, $62.6 million contract extension, ensuring the greatest shooter in NBA history will spend at least one more season right where he's always belonged.

The deal, confirmed by ESPN's Adrian Wojnarowski via Curry's agent Jeff Austin of Octagon, keeps Curry under contract through the 2026-27 season. It is, by any measure, a historic agreement — and not just for what it means to the Warriors faithful.

A Record-Breaking Contract

When the 2026-27 season tips off, Curry will become the first player in NBA history to earn more than $60 million in a single season. That milestone places him in a financial stratosphere that the league has never seen — and reflects both his sustained dominance and the NBA's ever-expanding salary cap.

The extension also propels Curry into a very exclusive club of all-time earners. With this deal, he becomes just the fourth player in league history to surpass $500 million in career earnings, joining LeBron James, Paul George, and Kevin Durant on that short list.

Why only one year, not the two-year maximum that had been widely discussed? The NBA's over-38 rule is the answer. The rule prevents teams from offering multi-year extensions that would carry a player past his 38th birthday at a certain length. For Curry, currently 36, any extension of three or more years would trigger the restriction. The one-year deal is the maximum he could sign this summer — and it gets done immediately.

The Only Franchise He Has Ever Known

In a league that has seen constant movement — superstars chasing rings, mega-deals taking players to new markets, loyalty tested at every turn — Stephen Curry has been the rare exception. Drafted by Golden State with the No. 7 pick in 2009, he has never worn another uniform. Not once. Not even when the Warriors went through lean years, and not when bigger markets or bigger checks beckoned.

That loyalty has been repaid many times over. Four NBA championships. Two MVP awards. The greatest shooting revolution in basketball history. And now, a contract that rewards him for everything he's given the Bay Area.

"Pretty confident" was how Warriors General Manager Mike Dunleavy Jr. described the team's position as recently as this past week. As it turned out, that confidence was well-placed — the deal came together on day one of the window.

What This Means for the Warriors

Golden State finished last season at 37-45, missing the playoffs. With Jimmy Butler arriving via trade and Curry locked in, the Warriors have retooled around their icon for what could be one final championship run.

The Christmas Day schedule offers a preview of the stakes: Golden State is set to host the Denver Nuggets on December 25, a marquee matchup between Curry and Nikola Jokic that will be one of the season's most-watched regular-season games.

At 36 — and shooting 42% from three last season — Curry shows no meaningful signs of decline. His combination of shooting, off-ball movement, and court vision remains unmatched, and for one more season, the Warriors will build their identity around the shooter who made them a dynasty.

What's Next

Training camps open September 29, with veterans able to report a day earlier on September 28. The Warriors will spend the preseason — beginning October 3 — integrating their new pieces around Curry before Opening Night on October 20.

The larger question of Curry's future beyond 2026-27 will resurface once this season concludes. At that point, he becomes an unrestricted free agent for the first time in his career. But for now, that's a conversation for another day. Today, the message is simple: Steph Curry is a Golden State Warrior — and he's got one more year to add to a legacy that already stands alone.
