Cunningham Drops 26, Collins Goes Off as Pistons Stay Perfect With 106-93 Win Over 76ers

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | October 26, 2026

Detroit's early-season excellence continues. Cade Cunningham and John Collins provided the firepower as the Pistons rolled Philadelphia on Sunday.

The Detroit Pistons are playing like a team with something to prove. After a 60-win regular season last year that ended too early in the playoffs, the Pistons have come out firing in 2026-27 — and Sunday's 106-93 dismantling of the Philadelphia 76ers was the latest entry on their highlight reel.

Cade Cunningham authored 26 points in another efficient, assertive performance, while John Collins supplied the Detroit frontcourt with a double-double: 16 points and 11 rebounds. The Pistons were in command for large stretches of the game, with Detroit's defense keeping Philadelphia at bay throughout.

Cunningham Playing Like an MVP Candidate

Heading into this season, the chatter around Cade Cunningham has grown louder. Preseason rankings placed him among the top 10 players in the NBA for 2026-27, and he has done nothing to contradict that assessment in the early going. Sunday's performance against Philadelphia was another display of his ability to score, push the pace, and command a game on both ends of the floor.

Cunningham came into the season off a year in which Detroit leaned on him heavily — and he delivered, helping build the Pistons into one of the Eastern Conference's top-seeded teams. The conference semifinals loss to Cleveland still stings, but Detroit's fast start suggests they have internalized that setback as motivation rather than trauma.

Collins the Perfect Partner

John Collins' double-double may have been the quieter storyline Sunday, but it was every bit as important. Collins — 16 points, 11 rebounds — gave Detroit the interior presence to dominate the boards and convert second-chance opportunities. His ability to stretch the floor while also protecting the paint is a major reason the Pistons are difficult to defend.

The Cunningham-Collins combination makes Detroit a genuinely dangerous unit. Collins frees up driving lanes for Cunningham by pulling big men to the perimeter; Cunningham's scoring gravity opens the paint for Collins to attack. It is a partnership that works, and it was on full display against the 76ers.

Philadelphia Struggles to Find Its Footing

The 76ers, meanwhile, continue to face questions about their roster construction and their early-season form. Sunday's 93-point outing against a Detroit defense that takes those low-output performances seriously was not an encouraging sign. Philadelphia has the pieces to get better, but there is work to do.

For Detroit, this win was simply more confirmation of what they believe about themselves. The Pistons are not sneaking up on anyone anymore — they are a known commodity, and they are still winning.

What's Next

Detroit's schedule continues with several key tests in the coming week. The NBA Cup group stage opens Thursday, Oct. 30, giving the Pistons an opportunity to add another layer of hardware to what they hope will be a rewarding campaign. For now, the message coming out of the Motor City is clear: this team is serious, and they are just getting started.
