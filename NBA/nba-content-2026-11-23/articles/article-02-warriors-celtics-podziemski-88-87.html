Podziemski Leads Warriors Past Celtics 88-87, Golden State Ends Five-Game Losing Streak

By Marcus Cole | November 23, 2026 | Hoop Heroes

Brandin Podziemski scored 18 points with seven rebounds and Golden State survived a 88-87 thriller over the Boston Celtics on Sunday night to snap a five-game losing streak. It was an ugly, grind-it-out victory — but after five consecutive losses, the Warriors would take it without hesitation.

Jayson Tatum led all scorers with 27 points for Boston, but the Celtics fell to their fourth straight defeat — their longest losing streak in five years — in a game that came down to defensive execution in the final moments. Golden State found a way. Boston did not.

Podziemski Answers When It Matters

Podziemski's 18-point, seven-rebound performance was the clearest storyline of the night. The young guard has taken on a larger role with Golden State this season, and on Sunday he delivered in a moment the Warriors desperately needed.

His play down the stretch — steady, composed, making the right read — reminded observers of the qualities that made Golden State gamble on his development. When bigger veterans around him have struggled, Podziemski has been a reliable source of production. Sunday was his clearest statement yet that he belongs at this level.

Boston's Cold Stretch Deepens

For Boston, Sunday's loss raised legitimate questions. Four consecutive defeats is not what anyone expected from a Celtics team that entered the season with championship aspirations. The fact that Tatum put up 27 points and Boston still could not convert a one-point victory underscores the team-wide issues that have emerged over this stretch.

Their longest skid in five years is a data point worth noting. The last time Boston dropped four or more in a row, the team eventually found its footing — but the current circumstances, with roster continuity and a full training camp, make this slide more puzzling than the previous ones.

Golden State's Narrow Path

The Warriors' 88 points were not a ringing endorsement of their offense, and the one-point margin of victory does not disguise the work that still needs to be done. But in the NBA, games count the same regardless of aesthetics, and Golden State gets to move forward having stopped a five-game bleed.

The number of teams capable of scoring in the high 80s and winning in today's pace-driven league is small, but Golden State's defensive identity has always been able to suppress opponents enough to make it work. Sunday was a reminder that when their defense is right, they remain hard to beat.

What's Next

Golden State will look to build on their win as the schedule advances into late November. For Boston, this week's games represent a critical opportunity to reverse a troubling trend before it becomes a full-blown crisis. The Celtics have the talent to right the ship, but they will need sharper execution on both ends to do it. The Warriors, meanwhile, will take their one-point win and try to find some momentum heading into the stretch.
