Bam Adebayo Triple-Double Extends Heat's Win Streak to Seven as Miami Beats Thunder 123-113

By Jake Torres | November 23, 2026 | Hoop Heroes

Bam Adebayo delivered a 24-point triple-double and Pelle Larsson added 21 points off the bench as the Miami Heat beat a shorthanded Oklahoma City Thunder 123-113 on Sunday night, extending their winning streak to seven games and further cementing their place as one of the Eastern Conference's most dangerous teams.

Miami was not at full strength either, yet the result was never seriously in doubt after the third quarter. The Heat's ability to grind out wins in adverse circumstances has become the defining characteristic of this run — seven games, different contributors stepping up each night, and a team-wide discipline that has made Miami increasingly difficult to beat.

Adebayo's Complete Night

Adebayo's triple-double was his first of the 2026-27 season, and it came at the right time. The All-Star center was active in every dimension of the game — scoring efficiently, controlling the glass on both ends and distributing to open teammates when Oklahoma City's defense collapsed on him.

His 24 points came on quality looks, not volume, and his defensive presence made life difficult for Thunder bigs trying to establish any interior rhythm. When Adebayo plays like this — covering the floor in all directions — Miami becomes a genuinely difficult team to scheme against.

Larsson Provides Off-Bench Spark

Pelle Larsson's 21 points off the bench gave Miami the kind of secondary punch that has fueled this winning run. The Heat have consistently found contributors beyond their starting five throughout the streak, and Larsson's performance against Oklahoma City continued that pattern.

The Thunder, playing without several key rotation pieces, could not match Miami's depth. Oklahoma City's regular season has featured flashes of their usual defensive intensity, but on Sunday night they had no answer for a Heat team firing on multiple cylinders.

What Seven in a Row Means

Seven consecutive wins is the longest active streak in the NBA right now. Miami has won these games against a mix of opponents and roster configurations, which suggests something real rather than a favorable schedule. The Eastern Conference standings are shifting, and the Heat are rising.

What is notable about this run is what has not happened — no single player carrying the team on a nightly basis, no one-man shows, just consistent execution on both ends and a rotating cast of contributors delivering when called upon. That formula is harder to replicate than a star-led surge, and it is a reason to take Miami seriously as the calendar moves toward December.

What's Next

The Heat will look to extend their streak when they return to action later this week. Miami's upcoming schedule will give a clearer picture of whether this run holds up against stiffer competition. For Oklahoma City, the loss was part of a rough stretch on the road, and they will need to regroup for the stretch run. The Heat, meanwhile, have earned the right to be mentioned among the East's legitimate contenders.
