Nickeil Alexander-Walker Scores 27 as Hawks Open Fourth Quarter on 18-0 Run, Beat Pistons 107-97

By Jake Torres | November 23, 2026 | Hoop Heroes

Nickeil Alexander-Walker scored 27 points and Atlanta opened the fourth quarter on a stunning 18-0 run to put away the Detroit Pistons 107-97 on Sunday, in a game that looked like it might be close until Atlanta decided it was not.

Detroit's Cade Cunningham was held to just five points — one of the more surprising individual performances of the day — as the Hawks' defense neutralized the Pistons' most important offensive weapon and the fourth-quarter surge did the rest.

Nickeil Leads the Charge

Alexander-Walker's 27 points were a team-high and represented his clearest statement of intent as a go-to scorer this season. The guard has shown the ability to get hot in stretches, and on Sunday he carried the Atlanta offense during the crucial fourth-quarter run that broke the game open.

His shot-making during that 18-0 swing was the defining factor. Detroit had no answer. When Alexander-Walker is in a rhythm from the perimeter, he generates a kind of contagious momentum — Atlanta's pace quickens, their defensive intensity rises, and opponents can find themselves buried before they process what happened.

The 18-0 Quarter-Opening Run

An 18-0 run to begin any quarter is rare in the modern NBA, where the pace of play and offensive efficiency of most teams prevent long droughts. For Detroit, it was a catastrophic start to the final period, turning what had been a competitive game into a double-digit defeat before they could call a timeout and regroup.

These kinds of runs are part scoring surge and part defensive lockdown. Atlanta's energy level shifted noticeably at the opening of the fourth, and Detroit's offensive execution fell apart simultaneously. It was the kind of sequence that decides games long before the final buzzer.

Cunningham Held in Check

Cade Cunningham's five-point performance was a significant factor in the outcome. Detroit's franchise cornerstone rarely disappears like this, and his struggles against Atlanta's defense reflect both a good defensive game plan and a difficult night from the field.

When Cunningham is not producing, the Pistons' offense becomes considerably more predictable. Detroit lacks the depth to absorb a quiet night from their best player without it showing in the final score, and Sunday's result was a clear illustration of that vulnerability.

What's Next

Atlanta will look to build on a satisfying Sunday performance as the week continues. For the Pistons, Sunday's loss is a reminder of how much they depend on Cunningham's production and how important it is to find secondary scoring options. The Hawks have shown they can deliver nights like this — the challenge is consistency across the full schedule.
