### STORY 1: Curry Extension — 8 Days

**Post 1 (Long-Form — 9:00 AM ET)**
Eight days. That's all that separates us from Warriors media day on September 28 — and Stephen Curry still hasn't signed the two-year, $136.7 million extension that would make him the first NBA player to clear $600 million in career earnings.

Both sides want it done. Curry has said publicly he has zero interest in testing free agency and wants to finish his career as a Warrior. The Warriors have given him full structural control: he decides the length, the breakdown, and therefore how much flexibility Golden State carries into next offseason.

Here's why the structure matters beyond the headline number: a full two-year extension drops the Warriors' projected cap space from roughly $130 million to somewhere between $45 million and $65 million. Draymond Green and Jimmy Butler III are both in the final year of their deals. What Curry signs — or doesn't sign — sets the table for every other decision this franchise makes in the next 24 months.

Eight days to media day. The clock is ticking.

### STORY 2: DOJ Clippers Probe — Week Seven

**Post 3 (Long-Form — 11:00 AM ET)**
The federal investigation into the Los Angeles Clippers has entered its seventh week — and a new development is quietly reshaping the case.

Joseph Sanberg, convicted of wire fraud and sentenced to 14 years in federal prison, has been reported as a potential cooperating witness in the EDNY probe into the Clippers' alleged no-show endorsement deals for Kawhi Leonard. The alleged scheme involved funneling approximately $18 million to Leonard through four companies — Aspiration Partners, Boingo Wireless, Daktronics, and Lockton Insurance — as a way to circumvent the NBA's salary cap.

The NBA already handed down its penalties: $30 million fine for the team, five consecutive first-round draft picks stripped (2029–2033), owner Steve Ballmer suspended one year, and Leonard fined $700,000. His trade to the Toronto Raptors has been ratified.

But the federal case moves on its own timeline. No charges have been filed. The investigation is described as in its "earliest stages." And in 30 days, the 2026-27 NBA season opens — with the Clippers beginning a new chapter while this case remains very much unresolved.

### STORY 3: Duren QO — 11 Days

**Post 5 (Long-Form — 11:00 AM ET)**
Eleven days. The October 1 qualifying offer deadline is coming — and the Jalen Duren standoff with the Detroit Pistons hasn't moved.

Here's where things stand: the Pistons' last reported offer was five years, $190 million ($38M per year). Duren wants at least $200 million. The gap hasn't publicly narrowed. And Marc Stein has reported that Duren is "increasingly prepared" to accept his $9.6 million qualifying offer and become an unrestricted free agent next summer.

If Duren accepts the QO: he plays this season in Detroit for $9.6 million, then hits the open market. The Pistons lose all matching rights. And nine teams — led by the Sacramento Kings — are reportedly ready to offer him a max contract.

That's the math Detroit is trying to avoid. Eleven days left to offer something that changes it.

### STORY 4: Knicks Banner Night — 30 Days

If the first night of the NBA season isn't already on your calendar, it should be.

### STORY 5: DeRozan to Nuggets

**Post 9 (Long-Form — 1:00 PM ET)**
DeMar DeRozan is going to Denver.

The six-time All-Star has agreed to a one-year, $3.9 million deal with the Nuggets — a veteran minimum contract for a player who averaged 18.4 points on nearly 50 percent shooting last season. At 37, DeRozan could have taken the money from multiple teams that wanted him. Instead, he took the pitch from Nikola Jokic, Jamal Murray, and Aaron Gordon.

The Nuggets needed a wing scorer after sending Peyton Watson to Cleveland in a multi-team trade earlier this offseason. DeRozan gives Denver a proven shot creator who can function off the ball, handle fourth-quarter possessions, and contribute immediately without a long adjustment curve.

Is $3.9 million a bargain for a former All-Star? Almost certainly. Is this the kind of contract a player signs when they want to compete for a championship? Absolutely.
