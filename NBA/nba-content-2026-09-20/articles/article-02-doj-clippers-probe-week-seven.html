DOJ Clippers Probe Enters Week Seven — What a Cooperating Witness Changes

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | September 20, 2026

Los Angeles Clippers

The Eastern District of New York's criminal investigation into the Los Angeles Clippers has entered its seventh week. No charges have been filed. The investigation remains, by official description, in its "earliest stages." But a new element has quietly shifted the case's texture: the reported emergence of a potential cooperating witness.

Joseph Sanberg — convicted of wire fraud and sentenced to 14 years in federal prison — has been identified in reports as a figure who may be cooperating with prosecutors in the EDNY probe. What this means for the trajectory of the case is not yet clear. Cooperating witnesses can accelerate investigations, provide testimony that aligns charges with specific individuals, and give prosecutors the narrative thread they need to build an indictment. They can also be complicated: cooperation comes with conditions, timelines, and its own legal negotiations.

What the Investigation Is About

The underlying facts of the NBA's probe have been public since the league announced its penalties in August. The NBA found that the Clippers, team owner Steve Ballmer, and Kawhi Leonard arranged a series of endorsement deals with four companies — Aspiration Partners, Boingo Wireless, Daktronics, and Lockton Insurance — that funneled an estimated $18 million to Leonard as a way to circumvent the salary cap. The deals were alleged to be "no-show" arrangements: Leonard received the payments without performing the services the contracts described.

The NBA's response was severe. The franchise was fined $30 million. Five consecutive first-round draft picks were stripped, running from 2029 through 2033. Ballmer was suspended from all team and league activities for one year. team president Lawrence Frank received a six-month suspension; executive Andy Zucker received one year; scout Gary Robertson five years. Leonard was fined $700,000. His trade to the Toronto Raptors — which had been pending ratification — was allowed to proceed.

The federal investigation goes further. The Department of Justice is examining whether the scheme constitutes criminal conduct, not merely a league rule violation. The four companies involved with the alleged endorsement deals — at least one of which, Daktronics, has confirmed receiving requests for information and cooperating with both the NBA and federal investigators — are presumably part of the documentary and testimonial record prosecutors are assembling.

What "Earliest Stages" Really Means

Federal investigations rarely announce their pace. The language of "earliest stages" is standard prosecutorial caution — it does not mean the case is moving slowly or that charges are unlikely. Major financial crime cases at the EDNY have moved from "earliest stages" to indictment in as little as six months; others have stretched past two years. The EDNY is one of the country's most experienced and aggressive federal prosecutors' offices for white-collar and financial crime cases.

The timeline that matters most is not when charges might come, but when the investigation reaches the point at which prosecutors have made a decision. Cases with cooperating witnesses inside the alleged scheme often accelerate that timeline, because the witness provides context that documentary evidence alone cannot. If Sanberg has firsthand knowledge of how the alleged deals were structured, communicated, or concealed, his cooperation could substantially narrow the gap between "earliest stages" and something more consequential.

The Season Opens in 30 Days

In 30 days, the 2026-27 NBA season tips off. The Clippers begin it without Kawhi Leonard, without five future first-round picks, with their owner suspended, and with a federal investigation still actively running in the background. The team has a roster and a schedule. They will play basketball. But the institutional cloud above the franchise is genuinely unusual in league history — no team has entered a season under active federal criminal scrutiny quite like this.

For the Clippers as a basketball franchise, the investigation is not the team's fault in the sense that the players on the floor had nothing to do with the scheme. For the organization, however, it is a weight that will not lift until the federal case reaches a resolution. Whether that resolution comes before the season, during it, or after it is not something prosecutors will announce in advance.

What's Next

The EDNY probe continues. No charges have been filed, and no timetable has been given. The cooperating witness development, if confirmed, is a meaningful shift in the case's architecture — but federal investigations do not operate on press conference timelines. The Clippers' media day is September 28. Training camp opens September 29. The season begins October 20. Every one of those dates arrives while this case is still open.
