8 Days and No Deal: Curry's Silence Has the Warriors' Offseason on Hold

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | September 20, 2026

Golden State Warriors

Eight days. Warriors media day is September 28, and Stephen Curry still has not signed the two-year, $136.7 million contract extension that would make him the first player in NBA history to eclipse $600 million in career on-court earnings. One more day has passed, and the silence from both sides has grown a full day louder.

The facts of the matter have not changed. Both Curry and the Warriors have confirmed through public statements and league sources that they share a mutual desire for Curry to finish his career in Golden State. Curry told CNBC he has no interest in exploring free agency. The Warriors, for their part, have given him something rare: full structural control over how the extension is designed. He gets to choose the term, the AAV breakdown, and — therefore — how much cap flexibility Golden State carries into the offseason that follows.

The Clock Is No Longer Background Noise

At 11 days out, the countdown was a footnote. At eight days, it is the story. Every reporter covering the Warriors has now placed September 28 on their calendar in red. Every league source knows the media day question is already written: "Steph, can you give us an update on the extension?"

That question is not difficult to answer. It is either "we got it done" or "we're still working on it." Both Curry and Warriors general manager Mike Dunleavy understand what the second answer does to a media day. It turns a celebration of a new season — the preseason opener is October 3, the regular season October 20 — into a contract negotiation conducted in front of 200 reporters and a dozen television cameras. Neither party benefits from that.

The pull toward closing before September 28 is not contractual. There is no deadline that disappears at midnight on September 27. But the informal pressure of a media day is real, and both camps have been navigating these situations long enough to understand the gravity.

Why Structure Matters More Than the Number

The headline figure — $136.7 million over two years — gets the attention. The structure is where the real decisions are made.

If Curry signs the full two-year maximum extension, the Warriors' projected cap space heading into next offseason drops from approximately $130 million to somewhere between $45 million and $65 million. That range is not a rounding error. It is the difference between a franchise that can make a transformational roster move and one that is limited to role-player additions and veteran minimums.

The context that makes structure especially consequential: Draymond Green and Jimmy Butler III are both entering the final year of their current contracts. After the 2026-27 season, neither is under any obligation to stay. If Golden State finishes the year with Curry locked in for two more seasons at max money and Green and Butler on the free agent market, the franchise needs flexibility to either retain its core or pivot. A smaller cap number buys different options than a larger one.

Curry is 38. He has four championships. He is, by every measure, one of the three or four most decorated players in NBA history. He does not need this extension to establish a legacy. What the extension does is determine whether Golden State's next competitive window is built around his remaining years or begins to take shape after them.

The Hawaii Factor

If the extension is not signed before media day, the Warriors leave for training camp in Hawaii on September 29 with the question still open. BYU-Hawaii is a beautiful setting for a training camp and a uniquely difficult one for running contract negotiations. The distance from Bay Area media does not eliminate scrutiny — it just moves it.

Curry has operated in contract situations before with characteristic calm. His past extensions were handled without drama, completed efficiently, and announced with minimal fanfare. There is no indication from either camp that this extension is contentious. The remaining question is not "if" but "when," and the calendar is now eight days deep into an answer.

What's Next

Warriors media day is September 28. Training camp opens September 29 in Hawaii. The preseason opener is October 3; the regular season tips off October 20 in New York with the Knicks' banner ceremony. If the extension is signed before media day, Golden State enters camp with its leadership structure settled for the next two years. If not, the question follows the team to the Pacific. Eight days. The countdown will not wait.
