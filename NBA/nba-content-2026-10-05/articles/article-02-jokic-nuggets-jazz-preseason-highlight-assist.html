Jokic Steals the Preseason — 7 Assists in 14 Minutes as Jazz Top Nuggets 109-97

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | October 5, 2026

The Utah Jazz beat the Denver Nuggets 109-97 in Sunday's NBA preseason opener, but by Monday morning, nobody was talking about the score. They were talking about the pass.

In just 14 minutes of preseason action, Nikola Jokic dropped seven assists — capped by a behind-the-back, no-look dime to Cam Johnson in the corner for a three-pointer that sparked immediate reaction across basketball's online universe. Less than 24 hours into the preseason calendar, Jokic had already produced the clip of the week. Maybe the month. Possibly the season's early claimant for play of the year.

This is, of course, exactly what Jokic does.

The Pass That Needed No Introduction

The specifics of the play are almost secondary to the feeling it produces. Jokic, operating somewhere in the mid-post, with defenders draped around him, somehow sensed Johnson cutting in the corner, reversed the ball behind his back, and delivered it with perfect timing and weight. Johnson caught, set, and fired. Nothing but net.

Jokic finished with three points, three rebounds, and seven assists in his 14 minutes of floor time. Denver's coaching staff is clearly being deliberate with his minutes early in the preseason — a wise approach for a player whose workload management has been a talking point in recent seasons. He did not need the full game to remind the basketball world what he is capable of. He never does.

Denver's New-Look Supporting Cast

The Jazz win does raise legitimate questions about Denver's depth and rotation. Jokic remains the unquestioned foundation of everything the Nuggets do, but the players around him have shifted this offseason, and preseason is where chemistry and continuity are built. A loss to Utah in the first preseason game is not a crisis — it is information.

Head coach Michael Malone has spent training camp installing new pieces and testing different lineup combinations. The Nuggets will not look like a fully assembled product in October. No serious contender does. The question is how quickly they find their rhythm, and how effectively the new names around Jokic learn to play in the specific, space-and-read style that makes Denver's offense hum when Jokic is at the controls.

That process started Sunday. The Jazz deserved the win. But Jokic's 14 minutes were a clear reminder of why Denver is still among the short list of Western Conference teams worth taking seriously.

What's Next

Denver continues the preseason this week as the coaching staff continues to evaluate depth and fit. Jokic's minutes will increase gradually as Opening Night on October 20 approaches. If Sunday's first glimpse is any indication, the chemistry work is secondary to what happens when No. 15 has the ball with a second to make a decision. That part, as always, will take care of itself.
