LaMelo Ball's Timberwolves Debut Opens One of Basketball's Most Fascinating Questions

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | October 5, 2026

LaMelo Ball has played a lot of basketball in his life. He has played in Lithuania, in Australia, in Charlotte, and in all-star games and celebrity events and summer leagues and arenas across the continent. But he had never played in a Timberwolves uniform before this NBA preseason, and that first glimpse — LaMelo alongside Anthony Edwards, Karl-Anthony Towns, and Rudy Gobert — may be the single most intriguing early look of the entire 2026-27 preseason calendar.

The trade that brought Ball to Minnesota from Charlotte was completed in June, and reactions ranged from euphoric to skeptical. There is no question Minnesota acquired generational playmaking talent. The question has always been about fit.

Two Engines in the Same Car

LaMelo Ball and Anthony Edwards are two of the most ball-dominant players in the NBA. Both are at their best when they have freedom to create, attack, and improvise. Edwards is a volume scorer who makes plays off the dribble and whose athleticism and finishing ability at the rim are among the most punishing in the league. Ball is a court-vision virtuoso — a player who sees angles that do not appear to exist and whose three-point shooting and playmaking off the bounce have made him one of the most entertaining players in basketball.

Putting them on the same floor requires answers. Who initiates? Who defers in half-court sets? How does the spacing work when both want to operate in similar areas? These are legitimate questions, and they will not be fully answered in preseason. They might not be fully answered until February. But preseason is where the initial language between players begins to develop, and this first official look at LaMelo and Edwards sharing a lineup told the story of a partnership still finding its footing — and one that has the upside to be genuinely special.

The Foundation Is Already There

Whatever the growing pains, Minnesota is not starting from scratch. Edwards arrived in this league as a No. 1 overall pick and has steadily become one of the most dominant two-way players in the Western Conference. Towns provides interior scoring and perimeter shooting at a level few centers in history have matched. Gobert remains one of the best defensive anchors in basketball, a player who transforms what a team can do on that end of the floor.

LaMelo does not need to redefine this team. He needs to add a dimension — a creator and ball-mover who takes pressure off Edwards in the half court, stretches defenses with his shooting, and makes the whole operation faster and more unpredictable. If he can do that, Minnesota becomes one of the most difficult matchups in the conference.

What's Next

The Timberwolves have a full preseason slate ahead before their regular season opens on October 20. The Ball-Edwards partnership will continue to develop with every practice and every game rep. The early returns are just that — early. But for a basketball world that has been wondering what this combination looks like since the trade was announced, the wait is finally over. The experiment is underway.
