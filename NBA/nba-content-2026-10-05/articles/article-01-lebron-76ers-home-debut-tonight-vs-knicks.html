LeBron James Makes His 76ers Home Debut Tonight Against the Knicks

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | October 5, 2026

The basketball world has been waiting all summer for this. Tonight, at 7 p.m. ET, LeBron James walks out of the tunnel at Wells Fargo Center wearing a Philadelphia 76ers uniform for the first time in an official game. The reigning champion New York Knicks are in town. The crowd will be sold out. And the most historic player in NBA history is about to begin his 24th season — a number that has never existed before.

It is impossible to separate the basketball from the moment. When the 76ers announced they had assembled LeBron James, Joel Embiid, and Jaylen Brown around Tyrese Maxey, the reaction from the league was immediate. Rivals recalibrated. Oddsmakers moved. Philadelphia fans, starved for a championship run that keeps arriving without the title, allowed themselves to believe again. And nearly 10,000 of them showed up to an open scrimmage last week just for a glimpse.

A Lineup Built to Win Now

The projected starting lineup for tonight reads like an All-Star ballot with no filler: LeBron James, Joel Embiid, Jaylen Brown, Tyrese Maxey, and sophomore VJ Edgecombe. It is the kind of construction that only happens once or twice in a generation — a roster assembled by a franchise willing to bet everything on a championship window that may already be at its widest.

Embiid, who spent last season dealing with a cascade of health issues, has reportedly been one of the standout performers of training camp. He shed considerable weight ahead of the preseason, looked explosive in practice, and received enthusiastic reviews from head coach Nick Nurse. Whatever questions surrounded his health heading into the summer, the early returns from camp have been the most encouraging sign in years. If Embiid is fully healthy and LeBron is healthy — and that second condition seems increasingly plausible at 41 — Philadelphia is the team no one wants to face in a seven-game series.

Jaylen Brown came from Boston in a stunning offseason deal that the Celtics will spend years second-guessing. Brown at 29 is still in the prime of his career and brings a defensive intensity and scoring versatility that Philadelphia desperately needed alongside Embiid's post game and Maxey's speed. There is depth here, and there is star power, and there is a head coach in Nurse who has proven he can win a championship.

What 24 Means

LeBron James in his 24th season is not a curiosity or a nostalgia act. It is one of the genuinely uncharted territories of professional sports. No player in league history has reached this number, and the fact that LeBron is here — healthy, motivated, and on a roster with genuine championship upside — is a testament to a commitment to longevity that has no parallel in the sport.

He arrived in Philadelphia this summer with clear eyes about what he wanted. Not just to play, but to compete. The 76ers gave him the environment to do exactly that. And while preseason outcomes are meaningless in the standings, tonight's game matters because it is the first real test of how this machine looks when it turns on. How do LeBron and Embiid play off each other in live action? How does Brown move without the ball? How does Maxey respond to sharing creation duties with someone who has spent two decades doing the same job?

The Knicks are the perfect first opponent — defending champions, a familiar Eastern Conference rival, and a team with its own star power in Jalen Brunson, OG Anunoby, and Josh Hart. This game will have the feel of a playoff preview even in October, and the crowd at Wells Fargo Center will treat it like one.

What's Next

Tonight is just the beginning. The 76ers have three more preseason games after this one, culminating in a road trip that sets up Opening Night — October 20 at Madison Square Garden, when Philadelphia goes to New York for the Knicks' Banner Night. LeBron James walking into MSG as a member of the Sixers, in the building where New York raises its first championship banner since 1973, will be one of the great atmospheric moments the league has produced in years.

But first, tonight. Seven PM Eastern. NBA TV. The 24th season begins in Philadelphia, and the league will be watching every minute of it.
