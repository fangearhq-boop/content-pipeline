Monday NBA Preseason: Five Games, One Historic Debut, and 15 Days Until Opening Night

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | October 5, 2026

Monday night gives basketball fans exactly what the preseason is supposed to provide: multiple games, fresh storylines, and the first extended look at rosters that have been rebuilt, retooled, or reinforced over a busy offseason. Five games are on the schedule for October 5, and together they paint a picture of a league that is fully awake and two weeks away from the real thing.

The headliner is in Philadelphia. LeBron James and the 76ers host the Knicks at 7 p.m. ET on NBA TV in the game that effectively launched the preseason's marquee storyline — the first official glimpse at the loaded Sixers starting lineup that has had the Eastern Conference reconfiguring its championship projections since June. That game alone would be enough to anchor the night.

Beyond Philadelphia

While Philly dominates the conversation, the rest of Monday's slate offers genuine intrigue.

Minnesota visits Milwaukee in a game that pits two overhauled rosters against each other. The Timberwolves continue to work in LaMelo Ball alongside Anthony Edwards and the rest of their newly constructed roster, while the Bucks — who enter 2026-27 with renewed ambitions of their own — get their second preseason test. These are two teams that could meet in a playoff series this spring, and every preseason game is another chapter in the scouting report.

In Detroit, the Pistons host the Phoenix Suns. Detroit is one of the league's stories of the summer — Jalen Duren's five-year, $200 million extension solidified a core that believes it is ready to compete for more than just a playoff spot. The Pistons are a franchise in ascent, and preseason games at Little Caesars Arena are a chance to feel that energy in real time.

Memphis heads to Atlanta in a matchup of two teams still figuring out their identity for 2026-27, and out west, the Lakers travel to Sacramento for a late-night tip-off that closes the Monday slate.

The Calendar

Opening Night is October 20. That is fifteen days from today. The NBA regular season starts with the Knicks hosting the 76ers — Banner Night at Madison Square Garden — in a game that will rival the energy of any event the league has produced in years. LeBron James making his first trip to MSG as a 76er, on the night New York raises its first championship banner since 1973, is the kind of appointment television that reminds everyone why this sport pulls people in and does not let go.

Between now and then, preseason is the only basketball available. Monday night offers five servings of it. That is more than enough to hold the attention of any fan counting down the days.

What's Next

The preseason calendar continues through October 17, giving rosters roughly two weeks to build chemistry before the real games begin. Watch for which teams look cohesive early, which new partnerships click quickly, and which rosters still have questions to answer. By the time October 20 arrives, the basketball world will have a clearer picture of where the championship race stands before it officially begins.
