NBA Training Camp Transactions: Nets Add Grant Nelson, Hornets Continue Pre-Camp Shuffle

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | September 7, 2026

With camp opening September 29, rosters are taking shape across the league. Brooklyn locked up a promising 7-footer on a Two-Way deal, while Charlotte keeps cycling through Exhibit 10 candidates.

The NBA's pre-training camp roster shuffle is in full swing, and two teams made notable moves this week as teams continue building out their 21-man camp rosters ahead of the September 29 opening.

The most significant transaction: the Brooklyn Nets have signed 24-year-old forward Grant Nelson to a Two-Way contract. The 7-footer impressed during a 10-day stint with the Nets at the end of the 2025-26 season, earning a longer look from Brooklyn's front office. The Nets are in full rebuild mode and actively identifying developmental talent, and Nelson fits the profile they are searching for.

Grant Nelson: A Two-Way Opportunity in Brooklyn

Nelson's path to a Two-Way deal is a classic developmental arc. The 7-footer earned a 10-day contract late last season and used it to demonstrate enough athleticism, shot-blocking potential, and court awareness to convince Brooklyn's coaching staff he was worth a longer commitment.

A Two-Way contract allows a player to split time between the NBA roster — the player can appear in up to 50 games for the NBA team — and their G-League affiliate. For Nelson, that means time with the Long Island Nets developing his skill set while remaining in proximity to the NBA team and its coaching staff.

At 24 with size and mobility, Nelson is exactly the kind of project that a rebuilding team should be investing in. If he develops his offensive game and proves he can defend at the NBA level, his Two-Way deal could convert into a standard contract within a year or two.

Charlotte's Exhibit 10 Carousel

The Charlotte Hornets have been among the league's most active teams in the Exhibit 10 market this pre-camp window. The team waived guard Jarkel Joiner — who had been signed just days earlier — and then brought Kyle Mangas back on a $1.36 million Exhibit 10 deal. Forward Josiah Allick was added to the camp roster and subsequently waived with a $91,000 G-League development bonus attached, directing him to the Greensboro Swarm.

Exhibit 10 deals are a nuanced tool that benefits both parties. For the team, they build G-League depth while keeping the main roster flexible. Players agree to a small bonus for reporting to the affiliate team if waived, giving them financial incentive to develop in the G-League rather than pursue opportunities elsewhere. Allick's $91,000 bonus upon spending 60 days with Greensboro is a good example of how these deals work in practice.

The Hornets have been aggressive in rotating through these deals this offseason, suggesting they are still searching for the right combination of players to fill their final camp spots.

The Broader Pre-Camp Picture

Across the league, teams are currently allowed to carry up to 21 players on their training camp rosters. The countdown to September 29 camp openings means front offices are making their final evaluation calls — who gets a camp invite, who is on a standard deal, who gets an Exhibit 10, and who is released outright.

For fringe players, the next three weeks are among the most important of their professional lives. A strong camp performance can earn a roster spot. A poor one can end a career. The transactions we see now — signings, waivers, Exhibit 10 deals — are the first moves in that evaluation process.

Rosters must be trimmed to 15 players by the regular-season start in late October. Between now and then, expect a steady stream of moves league-wide as camps shake out and preseason games provide coaches their final looks at the candidates competing for the last few spots.
