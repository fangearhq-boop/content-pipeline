Kevin Love Turns 38 Today — And a Minnesota Homecoming Might Be Next

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | September 7, 2026

The former All-Star big man and free agent is reportedly drawing mutual interest from the Timberwolves — the franchise where he had the best statistical seasons of his career before the LeBron trade.

Kevin Love turns 38 years old today. And if reports are accurate, the veteran forward may be spending the next chapter of his career back where his NBA story first flourished.

Love, who played for the Utah Jazz in 2025-26 before entering free agency, is drawing mutual interest from the Minnesota Timberwolves, according to multiple reports. Minnesota was the franchise where Love emerged as an elite player in his mid-twenties, and a reunion would represent one of the more sentimental roster moves of the offseason.

What Love Did in Minnesota

Love was drafted No. 5 overall by the Memphis Grizzlies in 2008 and traded to Minnesota on draft night. He spent his first six NBA seasons with the Timberwolves, and his best years statistically came in that purple and blue uniform.

In 2011-12, Love averaged 26.0 points and 12.5 rebounds per game over 55 games — a genuinely dominant double-double season that earned him an All-Star selection and significant recognition as one of the best power forwards in basketball. He followed it with 18.3 points and 14.0 rebounds in 2012-13. Love was a five-time All-Star during his prime years, and much of that prime played out in Minneapolis.

The 2014 trade that sent Love to Cleveland — where he would eventually win a championship with LeBron James and Kyrie Irving in 2016 — remains one of the most impactful moves of that era. But Love never forgot where he came from. He has spoken warmly of Minnesota in many interviews since, and the fanbase has retained a fondness for him in return.

What He Brings Now

Love at 38 is not the Love who terrified defenses in 2012. His scoring average has declined significantly since his Cavaliers heyday, and he has not averaged double-digit points since the 2021-22 season. His role has evolved into a veteran presence — a floor spacer who can hit mid-range shots, set high-post screens, and provide leadership in a locker room.

For the right team and the right role, that is still valuable. Minnesota has a young, athletic core that could benefit from a veteran big man who has been on championship teams and understands how to navigate a long season. If Love can give the Wolves 15-18 minutes per game as a shooting big off the bench, the fit makes sense.

The contract would almost certainly be a minimum deal. Love is not going to command significant money at this stage of his career, and that makes the risk for Minnesota essentially zero.

A Career Built on Resilience

Love's career has been defined as much by his perseverance as his statistics. He has been open about mental health struggles during his time in Cleveland, and he has spoken publicly about using those experiences to help others in the sport. His path from a dominant young force to a respected veteran who has seen everything the NBA has to offer is genuinely compelling.

Multiple teams have reportedly expressed interest beyond Minnesota, and nothing is official yet. But a return to the city where it all started — where the fans still cheer his name and where he built the foundation for everything that followed — would make for one of the nicer stories of training camp.

Happy birthday, Kevin Love. The next chapter may be closer to home than you think.

What's Next

Love's free agency decision could come before training camp opens September 29. The Timberwolves are expected to finalize their 15-man roster by mid-October ahead of the season opener. Watch for an official announcement from Minnesota's front office if a deal comes together in the coming weeks.
