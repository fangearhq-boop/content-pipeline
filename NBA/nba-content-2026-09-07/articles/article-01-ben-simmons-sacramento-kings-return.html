Ben Simmons Returns to the NBA, Signs One-Year Deal With Sacramento Kings

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | September 7, 2026

The former No. 1 overall pick is getting another shot after missing the entire 2025-26 season to rehab back and leg injuries. Sacramento signed him for $3.5 million on a one-year minimum deal.

Ben Simmons is returning to the NBA.

The Sacramento Kings have agreed to a one-year, $3.5 million contract with the former No. 1 overall pick, giving the 30-year-old his most significant opportunity to revive a career that has been derailed repeatedly by injury and controversy. Simmons missed the entire 2025-26 season while rehabilitating back and leg problems, leaving him on the outside of a league he once starred in looking in.

Multiple reports confirmed the deal, including ESPN's Marc Spears and Shams Charania, who noted that Kings general manager Scott Perry was impressed when Simmons worked out for the team in late August. A meeting followed, and Sacramento moved quickly to add Simmons before training camp opens on September 29.

A Career at a Crossroads

Simmons was selected No. 1 overall by the Philadelphia 76ers in 2016 and quickly established himself as one of the most unique players in basketball. A 6-foot-10 point guard capable of guarding four positions, facilitating from the perimeter, and finishing at the rim with authority, Simmons earned three All-Star selections and was widely considered one of the game's elite defensive playmakers during his peak years.

His 2019-20 season remains his career high-water mark: 16.4 points, 7.8 rebounds, and 8.0 assists per game, with a Defensive Player of the Year runner-up finish. But a well-documented inability to shoot from range — and a psychological hurdle that became central to a bitter public dispute with the 76ers — sent his career off the rails. He was traded to the Brooklyn Nets in the James Harden deal in 2022 and never fully recovered, dealing with back injuries that cost him most of his time in Brooklyn before he moved on to other organizations.

The 2025-26 season was lost entirely, and Simmons spent the year focused on his physical recovery and trying to put himself in position for exactly this kind of opportunity.

What Sacramento Gets

For the Kings, the risk is minimal and the upside is real. At $3.5 million on a one-year deal, Sacramento is paying near the veteran minimum for a player who, when healthy, projects as a starting-caliber defender and playmaker in the modern NBA.

Simmons' fit with the Kings is conceptually strong. Sacramento has emphasized defense and transition offense under their coaching staff, and Simmons' switchability — his ability to guard point guards, wings, and stretch fours — addresses a genuine team need. The Kings are a bubble playoff team looking for a differentiating edge, and a healthy Simmons at his defensive best would provide one.

The shooting questions remain. Simmons has never shot above 63 percent from the free-throw line and has attempted exactly one three-pointer in his career. Any offense he contributes will come from drives, cuts, and short-roll passes. The Kings will need to design around him accordingly.

But Perry's decision to act quickly suggests Sacramento's front office believes in what they saw during the workout. Kings head coach will now have the challenge of integrating Simmons into a rotation and slowly building his confidence back up.

A Redemption Arc Begins

There is something genuinely compelling about a player of Simmons' talent getting one more chance. He is 30 years old — still within the range where a healthy veteran defender and playmaker can contribute meaningfully — and the Sacramento market, while not a major market, offers the kind of low-pressure environment that could allow him to rebuild without the spotlight that followed him in Philadelphia and Brooklyn.

The Kings open training camp September 29. The preseason begins in early October. If Simmons can get through camp healthy and produce at something close to his former level, Sacramento may have found one of the offseason's hidden gems.

If not, the story of Ben Simmons' career will reach its final chapter after one forgettable season on the Sacramento floor. Either way, the league will be watching.
