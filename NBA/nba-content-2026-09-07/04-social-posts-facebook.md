### STORY 1: Ben Simmons — Kings Signing

**FB Long-Form**
Window: 10:00 AM ET

Ben Simmons is getting one more shot at an NBA career — and he's taking it with the Sacramento Kings.

The former No. 1 overall pick in the 2016 NBA Draft has agreed to a one-year, $3.5 million minimum contract with Sacramento, returning to the league after missing all of 2025-26 while rehabilitating back and leg injuries. He's 30 years old with nine years of service — this is very likely his last legitimate chance to prove he belongs.

What made the Kings interested? Kings GM Scott Perry was reportedly impressed when Simmons worked out for the team in late August, and a meeting between the two sides followed shortly after. Sacramento has a young, athletic core and a coaching staff that emphasizes defense and ball movement — areas where a healthy Simmons still projects as elite.

Here's the case for optimism: Simmons at his best is one of the most unique players in basketball. A 6'10" point guard who can guard four positions, run the break, and facilitate from anywhere on the floor. His 2019-20 season — 16.4 ppg, 7.8 rpg, 8.0 apg, Defensive Player of the Year runner-up — showed what he could be.

The questions, of course, are legitimate: Can he stay healthy? Can he function in a playoff environment without a reliable jump shot? Those doubts haven't gone away. But the price is right for Sacramento, and the upside is undeniable.

If Simmons can give the Kings 20-25 minutes of switchable defense and smart playmaking off the bench, this could be one of the best low-risk signings of the offseason. Training camp opens Sept. 29. Simmons' comeback story starts now.

### STORY 2: Michael Porter Jr. — Nets Trade Saga

**FB Long-Form**
Window: 12:00 PM ET

The Michael Porter Jr. situation in Brooklyn is one of the most compelling subplots heading into the 2026-27 NBA season — and it's only going to get louder.

MPJ just completed his best professional season, averaging a career-high 24.2 points per game with the Nets. He shot 46% from the field and 36% from three, and at 28, he's in the prime of his career. The problem? The Nets are clearly in rebuild mode, and Porter's $40.8 million salary — which expires at season's end — is a massive trade chip that competing teams are eyeing.

ESPN's Brian Windhorst was cautious: "I know everybody thinks that Porter's available — that would be news to me." But Marc Stein reported hearing "pushback about the Nets' willingness to part ways" at this past trade deadline. Those two readings aren't incompatible. Brooklyn may not want to move him right now, but the logic of trading an elite scorer at peak value, on an expiring contract, for future assets is hard to argue against.

The Golden State Warriors and Detroit Pistons have been the most consistently linked suitors. Both need scoring. Both have assets. The February 2027 trade deadline is the likely destination for any deal.

For now, MPJ heads into training camp as a Net. But every game he plays — every career-high scoring night, every efficient stretch — adds to his trade value. Brooklyn's front office has a fascinating decision to make: commit to Porter long-term with a new deal (he turned down their extension offer) or maximize his value in a trade.

The clock is ticking. This is the most interesting story in Brooklyn basketball heading into the new season.

### STORY 3: OKC/Spurs Title Odds

**FB Long-Form**
Window: 2:00 PM ET

With training camp just 22 days away, the NBA's title landscape is coming into focus — and two Western Conference powers are sitting at the very top of the oddsboards.

The Oklahoma City Thunder and San Antonio Spurs are currently listed as the favorites to win the 2026-27 NBA Championship, sitting above the defending champion New York Knicks, the Boston Celtics, the Philadelphia 76ers, and the Denver Nuggets in futures markets.

OKC's case is built around one of the most talented trios in basketball: Shai Gilgeous-Alexander, Chet Holmgren, and Jalen Williams. The Thunder won a championship with this group and are aiming for another. The offseason was not without sacrifice — Isaiah Joe, Aaron Wiggins, and Luguentz Dort were all dealt to avoid the NBA's second apron — but the front office kept Isaiah Hartenstein and Kenrich Williams while adding two top-20 draft picks. The core is intact, even if the depth took a hit.

The one real concern in OKC? Jalen Williams. The All-NBA wing missed 49 regular-season games last season dealing with wrist surgery and four separate hamstring strains. A fully healthy Williams is a difference-maker. A Williams operating at 80% is a vulnerability in a seven-game playoff series.

San Antonio's path runs through Victor Wembanyama's continued evolution. The Spurs were Western Conference Finalists last season and return most of their core. Wemby is the WCF opponent OKC faces on Opening Night, Oct. 20 — a preview of what could be the West's marquee matchup all season long.

Camp opens Sept. 29. The race for the 2026-27 championship begins then.

### STORY 4: Kevin Love — Timberwolves Interest

**FB Long-Form**
Window: 4:00 PM ET

Today is Kevin Love's 38th birthday — and if reports are accurate, he may be spending the 2026-27 season back where his NBA career truly began.

Love, who played for the Utah Jazz last season before becoming a free agent, is reportedly drawing mutual interest from the Minnesota Timberwolves. The Wolves were where Love established himself as an elite player, posting back-to-back seasons averaging over 26 points and 12 rebounds in 2011-12 and 2012-13, before a blockbuster trade sent him to Cleveland — where he'd win a championship alongside LeBron James.

At 38, Love is no longer the dominant force he was in his Wolves prime. He hasn't averaged double-digit points since 2021-22 and his role has evolved into a veteran shooting presence off the bench. But that's not nothing. A healthy Love shooting mid-range jumpers and high-post passes is still a useful NBA player, and Minnesota could use veteran stability as they push for a playoff return.

The Timberwolves connection makes emotional sense too. Minnesota fans have a long memory, and Love has never hidden his affection for the organization that gave him his shot.

Nothing is official yet, and Love has interest from multiple teams. But a return to Minnesota — the place he first made his name — would make for one of the nicer stories of the preseason.

Happy birthday, Kevin Love. The next chapter may be closer to home than you think.

### STORY 5: Training Camp Transactions

**FB Long-Form**
Window: 6:00 PM ET

Training camp is 22 days away, and the NBA's pre-camp roster shuffling is in full swing.

The Brooklyn Nets made a notable move this week, signing 24-year-old big man Grant Nelson to a Two-Way contract. Nelson, who stands 7 feet tall, earned the deal after impressing the Nets on a 10-day contract at the end of last season. Two-Way contracts allow players to split time between the NBA roster and the G-League, and Nelson's combination of size and mobility makes him an intriguing developmental piece for Brooklyn's young rebuild.

In Charlotte, the Hornets continue their aggressive Exhibit 10 campaign. The team waived Jarkel Joiner shortly after signing him, then brought Kyle Mangas back on a $1.36M Exhibit 10 deal. Forward Josiah Allick was added and then released, with a $91,000 G-League development bonus attached to send him to the Greensboro Swarm.

Exhibit 10 deals are a clever mechanism: teams can offer small bonuses to players who agree to report to the G-League if waived. It builds depth at the affiliate level while keeping the main roster flexible.

With NBA teams allowed up to 21 players on training camp rosters, the next three weeks will see a steady stream of signings, waivers, and Exhibit 10 moves league-wide. The Sept. 29 camp opening is the true deadline — rosters must be tightened to 15 by the regular-season start.

Follow along as the final rosters take shape heading into what looks like a wide-open 2026-27 season.
