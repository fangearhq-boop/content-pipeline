### STORY 1: Curry Extension — Long-Form Post

**Post Window:** 9:00 AM ET

Nine days.

The countdown clock to Warriors media day ticked another number off Friday, and Stephen Curry still has not signed the two-year, $136.7 million contract extension that would make him the first player in NBA history to eclipse $600 million in career on-court earnings.

Curry told CNBC recently that he has no interest in free agency. He wants to finish his career in Golden State. The Warriors have given him full control over how the deal is structured — an unusual and significant show of trust from an organization that has built its identity around him for 14 years.

None of that means there is a signed contract. And at nine days out, the gap between "mutual interest" and "ink on paper" is becoming harder to ignore.

Here is what makes the structure matter as much as the number: if Curry accepts the full $136.7 million maximum, the Warriors' projected cap space next offseason gets cut roughly in half — from more than $130 million down to somewhere between $45 and $65 million. Draymond Green and Jimmy Butler III are both entering the final year of their contracts. The extension Curry signs will set the parameters for every decision that follows.

Media day is September 28. Training camp opens September 29. The regular season tips off October 20.

Nine days is not a lot of time. But it is still enough.

### STORY 2: DOJ Clippers Probe — Long-Form Post

**Post Window:** 11:00 AM ET

Six weeks into the federal investigation, and the Department of Justice's probe into the Los Angeles Clippers shows no signs of accelerating toward a resolution before the NBA season begins.

Federal prosecutors in the Eastern District of New York are examining whether the Clippers arranged secret sponsorship contracts — worth an estimated $18 million through three companies — as covert compensation for Kawhi Leonard above his typical NBA salary. At least one subpoena has been issued. No charges have been filed. The probe remains in what investigators have described as its "earliest stages."

A new element has entered the picture: Joseph Sanberg, who was convicted of wire fraud and sentenced to 14 years in prison, is reported to be a potential cooperating witness in the case. That does not mean charges are imminent. Cooperating witnesses can be part of an investigation for months before any grand jury action occurs.

The NBA has already handed down its punishment: a $30 million fine, five forfeited first-round picks spanning 2029–2033, a one-year suspension for owner Steve Ballmer, six months for president Lawrence Frank, and a year for executive Gillian Zucker. Kawhi Leonard was fined $700,000. His trade to the Toronto Raptors has been ratified.

The federal investigation is a different animal — slower, more consequential, and running on its own timeline entirely separate from the NBA's calendar. The 2026-27 season opens in 31 days. The Clippers will start it with this case hanging over them.

### STORY 3: Duren QO — Long-Form Post

**Post Window:** 1:00 PM ET

Twelve days to the qualifying offer deadline, and the Jalen Duren standoff in Detroit has not moved.

The math on both sides has been clear for weeks. The Pistons have offered five years and $190 million — $38 million per year on average. Duren wants at least $200 million. That $10 million gap on the total, or roughly $2 million per year, is the distance between a franchise cornerstone locked in long-term and a 22-year-old center who becomes an unrestricted free agent next summer.

Marc Stein reported that Duren is now "increasingly prepared" to accept his $9.6 million qualifying offer if Detroit cannot close a deal by month's end. That is not a bluff — or at least it is not meant to be read as one. If Duren accepts the QO, the Pistons lose all matching rights. He plays out the 2026-27 season for $9.6 million, becomes a free agent next July, and the Sacramento Kings are reportedly waiting with a max offer.

The Pistons, for their part, spent last summer rebuilding around Duren and Ausar Thompson, who just signed a five-year, $155 million extension. Losing Duren to a qualifying offer gamble would reshape everything they've built.

Twelve days. That is how much road Detroit has left before the math becomes irreversible.

### STORY 4: Knicks Banner Night — Long-Form Post

**Post Window:** 4:00 PM ET

Thirty-one days from now, the New York Knicks will walk out onto the Madison Square Garden court for the most anticipated Opening Night in recent NBA memory.

The ceremony starts at 6:30 PM ET on October 20 — the Knicks raising their first championship banner since 1973. Fifty-three years. Jalen Brunson, the Finals MVP, leads the celebrating. His team, which beat the San Antonio Spurs in five games to win the 2026 NBA title, will receive their rings in front of a crowd that waited a lifetime for this moment.

Then the game tips off at 7:00 PM on NBC and Peacock.

And the opponent is not just any team.

LeBron James — now 41 years old, one of the greatest players in the history of the sport — makes his Philadelphia 76ers debut at Madison Square Garden. He walks into the building as the protagonist of the Knicks' home opener villain story, alongside Joel Embiid, Tyrese Maxey, and Jaylen Brown. This is the team the Knicks are supposed to fear most in the East.

The Knicks swept the 76ers in four games during the playoffs. They averaged 124.3 points and held Philly to 102 per game. That matters for context — but it doesn't make the rematch feel smaller. It makes it feel like the exact right matchup to begin the title defense.

There are 31 days left. The tickets already crashed the internet once.

### STORY 5: NBA Preseason Quebec City — Long-Form Post

**Post Window:** 6:00 PM ET

The NBA preseason begins October 3, and it starts with something the league has never done before.

For the first time in NBA history, a game will be played in Quebec City. The Toronto Raptors host the Miami Heat at Videotron Centre — a 18,000-seat arena in Canada's French-speaking cultural capital — on October 3, opening the 2026-27 preseason schedule.

It is a deliberate expansion of the league's Canadian footprint beyond Toronto and the brief experiment in Vancouver. Quebec City is one of the country's most passionate sports markets, and the Raptors' ownership has been pushing to get a game there for years. October 3 delivers on that promise.

Beyond Quebec City, the preseason schedule includes Dallas and Houston traveling to Macao for games on October 9 and 11 at The Venetian Arena — which is why both franchises reported to training camp on September 22, a week ahead of the rest of the league. The Raptors also play the Los Angeles Clippers in Vancouver on October 10.

League-wide training camps open September 29, following veteran report dates and media days on September 28. Preseason runs through October 16. The regular season tips off October 20.

The storylines heading into training camp are stacked: Curry's unsigned extension, Duren's contract standoff, Haliburton's return from an Achilles tear, the 76ers' new Big Four stepping onto a court together. All of that starts being answered in 10 days, when the first camps open.
