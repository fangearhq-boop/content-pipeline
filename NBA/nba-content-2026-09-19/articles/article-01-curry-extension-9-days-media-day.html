9 Days and No Deal: Curry Extension Window Is Closing Fast

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | September 19, 2026

Golden State Warriors

Nine days. Warriors media day is September 28, and Stephen Curry still has not signed the two-year, $136.7 million contract extension that would make him the first player in NBA history to eclipse $600 million in career on-court earnings. The window is not closed. But it is narrowing in a way that is beginning to feel different from the routine preseason silence.

Curry has been unambiguous about his intentions. He told CNBC recently that he has no interest in testing free agency and wants to finish his career as a Warrior — a statement that surprised exactly no one, but was significant for its directness. The Warriors, for their part, have given Curry what few players in NBA history have ever received: full structural control over how a contract extension is designed. He gets to decide the length, the form, and therefore how much cap space Golden State retains heading into the following offseason.

The Structure Question

Mutual interest is not a contract. What Curry and the Warriors have confirmed publicly is desire — the desire to stay together, the desire to close this chapter on their terms. The part that requires negotiation is structure, and structure is where the real decisions get made.

The maximum available extension is two years at $136.7 million. If Curry signs the full deal, his career on-court earnings clear $600 million — a threshold no NBA player has crossed. That milestone is meaningful. But it is secondary to what the deal does to Golden State's financial picture.

The Warriors project more than $130 million in cap space next offseason. A full Curry extension cuts that roughly in half, leaving the franchise somewhere between $45 million and $65 million to work with. That range is the difference between transformational offseason flexibility and limited incremental moves. Draymond Green and Jimmy Butler III are both entering the final year of their contracts. Whatever Curry decides becomes the ceiling for everything else.

The Media Day Dynamic

September 28 is a soft deadline, not a hard one. Curry can technically sign an extension at any point before the regular season opens on October 20. There is no contractual reason the deal must be done by media day.

But media day has its own gravity. If Curry walks into the Warriors' facility on September 28 without a signed extension, the first question every reporter in the room asks is obvious. The second question is the same. The third question is the same. The extension consumes media day entirely — not the Knicks' banner ceremony, not the 76ers' new roster, not the upcoming season's West contenders. Just this one unsigned piece of paper.

Everyone inside the building understands this. Beat reporters understand this. Curry's representation understands this. The pull toward September 28 as the natural resolution point is real even if no language in any negotiation makes it binding.

Where Things Stand

Curry is 38. He has four championships, two MVPs, the all-time three-point record, and a legacy so fully assembled that this extension is closer to a closing ceremony than an opening chapter. The question is not whether he will remain a Warrior — it is how he wants to frame the final act, and what the contract's structure says about the franchise's priorities after his playing days end.

The nine-day window is not an emergency. It is a narrowing. Nine days is still enough time to sign a contract. The question is whether September 28 arrives with the deal done — and whether Curry's first media day statement is about basketball, or about a number that has not yet been finalized.

What's Next

Warriors media day is September 28. Training camp opens September 29 in Hawaii. Preseason begins October 3; the regular season tips off October 20 in New York with the Knicks' banner ceremony. If the extension is signed before media day, Golden State begins camp with its next chapter already started. If not, the extension question follows the team to Hawaii. The clock is at nine days and counting.
