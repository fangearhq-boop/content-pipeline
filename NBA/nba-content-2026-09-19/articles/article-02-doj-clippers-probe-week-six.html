DOJ Clippers Probe Enters Week Six — A Cooperating Witness Emerges

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | September 19, 2026

Los Angeles Clippers

The federal investigation into the Los Angeles Clippers is entering its sixth week, and a new element has emerged that could shape where the case goes from here. Joseph Sanberg — convicted of wire fraud and sentenced to 14 years in federal prison — is being reported as a potential cooperating witness in the Eastern District of New York's ongoing criminal probe. No charges have been filed. The investigation remains, in the government's own words, in its "earliest stages."

But the Sanberg development adds texture to a case that has otherwise been largely silent since the Department of Justice announced the probe in mid-August. A cooperating witness can accelerate the pace of a federal investigation — or can be part of a case that grinds for another year before any public action occurs. Federal criminal cases do not move on the NBA's calendar.

What the Investigation Is About

Federal prosecutors are examining whether the Clippers secretly arranged endorsement contracts through four companies to pay Kawhi Leonard more than his standard NBA salary — effectively circumventing the league's salary cap rules with payments for work that was never performed. The total alleged through those arrangements is approximately $18 million across three of the involved firms.

The NBA concluded its own investigation and handed down penalties in early September: a $30 million fine, five forfeited first-round draft picks spanning 2029–2033, a one-year suspension for owner Steve Ballmer, six months for president Lawrence Frank, one year for executive Gillian Zucker, and five years for governor Peter Robertson. Leonard received a $700,000 fine. His trade to the Toronto Raptors was ratified after the penalties were confirmed.

The NBA's investigation and the federal investigation are parallel but independent. The league concluded its process. The DOJ is running on an entirely different timeline and set of legal standards.

Why the Federal Case Matters Separately

The distinction between league punishment and federal prosecution is significant. The NBA can fine teams, pull draft picks, and suspend executives. The federal government can pursue criminal charges, which carry consequences of a different magnitude entirely.

A wire fraud investigation at the federal level — which is the most likely charging theory if prosecutors proceed — does not require the same proof standard as a civil matter and can result in prison sentences. The Sanberg connection, if he is cooperating, may provide the government with direct testimony about the mechanics of the alleged payments — which companies were involved, who authorized the arrangements, and how money moved.

None of that means charges are coming. Federal investigations routinely close without any action. The "earliest stages" description from prosecutors suggests the investigation could extend for months, if not longer.

The Clippers' New Reality

The 2026-27 Los Angeles Clippers open the season without Kawhi Leonard, who is now with the Raptors. They carry the financial weight of the NBA's penalties — $30 million in fines and five lost first-round picks represents a significant long-term setback for a franchise that has been rebuilding its draft capital. Ballmer's one-year suspension means he cannot attend games or participate in team operations through the first part of the season.

The regular season is 31 days away. The Clippers will begin it under conditions unlike any franchise in recent NBA history — with a federal criminal investigation actively running in the background.

What's Next

There is no timeline the public can access for the DOJ's investigation. Grand jury activity, if it occurs, is sealed. Further subpoenas would not necessarily become public. The next visible development would be either charges announced by the Eastern District of New York, or a formal closing of the investigation with no action taken. The NBA season opens October 20. The federal case will be ongoing regardless.
