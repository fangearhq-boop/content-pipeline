SGA vs. Wembanyama: The WCF Rematch Headlines Opening Night's Late Game

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | October 20, 2026

Six months after San Antonio eliminated Oklahoma City in the Western Conference Finals, SGA and Wembanyama meet again at Frost Bank Center. Chet Holmgren is out. The stakes are only personal — but the basketball will be elite.

The final game of Opening Night carries a grudge match embedded in every possession. Oklahoma City at San Antonio, 9:30 PM ET on NBC and Peacock, is the Western Conference Finals rematch from last spring — the series where the Spurs took Game 7 on the Thunder's home court to advance, and where Shai Gilgeous-Alexander's bid for a second straight championship ended.

Holmgren is out, making this a depleted Thunder squad. That changes the power dynamic, but it doesn't remove the appointment-viewing quality of any game featuring both SGA and Victor Wembanyama on the floor at the same time.

Shai Gilgeous-Alexander: Chasing a Third MVP

SGA enters the 2026-27 season as a two-time MVP hunting a historic third consecutive award. Since winning his first MVP, he has only gotten better — a more efficient scorer, a more creative playmaker, and a defensive anchor who routinely draws the opponent's best offensive player.

Without Holmgren and Otega Oweh, Gilgeous-Alexander will carry an even heavier load tonight. The Thunder's offseason also saw Lu Dort, Isaiah Joe, and Aaron Wiggins depart, meaning a new supporting cast surrounds him. How OKC performs with this new group — and how SGA elevates them — is a genuine opening-night storyline.

Isaiah Hartenstein starts at center, giving OKC a reliable but limited frontcourt presence in place of Holmgren's versatility.

Victor Wembanyama: Year Three and a Championship Pedigree

Wembanyama arrives at year three of his NBA career as a Western Conference finalist who no one in the league views as a long-term underdog anymore. His combination of 7-foot-plus size, guard-level ball-handling, perimeter shooting, and interior shot-blocking has continued to develop at a pace that alarms opposing front offices.

The Spurs surrounded him with better talent this offseason, adding Tobias Harris to a core that already features Stephon Castle and Dylan Harper — two young playmakers who fit their structure. San Antonio is not yet a championship-level team, but they are a dangerous one, and Wembanyama is the reason why.

The Rematch Storyline

When the Spurs ended OKC's season in Game 7 last spring, it felt like a seismic shift in Western Conference power. The Thunder had been the consensus favorite. San Antonio had been the scrappy challenger. The outcome reversed expectations and established the Spurs as legitimate contenders.

Six months later, neither team is interested in relitigating the postseason — but the narrative is there whether they want it or not. Every SGA-Wembanyama matchup carries extra weight after the WCF. That weight is what makes tonight's late game must-watch television regardless of the regular-season stakes.

What to Watch

SGA's scoring load: With Holmgren out, how many isolation possessions does Gilgeous-Alexander absorb? Wembanyama's post footwork against Hartenstein will be a mismatch worth monitoring. And watch the bench contributions on both sides — OKC needs its new supporting cast to deliver, while San Antonio's depth has quietly improved.

What's Next

The Thunder and Spurs will meet multiple times this season, and with Holmgren potentially returning within the next few weeks, the full-strength version of this rivalry has yet to fully play out. Tonight is an early chapter in a Western Conference rivalry that figures to define the next several seasons. Tip-off is 9:30 PM ET on NBC and Peacock.
