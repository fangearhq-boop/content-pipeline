LaMelo Ball and Anthony Edwards Take the Floor: Timberwolves Training Camp is Open

By Damon Pierce | September 29, 2026 | Hoop Heroes

For the last several years, Anthony Edwards has been building toward something. The athleticism was always there — the combination of speed, strength, and sheer competitive electricity that has made him one of the most watchable players in basketball. What was sometimes missing was the right partnership, the right supporting cast, the right co-star to take Minnesota from good to genuinely dangerous.

On Tuesday, that co-star officially arrived. LaMelo Ball took the floor with the Minnesota Timberwolves for the first time at training camp. The Western Conference just got a new dynamic to fear.

How LaMelo Got to Minnesota

The July trade that brought LaMelo to Minnesota was one of the bigger moves of an active offseason. The Timberwolves sent Naz Reid and a significant package of picks — including a 2033 unprotected first-rounder and multiple swaps through 2030 — to the Charlotte Hornets. Julius Randle went to the Brooklyn Nets as part of the four-team transaction.

LaMelo then re-signed with his new team on a five-year, $112 million deal. At 24, he is entering what should be the prime years of a career that already includes Rookie of the Year honors and multiple All-Star appearances. His passing vision is elite. His ability to create offense out of nothing makes him one of the most creative point guards in the league. And in a system built around Edwards's scoring gravity, that creativity has room to thrive.

The Pieces Around Them

Minnesota did not stop at LaMelo. Jonathan Kuminga arrived in free agency, adding a powerful wing who can score in multiple ways. Jaden McDaniels continues his development as one of the league's more versatile defenders. And Rudy Gobert — two years remaining on a deal that includes a player option, worth approximately $75 million — anchors the paint with the kind of shot-blocking and rebounding that simplifies the lives of every perimeter player around him.

The projected starting lineup of Edwards, LaMelo, Kuminga, McDaniels, and Gobert gives the Wolves five players who can guard multiple positions while offering genuine offensive versatility. That is a genuine Western Conference contender structure.

There is one complication: Donte DiVincenzo remains on the trade market and could miss the season entirely depending on how his situation resolves. But even accounting for that uncertainty, Minnesota's roster depth has improved meaningfully from a year ago.

What's Next

Training camp means rotations, conditioning, and the early work of building chemistry. For a team with this much new talent, the first few weeks will be important. LaMelo and Edwards have never played together at a meaningful level. Kuminga is new. The Wolves need time to find their rhythm.

But the ingredients are there. The Western Conference will produce its usual collection of elite teams — the defending West champion, the always-dangerous Los Angeles Lakers with LeBron-era remnants still on the roster, and other contenders pushing from every angle. Minnesota enters that conversation now. Two players who can take over any game are on the floor at the same time in Minneapolis for the first time. Camp is where the story begins.
