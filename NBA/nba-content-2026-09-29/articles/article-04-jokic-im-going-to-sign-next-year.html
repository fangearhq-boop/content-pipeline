Nikola Jokic Says "I'm Going to Sign Next Year" — Denver Nuggets Breathe Easy

By Marcus Cole | September 29, 2026 | Hoop Heroes

Somewhere in the middle of an NBA media day circuit filled with polished talking points and carefully managed uncertainty, Nikola Jokic gave the most Nikola Jokic answer imaginable.

A reporter asked if he planned to sign a contract extension with the Denver Nuggets.

"I'm going to sign next year."

No hedging. No caveats. No agent-speak. Just five words from the three-time MVP, delivered in the matter-of-fact tone that has become one of the most recognizable personalities in professional basketball.

What "Next Year" Means in Dollars

When Jokic says he will sign next summer, the expected deal is a five-year extension projected at approximately $357 million — which would include a no-trade clause. That would make it one of the richest contracts in NBA history and cement Jokic in Denver for the foreseeable future.

The fact that this is not yet signed is less about uncertainty and more about timing. Jokic has never been a player who operates through leverage or public drama. He plays basketball in Serbia in the summer. He comes back in the fall. He is usually the best player on the court. He wins awards. The contract machinery will catch up eventually.

Denver's training camp opens Tuesday with Jokic as the unquestioned center of everything — literally and figuratively. The Nuggets built their championship contender around his singular combination of size, skill, vision, and basketball IQ. No other player in the league does what he does. No other contract structure makes sense for the Nuggets.

The No-Trade Clause That Changes Everything

Beyond the dollar figure, the no-trade clause component of Jokic's expected deal matters enormously. It means Denver cannot move him without his consent. It means the trade speculation that followed him for years — the "what if a big market comes calling" scenarios — effectively disappears the moment he signs. Jokic, who has never expressed any public desire to leave Colorado, would be as locked in as a franchise player can be in the modern NBA.

For a team that has built its entire organizational identity around one player, that security has enormous value. Other franchises have watched their cornerstone players force exits, demand trades, or simply opt out and walk. Denver will not have that problem — at least not from the player himself.

What's Next

The extension window opens next summer, and Jokic has already said what he plans to do. Until then, the Nuggets focus on training camp, roster health, and the familiar task of building a playoff run around the best player in basketball. The business of the contract will resolve itself in due time. Jokic does not appear to be losing sleep over it, and Denver fans probably shouldn't either.
