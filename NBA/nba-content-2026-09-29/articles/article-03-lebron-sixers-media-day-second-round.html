LeBron James at 76ers Media Day: "I Didn't Leave My Family to Lose in the Second Round"

By Jake Torres | September 29, 2026 | Hoop Heroes

There was no question about LeBron James's intentions when he walked into the Philadelphia 76ers' media day Tuesday. The suit was sharp. The tone was sharper.

"I didn't leave my family to lose in the second round."

That quote — short, clean, undeniable — is the lens through which every conversation about the Sixers will now be filtered. LeBron, 41, chose to uproot and move to Philadelphia in what may be the final championship run of the most decorated career in NBA history. He did not come for the soft opening. He came to win.

The Most Anticipated Media Day Appearance of the Season

LeBron signed with the Sixers this offseason without holding an introductory press conference — an unusual choice that made Tuesday's media day his first real public moment in a Philadelphia uniform. The league paid attention.

He shared the spotlight with Jaylen Brown, who arrived from Boston in a separate offseason trade, and the rest of a lineup that includes Joel Embiid, Tyrese Maxey, and a supporting cast assembled specifically around the shared goal of winning a championship. LeBron did not pretend otherwise: "In order for us to be great, we have to do things that just aren't comfortable."

Asked about what he can bring to Philadelphia, he did not hedge. "Whatever the team needs for me to do. I think the luxury of my game is I can do everything. Literally, I can do everything." At 41, that statement requires some qualification — LeBron can no longer operate as the primary ball-handler for 35 minutes a night. But his ability to read the game, make the right pass, set the right screen, and deliver in a playoff moment remains legitimately elite.

Jaylen Brown's Own Statement

LeBron was not the only Sixers star with things to say. Jaylen Brown — who gave up a path to a potential second Boston title to join this group — offered a different kind of declaration: "What I value is the human connection, to be able to bring people together, uplift your community and inspire people. Athletes are more than line items or stats on a sheet."

Brown and LeBron are not a traditional superstar pairing. They are not built the same way, do not play the same role, and have not always been in the same orbit. But their arrival in Philadelphia together creates a team with an unusual combination of winning pedigree, physical tools, and motivational fire.

October 20 at the Garden

The Sixers' first game is October 20 at Madison Square Garden — the same night the New York Knicks raise their first championship banner since 1973. LeBron will walk into that building as the most famous visitor of the night, in a rival uniform, on the city's biggest celebration. The NBA's schedule-makers understood the assignment.

The Knicks swept the Sixers 4-0 in last season's playoffs. The rematch begins on Philadelphia's first night of a new era. LeBron did not come to watch the Knicks celebrate. He came to make sure it is the last time they do.

What's Next

Training camp begins and the Sixers get to work. Head coach Nick Nurse has three weeks to find the rotations, set the defensive principles, and figure out how to best use a group with enormous talent and no shared history. The ceiling is championship-level. The floor depends on chemistry that cannot be scripted. Tuesday was the first official day they said it out loud. The rest happens on the court.
