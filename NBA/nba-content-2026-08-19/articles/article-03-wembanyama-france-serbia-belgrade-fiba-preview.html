Wembanyama vs. Serbia in Belgrade: France's FIBA Mission Starts Tomorrow

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | August 19, 2026

Victor Wembanyama plays competitive basketball tomorrow for the first time since the 2024 Olympic final, and the opponent waiting in Belgrade is not a soft introduction back to the game. Serbia — featuring Nikola Jokic — is among the top international programs on the planet, and August 20's matchup at a Belgrade arena is already being framed as one of the marquee events of the FIBA summer window.

Wembanyama confirmed he would play for France in the FIBA World Cup Qualifier window, choosing to compete rather than rest through the summer months. His reasoning was direct: "I miss FIBA basketball." For a player of his status, that choice carries weight — he did not have to be here, and he chose to be.

France's Roster and Mission

France arrives in Belgrade with real firepower. Wembanyama headlines the roster, joined by Rudy Gobert — the three-time NBA Defensive Player of the Year and one of the best interior defenders the international game has produced — and veteran guard Evan Fournier. New head coach Frederic Fauthoux is working with Wembanyama for the first time, which adds an element of genuine uncertainty about how the team will mesh on the floor.

France enters the qualifier window at 5-1 in Group L qualifying, positioning them well. The two games against Serbia — August 20 in Belgrade and August 23 in Orleans — are the most consequential of the window. Serbia consistently produces elite international competition, and their full qualifying roster includes Jokic alongside an experienced core that has competed at the highest levels of FIBA play for years.

Eurohoops described the Belgrade matchup as "a bigger test than just Nikola Jokic" — a reminder that Serbia is a complete team, not a one-man show. Wembanyama will face a physical, tactically sophisticated opponent in his first competitive action since Paris.

The Context: Post-Olympics, Pre-Spurs Season

France's last major competitive result was a silver medal at the 2024 Paris Olympics — a result that stung given that the host nation lost the final on home soil. This FIBA qualifier window is early groundwork for the 2027 FIBA World Cup cycle, and Wembanyama's presence signals that France is taking the qualifying process seriously rather than coasting on reputation.

For the San Antonio Spurs and their fanbase, there is always mild anxiety when Wembanyama competes internationally during the offseason. He is the franchise's generational asset, and minutes in Belgrade are minutes that carry risk. But Wembanyama has been clear about his desire to represent France, and the Spurs have not stood in the way.

The Belgrade game starts tomorrow. A second game follows on August 23 in Orleans. France will play qualifiers against Slovenia (August 27, home) and Sweden (August 30, away) before the window closes.

What's Next

The immediate next step is the August 20 game in Belgrade — follow the FIBA WorldCup accounts for live coverage. The Orleans game on August 23 will give France the home crowd advantage. After the qualifier window closes, Wembanyama returns to San Antonio ahead of the Spurs' training camp. His performance in these summer games will be closely watched as a barometer of his development heading into the 2026-27 season — his third in the NBA and the one where expectations have fully shifted from "prodigy" to "genuine superstar."
