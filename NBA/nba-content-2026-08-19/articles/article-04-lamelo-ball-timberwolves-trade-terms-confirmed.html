The Full Price Minnesota Paid for LaMelo Ball: Naz Reid, Seven Picks, and the Edwards Era

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | August 19, 2026

The full trade terms sending LaMelo Ball from Charlotte to Minnesota are now confirmed, and the price the Timberwolves paid to reshape their roster is steep enough to make even optimistic Wolves fans pause before celebrating.

Minnesota sent to Charlotte: Naz Reid — the 26-year-old big who was one of the better reserve forwards in the Western Conference — plus a 2033 unprotected first-round pick, three first-round pick swaps in 2028, 2029, and 2030, and three second-round picks in 2029, 2032, and 2033. In return, the Timberwolves received LaMelo Ball and Josh Green.

That is seven total picks and pick swaps, plus a rotation-caliber player, in exchange for a 25-year-old guard who has never played a single NBA playoff game in his career.

The Bet Minnesota Is Making

The Ringer described this trade as "the Wolves betting the Anthony Edwards era" on LaMelo Ball. That framing captures the stakes perfectly. Minnesota has committed heavily to a backcourt pairing — Ball and Edwards — that has enormous upside on paper and significant risk in practice.

The theory works like this: LaMelo Ball is one of the best playmakers in the league when healthy. His ability to generate clean catch-and-shoot opportunities for teammates off his passing is elite. If Ball handles primary ball-handling duties, Edwards is freed to play off the ball — catching, cutting, and scoring in the spaces that an elite creator opens up. Multiple analysts have described this as a "match made in heaven" for floor spacing.

Anthony Edwards leaned into the elevated expectations immediately. After the trade was confirmed, Edwards delivered what reporters described as a "championship reality check" — a statement that made clear he views the Timberwolves as championship contenders now, not merely a team trying to compete in the first round.

The Risk No One Can Ignore

But the caveat attached to LaMelo Ball is impossible to overlook: he has never played in the NBA playoffs. Not one game. His career has been marked by flashes of brilliance interrupted by injuries, and Charlotte's teams during his tenure were not built to win in April, May, or June.

That playoff inexperience does not disqualify him — plenty of stars have arrived at playoff-contending teams and thrived immediately. But it means Minnesota is operating without the most important data point in evaluating whether Ball can perform when the game slows down, the defense tightens, and every possession carries maximum weight.

Losing Naz Reid also has real costs. Reid was one of the more versatile bench pieces in the West — a capable scorer, rebounder, and floor-spacer who could start or come off the bench and consistently contribute. Replacing that production from a trade package is not guaranteed.

What's Next

LaMelo Ball and the Timberwolves will head into training camp in late September with expectations firmly in the championship conversation. The early-season games will tell us quickly whether the Ball-Edwards backcourt has the chemistry the Wolves are betting on. Josh Green adds defensive versatility and athleticism off the bench. Minnesota's front office has made its statement — now the players have to validate it on the court.
