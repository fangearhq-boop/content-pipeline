Stephen Curry's Extension Window Opens in 10 Days — And He Might Not Take the Full Max

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | August 19, 2026

Ten days. That is the gap between today and the most important date in the Golden State Warriors' offseason — August 29, 2026, when Stephen Curry becomes eligible to sign a contract extension.

The maximum offer available: two years, $136.7 million. If Curry signs it, he remains a Warrior through the 2028-29 season. If he does not, he becomes an unrestricted free agent for the first time in his career in the summer of 2027 — a scenario that would reshape the NBA landscape in ways the league has not seen since LeBron James's free agency decisions defined the 2010s.

Mutual Interest Is Real — But the Structure Is in Question

Every credible report says both sides want to get a deal done. ESPN's Anthony Slater reported there is "mutual interest in a Curry extension when he's eligible in late August." Warriors general manager Mike Dunleavy Jr. said he is "pretty confident Steph will finish his career" in Golden State. Curry himself has given no indication he wants out.

But the question of what the deal looks like is more open than the "will it happen" question suggests. Multiple reports indicate Curry may not want the full two-year max. ESPN's Brian Windhorst floated what he described as a "bold, risky" structure — something shorter-term that would give Curry flexibility to reassess his situation sooner. Swipe Sports reported Curry is considering a discount arrangement designed to help the Warriors build a more competitive roster around him.

The context for that consideration matters. Golden State finished 37-45 last season. Their pursuit of LeBron James in the 2026 offseason failed. Curry is 38 years old and shot 42 percent from three last year — still elite production, but on a team that is not yet built to compete for a championship. Whatever extension structure he signs, the underlying message will be clear: Curry wants to play for a team that has a realistic chance at a title, not just a team that is happy to have him.

Why August 29 Is Non-Negotiable

Extensions can only be signed during specific eligibility windows under the NBA's collective bargaining agreement. August 29 opens Curry's window. Both sides are expected to move quickly once it opens — the mutual interest makes a prolonged negotiation unlikely.

One floating figure — one year, $62.6 million — has appeared in some reports as a potential structure. It is worth noting that this figure appears to track Curry's existing 2026-27 contract salary rather than a new signed deal. The actual extension negotiations will produce their own numbers once the window opens.

Without a new extension, Curry's current contract runs through the 2026-27 season. He becomes eligible for unrestricted free agency in 2027 — his first time ever without a long-term deal in place. For a franchise that has built its identity around his presence since 2009, that is an outcome the Warriors' front office is highly motivated to avoid.

What's Next

August 29 is the date. Both sides appear motivated to reach an agreement quickly once the window opens. The structure — two years versus a shorter term — is the real variable. Watch for reporting from ESPN's Slater and Windhorst in the days immediately following August 29. If the extension gets done, the Warriors will have clarity on their franchise cornerstone heading into training camp. If talks stall, the 2026-27 season becomes a referendum on whether Golden State deserves to keep its greatest player.
