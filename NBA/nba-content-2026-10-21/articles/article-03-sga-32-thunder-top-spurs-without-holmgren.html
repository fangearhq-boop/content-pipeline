SGA Drops 32, Thunder Top Spurs 116-104 Without Holmgren — A Statement Win

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | October 21, 2026

Shai Gilgeous-Alexander had a message for the San Antonio Spurs on Opening Night.

Without his No. 2 player. In the Spurs' building. Against the team that eliminated Oklahoma City in seven games in last spring's Western Conference Finals.

SGA scored 32 points as the Thunder beat San Antonio 116-104 to open the 2026-27 NBA season, serving notice that Oklahoma City has unfinished business — and the roster to back it up.

The Holmgren Absence

Chet Holmgren, OKC's second-best player and one of the more unique centers in the league, missed the game with a quad contusion. He had been dealing with the injury throughout the preseason and was ruled out before tip-off at Frost Bank Center.

The Thunder didn't use it as an excuse. SGA was magnificent, operating in the mid-range and at the free-throw line, attacking San Antonio's defense from every angle. Oklahoma City's supporting cast stepped up around their two-time MVP, and the Spurs — coming off their deepest playoff run in years — couldn't keep pace.

The final score was 116-104. It wasn't even that close in the fourth quarter.

A WCF Rematch Turned Statement

The backdrop of this game was impossible to ignore. San Antonio knocked out Oklahoma City in seven games in the 2026 Western Conference Finals — the same series that sent the Spurs to the NBA Finals, where they lost to the Knicks in five games.

SGA came into Opening Night carrying that elimination. The Thunder had the talent and the seeding. They had the home-court advantage in Games 1 and 2. And they still couldn't close it out against a Victor Wembanyama-led team that plays with relentless two-way energy.

Tuesday night was the first answer. Not the last word — that comes in the playoffs — but a first, emphatic answer.

Wembanyama was held in check in the loss, a performance that will concern Spurs fans who believed San Antonio had the personnel to match OKC's firepower. The Thunder defense, even missing Holmgren's rim protection, made Victor's life difficult all night.

SGA and the MVP Race

Shai Gilgeous-Alexander enters the 2026-27 season as a two-time MVP chasing a third. He's doing it at 28 years old, in the prime of what has been one of the most quietly dominant runs in recent NBA history.

The performance against San Antonio on Opening Night — 32 points, in a hostile environment, without his center, against an elite defensive team — was the kind of game that sets the narrative for a season. The MVP conversation started Tuesday night.

Oklahoma City is a legitimate championship contender. The Spurs found that out again the hard way.

What's Next

The Thunder will wait for Holmgren's return and manage the quad contusion carefully — OKC cannot afford to lose their No. 2 player for an extended stretch in a conference this competitive. Holmgren's timeline will be one of the most closely watched injury stories of the early season.

For San Antonio, the loss stings. Wembanyama signed his max extension this summer and the Spurs are built to compete. But the Western Conference Finals hangover can be real, and Tuesday night showed OKC has no interest in letting the Spurs celebrate what happened last spring.

The WCF rematch is on. The real one comes in May.
