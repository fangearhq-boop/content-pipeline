The Banner Goes Up, the Game Goes Away: Knicks' Bittersweet Opening Night at MSG

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | October 21, 2026

For 53 years, Knicks fans waited to see a championship banner raised at Madison Square Garden.

On Tuesday night, it happened. The 2025-26 NBA Championship banner went up, and Madison Square Garden shook with the kind of celebration that only a half-century of waiting can produce. The franchise that last won a title in 1973 — the same year Wilt Chamberlain retired — is a champion again.

Then the Philadelphia 76ers handed them a 106-100 loss.

That's the reality of being the defending NBA champion. Every team in the league circled this date.

The Ceremony Delivered

Before tip-off, Madison Square Garden hosted what many are calling one of the great NBA ceremonies in recent memory. Jalen Brunson — the Finals MVP who scored 45 points in Game 5 to seal the title — received his ring to the loudest roar of the night. Veterans who had played through the long rebuild embraced each other on the court. The banner was raised, and for a few minutes, nothing else mattered.

The 2025-26 Knicks came back from 29 points down in Game 4 of the Finals — the greatest comeback in Finals history — before closing out the Spurs 4-1. What this organization has accomplished over the past few years is genuinely historic.

No game result changes that.

The Knicks' Rough Night

The ceremony ended. The 76ers arrived.

Jalen Brunson scored 21 points but shot just 6-for-19 from the field — a number that tells the story of the night for New York. On drives and pull-up attempts, Philadelphia's switching defense was consistently a step ahead. The Knicks' offense looked disconnected at times, failing to generate the rhythm that made them champions last spring.

Karl-Anthony Towns was another problem. Towns collected early fouls against LeBron James — who the 76ers deployed at center specifically to draw Towns into foul trouble — and played just 24 minutes. He finished with 19 points and 12 rebounds, but his impact on the game was limited. Without Towns as an effective presence, the Knicks' interior spacing suffered all night.

New York led by 10 in the first half, and the crowd was with them completely. Then the 76ers outscored the Knicks 29-18 in the third quarter and the night unraveled.

Year 2 Is Always Harder

History teaches a consistent lesson about defending champions: Year 2 is a gauntlet. Teams spend the entire offseason preparing specifically for you. The margin for error shrinks.

The 76ers assembled their LeBron-Embiid-Brown-Maxey squad with the Knicks squarely in mind. The Celtics got Paul George in a trade designed to give them another star-level option. The Thunder are chasing a championship after their playoff run last spring. The East is deeper in 2026-27 than it has been in years.

For the Knicks, one loss on Opening Night against a team this good should not be cause for alarm. Brunson will find his rhythm. KAT will avoid foul trouble. The defense, which was elite a season ago, will tighten.

The 0-1 start is a footnote. The banner is permanent.

What's Next

The Knicks will look to get right quickly. The Eastern Conference is going to test New York from the opening weeks. Defending a championship in the NBA requires sustained energy and a short memory — and 2026-27 already shows it won't be easy.

But Tuesday night delivered something no opponent can take. The banner is up. The rings are real.

For Knicks fans, that moment at Madison Square Garden is the one they'll remember from October 20, 2026 — not the final score.
