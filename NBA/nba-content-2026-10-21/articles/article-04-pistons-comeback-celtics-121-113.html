Detroit's Statement: Pistons Come Back to Stun New-Look Celtics 121-113

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | October 21, 2026

If you needed a sign that the Eastern Conference is genuinely wide open in 2026-27, the Detroit Pistons provided it on Opening Night.

The Pistons came back from a double-digit deficit, outscored Boston in the fourth quarter, and beat Paul George's new-look Celtics 121-113 in the afternoon game at Little Caesars Arena — stunning a Boston team that many predicted would compete for the Eastern Conference title.

Duncan Robinson delivered a significant fourth-quarter performance off the bench. The Pistons were relentless. The Celtics looked out of sync.

The Comeback

Boston built a double-digit first-half lead. The Celtics have championship-caliber talent — Jayson Tatum, Paul George, and Jrue Holiday form one of the better wing trios in the conference — and they looked the part in the early going.

Detroit didn't panic. The Pistons kept playing their game, grinding on defense and finding open looks in transition. By halftime the lead had shrunk, and when Robinson — a veteran shooter signed this past offseason — found his rhythm in the fourth quarter, the Pistons took over.

The final margin of 121-113 overstated Boston's closeness. This was a convincing win in the second half by a Detroit team that has quietly built something worth watching.

Questions for the Celtics

The questions about Boston's new-look roster will begin in earnest after this result. Paul George arrived from Philadelphia in the blockbuster trade that sent Jaylen Brown to the 76ers — the same deal that reshaped the Eastern Conference power structure. George brings scoring, versatility, and experience to a Celtics team that already had enough of all three.

But chemistry takes time. George and Tatum are both high-usage players who need the ball in their hands to be effective. Head coach Joe Mazzulla will need to figure out how the two coexist without disrupting either player's rhythm. Tuesday's game showed that hasn't been solved yet.

Jayson Tatum struggled to find his spots. George was involved but not impactful enough in the critical moments. For a team with this much talent, a first-game home loss to a younger, less-heralded opponent sets an unsatisfying tone.

What This Means for Detroit

The Detroit Pistons have been one of the more interesting rebuilding stories in the NBA over the past several years. Their young core, built through the draft, has been developing steadily — and Tuesday night looked like the arrival of a team ready to compete in the East.

Robinson's fourth-quarter production highlighted the depth that makes this Pistons team more dangerous than their seed suggests. Veterans who know how to win in critical moments are invaluable, and Detroit showed they have that element alongside their young stars.

The Eastern Conference is not a two-team race. The Pistons just proved it on Day 1.

What's Next

The Celtics have the talent and the coaching to work through early-season chemistry issues. One loss in Game 1 of a 82-game season is a data point, not a verdict. Boston will figure out the George-Tatum relationship, and when they do, they'll be a formidable team.

But the East won't wait. The 76ers are 1-0. The Knicks are looking to bounce back. Milwaukee, Miami, and now Detroit are all lurking. The Celtics cannot afford a slow start.

Detroit, meanwhile, is 1-0 and playing with house money. Nobody expected this result. Now the Pistons get to prove it wasn't a fluke.
