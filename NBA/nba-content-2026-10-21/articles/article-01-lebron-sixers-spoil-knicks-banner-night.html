LeBron Stuns at MSG: 76ers Spoil Knicks' Championship Night 106-100

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | October 21, 2026

The New York Knicks planned a celebration. The Philadelphia 76ers had other ideas.

On one of the most emotionally charged nights in Madison Square Garden history, the 76ers walked into the Knicks' long-awaited championship banner ceremony and left with a 106-100 opening night victory — spoiling New York's party in front of a sold-out, heartbroken crowd.

Tyrese Maxey led all scorers with 28 points. LeBron James — in his regular-season debut as a Sixer at the age of 41 — finished with 14 points, 9 rebounds, and 7 assists in 34 minutes. And in one of the most tactically fascinating decisions of Opening Night, head coach Nick Nurse deployed LeBron at center to matchup against Karl-Anthony Towns, limiting KAT to 24 foul-plagued minutes and 19 points on the night.

The Third Quarter Changed Everything

New York had a 10-point advantage at points in the first half. The MSG crowd was buzzing. The Knicks looked like defending champions playing at home with the confidence of a team that knew it was the best in the league.

Then Philadelphia went to work in the third quarter.

The 76ers outscored New York 29-18 in the third period, turning a deficit into a lead they would not relinquish. Maxey was relentless, creating off screens and getting to the line. Jaylen Brown — another new face in Sixers blue after arriving from Boston in the blockbuster George-Brown trade — added 19 points, 8 rebounds, and 6 assists in his own Philadelphia debut. Joel Embiid chipped in 13 points, 11 rebounds, and 6 assists.

It was a complete team performance that reminded the league why so many experts picked Philadelphia to challenge for the Eastern Conference title.

The King's Tactical Move

LeBron's decision to play center against Towns was the tactical story of the night. The move forced KAT to defend James on the perimeter while drawing KAT into foul trouble on the other end. By the time the third quarter arrived, Towns had burned through his fouls and could barely play.

James has reinvented himself before — from scoring machine to playmaker, from small forward to power forward. Playing the five against one of the best big men in the NBA in his first regular-season game at 41 was the latest chapter in his remarkable career.

"The game is the game," LeBron told reporters after the win. The headline from his preseason debut in Brooklyn — where he scored 10 points in 18 minutes — felt like a distant memory.

Brunson Couldn't Find His Rhythm

The Knicks' reigning star, Jalen Brunson, had 21 points but shot just 6-for-19 from the field. On a night when the offense needed him to be the architect, Brunson couldn't consistently find openings against Philadelphia's aggressive, switching defense.

KAT's foul trouble compounded the issue. Without Towns being an effective threat in the post, the Knicks' pick-and-roll game became more predictable. The 76ers — long criticized for their defensive shortcomings — looked like a team that had spent the summer building specifically for this kind of challenge.

What's Next

The Knicks have their banner and they have their ring. Nothing about Tuesday night changes that history. But the 2026-27 season serves notice that Year 2 as defending champions will be more contested than Year 1 ever was.

The 76ers, now 1-0, head home to South Philly to begin what could be the most anticipated regular season in franchise history. The Knicks, now 0-1, will look to bounce back as the schedule continues.

LeBron James won his first game as a Sixer. In his 24th NBA season. At Madison Square Garden. Against the defending champions.

The King doesn't lose many openings.
