Giannis and the Heat Open Camp Tuesday. The NBA's Most Watched Experiment Begins.

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | September 28, 2026

The Miami Heat have put together a roster that the NBA has not seen before. Tomorrow, they have to start figuring out if it actually works.

Official training camp opens Tuesday, September 29 at the Heat facility. The 21-man roster is set. Coach Erik Spoelstra will run his first full practice with Giannis Antetokounmpo in a Heat uniform, alongside Bam Adebayo, Klay Thompson, Bobby Portis, Tim Hardaway Jr., and Andrew Wiggins. The first preseason game is October 3.

What Happened Before Camp

Giannis skipped Greece's FIBA qualifiers this summer to give himself more time to embed with his new teammates. That is a decision that gets noted. National team participation is taken seriously, and opting out of it to focus on a new NBA situation sends a clear signal about his priorities and commitment level to Miami.

The group gathered in California for an unofficial minicamp before the official version begins Tuesday. Giannis, Adebayo, Thompson, Portis, Hardaway, and Wiggins spent time together on the court without Spoelstra running the sessions. The goal was basic: get comfortable with each other before the coaching staff installs the system. Multiple players posted from California. The vibe looked loose and genuine, which is worth something early in a team-building process.

Spoelstra runs one of the most demanding systems in the league. His defensive schemes require communication, trust, and buy-in. Giannis fits the physical profile perfectly. The question is how long it takes to build the fluency.

The Two-Big Question

Miami is betting on a frontcourt that has never been tested in a real NBA game. Giannis at power forward, Adebayo at center, on the floor together. Both are capable of defending multiple positions. Both can pass. Both can operate as decision-makers on offense.

In theory, that pairing does a lot of things other teams cannot match. Giannis is one of the two or three most physically dominant players in the league. Adebayo is one of the best defensive centers of his generation. The spacing questions are real, but if Thompson can shoot at his historical clip and Wiggins provides secondary perimeter creation, the Heat will have answers for most lineups they see.

The actual risk is fouls, fatigue, and offensive efficiency. Running two non-shooters near the basket at the same time compresses spacing. Teams that play two bigs together historically surrender corner three opportunities and struggle in transition. Miami's answer is Thompson. His presence at the three-point line theoretically opens driving lanes for both Giannis and Adebayo. Whether it actually works at the speed of an NBA regular season game is something camp will start to answer, but October 3 will give the first real look.

What's Next

Camp opens Tuesday. The Heat's first preseason game is October 3, which means this frontcourt pairing will face real NBA competition for the first time in less than a week. Spoelstra will keep his rotation tight early, but the Giannis-Adebayo pairing will play together in game conditions before the end of the first preseason week.

Pay attention to how quickly the Heat switch defensively, how Giannis uses his handle in pick-and-roll situations with Adebayo as the other big, and whether Thompson gets enough clean looks to space the floor. Those three things will tell you most of what you need to know about Miami's ceiling. The schedule is real. The experiment starts tomorrow.
