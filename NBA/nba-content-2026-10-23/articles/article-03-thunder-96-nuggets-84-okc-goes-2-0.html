Thunder 96, Nuggets 84: OKC Goes 2-0 as Defense Smothers Jokic

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | October 23, 2026

The Oklahoma City Thunder wanted to make a statement to the Western Conference. They had one ready.

In their home opener Thursday night, the Thunder held Nikola Jokic and the Denver Nuggets to 84 points — well below Denver's offensive average — and won 96-84 to move to 2-0 on the young season. Shai Gilgeous-Alexander led the offensive effort while OKC's defense did what it does: take away rhythm, force difficult shots, and grind opponents into submission.

The message was clear. Oklahoma City is not a team that wins despite its style. It wins because of it.

A Defense Built for Big Moments

Nikola Jokic is still the most versatile offensive player in basketball. Getting to 84 points total as a team — against a Jokic offense that can dissect any defense in the league — is the kind of performance that defines championship-caliber defense.

Oklahoma City's scheme has evolved each season. Coach Mark Daigneault's rotations take away Jokic's preferred looks, forcing Denver to rely on secondary options and contested perimeter shooting. When those shots don't fall — and on Thursday they didn't — Denver's offense stagnates.

The Thunder didn't need to score 120 points. They needed to outscore a limited Denver offense. They did that by 12, and it wasn't that close in the final minutes.

SGA Carries the Offensive Load

Shai Gilgeous-Alexander was the Thunder's difference-maker on offense, operating in the mid-range and at the rim with the efficiency that made him the MVP frontrunner entering this season. Against Denver's defensive rotations, he found the right shot on nearly every possession in the fourth quarter when Oklahoma City needed to salt away the game.

The Thunder's supporting cast held up their end. The ball movement that generates open threes was crisp. OKC's bench provided enough scoring to keep Denver from making a run in the third quarter when the Nuggets briefly threatened to cut into the lead.

What This Means for the Western Conference

Oklahoma City opened the season by beating San Antonio — a team they eliminated in the WCF — then followed it up with a home win over the two-time defending champion Nuggets. Two games in, the Thunder look like everything the preseason predicted.

Denver, at 0-2, faces early pressure. Jokic will average his numbers over a full season. Jamal Murray will find his rhythm. But the Nuggets' margin for error against elite defensive teams is slimmer than it used to be, and OKC is exactly that kind of opponent.

For the Thunder, the early résumé is building fast. Win over the WCF rematch opponents. Win over the defending champions. West title favorite status — already attached to this team — just got a little more legitimate.

What's Next

Oklahoma City will look to keep the winning streak alive as the schedule fills out. For a team built around depth, defense, and SGA's brilliance, the expectation is consistency — and through two games, that's exactly what they've delivered.

Denver needs to respond. Murray's health and return to form will be the key variable. But facing OKC twice in the first week of the season and going 0-2 is not the start the Nuggets needed. For the Thunder, it's the start they expected.
