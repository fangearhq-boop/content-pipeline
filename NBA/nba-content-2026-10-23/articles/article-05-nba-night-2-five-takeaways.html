NBA Night 2: Five Takeaways From the League's Monster Second Night

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | October 23, 2026

The NBA's second night of the 2026-27 season just told us more about this league than a month of training camp coverage ever could. A historic comeback in Philadelphia. A franchise-defining debut in Washington. Oklahoma City proving its defense is the real thing against the best offense in basketball. And Hollywood delivering on opening night.

Here are five takeaways from the second-best NBA night of the week.

1. The 76ers Are a Real Championship Contender

Erasing a 29-point deficit against the Cavaliers is not something good teams do. It's something great teams do. Philadelphia's win should end the preseason debate about whether Maxey, LeBron, Embiid, and company can coexist.

They can. Maxey's 32 points were the engine. LeBron's double-double was the foundation. Embiid's 24 points were the reliability. When all three are performing at that level simultaneously, there may not be a team in the East that can stop them over a seven-game series.

2. Anthony Davis in Washington Is a Different Player

There were legitimate questions about whether Anthony Davis — entering the final phase of his prime — would arrive in Washington energized or going through the motions. Tuesday's 34-point debut answered those questions with force.

Davis looked motivated. He looked engaged. He looked like a player who wants to prove he can lead a franchise to relevance. The franchise debut record he tied is a data point; the manner in which he played is the real signal. Davis wanted this.

Trae Young is the perfect backcourt complement — creative, relentless, and capable of creating advantages that big men can exploit. Washington's new era feels genuinely different from what came before.

3. Oklahoma City's Defense Is a Playoff-Level Problem

Holding Nikola Jokic and the Nuggets to 84 points in a game is a statement. Doing it at home in your own opener, in front of your own crowd, with the full attention of the basketball world — that's something different.

OKC's defensive schemes are sophisticated enough to affect Jokic without fouling him, to take away Murray's pull-up game, and to force rotation and late clock offense from a team that ordinarily doesn't need it. The Thunder went 2-0 in two days against two quality opponents. The Western Conference title favorite label is earned.

4. The West Is a Four-Team Race

Oklahoma City is the frontrunner. The Nuggets, Lakers, and an undefeated Warriors team will claim spots alongside them before the week is out. But through two nights, the Thunder are operating at a level that every other Western Conference contender will need to match.

The Lakers' dominant home performance over Golden State is encouraging for LA. If they stay healthy and consistent at home, they'll be right in the Western Conference conversation come April.

5. The AJ Dybantsa Story Is Just Getting Started

The No. 1 overall pick watched from the bench in Washington with a left ankle sprain while Anthony Davis showed the arena what its future could look like. Dybantsa's eventual debut — and the lineup combinations that follow — will be one of the most watched storylines of the early season.

When Dybantsa returns to a lineup featuring Davis and Young, Washington's ceiling rises significantly. The patience required to integrate a generational prospect while winning immediately is the challenge facing the Wizards' front office. But the foundation looks better now than it has in years.

What's Next

The NBA season is two nights old and already delivering on every preseason promise. Championship contenders are validating their hype. Franchise rebirths are underway. Defensive monsters are identifying themselves.

Night 2 was supposed to be a warmup. It turned out to be a revelation. The rest of October can't come fast enough.
