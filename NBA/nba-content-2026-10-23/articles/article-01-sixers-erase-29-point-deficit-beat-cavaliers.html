76ers 114, Cavaliers 106: Maxey and LeBron Erase Historic 29-Point Deficit

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | October 23, 2026

Down 29 points to the Cleveland Cavaliers. Season only two days old. And somehow, the Philadelphia 76ers walked out of the Wells Fargo Center with a 114-106 win that will be talked about for years.

Tyrese Maxey was at his dazzling best with 32 points. LeBron James — showing no signs of slowing down at 41 — posted 17 points and 10 rebounds. Joel Embiid anchored the paint with 24 points. Together, they produced one of the most stunning comebacks the NBA has seen on a second night of a season, erasing a deficit that had stopped 65 straight teams in NBA season openers.

The Cavaliers, led by Donovan Mitchell's 32 points, looked like they had the game wrapped up. They didn't.

A Deficit That Should Have Been Insurmountable

Through the first half, Cleveland was everything the experts expected. Mitchell was dynamic in the pick-and-roll. Evan Mobley controlled the paint at both ends. The Cavaliers' ball movement was crisp, their energy electric, and their lead — at one point 29 points — felt like a closing argument.

Teams entering a season opener with a 20-plus point advantage at halftime had gone 65-0 historically. Philadelphia was not concerned with history.

The 76ers opened the third quarter with a different level of urgency. Maxey created separation on every possession. LeBron began finding weak-side cutters with the kind of vision that made him a four-time champion. Embiid, more active than he'd been all night, started getting post touches that Cleveland couldn't handle.

By the end of the third quarter, the 29-point lead was gone.

Maxey Puts the Team on His Back

Tyrese Maxey's third quarter was the turning point and the performance that will define early conversations about this season's MVP race. Working off screens, attacking closeouts, and hitting three-pointers from the logo, Maxey simply refused to let Philadelphia lose.

His 32-point final total undersells the quality of the performance. There were moments — particularly late in the third and early in the fourth — where Maxey's confidence bordered on controlled recklessness, the kind of play that great guards make when they know the game belongs to them.

Philadelphia's supporting cast responded around him. LeBron's 10-rebound performance was the physical statement of a player determined to show he can still impact a game at elite levels. Embiid's 24 points came in waves — quiet stretches followed by dominant sequences that changed momentum.

Mitchell Was Brilliant — and Still Lost

Donovan Mitchell deserves credit. His 32 points were a legitimate star performance, and for three quarters he did everything right. The Cavaliers' offense functioned as well as it could against a defense that, by the second half, was playing with the kind of desperation that changes games.

Cleveland's miscalculation was assuming the lead would hold. Teams at plus-29 have that luxury. This Sixers team is different from past Philadelphia rosters — they have multiple closers, multiple creators, and the kind of star firepower that can score in bunches when the game is on the line.

The Cavaliers are still a playoff team. Mitchell will have better nights. But the 76ers served notice that their offense, when fully engaged, is difficult to contain for 48 minutes.

What's Next

Philadelphia is 2-0 and already building a narrative. The superteam construction that drew skepticism over the summer now has two wins, including the most improbable of the young season. LeBron James, in his 24th NBA season, is delivering on the promise of what a reunion of elite veterans can accomplish.

Cleveland drops to 0-2 and faces real questions about how they respond when games turn against them. For Mitchell and the Cavaliers, the season is long and this loss — as stinging as it feels — is two days old.

For the 76ers, it's a statement. They were down 29. They won anyway. The East knows who they are now.
