Anthony Davis Ties Wizards Franchise Record in DC Debut: 34 Points, Bucks Fall 128-116

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | October 23, 2026

The Capital One Arena crowd was chanting "MVP" before the final buzzer sounded. Anthony Davis made sure they had reason to.

In his debut as a Washington Wizard on Tuesday night, Davis scored 34 points on 11-of-20 shooting, pulled down 12 rebounds, dished 4 assists, and added 2 steals and a block in 34 minutes. The performance tied the franchise record for most points scored by a Wizard in their first game with the organization — a record that now belongs equally to Davis and the player he shares it with.

Washington beat Milwaukee 128-116, climbing above .500 for the first time since November 28, 2022 — a stretch of 1,423 days that felt like it would never end.

The Performance That Washington Needed

For a franchise that has been in rebuilding mode for the better part of three seasons, the Davis acquisition was the moment everything changed. Fans wanted validation. Davis gave them a masterclass instead.

He dominated in the post. He stepped out to hit mid-range jumpers. He caught lobs. He protected the rim on the other end. The versatility that made him one of the most coveted players available was on full display in a performance that left no doubt about the direction of this franchise.

Trae Young — Davis's new backcourt running mate — ran the offense with the precision that reminds fans why he's one of the best playmaking guards in the league. Young's 20 points and 13 assists were the perfect complement to Davis's dominance in the frontcourt. Together, they look like a combination Milwaukee had no answer for.

The Subplot: AJ Dybantsa Watches From the Bench

The No. 1 overall pick in the 2026 NBA Draft was in the building but not on the floor. AJ Dybantsa, who arrived in Washington as one of the most hyped prospects in recent memory, sat out Tuesday's opener with a left ankle sprain. His absence added an unexpected subplot to a night that was already full of them.

For now, Dybantsa is watching. When he returns — and his timeline remains unclear — he'll be stepping into a lineup that already includes Davis and Young. The learning curve will be steep. The opportunity is extraordinary.

Washington fans have waited years for a reason to get excited. They now have a veteran superstar producing franchise records and a generational prospect waiting in the wings. The future arrived faster than anyone expected.

Milwaukee's Stumble

The Bucks entered the season with plenty of their own storylines, and Tyler Herro's 24 points gave them a legitimate contributor in the backcourt. Jaime Jaquez Jr. added 22 as Milwaukee showed some offensive versatility. But the Bucks couldn't match the sheer force of Davis in the paint, and Washington's second-half execution was too clean.

Milwaukee fell behind early in the second half and never found the right adjustments to pull back within striking distance. Davis converted on high-percentage looks all night, and the Bucks' defensive rotations were consistently a step slow.

What's Next

Washington is 1-0, above .500 for the first time in years, and in the middle of a genuine buzz cycle that the league hasn't directed at the Wizards in over a decade. Anthony Davis, 33 years old and motivated, looks like a player who arrived in DC to win — not to collect a paycheck at the end of a career.

AJ Dybantsa's return will be the next major storyline. When he's healthy enough to step into this lineup, Washington has a trio that can make genuine noise in the Eastern Conference playoff race.

Tuesday night was the beginning. The chants of "MVP" at Capital One Arena weren't just gratitude — they were a declaration about what this team might become.
