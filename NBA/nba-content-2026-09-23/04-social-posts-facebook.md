### STORY 2: Kawhi Leonard Signs 2-Year, $115M Extension With Raptors

**FB Long-Form (9:00 AM ET)**
Kawhi Leonard is staying in Toronto — and he gave up money to do it.

The two-time Finals MVP and the Toronto Raptors have agreed to a two-year, $115 million contract extension, according to an AP source. The deal includes a player option for 2028-29, meaning Leonard controls his own destiny going forward.

Here's what makes this story different from any other contract news: Kawhi Leonard took $11 million less than his maximum extension number. He also waived a $7.45 million escalator that would have added to his total. That's approximately $18.5 million left on the table — voluntarily — to give the Raptors the flexibility to build around him.

Leonard was re-acquired from the Los Angeles Clippers in a July 2026 trade, ending a complicated chapter. He won his first championship in Toronto in 2019. He left for the Clippers. He had knee issues, missed time, and his Clippers career was defined more by its interruptions than its highlights. Now he's back.

The Raptors and Leonard had constant dialogue about his long-term future since July. This extension is the answer. He is expected to finish his career in Toronto — and he restructured his finances to make sure the organization had the room to stay competitive around him.

That is a player who understands legacy. That is a player who learned something from the years away.

### STORY 5: Ausar Thompson 5-Year, $155M Extension

**FB Long-Form (11:00 AM ET)**
The Detroit Pistons have secured their defensive cornerstone.

Ausar Thompson, 23, has agreed to a fully guaranteed five-year, $155 million rookie-scale extension. Last season, Thompson averaged 9.9 points, 5.7 rebounds, 3.1 assists, and 2.0 steals per game — leading the entire NBA in steals — while earning first-team All-Defense honors and finishing third in Defensive Player of the Year voting.

He's the heartbeat of a Pistons defense that helped the team win 60 games and earn the No. 1 seed in the Eastern Conference.

But the part of this story that deserves a wider audience: Ausar Thompson and his twin brother Amen Thompson have now become the first brothers in NBA history to each sign contracts worth $100 million or more. Ausar's $155M with Detroit, combined with Amen's deal with the Houston Rockets, adds up to $363 million for the Thompson twins this offseason.

Two brothers. Two franchises. Two generational contracts. It's a basketball story and a family story — and it's unlike anything the league has seen before.
