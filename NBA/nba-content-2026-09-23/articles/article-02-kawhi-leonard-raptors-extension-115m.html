Kawhi Leonard Signs 2-Year, $115M Extension With the Raptors — and Left $18.5M on the Table to Do It

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | September 23, 2026

Kawhi Leonard is staying in Toronto. The two-time NBA Finals MVP and the Toronto Raptors have reached a two-year, $115 million contract extension, according to an AP source who spoke to multiple outlets on September 22. The deal includes a player option for the 2028-29 season, giving Leonard control of his future while committing to the franchise he won a championship with in 2019.

What makes the agreement notable is not just the length or the money. It's what Leonard willingly gave up to make it happen.

The Discount That Tells the Story

Leonard took $11 million less than his maximum extension number. He also waived a $7.45 million escalator clause that would have increased his total payout based on certain performance thresholds. Combined, that's approximately $18.5 million left on the table — voluntarily — by one of the best players of his generation.

That is not a small gesture in a league where every dollar matters. That is a player making a calculated decision to prioritize the team's financial flexibility over his own maximum earnings. The Raptors now have salary room to make additional moves around Leonard rather than being stretched thin by a maximum deal.

Leonard and the Raptors had constant dialogue about his long-term future since July, when he was re-acquired from the Los Angeles Clippers in a trade. The AP source who confirmed the extension said Leonard is "expected to finish his career in Toronto." Those are not words used lightly in the transactional reality of the modern NBA.

The Journey Back

The road from 2019 to this extension has not been simple. Leonard won the Raptors their only championship in 2019, then exercised his right to choose his next destination and signed with the Los Angeles Clippers. What followed was a tenure defined by injury. Leonard missed significant time with knee issues, and while he remained one of the most talented players in the league when healthy, the Clippers chapter never fully delivered on its promise.

The trade back to Toronto in July 2026 was framed, by multiple sources, as a homecoming Leonard had been thinking about for some time. He had unfinished business with a city that embraced him during his one season there. He had relationships with the organization that never entirely dissolved. Coming back was a choice, not a fallback.

The extension confirms what the trade suggested: Kawhi Leonard views Toronto as his home.

What It Means for the Raptors

Leonard's willingness to take a below-market extension gives the Raptors a meaningful edge as they try to build a legitimate contender around him. The $18.5 million in flexibility he created with his discount is not theoretical. It is cap space that can be deployed to add complementary pieces, retain their own players, or absorb another contract in a future trade.

For a franchise that has had limited opportunities to retain elite talent long-term, this extension represents something significant. A player of Leonard's caliber choosing Toronto — and choosing to stay on terms that benefit the organization rather than himself — is a statement about the franchise's appeal and stability.

What's Next

With the extension finalized, Leonard will appear at the Raptors' media day on September 28, officially beginning his second chapter in Toronto. Training camp opens September 29. The Raptors now have their cornerstone under contract through at least the 2027-28 season, with Leonard holding a player option for 2028-29. The focus shifts to how the Raptors deploy the salary flexibility he created — and whether Toronto can build the kind of supporting cast that gives Leonard a genuine chance at a second championship banner at Scotiabank Arena.
