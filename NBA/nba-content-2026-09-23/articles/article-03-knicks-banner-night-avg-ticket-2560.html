Knicks Banner Night: Average Ticket Now $2,560 With 27 Days to Go

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | September 23, 2026

The market has made its judgment: the New York Knicks' Opening Night on October 20 is the most anticipated single game in recent NBA history. The average ticket price on secondary markets has climbed to $2,560 — higher than any other NBA game on the calendar — with the cheapest available seats still exceeding $1,000. Twenty-seven days from now, the Knicks will raise their first championship banner since 1973. The price reflects that.

What Is Being Priced In

To understand the ticket prices, you have to understand what is happening in the building on October 20. At 6:30 PM ET, the Knicks will raise a banner to the rafters at Madison Square Garden. It will be the franchise's first championship banner in 53 years — one that covers two generations of Knicks fans who grew up watching the team fall short year after year.

At 7:00 PM ET, following the ceremony, the game tips off on NBC and Peacock. The opponent is the Philadelphia 76ers — the same team the Knicks swept 4-0 in the 2025-26 playoffs, averaging 124.3 points and holding the 76ers to 102. Now the 76ers have rebuilt into something entirely different.

LeBron James, 41, will make his Philadelphia 76ers debut at Madison Square Garden. Jaylen Brown, acquired from the Boston Celtics in a trade that sent Paul George and four draft picks the other direction, will also be playing at MSG for the first time as a Sixer. Joel Embiid will be there. Tyrese Maxey will be there. The team that was swept is now loaded — and they are walking into the most hostile building in basketball on its most emotional night.

The Demand in Context

For comparison: the Celtics at Pistons game on the same night — Opening Night of the 2026-27 season — was recently priced at a get-in of approximately $195 on the secondary market. That is a legitimate NBA game between two playoff-caliber teams. It costs about one-tenth of a seat at Knicks-76ers.

The Knicks last raised a championship banner in 1973. The team that delivered it — Willis Reed, Walt Frazier, Bill Bradley — has been waiting decades for a successor. When the 2025-26 Knicks ended that wait, it did not just reward the current generation of fans. It redeemed the faith of everyone who stuck with a franchise through the long years between.

The market is pricing not just a game, but a resolution. And it is pricing the theater of LeBron James walking into that arena as the villain, 27 days after the Knicks made it their house.

The Broadcast Setup

The game airs on NBC — the network's return to NBA broadcasting has been one of the significant media stories of the 2026-27 offseason — and will also stream on Peacock. The combination of traditional broadcast reach and streaming availability means the audience for this event will be substantial. For fans at home, the barrier is low. For fans in the building, it is the highest it has ever been.

What's Next

Twenty-seven days from now, the Knicks and 76ers tip off on a national stage. Between now and then, both teams will hold media days on September 28, open training camps on September 29, and play preseason games beginning October 3. The narrative around this Opening Night matchup will only grow. The average ticket price of $2,560 reflects a market that already knows what it is: the most anticipated opening night in a generation — and 27 days from now, the Garden will deliver.
