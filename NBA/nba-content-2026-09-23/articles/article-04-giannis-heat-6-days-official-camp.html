Six Days Until Heat Camp — Giannis Has Been Ready for Weeks

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | September 23, 2026

The Miami Heat's official training camp opens September 29 — six days from now. For most of the NBA, that date marks the start of serious preparation. For Giannis Antetokounmpo and his new teammates in Miami, preparation has been underway for weeks already.

Giannis skipped Greece's FIBA qualifying games this summer to arrive at Miami's unofficial minicamp in California ahead of schedule. He wanted to learn Erik Spoelstra's system before anyone else. He has done exactly that.

An Unusual Offseason Commitment

International stars of Giannis's caliber typically represent their national teams during FIBA windows. Giannis, a two-time NBA MVP who spent 13 seasons with the Milwaukee Bucks before signing with Miami this summer, made a different calculation. The early months with a new franchise — learning a new offense, a new defensive system, new teammates — are the ones that matter most for a player of his age and his goals.

He said as much in comments that drew attention earlier in the offseason. "I want to know everything," Giannis said of his approach to learning the Heat's system. "Offense, defense — so when training camp starts, I'm already ahead."

The minicamp in California gave him that head start. Reports from those sessions described a player fully engaged, studying film, asking questions, and working to build the kind of instinctive familiarity with Spoelstra's system that usually takes a full season to develop.

The Roster Around Him

The Heat did not build a supporting cast around Giannis by accident. The offseason additions of Klay Thompson, Bobby Portis, and Tim Hardaway Jr. were targeted acquisitions aimed at surrounding a dominant two-way player with the shooting, toughness, and versatility that Spoelstra's system requires.

Thompson brings a Hall of Fame-caliber shooting stroke and championship experience. Portis brings energy, rebounding, and the kind of physicality that playoff basketball demands. Hardaway brings a secondary scoring option off the wing. Each piece was chosen with a clear understanding of what a team built around Giannis needs to be complete.

The chemistry those players are developing in the California minicamp is exactly the kind of investment that pays dividends in April and May, when the regular season gives way to something harder and more unforgiving.

The Calendar Ahead

Media day for the Miami Heat — and 27 other teams — is September 28. Official training camp opens September 29. The first preseason game is October 3. The regular season tips off October 20.

That is a tight timeline for a team assembling new pieces around a transformative talent. But the Heat have used the unofficial period better than almost anyone. By the time Giannis and his teammates walk into Kaseya Center for the first official practice, they will not be strangers to each other's games. They will already be a team in the ways that matter.

What's Next

September 28 brings media day, where Giannis will speak publicly about his Heat tenure for the first time in an official NBA context. September 29 brings official camp, where Spoelstra will begin the formal work of installing his system at full speed. Watch the first preseason game on October 3 for the first live look at how this roster translates from minicamp chemistry to competitive basketball. Six days from now, the Giannis Era in Miami becomes official.
