Patrick Ewing Joins the Washington Wizards — A Hall of Famer's Next Coaching Chapter

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | August 3, 2026

Patrick Ewing played thirteen seasons in Madison Square Garden, defined the New York Knicks through one of the franchise's finest eras, and spent two years after his playing career as an advisor and Basketball Ambassador with the team that made him famous.

Now he's leaving the Garden's orbit entirely and heading to Washington.

Ewing is joining the Wizards as an assistant coach under head coach Brian Keefe. His primary assignment: work with Washington's big-man rotation of Anthony Davis, Alex Sarr, and Deandre Ayton. It is a compelling portfolio — three centers with radically different games and development needs, all under the tutelage of a Hall of Fame center who spent his career mastering the craft they are still learning.

The Roster Ewing Is Walking Into

Anthony Davis came to Washington as part of the offseason that reshaped the Eastern Conference. He is one of the most physically gifted big men in league history — an eight-time All-Star capable of guarding all five positions and dominating at both ends when healthy. The question around Davis has always been durability and engagement; what he needs from a coaching staff is consistency and clarity.

Alex Sarr is the opposite: a young player still building the habits, footwork, and competitive confidence required to become the player he projects to be. A high-ceiling prospect with length and skill, Sarr's development will define Washington's future ceiling far more than any single trade or signing. Getting him right is the most important thing this staff can do.

Deandre Ayton is somewhere in between — a former No. 1 overall pick looking to recapture the form that made him an elite finisher and a force on the glass. Ayton at his best is a premium starting center. He needs a coaching voice that understands post play, pick-and-roll mechanics, and the mental side of playing physically in the paint.

Ewing can speak to all three of those situations.

The Coaching Carousel Wraps Up

The NBA's offseason coaching shuffle was among the most active in recent memory. Six teams hired new head coaches: Dusty May in Dallas, Taylor Jenkins in Milwaukee, Tiago Splitter in Chicago, Sean Sweeney in Orlando, Jamahl Mosley in New Orleans, and Micah Nori in Portland. Billy Donovan stepped down from Chicago on April 21. Doc Rivers resigned from Milwaukee on April 13.

Multiple staff reshuffles followed throughout the league as new head coaches assembled their personnel and veteran assistants moved to new assignments. Ewing's hiring in Washington is among the last significant moves of a carousel that churned through May, June, and July.

It also marks a meaningful next step in Ewing's coaching evolution. He has worked as a head coach at Georgetown, gaining experience running a program before returning to the NBA support structure. The Wizards' roster gives him a legitimate big-man laboratory — and gives him a franchise that is trying to build toward something real around its investments in Davis and Sarr.

What's Next

Washington begins training camp in late September with one of the most intriguing frontlines in the Eastern Conference, if not the most polished. Ewing will spend the pre-camp period working individually with Davis, Sarr, and Ayton before the collective work begins. How quickly that unit comes together — how well Davis leads, how fast Sarr grows, how much Ayton rediscovers his best game — will determine whether Washington makes noise in the East this season or files the pieces away for a future window. Patrick Ewing has worked with greatness. Whether he can help build it in Washington is his new challenge.
