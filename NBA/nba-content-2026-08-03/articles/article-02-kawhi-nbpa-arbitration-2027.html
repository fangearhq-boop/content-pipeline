The Kawhi Leonard Investigation Has a New Problem: The NBPA Can Demand Arbitration — and It Could Last Until 2027

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | August 3, 2026

Commissioner Adam Silver said the NBA's investigation into Kawhi Leonard and the Los Angeles Clippers must end before the 2026-27 season. On August 3, that directive is running out of road — and a new procedural reality is making things significantly more complicated.

The NBPA has the right under the CBA to demand that the case go before a neutral arbitrator if the parties cannot reach a settlement. That arbitration process has no hard deadline. And per ESPN's Baxter Holmes, the investigation could now extend deep into 2027, potentially consuming the entire regular season.

The case started in September 2025. It has already lasted longer than anyone expected. The end is not in sight.

Where Things Stand

The NBA retained Wachtell Lipton, one of the country's most prominent law firms, to conduct the fact-finding. That work is now complete. The NBA is reviewing the firm's findings.

The core allegation: the Clippers and Kawhi Leonard circumvented the league's salary cap rules through a $28 million endorsement deal with Aspiration Fund Adviser LLC, a financial services company. The CEO of Aspiration has since been convicted on federal fraud charges and sentenced to 14 years in prison. The Clippers maintain they were victims of the CEO's misconduct, not co-conspirators.

A second, previously undisclosed endorsement deal has also been added to the investigation's scope, per HoopsHype. The probe has grown in breadth and complexity since it began.

If the league and the relevant parties can agree on findings and a penalty, the case ends there. If they can't — if the NBPA contests the findings on behalf of Leonard — a formal arbitration hearing begins. And once it begins, it takes as long as it takes.

The Frozen Trade

Meanwhile, the Toronto Raptors and Los Angeles Clippers agreed to a trade on June 30 that would send Kawhi back to Toronto. The terms: Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, unprotected 2031 and 2033 first-round picks, additional selections, and a 2027 first-round swap.

It is now August 3. That trade has not been completed. It remains frozen pending the investigation's resolution.

The Raptors cannot finalize the deal because no one will agree to which team absorbs liability if penalties come down after the trade closes. The Clippers cannot move on. Kawhi, who carries a $50.3 million salary for 2026-27, cannot plan his future.

HoopsHype reported July 31 that Dillon Brooks told a streamer that the trade to Toronto simply won't happen — though that is one player's read, not official confirmation. The formal trade agreement still exists on paper. But the longer the investigation drags on, the more realistic scenarios emerge where it simply collapses.

What the Penalties Could Look Like

The worst-case scenario for Leonard and the Clippers, per reporting on the possible penalty structure: voiding of Leonard's contract, a suspension of up to 40 games, fines of $7.5 million, and forfeiture of draft picks.

Even a lesser outcome would have significant implications. Any confirmed finding of salary cap circumvention would alter how other teams have built their rosters — the Raptors, who agreed to the trade in part to reacquire a player they helped develop, would need to reassess their plans. The Clippers, who are trying to rebuild after the Leonard saga consumed multiple seasons, cannot move forward without resolution.

The NBPA is fully engaged, with formal arbitration demands ready if the league's findings are contested. That's the mechanism that turns a "nearly over" investigation into a "lasts all season" one.

What's Next

The NBA's review of Wachtell Lipton's findings is the immediate next step. If a settlement is reached, the investigation ends and the trade can proceed — or be renegotiated under new circumstances. If settlement talks break down and the NBPA demands arbitration, the case enters a new phase that has no set endpoint. Commissioner Silver's preference is clear. Whether the procedure allows him to honor that preference is the central uncertainty heading into August. Every passing day without a resolution makes October 20 — opening night — feel more like a date the Kawhi saga might actually outlast.
