Wembanyama, Gobert, and Fournier: France's FIBA Qualifying Roster Is Official

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | August 3, 2026

Victor Wembanyama said he missed FIBA. Now he gets to play.

France has officially announced its 15-man roster for the FIBA Basketball World Cup 2027 European qualifying window. Wembanyama leads the squad. Rudy Gobert is alongside him. And Evan Fournier makes a long-anticipated return to the national team for the first time since the Paris Olympics period.

The schedule: France plays Serbia in preparatory games on August 20 and 23, then enters FIBA qualifying action by hosting Slovenia on August 27 before traveling to Sweden on August 30. France enters the round at the top of Group L with a 5-1 record from the first qualifying stage — a stretch completed without Wembanyama in the lineup.

He's back now. The ceiling for this team has risen considerably.

The Frontcourt Nobody Wants to Face

The pairing of Wembanyama and Gobert on the same FIBA roster is not subtle. Wembanyama, at 22, is the most physically imposing and skill-diverse big man the sport has ever produced. Gobert, the four-time NBA Defensive Player of the Year, is one of the most effective rim protectors in basketball history.

Together, they give France a frontcourt that will challenge any offensive system built around paint scoring. Slovenia and Sweden will face the same structural problem: how do you attack the rim when the two best shot-blocking big men in the world are standing there?

The answer, for most teams, is that you don't — you try to stretch the defense, work the perimeter, and hope the French guard rotation makes mistakes. But France also has Fournier returning to provide scoring punch from the outside, and a supporting cast that has proven capable of winning without Wembanyama when they needed to.

Fournier's Return

Evan Fournier's presence on this roster is notable in its own right. The veteran guard made his return to the national team after an extended absence around the Paris Olympics period — a homecoming to the program that helped define the first half of his career.

Fournier provides something France's roster needs alongside its frontcourt anchors: a scorer with international experience who knows how the FIBA game operates, understands the spacing requirements at the European level, and can perform under pressure in two-possession games. His veteran IQ is a genuine complement to the younger players building around Wembanyama.

Wembanyama's Bigger Summer Picture

The FIBA qualifying window fits into a broader picture of how Wembanyama is spending his offseason. He signed a five-year, $252 million extension with the San Antonio Spurs — reportedly taking money below the supermax to give the franchise roster flexibility. Nike confirmed a signature shoe deal. And the Spurs' coaching staff and a group of players have gathered in Paris this summer for joint work sessions, turning the French capital into an informal pre-camp environment.

The FIBA games on August 27 and 30 give Wembanyama competitive basketball before Spurs training camp opens in late September. He gets game speed, game intelligence, and international positioning experience — all at a moment in the offseason when most NBA players are simply resting.

This is, to put it plainly, what it looks like when one of the most driven players in the sport treats the offseason as an extension of the season.

What's Next

France's prep games against Serbia on August 20 and 23 give the coaching staff a chance to integrate Wembanyama back into the squad before the games that count. Qualifiers against Slovenia on August 27 and Sweden on August 30 determine the path toward the 2027 FIBA Basketball World Cup. For Wembanyama, the window closes in time for him to return to San Antonio for training camp — arriving in October sharper, more tested, and more motivated than a conventional offseason would have allowed.
