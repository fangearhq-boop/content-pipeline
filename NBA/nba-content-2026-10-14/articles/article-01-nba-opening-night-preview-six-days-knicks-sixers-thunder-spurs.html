Six Days Until Opening Night: The Three-Game Slate That Has Everything

By Jake Torres | October 14, 2026 | Hoop Heroes

The NBA has one week. That is all that separates the preseason — the scouting reports, the training camp speculation, the soft preseason box scores — from the real thing. And the real thing, on October 20, begins with one of the most loaded Opening Night slates the league has ever scheduled. Three games. Three separate narratives. Zero games you want to skip.

NBC carries all three on national television, starting at 3:00 PM ET and ending sometime past midnight. Each game could have its own documentary. Together, they are a declaration of what the 2026-27 season intends to be.

Game 1: Celtics at Pistons — 3:00 PM ET, Little Caesars Arena

The afternoon game features two teams that finished as the East's top two regular-season franchises in 2025-26, back at each other before the summer has fully faded. The Celtics are always a storyline — they have been to the Finals, they have won championships recently, they run a system that makes them difficult to prepare for. The Pistons have been building toward a moment like this. An early-afternoon national broadcast is rarely a throwaway game in October, and this one is not.

The East's early power structure is being established through every preseason result and scouting adjustment. This game is the first chapter of that story.

Game 2: Sixers at Knicks — 7:00 PM ET, Madison Square Garden

This is the game the league is built for. Before tip-off, the New York Knicks will raise their first championship banner since 1973. The Garden will be at its loudest. Jalen Brunson — the 2026 Finals MVP who scored 45 points in the Game 5 clincher against the Spurs — will walk out in front of a crowd that has waited more than 50 years for this moment. The banner goes up. The rings come out. And then the game starts.

The first visitor through the door: LeBron James. In a Philadelphia 76ers uniform. At 41 years old. In his 24th NBA season. He signed a two-year $8 million deal in July, announced it himself on July 24, and somehow made sure his regular-season debut would be exactly this: the one game in the schedule that no one in New York wants to lose more than any other.

Alongside him: Jaylen Brown, acquired from Boston for Paul George and four future first-round picks; Joel Embiid; and Tyrese Maxey. This is a team built to compete for a championship. Their first real test is the hardest possible road game the schedule could have given them.

Game 3: Thunder at Spurs — 9:30 PM ET, AT&T Center

The late game brings the 2026 Western Conference Finals back for an encore. Oklahoma City — the betting favorites to win the 2026-27 championship at +230 — walks into San Antonio as the road team for Victor Wembanyama's regular-season debut on his new five-year, $252 million extension.

Context: The Spurs beat the Thunder in Game 7 of the Western Conference Finals before losing to the Knicks in five games in the NBA Finals. Wembanyama was extraordinary in that Finals at moments — 32 points in the only game San Antonio won — but the Knicks erased a 29-point deficit in Game 4 and Brunson closed it out. Now it is October again, and the first game of the new season brings the Western Conference's two best teams into the same building at 9:30 PM.

This is not incidental. This is the NBA's way of telling you: the West was decided in seven games last spring, and none of the principals are done competing.

What's Next

The NBA's roster deadline falls on October 17 at 4:00 PM CT, with final rosters set by October 19 at 4:00 PM CT. Six days after that, the season arrives. The tripleheader on NBC is not just an opening night — it is the announcement that everything that happened this past summer has consequences, and the 2026-27 NBA season is going to make you watch.
