### STORY 3: Giannis in Miami

**Post 3 (11:30 AM ET)**
The NBA's most intriguing new roster is taking shape in South Beach.

Giannis Antetokounmpo arrived in Miami this summer after the Bucks traded him in July. The deal sent Tyler Herro, Jaime Jaquez Jr., and Kel'el Ware to Milwaukee, with Giannis and Bobby Portis coming to Miami. For a player of Giannis's caliber, that is a remarkable haul for a franchise that simply needed a reset.

In two preseason games, the 31-year-old Antetokounmpo has looked sharp. In his debut on October 3 in Quebec City, he posted 9 points, 4 rebounds and 7 assists in just 14 minutes as Miami handled Toronto 129-105. His second game, at Kaseya Center against New Orleans, brought 18 points — punishing the defense inside all night. The Heat won 128-118.

Oh — and Klay Thompson is also on this team.

Giannis summed it up with characteristic directness: he told reporters the Heat will be a "nightmare defensively" for their opponents. That's not preseason hype. That's the Heat's identity meeting one of the most complete defenders in the modern game.

This team is going to be worth watching. 🔥

### STORY 4: NBA Roster Deadline

**Post 4 (10:00 AM ET)**
The NBA's preseason window is almost closed.

This week brings the most consequential deadline period of the offseason calendar. Here is the timeline every front office is counting down:

Friday, October 17: The last day teams can waive players on fully non-guaranteed contracts without counting the salary against the cap.

Sunday, October 19: At 4:00 PM CT, rosters must drop to 15 standard contracts and three two-way deals. Teams that entered camp with 20 or 21 players have to make their choices.

Monday, October 20: The season begins.

For some players, this week is the difference between an NBA contract and another shot in the G League. For teams, it's the moment where depth decisions get locked in for the long haul.

The Lakers entered training camp with 16 standard contracts. Several other franchises are in similar positions. The next five days will be busy ones for NBA front offices everywhere.

### STORY 5: The Wizards' New Core

**Post 5 (11:00 AM ET)**
Nobody saw this coming for Washington.

Eight months ago, the Wizards were firmly in rebuild mode. Then came Trae Young, acquired in January 2026. Then came Anthony Davis — a three-team February trade that sent Davis from Dallas to Washington — at a cost of $58.5 million for the 2026-27 season, with a $62.8 million player option for the year after.

Then the draft. With the No. 1 overall pick, Washington selected AJ Dybantsa — the 19-year-old forward from BYU who put up 27-point performances in Summer League before the team shut him down.

In his preseason debut against the Knicks on October 8, Dybantsa posted 11 points, 4 steals, 2 assists and 2 rebounds in 23 minutes. Washington won 111-109. Anthony Davis led Washington in scoring, and Trae Young directed traffic.

This is not the Wizards roster anyone expected to be talking about in October. But here we are.

If Anthony Davis is healthy — always a meaningful if — and Young and Dybantsa develop chemistry with him, Washington has legitimate Eastern Conference playoff potential by midseason. The franchise GM said it plainly in June: "With AD, he wants to be here and we want him here."

That's a starting point. The rest is on the court.
