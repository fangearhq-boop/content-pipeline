Anthony Davis, Trae Young, AJ Dybantsa — Washington's Unlikely New Core Is Real

By Marcus Cole | October 12, 2026 | Hoop Heroes

Twelve months ago, the Washington Wizards were one of the NBA's more predictable stories: a team rebuilding, watching games with an eye toward the lottery, accumulating assets and hoping for a high pick. That story changed quickly, and then changed again, until October 2026 arrived with Washington holding a roster that raises genuinely interesting questions about what this franchise could become.

Anthony Davis. Trae Young. AJ Dybantsa. That is an unusual combination in the best possible way.

How the Core Was Built

Trae Young came first, arriving in Washington in January 2026 as the Wizards acquired the All-Star guard looking to build around a proven playmaker. Then, in February 2026, a three-team trade involving Dallas and Charlotte brought Anthony Davis to Washington in exchange for Khris Middleton and other assets.

Davis, 33, earns $58.5 million in 2026–27 and holds a $62.8 million player option for the 2027–28 season. He is the most expensive player in Wizards history by a significant margin. His health record — a chronic concern throughout his career in Los Angeles and Dallas — is the most watched variable in Washington's plans. But the Wizards' front office made the call, and as of June 2026 the organization confirmed publicly: both sides want the partnership to continue.

Then came the 2026 NBA Draft. With the No. 1 overall pick, Washington selected AJ Dybantsa — the 19-year-old forward out of BYU who had already become one of the most discussed prospects in recent memory. In two Summer League games, he averaged approximately 27 points before the team shut him down to manage his workload.

Dybantsa's Preseason Debut

On October 8, Dybantsa walked into Madison Square Garden for Washington's preseason game against the Knicks. In 23 minutes, the 19-year-old posted 11 points, 2 rebounds, 2 assists, and 4 steals. The Wizards won 111–109.

The four steals stood out. Steals at that rate in a preseason debut are not a coincidence — they reflect awareness, anticipation, and competitive instinct. Davis led Washington in scoring. Young directed the offense. And Dybantsa, in his first real look at NBA competition, held up against Knicks guards who are part of the defending championship roster.

It was one game. But first impressions matter in a league that pays close attention to them.

What Could Go Right — and What Could Go Wrong

The optimistic version of Washington's season features Davis healthy and dominant at the five, Young running pick-and-roll with him the way only a true point guard can, and Dybantsa developing into a reliable third contributor who finishes with athleticism and defends. That team wins more games than most people project and earns a playoff seed in an Eastern Conference that remains congested.

The pessimistic version is familiar. Davis's injury history is not a rumor; it is a pattern. Young's defense has been a liability throughout his career. And a 19-year-old No. 1 pick, however talented, is still 19 years old. Chemistry takes time.

The Wizards have bet on the optimistic version. The roster construction says they believe it is possible. The June statement from their front office says they believe Davis wants to be there. The evidence from Dybantsa's preseason debut says the youngest part of their core is already competing.

What's Next

Washington's roster must be finalized by October 19, two days before their regular-season opener. With the Davis-Young-Dybantsa core in place, the final roster decisions are about complementary depth — the players who will fill out a lineup that needs shooting, versatility, and rim protection around its three key pieces.

The NBA's Opening Night spotlight on October 20 does not include Washington. But the Wizards — unexpected, interesting, and harder to predict than any team in the East — may end up being one of the better stories of the 2026–27 season. The core is assembled. The question now is what they do with it.
