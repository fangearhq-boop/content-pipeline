The Kings Just Waived a 6x All-Star. Now the Phone Lines Are Open for DeMar DeRozan

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | July 26, 2026

Six All-Star selections. Over 19,000 career points. An elite mid-range game that has survived every shift in NBA trends toward three-point shooting and analytics-first offense. DeMar DeRozan is 36 years old, recently waived by the Sacramento Kings, and available to any team willing to call his agent.

The Kings made their decision as part of a broader roster realignment. Sacramento has its own trajectory to manage, and DeRozan — who had contributed efficiently as a veteran scorer — no longer fit the direction the franchise is heading. In the language of NBA transactions, he was released. For any team looking at their depth chart and seeing a hole at a scoring forward or veteran guard spot, that release is a gift.

DeRozan's mid-range game is not just stylistically distinctive — it is historically rare. He is one of the best practitioners of the pull-up mid-range shot in the post-Analytics NBA era, a player who mastered a skill that most front offices have spent years trying to coach out of their rosters. The difference is that DeRozan converts at an elite efficiency rate. When he catches the ball at the elbow, it's a good shot — because he makes it.

What DeRozan Still Offers

At 36, DeRozan is not a 30-point scorer anymore. Asking him to carry an offense for 35 minutes per game would be a mismatch for where he is in his career. But asking him to be a 15-18 point per game contributor off the bench or in a sixth-man role? That's squarely within his current capability.

DeRozan has also demonstrated throughout his career that he is an effective closer. He can create his own shot in isolation situations when a team needs a bucket in the final two minutes — the kind of self-creation that is actually more scarce than pure athleticism at the NBA level, because it comes from read and technique rather than raw speed. Teams in the Western Conference with questions about their half-court offense in late-game situations should be particularly interested.

The defensive limitations are real. DeRozan at 36 is not the player teams want guarding quick wings or elite point guards for extended possessions. Any team that signs him will need to be thoughtful about defensive rotations and matchups. But in a win-now environment with a strong defensive core already in place, DeRozan's offensive value outweighs those concerns.

The Market

DeRozan on the open market, post-waiver, will sign at the veteran minimum or something close to it. He is 36 and coming off a season where he was ultimately released — the market price reflects reality. But at the minimum, he is potentially the best offensive bargain available. Any team finishing its roster for under the luxury tax threshold can add a six-time All-Star for effectively nothing.

The Sacramento Kings' front office made a business decision. For the team that figures out how to use DeMar DeRozan correctly, that decision becomes someone else's windfall.

What's Next

DeRozan will enter the free agent market immediately following his waiver clearance. The expectation is that multiple teams — particularly those in contention or those building toward a playoff push — will reach out in the coming days. A decision could come quickly, especially if a contender offers a role that makes sense. The league's free agent signing deadline is not until October, but DeRozan and his camp will want clarity before training camp opens in late September. The mid-range maestro's next chapter is about to begin — someone, somewhere, is making a very smart phone call.
