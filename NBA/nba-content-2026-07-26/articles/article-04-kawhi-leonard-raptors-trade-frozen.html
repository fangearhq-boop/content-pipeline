One Month Later, the Kawhi Leonard Trade Is Still On Hold — and the NBA Clock Is Ticking

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | July 26, 2026

The Toronto Raptors and the Los Angeles Clippers agreed on the trade nearly five weeks ago. The compensation was confirmed: Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, unprotected first-round picks in 2031 and 2033, a 2027 pick swap, and two second-round picks heading to Los Angeles. Kawhi Leonard would go the other way, returning to the franchise where he won his only championship in 2019.

Toronto fans celebrated. Raptors social media lit up. Former teammates like Kyle Lowry posted welcome-back messages. It was, by every measure, done.

Except it wasn't. And as of July 26, it still isn't.

The NBA opened an investigation into allegations that the Toronto Raptors paid Kawhi Leonard impermissible benefits — and until that investigation concludes, the league will not process the trade. The deal sits in official limbo while both franchises wait for a resolution they cannot control.

What the Investigation Involves

The specific allegations center on claims that the Raptors provided Leonard with benefits that violated the NBA's player-team negotiation rules — payments or arrangements that fall outside what a team is permitted to offer a player during free agency or trade discussions. The league takes these violations seriously; outcomes can range from fines and draft pick forfeiture to voiding the transaction itself.

The investigation has been ongoing for approximately 11 months, according to sources familiar with the timeline. The scope has reportedly expanded beyond the initial allegation, though the NBA has not provided public updates on its findings. Commissioner Adam Silver has publicly stated that the investigation must be resolved before the start of the 2026-27 season — a deadline that means some resolution, one way or another, is coming within roughly three months.

For the Raptors, the uncertainty is maddening. They cannot build their roster around Leonard if they don't know he's officially theirs. They cannot plan lineups, offseason schedules, or marketing campaigns around a player who is technically still a Clipper. And Brandon Ingram — one of their best young players — has already been committed to the Clippers as part of the deal, leaving Toronto without either the player they're sending or the player they're receiving.

Kawhi Leonard's Unique Situation

At 35 years old, Kawhi Leonard has seen more adversity in his career than almost any player of his caliber. Injuries, legal disputes, and front office controversies have defined large stretches of his time in the league. This latest chapter — waiting for a league investigation to decide the next step in his career — is consistent with the complicated narrative that has followed him throughout.

Leonard played all or most of the 2025-26 season with the Clippers, which provided some good news on the injury front. His health, when intact, is still his biggest variable. A fully healthy Kawhi Leonard in a Raptors uniform — in a building where he won the franchise's only title — would be one of the better stories in the sport.

But first, the NBA has to say the trade is real.

What's Next

Commissioner Silver's October deadline is the key date. If the investigation concludes before training camp opens, the trade can be processed in time for Leonard to join the Raptors for the start of the season. If it extends further or produces a finding that voids the deal, both franchises return to square one — and two rebuilding timelines are thrown into chaos.

For now, the Raptors wait. Kawhi Leonard waits. Raptors fans wait. And the NBA's investigation grinds forward at its own pace, unmoved by the urgency of any of the parties involved. In a league that moves at the speed of a trade deadline notification, this case is running on a different clock entirely.
