Wembanyama's Sacrifice Pays Off: Spurs Sign Tobias Harris, Deploy Their Championship Blueprint

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | July 26, 2026

When Victor Wembanyama agreed to his five-year, $252 million rookie extension last month, he did something counterintuitive for a player the league had anointed as a generational talent. He took the 25% max — not the 30% supermax. He left roughly $50 million on the table. And he did it on purpose.

The San Antonio Spurs are now showing exactly what that sacrifice purchased.

The Spurs have signed Tobias Harris in free agency, adding a proven 20-point-per-game scorer who provides the veteran finishing touch this young roster needed. Harris joins a team whose starting five is absurdly affordable by modern NBA standards: Wembanyama's base salary is $16.9 million, Dylan Harper earns $13 million, Stephon Castle comes in at $10 million, and Carter Bryant costs $5.1 million. That's roughly $45 million for four players who would rank among the most talented starting groups in the league.

The Wemby discount gave San Antonio room to make exactly these kinds of moves. Championship teams don't just need talent — they need complementary pieces, and pieces cost money. By deferring tens of millions in annual salary, Wembanyama gave his front office the ammunition to build properly.

The Fox-Wemby Two-Man Game

At the center of the Spurs' design is the pairing of De'Aaron Fox and Victor Wembanyama. Fox signed his own four-year, $228 million extension in the same window as Wembanyama, locking in the best pick-and-roll combination in the Western Conference for the foreseeable future.

Fox is one of the fastest players in the NBA — a relentless attack guard who can score at all three levels and draw fouls at an elite rate. Wembanyama is the most versatile big the game has seen since Dirk Nowitzki, with the added dimension of an elite shot-blocking instinct that redefines what a center can do. Their two-man game on pick-and-roll is theoretically unguardable: Fox can pull up, drive, or find Wemby rolling, and the defense has no good answer for any of those options.

Add a proven wing in Tobias Harris and two ascending guards in Harper and Castle, and this is a team with something to say in the Western Conference.

The San Antonio Way

It would be easy to look at this Spurs roster and see youth. Look closer and you see a system. San Antonio has built contenders before by layering veterans onto young talent — David Robinson and Tim Duncan, Duncan and Tony Parker, Parker and Kawhi Leonard. The formula has always been patience, talent development, and the kind of front office discipline that spends money where it matters most.

Wembanyama's willingness to take less speaks to that culture. He has absorbed the Spurs' ethos in a remarkably short time. At 22 years old, he's already made the kind of financial decision that NBA stars a decade older typically can't bring themselves to make. The team first. The mission matters more than the number.

The Spurs didn't rebuild after their last contending window. They reloaded — with the most talented prospect in NBA draft history at the center of everything, and a front office wise enough to build around him correctly.

What's Next

The Spurs may not be finished in free agency. With the cap flexibility Wembanyama created, there is room to add more veteran depth and potentially another rotation player. Training camp opens in late September, and San Antonio's preseason slate will be one of the most closely watched in the Western Conference.

Projections for the 2026-27 Spurs have them in the playoff picture with ceiling potential of a deep run. Wembanyama is 22. Fox is 28. Harper and Castle are 20. The window is now — and it's open for a very long time. This is what it looks like when a franchise plays the long game and executes it perfectly.
