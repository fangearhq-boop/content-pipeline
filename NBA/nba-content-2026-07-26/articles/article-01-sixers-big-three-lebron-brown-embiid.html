King James, Jaylen Brown, and Joel Embiid: The 76ers Build Their Most Audacious Big Three

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | July 26, 2026

The Philadelphia 76ers have spent decades waiting for a moment like this. After years of rebuilding, near-misses, and heartbreak, the franchise has assembled something that looks unmistakably like a championship team: LeBron James, Jaylen Brown, and Joel Embiid — three of the most accomplished players of their era, sharing one locker room and one mission.

LeBron James announced his signing on Saturday, choosing a 2-year, $8 million contract with Philadelphia over retirement and a half-dozen other suitors. He called it his "last decision," and the weight of that phrase is real. The 41-year-old King is entering his 24th NBA season. He'd admitted publicly that he was "pretty sure I played my last game" as a Laker. Instead of hanging up his sneakers, he chose one more run — and he chose Philly.

"I believe I can help make the Philadelphia 76ers a championship team," James said in his statement, "and I am so excited to energize a new fan base and start this incredible journey one last time."

The Numbers Behind the Trio

Take a moment to absorb the combined résumé sitting inside the Wells Fargo Center's practice facility: LeBron James has four championships and four Finals MVP awards. Joel Embiid has won the regular-season MVP award. Jaylen Brown has been a two-time All-Star and rising star in the Eastern Conference. Together, these three players account for five MVP trophies, 32 All-Star appearances, and 19 combined seasons of 20-plus point-per-game production.

Brown came to Philadelphia via a blockbuster trade that sent Paul George back to Boston along with two first-round picks and two second-rounders. The Celtics were confident enough in their depth to let Brown go; the 76ers were confident enough in this window to pay the price. At 29, Brown is entering the prime of his career, a two-way wing who averaged All-Star numbers last season and has yet to hit his ceiling as a scorer.

Embiid, when healthy, is the most dominant big man in the game. The center has won the MVP award and remains arguably the toughest offensive assignment in the NBA on any given night. The durability questions are real — Embiid has missed games every season of his career — but his upside when available is historic.

The Eastern Conference Just Got Harder

Before James inked his deal, the Eastern Conference was a wide-open race. The Miami Heat have Giannis Antetokounmpo. The Boston Celtics were building around their core. Now the 76ers have answered with something that none of those teams can match on paper: three elite players in the same starting lineup.

Oddsmakers moved quickly after the announcement. Championship probability models that had the 76ers as fringe contenders now have them among the favorites heading into the 2026-27 season. Vegas doesn't wait — and neither does the basketball world.

The 76ers front office isn't done. Multiple reports indicate the franchise is pursuing additional roster moves to fill out the supporting cast around the trio. A James-Brown-Embiid core needs reliable shooters, defensive depth, and a bench that can protect leads. Those are the pieces being assembled now.

What's Next

The next milestone is an official introductory press conference where LeBron James addresses the Philadelphia media and fan base for the first time as a 76er. After that, the roster-building phase continues through the end of the free agency period and into training camp in late September.

The Eastern Conference will be watching. So will everyone else. When LeBron James says "last decision," the league tends to pay attention — because his previous last decisions have a way of reshaping everything around them. This time, he's chosen the city of brotherly love, a Hall of Fame center, and a rising All-Star as his running mates. The 2026-27 NBA season cannot get here fast enough.
