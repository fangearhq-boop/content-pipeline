Stephen Curry Is 19 Days Away From Becoming a Historic Earner — and Everyone Already Knows It's a Done Deal

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | August 10, 2026

NBA Contracts / Golden State Warriors / Stephen Curry

August 29. That's the date. Nineteen days from now, the window opens for Stephen Curry to sign the contract extension the Golden State Warriors have been ready to offer for months. When it happens — and there is near-universal consensus that it will happen — Curry would become the first NBA player in history to earn more than $60 million in a single season.

The number alone is remarkable. But the context makes it historic.

How $60 Million Happens

The mechanics are straightforward. Curry is entering the final year of his existing Golden State contract, worth approximately $62.6 million in 2026-27. The Warriors are prepared to offer a two-year, $136.7 million maximum extension. Because the extension year money layers on top of the existing salary structure for the 2026-27 earnings calculation, per CBS Sports the combined figure would push Curry past the $60 million threshold — a number no NBA player has ever reached in a single season.

For context: LeBron James's entire four-year rookie contract in 2003 totaled $18.8 million. The NBA's salary cap has grown dramatically over two decades, but even in today's inflated salary environment, $60 million in one year is a genuinely unprecedented marker.

Why Golden State Is All In

After a 37-45 season that missed the playoffs, the Warriors spent the offseason rebuilding around Curry rather than around him. Draymond Green returned on a one-year, $27.7 million deal. Gary Payton II returned on a one-year, $3.9 million deal. The roster core is intact. Curry, 38, remains the engine.

ESPN's Anthony Slater reported mutual interest between Curry and the organization, with discussions expected once the August 29 window opens. The Warriors' failure to land LeBron James in free agency earlier this summer made one thing unmistakably clear: the franchise's path runs through Curry, and only through Curry, for the foreseeable future.

Without an extension, Curry would become an unrestricted free agent next summer — for the first time in his NBA career. The Warriors have no intention of letting that happen. Curry has no intention of testing the market. September, multiple outlets report, is when the paperwork gets done.

The Broader Picture

Curry signing at this level isn't just a Warriors story. It's a window into how dramatically the league's economics have evolved — and a reflection of what Curry represents as a commercial and competitive force. The man who reinvented how the game is played, who shattered three-point records and won four championships, will close his career as the sport's highest single-season earner. The arc of his legacy is nearly complete.

What's Next

The extension window opens August 29, 19 days from today. A September signing is widely expected. Once signed, Curry is locked in through the 2027-28 season. Golden State's roster-building focus then turns to the surrounding cast — what they add around Curry, Draymond, and the returning core to make another playoff push after last season's 37-45 finish. The extension isn't the end of the Warriors' story. It's the foundation everything else gets built on.
