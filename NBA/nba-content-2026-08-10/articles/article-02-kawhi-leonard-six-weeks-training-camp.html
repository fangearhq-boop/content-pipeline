Six Weeks to Training Camp — and the Kawhi Leonard Trade Is Still Completely Frozen

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | August 10, 2026

NBA Investigation / Los Angeles Clippers / Toronto Raptors / Kawhi Leonard

NBA training camps open in approximately six weeks. The most complicated trade in league history isn't any closer to a resolution than it was six weeks ago. The Kawhi Leonard saga — involving the Los Angeles Clippers, the Toronto Raptors, a bankrupt green bank, a second electronics company, and a salary cap investigation that could run into 2027 — is still frozen, still unresolved, and still without a hard deadline.

Two franchises are waiting. One player is in limbo. And the clock is ticking louder every day.

The Investigation Is Growing, Not Shrinking

When the NBA's probe into the Clippers and Kawhi Leonard first became public, it centered on a single deal: a $28 million endorsement arrangement between Leonard and Aspiration, a green bank that also held a $300 million, 23-year sponsorship deal with the Clippers franchise before going bankrupt. The allegation was that the Clippers used Aspiration to effectively compensate Leonard outside the salary cap system.

Now there's a second deal under review. CBS Sports reports that a Daktronics endorsement arrangement is also part of the expanding investigation. The probe isn't narrowing toward a conclusion. It's widening.

ESPN has reported explicitly that the investigation could extend into 2027. That's not a leak or speculation — it's sourced reporting about an investigation with no mandated timeline and a process that could include neutral arbitration if the parties can't agree on findings or proposed penalties.

The Trade and Why It Remains Frozen

In late June, the Clippers and Raptors agreed on a significant exchange. Kawhi Leonard goes to Toronto. In return, the Clippers receive Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, and two unprotected first-round picks in 2031 and 2033. Both franchises reportedly remain committed to completing the deal.

The problem is the risk structure. The NBA told Toronto that if the Raptors finalize the trade before the investigation concludes, they assume all liability for whatever discipline follows. That could mean completing the deal and then watching it get unwound, facing a suspended player, or absorbing draft pick penalties stemming from the Clippers' conduct. The Raptors have decided that is not a risk they're willing to accept — so the trade sits.

The NBPA adds another layer. The union has warned it "wouldn't hesitate" to demand neutral arbitration if the league pursues penalties without sufficient evidence. Any arbitration ruling would face a binding three-person appeals panel jointly appointed by both sides. That process, with no hard deadline, is precisely what could carry this into 2027.

The Human Element

Kawhi Leonard is owed $50.3 million in the 2026-27 season. He is 35 years old. He is technically a Clipper but has no team to prepare with, no coaching staff to report to, and no certainty about where — or whether — he plays this fall. He agreed to return to Toronto, the city where he won his only championship in 2019. He won't know if that's happening until an investigation with no timeline decides it's complete.

What's Next

There is no announced deadline and no confirmed next step in the NBA's investigation. The most likely outcome, per multiple league observers, remains a negotiated settlement — but that has been the stated expectation for months without materializing. Training camps open in approximately six weeks. Every day without resolution is another day both franchises operate with a planning gap at the center of their roster. Kawhi Leonard watches, and waits.
