10 Days to Belgrade: Wembanyama Returns to France and the International Stage He Missed

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | August 10, 2026

NBA Offseason / San Antonio Spurs / Victor Wembanyama / France FIBA

Ten days from now, in Belgrade, Serbia, Victor Wembanyama plays his first international basketball game since the 2024 Paris Olympics. For the San Antonio Spurs star — who said plainly this summer "I don't miss the NBA at the moment, but I do miss FIBA" — the two-year wait is almost over. France vs. Serbia, August 20. The return begins.

The August Schedule

Wembanyama's summer with France is a four-game stretch that builds from exhibitions into stakes:

  • August 20 — Exhibition vs. Serbia (Belgrade)
  • August 23 — Exhibition vs. Serbia (Orleans, France)
  • August 27 — 2027 FIBA World Cup qualifier vs. Slovenia (home)
  • August 30 — 2027 FIBA World Cup qualifier at Sweden (away)

The exhibitions against Serbia set the tone. The qualifiers are what matter. France enters this window leading Group L at 5-1 after the first qualifying round. With qualification for the 2027 World Cup as the objective, the August games carry real competitive weight — not just exhibition significance.

The French Roster and the New Coach

This is the first time since the Paris Olympics that France will have Victor Wembanyama, Rudy Gobert, and Evan Fournier on the same roster. That trio was the core of the gold medal run on French soil in 2024. Reassembling them — even for four games — represents one of the most formidable collections of talent in international basketball.

The head coach is new. Frederic Fauthoux replaced Vincent Collet after the Paris Olympics and has spent the past two years building toward this moment. August 20 in Belgrade will be the first time Fauthoux coaches Wembanyama at the senior national team level. For both coach and player, this window is a first impression that will define their working relationship heading into the 2027 World Cup cycle.

The Spurs' Investment

San Antonio didn't just let Wembanyama go. Spurs coaches and staff made the trip to Paris to support his preparation through the national team window. That organizational commitment to a 22-year-old player's development — including supporting him during an international break that most franchises would simply endure — reflects the unusual relationship between the Spurs and their franchise cornerstone.

Wembanyama signed his extension in the offseason, deliberately choosing the 25% maximum rather than the supermax 30%, reportedly to preserve the team's financial flexibility to build around him. The Spurs responded by showing up in Paris. The investment goes both ways.

What's Next

The first exhibition against Serbia is August 20 in Belgrade — 10 days away. The second is August 23 in Orleans. World Cup qualifying games follow August 27 (vs. Slovenia) and August 30 (at Sweden). If France performs well through the August window, they strengthen their Group L position and build momentum toward the 2027 World Cup. For Wembanyama personally, the games are a chance to reconnect with the style of play he says he genuinely misses — and to showcase his continued development to a global audience before the NBA season begins October 20.
