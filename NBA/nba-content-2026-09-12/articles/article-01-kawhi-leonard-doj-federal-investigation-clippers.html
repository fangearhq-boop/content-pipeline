The Kawhi Leonard Case Just Went Federal

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | September 12, 2026

The NBA concluded its investigation and the Raptors trade is proceeding — but now the Department of Justice has opened a criminal probe into the same Clippers-Leonard scoreboard kickback arrangement that cost Steve Ballmer $30 million and five first-round picks.

The Los Angeles Clippers entered September facing historic NBA discipline. They were stripped of five consecutive first-round draft picks, fined $30 million, and had both their governor and president of basketball operations suspended for off-court financial arrangements that the league's investigation concluded amounted to salary-cap circumvention. The arrangement at the center of it: an allegedly directed kickback from a scoreboard contract at Intuit Dome routed into a Kawhi Leonard endorsement deal.

The NBA's case is closed. A federal one is just beginning.

The United States Attorney's Office for the Eastern District of New York has opened a criminal investigation into the same conduct the league penalized. Prosecutors are examining whether the Clippers' arrangement with Leonard — which the league described as steering him toward sponsors for off-court income and directing a portion of a Daktronics arena deal into an endorsement contract — constitutes a federal crime. The Securities and Exchange Commission has separately contacted Daktronics about its role in the transactions.

What the NBA Found

The league's investigation concluded that the Clippers violated circumvention rules by arranging off-court income opportunities for Leonard that functioned as indirect salary. Kawhi himself was fined $700,000 for his participation in the arrangement. His uncle and longtime business partner Dennis Robertson was banned from engaging in any NBA business dealings for five years. Steve Ballmer received a one-year suspension from all league and team activities. Lawrence Frank, the team's president of basketball operations, was suspended for six months.

The Clippers were required to forfeit five first-round draft picks in 2029, 2030, 2031, 2032, and 2033 — five consecutive years of first-round selections, one of the most severe draft penalties in the league's history.

Critically for the Toronto Raptors, the NBA declined to void Kawhi Leonard's contract or impose a suspension on Leonard himself. That decision cleared the path for the June 30 trade — which had been agreed upon but placed under NBA review — to now proceed. Toronto training camp opens September 28 in Quebec City, and Leonard is expected to be present.

What the DOJ Is Investigating

Federal prosecutors are operating under a different legal framework than the NBA's internal enforcement system. The league governs its own rules; the DOJ enforces federal statutes. The conduct that drew a $30 million fine from the NBA could, if it involved wire transfers, false documentation, or fraudulent business records, cross into territory covered by federal wire fraud, mail fraud, or conspiracy statutes.

The investigation is in its earliest stages, and prosecutors have not indicated what specific charges, if any, are being considered. Criminal investigations at this stage often conclude without charges. But the opening of the probe means federal law enforcement has found sufficient basis to look closely at what happened between the Clippers organization and Leonard's business arrangements over the course of his tenure in Los Angeles.

A separate potential pressure point: reports indicate that a Clippers executive — identified in some outlets as Joe Sandberg — entered a guilty plea in a connected proceeding, which could provide prosecutors with additional cooperation and testimony to work from. That development has been described as potentially significant to the trajectory of the federal case.

What This Means for the Raptors

For Toronto, the immediate picture is clearer than it was two weeks ago. The NBA removing any threat of Leonard suspension or contract voiding gives the Raptors the certainty they need to bring him into camp. The trade terms — Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, unprotected first-round picks in 2031 and 2033, second-round selections in 2030 and 2033, and a pick swap in 2027 going to Los Angeles — are set.

The federal investigation is not a direct threat to the trade itself. Leonard was not suspended by the league, and the DOJ probe targets the Clippers organization and its executives far more directly than Leonard personally. His $700,000 fine and Robertson's five-year ban represent the totality of the personal consequences he has faced from the NBA's findings.

But the criminal probe creates a cloud that will follow Kawhi — and the story — into the season. Every press conference, every milestone in his return to Toronto, will carry the backdrop of an open federal investigation into his former team's conduct on his behalf.

What's Next

Training camp opens September 28. Leonard is expected to report to Quebec City and begin the work of integrating into a Raptors roster that has been reshaped around his return. The federal investigation has no set timeline; it will proceed on its own schedule independent of the NBA season.
