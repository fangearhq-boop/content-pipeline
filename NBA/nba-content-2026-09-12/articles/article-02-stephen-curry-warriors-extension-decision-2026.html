Stephen Curry Has a Decision to Make. The Warriors Have 16 Days to Get an Answer.

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | September 12, 2026

Curry is eligible for a two-year, $136.7 million extension with Golden State and has yet to sign it. Media day on September 28 is emerging as the natural checkpoint — not a hard deadline, but the moment when the question becomes impossible to avoid.

The Golden State Warriors handed Stephen Curry an unusual amount of control over his own future. They told him he could sign a one-year max extension. He could sign a two-year max extension at $136.7 million. He could let his contract run down and enter free agency next summer. Whatever he wants — essentially — the Warriors are willing to accommodate.

Since August 29, when Curry became officially eligible to sign the extension, he has not signed it.

Curry has been clear about the overriding principle: he wants to finish his career in Golden State. What he has not specified is the mechanism, the timeline, or what exactly "the right time" means when he says the decision will happen then. With media day 16 days away, the right time is drawing near whether or not Curry feels ready to trigger it.

What the Extension Would Mean

The two-year option at $136.7 million would lock Curry in through the 2028-29 season — his age-40 campaign. At 38, the four-time NBA champion and two-time MVP is operating in unprecedented territory as a basketball player. There is no reliable precedent for what an elite shooter does at 40, because elite shooters at 40 almost do not exist. Curry's combination of movement shooting, off-ball craft, and conditioning has sustained him through an era when most players his age have long since declined into complementary roles.

The one-year option would give the Warriors flexibility heading into the 2027-28 offseason and give Curry the ability to reassess at 39. The free agency option keeps every door theoretically open, though no credible reporting suggests Curry is entertaining a move.

From the Warriors' perspective, the two-year extension removes uncertainty and signals to the franchise's fan base and coaching staff that the Curry era continues in earnest. It also carries real financial risk: $136.7 million is a significant commitment to a player turning 38 before the season starts.

The Media Day Clock

Warriors media day falls on September 28, one day before the team heads to Hawaii for training camp. The day is designed around press access: reporters file into the facility, players rotate through interviews, and every question that has been building over the summer finds its moment.

The contract question will be in every interview. It will be asked of Curry's teammates. It will be the first thing reporters ask the Warriors' coaching staff and front office. If Curry arrives at media day without having signed, the day becomes largely a referendum on a decision he has not made. If he arrives having signed, the day becomes a celebration of continuity — and the next chapter of a franchise story that has already been told over four championships.

Veteran Warriors reporters Marcus Thompson and Tim Kawakami identified media day as the natural signpost for the extension situation, not because it creates a hard deadline, but because of what happens if he doesn't have an answer ready. "That does not necessarily mean that Curry and the Warriors will have the contract filed on media day," Thompson noted on the Warriors Plus/Minus podcast. But the gravity of that date is real.

What's Actually at Stake

The Warriors built their dynasty around Curry's gravity. Defenses have spent more than a decade accounting for him in ways that create opportunities for every teammate he has ever played with. The question of whether he is a one-year or two-year commitment affects how the team builds around him — which veterans they pursue, which cap space calculations matter, and how aggressively they spend in what might be Curry's final seasons.

A two-year extension is the most likely outcome given Curry's repeated statements about finishing in Golden State. But "likely" and "signed" are different things, and the Warriors are waiting in the same position as everyone else who follows the league: watching for confirmation of what Curry has strongly implied but not yet made official.

What's Next

September 28 is media day. September 29, the Warriors depart for Hawaii and training camp. If Curry signs before either date, the question is answered and Golden State enters camp with clarity.
