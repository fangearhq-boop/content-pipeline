Tyler Herro Went to High School 20 Minutes From Fiserv Forum. Now He's Building Something There.

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | September 12, 2026

The Miami Heat's trade sent Tyler Herro to the one city where his journey has a pre-existing beginning — Milwaukee. The kid from Greenfield, Wisconsin is now the face of the Bucks' rebuild, entering training camp with a new coach, a roster full of young talent, and an explicit chip on his shoulder.

There is a version of the Giannis Antetokounmpo trade that the Milwaukee Bucks organization has been telling itself since the deal was agreed in June. It goes like this: the era that built this franchise into a championship contender is over, the pieces acquired in return represent a legitimate foundation, and the person positioned to be the face of what comes next is one of their own.

Literally, in this case.

Tyler Herro grew up in Greenfield, Wisconsin, attended Whitnall High School, and became an NBA starter about 20 minutes from Fiserv Forum. He went to Kentucky, got drafted by Miami, became a Sixth Man of the Year and one of the better scorers in the Eastern Conference — and then was sent back to the one city where his story has a pre-existing beginning. The Bucks did not set out to make this trade a homecoming narrative. But that is what it is.

The Roster and the Reset

The Milwaukee Bucks' haul in the Giannis trade was substantive. Along with Herro, the team received Kel'el Ware, a 20-year-old center with defensive upside and developing offensive tools; Jaime Jaquez Jr., a versatile two-way wing; Kasparas Jakucionis, an Estonian prospect with playmaking skill; and Nate Ament, the No. 13 overall pick in the 2026 draft. Three first-round picks, a second-round selection, and a pick swap round out the package.

On paper, that is a collection of young talent that could coalesce into a competitive team within two or three seasons. The immediate ceiling is uncertain. The foundation is real.

Adding Myles Turner — an established veteran center with shooting range — gives the roster a bridge between the youth movement and playoff experience. Head coach Taylor Jenkins, newly hired from Memphis, brings a history of player development and a system that maximizes athleticism and pace. Jenkins turned Ja Morant and Desmond Bane into All-Stars in Memphis. His ability to develop young guards and wings is exactly what the Bucks need around Herro in Year 1.

Herro's Response to the Underdog Frame

When asked about the rebuild at a recent youth camp, Herro did not pretend the Bucks were being installed as East favorites. He leaned into the underdog framing and gave it back with interest.

"Obviously there's going to be people who probably don't think we'll win many games," he said, "but that's the opposite of what we're thinking. We want to win as many games as possible and really set a culture from day one."

Herro has also said he will play with a chip on his shoulder — a posture that fits someone who spent several seasons in Miami as one of the best players who regularly got left off the All-Star ballot. He has been underestimated before. The difference now is that he is the No. 1 option on his team, not the No. 3 or No. 4.

The First Camp Without Giannis

Milwaukee's training camp this September will be the first since 2013 without Giannis Antetokounmpo on the roster. Twelve years of a franchise anchor, two MVP trophies, one championship, and an entire generation of Bucks fans growing up in the Giannis era. The first camp without him will feel different in ways that are hard to quantify — and the Bucks will be asking Herro and Jenkins to make it feel like a beginning rather than an end.

The homecoming narrative helps. The chip on Herro's shoulder helps. The young roster, if it develops, helps most of all.

What's Next

Training camp opens September 29. The early preseason will reveal what Taylor Jenkins' system looks like in Milwaukee, how Herro adjusts to top-option usage, and whether the young players around him are as ready as the front office believes.
