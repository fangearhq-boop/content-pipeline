Pistons and Jalen Duren Are $10 Million Apart. October 1 Is 19 Days Away.

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | September 12, 2026

Detroit has offered five years and $190 million. Duren wants $200 million. The gap has not moved. If no deal is done before the qualifying-offer deadline, the Pistons get one more year of their All-NBA center at $9.6 million — and then open bidding to the entire league next summer.

The staring contest between the Detroit Pistons and Jalen Duren has a date attached to it now: October 1. That is when the team's qualifying offer must be filed if no extension has been signed. After that, Duren plays this season on a $9.6 million deal — a number that bears no relationship to his market value — and enters unrestricted free agency in the summer of 2027.

As of September 12, both sides remain apart. The Pistons have formally tabled a five-year, $190 million extension. Duren's number is five years and $200 million. The $10 million gap over the life of the deal — roughly $2 million per year — has not budged despite weeks of reported back-and-forth between the two sides.

NBA executives watching from outside described the situation to ESPN as a staring contest, with neither side ready to flinch.

What Duren Brings to the Table

Jalen Duren's case for $200 million is straightforward. He is 22 years old. He was a third-team All-NBA selection last season. The Detroit Pistons finished as the Eastern Conference's No. 1 seed with Duren anchoring their interior defense. At 6-foot-11 with elite athleticism, shot-blocking, and rebounding, he represents the kind of center that teams spend years and entire rebuilds trying to acquire.

He also has one trait that the Pistons cannot manufacture: he is already on their roster. The cost of replacing Duren if he leaves — in draft capital, in years of development, in pure market competition for a true center at his age and skill level — would almost certainly exceed the $10 million separating the two sides.

The Pistons' Calculation

Detroit's reluctance to close the gap suggests something beyond the $10 million figure itself. League sources indicate that the issues go deeper than the financial difference — that there may be elements of term structure, incentive clauses, or expectations about future max extensions that are complicating negotiations beyond the headline number.

The Pistons are building. Their No. 1 seed last season was a significant organizational milestone, and the franchise's front office is managing a roster that needs to remain flexible as it continues to add pieces. Locking in a $200 million commitment on a 22-year-old — however talented — is a calculation that carries risk, and Detroit's front office appears to be weighing that risk carefully.

The QO Scenario

If no deal is done by October 1, Duren signs the $9.6 million qualifying offer. The Pistons retain his rights for one year. At the end of the 2026-27 season, Duren becomes a restricted free agent — meaning the Pistons can match any offer sheet another team presents.

The restricted free agency path sounds safer for Detroit than it is. An All-NBA center entering his free agent summer will generate significant offers from teams with cap space. The Pistons would be placed in a position of matching offers under public pressure, potentially at a value higher than $200 million if the market moves during the 2026-27 season. And there is always the possibility — however small — that a restricted free agent player finds a way to force through a move regardless of matching rights.

Duren's camp is reportedly aware of this leverage. The holdout may be designed precisely to force the Pistons to reconsider whether $10 million is worth the risk of a messy 2027 free agency process.

What's Next

Detroit training camp opens September 29, two days before the October 1 qualifying-offer deadline. That tight window means the Pistons and Duren have roughly 17 days to find the missing $10 million — or watch camp begin with their cornerstone center on a nine-figure gap from the deal they want.
