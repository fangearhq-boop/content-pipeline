Portland Built the Most Electric Backcourt in the West. Now It Has to Stay Healthy.

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | September 12, 2026

Damian Lillard returns from a torn Achilles that cost him the entire 2025-26 season. Ja Morant arrives from Memphis after a June trade. Together, they give Portland one of the most watchable pairings in the Western Conference — and one of its most fragile.

The trade that brought Ja Morant to Portland on June 29 was not the kind of move that rebuilding teams make. It was the kind of move that teams make when they think they are one piece from doing something real. The Trail Blazers already had Scoot Henderson, Jrue Holiday, and Shaedon Sharpe in the backcourt rotation. They had a returning Damian Lillard, who missed all of 2025-26 with a torn Achilles but is expected back in camp.

Adding Morant meant the Blazers now have two elite lead guards on the roster at the same time — a pairing that does not often work and, when it does, tends to produce seasons worth watching.

What Each Player Brings

Damian Lillard at 36 is returning from one of the most serious injuries a basketball player can face. A torn Achilles typically costs at least one season and often results in diminished explosiveness afterward, even in cases of full anatomical recovery. Lillard sat out all of 2025-26. Early reports from Portland ahead of training camp have been described as positive, with nothing indicating complications from the rehabilitation.

The pre-injury version of Lillard was one of the best late-game scorers in NBA history. His pull-up three-pointer off screens, his ability to create space against elite defenders, and his durability over a decade of Portland basketball defined the Blazers' identity for an entire era. Whether that version returns — or something close to it — is the central health question for the team's season.

Morant at 27 is younger and theoretically more resilient, though he has missed significant time in past seasons due to injury and suspension. His 2026-27 campaign with Portland represents a fresh chapter — his first with a team that acquired him specifically for what he does. The Memphis-trained burst, the pull-up floater, the ability to manipulate a defense in open transition — those skills translate anywhere. The question is how they translate alongside another ball-dominant guard.

Can They Actually Play Together?

The skeptical read on the Lillard-Morant pairing is the obvious one: two guards who need the ball, who create off the dribble, who have historically played best as the unquestioned No. 1 option on their teams. Basketball does not always accommodate two lead creators gracefully. The team that plays through its best scorer tends to win; the team that manages two scoring personalities tends to negotiate.

The optimistic read is more nuanced. Lillard's shooting range means he can operate effectively as a spot-up shooter when Morant is pushing in transition — a role Lillard played in limited contexts in Milwaukee and could embrace fully in Portland when fresh off the Achilles rehab. Morant's game in the mid-range and at the rim creates a complementary angle to Lillard's three-point gravity. The two-man game potential between them is genuinely elite.

Portland also has Scoot Henderson, who profiles as a backup point guard with starting upside, and Jrue Holiday, who has spent his career as one of the best defensive guards in the league regardless of offensive role. The supporting cast around the Lillard-Morant pairing provides enough versatility that coach Chauncey Billups has real lineup options.

The Western Conference Context

The Western Conference heading into the 2026-27 season features Oklahoma City as the established standard-bearer, San Antonio challenging from below, and several middle-tier teams fighting for playoff positioning. Portland occupies an interesting slot: good enough to win in the West if the backcourt delivers, inconsistent enough that a serious injury to either guard derails the season entirely.

Both Lillard and Morant have logged enough missed time over their careers that health projections for both of them carry larger error bars than most players their age. The Blazers are betting that 2026-27 is the year both stay on the court, and that the version of the team that emerges when they do is something worth building around for the next several years.

What's Next

Portland training camp opens September 29. The early preseason will give the first real look at how Lillard moves after the Achilles recovery, whether Morant fits naturally in the Blazers' system, and what lineup arrangements Chauncey Billups prefers when both guards are available.
