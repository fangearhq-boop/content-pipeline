### STORY 1: Kawhi Leonard — DOJ Federal Criminal Investigation

**Post 1 — Long-Form**
Window: 9:00 AM ET

The NBA's investigation is over. A federal one is just beginning.

Last week, the league concluded its probe into the Los Angeles Clippers' financial arrangements with Kawhi Leonard and imposed historic penalties: a $30 million fine, five consecutive first-round draft picks stripped (2029-2033), Clippers governor Steve Ballmer suspended for a full year, and president of basketball operations Lawrence Frank suspended for six months. Leonard himself was fined $700,000. His uncle Dennis Robertson was banned from NBA business dealings for five years.

The NBA also declined to void Leonard's contract or suspend him — clearing the path for the long-delayed June 30 trade to the Toronto Raptors to now proceed. Training camp opens September 28 in Quebec City.

But now the Department of Justice has entered the picture. Federal prosecutors in the Eastern District of New York have opened a criminal investigation into the same scoreboard kickback arrangement the league penalized. The alleged conduct: the Clippers routed money from an Intuit Dome scoreboard contract with Daktronics into a Leonard endorsement deal, steering compensation to him through a back channel that circumvented the salary cap. The SEC has also contacted Daktronics about the arrangement.

Criminal investigations at this stage often go nowhere. But federal exposure is a different category of legal risk entirely — one that the Clippers, Ballmer, and their executives are now navigating alongside the NBA penalties already handed down.

For Kawhi and the Raptors, the immediate picture is clearer: he is going to Toronto. The legal cloud follows him there regardless.

### STORY 2: Stephen Curry — Extension Decision

**Post 2 — Long-Form**
Window: 11:00 AM ET

Stephen Curry has a decision to make — and 16 days before it becomes the only question in every Warriors press conference.

Since August 29, Curry has been eligible to sign a two-year, $136.7 million extension with Golden State. He has not signed it. The Warriors have given him unusual flexibility: he can take the one-year max, the two-year max, or wait for free agency next summer. They have essentially told their franchise player to choose his own terms.

Curry has said one thing clearly: he wants to finish his career in Golden State. What he has not provided is a timeline for making it official.

Warriors media day falls on September 28 — 16 days from now. That is when every reporter in the room will have a contract question ready. When teammates will start absorbing the uncertainty. When the story becomes unavoidable regardless of where the extension talks stand.

Veteran Warriors reporters have described media day as the natural signpost for the situation. Not a hard deadline — Curry has said the right time will present itself — but the date when "at the right time" will need a closer translation.

At 38, Curry remains one of the most effective shooters the game has ever produced. His decision is not just personal; it defines the Warriors' competitive window for the next two seasons. Two years of committed Curry is a championship-contending team. One year of Curry heading toward free agency is a different calculation entirely.

The right time is getting closer.

### STORY 3: Jalen Duren / Pistons Stalemate

**Post 3 — Long-Form**
Window: 11:00 AM ET

The Detroit Pistons and Jalen Duren are $10 million apart. October 1 is 19 days away.

The Pistons have formally tabled a five-year, $190 million extension to their All-NBA center. Duren wants five years and $200 million. The gap — $2 million per year — has not moved. NBA executives watching from outside described the situation to ESPN as a staring contest.

The stakes are clear. Duren is 22 years old. He was a third-team All-NBA selection last season. The Pistons finished as the Eastern Conference's No. 1 seed with him anchoring the interior. He is not a player you let walk.

If no deal is done by October 1, Duren signs the $9.6 million qualifying offer. He plays one year under that deal, then enters restricted free agency in 2027 — which means the Pistons can match any offer sheet from other teams. On paper, that preserves their rights. In practice, it means a year of contract drama, competing offer sheets, and bidding wars that the $10 million gap today would have avoided entirely.

Duren's camp appears to understand the leverage the QO path creates. Entering restricted free agency as an All-NBA center in 2027 could mean an offer sheet at a value higher than $200 million if the cap rises. The Pistons are calculating whether holding firm saves them money or costs them more in the long run.

Training camp opens September 29. The deadline arrives two days later. Close the gap.

### STORY 4: Tyler Herro / Bucks Rebuild

**Post 4 — Long-Form**
Window: 4:00 PM ET

Tyler Herro is going home. And he is not arriving quietly.

Herro grew up in Greenfield, Wisconsin — about 20 minutes from Fiserv Forum. He attended Whitnall High School, became a first-round pick, developed into one of the better scorers off the bench in the Eastern Conference with the Heat, and won a Sixth Man of the Year award in Miami. Then the Giannis trade sent him to the one city that already knew his name before his career began.

He has leaned into the underdog framing that comes with leading a rebuilding team. At a recent youth camp, he said: "Obviously there's going to be people who probably don't think we'll win many games, but that's the opposite of what we're thinking. We want to win as many games as possible and really set a culture from day one."

New coach Taylor Jenkins arrives from Memphis with a track record for developing young offensive talent — Ja Morant, Desmond Bane, and others were products of his system. The Bucks' rebuild haul — Kel'el Ware, Jaime Jaquez Jr., Kasparas Jakucionis, Myles Turner, No. 13 pick Nate Ament, and several future firsts — is one of the more intriguing collections of young players in the league.

This is the first Milwaukee training camp since 2013 without Giannis Antetokounmpo on the roster. That carries weight. It also means the franchise needs a new story, and a kid from Greenfield coming home to lead the rebuild is a genuinely compelling one.

### STORY 5: Portland Trail Blazers — Lillard-Morant Backcourt

**Post 5 — Long-Form**
Window: 1:00 PM ET

The Portland Trail Blazers just built one of the most exciting backcourts in the Western Conference. Now it has to stay healthy.

On June 29, Portland acquired Ja Morant from the Memphis Grizzlies. In doing so, they created a pairing alongside Damian Lillard that few expected to exist: two All-Star lead guards, both returning from significant missed time, on the same roster heading into the 2026-27 season.

Lillard missed all of 2025-26 with a torn Achilles. At 36, the recovery is meaningful. Achilles injuries at that age have ended careers; they have also been overcome by elite athletes who do the work. Early camp reports out of Portland are described as positive — no complications reported from the rehabilitation.

Morant is 27 and carries his own injury history. His 2026-27 season with Portland represents a fresh start — a team that acquired him specifically for what he does at the top of the key, in transition, and finishing around the rim.

The questions are real. Two ball-dominant creators on the same team requires negotiation — who runs the offense, who closes, how do they defend together against elite competition. Coach Chauncey Billups has Scoot Henderson, Jrue Holiday, and Shaedon Sharpe to work with as depth and versatility, which gives him real lineup options.

The optimistic scenario: Portland becomes one of the most entertaining teams in the West, both players healthy, both performing near their peaks. The pessimistic scenario: one goes down in October and the experiment never gets to prove itself.

Training camp opens September 29. We will start getting answers then.
