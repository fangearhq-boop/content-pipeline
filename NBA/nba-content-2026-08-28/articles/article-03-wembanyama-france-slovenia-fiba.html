Captain Wemby Delivers: France Beats Slovenia as FIBA Qualifying Push Continues

By Marcus Cole | August 28, 2026

Victor Wembanyama said earlier this summer that he does not miss the NBA right now — that he misses FIBA basketball. On Thursday night, he showed exactly what he meant. The San Antonio Spurs center posted 22 points and 9 rebounds as France defeated Slovenia in a 2027 FIBA World Cup qualifying contest, lifting the national team's record to 6-1 in Group L.

Next up for France: Sweden on August 30.

A Different Kind of Motivation

Wembanyama was named captain of the French national team for this qualifying window, a designation that carries real weight for a 22-year-old still establishing himself at the highest levels of the sport. He has spoken openly about how much representing France means to him, and his play has matched the rhetoric.

After a standout NBA season in which he averaged 25.0 points, 11.5 rebounds, and a league-best 3.1 blocks per game for San Antonio — earning him Defensive Player of the Year honors and All-NBA recognition — Wembanyama chose to spend his offseason on the international stage rather than resting. Thursday's performance was a reminder of why.

France's Position in Group L

At 6-1, France sits in a commanding position within the European qualifying group. The qualifying structure for the 2027 FIBA World Cup requires consistent results across multiple windows, and France's combination of Wembanyama and a deep roster of European professional players gives them the kind of ceiling that makes them a serious candidate to qualify in strong fashion.

The Slovenia game was not expected to be a walkover — but France made it look relatively comfortable. Sweden on August 30 will serve as another measuring stick before this qualifying window closes.

The Biggest Player in the World

At 7-foot-4, Wembanyama is a physical anomaly — a player whose combination of size, ball-handling, shot-creation, and rim protection has no true historical parallel at this stage of a career. Watching him operate in international competition, where spacing and pace differ from the NBA, is its own kind of revelation.

France has not always had a player capable of single-handedly altering the outcome of a FIBA game. With Wembanyama, they do. And he appears to be embracing that responsibility fully.

What's Next

France faces Sweden on August 30 to close out this qualifying window. A win would further cement their position near the top of Group L. After that, Wembanyama will return to San Antonio for the start of training camp ahead of the 2026-27 NBA season.

For now, though, he is exactly where he wants to be — in a France uniform, playing FIBA basketball, leading his country.
