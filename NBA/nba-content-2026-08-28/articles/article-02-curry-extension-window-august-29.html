The $136.7 Million Question: Stephen Curry's Extension Window Opens Tomorrow

By Jake Torres | August 28, 2026

For weeks, it has been the subplot humming underneath every other Warriors storyline. Now the moment has arrived. Stephen Curry becomes eligible to sign a two-year, $136.7 million contract extension with the Golden State Warriors on August 29 — and the decision he makes will define the franchise's direction for the next several years.

As of Thursday, no deal had been finalized. But the signals from both sides are encouraging — though not without nuance.

The Warriors' Perspective

Warriors general manager Mike Dunleavy Jr. has been unequivocal in his public stance. According to ESPN's Anthony Slater, Dunleavy has said he is "pretty confident" Curry will finish his career in Golden State, adding that Curry "really, really wants to win and he wants to finish his career as a Warrior."

The maximum two-year extension is valued at $136.7 million. For a 38-year-old — even one who remains one of the most dangerous shooters in basketball history — that is a significant commitment. The Warriors are apparently prepared to make it.

The Case for Waiting

Not everyone thinks Curry should rush to sign. ESPN's Brian Windhorst has publicly encouraged the superstar to hold off, arguing that the financial structure of his deal could have major implications for the Warriors' roster-building ability.

The logic: if Curry signs a shorter-term deal — a one-year structure around $62.6 million has reportedly been discussed — or takes less per year, the Warriors could potentially create up to $100 million in cap space. That would open the door to signing a second star-level player, something Golden State has not been able to do in recent seasons. The Warriors finished 37-45 last year, well outside the playoff picture.

Curry at a slightly reduced salary, paired with a second significant free agent, could represent the most competitive path for a team that still has championship aspirations around its franchise cornerstone.

What Curry Has to Weigh

At 38, Curry's window to compete for another championship is finite but not closed. He remains an elite offensive weapon — his shooting gravity alone changes what defenses can do against any roster he is on. The question is whether he prioritizes personal financial security at the maximum level, or whether he is willing to bet on his own value as a lure to attract another star to San Francisco.

The shorter deal also carries risk. If Curry's play declines or he faces injury, he would not have the security of the longer extension. The max deal, by contrast, guarantees him financial protection regardless of on-court outcomes.

What's Next

The extension window opens Friday, August 29. Both Curry and the Warriors are expected to be in contact immediately. The basketball world will be watching closely — not just for news of a deal, but for any indication of what structure Curry might ultimately prefer.

If he signs the max, Golden State's future is largely set. If he waits, or opts for the shorter structure, the Warriors' offseason just got a great deal more interesting. One way or another, the answer is coming.
