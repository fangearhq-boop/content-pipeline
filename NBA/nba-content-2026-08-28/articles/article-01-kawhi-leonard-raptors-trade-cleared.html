NBA Clears Clippers of Cap Circumvention: Kawhi Leonard's Return to Toronto Is Finally Happening

By Damon Pierce | August 28, 2026

The longest offseason soap opera in recent NBA memory has a resolution. The league has cleared the Los Angeles Clippers of cap circumvention in its investigation into Kawhi Leonard's financial arrangements with the Aspiration company, removing the final obstacle to one of the summer's most consequential trades.

With the ruling in hand, Leonard's blockbuster move to the Toronto Raptors — the franchise where he won the 2019 NBA championship and Finals MVP — can now be finalized ahead of training camp.

The Deal That Hung in Limbo

The trade was agreed upon in principle at the end of June. In exchange for Leonard, the Raptors are sending Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, and draft compensation to Los Angeles. For the Clippers, it is a significant haul of assets; for Toronto, it is a reunion with the player who delivered the franchise's only championship.

But almost immediately after the deal was struck, the NBA put it on hold. The investigation centered on Aspiration, a now-bankrupt company, and whether the Clippers had used a sponsorship arrangement with the firm to compensate Leonard in a way that circumvented the salary cap. Both the Clippers and the NBA players' association prepared responses to any potential ruling.

The wait stretched through July, then August. Leonard's future — and Toronto's offseason planning — was frozen. Training camp for the Raptors is set to open September 28, and there was real concern about whether the roster situation would be resolved in time for meaningful preparation.

The NBA's Decision and What It Means

With the NBA clearing the Clippers of cap circumvention, the trade is now set to proceed. Leonard will return to a city that has never stopped adoring him. He was Finals MVP in 2019, the author of one of the most famous shots in NBA history — a four-bounce corner buzzer-beater in Game 7 against Philadelphia — and a player whose departure left Raptors fans with complicated feelings for years.

Now 35, Leonard is not the same explosive two-way force he was at his peak. But he remains an elite defensive presence and a player who has demonstrated, repeatedly, that he elevates his game in meaningful moments. For a Raptors organization looking to compete and recapture something of its championship identity, he represents exactly that kind of veteran anchor.

The Clippers' New Direction

For the Clippers, the ruling and the ability to complete the trade finally allows them to pivot. Ingram gives them a dynamic, versatile scorer in his prime. Gradey Dick brings youth and shooting ability. The draft compensation adds future flexibility.

The Clippers' chapter with Leonard — marked by his limited availability due to injuries and now the legal and regulatory complexity surrounding his departure — is closed. They can begin building toward their next identity.

What's Next

With the NBA's ruling delivered, the expectation is that the Leonard trade will be officially completed in the coming days. Raptors training camp opens September 28 in Toronto, where Leonard will reunite with fans who celebrated a title with him seven years ago.

For the Raptors, the front office can now finalize preparations around a roster that includes a proven champion. For Leonard, it is a chance at something fitting: finishing a complicated chapter of his career in the city that embraced him most completely.

The investigation is over. Kawhi is coming home.
