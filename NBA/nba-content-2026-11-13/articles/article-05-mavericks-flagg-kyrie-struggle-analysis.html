Dallas Reality Check: Why the Mavericks' Blowout Loss to Minnesota Should Raise Alarms

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | November 13, 2026

Cooper Flagg went from 43 points to 12. Kyrie Irving managed 10. Minnesota's 39-point blowout win over Dallas exposed real structural concerns with this Mavericks roster.

Two days after Cooper Flagg announced himself to the NBA with a stunning 43-point performance against Indiana, the Dallas Mavericks received a harsh dose of reality.

Minnesota's Timberwolves won 133-94 — a 39-point beatdown that snapped Dallas' three-game win streak and dropped the Mavericks to 7-5. Flagg was held to 12 points. Kyrie Irving managed 10. Zaccharie Risacher's 22 were the only saving grace on a night that exposed everything this Dallas team still needs to figure out.

The Two-Star Problem

The Mavericks have constructed their identity around Flagg and Irving, two players capable of explosive individual nights. When one or both is rolling, Dallas is very hard to beat. When a defense like Minnesota's takes away those options, the entire offense deflates.

Against the Timberwolves, that's exactly what happened. LaMelo Ball and Minnesota's defense were surgical in their approach, crowding Flagg's driving lanes and forcing Irving into contested mid-range attempts early. Neither star was able to establish rhythm, and without a reliable third scorer to ease the pressure, the Mavericks offense stalled completely.

The Bench Equation

Perhaps more alarming than Flagg's 12-point night was the near-total silence from Dallas' bench unit. When star players are shut down, it falls on reserves to provide a spark — and against Minnesota, that spark never came.

Minnesota, by contrast, got 24 points from Bones Hyland off the bench, plus double-figure contributions from Ayo Dosunmu and Jaden McDaniels. The depth disparity between these two rosters was visible from the first quarter.

7-5 is Not a Crisis — Yet

To be clear, a 7-5 record is not a cause for panic. Dallas still has time to course-correct, and both Flagg and Irving are talented enough to put together the kind of runs that make regular-season blips irrelevant by April.

But the pattern is worth watching. The Mavericks' losses are not close games where one more bucket makes the difference — they are blowouts that reveal systemic weaknesses. Finding a consistent third scoring option and strengthening bench production must be priorities heading into December.

What's Next

Dallas has the talent to be a playoff team, and potentially a deep one, if Flagg continues his upward trajectory and Irving stays healthy. But Thursday's loss to Minnesota underscored the gap between this Mavericks roster and the Western Conference's elite.

The next few weeks will reveal whether Dallas can patch these holes in-house, or whether the front office needs to look at the trade market for a third scoring option that can relieve pressure on their two stars. For now, the blowout loss to Minnesota is a wake-up call that the Mavericks cannot afford to ignore.
