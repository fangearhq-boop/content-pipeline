Cade Cunningham's Historic 52-Point Night Lifts Pistons Over Wizards in Double OT

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | November 13, 2026

Cunningham became the first NBA player since 1977-78 to record 50-plus points, 10-plus assists and zero turnovers in a single game, powering Detroit to a 144-140 double-overtime victory.

When Cade Cunningham stepped onto the floor at Capital One Arena on Thursday night, the Detroit Pistons needed a hero. When he walked off, he had authored one of the most historically complete individual performances the NBA has seen in nearly five decades.

Cunningham poured in a career-high 52 points to go along with 11 assists and zero turnovers, lifting the Pistons past the Washington Wizards 144-140 in double overtime. It was a night that will live in Detroit basketball history alongside the greatest individual performances the franchise has ever witnessed.

Making History in Real Time

The numbers themselves are staggering, but the context makes them even more remarkable. According to OptaSTATS, Cunningham became the first NBA player to record 50-plus points, 10-plus assists and zero turnovers in a single game since the league began tracking turnovers in the 1977-78 season — a span of nearly 50 years.

He now joins George Yardley, who accomplished the feat twice in the 1957-58 season, and Blake Griffin, who did it in 2018-19, as the only Detroit Pistons players ever to log a 50-point double-double. The list is as short as it is impressive.

Cunningham's previous career high was 46 points, set in an overtime win over these same Wizards in November 2025. This time, he shattered that mark with room to spare, shooting an efficient 16-for-25 from the field.

A Thriller That Went the Distance

The game itself was a classic. Washington scored the first seven points of the first overtime period to build a 128-121 lead, seemingly on the verge of pulling off the upset. But Cunningham refused to let the Pistons fold. He orchestrated a rally, and AJ Dybantsa forced a second overtime with a pull-up jumper that silenced the Capital One Arena crowd.

In the second overtime, Cunningham delivered the decisive blow — a cold-blooded fadeaway that put Detroit ahead by four with just 25 seconds remaining. This time, there was no Washington comeback. The Pistons held on for a 144-140 victory.

Thirty of Cunningham's 52 points came between the fourth quarter and the overtime periods, underscoring his ability to elevate when the game was on the line.

Washington Had Its Own Stars

It would be a disservice to minimize what Washington brought to the floor. Trae Young was magnificent, finishing with 37 points and 14 assists. Anthony Davis was a force inside, tallying 30 points and 15 rebounds. On another night, those performances would have been more than enough to win.

But on this Thursday evening in Washington, D.C., Cade Cunningham was simply operating on a different plane.

What's Next

The Pistons' win reinforces their status as a legitimate Eastern Conference contender. With Cunningham playing at this level — elite scoring, elite playmaking, and the discipline to protect the basketball — Detroit has a chance to make serious noise in the postseason.

Washington, meanwhile, will need to regroup after squandering their first-overtime lead. The Wizards have the talent to compete on any given night, as Young and Davis proved, but converting close games remains a work in progress.

For now, the basketball world can marvel at what Cade Cunningham produced in double overtime on a November night. Historic performances don't come around often. This was one of them.
