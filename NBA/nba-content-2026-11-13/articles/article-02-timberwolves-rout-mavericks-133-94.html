Timberwolves Dismantle Mavericks 133-94, Extend Win Streak to Five

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | November 13, 2026

LaMelo Ball posted 22 points and a season-high nine assists as Minnesota turned in its most lopsided win of the season, crushing Dallas by 39 points and improving to 9-3.

If the Western Conference wasn't taking the Minnesota Timberwolves seriously before Thursday night, they have no excuse now.

Minnesota routed the Dallas Mavericks 133-94 at Target Center — their most dominant performance of the season — to extend their winning streak to five games and push their record to 9-3. The 39-point margin of victory was emphatic, the kind of statement win that changes the conversation about a team's ceiling.

LaMelo Ball in Command

LaMelo Ball was the engine of Minnesota's offensive machine, finishing with 22 points and a season-high nine assists while draining six three-pointers. His ability to push the pace, find cutters, and create space from the arc gave Dallas no answers all night.

The supporting cast made Dallas pay at every turn. Bones Hyland delivered 24 points and eight assists off the bench — a matchup nightmare that Minnesota's depth continues to produce. Ayo Dosunmu added 18 points and Jaden McDaniels chipped in 16, giving the Wolves four players in double figures before halftime.

Dallas Gets Dominated

The numbers on the Dallas side tell a troubling story. Cooper Flagg, who had electrified the NBA with a 43-point, 10-rebound performance against Indiana just two days earlier, was held to 12 points by Minnesota's smothering defense. Kyrie Irving managed just 10. Zaccharie Risacher's 22 points were the lone bright spot in an otherwise forgettable night for the Mavericks.

Dallas had entered Thursday with a three-game win streak and sat at 7-5. Their exit from Target Center left them at 7-5 with far more questions than answers about their defensive identity and second-unit production.

Minnesota's West Title Credentials

At 9-3, the Timberwolves are legitimate Western Conference title contenders. Their five-game winning streak has included multiple statement wins, and this blowout of a Dallas team that features two elite stars shows the depth and defensive cohesion that makes Minnesota so dangerous.

LaMelo Ball's development as a lead playmaker, combined with Hyland's bench scoring and a roster that can defend across all five positions, gives Minnesota a template that is very difficult to game-plan against.

The Wolves are not sneaking up on anyone anymore. They are a force.

What's Next

Minnesota will look to extend their streak as the schedule continues, while Dallas faces an urgent need to find solutions before their inconsistency costs them playoff seeding. The Mavericks have too much talent to spiral, but nights like Thursday reveal real structural gaps in their roster.

For the Timberwolves, the message is clear: they are playing championship-caliber basketball right now, and the rest of the West is on notice.
