AJ Dybantsa, Darryn Peterson, and the 2026 NBA Draft Class Are Ready to Make Their Mark

By Marcus Cole | October 11, 2026 | Hoop Heroes

Summer League is over. Preseason is underway. And the 2026 NBA Draft class — widely considered one of the deepest and most talented in years — is transitioning from controlled workouts to real NBA competition. Nine days from now, these rookies will be fighting for minutes on Opening Night. Between now and then, every preseason game matters.

Here's a look at the players who have already made their mark — and what to expect once the real games begin.

AJ Dybantsa — Washington Wizards (No. 1 Overall Pick)

The No. 1 pick in the 2026 draft announced himself to the basketball world in his very first Summer League game. Dybantsa posted 27 points, 7 rebounds, 2 assists, 2 steals, and a block in 26 minutes as the Washington Wizards beat the Utah Jazz 92–88. He shot 7-of-18 from the field — an efficiency figure that scouts noted, though his midrange creation and defensive engagement were immediately NBA-caliber.

After the game, Dybantsa briefly left the floor with leg soreness before returning and saying he was fine. It was a minor scare for a franchise that had waited years for a prospect of his caliber. The Wizards are not expected to contend immediately, but Dybantsa's development will be one of the most-watched stories in the league over the next two seasons.

Darryn Peterson — Utah Jazz (No. 2 Overall Pick)

In that same game where Dybantsa shined, Peterson countered with 24 points of his own — and then followed it up with 28 points in another summer league contest, including a go-ahead three-pointer in overtime. Peterson is a pure scorer, a player who can create his own shot from multiple levels, and Utah's front office clearly believes he's a building block for years to come.

The Dybantsa-Peterson rivalry that began in summer league — the top two picks going at it in Las Vegas — is already being called one of the best ongoing storylines in the young careers of both players. Expect that narrative to develop further once the regular season begins.

Caleb Wilson — Chicago Bulls

If Dybantsa and Peterson are the headliners, Wilson is the player who surprised everyone most. His Summer League debut line — 35 points and seven three-pointers — was one of the most electric performances in Las Vegas in years. Chicago has been searching for a perimeter scorer who can change a game in the fourth quarter. Wilson looks like he may have arrived at the right time.

The Bulls open the regular season on October 20 with one of the youngest rosters in the East. Wilson's ability to immediately contribute as a secondary scorer alongside veteran leadership will be closely watched as Chicago tries to turn a corner.

Cameron Boozer and the Rest of the Class

Cameron Boozer has already been logging preseason minutes, giving fans a look at what the Duke product can do against NBA competition. His preseason action this month continues the momentum he showed in Summer League. Boozer's combination of size, skill, and basketball IQ makes him one of the more NBA-ready prospects in the class.

Other names to watch: Morez Johnson Jr. of the Dallas Mavericks, a summer league standout who posted elite defensive numbers (75% on two-point shots, 6 blocks and 5 steals in just two games). And Tre Johnson, the Washington Wizards' contributor who also impressed in summer league play.

What's Next

The 2026 NBA Draft class has the look of a generation-defining group — the kind that will be remembered the way the 2003 class (LeBron, Carmelo, D-Wade, Bosh) is still discussed today. But Summer League is a long way from a regular season. Preseason is one step closer. The real test starts October 20.

Which rookie makes the biggest immediate impact? Which one needs another year to develop? The answers start this week. The basketball world is watching — and these young players know it.
