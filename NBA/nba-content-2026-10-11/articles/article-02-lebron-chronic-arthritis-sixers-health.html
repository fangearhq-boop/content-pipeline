LeBron James' Chronic Arthritis Is the Most Important Health Story in the NBA Right Now

By Marcus Cole | October 11, 2026 | Hoop Heroes

Before the Philadelphia 76ers take the floor for Opening Night on October 20, before LeBron James walks into Madison Square Garden in a Sixers uniform on the night the Knicks raise their first championship banner since 1973, one question is hanging over everything: will LeBron be healthy enough to play?

ESPN's Dave McMenamin reported that James, 41, is managing chronic arthritis in his left foot as he prepares for his 24th NBA season — his first in Philadelphia. It's not a new injury, and it's not a tear or a fracture. But chronic arthritis is, by definition, a condition that does not go away. It requires management. And at 41, the margin for error is considerably smaller than it was at 31.

The Timeline So Far

LeBron sat out the Sixers' October 5 preseason opener against the Knicks. The team cited rest. He played on October 8 when Philadelphia traveled to Brooklyn, but the Nets won 114–108 in a game that highlighted some of the depth concerns Philadelphia now faces.

Center Ariel Hukporti was injured during Philadelphia's preseason opener and has since been ruled out for the entire season with an Achilles tear — a devastating blow to a team already counting on LeBron to anchor large stretches. Additional centers Adem Bona, Dominick Barlow, and Tacko Fall are also unavailable, leaving the Sixers remarkably thin in the frontcourt behind Joel Embiid.

The injury situation around LeBron didn't cause the arthritis report, but it adds context. The Sixers need their 41-year-old franchise cornerstone healthy, productive, and available — and right now, none of that is guaranteed.

What the Sixers Built Their Window On

Philadelphia's offseason was audacious. They acquired LeBron James — still the most recognizable player in the world — alongside Jaylen Brown, who arrived from Boston in a blockbuster trade that sent Paul George and four draft picks to the Celtics. Pairing Brown with Tyrese Maxey and Joel Embiid alongside LeBron created one of the most star-studded rosters in recent league history.

The calculation was simple: give LeBron a supporting cast capable of winning a championship in 2026–27, because that window may be the last time his presence creates that kind of ceiling. It's a legitimate theory. But the theory only holds if LeBron can stay on the floor.

Last season with the Los Angeles Lakers (in what many assumed was his final year), LeBron managed minutes carefully. A 41-year-old managing chronic foot arthritis through an 82-game regular season and into the playoffs is a medical and athletic challenge with no real modern precedent. He has always defied expectations. Whether he can defy this one is the biggest unknown in the NBA.

Opening Night at MSG

Nine days from now, the Sixers walk into Madison Square Garden. The Knicks hang their banner. The crowd will be electric. And LeBron James, 41, with arthritis in his left foot and three weeks into his first season in Philadelphia, will be asked to compete at the highest level in that environment.

If he's healthy, this is one of the greatest storylines the NBA has produced in years. If he's not — if he's limited or absent — the Sixers' ceiling drops significantly, and the window that looked so wide this summer starts to narrow in a hurry.

Philadelphia is monitoring the situation carefully. So should everyone watching.

What's Next

The Sixers have several more preseason games between now and October 20. Every appearance LeBron makes — and every one he sits out — will be scrutinized through the lens of this health report. The medical staff in Philadelphia is doing everything it can to get him to the regular season in peak condition.

Whether that's possible won't be fully known until the season begins. But the most important health story in the NBA right now isn't in a training room in Houston, Boston, or Oklahoma City. It's in Philadelphia, where the most decorated player in league history is trying to add one more chapter — if his body lets him.
