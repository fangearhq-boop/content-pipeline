NBA China Games Game 2: Can Cooper Flagg and the Mavericks Bounce Back Against Sengun's Rockets?

By Jake Torres | October 11, 2026 | Hoop Heroes

Alperen Sengun walked off the floor in Macao on Friday night with a triple-double in his back pocket, 22 minutes of work on the clock, and a 135–117 blowout secured. The message from Houston's star center was unmistakable: the Rockets are ready, and so is he.

Now comes Game 2 of the 2026 NBA China Games, scheduled for today at The Venetian Arena in Macao (approximately 6 a.m. ET). The Dallas Mavericks need a response. Cooper Flagg, the 18-year-old No. 1 pick who led Dallas with 19 points in Game 1, needs a performance that signals he's not just a highlight waiting to happen — but a player ready to build something real in a Mavericks uniform.

What Happened in Game 1

There was no mystery in Game 1. Sengun took over from the opening tip, doing everything at once. His final line — 16 points, 10 rebounds, and 10 assists — came in just 22 minutes. Fred VanVleet added 17 points in a balanced Houston effort that never really gave the Mavericks a chance to find their footing.

For Dallas, Flagg was the story worth watching. The Maine native, fresh off his No. 1 draft selection, played with poise beyond his years. His 19-point effort showed flashes of the two-way brilliance that made him the consensus top pick, but it wasn't enough to keep the Rockets at bay on a night when Houston was simply clicking.

The margin — 18 points — was significant. In preseason, blowouts can happen for any number of reasons. But this one felt like it carried a message: the Rockets are a well-oiled machine entering Year 2 of their contender phase, and young Dallas needs to grow up fast.

Sengun's Case for Elite Status

Sengun has quietly become one of the NBA's most complete big men over the past two seasons. At 23, he has developed into a legitimate facilitator from the high post, a reliable midrange scorer, and a rebounding presence who doesn't need to be a rim-runner to impact a game. A triple-double in 22 minutes in any context is remarkable. In a preseason China Games setting, it says even more about his conditioning and preparation entering the season.

Houston's ability to win without heavy reliance on any single star — VanVleet, Sengun, and multiple contributors all factored in Game 1 — speaks to the depth and trust head coach Ime Udoka has built. The Rockets are not just back; they are genuinely dangerous.

Flagg's Moment to Respond

This is precisely the kind of game Cooper Flagg needs. Not to put up 30, not to win by 20. Just to show the follow-through — to come back the next day, adjust, compete, and chip away at a veteran team. Rookies who respond to adversity in the preseason often carry that mentality into the regular season.

Flagg enters today's rematch as one of the most scrutinized 18-year-olds in the country. His Summer League numbers were promising, but the China Games are something different: actual NBA players, real game speed, and a global audience watching. His 19-point performance in defeat was a solid entry point. What he does in Game 2 will reveal a great deal about his maturity and competitive instinct.

What's Next

After the China Games conclude, both teams return stateside to continue their preseason schedules leading up to Opening Night on October 20. For the Mavericks, that opener is on the road against the San Antonio Spurs. For the Rockets, they host the Memphis Grizzlies.

The China Games have always served as an early window into the league's competitive balance. This year, the early reading is clear: Houston is a legitimate contender, and Dallas is still finding out just how good it can be. Today's rematch won't settle that question definitively — but it will add another chapter to what promises to be a compelling story between two franchises heading in very different directions.

Game 2 tips off at approximately 6 a.m. ET. Don't sleep on it.
