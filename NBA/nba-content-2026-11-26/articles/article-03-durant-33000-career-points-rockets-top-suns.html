Durant Becomes Fifth Player in NBA History to Reach 33,000 Career Points as Rockets Top Suns

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | November 26, 2026

Some nights, the numbers tell the story better than the highlights. Wednesday night in Houston, Kevin Durant added 20 points to his career total and crossed the 33,000-point threshold — becoming just the fifth player in NBA history to reach that mark. The Houston Rockets defeated the Phoenix Suns 116-104, and while Devin Booker led Phoenix with 25 in a losing effort, the night belonged to KD and to the quiet accumulation of a historic career.

Four players had been in the 33,000-point club before Durant: LeBron James, Kareem Abdul-Jabbar, Karl Malone, and Kobe Bryant. Durant is now among them. The list is not long. It is not supposed to be. Scoring 33,000 NBA points requires not just elite talent but extraordinary longevity, consistency, and the kind of professional commitment that spans nearly two decades of competition at the highest level.

Durant has all of it, and Wednesday night confirmed it officially.

A Career That Needed No More Validation

The debate about Kevin Durant's legacy has never really been about whether he belongs among the all-time greats. Two NBA championships, two Finals MVP awards, an MVP trophy, and 14 All-Star selections had long since settled that question. What the milestone does is put a number on something that basketball fans have watched unfold over nearly two decades: one of the most gifted pure scorers the game has ever produced, adding to his total night after night with the kind of precision and variety that few defenders have ever been able to solve.

Durant's scoring arsenal — the step-back three, the mid-range pull-up, the ability to draw fouls in traffic, the post moves that few perimeter players have ever mastered — has evolved from a singular weapon into a complete offensive system in a single body. He has averaged more than 27 points per game for his career, and even now, in the later stages of his career with the Rockets, he is still the player opponents need a game plan specifically to contain.

Wednesday's 20-point effort against Phoenix was not a career night. It was a typical professional performance from a player who has been delivering typical professional performances at an extraordinary level since he entered the league in 2007.

Suns Fall to 116-104 as Durant Steals Headlines

Devin Booker was excellent for Phoenix with 25 points, but the Suns couldn't overcome Durant's milestone night or the collective Rockets effort. Houston controlled the game throughout, and Phoenix's inability to find a consistent second scorer behind Booker proved costly. The loss continues a difficult stretch for a Suns team that has underperformed relative to the talent on its roster.

For the Rockets, the win represents another step forward for a franchise that has built something worth watching around Durant and their younger core. Houston is not just a backdrop for Durant's milestone season — they are a genuinely competitive team, and games like Wednesday demonstrate why.

What's Next

Kevin Durant will keep scoring. That much is certain. The chase for Kobe Bryant's 33,643 career points — the next marker on the all-time list — now becomes the conversation for basketball analysts and statisticians. At Durant's current pace, it is a question of when, not if.

For the Rockets, the season continues with a team now firmly established as a Western Conference factor. For Durant personally, the milestone will be mentioned in every arena he visits until the next one arrives. He has earned that.

LeBron James. Kareem Abdul-Jabbar. Karl Malone. Kobe Bryant. Kevin Durant. The fifth entry on a list that might not see a sixth for a very long time.
