Alvarado's Season-High 21 Fuels Knicks' Sixth Straight Win, 121-102 Over Cavaliers

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | November 26, 2026

The New York Knicks have found something special in this six-game winning streak, and Wednesday night's 121-102 demolition of the Cleveland Cavaliers offered the clearest proof yet: this team wins even when its best player has his worst night.

Jalen Brunson — the engine of everything the Knicks do — shot 6-for-22 from the field, finishing with 19 points on a night he could not find his rhythm. On most teams, that is a loss. At Madison Square Garden on Wednesday, it barely registered. Jose Alvarado erupted for a career-high-equaling season-high 21 points off the bench, Mikal Bridges added a season-best 19, and the Knicks closed the game with a staggering 39-17 run in the fourth quarter to put Cleveland away for good.

New York now stands at 12-6 on the season and has pulled themselves back from the brink in the NBA Cup standings, improving to 1-2 in East Group B after a 0-2 start that had Cup hopes looking bleak.

The Bench Carries the Night

Alvarado's performance was the story the Knicks needed. For much of this season, the conversation around New York has centered on Brunson and Karl-Anthony Towns — the star duo that the team has been built around. But Alvarado's 21 points on Wednesday expanded that conversation. When Brunson went cold, Alvarado kept the offense humming, attacking closeouts and hitting timely shots when Cleveland's defense tried to adjust.

Bridges' season-best 19 added another dimension. His ability to function as both a scorer and defensive anchor gives the Knicks a flexibility that few teams can match. On a night when Brunson struggled, two role players combined for 40 points — that is not an accident. That is the product of months of system building under head coach Tom Thibodeau.

Cleveland did everything right for three quarters. Donovan Mitchell poured in 30 points, and James Harden added 28 — 58 combined points from two of the game's elite offensive players. At various points the Cavaliers led by as many as 11, and it looked as though the Knicks might finally have their win streak snapped.

The Fourth Quarter Belonged to New York

Then came the fourth. The Knicks outscored Cleveland 39-17 in the final period, turning a game that had been competitive into a statement. The fourth-quarter surge was the kind of run that defines not just individual games but entire seasons — a moment that tells a team, and a city, who they are when it counts most.

Mitchell and Harden fought hard but could not stop the bleeding. Cleveland falls to 9-10 on the season, a disappointing record for a team with that level of star power. When two players combine for 58 points and you lose by 19, the structural questions about the roster will only get louder in the days ahead.

For the Knicks, those questions don't apply right now. They have six straight wins, they're alive in the NBA Cup, and they proved Wednesday night that this team is deeper than perhaps anyone outside the organization believed.

What's Next

The Knicks will host the Miami Heat on Black Friday in a critical NBA Cup East Group B game on Prime Video. The Heat come in having just lost to the 76ers on Wednesday, meaning this game carries enormous implications for which teams advance to the knockout round. A Knicks win would be a massive step toward Cup contention; a Heat win could flip the group standings entirely.

Cleveland will look to regroup, but the schedule offers no easy path back. The Cavaliers' combination of talent and disappointing results is a conversation that will intensify the longer the losses pile up. Mitchell and Harden are capable of pulling things together — the question is whether the system around them is calibrated correctly.

For now, the story belongs to New York. Six straight. Brunson cold. Alvarado hot. Knicks dominant. That is the November 2026 version of Madison Square Garden basketball.
