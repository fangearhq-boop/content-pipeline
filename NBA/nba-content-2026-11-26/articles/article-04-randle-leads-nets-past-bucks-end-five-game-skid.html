Randle's 28 Points Snap Brooklyn's Five-Game Skid as Nets Outlast Bucks 104-97

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | November 26, 2026

It has been an up-and-down start for the Brooklyn Nets, but on Wednesday night, they found a reason to feel good about the season's direction. Julius Randle delivered 28 points and the Nets knocked off the Milwaukee Bucks 104-97, snapping a five-game losing streak that had raised questions about whether this new-look Brooklyn squad could translate its offseason ambitions into actual wins.

Wednesday's answer was yes — at least for one night. Randle was aggressive from the opening tip, attacking the paint, drawing fouls, and hitting the mid-range jumpers that have defined his game throughout his career. The Bucks threw different defenders at him and none of it worked. When Brooklyn needed a basket in a tight fourth quarter, Randle found a way to provide it.

The 104-97 final might not look like a blowout, but the Nets controlled the game's key stretches in a way that suggested genuine competitive capability. Milwaukee, by contrast, struggled to generate consistent offense and couldn't put together the defensive stops that would have changed the game's trajectory.

Randle Making His Case in Brooklyn

When the Nets acquired Randle from the Minnesota Timberwolves in a four-team trade before the draft, there were questions. Could a player known for heavy usage on bad-to-mediocre teams thrive in Brooklyn's system? Could coach Jordi Fernández build a winning culture around a veteran known for occasional inconsistency?

Wednesday's performance offered an encouraging answer. Randle's 28 points were his most efficient scoring effort in a Nets uniform, and his ability to function as the primary option while also facilitating for teammates showed a versatility that makes Brooklyn genuinely difficult to game-plan against. His pairing alongside Michael Porter Jr. in the starting lineup creates a frontcourt that opponents will need to respect on both ends of the floor.

The five-game skid was concerning, but context matters: several of those losses came against playoff-caliber opponents in close games. Wednesday showed that when Brooklyn is at its best, with Randle asserting himself and the supporting cast contributing, wins are possible against anyone in the Eastern Conference.

Bucks' Difficult Season Continues

For Milwaukee, the loss extends a troubling stretch that has become a defining theme of this early season. The Bucks have the talent to compete at the top of the Eastern Conference, but inconsistent performance on both ends of the floor has prevented them from stringing together the wins a contender needs to establish its identity.

Losing to a Brooklyn team that had just dropped five in a row is precisely the kind of result that keeps opponents from taking you seriously. Milwaukee will need to find answers sooner rather than later if they want to remain relevant in the East's playoff picture as the season progresses toward December.

What's Next

Brooklyn's win snaps their skid, but the real test is what comes next. Can the Nets build on this performance, or will it stand as an isolated bright spot in an otherwise inconsistent stretch? The coming weeks will tell us whether Randle and this Nets roster have discovered something sustainable.

Milwaukee will be eager to put Wednesday behind them quickly. The Bucks have too much talent to continue struggling at this level, and the front office will be watching carefully to see whether the roster needs adjustments or whether time and reps will solve the problems that have plagued this start.

For now, Brooklyn celebrates an overdue win, and Julius Randle has given the Nets — and their fans — a genuine reason to believe the offseason's ambitions might still be realized.
