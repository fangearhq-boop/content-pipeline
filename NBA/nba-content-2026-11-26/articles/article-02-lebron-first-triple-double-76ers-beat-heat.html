LeBron's First Triple-Double as a Sixer Powers 76ers Past Heat 111-104 in NBA Cup

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | November 26, 2026

In a career defined by milestone moments, LeBron James added another one on Wednesday night. Recording his first triple-double as a Philadelphia 76er — 25 points, 12 assists, and 10 rebounds — he helped the team to an 111-104 NBA Cup victory over the Miami Heat that carries significant implications for the East Group D standings heading into the weekend.

Tyrese Maxey was brilliant alongside him, dropping 30 points in a performance that underscored exactly why the pairing of these two was considered among the most dangerous in the Eastern Conference. When Maxey went to work in the mid-range and LeBron orchestrated the offense with the vision and precision that has defined his two-plus decades in the league, the Heat had no satisfactory answer.

Giannis Antetokounmpo gave everything he had for Miami. His 32 points and 14 rebounds were the kind of performance that wins most games — but on this particular Wednesday, in Philadelphia, with LeBron in his current form and Maxey locked in, 32-and-14 from Giannis wasn't enough.

Year 24 and Still Making History

LeBron James is 39 years old. He is in his 22nd NBA season. He is now playing alongside Tyrese Maxey in Philadelphia, a chapter of his career that few would have predicted even three years ago. And yet here he is, posting triple-doubles in NBA Cup games in November, refusing to slow down, refusing to let the conversation shift to anything other than winning.

The triple-double was his first in a 76ers uniform, a small but meaningful marker in a career filled with them. What made it significant wasn't the statline itself but the context: a close game, meaningful stakes, and LeBron finding ways to be the best player on the floor even as his role evolves. He is no longer the player who carries a team on pure athleticism. He is the architect now — reading the floor, delivering passes that arrive exactly where teammates need them, and scoring efficiently when the opportunity presents itself.

Maxey benefits enormously from that architecture. The two have developed a chemistry that the 76ers' front office clearly envisioned when they made the roster move to bring LeBron to Philadelphia, and games like Wednesday are the payoff.

Heat's Cup Situation Grows Urgent

For Miami, the loss stings in ways that extend beyond the final score. With group play ending on Black Friday, the Heat's Cup fate is now hanging by a thread. Giannis was everything Miami needed him to be — but the team needs more than one dominant performance to win playoff-intensity games. The supporting cast has been inconsistent, and Wednesday's loss in Philadelphia is a warning sign heading into what will be a defining game on Friday.

The Heat will travel to Madison Square Garden on Black Friday to face the Knicks in what amounts to a Cup-or-go-home game. New York has won six straight. Miami has just lost to the 76ers with Giannis putting up 32. The stakes could not be higher.

What's Next

The 76ers head into Black Friday watching the bracket shape up around them. If they've done enough in Cup group play, the knockout round awaits. Philadelphia's combination of LeBron's orchestration and Maxey's scoring punch makes them a genuinely dangerous Cup contender regardless of seeding.

For the Heat, everything rides on Friday. A win over the Knicks at MSG keeps Cup hopes alive. A loss closes the chapter entirely. Giannis will need another monster performance, and Miami will need to execute in ways that Wednesday's loss suggested they couldn't. The NBA Cup, as always, has a way of separating the teams that want it from the teams that need it.

LeBron James, at 39, in his 22nd season, still delivering triple-doubles in meaningful NBA Cup games. The year is 2026, and he is still making the argument. Take note.
