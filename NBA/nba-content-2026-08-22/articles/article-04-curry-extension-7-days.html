Seven Days Until Steph Curry's Extension Window Opens — and Warriors Aren't Worried

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes

August 22, 2026

The clock is ticking.

In seven days — August 29 — Stephen Curry becomes eligible to sign a contract extension with the Golden State Warriors. The maximum available: two years, $136.7 million. But emerging reports suggest the structure may look different than many expected, with a shorter-term deal increasingly under discussion.

Warriors general manager Mike Dunleavy Jr. is not losing sleep over it.

"He really, really wants to win and he wants to finish his career as a Warrior," Dunleavy said in a recent interview. "I don't think that changes tomorrow, August 29th, start of the season, middle of the season, end of next season."

The Structure Under Discussion

The two-year maximum extension worth $136.7 million remains the most lucrative option available to Curry. But ESPN's Brian Windhorst floated a "bold, risky" shorter-term approach in recent weeks, and reporting from flashscore and SI suggests a one-year, $62.6 million structure may be the direction the two sides are leaning.

The appeal of the shorter deal is flexibility. At 38 years old, Curry is coming off a season where he shot 42% from three — still elite, still one of the best shooters in basketball history — but the Warriors finished 37-45 and missed the playoffs entirely. A one-year deal keeps both sides nimble: Curry evaluates the roster, and Golden State maintains optionality for what may be their final offseason of meaningful assets in the Curry era.

Without any extension, Curry would become an unrestricted free agent in 2027 — the first time in his career he would be able to choose his next team without restriction. That scenario is the one both sides appear motivated to avoid.

The Warriors' Summer

Golden State's offseason was a long series of near-misses. The Warriors pursued Giannis Antetokounmpo, LeBron James, Jaylen Brown, and Anthony Davis — four All-NBA-caliber players who all chose different destinations. Giannis went to Miami. LeBron went to Philadelphia. Brown stayed committed to Boston before eventually landing with the 76ers. Davis chose another path.

The result: Golden State enters 2026-27 with largely the same roster that went 37-45 last season. That context is not lost on Curry or Dunleavy. The extension — whatever the structure — is a statement as much as a contract.

"If Steph wants to play for years and years," one source close to the situation told reporters, "he can. But the question is whether the Warriors are built to make that worthwhile."

The Legacy Weight

Curry has spent his entire NBA career in Golden State. Four championships. Three Finals MVP awards. Two Most Valuable Player awards. The man who changed how basketball is played — more than any player since Magic Johnson or Michael Jordan — has never worn another NBA jersey.

That matters to him. Dunleavy knows it. The Warriors' entire positioning in these extension talks is built on that loyalty, and on the belief that Curry's desire to finish in gold and blue outweighs any other consideration.

August 29 will tell us whether that's right.

What's Next

The extension window opens August 29. If a deal is done that day or shortly after, expect it to be announced quickly — this is one of the most anticipated contracts in recent Warriors history. If negotiations extend past the start of training camp (late September), the dynamic changes and the story gets more interesting.

For now, seven days. And the Warriors say they're not worried. 🍿
