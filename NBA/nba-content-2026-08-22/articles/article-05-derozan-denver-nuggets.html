DeMar DeRozan Signs With Denver Nuggets, Adding Midrange Star Next to Jokic and Murray

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes

August 22, 2026

Denver quietly keeps building.

Six-time NBA All-Star DeMar DeRozan has agreed to a one-year, $3.88 million veteran minimum deal with the Denver Nuggets, per ESPN and NBA.com, joining Nikola Jokic and Jamal Murray as a third playmaker on a Nuggets team that is quietly reassembling itself for another championship run.

DeRozan, 37, was released by the Sacramento Kings in July after two seasons. He averaged 18.4 points over 77 games last season, shot efficiently in the midrange, and remained one of the most effective isolation scorers in the league despite his age. He chose Denver over reported interest from the Miami Heat, Washington Wizards, and New Orleans Pelicans.

He chose the Nuggets because he wanted to compete.

The Fit Next to Jokic

DeRozan's skill set is almost perfectly calibrated to work alongside the two-time MVP. Nikola Jokic commands defensive attention the moment he touches the ball — his passing draws defenders out of position, opens cutting lanes, and creates opportunities that most centers never see. For a player like DeRozan, who operates best as a cutter and midrange scorer off the pick-and-roll, playing next to Jokic is an ideal setup.

Jokic has repeatedly made above-average players better by creating the exact look DeRozan most wants. The pick-and-roll with DeRozan as the ball-handler, with Jokic rolling to the elbow for a pass or a midrange shot, is one of the more dangerous two-man games the Western Conference will face this season.

Denver's coaching staff will use that combination. Expect it in late-game situations, where DeRozan has historically been one of the NBA's most reliable shot-creators in crunch time.

The Nuggets' Quiet Offseason

Denver doesn't make headlines in July and August. They don't chase free agent superstars or engage in the kind of blockbuster trade talk that fills social media timelines. They identify the pieces that fit their system and add them deliberately.

DeRozan fits that philosophy. He's not a rotation change or a stylistic overhaul — he's a proven veteran who knows how to operate in the system Jokic requires without disrupting it. He's comfortable in a third or fourth offensive role. He defends at a serviceable level. He's been in big moments and won't be rattled by a Game 7.

The Nuggets won the championship in 2023. They've been building back toward that level ever since. DeRozan is the latest piece in that project.

DeRozan's Career Stage

DeRozan entered the NBA in 2009 with the Toronto Raptors, where he became the franchise cornerstone before being traded to San Antonio in 2018. After the Spurs, he went to Chicago, then Sacramento, and now Denver. Six All-Star selections. More than 26,000 career points across 17 seasons. A player who has evolved from explosive dunker to midrange craftsman to veteran orchestrator.

At 37, DeRozan turns 38 in August. He is not going to score 25 points a game in Denver. But he doesn't need to. The Nuggets need exactly what he provides: a reliable scorer who demands attention, makes the right reads, and frees up Jokic to operate at his highest level.

What's Next

DeRozan joins Denver for training camp, which opens in late September. The Nuggets open the 2026-27 regular season on October 20, with their first major showcase on Christmas Day: Nuggets vs. Warriors at 10:30 PM ET — Jokic, Murray, and now DeRozan against Steph Curry in a Western Conference clash that should be one of the night's best games.

Denver isn't the trendy pick heading into next season. They rarely are. But with Jokic, Murray, and now a proven 18-point scorer added on a minimum deal, they have the pieces for a deep run. Don't count them out.
