LeBron James Confirms 24th Season at Fanatics Fest: 'This Was My Last Decision'

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes

August 22, 2026

LeBron James came close to walking away.

Sitting across from Tyrese Haliburton at Fanatics Fest NYC on Saturday, hosting a live version of his podcast with the Indiana Pacers All-Star, LeBron James confirmed what many had suspected: his decision to sign with the Philadelphia 76ers was the hardest of his career — and retirement was a genuine option before he made it.

"This was my last decision," LeBron said at the event. He will return for his 24th NBA season, suiting up for the 76ers alongside Joel Embiid, Maxey, and Jaylen Brown in what he framed as a final chapter rather than just another chapter.

Why Philadelphia

LeBron signed a two-year, $8 million deal with the 76ers in July — a below-market contract for a player whose market value, even at 41, would have commanded more. The move prompted Philadelphia's title odds to move from 20-1 to 10-1 overnight.

The 76ers' pitch was straightforward: a team already built with Joel Embiid returning from injury, a backcourt led by Tyrese Maxey, and Jaylen Brown arriving from Boston. It's a legitimate contender built around players who have been to the Finals before — or who, in Embiid's case, have carried a franchise through years of playoff disappointment to the brink of something real.

Cleveland, Miami, Golden State, and Minnesota were all believed to have made serious pitches for LeBron. He chose Philadelphia because, according to those close to the process, he wanted one final run at a title on his own terms — not as a savior, not as the franchise player, but as the final piece of something that already had a foundation.

A Season 24 Nobody Saw Coming

When LeBron finished last season with the Los Angeles Lakers — his eighth season in L.A. after stints in Cleveland and Miami — retirement conversations were louder than they'd been at any previous point in his career. He was 40. The Lakers missed the playoffs. His son Bronny had entered the league with the team, and there was a narrative available: walk away on your own terms, at the right time, in the city where you'd spent nearly a decade.

He didn't take it.

Instead, he chose a new city, a new team, and a new challenge. "I couldn't leave the game with something still left to prove," he told Haliburton at Fanatics Fest.

The record books already belong to LeBron in ways that may never be touched. He's the NBA's all-time leading scorer. He's played more playoff minutes than any player in league history. He has four championship rings from three different franchises. Season 24 isn't about statistics.

It's about one more ring.

The 76ers' Window

Philadelphia opens the 2026-27 season on October 20 at Madison Square Garden against the New York Knicks — a marquee Opening Night matchup that will draw one of the largest NBA audiences in years. LeBron in New York, on the road, opening a new chapter.

The 76ers' schedule also includes a Christmas Day game: 76ers at Lakers on December 25 at 5 PM ET — LeBron's return to Los Angeles in one of the NBA's highest-profile annual showcases.

If Embiid stays healthy, if the Maxey-Brown-LeBron perimeter trio clicks, if the Eastern Conference opens up the way it might — Philadelphia has a legitimate path to the Finals for the first time in decades.

What's Next

LeBron and the 76ers head into training camp in late September. Preseason begins October 3. The regular season opens October 20.

For a player who has spent 24 years refusing to go quietly, one thing seems clear: LeBron James is going to make this season worth watching, whatever it takes. He said so himself at Fanatics Fest — and at this point, there's no reason not to believe him.
