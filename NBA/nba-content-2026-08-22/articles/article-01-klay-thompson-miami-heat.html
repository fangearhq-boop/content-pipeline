Klay Thompson Signs With Miami Heat, Joins Giannis and Bam in South Beach

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes

August 22, 2026

Klay Thompson is going to Miami.

After the Dallas Mavericks bought out the final year of his contract — worth $17.5 million for the 2026-27 season — Thompson has agreed to a two-year, nearly $13 million deal with the Miami Heat, completing one of the most intriguing late-offseason moves in recent memory. The Heat confirmed the signing after Thompson cleared waivers.

The move unites Thompson with Giannis Antetokounmpo and Bam Adebayo in South Beach, giving Miami a Big Three built on contrasting but complementary skill sets: Giannis as the bulldozing primary scorer, Bam as the defensive anchor and playmaking center, and Klay as the off-ball shooter who has been terrorizing defenses — even without the ball in his hands — since 2011.

What Klay Brings to the Heat

Miami needed shooting. After losing Tyler Herro and Norman Powell this offseason, the Heat's perimeter depth thinned considerably. Thompson steps in as the veteran floor-spacer who makes life easier for both stars without requiring the ball.

Last season in Dallas was a rough one for Thompson by the numbers: 11.7 points per game on 39.3% shooting across 69 games — career worsts across the board. But Miami's front office made a calculated bet that a change of scenery, a defined supporting role, and playoff-caliber competition can reignite the Thompson that won four championships in Golden State.

"He's a champion who knows how to win," one league source told reporters ahead of the signing. "You don't put four rings on your hand by accident."

Thompson is 36 years old and entering the final chapter of his career. The Heat offered him something Dallas couldn't: a genuine contender, a clear role, and a shot at championship No. 5.

Miami's New Big Three

The Giannis era in Miami is still brand new — the two-time MVP arrived via trade from Milwaukee this past offseason — and Heat management has been methodically assembling the supporting cast around him. Adding Thompson completes the shooting corps in ways that matter tactically.

With Giannis drawing defensive attention in the paint and Bam operating as a playmaking center, Thompson will have plenty of opportunities to attack off the catch, spot up from the corners, and play the kind of movement basketball the Heat have built their culture around. His familiarity with Steve Kerr's motion offense in Golden State translates naturally to a team that prizes off-ball movement.

The Heat also retain the championship culture Thompson thrives in. Miami has been to four NBA Finals since 2011. Klay has won four rings. The fit — beyond the numbers — is hard to argue with.

The Dallas Chapter Closes

Thompson joined Dallas last season after departing Golden State, where he spent 13 seasons and won four titles alongside Steph Curry. The Mavericks move was supposed to be a renaissance but never quite found its footing. The career-worst shooting percentages, the defensive slide, and a Dallas team that went in a different direction all pointed toward an exit.

The Mavericks chose to buy out his contract rather than let it play out. Thompson cleared waivers — meaning no team claimed his contract at the buyout cost — and immediately attracted Heat interest that had been building throughout the summer.

What's Next

Thompson's contract includes a player option in the second year, giving him flexibility depending on how the Giannis-era Heat develop. Training camp opens September 29, with the preseason beginning October 3 and the regular season tipping off October 20.

The Heat open the 2026-27 season with Christmas Day circled: Heat vs. Celtics on December 25, Giannis's first nationally-televised showcase in a Miami uniform against his old Eastern Conference rivals. Thompson will be on the court for it.

South Beach just got a lot more dangerous.
