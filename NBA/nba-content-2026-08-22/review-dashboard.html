Kawhi Leonard Case Could Create League-Wide Salary-Cap Loophole — Tier 1
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Window: 11:00 AM ET The Kawhi Leonard investigation just became everyone's problem. We've been following this story for weeks — the Clippers under investigation for allegedly funneling money to Leonard through inflated sponsorship deals (a $28 million arrangement with Aspiration and a separate deal with Daktronics, the scoreboard maker at Intuit Dome). Now Forbes is raising a flag that few people are talking about: Whatever the NBA rules here could create a league-wide precedent. If the league says teams can introduce players to sponsors — and those sponsors pay players above market rates — where's the line? Could every team in the NBA start using "sponsor introductions" as a quiet workaround to the salary cap? The Clippers call it common practice. The NBA says the deals were significantly above fair market value. The NBAPA and Steve Ballmer are both preparing legal challenges if the ruling restricts these introductions. The Board of Governors meets in mid-September. Resolution is expected around September 22. And when a decision finally comes, it won't just affect the Clippers or the Kawhi Leonard trade. It might change how every front office does business. Stay tuned. This one matters. 👀
