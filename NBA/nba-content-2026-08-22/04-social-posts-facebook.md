### STORY 2: Kawhi Leonard Case — Salary Cap Loophole

**Long-Form Post**
Window: 11:00 AM ET

The Kawhi Leonard investigation just became everyone's problem.

We've been following this story for weeks — the Clippers under investigation for allegedly funneling money to Leonard through inflated sponsorship deals (a $28 million arrangement with Aspiration and a separate deal with Daktronics, the scoreboard maker at Intuit Dome).

Now Forbes is raising a flag that few people are talking about:

Whatever the NBA rules here could create a league-wide precedent.

If the league says teams can introduce players to sponsors — and those sponsors pay players above market rates — where's the line? Could every team in the NBA start using "sponsor introductions" as a quiet workaround to the salary cap?

The Clippers call it common practice. The NBA says the deals were significantly above fair market value. The NBAPA and Steve Ballmer are both preparing legal challenges if the ruling restricts these introductions.

The Board of Governors meets in mid-September. Resolution is expected around September 22. And when a decision finally comes, it won't just affect the Clippers or the Kawhi Leonard trade.

It might change how every front office does business.

Stay tuned. This one matters. 👀

### STORY 4: Stephen Curry Extension — 7 Days

**Long-Form Post**
Window: 4:00 PM ET

One week.

Seven days from today — August 29 — Stephen Curry becomes eligible to sign a contract extension with the Golden State Warriors.

Warriors GM Mike Dunleavy Jr. isn't worried. "He really, really wants to win and he wants to finish his career as a Warrior," Dunleavy said. "I don't think that changes tomorrow, August 29th, start of the season, middle of the season..."

The available max is 2 years, $136.7 million. But reports suggest Curry might prefer a shorter structure — potentially 1 year around $62.6 million — giving both sides more flexibility heading into 2027-28.

The context matters: the Warriors finished 37-45 last season and couldn't land LeBron, Giannis, Jaylen Brown, or Anthony Davis in the offseason. If Curry signs short-term, it could open the door for one more major roster move.

If he doesn't sign an extension at all? He becomes a free agent for the first time in his career in 2027. The FIRST time. At 39 years old.

But Dunleavy says he's not worried. And for what it's worth — neither are most Warriors fans.

The countdown is on. August 29. ⏰
