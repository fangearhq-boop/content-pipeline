NBA Title Odds One Week Out: Thunder and Spurs Favored Over Defending Champion Knicks

By Marcus Cole | October 13, 2026 | Hoop Heroes

Seven days before the 2026–27 NBA season tips off, the betting markets have delivered a clear verdict: the Oklahoma City Thunder are the favorites to win the next NBA championship, and the defending champion New York Knicks are not even the second choice. At FanDuel, the Thunder sit at +230, the San Antonio Spurs at +270, and the Knicks — the team that won the title six months ago — at +950.

This is not disrespect. It is a genuine assessment of a league that might be more competitive at the top than at any point in the last decade.

The Top Three — and Why They're Clustered

ESPN analyst Zach Kram's preseason rankings placed the Knicks, Thunder, and Spurs alone at the top tier of his 30-team evaluation, noting they were "the only three teams to win a game in the conference finals" last season. These three franchises are collectively returning 14 of 15 starters from a season ago. The one departure from that group is Luguentz Dort, who moved from Oklahoma City to Atlanta.

Oklahoma City's case for favorites status rests on offensive depth, elite defense, and a roster full of players who peaked in the 2026 playoffs. The Thunder lost to the Spurs in the Western Conference Finals — a brutal seven-game exit — but the gap between them and San Antonio was tight enough that a different series could easily have ended differently.

The Spurs' case centers on one player: Victor Wembanyama. At 22 years old, already carrying a $252 million extension, and having watched the Knicks celebrate on his home floor in the Finals, he is operating with the kind of motivation that is difficult to quantify. Hard Rock Bet actually has San Antonio slightly ahead at +225.

The Knicks at +950

The defending champions at nearly 10-to-1 feels like a market inefficiency. New York won the title by erasing a 29-point deficit in Game 4 of the Finals — the greatest comeback in Finals history — and then closing it on the road in Game 5 with Jalen Brunson's 45-point masterpiece. The Knicks return their core intact and add a banner that has not flown at Madison Square Garden since 1973.

The market's hesitance likely comes from two places: the strength of the Western Conference and the perceived ceiling concerns about New York's roster construction compared to the peak of OKC's depth or Wembanyama's individual dominance. But betting markets have been wrong about defending champions before — and the Knicks at +950 offer significantly better value than the Sixers at +1,000, who have not yet fully integrated their new pieces.

The East Behind New York

Philadelphia (LeBron, Embiid, Brown, Maxey) and Boston (Paul George, Derrick White, Cade Cunningham on the Pistons but that's irrelevant) are the main Eastern threats. The Celtics at +1,400 still carry real championship DNA despite losing Jaylen Brown to the Paul George trade. Detroit's Cade Cunningham has emerged as a genuine star. And Miami — with Giannis now posting early preseason numbers that have Spoelstra invoking Magic Johnson — is sitting at +3,000 as a potential value play.

Seven days from now, the season starts. The odds reflect one version of the truth. Six months of basketball will write another one.

What's Next

The 2026–27 NBA regular season tips off October 20 with a tripleheader on NBC: Celtics at Pistons (3:00 PM ET), Sixers at Knicks (7:00 PM ET), Thunder at Spurs (9:30 PM ET). The roster deadline is October 17 and final rosters are set October 19. Every team's real work begins a week from today. One of these teams will be holding the trophy come June. The market currently thinks it will not be New York — but the Knicks have some thoughts about that.
