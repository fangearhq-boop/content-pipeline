Anthony Edwards, Jaden McDaniels Drop 48 Combined Points as Wolves Rout Champion Knicks 122-104

By Jake Torres | October 13, 2026 | Hoop Heroes

Seven days before the New York Knicks raise their first championship banner since 1973, Anthony Edwards and Jaden McDaniels walked into their building and handed them a preseason beatdown. Final score: Timberwolves 122, Knicks 104. Edwards led all scorers with 26 points. McDaniels added 22. Minnesota's starters had barely taken their seats on the bench when the lead was already comfortable.

The defending champions looked flat. Jalen Brunson scored 20 to lead New York — a productive night that was not enough to change the outcome or the storyline. The Knicks, playing the final stretch of their preseason before the real season begins, gave up early buckets and never fully recovered.

Edwards at His Best

Anthony Edwards turned 25 this summer, and what the Timberwolves have in him at this stage of his career is something increasingly difficult to plan for. His combination of size, athleticism, pull-up shooting, and finishing at the rim gives him angles that most defenders simply cannot account for. Twenty-six points against a team that just won an NBA championship — even in a preseason setting — is the kind of performance that sends a message.

McDaniels' 22 points added a secondary storyline. The 25-year-old forward has developed into one of the better two-way wings in the Western Conference, and Monday night was a reminder of what he brings when the offensive rhythm is flowing. Together, Edwards and McDaniels combined for 48 points before the Wolves' starters exited in the third quarter.

Does This Loss Matter?

The honest answer is: not really. Every October, NBA coaches are more interested in integrating new rotation pieces, running their base offensive sets, and evaluating depth than in winning preseason games. The Knicks — fully healthy, fully intact from their championship run — have nothing to prove between now and October 20.

But context matters. The Knicks are 7 days away from one of the most anticipated home openers in the league's recent history. They are hanging a banner. They are receiving their rings. And LeBron James will walk into Madison Square Garden in a Sixers uniform as the first visitor on that night. Losing a preseason tune-up to Minnesota is not the story — it is the soundtrack. It keeps the pressure and the anticipation at a simmer that no one in New York will ignore.

The Knicks' Response

Brunson's 20-point outing showed that the championship engine is still running. New York's biggest question entering 2026–27 is not whether it can repeat — it is whether the rest of the Eastern Conference has caught up enough to make that repeat genuinely difficult. Based on how the Sixers retooled with LeBron and Jaylen Brown, and how the Celtics responded to losing Jaylen Brown by adding Paul George, the competition is real.

One week from tonight, the Knicks will have their moment. The Garden will be at its loudest. The banner will go up. And then the game will start — and the question of whether New York is still the best team in the East gets answered through six months of basketball, not one preseason loss to Anthony Edwards.

What's Next

The NBA's regular-season roster deadline is October 17, with final rosters set at 4:00 PM CT on October 19. Opening Night follows on October 20 — a tripleheader on NBC featuring Celtics at Pistons (3:00 PM ET), Sixers at Knicks (7:00 PM ET), and Thunder at Spurs (9:30 PM ET). The Knicks' banner goes up before the 7:00 PM tip. The rest of the NBA takes notice — and gets back to work.
