Spurs Drop to 0-2 in Preseason After Jazz Win 119-109 — But Wembanyama Has His Eyes on October 20

By Marcus Cole | October 13, 2026 | Hoop Heroes

The Utah Jazz beat the San Antonio Spurs 119-109 at the Delta Center on Monday night, leaving San Antonio with an 0-2 preseason record and precisely zero reasons to panic. Victor Wembanyama is being managed through limited minutes. The coaching staff is evaluating depth. The result is irrelevant.

The only date that matters in San Antonio right now is October 20.

A Rough Preseason Start

The Spurs' October has been forgettable on the scoreboard. They blew a 23-point lead in their preseason opener against Atlanta and lost 123-116. Then came Monday's loss in Salt Lake City. Through two games, Wembanyama — the franchise cornerstone who signed a five-year, $252 million extension in July — has been used sparingly. Against the Hawks, he logged 17 minutes and scored 9 points with 6 rebounds and a three-pointer.

Those numbers are fine for a player whose coaching staff is keeping a careful eye on his workload. Wembanyama turns 23 in January. This is his fourth NBA season. The goal in October is not to peak — it is to be ready to peak in June.

The $252 Million Commitment

When Wembanyama signed his extension in July, the structure told a story. He took the 25 percent maximum — rather than the 30 percent supermax he could have earned — as a deliberate choice that kept flexibility in San Antonio's cap sheet. It was a team-first gesture from a 22-year-old who has already won a Western Conference Finals MVP, earned multiple All-NBA selections, and taken his franchise to within five games of an NBA championship.

The Spurs beat Oklahoma City in seven games in the 2026 Western Conference Finals. They lost to the New York Knicks in five games in the Finals — a series that included a 29-point deficit comeback by New York in Game 4 that was called the greatest comeback in Finals history. Wembanyama scored 32 points in the one game San Antonio won, Game 3. Those facts sit in his memory. They motivate everything he does now.

Opening Night: The Real First Game

On October 20, the Spurs open their home schedule against the Knicks at 9:30 PM ET on NBC. It is the third game of the league's Opening Night tripleheader, and it is the most historically loaded matchup of the three. Two teams that went to the Finals six months ago. A $252 million man looking to avenge a loss. A defending champion that erased a 29-point hole and is carrying a full season's worth of confidence into the building.

Preseason records are a footnote. Wembanyama knows it. Pop knows it. The 0-2 start is meaningless. October 20 at the AT&T Center — that is where the Spurs' 2026–27 season actually begins.

What's Next

San Antonio will play its final preseason games before the October 17 roster waiver deadline and the October 19 finalization of rosters. Then the real basketball begins — the Spurs hosting the Knicks on Opening Night at 9:30 PM ET on NBC. The Finals rematch. The preseason is a prologue. The story starts in seven days.
