LeBron James Makes His 76ers Debut: 10 Points, 5 Assists, One Week Until MSG

By Jake Torres | October 13, 2026 | Hoop Heroes

LeBron James is 41 years old. He walked onto the court in Brooklyn on October 8, played 18 minutes in the first half, scored 10 points on 8-of-8 free throw shooting, handed out 5 assists, and sat down for good at halftime. The Brooklyn Nets won 114-108. None of it mattered in the way you might think.

This was an introduction, not a statement. A first look at something that will spend the next six months trying to do something remarkable: win the Eastern Conference with a 41-year-old superstar as a central piece.

The Debut Numbers

LeBron's box line from his first Philadelphia preseason appearance — 10 points, 4 rebounds, 5 assists in 18 minutes — understates what it was actually like to watch. He attempted barely any field goals, instead working to get fouled and making all eight trips to the free-throw line count. The five assists, which led the Sixers in his limited time on the floor, showed the court vision that has defined his game for two decades.

Jaylen Brown led Philadelphia with 18 points. Julius Randle scored 14 for Brooklyn. The Nets outscored the Sixers 37-23 in the third quarter after LeBron had already exited for the night, and that is how a preseason game that was close at halftime turned into a comfortable 114-108 loss.

The Lineup That Will Scare Everyone

Philadelphia's opening-night starting five is one of the more intimidating group of names in recent Eastern Conference history. LeBron James. Jaylen Brown — acquired from Boston in the trade that sent Paul George to the Celtics. Joel Embiid. Tyrese Maxey. VJ Edgecombe.

That is a lineup with a combined four All-Star appearances in the last two seasons, legitimate two-way defense, and multiple creators who can handle the ball and make plays. The ceiling of that group — fully healthy, fully integrated — is an Eastern Conference championship. LeBron signed a two-year, $8 million deal this past summer, betting on himself and on what Philadelphia put together around him.

One Week Until the Garden

On October 20, the 76ers travel to Madison Square Garden for Opening Night. The New York Knicks will raise their first championship banner since 1973. They will receive their rings. The Garden will be at its loudest. And then LeBron James will step out onto the floor as the road team — a role he has been in before, in circumstances just as intense.

It is a story that writes itself. LeBron on the road, in New York, on banner night. The defending champions celebrating while the season's most anticipated visitor arrives to spoil the party. FanDuel has the Sixers at +1,000 to win the title — behind the Thunder, Spurs, and Knicks — but Opening Night at MSG is not a situation that respects betting odds. It is one of the most compelling early-season matchups the schedule could have produced, and it kicks off at 7:00 PM ET on NBC.

What's Next

Philadelphia continues its preseason before the October 17 roster waiver deadline and final roster construction on October 19. Then the real season begins — and LeBron's first regular-season test comes in the most hostile possible environment: Madison Square Garden, Knicks banner night, primetime on NBC. The debut in Brooklyn told us the pieces are in place. Opening Night will tell us whether they are ready.
