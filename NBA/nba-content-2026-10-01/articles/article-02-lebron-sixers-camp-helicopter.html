LeBron James Took a Helicopter Home From His First 76ers Practice

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | October 1, 2026

LeBron James' first day as a Philadelphia 76er unfolded exactly the way you might expect from a 41-year-old beginning his record 24th NBA season: he arrived early, set the tone, impressed everyone in the building, and then left by helicopter.

Training camp at the Penn Medicine Philadelphia 76ers Training Complex in Camden, New Jersey opened Monday, and by every account inside the facility, Day 1 was a statement. But it was the scene outside that captured the broader context of what LeBron James brings to a franchise — and what it means to build a title contender around a player who quite literally operates at a different altitude.

Inside the Practice

Head coach Nick Nurse had spent years coaching against LeBron as an opponent. Monday was the first time they shared the same sideline, and Nurse didn't pretend the experience was ordinary.

"To me it looked the same as it looked coaching against him," Nurse said. "When you're out there and looking, you say this guy is really tough, really good. Like, that's what it looked like to me. Glad he's on our team."

Nurse described LeBron as "very vocal" and an "example setter," particularly during defensive drills — a meaningful detail for a team that has historically been inconsistent on that end of the floor. LeBron's reputation as a defensive communicator stretches back decades, but it still carries weight when implemented in a new system with a new group of teammates.

Anfernee Simons noted that LeBron was "already getting warmed up by the time I walked through the door." VJ Edgecombe, the rookie thrust into the starting five, offered the most revealing comment about what it actually feels like to play alongside one of the greatest players in history: "We've got to switch that. We're holding each other accountable. We're teammates now, man. There's no pressure playing alongside him. It's more fun than pressure."

The Helicopter and the Context

LeBron revealed at media day that he plans to live in both New York City and Philadelphia during the 2026-27 season. His home is in New York. The training facility is in Camden. The distance between them is roughly 95 miles. His solution is to helicopter between cities when necessary.

After his first practice on September 29, a helicopter was audible departing from outside the Camden complex, headed in the direction of Manhattan. The headline from Complex captured the moment: "LeBron James Lifts Off From Sixers Camp in a Helicopter Bound for New York."

There is an obvious framing available here — the distant star, more celebrity than teammate — but it fundamentally misreads what the 76ers acquired. LeBron's willingness to helicopter between practice sessions isn't a sign of disengagement. He signed a contract knowing the commute would be unusual. He arrived early on Day 1. He set the defensive tone. He flies home and comes back tomorrow. That is, at minimum, the behavior of a player who is present in the ways that matter.

What the Sixers Looked Like on the Floor

The early returns from Philadelphia's camp are encouraging. The Sixers ran a preseason scrimmage against the Minnesota Timberwolves and won comfortably, 126-110. That result carries limited predictive weight — preseason scrimmages are controlled environments with capped intensity — but the margin and the reported energy inside the facility suggest the roster's pieces are starting to connect.

The starting lineup Nick Nurse has assembled — LeBron James, Joel Embiid, Tyrese Maxey, Jaylen Brown, and VJ Edgecombe — is a genuinely unusual group. Embiid is a two-time MVP. Brown is a Finals MVP. LeBron is LeBron. The question of how three alpha competitors co-exist in the same system, under the same coach, competing for the same title, doesn't have an easy answer yet. But Day 1 produced no visible friction and plenty of optimism.

What's Next

Day 3 of training camp is underway. The Sixers' first official preseason game is not yet scheduled, but the countdown to Opening Night — October 20 against the Knicks at Madison Square Garden — is 19 days. LeBron's first regular-season appearance as a 76er will come against the defending champions, in their own building, on national television. The helicopter commute will continue until then and beyond.

The story of LeBron James' final chapter is just beginning to be written in Philadelphia. Based on what the facility reported from Day 1, the pages so far look promising.
