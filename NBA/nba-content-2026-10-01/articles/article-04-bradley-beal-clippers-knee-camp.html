Bradley Beal's Knee Inflammation Is the Latest Blow for the Injury-Cursed Clippers

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | October 1, 2026

The Los Angeles Clippers had simple goals entering the 2026 offseason: stay healthy, rebuild their roster around new faces, and stop being the league's cautionary tale about injury management. They are 0-for-3 before training camp has officially begun.

Bradley Beal is dealing with knee inflammation and won't be ready for the start of Clippers camp, adding the third significant setback to an organization that entered September hoping for a clean slate. He joins Brandon Ingram — who was announced as out indefinitely with a partial Achilles tear last week — among the team's offseason acquisitions who will not be available for the beginning of the season.

What the Clippers Lost Before Camp Started

The offseason started with a significant subtraction. Kawhi Leonard, the player the franchise has revolved around for years despite his perpetual availability questions, was traded to the Toronto Raptors on September 14. The return included young assets and draft capital, but the move signaled a philosophical shift — Los Angeles was moving away from the Kawhi era and toward a rebuilt identity.

That rebuilt identity was supposed to include Beal and Ingram as its offensive pillars. Beal, the former six-time All-Star who spent the last season recovering from hip surgery and appeared in only six games, was meant to return fully healthy this fall. Instead, he arrives at camp with knee inflammation that further delays his return.

Ingram's partial Achilles tear, discovered after recent heel surgery, will keep him out for a significant stretch of the season. The Clippers revealed that finding just last week — a discovered injury rather than an acute one, which raises its own questions about monitoring and communication.

Who the Clippers Have Left

The injury carnage leaves Los Angeles relying on a supporting cast to carry the early season. The Clippers do have depth — role players, defensive specialists, young players looking to establish themselves — but the gap between what they hoped to be and what they currently are is significant. A team that was supposed to announce its rebuilt identity is instead limping into the season's first weeks without its two headline additions.

The Clippers' season outlook remains intact in theory. Beal's knee inflammation is described as a short-term issue that won't affect the entire year. Ingram's Achilles timetable is uncertain, but partial tears can have varying recovery windows. If both players return healthy by mid-November or December, Los Angeles might still have a functional top-end roster for the second half of the season.

But the organization's history with injury promises has earned skepticism. Kawhi Leonard never played a full Clippers season. Paul George missed time every year. Now the new stars are sidelined before camp even concludes its first week.

What's Next

The Clippers will begin training camp without their two principal additions. The organization will monitor Beal's knee situation daily and make decisions about his ramp-up based on his response to treatment. Ingram's timeline will be evaluated separately as the season progresses.

For now, the question in Los Angeles is a familiar one: which players are available to take the floor? The Clippers have asked that question year after year. The 2026-27 season was supposed to be different. It does not look different yet.
