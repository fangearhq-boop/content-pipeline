Opening Night in 19 Days: The Most Expensive Season Opener in NBA History

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | October 1, 2026

There is a number that tells you everything about October 20, 2026. It's not the years — 53 — since the New York Knicks last raised a championship banner. It's not the number 41, which is LeBron James' age as he walks into MSG for the first time as a Philadelphia 76er. It's $9,283.

That is the current asking price for a court-adjacent seat at the Knicks' home opener against Philadelphia. NBC New York reported it as the most expensive season-opening game in NBA history. Get-in at the 400-level is $1,845. The 100-level has been clearing $2,700. The gap between a typical Knicks home game and what the market has decided October 20 is worth tells you everything the ticket prices won't say directly: this game matters.

What the Night Actually Is

Before tip-off, the Knicks raise their first championship banner since 1973. Fifty-three years. Two generations of fans who watched the rafters stay empty while every rival franchise — the Celtics, the Lakers, the Bulls, the Heat, the Warriors — added banners to their own ceilings. The ceremony begins at 6:30 PM ET. The game tips at 7:00. NBC and Peacock carry it nationally.

The opponent they drew for that ceremony is the 76ers, who arrive as the most decorated offensive assembly in recent NBA history. LeBron James. Jaylen Brown. Joel Embiid. Tyrese Maxey. The Sixers built this roster specifically because of what the Knicks did to them last spring — a four-game sweep that ended Philadelphia's season and ended LeBron's patience with Los Angeles. He chose Philly, he has explained, in part to fix exactly that kind of postseason failure.

LeBron's quote from media day has already taken on its own life: "I didn't leave my family to lose in the second round." He was talking about his priorities. He may as well have been talking about October 20.

The Stakes for Both Franchises

For the Knicks, this is a moment of arrival. The 2025-26 championship ended a wait that outlasted decades of heartbreak and front-office dysfunction. A banner is a permanent answer to all of it. The franchise wanted to open the defense against the most dangerous challenger in the Eastern Conference, and the schedule delivered exactly that.

For the 76ers, the game is a statement before the statement. Losing this one — in the building where the Knicks will be at their loudest and most emotional — would be a damaging early data point. Winning it would immediately announce that Philadelphia is as dangerous as advertised. Neither outcome decides the season, but both shape the narrative of how the conference's contenders see each other.

Nick Nurse has a team built around size, versatility, and the specific ability to guard the Knicks' system. Jaylen Brown was acquired, in part, because of what he did defensively against New York in previous playoff series. LeBron's defensive awareness and communication — on display in Day 1 of training camp — was built for exactly the kind of game MSG will demand on October 20.

The Market's Verdict

The secondary ticket market has been an unusually honest judge of significance throughout LeBron's career. The games that matter most — the Finals, the elimination games, the statement moments — carry prices that reflect what fans are willing to surrender to be present. October 20, by the market's measure, is a Finals-adjacent event disguised as a regular-season opener.

The average NBA season opener typically runs $350 to $500 on the secondary market, depending on the teams involved. Knicks vs. 76ers on October 20 is running at $1,845 to get in the door. The spread between those numbers is a rough measure of what this game means — not just as basketball, but as cultural event.

What's Next

Nineteen days. Both teams are in training camp now, both running their systems and establishing identities before the real season begins. The Knicks' preseason schedule will give Thibodeau a chance to set rotations without revealing too much of the defensive hand that dismantled Philadelphia last spring. The 76ers will work on the chemistry that makes three superstars and a rising point guard function as one coherent unit.

October 20 will answer some early questions and raise new ones. Whatever it does, the NBA's most expensive opening night in history is 19 days away, and both teams are ready for it.
