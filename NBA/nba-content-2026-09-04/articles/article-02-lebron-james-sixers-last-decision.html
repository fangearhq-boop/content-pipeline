LeBron James Signs With Philadelphia 76ers in "Last Decision" — 2-Year, $8M Deal

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes

September 4, 2026

LeBron James had one more decision to make. He chose Philadelphia.

James, 41, has agreed to a two-year, $8 million deal with the Philadelphia 76ers, calling it his "last decision" on social media and ending weeks of speculation about whether the all-time scoring leader would retire or continue playing. The contract includes a player option on the second year, giving James flexibility as he winds down his legendary career.

"I thought long and hard about walking away," James posted. "But I've got one more championship in me. Philadelphia is where I need to be."

The Team He's Joining

James is not walking into a rebuilding situation. The 76ers have assembled one of the most star-studded rosters in NBA history around him. Joel Embiid, the reigning MVP candidate, anchors the frontcourt. Jaylen Brown — acquired in a blockbuster trade — brings elite two-way wing play. Tyrese Maxey provides speed and scoring off the bench or in the starting lineup. VJ Edgecombe adds athleticism and defensive versatility.

On paper, the Sixers now boast three players (James, Embiid, Brown) who have each been named to All-NBA teams in recent seasons. Getting all three healthy and in sync will be the challenge — but the ceiling is unmistakably high.

A Departure From Los Angeles

James's departure from the Los Angeles Lakers closes one of the most storied chapters in franchise history. After arriving in L.A. in 2018, James won a championship in the NBA's bubble season in 2020 and spent six seasons as the face of the franchise.

He informed the Lakers of his intent to play elsewhere at the start of free agency. The Golden State Warriors, Miami Heat and Minnesota Timberwolves were among the teams that pursued him before James ultimately chose Philadelphia.

The Lakers now enter a full rebuild, having already traded DeAndre Ayton to Washington and acquired Walker Kessler from Utah as they reshape their roster around a post-LeBron future.

Eastern Conference Implications

LeBron's move to Philadelphia instantly reshapes the Eastern Conference race. The New York Knicks, last season's champions, remain the team to beat. But the Sixers now loom as the biggest threat — a team that could challenge anyone in a seven-game series if the stars stay healthy.

The Miami Heat (Giannis Antetokounmpo) and Toronto Raptors (pending Kawhi Leonard trade) round out what looks like an extraordinarily competitive East. James has always raised the level of competition wherever he has played, and Philadelphia is no different.

What's Next

Training camp opens later this month. James will arrive in Philadelphia focused on one thing: winning his fifth championship. If he can stay healthy — and the Sixers' entire stars-and-scrubs roster can gel quickly — that goal is not just possible, it's realistic.

At 41, LeBron James is still chasing greatness. And now he's doing it in a city that desperately wants a title.
