Cooper Flagg Drops 43 Points as Mavericks Rally Past Pacers 125-122

By Jake Torres | November 10, 2026

They were down 10 at halftime. Cooper Flagg had a different idea about how the night would end.

The Dallas Mavericks teenager erupted for 43 points, 10 rebounds, six assists, three steals, and a block Monday at American Airlines Center to lift Dallas over the Indiana Pacers 125-122. Flagg shot 16-of-25 from the field and made all eight of his free-throw attempts, and he combined with Kyrie Irving — who added 35 points of his own — for 26 fourth-quarter points as the Mavericks outscored Indiana 35-29 in the final frame to complete the comeback.

A Performance for the Record Books

At 19 years old, Flagg delivered a stat line that belongs among the greatest individual performances in modern NBA history. His 43 points were a season high, and according to multiple reports, the game made him just the second teenager in NBA history to record at least 40 points, 10 rebounds, and five assists in a single game.

What made it more remarkable was the precision. Flagg's 16-of-25 shooting represents a 64 percent mark from the field — not a volume scorer hunting numbers, but an efficient performer who was making everything count. The 8-for-8 night at the free-throw line underscored the same composure under pressure that defined his entire evening.

Dallas trailed by double digits entering the third quarter, and few outside the locker room expected a comeback against an Indiana team that had been playing with more urgency than its 3-8 record suggested. Flagg made those doubts look silly.

Kyrie's 35 and the Dynamic Duo

Kyrie Irving is no stranger to big-game moments, and he played a crucial role in keeping Dallas competitive before Flagg took over. Irving's 35 points meant Indiana had to pick its poison — focus on the teenager and let the veteran beat you, or shade toward Irving and watch Flagg carve you up.

The Pacers had no good answer. Flagg and Irving combined for 26 of Dallas's 35 fourth-quarter points, an offensive surge that turned a potential loss into a victory that improves the Mavericks to 7-4 on the season.

Indiana's Troubles Deepen

For the Pacers, the loss drops them to 3-8 and raises genuine questions about where this team is headed. Indiana showed enough fight to build a 10-point halftime lead, but the inability to close out a game against a Dallas squad completing a back-to-back will sting. The Pacers are running out of margin for error in a conference that punishes inconsistency.

What's Next

Dallas at 7-4 is legitimate Western Conference competition, and Flagg is quickly becoming one of the NBA's must-watch players. The Mavericks have a balanced roster with Kyrie Irving providing veteran leadership and Flagg delivering the explosive upside of a potential franchise cornerstone. If this partnership continues to click, Dallas will be a team no opponent wants to face in April.

Indiana needs to regroup quickly. At 3-8, the Pacers are already in recovery mode, and the remaining schedule will not wait for them to find themselves.
