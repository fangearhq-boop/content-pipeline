Allen's 27 and Reid's 25-13 Snap Hornets' 6-Game Skid in 124-112 Win Over Cavaliers

By Jake Torres | November 10, 2026

The Charlotte Hornets were overdue for a win. On Monday night, they finally got one.

Grayson Allen scored 27 points and Naz Reid delivered an impressive 25-point, 13-rebound performance as Charlotte snapped a six-game losing streak with a 124-112 victory over the Cleveland Cavaliers. The Hornets were patient, they were physical, and they were emphatic — a combination that had been missing during their recent slide.

Allen and Reid Carry the Load

Allen's 27-point night gave Charlotte the perimeter scoring punch they needed to keep Cleveland honest, while Reid's inside presence made the Cavaliers pay for any lapse in their defensive rotations. The 25-and-13 line from Reid was a double-double in every sense, and his play in the paint gave the Hornets an interior advantage that proved decisive.

Having two players step up simultaneously was exactly what Charlotte's coaching staff had been hoping to see. During the six-game losing skid, the Hornets struggled to find consistent contributions across the roster. Monday's win showed that when Allen and Reid are both locked in, this team is capable of beating quality opponents.

Cavaliers Come Up Short

Cleveland had been playing reasonably well heading into Monday, but they ran into a Charlotte team that was hungry for a victory. The 112 points Cleveland scored was not an insignificant offensive output, but the Hornets' 124 meant it was never enough. The Cavaliers' road difficulties continue to be a question mark in what should otherwise be a productive season.

What's Next

For Charlotte, one win does not erase the memories of six losses, but it is the kind of statement game that can change a team's energy and direction. The Hornets have the talent to compete in the Eastern Conference and Monday's performance was a reminder of that. Building on this showing will be the challenge.

Cleveland needs to address its issues away from home before they become a bigger problem in the second half of the season. The talent is there; the consistency on the road is not.
