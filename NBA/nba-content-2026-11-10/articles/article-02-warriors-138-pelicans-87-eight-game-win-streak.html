Warriors Rout Pelicans 138-87, Curry Hits All-Time Scoring Milestone During 8-Game Win Streak

By Marcus Cole | November 10, 2026

When Stephen Curry plays 21 minutes and scores 25 points, it barely feels like he broke a sweat. That was Monday night at Chase Center, where the Golden State Warriors dismantled the New Orleans Pelicans 138-87 — a 51-point blowout that extended their winning streak to eight games and announced their intentions to the rest of the Western Conference.

Somewhere in the middle of all that efficiency, Curry moved into 16th place on the NBA's all-time scoring list. The milestone received polite applause. The 51-point final margin received a standing ovation.

Curry's Night by the Numbers

Curry finished 6-of-10 from the field, 4-of-7 from three, and a flawless 9-of-9 from the free-throw line in his 21 minutes of action. The Warriors were winning by so much that there was little reason to push him deeper into the second half. That Curry could move up the all-time scoring leaderboard in a game where he barely played a full two quarters is a testament to the kind of career he has built.

Brandin Podziemski matched Curry's output with 25 points of his own, and Lendeborg chipped in 23 to give Golden State three players with 20-plus points on the night. The Warriors went 21-for-many from three-point range and racked up 37 assists — basketball that looked more like a clinic than a regular-season game.

The Win Streak Is Very Real

Eight consecutive wins is not a fluke. The Warriors at 8-3 have established themselves as one of the elite teams in the West, and the manner of this particular victory — a 51-point margin against a Pelicans team that entered the game at 4-7 — speaks to the depth and execution Golden State has found this season.

It is fair to note that New Orleans was without Zion Williamson and Trey Murphy III and was playing the second game of a back-to-back. Those are real factors. But even heavily discounted, the Warriors' performance demonstrated a level of collective execution that most teams simply cannot match. The 37 assists on 21 threes paint the picture of a team sharing the ball beautifully.

What's Next

Golden State at 8-3 is in serious Western Conference title contention. The streak will inevitably end — all streaks do — but the foundation being built here is about more than eight games. The Warriors appear to have found the kind of team identity and execution that has historically made them dangerous.

For New Orleans at 4-7, the losses are piling up and the schedule is not about to get easier. The Pelicans need to find their defensive identity and get healthy quickly before the early portion of the season slips entirely away from them.
