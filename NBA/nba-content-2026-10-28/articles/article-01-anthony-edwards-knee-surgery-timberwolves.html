Anthony Edwards Faces Knee Surgery, Out Up to Two Months

By Jake Torres

October 28, 2026

Minnesota Timberwolves

Anthony Edwards will undergo an arthroscopic procedure on his right knee and is expected to miss up to two months, the Minnesota Timberwolves confirmed Tuesday. The 25-year-old star will be sidelined until at least December in what is a significant early-season blow to one of the Western Conference's most dangerous teams.

The timing is crushing for Wolves fans. Just two days before the surgery announcement, Edwards put up 47 points in a 127-125 overtime loss to the Cleveland Cavaliers — one of the most breathtaking individual performances of the young season. That game now feels like a farewell for an extended stretch.

A Recurring Problem

This is not the first time Edwards has battled right knee issues. He dealt with patellofemoral pain syndrome last season and was limited down the stretch, missing 11 of Minnesota's final 14 regular-season games. The team reinjured the knee during the season opener in Miami this year.

Rather than manage the issue through the season, the Timberwolves opted for surgery to address it properly. Team sources expressed confidence that the procedure would allow Edwards to return healthier and stronger than if he had played through it.

The expectation is that he will be back in December, though the organization did not set a firm date. Minnesota will update his status after the procedure and initial recovery period.

What the Timberwolves Lose

Edwards had established himself as one of the most electric players in the NBA heading into this season. He is not just a scorer — he is Minnesota's identity, its most reliable defender, and the engine of everything the Wolves do offensively.

Minnesota entered Tuesday sitting at 3-1, one of the better starts in the Western Conference. Without Edwards, the team will need contributions from its entire roster. Players like Rudy Gobert, Jaden McDaniels, and whoever the Wolves can piece together in the backcourt will be asked to carry more of the load.

The schedule before the All-Star break features games against several Western contenders. How the Wolves navigate this stretch will define whether they remain legitimate playoff contenders or slip in the standings.

What's Next

Anthony Edwards is expected to have his arthroscopic procedure in the coming days, with the Timberwolves planning to provide a clearer recovery timeline after surgery. Minnesota begins its stretch without its franchise player when it hosts the Orlando Magic on Thursday, October 30. The Wolves have shown enough early-season depth to stay competitive — but with the West as loaded as ever, the margin for error without Edwards is razor-thin.
