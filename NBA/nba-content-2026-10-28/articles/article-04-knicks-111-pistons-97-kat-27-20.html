Karl-Anthony Towns Posts 27 Points and 20 Rebounds as Knicks Beat Pistons 111-97

By Jake Torres

October 28, 2026

New York Knicks | Detroit Pistons

Karl-Anthony Towns delivered a dominant double-double with 27 points and 20 rebounds Tuesday night at Madison Square Garden, leading the New York Knicks to their first win of the season with a 111-97 defeat of the Detroit Pistons. Jalen Brunson added 23 points as New York snapped a 0-3 start and handed Detroit its first loss of the year.

The game turned decisively in the third quarter. The Knicks outscored Detroit 37-13 in the period, turning a competitive game into a rout in the span of 12 minutes. It was exactly the kind of burst Madison Square Garden fans had been waiting to see from a team that had underperformed in its first three outings.

KAT Takes Over

Towns was a force from the opening tip, using his combination of size, touch, and motor to overwhelm Detroit's frontcourt. His 20 rebounds — an eye-catching total at any point in the season — reflected how thoroughly he controlled the boards on both ends. On offense, he worked in the post, stretched the floor from the elbow, and crashed for second-chance opportunities whenever a shot missed.

The 20-rebound game served as a reminder that when Towns is engaged and healthy, he remains one of the most unique offensive weapons in the league. Few centers in NBA history can score 27 and grab 20 boards in the same night, and when he does it against a previously undefeated opponent, it carries extra weight.

Brunson Complements Perfectly

While Towns dominated the paint, Brunson controlled the pace and punished Detroit's defense with smart mid-range pull-ups and late-shot-clock improvisation. His 23 points were efficient and came in key moments. The chemistry between Brunson and Towns — two of New York's most important offseason investments — showed more promise Tuesday than it had in the team's first three games.

The 37-13 third quarter was a product of that connection. Detroit, which had been playing fast and attacking all season, suddenly found itself unable to stop either player. The collapse in that period was swift and absolute, and the Pistons never fully recovered.

Detroit Loses Its Perfect Record

The Pistons entered Tuesday undefeated and riding legitimate momentum. Cade Cunningham had been excellent, the team's energy was infectious, and Detroit was drawing national attention as one of the league's feel-good stories. Tuesday night brought the first dose of adversity, and how the Pistons respond will tell us more about the ceiling of this team.

Detroit's 0-3 opponent Knicks were exactly the kind of team the Pistons could have beaten to build further confidence. Instead, the Knicks showed that their talent level demands respect regardless of their record.

What's Next

The Knicks face a quick turnaround, playing the Chicago Bulls at Madison Square Garden Wednesday night at 8:00 PM ET. A win would give New York back-to-back victories and real momentum. The Pistons look to regroup at home on Thursday. For both teams, Tuesday's final score reshuffled early-season narratives — and the rest of the East took notice.
