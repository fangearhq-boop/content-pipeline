NBA Wednesday Night Preview: Four Games on Tap Before NBA Cup Tips Off Thursday

By Marcus Cole

October 28, 2026

NBA Preview | NBA Cup

Wednesday brings four NBA games to the schedule, and then the stakes get higher. The NBA Cup officially kicks off on Thursday, October 30, adding an extra layer of competition to a slate that was already heating up after a wild Tuesday night.

Before the Cup begins, there is a full evening of basketball to watch. Here is what to follow Wednesday and how the day connects to the bigger storyline building all week.

Wednesday's Schedule

The Washington Wizards host the Cleveland Cavaliers at 7:00 PM ET. Cleveland, which beat the Timberwolves on Monday in overtime, will want to build on that momentum before learning that Minnesota's Anthony Edwards will now miss months with a knee procedure. The Cavs' strong start is worth tracking closely.

The Orlando Magic visit the Toronto Raptors at 7:30 PM ET. Orlando has been a reliable story early in the season, and tonight's game on the road in Canada will test how far their depth can carry them.

The Houston Rockets take on the Milwaukee Bucks at 8:00 PM ET in arguably the most compelling matchup of the evening. Houston has been pushing early in the season, and Milwaukee is looking to find its footing after a mixed start.

Finally, the New York Knicks visit the Chicago Bulls at 8:00 PM ET. After Karl-Anthony Towns' dominant 27-point, 20-rebound performance in Tuesday's win over Detroit, New York will be tested on whether they can sustain that level on a back-to-back. The Bulls, meanwhile, are looking for their first win of the year.

NBA Cup Preview: Thursday Tips Off a New Competition

Beginning Thursday, October 30, the NBA Cup adds meaningful stakes to the regular season. The format groups teams by conference and division, with in-group games counting toward Cup standings. The top teams from each group advance to a knockout round, with the final held in December.

For players and teams, the Cup offers something different from the grind of the 82-game schedule — a short-format tournament within the season where wins matter immediately. In recent years the competition has produced memorable moments and sparked genuine rivalries between teams that otherwise play only once or twice during the regular season.

With the early season delivering unexpected storylines — Portland's unbeaten run, Anthony Edwards' injury, Tatum's buzzer-beaters — the NBA Cup arrives at exactly the right moment to keep energy high through November.

What's Next

Wednesday's results will shape how teams enter the Cup on Thursday. For teams like the Knicks and Bucks, winning Wednesday night on a back-to-back is no small ask — but carrying momentum into the Cup's opening round matters.
