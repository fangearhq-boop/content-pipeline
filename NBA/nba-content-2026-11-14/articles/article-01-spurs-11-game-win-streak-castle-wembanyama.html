Castle's Season-High 32 Fuels Spurs' 11th Straight Win as San Antonio Routs Warriors 123-85

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | November 14, 2026

The San Antonio Spurs extended the NBA's longest active winning streak to 11 games Friday night, overpowering the Golden State Warriors 123-85 behind Stephon Castle's season-high 32 points and a dominant double-double from Victor Wembanyama.

It has become a Friday night ritual in the NBA. The San Antonio Spurs show up, they dominate, and the winning streak gets one game longer. On Friday night at Paycom Center, the Spurs made it 11 straight, crushing the Golden State Warriors 123-85 in a performance that looked less like a basketball game and more like an extended highlight reel.

Stephon Castle was the catalyst, exploding for a season-high 32 points in a performance that announced to the entire league just how dangerous San Antonio has become. Victor Wembanyama, meanwhile, delivered the quiet double-double that has become his calling card this season — 12 points and 12 rebounds in a game the Spurs controlled from wire to wire.

Castle Takes Center Stage

The 32-point outburst from Castle was the story of the first half and never relented. The second-year guard out of Connecticut has shown flashes of brilliance all season, but Friday night represented a new ceiling — a performance that left observers reaching for superlatives and opponents shaking their heads.

Castle attacked the paint with purpose, knocked down perimeter shots with confidence, and controlled the game's tempo in ways that coaches typically ask veterans to handle. The Warriors had no answers. San Antonio's offense moved with crisp efficiency from the opening tip, and by the time the final margin settled at 38 points, it was clear this was not a close contest that spiraled away late — it was a systematic dissection of a franchised that once ruled the Western Conference.

Wembanyama's Quiet Dominance

If Castle was the highlight, Wembanyama was the foundation. The French center's 12-point, 12-rebound double-double continued the season-long pattern of producing without demanding the spotlight. He altered shots in the paint, controlled the defensive glass, and ensured Golden State had no second-chance opportunities to claw back into a game that was decided long before halftime.

The 38-point victory margin tells the story of San Antonio's current form succinctly. This is not a team winning with grit and clutch plays. The Spurs are winning with authority, and they are doing it consistently — 11 times in a row now.

The Streak in Context

Eleven straight wins is the longest active winning streak in the NBA. San Antonio's Cup record is perfect, and their combination of elite defense and an increasingly multi-dimensional offense has made them one of the league's most compelling teams to watch this November.

Warriors fans will point to their injury situation and the demands of a grinding season. But for San Antonio, the narrative is simple: this team, led by a generational talent in Wembanyama and an ascending star in Castle, is playing championship-caliber basketball right now.

What's Next

The Spurs will look to extend the streak when they face their next opponent. At this pace, the question isn't whether San Antonio belongs in the Western Conference contention conversation — it is how high their ceiling goes, and when the rest of the league starts genuinely fearing them.

For Golden State, Friday night's 38-point beatdown raises urgent questions about roster construction and competitive identity. The Warriors of the dynasty era would not have absorbed this kind of defeat quietly — and their current iteration will need answers fast in a Western Conference that shows no mercy.
