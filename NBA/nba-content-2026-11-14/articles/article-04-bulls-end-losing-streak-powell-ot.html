Powell's 33 Points — 9 in OT — End Chicago's 8-Game Skid in Bulls' 125-117 Victory

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | November 14, 2026

Norman Powell scored 33 points including nine in overtime Friday night, ending Chicago's painful eight-game losing streak with a 125-117 victory over the Atlanta Hawks in NBA Cup action.

When an eight-game losing streak finally ends, somebody has to be the one to end it. On Friday night in Chicago, that somebody was Norman Powell. The veteran guard delivered 33 points — nine of them in the overtime period that sealed the game — as the Chicago Bulls beat the Atlanta Hawks 125-117 in NBA Cup play.

The win was more than a box-score result. For a franchise that had been searching for answers through a brutal stretch of eight consecutive defeats, Powell's overtime heroics provided both a result and a reminder: this team, when it finds its footing, is capable of competing.

Powell Takes Over When It Mattered

Through three quarters and regulation, the game was competitive — neither team able to pull away and establish clear control. When overtime arrived, Powell elevated. Nine points in the extra period — the kind of clutch scoring that separates playoff-caliber players from the rest — gave Chicago the cushion they needed to hold off Atlanta.

The 33-point total represented one of Powell's best individual showings of the season, and his timing could not have been better. When a team is mired in a losing streak, individual players who step into the moment and produce change the narrative. Powell did exactly that.

Eight Games of Frustration, Finally Over

Losing eight in a row in the NBA is as demoralizing as it sounds. Each defeat carries forward the psychological weight of the ones before it, and breaking through requires not just talent but mental fortitude. The Bulls found it on Friday night, helped by a crowd at the United Center that refused to stop believing even as the losses piled up.

Chicago's coaching staff will be quick to point out that one win doesn't erase the problems that created the eight-game slide in the first place. But it does provide a foundation — a moment to point to and say: this is what we can be. That matters.

What's Next

The Bulls need to make Friday's win the first step of a genuine turnaround rather than a brief respite between losing streaks. The NBA Cup schedule provides opportunities, and Chicago has the talent to string together wins. The question is whether they can play consistently — something that eluded them through the first weeks of November.

Powell has shown what his ceiling looks like. If the Bulls can build around performances like his and find the defensive consistency that was missing during the skid, there is still a path toward a competitive second half of the season for Chicago.
