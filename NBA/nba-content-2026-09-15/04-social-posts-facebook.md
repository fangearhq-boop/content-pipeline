### STORY 3: DOJ Clippers Investigation

**FB Long-Form** | Window: 12:00 PM ET
The federal investigation into the Los Angeles Clippers' Kawhi Leonard endorsement deals is still moving — quietly, but forward.

The U.S. Attorney's Office in Brooklyn (EDNY) is running a criminal probe into whether the Clippers used secret endorsement deals to funnel income to Leonard outside the NBA's salary cap. The probe is in its "earliest stages," but prosecutors have already issued a subpoena to Daktronics — one of three firms that paid Leonard $18 million in endorsement money by August 2021.

The NBA has already levied historic penalties: a $30 million fine, five first-round picks from 2029-2033, owner Steve Ballmer suspended for one year, Lawrence Frank banned six months, Joe Zucker banned one year, Robertson banned five years, and Leonard fined $700,000. Kawhi's trade to the Toronto Raptors was ratified.

The federal side of this story is still just getting started.

### STORY 4: Jalen Duren Pistons QO

**FB Long-Form** | Window: 11:00 AM ET
The clock is ticking on Jalen Duren's future in Detroit.

With the October 1 qualifying offer deadline just 16 days away, Duren and the Pistons remain far apart in contract negotiations. Detroit has offered a five-year deal worth roughly $190 million ($38M per year); Duren is asking for $40 million-plus annually. The Pistons have said they have no plans to increase their offer before training camp.

If Duren accepts the qualifying offer — worth $9.6 million for 2026-27 — he plays out the season and becomes an unrestricted free agent next summer, and Detroit loses the right to match other teams' offers. At 22 years old, coming off the best regular season of his career (though he struggled in the playoffs), Duren is weighing the short-term hit of $9.6M against the long-term upside of the open market.

He's the last unsigned restricted free agent in the NBA. Something has to give.
