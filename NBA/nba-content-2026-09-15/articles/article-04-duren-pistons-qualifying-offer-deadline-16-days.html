Oct. 1 Is 16 Days Away — Duren's $9.6M Decision Could Upend the Pistons' Future

By Jake Torres | September 15, 2026 | Hoop Heroes

Jalen Duren is the NBA's last unsigned restricted free agent. October 1 is the qualifying offer deadline. And the Detroit Pistons have made their position clear: they are not raising their offer before training camp begins.

That sets up one of the most consequential decisions a 22-year-old in professional basketball has had to make in recent memory. Accept the $9.6 million qualifying offer and play for one year with the chance to hit the open market — or hold out and hope the Pistons eventually blink.

Where the Talks Stand

The Pistons' offer is five years at roughly $190 million — approximately $38 million per year annually. Duren's camp wants $40 million-plus per year on average. The gap is meaningful but not enormous, and yet it has persisted all offseason without movement.

Detroit's position, as reported by multiple outlets, is that the $190 million offer is fair and reflects Duren's value to the franchise. The team does not plan to increase the offer in the final weeks before camp. Pistons officials reportedly believe they have been reasonable and that Duren's asking price does not reflect his track record, particularly his struggles in both of Detroit's playoff series this past spring.

The QO Option

The qualifying offer — worth $9.6 million for the 2026-27 season — is a one-year deal that would make Duren an unrestricted free agent the following summer. Critically, accepting the QO means Detroit loses the right to match offers from other teams after the season, which is a significant concession for a franchise that views Duren as a cornerstone piece of its rebuild.

Reports indicate Duren is increasingly willing to accept the QO. That framing — "increasingly willing" — has been a consistent thread in coverage over the past two weeks, suggesting his camp has moved away from the expectation of a long-term deal getting done before camp and toward preparing for the one-year path.

What It Would Mean

If Duren accepts the QO and plays out 2026-27, he enters next summer's free agency as one of the most sought-after big men available, at just 23 years old. A strong season — and another showing in the playoffs — would put him in position to command exactly the kind of contract his camp is seeking now, potentially from multiple teams, without Detroit having a say.

For the Pistons, the scenario is a nightmare: building a young team around a player for years, only to lose him for a max deal elsewhere because the negotiation stalled over what amounts to a roughly $10 million-per-year disagreement on a five-year contract.

What's Next

October 1 is the hard deadline. Between now and then, there are two possible outcomes: a long-term deal gets done — requiring one side to move — or Duren accepts the qualifying offer. A third path, in which the deadline passes without either, would leave Duren a restricted free agent without a qualifying offer, a complex and unusual scenario with its own set of consequences. The most likely resolution is that something gives before October 1. The question is which side blinks, and by how much. Watch closely.
