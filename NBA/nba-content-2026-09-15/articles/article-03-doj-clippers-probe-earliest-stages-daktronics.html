DOJ Clippers Probe Stays in Shadows — Federal Investigation Remains in "Earliest Stages"

By Damon Pierce | September 15, 2026 | Hoop Heroes

Nearly two weeks after the Justice Department's criminal investigation into the Los Angeles Clippers became public knowledge, one thing has not changed: the probe is still in its "earliest stages," and federal prosecutors have not made any new announcements about charges, targets, or a timeline.

The investigation, being conducted by the U.S. Attorney's Office in the Eastern District of New York (Brooklyn), centers on allegations that the Clippers used secret endorsement deals to funnel income to Kawhi Leonard while circumventing the NBA's salary cap rules. The DOJ has issued at least one subpoena — to Daktronics, a scoreboard and digital display company — as part of the probe.

What Led to the Federal Probe

The federal investigation follows a third-party probe commissioned by the NBA, which found that Daktronics and two other firms paid Leonard $18 million by August 2021 through "highly unusual" endorsement agreements. A portion of the money was traced to arrangements that appeared to be tied to Leonard's decision to join or stay with the Clippers — effectively supplementing his player salary in a way that would not count against the cap.

Separately, fintech company Aspiration was found to have made a $28 million endorsement deal with Leonard and a $50 million investment with Clippers owner Steve Ballmer, with the NBA determining that the Aspiration deal was a "precondition" for the Leonard endorsement.

NBA Penalties Already in Place

The league did not wait for the federal government. The NBA's penalties — already handed down — are among the most severe in league history: a $30 million fine on the organization, five first-round draft picks stripped for the 2029, 2030, 2031, 2032, and 2033 NBA Drafts, owner Steve Ballmer suspended for one year, team president Lawrence Frank banned for six months, executive Joe Zucker banned one year, and former executive Gary Robertson banned for five years. Leonard himself was fined $700,000. Kawhi's subsequent trade to the Toronto Raptors was formally ratified by the league.

The Federal Track

While the NBA's process concluded with the penalties above, the DOJ's criminal investigation is a separate and more unpredictable track. Federal prosecutors in Brooklyn are under no obligation to mirror the NBA's findings or timeline. The nature of the specific crimes being investigated has not been publicly disclosed. The Daktronics subpoena remains the most concrete public detail known about the scope of the probe.

Experts in sports law have noted that the "earliest stages" characterization is consistent with how federal white-collar investigations routinely operate — quietly, over months or even years, before charges are filed or declined.

What's Next

There is no public timeline for the DOJ investigation to conclude, and no indication that charges are imminent. The Clippers — operating now under Ballmer's suspension and Frank's absence — are moving forward with their 2026-27 season preparations with a rebuilt roster. For everyone involved, the federal probe remains an open question that could resurface at any time with significant consequences. The next development to watch for: any grand jury activity, additional subpoenas, or a public statement from the U.S. Attorney's Office. Until then, the investigation continues in the background.
