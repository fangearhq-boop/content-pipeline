Shai Gilgeous-Alexander and the Thunder Are Ready to Take What Last Year Took From Them

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | October 15, 2026

Oklahoma City Thunder | Shai Gilgeous-Alexander | MVP | Season Preview

The Oklahoma City Thunder won 64 games last season. They had the best record in the NBA. And they went home in Game 7.

That ending — a Western Conference Finals loss to the San Antonio Spurs in which injuries to Jalen Williams and Ajay Mitchell left the Thunder short-handed down the stretch — is the fuel the franchise carried into the offseason. It's the fuel Shai Gilgeous-Alexander is carrying into a season where he'll attempt to do something only a handful of players have ever accomplished: win a third consecutive MVP award.

OKC opens the 2026-27 season in five days — on the road, at San Antonio, the team that beat them. The NBA scheduled it that way on purpose.

The MVP Chase

Gilgeous-Alexander won his first MVP in 2024-25 and backed it up with a second consecutive award last spring. He's 28 years old, entering his ninth NBA season, and by every measure available, still ascending. At media day last week, he said he's been working on becoming "more versatile" and arrived in camp in the best shape of his career.

Winning a third straight MVP would put him in rare company. Only Wilt Chamberlain (four consecutive, 1960-66), Bill Russell (back-to-back twice), Moses Malone (three in four years), Larry Bird, and Magic Johnson have won three or more in a row. Gilgeous-Alexander is the consensus early favorite to do it in 2026-27.

The case is simple: he's the best player on the best team in the conference, with no obvious reason to regress.

A Leaner Roster, Bigger Roles

General manager Sam Presti made targeted adjustments over the summer. The most notable moves were subtractions: Lu Dort, Isaiah Joe, and Aaron Wiggins were all traded or waived, trimming payroll and clearing space for the next wave of Thunder contributors.

Cason Wallace, Ajay Mitchell, and Jared McCain — all young, all on cost-controlled contracts — will carry expanded roles this season. Mitchell, still recovering from the shoulder issue that shortened his playoff run, is projected by analysts as a potential Sixth Man of the Year candidate. The core stays intact: SGA, Williams, Chet Holmgren, Josh Giddey.

The Thunder don't need to be dramatically better than last year. They need to stay healthy.

Opening Night in San Antonio

The Western Conference Finals rematch caps Opening Night on NBC. Oklahoma City at San Antonio, 9:30 PM ET, October 20. Victor Wembanyama — who signed a five-year, $252 million extension in July after reaching the Finals — hosting the team he beat in Game 7.

OKC's FanDuel championship odds are +230, tied for the best mark in the league alongside the Spurs (+270). The margin between those two teams is essentially zero in the eyes of oddsmakers. The gap, OKC believes, was injury. Five days from now, they start proving it.

What's Next

Opening Night at San Antonio is October 20 at 9:30 PM ET on NBC. The Thunder then travel to Houston, Dallas, and Memphis in their first road stretch before returning home on October 28. Gilgeous-Alexander's third MVP race begins the moment the ball goes up Tuesday night — and the Thunder have spent all summer preparing for exactly that moment.
