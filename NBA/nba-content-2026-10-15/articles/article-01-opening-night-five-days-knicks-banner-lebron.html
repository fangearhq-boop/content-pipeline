Five Days to Opening Night: The Knicks' Banner, LeBron's MSG Debut, and One Historic Tripleheader

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | October 15, 2026

NBA Opening Night | New York Knicks | Philadelphia 76ers | Oklahoma City Thunder

The NBA's best night of the year arrives in five days, and for once, the pregame ceremony might outshine every basket that follows.

October 20, 2026. Three games. One network. And a storyline that begins before the opening tip: the New York Knicks will raise the first championship banner in Madison Square Garden since 1973, then immediately go to work trying to defend it against LeBron James and a revamped Philadelphia 76ers team that has its own championship dreams.

This is the Opening Night tripleheader NBC and the NBA have built toward since the schedule dropped in August, when secondary-market tickets for the Sixers-Knicks game quickly climbed past $2,200 on SeatGeek. Not a playoff game. Not a Finals rematch. A regular-season opener. That's how much this night means.

The Banner Ceremony

Jalen Brunson scored 45 points in Game 5 of the NBA Finals, his best performance in a career full of them, as the Knicks closed out San Antonio 4-1 and ended a 53-year championship drought. He was named Finals MVP. The city of New York celebrated in a fashion not seen since 1973, and now, on the night of October 20, Brunson and his teammates will watch a banner rise to the rafters of the building they call home.

The ceremony will take place before the Sixers tip-off at 7 PM ET. Players will receive championship rings. The crowd will do what MSG crowds do. And then, 48 minutes later, Brunson will have to guard LeBron James on the same floor where he just soaked in the city's gratitude.

That transition — from celebration to competition — is one of the strangest and most compelling moments in all of sports.

LeBron James Arrives

LeBron James is 41 years old, entering his 24th NBA season, and playing for his fifth franchise. He signed a two-year, $8 million deal with the 76ers in July, famously announcing: "I believe I can help make the 76ers a championship team."

His preseason debut with Philadelphia — 10 points, four rebounds, five assists in 18 minutes against Brooklyn on October 8 — gave just a glimpse of what the Big Four looks like. Brown, Embiid, Maxey, and James. Four All-Stars. A city that remembers the last time they tried to build something like this.

His first regular-season game will be at MSG. As the villain. Against the defending champions. The NBA has never been more deliberate about its own storytelling than it was when it put this game on the Opening Night slate.

The Full Tripleheader

The night opens at 3 PM ET with the Boston Celtics at the Detroit Pistons — Paul George in green, Jalen Duren on the franchise extension, a rematch of last season's first round. Then comes the Sixers-Knicks game at 7 PM. The night closes at 9:30 PM ET with the Oklahoma City Thunder visiting the San Antonio Spurs, a Western Conference Finals rematch from last June when the Spurs eliminated OKC in Game 7.

Victor Wembanyama — who signed a five-year, $252 million extension in July — hosts Shai Gilgeous-Alexander, who won his second consecutive MVP award last spring and enters 2026-27 as the favorite to win a third. It's the best young-player matchup in the game, wrapped in playoff history, on national television.

Three games. Nine hours of basketball. Every major storyline from an extraordinary offseason playing out in real time.

What's Next

Opening Night is Tuesday, October 20, on NBC. The Knicks' banner ceremony precedes the 7 PM ET tip. Tickets are scarce and expensive. For those watching from home, the evening unfolds across nearly nine hours of basketball, with a tripleheader that covers everything the 2026-27 season is about to offer.

Five days away. The season is nearly here.
