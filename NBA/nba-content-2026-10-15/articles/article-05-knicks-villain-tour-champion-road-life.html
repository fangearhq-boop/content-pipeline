After October 20, the Knicks Go on the Road. No One Is Happy to See Them.

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | October 15, 2026

New York Knicks | Jalen Brunson | NBA Champions | Season Preview

Every champion gets one night.

For the New York Knicks, it arrives October 20 at Madison Square Garden: the banner rises, the rings come out, the 53-year wait ends in celebration. Jalen Brunson — Finals MVP, 45 points in Game 5, the most celebrated player in New York since Walt Frazier — takes a bow.

After that, they go on the road.

And the road, for the defending champions, is a very different place than it used to be.

What It Means to Be Champion

The Knicks have not won an NBA championship since 1973. That drought — 53 years of near-misses, heartbreaking losses, and playoff-less seasons — made New York basketball something fans around the league could sympathize with. Even people who didn't like the Knicks could appreciate what they were going through.

That sympathy expires the moment the banner goes up.

Now, the Knicks are what every other team's fan base points to as the enemy. They're the rich franchise from the big market with the Finals MVP and the freshest hardware. Every road game in 2026-27 carries extra edge — from Brooklyn to Boston to San Antonio. The city that was once an underdog becomes the target.

Brunson and the Target on His Back

Jalen Brunson averaged 45 points per game in the 2026 NBA Finals. He made all 20 of his free throw attempts in the clinching Game 5. He hit four consecutive fourth-quarter shots to close out the Spurs and deliver the franchise its first title in his generation's lifetime.

He is, by the standards of this city, a hero. By the standards of every other city, he's the man who broke their team or their bracket or their belief that this wasn't the Knicks' year.

Every arena will have something to say about that. Brunson has already indicated he understands what comes next. The villain role doesn't scare him.

The Road Schedule

The Knicks' first 10 road games include stops against the Minnesota Timberwolves, Golden State Warriors, Phoenix Suns, and Boston Celtics. That's four of the league's most passionate home fan bases, all of which have recent grudges. The Wolves just beat the Knicks in the final preseason game, 122-104. The Celtics lost their star, Jaylen Brown, in a trade for Paul George. The Suns missed the playoffs again. The Warriors are trying to stay relevant.

All of them want to beat the team holding the trophy. All of them will have their crowds ready.

What's Next

The banner ceremony is October 20 at 7 PM ET on NBC, preceding the Sixers-Knicks opener. After that night, the Knicks' "home team" status expires until someone takes the title from them. The next marquee road game is October 25 in Boston, where the Celtics will set the tone for how the defending champions are greeted in their division. One more banner night, then the villain tour begins in full.
