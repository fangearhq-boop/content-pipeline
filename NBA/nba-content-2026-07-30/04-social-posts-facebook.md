### STORY 1: Kawhi Leonard Investigation Could Now Drag Into 2027

#### Long-Form Post — 9:00 AM ET

🚨 BREAKING: The NBA's Kawhi Leonard investigation could now drag into 2027 — and that changes everything.

Commissioner Adam Silver publicly stated the probe must conclude before the 2026-27 season begins. But multiple sources — including ESPN, HoopsHype, and Yahoo Sports — reported Wednesday that a resolution this calendar year is no longer certain.

Here's the key complication: The NBA's outside law firm has finished its fact-finding phase. But before any penalty can be imposed, the NBPA has the right under the Collective Bargaining Agreement to demand arbitration. If the league and the union can't agree on a punishment, the case goes to a neutral arbitrator — a process with its own timeline that Commissioner Silver cannot rush.

What's being investigated: The league is examining whether the Clippers used their former sponsor Aspiration Partners Inc. to funnel $28 million in compensation to Kawhi Leonard outside his NBA contract — a potential salary-cap circumvention. Aspiration's CEO has been convicted of wire fraud and sentenced to 14 years in federal prison. The Clippers claim they were victims of fraud, not co-conspirators.

Who's most affected: The Toronto Raptors. They agreed to a trade on June 30 — Leonard to Toronto in exchange for Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, multiple first-round picks, and pick swaps. The NBA has blocked that trade from closing until the investigation resolves. Toronto has been frozen for a month. Could be frozen for much longer.

This is the biggest unresolved storyline in the NBA heading into August.

### STORY 2: DeMar DeRozan Returns to Chicago — 2 Years, $30 Million

#### Long-Form Post — 11:00 AM ET

🐂 HE'S COMING HOME: DeMar DeRozan is officially returning to the Chicago Bulls.

Per league sources, DeRozan has agreed to a two-year, $30 million deal — a significantly more modest number than what was reported last week, but the destination is what always mattered most.

DeRozan, 36, was waived by the Sacramento Kings on July 6 after one-plus seasons with the team. Multiple franchises came calling: the Heat (now with Giannis), the Nuggets, the Cavaliers, and the Wizards all expressed interest. He chose Chicago.

The Bulls are where DeRozan had his best post-prime seasons — four years, four All-Star appearances, consistently playing at a 24+ point-per-game level when the team gave him the ball and trusted him to deliver. He never won a championship in Chicago, but he made the franchise relevant again at a moment when it desperately needed a reason to believe.

Now he returns under new head coach Torsten Splitter, one of six new NBA head coaches this offseason. At $15 million per year, this is a bet on what DeRozan can still provide: mid-range excellence, veteran leadership, and a commitment to a city that gave him a second act.

He could have chased a title contender. He chose Chicago instead. That tells you exactly who DeMar DeRozan is.

### STORY 5: Why Wembanyama Left $50 Million on the Table

#### Long-Form Post — 7:00 PM ET

💡 Victor Wembanyama is 22 years old and already thinking like a franchise builder.

When Wembanyama signed his rookie max extension with the San Antonio Spurs, he faced a choice: the 25% max ($252 million over five years) or the 30% supermax (approximately $303 million). The difference is $51 million. He chose the 25% max.

At 22, the youngest player to make an argument for generational greatness since LeBron James voluntarily left $51 million on the table — not because he couldn't get it, but because he understood what that money would mean for the team he intends to lead to championships.

Here's what San Antonio did with that cap flexibility: The Spurs acquired De'Aaron Fox and committed to a four-year, $228 million extension to keep him. Fox is 29, in his prime, and the playmaking partner Wembanyama has never had. San Antonio also retained Stephon Castle (20 years old, instant-offense specialist), Ron Holland (explosive wing), and Harrison Ingram (floor spacer).

No comparable example exists in the NBA's max-contract era. Not LeBron. Not Curry. Not Duncan. A 22-year-old franchise player — already described as one of the best players in the world — deliberately choosing a $51 million smaller contract to give his team a real chance to win.

Whether it pays off in championships is the question San Antonio spends the next decade answering. The intent behind it, and the maturity required to make that decision at 22, is already remarkable.
