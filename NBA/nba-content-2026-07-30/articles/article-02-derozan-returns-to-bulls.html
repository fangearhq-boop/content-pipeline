DeMar DeRozan Is Going Back to Chicago — and This Time He Chose It

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | July 30, 2026

The league sources are confirmed. DeMar DeRozan returns to the Chicago Bulls on a two-year, $30 million deal. He passed on the Heat, the Nuggets, the Cavaliers, and the Wizards to go back where he played his best basketball.

DeMar DeRozan is going home.

According to league sources, the 36-year-old guard has agreed to a two-year, $30 million contract to return to the Chicago Bulls — the franchise where he spent four seasons producing some of the best basketball of his career and rebuilding his reputation as one of the most reliable mid-range scorers of his generation.

The deal comes after a months-long sequence that began when the Sacramento Kings waived DeRozan on July 6, making him a free agent for the first time in two years. Multiple franchises came calling. The Miami Heat, now built around Giannis Antetokounmpo and Tyler Herro, expressed serious interest. So did the Denver Nuggets, the Cleveland Cavaliers, and the Washington Wizards. He had no shortage of options.

He chose Chicago.

A Reunion Built on History

DeRozan's first tenure in Chicago ran from 2021 to 2024. He arrived as a reclamation project — a player the San Antonio Spurs had shipped out as part of a rebuilding process. What he produced in Chicago was the best chapter of his later career: four consecutive All-Star selections, a scoring peak that averaged 24.5 points per game across those four seasons, and a city that embraced him as one of its own.

The Bulls made the playoffs twice during that run. They never advanced past the first round. But DeRozan's consistency — and specifically his iconic mid-range game in the fourth quarter — gave Chicago fans something to believe in during a period when the franchise had given them very little reason to believe.

When he left for Sacramento in July 2024, it was the kind of departure that stings: a business decision framed as a new opportunity, with the implicit understanding that the fit in Chicago was beginning to unravel. He played one season with the Kings. They waived him this summer. The door back to Chicago was open.

What $30 Million Says

The contract figure is notable precisely because of the gap between what was reported and what was agreed. Last week, multiple outlets referenced a figure in the $40 million per year range for DeRozan's market. The $15 million per year he actually signed for reflects either a correction to an inflated rumor, or a meaningful concession from DeRozan to get back to Chicago at any price point that made sense for the Bulls.

Either way, the number tells you something. At 36, DeRozan is not a player chasing a maximum contract. He is a player chasing relevance, competition, and a return to a city that made him feel valued. The Bulls, under new head coach Torsten Splitter, are in the early stages of a rebuild — not a championship window. DeRozan is not going to Chicago to win a title. He is going there to lead a team that needs veteran direction at a critical inflection point.

What's Next

The Bulls open the 2026-27 season with DeRozan as their most experienced offensive weapon and a signal to younger players on the roster about how to approach the game professionally. Splitter's first full season as an NBA head coach will benefit from a veteran who has seen nearly everything this league offers.

Chicago did not build a championship contender this offseason. But they kept their identity — and they brought back the player who gave that identity its sharpest definition. For a city that has spent years waiting for something to celebrate, DeRozan's return is not nothing. It's a promise that the Bulls are not giving up on the present while they plan for the future.

He chose Chicago. That matters.
