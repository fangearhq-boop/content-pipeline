The Kawhi Leonard Probe Could Now Drag Into 2027 — and the Raptors Are Stuck

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | July 30, 2026

Commissioner Silver promised the investigation would end before the season. Multiple sources now say it could extend into next year. Toronto is frozen. The trade is blocked. And the NBPA may be the reason nothing gets resolved anytime soon.

When Adam Silver spoke publicly about the NBA's investigation into Kawhi Leonard and the Los Angeles Clippers, he gave the league a timeline to hold onto: the probe must conclude before the 2026-27 season begins. That deadline sounded firm. It no longer is.

Multiple reports published Wednesday — including from ESPN, HoopsHype, and Yahoo Sports — confirm that the investigation could extend into 2027. The NBA's outside law firm has completed its fact-finding phase. But completing fact-finding is not the same as issuing a ruling. And before any penalty can be imposed, the NBA Players Association has rights under the Collective Bargaining Agreement that could delay resolution for months.

The specific mechanism is arbitration. If the NBA determines a violation occurred and proposes a punishment, the NBPA can challenge that determination and demand the case go to a neutral arbitrator — jointly appointed by the league and the union. That process unfolds on its own timeline, separate from whatever deadline the Commissioner set publicly. Silver cannot unilaterally rush it.

What the Investigation Is Actually About

The probe centers on a $28 million endorsement arrangement between Leonard and Aspiration Partners Inc., a now-bankrupt digital bank that simultaneously held a 23-year, $300 million naming rights deal with the Clippers. Investigators are examining whether Clippers ownership used the Aspiration relationship to funnel additional compensation to Leonard outside his NBA contract — a potential salary-cap circumvention under CBA rules.

The Clippers have denied wrongdoing. Leonard has denied wrongdoing. The complicating factor for both parties is that Aspiration's CEO was convicted on wire fraud charges and sentenced to 14 years in federal prison. The Clippers claim they were victims of that fraud, not co-conspirators. How the NBA weighs that argument — and how much the NBPA scrutinizes any penalty that emerges — will determine how long this takes.

Who Pays the Price

The easy answer is "the Clippers." The accurate answer is "the Toronto Raptors."

Toronto agreed to a trade on June 30 that would bring Leonard back to the franchise where he won a championship in 2019. The terms: Kawhi Leonard to Toronto in exchange for Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, unprotected first-round picks in 2031 and 2033, a 2027 first-round pick swap, and two second-round picks. The NBA has blocked the trade from closing until the investigation resolves.

That was one month ago. Toronto cannot finalize its roster. Cannot make complementary moves around a projected core that may or may not include its primary acquisition. Cannot communicate to its own players, coaches, and staff whether the direction of the franchise has fundamentally changed. The Raptors entered free agency with a plan. That plan has been on hold since July 1.

If the investigation genuinely extends into 2027, Toronto faces the possibility of entering training camp — and perhaps the entire regular season — without resolution. That is an extraordinary situation for a franchise to manage.

What's Next

The NBA has limited public options here. Silver cannot retroactively move the timeline, and demanding a faster resolution risks the legal integrity of whatever finding the league eventually issues. The NBPA's role as an institutional check on punishment is a feature of the CBA, not a loophole — the union will use it if it believes Leonard is being treated unfairly.

For the Raptors, the realistic near-term scenario involves patience they didn't sign up for. For the Clippers, it involves months of continued uncertainty about what their post-Leonard era actually looks like. And for the NBA, it involves watching the league's most significant unresolved storyline remain unresolved long after the Commissioner said it would be finished.

The probe enters its 12th month in September. The season opener is October 20. The math no longer works the way Silver described it. And Toronto — more than anyone — is feeling every day of the delay.
