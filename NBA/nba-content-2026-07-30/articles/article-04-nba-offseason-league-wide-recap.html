While the 76ers Built a Superteam, Everyone Else Was Swinging Too

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | July 30, 2026

Giannis went to Miami. LaMelo went to Minnesota. Austin Reaves turned down free agency. DeMar DeRozan went home to Chicago. The 2026 NBA offseason didn't just reshape one team — it reshaped the entire league.

The story of the 2026 NBA offseason has been told mostly through the lens of the Philadelphia 76ers. LeBron James choosing the franchise that gave him a path to compete with Joel Embiid is a narrative that consumes everything around it. But while the basketball world processed what Philadelphia was building, the rest of the league was not standing still.

This is the full picture of what actually happened — and what it means for the 2026-27 season.

The Eastern Conference Transformation

The Milwaukee Bucks traded Giannis Antetokounmpo and Bobby Portis to the Miami Heat. In exchange, Miami sent Tyler Herro, Kel'el Ware, Jaime Jaquez Jr., Kasparas Jakucionis, three first-round picks, a pick swap, and a second-round pick. For the Heat, this is the completion of a rebuild around Giannis — a two-time MVP who now joins a Miami culture that has consistently overperformed its talent level. For Milwaukee, it is the end of an era that produced one championship and multiple heartbreaking playoff exits.

Jaylen Brown moved to Philadelphia as part of the trade that brought Paul George and draft capital back to Boston. The Celtics are now rebuilding around a core that does not yet have a confirmed identity — but they have assets to reshape it. Brown, meanwhile, gets to chase a title alongside LeBron and Embiid.

DeMar DeRozan returned to the Chicago Bulls on a two-year, $30 million deal after the Sacramento Kings waived him on July 6. The Bulls enter 2026-27 with a veteran anchor and a new head coach in Torsten Splitter — one of six new NBA head coaches named this offseason.

The Western Conference Reshuffling

Ja Morant was traded from Memphis to the Portland Trail Blazers for Jerami Grant, Kris Murray, and cash. Portland's vision — a backcourt featuring Morant and Damian Lillard — is either the most exciting combination in the Western Conference or a significant injury risk given Morant's health history over the last three seasons. The answer will emerge quickly once the regular season begins.

LaMelo Ball was shipped to the Minnesota Timberwolves in a multi-team deal that sent Josh Green to Dallas and Naz Reid plus picks back to Charlotte. The Wolves, already built around Anthony Edwards and Karl-Anthony Towns, add a shot creator whose style is genuinely unlike anyone else in the league.

Austin Reaves made the statement his teammates couldn't: he declined his $14.9 million player option and signed a new four-year, $185 million deal to remain with the Lakers. The decision underlined a commitment to Los Angeles at a moment when the franchise's direction was uncertain. Reaves is the highest-paid player on a team that lost LeBron James to Philadelphia.

Victor Wembanyama signed a five-year, $252 million extension in San Antonio — the 25% max, intentionally leaving approximately $51 million on the table to give the Spurs cap flexibility to build around him. San Antonio used that flexibility to acquire and extend De'Aaron Fox ($228 million over four years), giving Wembanyama a legitimate co-star for the first time in his NBA career.

What's Next

The 2026-27 NBA schedule releases August 13-14. The regular season opens October 20. The offseason that reshaped every power structure in the league ends with one question unanswered: the Kawhi Leonard investigation, which sources now say could extend into 2027, leaving the Toronto Raptors frozen and the Clippers in organizational limbo.

Every other piece is in place. The season can't start soon enough.
