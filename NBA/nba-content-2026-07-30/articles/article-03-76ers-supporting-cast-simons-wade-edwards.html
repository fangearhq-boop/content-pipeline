The 76ers Know Superteams Are Built Beyond the Starters — And They're Acting Like It

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | July 30, 2026

LeBron, Embiid, Brown, and Maxey absorb all the headlines. But Philadelphia is quietly assembling a supporting cast built for a championship — and the details matter.

Every championship team in NBA history has been remembered by its stars. The role players who made those stars possible are the ones the box scores forget. Philadelphia knows this. The 76ers have spent this offseason proving they haven't forgotten it.

While the basketball world processed the formation of a starting five featuring LeBron James, Joel Embiid, Jaylen Brown, Tyrese Maxey, and V.J. Edgecombe — a unit carrying five MVP awards and five Finals MVP trophies — the Sixers front office was quietly constructing the roster around it. The names being added are not marquee names. But the names being added are the right names.

The Supporting Cast

Kentavious Caldwell-Pope signed first, locking in a one-year, $3.9 million deal after receiving a buyout from the Memphis Grizzlies. KCP is a two-time champion — part of the 2020 Lakers and 2023 Nuggets rosters — and spent three seasons as LeBron's teammate in Los Angeles. His role in Philadelphia requires zero adjustment: catch, shoot, defend, don't turn it over. He has done that alongside the league's best player before.

Anfernee Simons arrives from Portland, where he developed into a legitimate perimeter scoring threat capable of creating off the dribble. Simons gives the bench unit an offensive initiator who doesn't depend on the starting five to generate his shot. In a system where LeBron controls the ball and Maxey handles primary creation duties, Simons provides the second unit with an option that doesn't require anyone else to set the table.

Dean Wade joins from Cleveland, where he spent four seasons as a reliable frontcourt piece with championship-level IQ. Wade is not asked to guard the opposing team's best big. He is asked to hit open threes from the corners, set screens with purpose, and protect the ball. He does those things at a high level.

Justin Edwards, entering his third NBA season, provides the young wing depth every contender needs. Edwards is not ready for playoff minutes yet. But depth requires having players who can grow into it, and Edwards has the physical tools and work ethic to take the next step in his development under a staff with LeBron James on the bench every night, watching him practice.

What Championship Rosters Actually Require

The 2016 Warriors had Harrison Barnes. The 2017 and 2018 Warriors had Andre Iguodala. The 2020 Lakers had KCP himself. Every championship team in the modern era has leaned on players who understood their role completely and executed it without demanding more.

Philadelphia's front office appears to be building toward exactly that profile. The stars attract attention; the role players determine outcomes. The Sixers are not treating roster construction as an afterthought once the headliners were signed. They are treating it as the work that turns a great starting lineup into a championship team.

What's Next

Philadelphia still has roster spots to fill before training camp opens. Expect additional veteran minimum signings focused on players who fit the defensive profile the team needs to compete at the highest level of the Eastern Conference. The 2026-27 schedule releases August 13-14. The season opens October 20.

The Big 4 is real. The supporting cast is being built with care. What happens when the season starts will determine whether all of it was enough — but Philadelphia is making every decision as if the details matter, because they do.
