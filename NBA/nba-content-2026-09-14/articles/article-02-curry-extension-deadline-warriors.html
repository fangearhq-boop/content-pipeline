14 Days to Decide: Stephen Curry's Extension Deadline Arrives as Warriors Media Day Looms

By Marcus Cole | September 14, 2026

Warriors media day is September 28 — and it's also the unofficial deadline for the most consequential contract negotiation of this NBA offseason. Stephen Curry has a decision to make, and it will shape Golden State's roster for years.

September 28 is circled on every calendar at Chase Center.

That's Warriors media day — the date players gather for the first official team event of the 2026-27 season. It's also, according to multiple reports, the date looming largest in negotiations between the Warriors and Stephen Curry over a contract extension that remains unsigned with 14 days to go.

Curry is entering the final year of his current deal, which pays him approximately $62.5 million for the coming season. He's eligible for a two-year, $137 million extension. Both sides have expressed mutual interest. No deal has been struck.

The Cap Math Complication

The extension calculus is not simple. If Curry signs the full $137 million deal, it roughly cuts the Warriors' projected $130 million-plus cap space in half heading into the following offseason. That's $65 million or more the Warriors would no longer have to chase free agents or make trades. For a franchise already navigating a transitional roster, that's a significant constraint.

If Curry takes a discount — a number meaningfully below $137 million — the Warriors gain flexibility. They could use that space to add a co-star or deepen the roster around him for what many presume is his final contract. The question is whether Curry wants to leave money on the table for a franchise that hasn't won a championship since 2022.

There's precedent for players taking team-friendly extensions late in their careers, and precedent for players declining to. Neither outcome would be surprising.

What "Mutual Interest" Actually Means

The phrase "mutual interest" is doing a lot of work in the reporting on this situation. It means neither side has walked away from the table. It does not mean a deal is imminent or inevitable. Contract negotiations between a player of Curry's stature and a franchise like the Warriors involve layers of agent strategy, marketing considerations, and legacy positioning that go far beyond the dollar amount.

What is clear: Curry, who turns 38 this season, remains one of the best shooters — and one of the most marketable athletes — on the planet. His ability to play at an elite level gives him leverage. His genuine affection for the Warriors organization and the Bay Area gives him reason to take less. Both things are true simultaneously.

The Bigger Picture

This negotiation isn't just about two years and $137 million. It's about how Curry's career ends. A deal signed now locks him in through the 2028-29 season, when he'll be 40 or 41. That's a significant commitment for both sides.

The Warriors, for their part, are threading a needle: keeping the greatest player in franchise history happy and on the roster while also positioning themselves to compete in a Western Conference that includes OKC's Shai Gilgeous-Alexander, Denver's Nikola Jokic, and Portland's Damian Lillard-Ja Morant backcourt. A cap-hamstrung Warriors team with an aging Curry cannot win a title. A Warriors team with Curry playing at his current level, surrounded by the right pieces, absolutely can.

Fourteen days will answer a lot of questions.

What's Next

If no extension is signed by September 28, Curry will play out the final year of his current deal. He would then become an unrestricted free agent in the summer of 2027 — free to sign with any team in the league. That scenario is not in anyone's stated interest, but it remains possible if the two sides cannot bridge the gap on terms.

Training camp opens September 29 — the day after media day. By then, either the Warriors will have locked up their franchise icon for two more years, or the most consequential negotiation of this offseason will have carried over into the season itself. Watch September 28 closely.
