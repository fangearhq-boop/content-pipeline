DOJ Probe Widens: Daktronics Subpoenaed as Clippers Criminal Investigation Deepens

By Jake Torres | September 14, 2026

Federal prosecutors have reached Daktronics — the scoreboard manufacturer at the center of the Clippers' alleged kickback scheme — widening the criminal probe beyond team ownership and into the financial chain that allegedly funded Kawhi Leonard's no-show endorsement deal.

The federal investigation into the Los Angeles Clippers just got bigger.

The Department of Justice's criminal probe — led by the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Eastern District of New York — has expanded to include contact with Daktronics, the South Dakota-based scoreboard manufacturer whose contract with the Clippers is central to the alleged kickback scheme. At least one subpoena has already been issued in the investigation. Together, these developments paint a picture of federal prosecutors building out a full financial picture of the alleged scheme rather than zeroing in on any single target.

The core allegation: the Clippers used a massive scoreboard contract with Daktronics as a vehicle to fund endorsement deals for Kawhi Leonard that went far beyond his NBA contract — effectively circumventing the league's salary cap rules. Separately, Clippers owner Steve Ballmer invested $50 million in Aspiration, a green banking company that in turn paid Leonard a reported $28 million endorsement deal for work investigators say he never performed.

What the NBA Already Did

The NBA levied its largest punishment in league history when its own investigation concluded. The Clippers lost five first-round draft picks — 2029 through 2033 — and were fined $30 million. Ballmer is suspended for one year from all team and league activities. General manager Lawrence Frank received a six-month suspension. Executive Gillian Zucker was suspended for one year.

On the player side, Kawhi Leonard was fined $700,000. His business manager and uncle, Dennis Robertson, was banned from conducting NBA business for five years. The league found that Leonard benefited from the arrangement but did not characterize him as its architect.

Why Daktronics Changes Everything

The addition of Daktronics to the federal inquiry matters for one key reason: it creates another node of potential cooperating witnesses and documentary evidence outside Clippers ownership. A company that signed a contract with the Clippers, received payment, and whose deal is alleged to have been structured to benefit a player has its own executives, accountants, and records.

Federal investigators typically don't subpoena third parties unless they're trying to corroborate a financial chain. The presence of Daktronics in the probe suggests prosecutors are building toward a documentary case — paper, emails, contracts — rather than relying solely on witness testimony from inside the Clippers organization.

Potential charges in a case like this could include wire fraud, conspiracy to commit fraud, or obstruction, depending on what investigators find. None of those charges have been filed. The probe is described as being in its "earliest stages."

Where Kawhi Leonard Stands

As of this writing, Kawhi Leonard faces no reported criminal scrutiny. He is characterized in reporting as a beneficiary of the alleged scheme, not its architect. His NBA fine of $700,000 has been paid. His trade to the Toronto Raptors was ratified by the league alongside its penalty announcement.

Raptors training camp opens September 28 in Quebec City. Leonard — who won Toronto's only NBA championship in 2019 before leaving in free agency — is returning to the city where he became one of the most beloved figures in franchise history. His first official workout as a Raptor is coming, even as the legal cloud over his former team continues to grow.

What's Next

The DOJ's timeline is unpredictable. Federal investigations of this complexity rarely move on a sports calendar, and prosecutors typically don't provide updates until they file charges or close investigations. Any developments are likely to come through reporting rather than official channels.

For the Clippers, the practical reality is bleak: no first-round picks until 2034, $30 million gone, their owner sidelined, and a federal investigation ongoing. For the NBA, this is unmapped territory — a franchise under active criminal investigation while its season is scheduled to tip off October 20.

The league, and the country, will be watching.
