Ring Hangover or Ready to Repeat? Three Wild Cards That Will Define the Knicks' Training Camp

By Marcus Cole | September 14, 2026

The defending champion Knicks open training camp September 29 with most of their title roster intact. But three unresolved questions could determine whether New York repeats — or stumbles in the defense of its crown.

Being the defending champion in the NBA is a peculiar burden.

Every team in the league spent the summer studying the New York Knicks. Every coaching staff has a plan for Jalen Brunson. Every GM made at least one move designed to close the gap. And when Brunson walks into New York's training facility on September 29, he'll carry the weight of a championship ring alongside the expectation of winning another.

The Knicks enter camp in a stronger position than most defending champions. Brunson, Karl-Anthony Towns, OG Anunoby, and Josh Hart are all back. The system under Tom Thibodeau is mature and battle-tested. But three wild cards — if not addressed in camp — could complicate what everyone assumes will be a smooth title defense.

Wild Card No. 1: Can Andre Drummond Replace Mitchell Robinson?

Mitchell Robinson was not the most glamorous player on the Knicks' championship roster. He didn't score. He didn't create offense. What he did do was anchor the paint defensively with a physicality and verticality that complemented Thibodeau's defensive system perfectly.

Andre Drummond is a different kind of big. He's a more accomplished rebounder — one of the best in the league when fully motivated. He can score more around the basket. He's also a different defensive profile: more of a body, less of a rim-protector in the Robinson sense.

How Thibodeau integrates Drummond's strengths while compensating for the gap Robinson leaves on the defensive end is the most important personnel question the Knicks face in camp. Get it right, and Drummond is an upgrade. Get it wrong, and the frontline that made the Knicks a champion is weakened at a critical moment.

Wild Card No. 2: Who Wins the Final Roster Spots?

The Knicks' top eight or nine players are set. But the back end of the roster — the players who fill out the 15-man group and take on two-way contracts — remains genuinely competitive. Mo Diawara locked up his spot with a four-year deal. Igor Milicic Jr. is on an Exhibit 10 contract, fighting for a two-way spot or a final roster position.

In a season where depth matters — the Knicks will be playing 34 national TV games and carrying championship-level expectations every night — the guys at the end of the bench matter more than usual. Thibodeau's ability to identify and develop fringe players has always been a strength. Training camp is where that process begins.

Wild Card No. 3: Can This Team Find Its Hunger Again?

The most important wild card isn't about personnel. It's about motivation. The Knicks built their 2026 championship run on an identity: overlooked, underestimated, playing with an edge that the city needed. That chip-on-the-shoulder mentality produced a Finals MVP performance from Brunson and a defensive intensity that made New York nearly impossible to beat in a seven-game series.

Now they're the hunted. They're the team everyone else wants to beat. The chip has been replaced by a crown, and crowns are heavy. Every great defending champion in NBA history found a way to manufacture new motivation — whether from outside disrespect, internal competition, or the simple desire to validate the first ring by winning another.

Whether Brunson, Thibodeau, and the Knicks core can find that edge again will be the defining storyline of the entire 2026-27 season.

What's Next

Training camp opens September 29. The Knicks' first preseason game is against LeBron James and the 76ers at home on October 5 — which doubles as the first real test of how this team handles its new status as a championship target.

Opening Night is October 20 at Madison Square Garden: ring ceremony first, then the 76ers arrive to try to spoil it. If the Knicks can answer the three wild cards in camp and come out of preseason looking like a team still hungry for more, they remain the team to beat in the Eastern Conference. If the ring hangover is real, the challengers are already loading up.
