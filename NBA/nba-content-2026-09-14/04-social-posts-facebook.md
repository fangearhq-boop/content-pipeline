### STORY 1: DOJ Criminal Probe — Long-Form

The Clippers cap scandal is now a full federal criminal investigation — and it just got wider.

The Department of Justice's probe into the Los Angeles Clippers isn't just about Kawhi Leonard's $28 million Aspiration endorsement deal anymore. According to reporting from multiple outlets, DOJ prosecutors have also been in contact with Daktronics, the South Dakota-based scoreboard manufacturer whose contract with the Clippers is at the center of the alleged kickback scheme.

Here's what that means: federal investigators are now looking at the full financial chain — from Clippers ownership to the scoreboard company to the endorsement deals that allegedly benefited Kawhi Leonard far beyond his NBA contract.

The NBA already dropped the largest punishment in league history: five first-round picks (2029–2033), a $30 million fine, one-year suspensions for owner Steve Ballmer and executive Gillian Zucker, a six-month suspension for GM Lawrence Frank, a $700,000 fine for Leonard, and a five-year ban from NBA business for Leonard's adviser Dennis Robertson.

The DOJ can do far worse.

Wire fraud. Conspiracy. Obstruction. These are the charges that could theoretically apply if prosecutors build their case. The probe is described as being in its "earliest stages" — but the fact that a subpoena has already been issued signals this is moving forward, not stalling.

For Kawhi Leonard personally, there's no reported criminal scrutiny — he's a beneficiary, not an architect, of the alleged scheme. His trade to Toronto has been ratified. Raptors camp opens September 28. He's going back to the city where he became a legend.

But the shadow of a federal investigation will follow this story all season long. And with Daktronics now in the picture, there are more potential cooperating witnesses — and more financial threads to pull.

This one is far from over.

### STORY 2: Stephen Curry Extension — Long-Form

Fourteen days. That's how long Stephen Curry has before the most important contract deadline of the NBA offseason.

September 28 is Warriors media day — and it's also the date that looms largest in the Curry extension negotiations. Curry is entering the final year of his current deal, worth $62.5 million for the 2026-27 season. He's eligible for a 2-year, $137 million extension. Both sides have reported mutual interest. But no deal has been signed.

The cap math is complicated. If Curry takes the full $137 million extension, it essentially cuts the Warriors' projected $130 million-plus cap space in half going into the following offseason. That limits Golden State's ability to add talent around Curry in free agency. If he takes a discount, the Warriors have more flexibility — but Curry's earning power is real and he's earned every dollar.

Here's the deeper question: is this the last contract negotiation of Curry's career with Golden State?

He's entering a season where he'll be 38 years old. He still plays at an elite level. But the Warriors are at a crossroads — rebuilding around their franchise icon while trying to remain competitive in the Western Conference. The extension isn't just about money. It's about Curry's vision for his final NBA chapter.

Media day is September 28. Camp opens September 29. By the time players start running three-man weaves at the Warriors' facility, we'll know whether the greatest shooter in NBA history has decided to see this through in Golden State.

### STORY 3: Jalen Duren QO — Long-Form

The Detroit Pistons and Jalen Duren are $10 million apart on a contract extension — and the clock is running out.

Here's where things stand: Detroit has offered a five-year deal in the neighborhood of $190 million. Duren and his camp want north of $200 million, essentially the full max. Neither side has moved. The qualifying offer deadline is October 1 — just 17 days away.

If no extension is signed by then, Duren can accept the qualifying offer and play out the 2026-27 season on a one-year, $9.6 million deal. At that point, he becomes an unrestricted free agent — meaning Detroit loses its exclusive right to match outside offers. Duren could sign with any team next summer.

For Duren, it's a calculated risk. He's confident in his ability to perform and earn a bigger payday as a UFA. The one-year "bet on yourself" approach has paid off for plenty of NBA players.

For the Pistons, the math is brutal. If Duren walks, they lose one of the best young centers in basketball without getting back any assets. Detroit's rebuild has been building toward a core of Duren and the players around him. Letting him play out the season on $9.6 million — and then watching him bolt — would be a catastrophic outcome for a franchise that has worked hard to get to this moment.

Seventeen days. One $10 million gap. The pressure is mounting.

### STORY 5: Knicks Training Camp — Long-Form

The New York Knicks are NBA champions. Their core is intact. And their training camp — opening September 29 — carries a very different energy than every other camp across the league.

Champions don't get to sneak up on anyone. They don't get to fly under the radar. Every team in the NBA spent the summer studying the Knicks, watching their film, drafting their blueprint for stopping Jalen Brunson.

So as New York heads into camp, three wild cards will determine whether this group has what it takes to repeat.

Wild Card No. 1: Andre Drummond replacing Mitchell Robinson. Robinson was a defensive anchor for the Knicks — not a scorer, but a rim protector who fit perfectly into the system. Drummond is a different player: bigger rebounder, different defensive footprint. How quickly Tom Thibodeau finds the right minutes and role for him matters enormously.

Wild Card No. 2: The back-end of the roster. Mo Diawara signed a four-year deal and secured his spot. But the final two or three roster positions are genuinely competitive. Igor Milicic Jr. is on an Exhibit 10 deal. Two-way contract spots are open. Thibodeau will need to find the right role players to handle the increased attention the champions will receive.

Wild Card No. 3: Hunger. It sounds clichéd, but defending a championship requires a different kind of motivation. The Knicks built their 2026 run on a chip-on-their-shoulder mentality. Now they ARE the shoulder everyone else has a chip on. Can Brunson, Towns, and company manufacture that same edge?

Camp opens September 29. The answers — and the Knicks' chances of a repeat — will start becoming clear very quickly.
