The Giannis Era Is Over in Milwaukee — Now What?

By Marcus Cole | October 7, 2026

For the first time since 2021, the Milwaukee Bucks will open a new season without Giannis Antetokounmpo on their roster. Tonight at 8 PM ET, when Milwaukee tips off their 2026-27 preseason against the Oklahoma City Thunder, it will be the first official look at what the Bucks are becoming — rather than what they used to be.

Giannis is in Miami now. He was traded to the Heat in a blockbuster deal that brought back five draft picks and four players, ending one of the most remarkable individual runs in Bucks franchise history. The two-time MVP, the 2021 championship centerpiece, the player who turned Milwaukee into a perennial contender — he is putting on a different uniform this season.

So what do the Bucks do now?

What the Bucks Got in Return

Milwaukee received five draft picks and four players in the Giannis trade. The exact composition of that haul will shape what kind of rebuild this is. If the picks are unprotected and early, the Bucks have the raw materials for a traditional rebuild — tank, draft well, develop, and compete again in three to five years. If they are mid-round or swap-only, the path back to relevance is longer and harder.

What is clear is that Milwaukee is not in a position to compete for a championship in 2026-27. The talent gap between a Giannis-less Bucks team and the top tier of the Eastern Conference — the Knicks, the 76ers, the Celtics — is significant. Tonight's opponent illustrates the point: the Oklahoma City Thunder are among the West's elite, and they will give Milwaukee a very clear picture of where they stand.

The Thunder as a Measuring Stick

OKC is playing in a West Finals rematch on NBA opening night against the San Antonio Spurs — a sign of how seriously the league takes them as a contender. The Thunder's core is young, athletic, and deep, and they have been one of the league's most consistent teams for several years.

Playing them in Week 1 of preseason is not the ideal measuring stick for a rebuilding team. But it does serve a purpose: it tells the Bucks — and the league — where the baseline is. A competitive performance means Milwaukee still has useful pieces. A lopsided result means the rebuild is deeper than hoped.

The Legacy of Giannis in Milwaukee

It would be wrong to say goodbye to the Giannis era without acknowledging what it was. Giannis Antetokounmpo arrived in Milwaukee as a teenager with barely any basketball pedigree and became one of the greatest players in NBA history. He won back-to-back MVP awards. He won a championship in 2021, took the Finals MVP, and did it while carrying a franchise that had not won a title since 1971 — 50 years of waiting, answered by a 26-year-old who grew up in Athens, Greece.

That run is over now. The Bucks gave him the best offer they could, and Giannis decided his path to another championship ran through Miami. That is his right, and it is hard to argue the math. The Heat assembled a roster around him specifically designed to win.

In Milwaukee, the task now is different: build something worth rooting for again from the ground up.

What's Next

Tonight's preseason opener is the first data point in a long process. The Bucks will use the preseason to evaluate roster pieces acquired in the Giannis trade, identify which players from their existing core are worth building around, and get a read on their developmental trajectory. The full story of what Milwaukee becomes post-Giannis will take multiple seasons to tell. Tonight is just the first page.
