LaMelo Ball Makes His Timberwolves Debut Tonight — Can He and Edwards Become the West's Next Great Duo?

By Damon Pierce | October 7, 2026

When LaMelo Ball and Josh Green were formally introduced as Minnesota Timberwolves back in July, the arena buzzed with the kind of energy that comes with a genuinely exciting roster overhaul. Tonight, at 7 PM ET against the Indiana Pacers, that excitement gets its first real test.

LaMelo Ball makes his Minnesota Timberwolves preseason debut, pairing up with Anthony Edwards for the first competitive look at what could become one of the NBA's most dynamic backcourts. The question is no longer whether this pairing can work. Tonight, the Wolves start finding out whether it actually does.

What Minnesota Is Getting in LaMelo

Ball averaged 20.1 points, 7.1 assists, and 4.8 rebounds per game last season with the Charlotte Hornets. He is a playmaker first — a guard with an unusually advanced feel for the game, capable of creating for himself and others and pulling up from range with confidence. He draws defenders into difficult rotations and rarely forces the issue. For a team that has Anthony Edwards as a primary scorer and closer, having a second creator who can keep defenses honest is exactly the kind of addition that changes a ceiling.

Minnesota gave up real assets to get Ball: Naz Reid, an unprotected 2033 first-round pick, three first-round pick swaps, and three second-round picks. That is a significant price. But the Timberwolves clearly believe they are in their contention window, and they went out and added a player whose skill set is specifically designed to complement Edwards.

What Edwards Brings to the Pairing

Anthony Edwards has been one of the most unstoppable scorers in the Western Conference. His combination of athleticism, shot creation, and finishing ability makes him a nightmare for opposing defenses — but historically, he has often had to manufacture his own shots without a consistent co-star to draw defensive attention. Ball changes that equation entirely.

With LaMelo setting the table in pick-and-roll, in transition, and from the mid-range, defenses now have two players to account for simultaneously. That is the kind of dynamic that turns a very good team into a championship contender, and it is something Minnesota has not had in recent seasons.

What to Watch Tonight

This is a preseason game, so the numbers matter less than the feel. The things to watch tonight are the small stuff: do Ball and Edwards play comfortably in the same lineups? How do they share the ball in pick-and-roll sets? Does Ball's style — often reliant on slow-it-down, probe-and-react playmaking — create any friction with Edwards' preference for pushing pace?

If they look comfortable tonight, even in a short preseason stint, that should encourage Minnesota fans. If there are rough spots — and there will be some — that is also expected in Game 1. The arc that matters runs from October through June.

What's Next

Ball and the Timberwolves will have multiple preseason outings to develop chemistry before the regular season begins October 20. Their most anticipated matchup of the preseason will come when LaMelo faces Giannis Antetokounmpo's Miami Heat for the first time — a loaded game that pits two of the biggest trade destinations of the summer against each other. But that comes later. Tonight, the focus is on the first chapter of a partnership that Minnesota is betting its future on.
