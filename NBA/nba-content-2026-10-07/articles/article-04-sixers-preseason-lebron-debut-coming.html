Brown's Hot Start Is the Opening Act — The 76ers' Main Event Is Still Coming

By Jake Torres | October 7, 2026

What Jaylen Brown did in the Philadelphia 76ers' preseason opener tells you almost everything you need to know about this team — except for the parts that matter most.

Brown scored 22 points on 8-of-13 shooting and 2-of-3 from three in just 17 minutes, looking immediately comfortable in the Sixers' system as Philadelphia routed the New York Knicks 120-97 on October 5. He made it look easy. He found his spots, attacked closeouts, and never forced the issue. For a player who joined this team just weeks ago, it was an encouraging opening statement.

But LeBron James did not play. Neither did Joel Embiid. Nor Tyrese Maxey.

Philadelphia's real starting lineup has yet to take the court together. That is both a practical reminder — this was a preseason game managed for load purposes — and the most compelling storyline in basketball right now.

What Brown's Performance Tells You

A lot, actually. The concern heading into this offseason was always whether Brown — used to being a No. 1 option in Boston — could fit alongside three other star players without getting lost in the shuffle. The preseason opener gave an early answer: he does not need the ball in his hands constantly to be effective. He moves without the ball, gets to his spots, and is efficient enough that when he does touch it, he makes the most of it.

Brown's 22 points in 17 minutes represents elite production. If he approaches that output in a reduced role — 25 to 30 minutes per night as the No. 3 or No. 4 option — the Sixers are going to be genuinely difficult to guard.

The LeBron Question

The elephant in the room, of course, is LeBron James. The 41-year-old signed with Philadelphia on a two-year deal after the Sixers' offseason overhaul, and his debut in a 76ers uniform is the most anticipated moment of the preseason. Whenever it happens, it will be broadcast everywhere and watched by everyone.

The Sixers' title odds improved from +2000 to +900 after LeBron's signing. That is a significant market shift — the kind you see only when smart money decides a team is serious. Philadelphia is scheduled to play in 30 national TV games in 2026-27, which speaks to the league's belief that the Sixers will be appointment television all season.

But LeBron cannot contribute sitting on the bench in preseason. His arrival in game action is the signal that Philadelphia is fully operational.

Opening Night Is the Real Premiere

The Sixers open the regular season October 20 at Madison Square Garden against the defending champion Knicks — the same team they will be playing to overcome all season in the Eastern Conference. The Knicks swept Philadelphia 4-0 in last year's Eastern Conference semifinals; now Philadelphia walks back into that building with a completely transformed roster.

LeBron James making his first competitive appearance as a Sixer — in New York, on opening night, against the team that eliminated them — is the kind of story the NBA was built to tell. Philadelphia has 13 days to get their stars ready. Based on what Brown showed in the opener, the supporting cast is more than capable of carrying the load until the main event arrives.

What's Next

The Sixers continue their preseason schedule with multiple games before October 20. The next time LeBron suits up will be one of the most-watched preseason moments in years — and when he, Embiid, Brown, and Maxey finally share the court together, that will be the first true preview of what Philadelphia could do in a playoff series. For now, Brown has done his part. The rest of the cast is warming up.
