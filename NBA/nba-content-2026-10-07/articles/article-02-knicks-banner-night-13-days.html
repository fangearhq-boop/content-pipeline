Knicks Banner Night Is 13 Days Away — and the NBA Has Never Hyped an Opener Like This

By Marcus Cole | October 7, 2026

Thirteen days. That is all that stands between New York Knicks fans and the moment they have waited 53 years to see: a championship banner rising to the Madison Square Garden rafters.

October 20 is NBA opening night, and it comes packaged with more story than any regular-season opener in recent memory. The Knicks will raise their first championship banner since 1973 at 6:30 PM ET before tipping off against the Philadelphia 76ers at 7 PM ET on NBC and Peacock. And the Sixers are not the same team that got swept 4-0 in the Eastern Conference semifinals last spring. They are now led by LeBron James.

Why This Game Is Different

The NBA has scheduled plenty of hyped opening nights over the years, but the combination of factors in play on October 20 is genuinely rare. You have a championship banner ceremony for the first time in 53 years. You have a revenge narrative — the Knicks ended Philadelphia's season last year, and Philly went out and signed LeBron specifically to build a team capable of beating New York. And you have the most storied arena in basketball, packed with fans who have spent a lifetime waiting for this night.

Secondary market ticket prices reflect the demand. The average secondary market price has held around $2,560, and the cheapest tickets available still top $1,000. That is not a preseason game; that is an event.

The Storylines Stacking Up

Start with the banner itself. The Knicks won their first NBA championship since 1973 this past June, defeating the San Antonio Spurs in five games. Finals MVP Jalen Brunson led the charge. For a city and a fanbase that has endured decades of near-misses, rebuilds, and disappointments, this ceremony is a catharsis. It will not be quick, and it will not be quiet.

Then the 76ers step onto the court. Philadelphia went out and built one of the most star-studded rosters in the league, adding LeBron James on a two-year deal alongside All-NBA wing Jaylen Brown and retaining Joel Embiid and Tyrese Maxey. The Sixers' title odds improved from +2000 to +900 after LeBron's signing — a genuine contender walking into MSG on a mission.

The Knicks swept this Sixers team last spring without LeBron. This is the rematch, with the full cast, and everyone knows it.

The Weight of 53 Years

For context on why October 20 matters beyond basketball: the Knicks last won an NBA championship in 1973. Players from that team are now in their seventies. Generations of New York fans grew up, raised families, and in some cases passed away without ever seeing their team win a title. The banner going up is not just a ceremony — it is the end of a half-century of waiting.

The NBA understood what they had when they scheduled this game. Opening the season with the Knicks banner night against the LeBron-led 76ers is not an accident. It is the league putting its marquee matchup on its biggest stage for opening night on a broadcast network.

What's Next

The Knicks will continue their preseason in the coming days, hosting Washington on October 8. The 76ers are also still working out their rotation — LeBron James and Joel Embiid have yet to play in preseason action, meaning the full Sixers lineup has not been tested together yet. Opening night on October 20 could genuinely be the first time New York fans see what Philadelphia's "super team" looks like in a live setting. That alone makes this the most compelling first game of an NBA regular season in years.
