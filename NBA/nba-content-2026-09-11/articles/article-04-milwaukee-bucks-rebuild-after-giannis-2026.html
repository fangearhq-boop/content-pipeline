The Milwaukee Bucks Enter Their First Training Camp Without Giannis in 12 Years

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | September 11, 2026

After trading Giannis Antetokounmpo to the Miami Heat in June, the Bucks open a new chapter this fall built around Tyler Herro, young prospects, and a significant package of future draft picks.

Giannis Antetokounmpo was drafted by the Milwaukee Bucks in 2013. He was 18 years old, a raw teenage prospect from Athens with an unusual wingspan and a growth trajectory nobody could fully predict. Thirteen years later, he delivered the franchise's first championship in five decades, won back-to-back MVP awards, and became the defining player of a generation in Wisconsin.

This fall, for the first time since that 2013 draft, Giannis will not be in a Bucks uniform when training camp opens.

What Milwaukee Received

The trade — completed in June — sent Giannis Antetokounmpo and Bobby Portis to the Miami Heat in exchange for Tyler Herro, Kel'el Ware, Jaime Jaquez Jr., Kasparas Jakucionis, three first-round picks (including No. 13 in the 2026 draft), a second-round pick, and a pick swap. For a franchise in full rebuild mode, the return is meaningful: a proven scorer in Herro, a developmental big in Ware, and enough draft capital to shape the roster for multiple years.

Tyler Herro, who averaged 21.9 points per game in his best Miami seasons before the trade, becomes the new face of the franchise by default — the player most fans in Milwaukee will attach to as the bridge between the Giannis era and whatever comes next. At 26 years old, Herro has real scoring ability and the kind of shot creation at the guard spot that can anchor an offense even during a transitional period.

Doc Rivers and the Blank Page

Head coach Doc Rivers takes over a Bucks roster that has been almost completely reconstituted. He is coaching a team that, in some ways, he is meeting for the first time — young players who did not come up in the Giannis system, draft picks who are just learning what professional basketball demands, and a culture that has to be rebuilt from the baseline after a decade of operating at championship expectations.

Rivers has experience with exactly this kind of transition. He has coached rebuilds and retooling projects across multiple franchises. The approach that tends to work in early rebuild years — getting young players real minutes in high-pressure game situations, establishing a defensive identity, and managing the psychological challenge of losing more than winning — is precisely the mandate in Milwaukee this fall.

The Bucks will not be competing for a championship in 2026-27. That is not defeatism; it is realism about the math of where they are. What they can do is develop Kel'el Ware, find a role that maximizes Herro's scoring, assess what they have in Jaime Jaquez Jr., and make the kind of hard evaluations about the young roster that only real game minutes can produce.

What the Draft Capital Means

The most valuable asset in the return package might be the draft picks. Three first-rounders give the Bucks meaningful upside in the lottery. How valuable those picks become depends on how long the rebuild takes and what their draft position looks like when those selections arrive in 2031 and 2033 — years when Herro will be 31 and 33 and the trajectory of this rebuild will be long since determined.

The best rebuild stories in the NBA follow a consistent structure: build the asset base, develop players in real situations, find the next cornerstone in the draft. Whether the Bucks execute that well is the question that will define the next five years in Milwaukee.

What's Next

Training camp this fall is the honest starting point. It will be the first real accounting of what this Bucks team can do without Giannis, without championship pressure, and with a new identity still being constructed. Tyler Herro's performance will be the most watched storyline entering camp — whether he can be a genuine No. 1 option on a developing team is the foundational question for Milwaukee's immediate future.
