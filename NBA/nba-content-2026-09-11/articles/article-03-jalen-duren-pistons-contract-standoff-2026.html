Jalen Duren and the Pistons Are Still at an Impasse — and Camp Opens September 29

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | September 11, 2026

With training camp 18 days away, the Detroit Pistons and Jalen Duren remain separated by a reported $5 million per year gap, and Duren is reportedly considering signing his qualifying offer if no long-term deal is reached.

The number at the center of the Detroit Pistons' most pressing offseason business is deceptively simple: five million dollars a year. The Pistons are offering Jalen Duren roughly $35 million annually on a five-year extension. Duren and his camp want something above $40 million per season. The gap between those positions represents $25 million in total contract value over the life of the deal — real money by any measure, and a gap that has not meaningfully closed since negotiations began in the summer.

Training camp opens September 29. Eighteen days from now. And nobody appears to be moving.

Why the Number Matters So Much for Both Sides

Jalen Duren is 22 years old and already one of the most physically dominant centers in the Eastern Conference. He is a legitimate rim-protecting presence, an elite rebounder, and a player whose development trajectory points toward a franchise cornerstone classification. From Duren's perspective, asking for $40 million per year for a player of his defensive impact and age is not an unreasonable market calibration — particularly in an era where the salary cap continues to rise and centers with genuine two-way ability command premium rates.

From the Pistons' side, $35 million per year over five years is $175 million in committed money to a 22-year-old center who has not yet made an All-Star team. The Detroit front office is not wrong to consider that an enormous financial commitment for a player who is still in development, and the gap between what he is and what he might become is the precise source of the disagreement.

Both positions are defensible. Neither side appears ready to move significantly. And that is a problem with a ticking clock attached to it.

The Qualifying Offer Scenario

The most significant development in reports out of Detroit is Duren's camp reportedly considering the qualifying offer. This is not a dramatic move — it is a calculated one. By signing the QO, Duren would fulfill his existing contract obligation for 2026-27, play out the season, and then become a restricted free agent after the year ends. That means any team could offer him a contract in the summer of 2027, and the Pistons would have the right to match.

From Duren's perspective, the QO scenario has appeal: he plays on his timeline, demonstrates his value in a full season, and potentially commands a higher price from multiple suitors in 2027 than the Pistons are currently willing to pay. From the Pistons' perspective, it creates a year of uncertainty about their most important young asset, potentially depresses their ability to plan the roster around him long-term, and risks losing him in 2027 if they cannot match a competitive offer.

Detroit remains the favorite to keep Duren in any scenario. The franchise's genuine interest in building around him has been consistent and public. But the QO path is more expensive and more complicated than simply agreeing on an extension now. The Pistons would prefer to close this before camp.

What's Next

The most pressing question is whether either side moves before September 29. A deal done in the next two weeks keeps the Pistons' rebuild on the clearest possible trajectory. A camp that opens without an extension would not immediately end the relationship, but it would add a layer of uncertainty to a franchise that is trying to build stability around a young core.
