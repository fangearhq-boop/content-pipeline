Kawhi Leonard's Trade to Toronto Is Still Not Official — and Now It's 17 Days to Raptors Camp

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | September 11, 2026

The NBA's investigation into the Kawhi Leonard trade continues without a public timeline, while Leonard works out with Raptors players in Miami and training camp in Quebec City is 17 days away.

The countdown has moved from 18 to 17. The investigation has not moved at all.

As of Friday morning, Kawhi Leonard remains, on the official league ledger, a member of the Los Angeles Clippers. The NBA has not ratified his trade to the Toronto Raptors. The league has provided no public timeline for when it expects to conclude its review. And the Raptors open training camp in Quebec City on September 28.

Seventeen days. The math is no longer abstract.

What the Investigation Is About

The NBA's review centers on an alleged undisclosed sponsorship arrangement between Kawhi Leonard and Aspiration, a financial services company that was previously a Clippers partner. The league's collective bargaining agreement requires that all compensation-related arrangements involving players be disclosed. Any arrangement that bypasses that requirement is treated as a serious violation of the CBA — the kind of side agreement that could affect cap accounting and the competitive integrity of contracts.

Clippers owner Steve Ballmer and team president Lawrence Frank have already received suspensions in connection with the investigation's findings. That disciplinary action indicates the league found sufficient evidence of organizational misconduct. What remains unresolved is what that finding means for the trade itself. The NBA has the power to approve the deal despite the prior violations, require modifications to the terms, or in extraordinary circumstances decline to ratify it — though the last outcome would be historically unusual given that both franchises have been planning around the deal for more than two months.

Leonard Is Already Acting Like a Raptor

The formal reality and the practical reality are increasingly divergent. Kawhi Leonard flew to Miami to work out alongside Raptors players at an informal gathering that has been running for several days. He is not constrained in individual workouts — the trade restriction prevents only official team activities, and an informal session in Miami does not meet that threshold. He is preparing for the season as though the Raptors are his team, because by every reasonable expectation they are.

When reporters caught up with Leonard in Miami on Tuesday, he was characteristically brief but unusually direct. "I hope so...we gonna win," he said when asked about coming to Toronto. It is a sentence that sounds very much like a declaration from a man who considers the matter settled and is impatient for the process to match his reality.

The trade terms, when and if ratified, would send Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, unprotected first-round picks in 2031 and 2033, second-round picks in 2030 and 2033, and a first-round pick swap in 2027 to Los Angeles. Toronto would receive a player who delivered their only championship in 2019 and is reportedly motivated to return to the city where that happened.

The Clock Has Operational Consequences

Training camp is not just a logistical deadline — it is the beginning of the competitive process that determines how a team will function in October. Roster construction, system installation, chemistry development, and conditioning programs all begin in camp. If Leonard is not officially a Raptor when camp opens September 28, he cannot participate in any of that with the team. He would be practicing separately, staying in shape, and waiting — while his new teammates run plays he is not a part of.

Raptors president Masai Ujiri has publicly expressed optimism that the trade will clear before camp. Multiple league sources have indicated that timeline is realistic. The NBA typically moves to resolve investigations before they disrupt operational planning for multiple franchises. But this investigation has already exceeded the timeframe most observers anticipated when it began, and the league will not rush its process for anyone's training camp schedule.

What's Next

Any NBA statement or signal regarding the investigation's timeline is the immediate thing to monitor. The closer the calendar gets to September 28 without ratification, the louder the questions become — both about the investigation's scope and about what happens logistically if a Raptor who cannot practice with his team shows up to Quebec City.
