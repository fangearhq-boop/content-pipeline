Fred VanVleet Opts In for $25 Million — Houston Is Betting on a 32-Year-Old ACL Comeback

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | September 11, 2026

After missing the entire 2025-26 season with a torn ACL suffered in an offseason workout, Houston Rockets guard Fred VanVleet is exercising his $25 million player option, setting up one of the more closely watched comeback stories of the 2026-27 season.

A year ago, Fred VanVleet was preparing for another season in Houston — working out in the offseason, as NBA players do in the weeks before training camp opens. Then came the injury that erased an entire year of his career: a torn ACL, suffered in a workout that is supposed to be routine.

He missed 82 regular-season games. He watched the Rockets from the sideline. He turned 32 during the recovery. And now he is opting in for $25 million to return for 2026-27.

The NBA has seen plenty of ACL comebacks. The surgical outcomes have improved dramatically in recent years, and most players return to something very close to their previous performance level within 12 to 18 months. But "most players" and a specific 32-year-old guard are not the same thing — and what the Rockets are counting on is not just a functional VanVleet but one who can play at the level that made him a priority signing in the first place.

The $25 Million Question

The decision to opt in is, on its surface, straightforward: $25 million in guaranteed money is a sum nobody walks away from. But opting in also means VanVleet is declaring himself ready, that the recovery has gone well enough to commit to a full season, and that both he and the Rockets believe his presence helps the team more than whatever cap space the voided contract would open.

The Rockets' acceptance of that logic signals their genuine belief that VanVleet, at 32 and one year removed from an ACL tear, is worth more to them on the court than the flexibility his opt-out would create. That is not a trivial statement. The Rockets are a franchise with legitimate playoff ambitions and a young core they are actively trying to push into contention. Spending $25 million on a recovering veteran only makes sense if you believe that veteran can actually contribute at a meaningful level.

What VanVleet Brings When Healthy

At his best, Fred VanVleet has been one of the better two-way guards in the league — a tenacious on-ball defender with the length and anticipation to generate turnovers, combined with a shot creation ability and pull-up range that makes him a reliable option at the end of shot clocks and in high-leverage moments. He won a championship with the Toronto Raptors in 2019, was an All-Star with the Raptors in subsequent seasons, and brought that winning culture experience to Houston when he signed.

The game that VanVleet has built is one that relies on quickness, lateral movement, and decision-making speed. Those are the precise attributes that a torn ACL most directly tests on the other side of recovery. A guard who cannot move the way he did before the injury is a different player — and VanVleet's value to the Rockets depends heavily on whether the defensive tenacity and quickness have come all the way back.

What's Next

Houston opens training camp September 29. Preseason games will begin in mid-October and offer the first real on-court evidence of where VanVleet stands. A strong preseason performance would settle most of the questions around his comeback. A cautious, managed preseason would leave the big questions open into the regular season. Either way, the Rockets have committed $25 million to finding out. Follow Hoop Heroes as VanVleet's comeback gets its first test in Houston's training camp.
