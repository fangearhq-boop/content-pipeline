### STORY 1: NBA Hammers Clippers — 5 Picks, $30M Fine, Ballmer Suspended

**Long-Form Post | 9:30 AM**

The NBA made history yesterday — and not in the good way for the LA Clippers.

After a yearlong investigation by the law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz, the league found that the Clippers engaged in a pattern of salary cap circumvention, funneling improper off-court endorsements to star forward Kawhi Leonard through corporate partners including Aspiration Partners, Boingo Wireless, and Daktronics. The Clippers are a repeat offender — and the NBA made sure the penalties reflected that.

The punishment is brutal: five consecutive first-round draft picks forfeited starting in 2029, a $30 million fine, and suspensions for owner Steve Ballmer, team president Lawrence Frank, and CEO Gillian Zucker. Kawhi Leonard himself avoids a suspension but must pay $700,000 in restitution. His former business rep and uncle, Dennis Robertson — who was fired by Leonard in June — has been banned from all NBA business dealings.

Kawhi's quote: he had "no knowledge of any intent on anyone's part to circumvent the salary cap."

This is the most severe team punishment the NBA has handed down in years. Losing five first-rounders over a five-year span starting in 2029 could set the Clippers back for the rest of the decade. The silver lining? The investigation is over, and Kawhi's trade to the Toronto Raptors can now officially proceed.

What do you think — was the NBA's punishment fair, too harsh, or not harsh enough?

### STORY 2: Kawhi Leonard Trade to Toronto Now Clear to Proceed

**Long-Form Post | 11:30 AM**

After two months stuck in limbo, Kawhi Leonard is finally heading back to Toronto.

The trade was agreed to on June 30: Leonard goes to the Raptors in exchange for Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, unprotected first-round picks in 2031 and 2033, a 2027 pick swap, and two second-round picks. But the deal was put on hold on July 9 while the NBA investigated the Clippers for salary cap violations. With the investigation now concluded, Shams Charania reports the trade is expected to become official in the next few days.

Leonard himself expects to be a Raptor by the time training camp opens on September 28 in Quebec City.

This is a full-circle moment. Kawhi won his only championship with Toronto in 2019 before leaving in free agency — a departure that stung the city deeply. Now, after six seasons in Los Angeles where he never returned to the Finals, he's coming back to the franchise that gave him his ring.

For the Raptors, this is a franchise-shifting move. Leonard at his best is a two-way force capable of carrying a team deep into the playoffs. Toronto now has a genuine star to build around.

The East just got more interesting.

### STORY 3: Wemby Leads France to FIBA Window Sweep

**Long-Form Post | 2:00 PM**

Victor Wembanyama looked every bit like the best player in the world this week — and his opponents were the best Europe has to offer.

In France's final game of the August qualifying window, Wemby dropped 18 points on a remarkable 6-of-7 shooting performance as France defeated Sweden 84-69 in Stockholm on August 30. The Avicii Arena hosted a record crowd of 14,750 fans — and most of them came to see the 7-foot-3 phenom who plays for the San Antonio Spurs.

France outscored Sweden 20-5 in the closing stretch to put the game away comfortably. With the win, France improves to 7-1 in Group L of the European qualifiers for the 2027 FIBA Basketball World Cup — and they look like one of the favorites to qualify.

Wembanyama, who was named France's captain earlier this summer, is only 22 years old. He averaged 25.0 points, 11.5 rebounds, and 3.1 blocks per game in the 2025-26 NBA season, becoming the first unanimous Defensive Player of the Year in NBA history.

Internationally, he's doing the same thing. The qualifying window closed September 1, and France swept it. Turkey became the first nation to officially qualify for the 2027 World Cup (8-0), while Canada also stayed perfect in the Americas (8-0).

Training camp for Wemby and the Spurs opens in less than a month. If this international stretch is any preview, the league should be very afraid.

### STORY 5: Blazers Backcourt Reset After Sharpe's Meniscus Tear

**Long-Form Post | 6:30 PM**

Shaedon Sharpe is going to miss most of the 2026-27 season. And for the Portland Trail Blazers, that means Ja Morant and Damian Lillard are now the show.

Sharpe suffered a torn meniscus in his right knee during an offseason workout and is expected to miss approximately six months — putting his return around late February or early March 2027. At 22 years old and coming off a career-high 20.8 points per game last season, it's a brutal setback for one of the league's rising stars.

But Portland isn't folding. They acquired All-Star guard Ja Morant from the Memphis Grizzlies in a trade this offseason, and Damian Lillard is returning from the Achilles tear he suffered in the 2025 playoffs. Add in veteran point guard Jrue Holiday, and the Blazers suddenly have three experienced guards to anchor their backcourt.

Morant averaged 19.5 points and 8.1 assists per game in 20 games last season before a left elbow injury ended his year. Memphis confirmed he was expected to make a full recovery ahead of 2026-27. At his best, Morant is a top-10 player. The Blazers gave up real assets to get him — and with Sharpe hurt, this trade now carries even more weight.

The question going into training camp? Can Morant finally stay healthy for a full season? And can Lillard rediscover his form after an Achilles?

If the answer to both is yes, Portland might be more interesting than anyone expected.
