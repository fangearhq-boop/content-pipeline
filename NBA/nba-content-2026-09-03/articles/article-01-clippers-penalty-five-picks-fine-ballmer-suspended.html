NBA Hammers Clippers: 5 Picks Gone, $30M Fine, Ballmer Suspended

By Jake Torres | September 3, 2026 | Hoop Heroes

The NBA handed down the most severe team punishment in recent history on September 2, stripping the Los Angeles Clippers of five consecutive first-round draft picks, levying a $30 million fine, and suspending owner Steve Ballmer — the result of a yearlong investigation into salary cap circumvention centered on star forward Kawhi Leonard.

The investigation, conducted by the law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz, found that the Clippers engaged in a pattern of misconduct, funneling improper off-court endorsement opportunities to Leonard through corporate partners — including Aspiration Partners, Boingo Wireless, Daktronics, and Lockton Insurance — in exchange for team business. The Clippers are a prior offender of salary cap circumvention rules, a factor the NBA cited explicitly in determining the severity of the penalties.

The Penalties: A Historic Punishment

The Clippers must forfeit five consecutive first-round picks beginning in 2029. That means Los Angeles will lose its first-round selection every year from 2029 through 2033 — a five-year stretch covering a critical rebuild window. Beyond the picks, the $30 million fine is one of the largest in league history.

Ballmer's suspension is joined by sanctions for team president Lawrence Frank and CEO Gillian Zucker. The league also placed the Clippers under five years of strict monitoring.

Kawhi Leonard, who the investigation found received improper benefits, will not be suspended. He must pay $700,000 in restitution. Leonard maintained that he had "no knowledge of any intent on anyone's part to circumvent the salary cap." The NBA appeared to accept that characterization — his penalty reflects involvement without direction.

Dennis Robertson — Leonard's uncle and former business representative — was fired by Kawhi in June and is now banned by the NBA from all business dealings with the league.

What This Means for the Clippers

Losing five first-round picks across five straight seasons is catastrophic for any franchise attempting to rebuild through the draft. The Clippers have James Harden under contract and a roster that was already aging. Without draft capital, their options for retooling are severely limited.

The penalty echoes — and in some ways exceeds — the punishments seen in prior cap circumvention cases. The NBA has long warned that repeat offenders would face the maximum consequences, and the Clippers pushed that boundary far enough to finally see it enforced.

The Kawhi Trade: Now Clear to Proceed

One immediate consequence of the ruling's conclusion: the agreed-upon trade sending Kawhi Leonard to the Toronto Raptors can now move forward. The deal — agreed June 30 but frozen on July 9 pending the investigation — is expected to become official within days.

Leonard heads to Toronto in exchange for Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, and multiple future first-round picks. It's a bittersweet ending for the Clippers: they lose their star, their picks, and their competitive window simultaneously.

What's Next

Steve Ballmer's suspension length and specific terms will be clarified in coming days as the NBA releases full documentation of the ruling. The Clippers can appeal, though overturning such findings has proven difficult historically.

The franchise now faces a 2026-27 season without their franchise player and a draft future stripped to the bone. The path back to contention will require exceptional front-office creativity — or a stroke of luck in the years ahead when those picks would have mattered most.

For the rest of the league, the ruling sends an unambiguous message: the NBA is watching, the CBA has teeth, and no market size or billionaire owner shields a franchise from accountability.
