2026-27 NBA Power Rankings: Who's Winning It All?

By Jake Torres | September 3, 2026 | Hoop Heroes

NBA training camp opens in 25 days. The offseason is nearly done. And the landscape for the 2026-27 season might be the most competitive in a generation.

Eleven teams enter as genuine championship contenders. Superstars have shuffled to new homes. The New York Knicks carry the pressure of defending their first title since 1973. And the Western Conference has three teams — Oklahoma City, San Antonio, Denver — that collectively own five of the last six NBA MVP awards.

Here's how the contender class stacks up before the first practice ball hits the floor.

Eastern Conference

No. 1 — New York Knicks (Defending Champions)

The Knicks ended 53 years of suffering by beating the San Antonio Spurs in five games last June. They return essentially the same core group, minus center Mitchell Robinson. In a city starving for a dynasty, the pressure to repeat is immense — but the talent is there to deliver.

No. 2 — Miami Heat (Giannis Era Begins)

Miami is dangerous. Giannis Antetokounmpo arrived via blockbuster trade from Milwaukee (for Tyler Herro, Kel'el Ware, Jaime Jaquez Jr., and picks), and the Heat now have a two-time MVP surrounded by Bam Adebayo, Klay Thompson, and a proven coaching staff. Giannis's $275 million supermax extension becomes eligible in January 2027. If signed, this becomes a long-term dynasty-building project. Heat culture does not waste elite talent.

No. 3 — Toronto Raptors (Kawhi Incoming)

Once Kawhi Leonard's trade is officially processed in the next few days, the Raptors vault into top-three conversation. The 2019 champion returning to the franchise he lifted to its only title is a story — and it's also a legitimately strong roster. Toronto with Leonard is a different team.

No. 4 — Philadelphia 76ers (Highest Ceiling, Highest Risk)

LeBron James. Jaylen Brown. Joel Embiid. If all three are healthy, the 76ers may have the most talented roster in the league. If any one of them goes down — and all three have injury histories — the season collapses. Philadelphia is the most fascinating team in basketball, for better or worse.

No. 5 — Indiana Pacers (Haliburton Returns)

Tyrese Haliburton missed all of 2025-26 with a torn Achilles. When last fully healthy, the Pacers were one win away from the 2025 NBA Finals. He's back. Don't sleep on Indiana.

Western Conference

No. 1 — Oklahoma City Thunder

Shai Gilgeous-Alexander has led the Thunder to two Western Conference Finals in three seasons. They have youth, depth, and a front office that builds correctly. Expect OKC to make another deep run.

No. 2 — San Antonio Spurs (Wemby's Mission)

Victor Wembanyama is 22 years old and already the first unanimous Defensive Player of the Year in NBA history. The Spurs pushed the Knicks to five games in the Finals and believe they'll go further in year four of the Wemby era. This team is built to win now and sustain it.

No. 3 — Denver Nuggets (Jokic Never Stops)

Nikola Jokic is simply one of the best players in history. With Michael Porter Jr. healthy and a supporting cast capable of winning any series, Denver is never to be underestimated.

What's Next

Training camps open September 28-29. Preseason games begin October 3. The regular season tips off October 20 with a tripleheader on NBC, including the Knicks' championship banner ceremony against the 76ers — where LeBron James and Jaylen Brown will make their Philadelphia debuts at Madison Square Garden.

With this many genuine contenders, the 2026-27 season might produce the most unpredictable playoff field in years. Pick your champion now — and prepare to be surprised.
