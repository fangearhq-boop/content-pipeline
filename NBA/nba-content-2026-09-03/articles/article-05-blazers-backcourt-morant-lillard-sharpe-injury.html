After Sharpe's Meniscus Tear, Portland's Answer Is Morant and Lillard

By Marcus Cole | September 3, 2026 | Hoop Heroes

The Portland Trail Blazers came into the offseason with a plan built around emerging star Shaedon Sharpe as their franchise centerpiece. Then Sharpe tore the meniscus in his right knee during an offseason workout.

Six months. Gone for most of the 2026-27 season. The 22-year-old who posted a career-high 20.8 points per game last year — with legitimate All-Star potential on the horizon — will watch the first half of the season from the sideline.

So Portland pivots. And the pivot involves two names that might make Blazers fans forget the pain for a while: Ja Morant and Damian Lillard.

The New Starting Backcourt

The Blazers acquired Morant from the Memphis Grizzlies in a trade this offseason, giving up real assets to land one of the most explosive guards in the game. Before a left elbow injury shortened his 2025-26 season to 20 games, Morant averaged 19.5 points and 8.1 assists — numbers that, when paired with his gravity-defying athleticism, make him one of the most dangerous players on the court in any given night.

Memphis confirmed before the trade that Morant was expected to make a full recovery ahead of 2026-27. Portland is banking on it.

Lillard, meanwhile, is returning from an Achilles tear suffered in the 2025 playoffs — the most uncertain variable in the Blazers' calculations. When healthy, Lillard is a perennial All-Star and one of the best clutch performers in basketball. The question heading into camp is not whether he's motivated — it's whether the Achilles has healed completely enough to support a full season of his explosive style of play.

Holiday: The Unsung Piece

Rounding out the backcourt is Jrue Holiday, one of the most underrated two-way guards in the league. Holiday provides championship-level defense, playoff experience, and the kind of steady leadership that volatile backcourts need to function. With Morant and Lillard carrying the offensive load, Holiday gives the Blazers an elite stopper on the other end.

Three veteran guards in a system where all three have proven they can impact games — Portland may have more depth and resilience than observers are giving them credit for.

Sharpe's Setback: A Timeline Reset

Sharpe's return is projected for late February or early March 2027, meaning he could be back for a playoff push if the Blazers are positioned correctly. The No. 7 pick in the 2022 Draft, Sharpe has shown the kind of scoring versatility that translates to any level of competition. At 22, he has time to recover and reclaim his trajectory.

In the short term, the Blazers face the challenge of remaining competitive and keeping their veteran guards healthy through the first half of the season. That's not a given with a roster featuring players coming back from significant injuries.

What's Next

Portland opens training camp in late September. Morant's health will be the primary storyline from day one — every workout, every scrimmage will be evaluated through the lens of "is he back to his full explosive self?"

If he is, and if Lillard's Achilles holds, the Blazers could be a legitimate surprise team in the Western Conference. A backcourt of Morant, Lillard, and Holiday — even without Sharpe — is one of the better collections of guard talent in the league.

The plan changed. The ceiling might have gotten higher anyway.
