NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post The Detroit Pistons thought they had a deal to keep Jalen Duren — then they added a weight clause, and everything fell apart. New reporting reveals the Pistons included a provision in their $200M offer requiring Duren to submit to monthly weigh-ins, with performance incentives tied to the results. The Pistons' rationale was that Duren's weight contributed to a poor playoff performance in the second-round exit against Cleveland. But Duren and his camp were caught completely off guard — the clause was never discussed in prior negotiations. He rejected the offer. And now, according to multiple reports, his future in Detroit is "in real limbo." This is no longer just a contract dispute. The gap between what Duren wants ($287M maximum over five years) and what the Pistons are offering ($200M) is $87 million. But it's the weight clause — surprise, unprecedented, and apparently deeply offensive to the player — that has turned a negotiation into a crisis. Sacramento and Milwaukee are both reportedly circling with serious interest. If Duren accepts the $9.6M qualifying offer by October 1, he becomes an unrestricted free agent next summer — and the Kings are reportedly ready to offer him a maximum deal. Media day is September 28. Duren's presence or absence will signal everything about where this ends up. ---

NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post The most interesting financial decision in the NBA this offseason still hasn't been made — and it belongs to the greatest shooter who ever played. Stephen Curry is heading into Warriors training camp next week on an expiring contract. He's eligible to sign a two-year extension worth up to $136.7 million. Or he can let the clock run out and become an unrestricted free agent in the summer of 2027, at 39 years old. Everyone — including Curry himself — has said he wants to finish his career in Golden State. The Warriors will give him whatever he wants. But the interesting question is whether he takes less than the maximum to help the organization build one more contender around him. Victor Wembanyama just signed a 5-year, $252 million extension with San Antonio — and left roughly $50 million on the table by not taking the supermax. For a generational player, that's a statement: I'm here to win, not just to bank. Could Curry do something similar? He's already earned over $500 million in his NBA career. The cap calculation is different at 36 than it is at 22. But the mindset isn't. Camp opens in Hawaii on September 29. Media day is September 28. The decision window is open. ---

NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Victor Wembanyama was eligible for the biggest contract in NBA history. He turned it down. When the 22-year-old signed his 5-year extension with the San Antonio Spurs in July, he chose the 25% maximum designation rather than the 30% supermax he was also eligible for. The difference: roughly $50 million over five years. He signed for $252 million instead of a potential $301 million. Why leave that money behind? The answer is simple and remarkable: he gave the Spurs cap flexibility to keep building around him. It's the kind of franchise-first move that, historically, only a handful of all-time players have ever made at this stage of their career. The decision sits alongside De'Aaron Fox's own 4-year, $228 million extension as the foundation of what could become the most intriguing young core in the league. Two stars. Locked in long-term. Wembanyama is the face of it all. San Antonio hosts Oklahoma City on Opening Night, October 20. The best rivalry of the next decade starts in 25 days. Pay attention now, before the rest of the league catches up. ---

### Long-Form Post Jayson Tatum ruptured his right Achilles tendon in May 2026. The standard recovery timeline for that injury is 9 to 12 months, with a cautious return to full speed taking even longer. Tatum returned to the court in early March 2026. That's roughly 10 months — and the people around him are calling it historic. The Boston Celtics open training camp September 29, and every indication is that Tatum will be a full participant. He spent part of his recovery leaning on Kevin Durant and Damian Lillard, two All-Stars who have navigated the same injury — both returning to All-Star caliber play post-surgery. The Celtics' title defense was already complicated by the New York Knicks ending their season in the Eastern Conference Finals. A diminished Tatum — slower first step, less explosiveness at the rim — would make it even harder. The next 6-8 weeks of preseason will be the real test of whether the recovery is as historic as advertised. Boston opens the regular season October 20 at Detroit — on national television. If Tatum looks like himself, the Celtics are still a threat. If he doesn't, the East belongs to someone else. ---
