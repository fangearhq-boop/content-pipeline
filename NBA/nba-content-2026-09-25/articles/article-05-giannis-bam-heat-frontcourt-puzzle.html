The Heat's Camp Opens Sept 29 — And Everything Depends on Spoelstra Figuring This Out

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | September 25, 2026

The unofficial work is done. Giannis Antetokounmpo arrived in California earlier this month for an unofficial minicamp with Bam Adebayo, Klay Thompson, and other Heat teammates — getting early reps together, learning tendencies, building familiarity with the schemes Erik Spoelstra plans to run. Official training camp opens September 29 at Kaseya Center in Miami.

And now begins the most interesting coaching challenge in the NBA this season: building a system that maximizes both Giannis and Bam on the same floor.

The Puzzle

Giannis Antetokounmpo is the most physically dominant player in the league — a 6'11" forward who can initiate offense from the perimeter, attack downhill, and punish smaller defenders in the post. Bam Adebayo is one of the best facilitating centers in basketball, a versatile big who reads the floor, passes out of the short roll, and anchors a defense with his positioning and instincts.

Both players are at their best when the offense flows through them. Both excel as initiators. The question isn't whether they can coexist — talented players figure out coexistence in any well-coached environment — it's whether the pairing creates advantages that outweigh the structural tensions.

The most obvious challenge: spacing. The Heat need shooters around two bigs who prefer to operate from the elbow and high post. Klay Thompson — one of the best shooters in league history — provides a release valve. Andrew Wiggins stretches the floor on the wing. But five-out lineups are impossible with both Giannis and Bam on the floor, and that limits the offensive geometry Spoelstra can work with.

Spoelstra's Track Record

If any coach can figure this out, it's Erik Spoelstra. He built the system that made LeBron James, Dwyane Wade, and Chris Bosh work together in 2010 — a partnership of three players who each believed they were the primary option on their previous teams. He did it by finding the specific role each player filled best and building the offense around those complementary functions.

Giannis and Bam present a different challenge than LeBron and Wade — less about ego management, more about the specific overlapping skill sets. Spoelstra will likely experiment with lineups where Bam operates as a traditional screen-and-roll center while Giannis operates as a wing-to-post initiator, or where Bam plays higher on the floor to allow Giannis to operate in the short corner. The exact answer is unknowable until real games are played.

The Heat's Timeline

Miami's first preseason game is October 3 in Quebec City, Canada — a road game against the Toronto Raptors that will generate significant attention given Kawhi Leonard's return to the team he won his only championship with. The Heat's own storyline will compete for headlines with Kawhi's homecoming.

The regular season opens October 20. The Heat will face an Eastern Conference that includes the Knicks (defending the conference title), a Celtics team with Jayson Tatum returning from an Achilles injury, and the Sixers with LeBron James and Jaylen Brown making their Philly debut. Every team in the conference is navigating its own version of reinvention. The Heat's version happens to involve the most physically gifted player on the planet.

What's Next

Official camp opens September 29. The next two weeks of training camp will be the most watched in Heat history since the Big Three era — every drill, every five-on-five session, every Spoelstra media availability will be parsed for clues about how the Giannis-Bam partnership is developing. The first preseason results in early October will give the first real answer. Until then, the California minicamp was a preview. The actual story starts Monday.
