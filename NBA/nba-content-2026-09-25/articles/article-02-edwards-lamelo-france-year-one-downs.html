Edwards and LaMelo Share the Court — But Edwards Warns Year One Will Have 'a Lot of Downs'

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | September 25, 2026

Anthony Edwards organized a team trip to France before training camp even opened. Full roster, workouts, sightseeing — a deliberate bonding exercise for a group that hasn't played a single game together yet. And while they were at it, Edwards and LaMelo Ball hit the practice court for a halfcourt shooting contest.

The most anticipated new backcourt in the Western Conference is already sharing a floor. But Edwards, who has never been prone to overselling himself or his team, is managing expectations with unusual candor: "Year One is going to have a lot of downs."

What Was Spent to Get Here

The Timberwolves paid an enormous price for Ball. The original deal sent Naz Reid and a first-round pick to Charlotte — but the full cost grew: three first-round pick swaps and three second-round picks were also part of the transaction, which involved the Chicago Bulls and Brooklyn Nets in additional legs of a multi-team structure.

Josh Green, who was in the original deal, was subsequently flipped to the Utah Jazz for Cody Williams and John Konchar, a sign that Minnesota was actively reshaping the roster around the new pairing. Rudy Gobert remains. The projected starting five — Edwards, Ball, Gobert, Jaden McDaniels, and Jonathan Kuminga — has the raw talent to compete for a top seed in the West.

Whether that lineup becomes coherent is a different question. And Edwards knows it.

The Leadership Statement

Organizing a team trip to France before training camp is not standard procedure. It's the kind of thing a franchise player does when he understands that chemistry doesn't build itself — that the investment in relationships off the court pays dividends when the games get hard.

Edwards has grown into one of the most compelling personalities in the league, partly because he pairs genuine star talent with a disarming honesty. He doesn't oversell. When he says Year One will have downs, he means it — and that kind of realism actually steadies a locker room. If the star player is already accounting for the rough patches, the rest of the team can absorb them without panic.

The Questions Entering Camp

The biggest structural questions don't have answers until the ball is tipped. Does Ball's style — creative, sometimes passive, built around sudden bursts of brilliance — complement Edwards' physically dominant two-way game, or do they step on each other's possessions? How do Gobert's rim-anchoring defensive tendencies interact with Ball's need to attack in space?

These are real questions that a halfcourt shooting contest can't answer. Camp opens September 29. The preseason begins October 3. By late October, the Wolves will have their first real read on whether the "one of the most exciting backcourts in the NBA" framing is earned or aspirational.

What's Next

Training camp opens September 29, with the Timberwolves heading into their first preseason games shortly after. Watch for early on-court reps between Edwards and Ball — how they operate in pick-and-roll, in transition, and in clutch moments will define the early narrative of this partnership. Edwards' honesty about "lots of downs" sets a healthy baseline. Now the team has to show that the ups outweigh them.
