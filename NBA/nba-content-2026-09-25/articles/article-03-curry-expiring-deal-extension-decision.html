Stephen Curry Is Entering Camp on an Expiring Deal — And the Extension Window Is Now Open

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | September 25, 2026

The most interesting financial negotiation in the NBA this offseason doesn't involve a young player chasing his first big payday. It involves a 36-year-old who has already made over half a billion dollars in his career, heading into training camp as the biggest star in basketball on an expiring contract.

Stephen Curry can sign a 2-year extension with the Golden State Warriors worth up to $136.7 million. Or he can let the clock run out and become an unrestricted free agent in the summer of 2027. Warriors media day is September 28. Camp opens in Hawaii on September 29. The decision window is open.

What Everyone Already Knows

There is no mystery about what Curry wants or what Golden State will offer. He has publicly called himself a "lifer" in Golden State multiple times. Warriors management has stated clearly that they will give him whatever structure he wants. The desire to stay is mutual, the relationship is unambiguous, and the only question left is the number and the term.

The standard move here is to sign the maximum extension immediately. At 36, Curry is still playing at an All-Star level, and two additional years at $136.7 million is reasonable compensation for the greatest shooter in the history of the game. Case closed, move on.

But basketball in 2026 is more complicated than that. And Curry has always thought bigger than the next contract.

The Wembanyama Precedent

In July, Victor Wembanyama signed a 5-year extension with San Antonio worth $252 million. He was eligible for roughly $50 million more through the 30% supermax designation. He chose the 25% max instead, gifting the Spurs cap flexibility to keep building around him.

Multiple sources have raised a similar scenario for Curry: what if he takes less than the $136.7 million maximum — say, $100 million or $115 million over two years — to preserve Golden State's ability to pursue a third star in the summer of 2027? That conversation has an obvious logic. Curry at 38 is still an asset. Curry at 38 alongside a co-star capable of taking over games he can't carry alone is a different equation entirely.

There's no confirmed reporting that Curry is considering a discount. But there's no smoke without fire, and the Wembanyama conversation has clearly given league insiders a framework to think about what "selfless stars" can mean in practice.

Camp in Hawaii

Whatever the number ends up being, the Warriors open camp this week at BYU-Hawaii. The setting is appropriate for a team and a star who project laid-back assurance even in the middle of a financial negotiation that would stress most organizations. Warriors training camps have a character to them — easygoing on the surface, intensely competitive underneath — that mirrors their franchise player.

Media day on September 28 is the first opportunity for Curry to address the extension publicly. His answers will be closely watched — not because anyone expects a bombshell, but because the way he frames the conversation will shape the narrative heading into camp.

What's Next

The extension decision is expected within weeks — either before media day, during camp, or shortly after the preseason begins. For the Warriors, the most important thing is clarity: knowing Curry's contract situation heading into the season allows them to plan around it. For the rest of the league, it's a window into how one of the greatest players in NBA history thinks about the final years of his career. The number, whenever it comes, will tell that story.
