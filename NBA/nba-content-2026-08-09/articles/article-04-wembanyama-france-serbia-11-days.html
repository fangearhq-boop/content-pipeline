Wembanyama Returns to France in 11 Days — and His National Team Has Never Looked Better

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | August 9, 2026

NBA Offseason / San Antonio Spurs / Victor Wembanyama / France FIBA

Eleven days from now, Victor Wembanyama will step back onto a court for the first time since the end of the NBA season — and he will be wearing France's red, white, and blue, not the silver and black of the San Antonio Spurs. His France national team return, a full two years after the 2024 Paris Olympics, begins August 20 with an exhibition against Serbia in Belgrade.

It is a highly anticipated return for reasons that go beyond one player. France enters August leading Group L with a 5-1 record in 2027 FIBA World Cup qualifying. Wembanyama, Rudy Gobert, and Evan Fournier will be together under a new head coach for the first time since Paris. And a new era of French basketball begins in Belgrade.

The August Schedule

The exhibition window serves two purposes: ramp up competition and build chemistry under Frederic Fauthoux, who replaced longtime France coach Vincent Collet after the Olympics. The schedule is demanding by design.

August 20, the opener against Serbia in Belgrade, is the first true test. Serbia is typically one of Europe's strongest teams — a physical, experienced squad that will challenge France's new system immediately. The second exhibition on August 23 comes in Orleans, France, giving the home crowd its first look at the Fauthoux-Wembanyama partnership.

Then the games count. August 27, France hosts Slovenia in a 2027 World Cup qualifier. August 30, France travels to Sweden. Both games are critical for Group L positioning, though France's 5-1 record gives them a comfortable cushion heading in.

A New Coach, a New Era

Frederic Fauthoux is stepping into one of the most coveted — and scrutinized — head coaching roles in international basketball. He is inheriting a roster with an all-time talent at center and a core that reached the Olympic final in Paris. He is also coaching Wembanyama at the national team level for the first time.

The Fauthoux-Wemby dynamic is the story within the story. Wembanyama has spoken about how much he values the FIBA game — the spacing, the pace, the collaborative style that differs from the NBA's more isolation-heavy tendencies. "I don't miss the NBA at the moment," Wembanyama said. "But I do miss FIBA." That enthusiasm is a gift for any coach trying to establish a new system.

San Antonio Is Fully Invested

The Spurs organization is not sitting this out. Spurs coaches and staff flew to Paris for Wembanyama's offseason camp — an organizational investment that goes well beyond a standard summer check-in. San Antonio is building its entire franchise around Wembanyama's development. Being present during his international preparation is part of that commitment.

It is an unusual arrangement — NBA teams rarely send coaching staff to national team environments — but the Spurs' approach to Wembanyama has been consistently unconventional. They are playing the long game, and August in Paris is part of it.

What's Next

France opens against Serbia in Belgrade on August 20. The second Serbia exhibition is August 23 in Orleans. The 2027 FIBA World Cup qualifiers follow: Slovenia at home on August 27 and Sweden away on August 30. France leads Group L at 5-1. Watch for Wembanyama's conditioning — this is the first competitive action of his 2026-27 preparation window — and for the first signs of how Fauthoux deploys his most important player.
