Stephen Curry Is About to Make NBA History With a Number No Player Has Ever Reached

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | August 9, 2026

NBA Contracts / Golden State Warriors / Stephen Curry

Stephen Curry's extension window opens August 29. The Golden State Warriors are prepared to offer the full two-year, $136.7 million maximum deal. Per CBS Sports reporting, the structure of that extension — layered on top of Curry's existing $65 million salary for the 2026-27 season — would make him the first NBA player in history to earn more than $60 million in a single season.

No player has ever crossed that threshold. Curry would be the first. It is one of those salary milestones that, when you say it plainly, sounds almost implausible. Sixty million dollars in one year, from one basketball team, for one player. And yet: it is almost certainly going to happen in September.

How the Numbers Work

Curry is already under contract for 2026-27 at $65 million — a figure that makes him one of the highest-paid players in the league on its own. When he signs the extension, the money from the new deal's first year stacks onto that base figure for salary cap and earnings purposes.

CBS Sports reports that the combination would push his total earnings for 2026-27 above the $60 million mark. For context: the entire roster cap for the 2005-06 NBA season was approximately $53 million. One player. One year. Sixty million.

Why He's Still Worth It

Curry turned 38 this past March. He is playing in his 17th NBA season. Golden State finished 37-45 last year — a disappointing result that had nothing to do with Curry's individual performance, which remained elite. His shooting efficiency was among the best in the league. His floor-spacing impact remained transformative.

The Warriors have retooled around him for one final run: Draymond Green re-signed on a one-year deal worth $27.7 million, and Gary Payton II returned on a one-year, $3.9 million deal. The supporting cast is being rebuilt. Curry is the constant around which everything else rotates.

Multiple sources confirm Curry has no interest in testing free agency. He is finishing his career in Golden State — the only franchise he has ever known, the one that produced four championships and a complete reinvention of how the game is played. The extension is mutual, expected, and overdue.

Without It: Unrestricted Free Agency

If Curry does not sign the extension — a scenario essentially no one believes will happen — he becomes an unrestricted free agent next summer for the first time in his NBA career. He would be 39 years old and able to sign with any team that can offer max money. The Warriors clearly have no intention of finding out what that market looks like.

The extension window opens August 29. September is when everyone expects the papers to be signed. At that point, Curry becomes the first player in NBA history to surpass $60 million in a single season — and Golden State locks in its franchise centerpiece through 2027-28.

What's Next

The extension window opens August 29, now 20 days away. Per multiple sources, a September signing is widely expected. Once the deal is done, Curry's full 2026-27 earnings will be confirmed — and the $60 million milestone will officially be on the books.
