The Kawhi Leonard Investigation Could Run Into 2027 — And the Raptors Are Running Out of Time

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | August 9, 2026

NBA Investigation / Los Angeles Clippers / Toronto Raptors / Kawhi Leonard

ESPN is reporting that the NBA's salary cap investigation into the Los Angeles Clippers and Kawhi Leonard could extend into 2027. That is not a typo. The probe — which has already spanned 12 months — could continue well past the start of the 2026-27 season, with no hard deadline forcing a resolution on either the investigation or the blockbuster trade that has been frozen since late June.

Training camp opens in approximately seven weeks. The Raptors have a deal they cannot complete. The Clippers have a player they cannot move. And the NBA has an investigation that is apparently nowhere near finished.

What the Investigation Is Actually About

The probe centers on a $28 million endorsement deal between Leonard and Aspiration, a green banking company that also held a $300 million, 23-year naming rights deal with the Clippers franchise. Aspiration has since gone bankrupt. The NBA is investigating whether the Clippers used the endorsement deal to effectively compensate Leonard outside the salary cap — a form of circumvention that would violate league rules.

It is, in theory, the kind of arrangement the league's salary cap rules explicitly prohibit. But proving that a third-party endorsement deal was secretly coordinated by a franchise — especially one where a key company has gone bankrupt and its records may be incomplete — is complex legal territory. The complexity is exactly why the investigation has stretched this long.

The Trade That Won't Move

In late June, the Clippers and Raptors agreed to a trade: Kawhi Leonard to Toronto for Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, and two unprotected first-round picks in 2031 and 2033. The deal was agreed to in principle but has not been officially consummated by the NBA, which told the Raptors that if they choose to finalize the trade before the investigation concludes, they assume the risk of any outcome.

That is an enormous gamble for Toronto. If the NBA ultimately determines that Leonard's contract was improperly supplemented — and voids that contract or suspends him — the Raptors would have given up Ingram, Dick, and two premium first-round picks for a player who either cannot play or whose deal is invalidated. The Raptors have declined to take on that liability. And so the trade sits.

The NBPA's Arbitration Warning

Complicating matters further: the National Basketball Players Association has stated it "wouldn't hesitate" to demand that the matter be sent to a neutral arbitrator if the league pursues penalties against Leonard without what the union views as sufficient evidence. Any arbitration ruling would then be subject to a three-person appeals panel jointly appointed by the NBA and NBPA — a process without a hard deadline. That path alone could push a final resolution well into next year.

The most likely outcome, per multiple league insiders including reporters at ESPN and CBS Sports, remains a negotiated settlement between the NBA and the Clippers. That would theoretically allow the trade to proceed — but it would require the league, the Clippers, and potentially Leonard's camp to agree on terms. So far, no settlement has been announced.

What's Next

Kawhi Leonard is owed $50.3 million for the 2026-27 season. He is technically still a Clipper. Training camp opens in approximately seven weeks. The Raptors have not officially finalized the trade. The NBA's investigation has no declared end date — and now ESPN reports that 2027 is a realistic timeline. Watch for: any settlement announcement from the league and the Clippers, any official arbitration filing from the NBPA, or any statement from Toronto on whether they will proceed before the investigation concludes.
