### STORY 2: Kawhi Leonard — Probe Could Extend Into 2027

**Long-Form Post**
⚖️ The most complicated trade in NBA history just got a new timeline: 2027.

ESPN is reporting that the NBA's salary cap investigation into the Los Angeles Clippers and Kawhi Leonard could extend into next year. That is a staggering development — because training camp opens in approximately seven weeks.

Here's the full picture:

**The deal:** In late June, the Clippers and Toronto Raptors agreed to a blockbuster trade. Kawhi Leonard goes to Toronto. In return, the Clippers get Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, and two unprotected first-round picks (2031 and 2033). The deal is currently frozen.

**The investigation:** The probe centers on a $28 million endorsement deal between Leonard and Aspiration — a green bank that also held a $300 million, 23-year deal with the Clippers franchise. Aspiration has since gone bankrupt. The NBA is investigating whether the Clippers used that deal to effectively compensate Leonard outside the salary cap system.

**The complication:** The NBPA has warned it "wouldn't hesitate" to demand neutral arbitration if the league pursues penalties without sufficient evidence. Any arbitration ruling would then face a three-person appeals panel jointly appointed by both sides. There is no hard deadline. That process could — per ESPN — run all the way into 2027.

**What this means for Toronto:** The Raptors have been told they'll assume the risk of any investigation outcome if they choose to finalize the trade before the probe concludes. That's an enormous liability to take on — they could complete the trade and then have it unwound or have Kawhi face a suspension. So they wait.

Kawhi Leonard is owed $50.3 million in 2026-27. He is a Clipper with no team to play for. Seven weeks to training camp.

The clock is ticking — very slowly. 🕐

### STORY 3: Stephen Curry — On Track to Make History at $60M

**Long-Form Post**
💰 Stephen Curry is about to do something no NBA player has ever done.

When Curry's extension window opens August 29, the Warriors are prepared to offer the full two-year, $136.7 million maximum deal. Per CBS Sports, the extension structure — layered on top of his existing $65 million 2026-27 salary — would make Curry the first NBA player in history to earn more than $60 million in a single season.

Let that land: sixty. million. dollars. In one year.

To put it in context: in 2003, LeBron James signed his first NBA contract for $18.8 million — his entire four-year rookie deal. The max salary has evolved with the salary cap, but Curry surpassing $60 million in a single season is a genuine historical marker for how dramatically the NBA's economics have changed.

Here's the situation heading into the extension window:

Curry is 38 years old. He finished the 2025-26 season as one of the most efficient shooters in the league despite Golden State's 37-45 record. The Warriors have retooled around him with Draymond Green (1yr/$27.7M) and Gary Payton II (1yr/$3.9M) both re-signed. Curry has no interest in leaving Golden State — multiple sources confirm he is finishing his career there. The extension, which should be signed in September, locks that in through the 2027-28 season.

Without the extension, Curry becomes an unrestricted free agent next summer for the first time in his career. The Warriors have absolutely no intention of letting that happen.

For the record books and for Warriors fans: August 29 opens the window. September closes the deal.

### STORY 4: Wembanyama and France — 11 Days Until the Comeback

**Long-Form Post**
🇫🇷 Victor Wembanyama is going home — and the return is only 11 days away.

New France head coach Frederic Fauthoux — who replaced Vincent Collet after the Paris Olympics — will be coaching Wembanyama at the national team level for the very first time.

Here's the August schedule:

- **August 20** — Exhibition vs. Serbia (Belgrade)
- **August 23** — Exhibition vs. Serbia (Orleans)
- **August 27** — 2027 FIBA World Cup qualifier vs. Slovenia (home)
- **August 30** — 2027 FIBA World Cup qualifier at Sweden (away)

France currently leads Group L with a 5-1 record after the first qualifying round. The August games are critical for securing qualification to the 2027 World Cup. Add Wembanyama, Rudy Gobert, and Evan Fournier to a team already 5-1 — this is one of the most dangerous national teams in the world.

Wembanyama's quote says it all: "I don't miss the NBA at the moment, but I do miss FIBA." That's not a knock on the NBA — it's a testament to how much he loves the international game. The pace. The teamwork. The way FIBA basketball is played.

And now, Spurs coaches and staff made the trip to Paris too, investing in Wembanyama's development even through the national team window. That kind of organizational commitment is rare.

The NBA won't start for another 72 days. But for basketball fans paying attention, August 20 in Belgrade is must-watch.

Trust the process — it's just getting started. 🏀
