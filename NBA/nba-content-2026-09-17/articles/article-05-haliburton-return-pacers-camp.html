16 Months After Torn Achilles, Tyrese Haliburton Returns to Pacers Camp

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | September 17, 2026

Indiana Pacers

The last time Tyrese Haliburton played in an NBA game, the Pacers were in the Finals. It was June 2025, and he tore his Achilles tendon in the kind of moment that rewrites the final paragraph of a basketball story before you thought it was done being written.

That was sixteen months ago. Training camp opens September 29, and Haliburton is expected to be there — healthy, cleared, and ready.

The Recovery

An Achilles tear is the most feared soft-tissue injury in professional basketball. The recovery timeline is long, the physical and psychological demands are significant, and the risk of not returning to the same athletic level is real. Every player who comes back from one does so with something to prove — to the league, to themselves, to every skeptic who read the injury report and quietly wrote off the season ahead.

Haliburton, 26, has given Indiana every positive signal through his recovery process. He has been working, he has been cleared for full basketball activity, and the Pacers have not treated his return as a question. They have treated it as a certainty. That kind of institutional confidence matters. When an organization believes in a player's return, it tends to be because the player has given them reason to.

What Haliburton Means to Indiana

The simplest way to measure Haliburton's impact on the Pacers is to compare what they were before and after his arrival. Before he came to Indiana, the Pacers were a middling team — capable enough to make the playoffs, not built for a long run. After Haliburton started orchestrating the offense, the Pacers became an Eastern Conference contender. They reached the Finals in 2025. Without him healthy, they were something different — still competitive, but missing the architect.

Haliburton is a pass-first point guard who makes the game slower for everyone around him. His ability to set up teammates, stretch defenses from the three-point line, and control offensive pace gives Indiana a dimension most teams in the East cannot match. When he is healthy and engaged, the Pacers are a legitimate threat.

A Better Team Around Him

The Indiana roster that Haliburton walks back into is meaningfully improved from the one that reached the Finals. The Pacers acquired center Ivica Zubac from the Los Angeles Clippers at the 2025-26 trade deadline. Zubac is a physical rim protector — exactly the kind of presence the Pacers lacked in their Finals run, when the absence of a reliable defensive anchor became apparent against the Knicks' physical frontcourt.

Haliburton plus Zubac is a different construction. Haliburton controls the speed of the game on offense. Zubac controls the paint on defense. For a team that plays fast and wants to run, having that defensive anchor allows the Pacers to push pace without conceding easy interior buckets every time they do.

What's Next

Pacers training camp opens September 29. Preseason games begin October 3. The regular season starts October 20. Haliburton's return is the biggest storyline entering camp for Indiana — and arguably one of the bigger Eastern Conference stories of the preseason. If he is truly back to full health, the Pacers should be on every contender's radar. Watch his preseason minutes and production closely. A healthy Haliburton does not just return the Pacers to where they were in 2025. It makes them better.
