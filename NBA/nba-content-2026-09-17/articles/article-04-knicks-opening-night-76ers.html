Knicks Banner Night vs. 76ers: The NBA Couldn't Have Written a Better Opening Night

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | September 17, 2026

New York Knicks

The 2026-27 NBA schedule was released this week, and the league buried the lead somewhere — except it didn't, really. There it was, right at the top: October 20, 7 p.m. ET, Madison Square Garden, Philadelphia 76ers at New York Knicks, on NBC and Peacock.

The Knicks will raise their first NBA championship banner since 1973. Then they will play LeBron James and Jaylen Brown.

If this were a film pitch, someone would call it too on-the-nose. In real life, it is simply the best possible Opening Night storyline the NBA has produced in years, and it arrived organically from a summer of extraordinary player movement.

What Banner Night Means for New York

The Knicks' championship — their first since Willis Reed hobbled onto the court in 1973 — was earned against the San Antonio Spurs in five games. It ended a 53-year drought that had become one of the central jokes of American professional sports. Every year the Knicks didn't win, the banner count at MSG stayed at two. Two banners. From 1970 and 1973.

The arena at 7th Avenue is about to gain a third. The pregame ceremony on October 20 will be the most emotionally charged banner-raising Madison Square Garden has seen in more than half a century. For a fanbase that lived through decades of dysfunction, bad trades, worse ownership, and sustained irrelevance, this is a moment that does not feel small. It feels earned.

The Perfect Villains

LeBron James did not sign with the Knicks this summer. He signed with the Philadelphia 76ers. Jaylen Brown, who spent years as a Boston Celtic — a Knicks rival by geography and history — also signed with the 76ers. Together, they assembled what many observers described as a championship-or-bust superteam in Philadelphia.

And both of them will make their 76ers regular-season debuts on banner night at MSG.

The script almost writes itself: the defending champions celebrating in their own building, and the team assembled to dethrone them walking in on the exact same night. The crowd will be hostile in the specific way MSG crowds get hostile — loud, relentless, deeply personal. LeBron has experienced many hostile environments across a 24-year career. This one might be different.

Meanwhile, the Knicks get to welcome them as champions. They hold the trophy. The banner goes up first. Then the game tips.

The Full Opening Night Slate

The NBA is running three games on Opening Night. The Boston Celtics visit the Detroit Pistons at 3 p.m. ET — a strong matchup in its own right. The Oklahoma City Thunder host the San Antonio Spurs in a Finals rematch to close out the night. But the marquee, the one with national implications and cultural stakes, is at 7 p.m. in New York.

NBC and Peacock carrying the Knicks-76ers game signals the league's belief in that matchup as a flagship broadcast — the kind of game that brings back casual fans who stopped watching during the offseason and reminds them why October matters.

What's Next

Training camps open September 29 for the full league. Preseason runs from October 3 through mid-October. October 20 is Opening Night. The Knicks-76ers banner game tips at 7 p.m. ET on NBC and Peacock. Between now and then, watch for how both teams take shape in camp — the Knicks defending, the 76ers installing a new offense around LeBron and Brown's skill sets. This is a rivalry that did not exist six months ago. By October 21, it will feel like it always has.
