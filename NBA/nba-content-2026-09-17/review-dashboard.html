DOJ Clippers Probe — Federal Investigators Press On
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Window: 11:00 AM ET The NBA's chapter on the Clippers scandal closed September 2. The federal government's chapter has not. The U.S. Department of Justice — operating out of the Eastern District of New York — has been running a criminal investigation into whether the Los Angeles Clippers arranged secret sponsorship deals worth approximately $18 million to pay Kawhi Leonard. At least one subpoena has been issued. No charges have been filed. Prosecutors describe the probe as being in its "earliest stages." Meanwhile, the NBA's penalties are in place: a $30 million fine, five lost first-round picks, and suspensions for owner Steve Ballmer (one year), Lawrence Frank (six months), and Gillian Zucker (one year). Leonard was fined $700,000 and traded to Toronto, where he remains. These are two parallel tracks with very different powers. The NBA can fine and suspend. The DOJ can indict and prosecute. Federal investigations of this type have no public timeline — they can take months or years to resolve, or end with no charges at all. For now, the Clippers will play this season under league penalties, while the federal inquiry continues in the background. #Clippers #NBA | Follow Hoop Heroes for the latest on this developing story. ---
