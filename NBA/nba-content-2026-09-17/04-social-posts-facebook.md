### STORY 1: Curry Extension — 11 Days

**Long-Form Post**
Window: 9:00 AM ET

Eleven days until Warriors media day — and Stephen Curry still hasn't signed.

The extension available to him is two years and $136.7 million. Signing it would make Curry the first player in NBA history to earn more than $600 million in career on-court money. Both Curry and the Warriors have confirmed mutual interest. Warriors brass has reportedly told Curry he can decide the structure himself.

Curry has earned the right to make this call however he wants — four titles, two MVPs, a decade-plus of franchise loyalty. But the deadline is coming. Media day is September 28. Training camp follows the next morning.

Walking into media day unsigned turns the extension into the only story anyone covers. Walking in with a deal signed lets the Warriors focus on basketball. Either way, eleven days settle it.

### STORY 2: DOJ Clippers Probe

**Long-Form Post**
Window: 11:00 AM ET

The NBA's chapter on the Clippers scandal closed September 2. The federal government's chapter has not.

The U.S. Department of Justice — operating out of the Eastern District of New York — has been running a criminal investigation into whether the Los Angeles Clippers arranged secret sponsorship deals worth approximately $18 million to pay Kawhi Leonard. At least one subpoena has been issued. No charges have been filed. Prosecutors describe the probe as being in its "earliest stages."

Meanwhile, the NBA's penalties are in place: a $30 million fine, five lost first-round picks, and suspensions for owner Steve Ballmer (one year), Lawrence Frank (six months), and Gillian Zucker (one year). Leonard was fined $700,000 and traded to Toronto, where he remains.

These are two parallel tracks with very different powers. The NBA can fine and suspend. The DOJ can indict and prosecute. Federal investigations of this type have no public timeline — they can take months or years to resolve, or end with no charges at all.

For now, the Clippers will play this season under league penalties, while the federal inquiry continues in the background.

### STORY 3: Duren Emboldened

**Long-Form Post**
Window: 11:00 AM ET

The Jalen Duren standoff just changed — and the Pistons are in a difficult spot.

New reporting indicates the Sacramento Kings are prepared to offer Duren a maximum contract next offseason. That specific intel has made Duren "emboldened" to accept the Detroit Pistons' $9.6 million qualifying offer and become an unrestricted free agent next summer.

Here's the math: Sign the $9.6M QO, play 2026-27 in Detroit, hit free agency, take the Kings' max. Over two seasons, that path could yield more total money than the Pistons' extension offer of roughly $190 million over five years ($38M/yr). If Sacramento is genuinely prepared to go higher, the Pistons' take-it-or-leave-it posture may have cost them their franchise center.

The October 1 deadline is 14 days away. Detroit can push the deadline later (the hard cutoff is March 1, 2027), but more time likely helps Duren, not the team. If no deal gets done, the Pistons keep Duren one more year at $9.6 million — and then watch him go to Sacramento without compensation.

### STORY 4: Knicks Opening Night

**Long-Form Post**
Window: 9:00 AM ET

The NBA schedule dropped this week, and Opening Night is already the most compelling single game the league has booked in years.

October 20. Madison Square Garden. 7 PM ET. Philadelphia 76ers at New York Knicks. National TV on NBC and Peacock.

The Knicks will raise their first NBA championship banner since 1973. The 53-year drought is over, the trophy has been hoisted, and on the first night of the new season, the entire ceremony plays out in front of a sold-out MSG crowd.

Then LeBron James and Jaylen Brown walk in wearing 76ers uniforms.

LeBron signed with Philadelphia this offseason. Jaylen Brown — a Boston Celtic until this summer — joined him. Together, they represent the team assembled to dethrone the reigning champions. And their first regular-season game in 76ers uniforms will be played at Madison Square Garden, directly after the Knicks celebrate winning without them.

The narrative writes itself. Banner ceremony, then the villain debut, then 48 minutes of basketball. The league could not have drawn this up better if it tried.

The Celtics-Pistons (3 PM ET) and Thunder-Spurs games round out the three-game Opening Night slate, but there is no question which game is the centerpiece.

### STORY 5: Haliburton Pacers Return

**Long-Form Post**
Window: 11:00 AM ET

In June 2025, Tyrese Haliburton tore his Achilles tendon in the NBA Finals. Sixteen months later, he is expected to be full-go for Pacers training camp when it opens September 29.

The comeback is complete.

Haliburton, 26, has given Indiana every positive signal through his recovery. The organization has not treated his return as a question — they have treated it as a certainty, and every public update has matched that confidence.

Here is why this matters beyond the human interest story: the Pacers are better now than they were when Haliburton got hurt. Indiana acquired center Ivica Zubac from the Clippers at the 2025-26 trade deadline — a rim-protecting big who gives the Pacers something they did not have in their Finals run. With Haliburton orchestrating the offense and Zubac anchoring the paint, Indiana has a roster capable of making a real Eastern Conference run.

Haliburton lifted this team twice. He turned the Pacers into contenders in 2024, brought them to the Finals in 2025, and now walks into camp as a healthy returning star with a stronger team around him.

Twelve days to training camp. Watch Indiana this season.
