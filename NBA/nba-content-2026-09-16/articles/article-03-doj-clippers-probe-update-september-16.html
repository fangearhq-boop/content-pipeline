DOJ Clippers Probe Enters Third Week With No New Developments — Federal Investigation Still in "Earliest Stages"

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | September 16, 2026

The Department of Justice criminal inquiry into the Los Angeles Clippers and their alleged financial arrangements with Kawhi Leonard entered its third week on September 16 with no new public developments. Federal prosecutors in Brooklyn's Eastern District of New York have described the investigation as being in its "earliest stages," and no charges have been filed.

At least one subpoena has been issued in connection with the probe. The federal investigation centers on allegations that the Clippers arranged no-show endorsement deals with four companies to funnel roughly $18 million to Leonard above his NBA-contracted salary — a potential violation of federal fraud statutes.

Context: What the NBA Already Did

The DOJ's involvement follows the NBA's own sweeping punishment, which was finalized on September 2, 2026. The league stripped Los Angeles of five first-round draft picks (2029 through 2033), levied a $30 million fine against the franchise, suspended owner Steve Ballmer for one year, suspended president of basketball operations Lawrence Frank for six months, and suspended president of business operations Gillian Zucker for one year. Team counsel Rick Robertson received a five-year ban from the league.

Kawhi Leonard was fined $700,000 by the NBA. His trade to the Toronto Raptors was ratified by the league despite the investigation, and Leonard is no longer a Clipper.

What Federal Scrutiny Adds to the Picture

Criminal investigations by the DOJ carry a different weight than league sanctions. The NBA can fine teams, strip picks, and suspend executives; it cannot bring criminal charges. Federal prosecutors can. Wire fraud, conspiracy, and money laundering statutes potentially apply to the alleged scheme, depending on what the investigation uncovers.

That said, a criminal inquiry is not an indictment. Many investigations are opened and closed without charges. The "earliest stages" language used by sources suggests federal prosecutors are still in the fact-gathering phase — building a picture of what happened before deciding whether to pursue a grand jury indictment.

What's Next

Watch for any additional subpoenas, potential grand jury activity, or DOJ press releases as the fall progresses. The franchise has navigated the NBA's disciplinary process; the federal track runs on an entirely different timeline and is largely opaque until prosecutors choose to act publicly. Leonard's first games with Toronto and the Clippers' opening of training camp under new circumstances will provide the backdrop as the investigation continues in the shadows.
