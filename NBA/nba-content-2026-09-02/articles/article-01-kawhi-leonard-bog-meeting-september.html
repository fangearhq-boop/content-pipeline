Kawhi Leonard Trade Saga Reaches Final Act: BOG Meeting Expected This Month

By Damon Pierce

September 2, 2026

After nearly three months of waiting, the NBA's most prolonged offseason drama is entering what could be its final chapter. The league's Board of Governors is expected to hold a pivotal meeting this month that will determine the fate of the Kawhi Leonard trade between the Los Angeles Clippers and the Toronto Raptors — and with training camp opening on September 28, the clock has never been ticking louder.

Leonard was personally cleared by the NBA following an investigation into whether Clippers owner Steve Ballmer funneled money to Leonard through Aspiration, a financial services company that served as a team sponsor. The NBA found no evidence to support that allegation. However, the Clippers still face potential sanctions related to the broader investigation, which also examined Daktronics, a scoreboard maker that installed equipment at Intuit Dome. Outside law firm Wachtell, Lipton completed its fact-finding phase around August 10, and the case has since been with the league.

A Trade Three Months in the Making

The original trade was agreed upon on June 30: Leonard would return to Toronto in exchange for Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, two unprotected first-round picks (2031 and 2033), a 2027 first-round pick swap, and additional second-round selections. The deal was placed on hold on July 9 when the NBA announced it would not immediately ratify the trade pending the investigation's conclusion.

Since then, Kawhi Leonard and the Raptors have continued to operate as though the trade will be completed. Leonard has stated publicly that he expects to be in Quebec City for Toronto's September 28 camp opener. Raptors personnel have been planning accordingly. Meanwhile, the Clippers, per a report from HoopsHype dated August 28, would reportedly be "fine" with Leonard remaining in Los Angeles if the trade were ultimately voided — though that appears to be a contingency plan, not a preference on Leonard's part.

What the BOG Meeting Could Determine

The Board of Governors meeting is expected to serve as the key inflection point. If the NBA finalizes the sanctions against the Clippers at the meeting — or grants approval for the trade to proceed despite pending penalties — Leonard could be cleared to report to Toronto within days. Earlier reports suggested a resolution date of approximately September 22, with some observers noting the timeline could slip into October if complications arise.

For the Raptors, the stakes go beyond one player. Building around Leonard's return was central to their offseason strategy. Ingram and Dick are significant pieces leaving the roster, and the draft capital sent to Los Angeles was substantial. Toronto's front office has bet on this trade materializing, and that bet looks likely to pay off — but not without one more anxious wait.

Clippers Navigate Uncertain Territory

The Clippers, for their part, have not stood still during the limbo. Bradley Beal signed a 2-year, $13.17M deal with Los Angeles, and the team has been quietly constructing a contingency roster. Whether Kawhi's eventual departure — if and when the trade clears — creates a rebuild or a retool depends heavily on what sanctions the league ultimately imposes.

The investigation into the Aspiration and Daktronics sponsorship arrangements was always about the institution, not the player. Leonard was working within a framework set by the organization. The NBA's findings have reflected that distinction, and the expectation league-wide is that the trade proceeds — just later than anyone hoped when the deal was struck in late June.

What's Next

Watch for the Board of Governors meeting date to be announced in the coming days. If the NBA ratifies the trade at or shortly after the meeting, Leonard could be in a Raptors uniform before training camp officially opens on September 29. If the timeline slips further, the league will need to navigate how Leonard participates — or does not — in the early days of camp. Either way, the longest offseason story in the NBA is nearly over.
