Bradley Beal Signs With Clippers: The Final Moves of a Wild NBA Offseason

By Damon Pierce

September 2, 2026

The NBA's 2026 offseason will be remembered for its blockbuster moves: Giannis to Miami, LeBron to Philadelphia, the Kawhi Leonard trade saga. But every offseason ends not with a bang but with a series of quieter transactions that complete the league's roster puzzle. As training camp approaches, Bradley Beal has provided one of the final notable signatures of the summer.

Beal Heads to LA

Bradley Beal signed a 2-year, $13.17M deal with the Los Angeles Clippers, joining a franchise that continues to build around its post-Kawhi uncertainty. Beal, 33, is no longer the 30-point-per-game scorer he was in his prime with Washington, but he brings playoff experience, veteran leadership, and a capable offensive floor to a Clippers team still navigating the investigation that has kept Kawhi Leonard's trade status in limbo.

If Leonard departs to Toronto as expected when the NBA's Board of Governors meets this month, Beal becomes part of a Clippers squad attempting a significant rebuild. If the trade is voided and Leonard stays, Beal is a complementary piece on a team that suddenly looks more competitive. Either scenario, Los Angeles wanted a veteran on a team-friendly deal, and Beal fits that mold precisely.

DeRozan, LaVine, and the Rest

DeMar DeRozan agreed to a 1-year, $3.88M deal with the Denver Nuggets, taking a veteran minimum slot to chase a championship alongside Nikola Jokic. DeRozan, who averaged double figures in scoring as recently as 2025, brings a mid-range game and veteran scoring that Denver's bench has lacked. The signing adds quality depth without straining the salary cap.

Zach LaVine exercised his $49M player option and will remain with the Chicago Bulls for the 2026-27 season. The decision gives Chicago continuity and LaVine the security of a guaranteed contract rather than the uncertainty of free agency. The Bulls have been retooling around a younger core, but LaVine's offensive ceiling makes him difficult to move off the roster without a strong return.

Golden State added Brandon Williams on a 1-year, $2.6M deal. Williams, who averaged 13 points and 3.9 assists per game for the Dallas Mavericks last season, gives the Warriors backup point guard depth behind Stephen Curry. For Golden State, which missed the playoffs last year and is hoping a healthier roster can push them back into the postseason picture, Williams is a cost-effective gamble on a player who showed real promise in Dallas.

The Offseason in Context

The 2026 NBA offseason was defined by star movement: Giannis, LeBron, Kawhi in limbo, Stephen Curry's historic extension, the LaMelo Ball trade to Minnesota, Russell Westbrook's retirement. The later moves — Beal, DeRozan, LaVine's option exercise — are the finishing coat on a summer of dramatic changes across the league. Training camp rosters are now nearly set. Whatever story emerges from the 2026-27 season, most of its characters are now in place.

What's Next

Veterans report to training camps on September 28. Camps open September 29. Beal will be in LA for the Clippers' first practice, though whether Kawhi Leonard joins him or not remains the outstanding question that will be answered before then. DeRozan opens in Denver, LaVine in Chicago, Williams in Golden State. The offseason is effectively over — now the basketball begins.
