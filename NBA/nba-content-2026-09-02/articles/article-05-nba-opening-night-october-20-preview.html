NBA Opening Night Is 48 Days Away: Your Complete Guide to October 20

By Jake Torres

September 2, 2026

Circle the date. October 20, 2026 is 48 days away, and the NBA's Opening Night promises to be the most historic in a generation. Three games on NBC in primetime. A championship banner going up for the first time since 1973. LeBron James playing at Madison Square Garden as a visiting player for the first time in his career as a Philadelphia Sixer. A Western Conference Finals rematch between the league's two most exciting young rosters. This is not an ordinary season opener. Here is everything you need to know.

Game 1: Boston Celtics at Detroit Pistons (3:00 PM ET)

The afternoon opener sends Boston to Detroit for a 3:00 PM ET tip. The Celtics are navigating a significant transition: Jaylen Brown was traded to Philadelphia this offseason as part of the deal that brought LeBron James to the 76ers. Boston will be looking to its younger core and whatever moves Brad Stevens made in the offseason to remain competitive in the East. Detroit, meanwhile, has continued its upward trajectory and relishes hosting a marquee opponent on the league's biggest stage to open the year.

Game 2: Philadelphia 76ers at New York Knicks (7:00 PM ET)

This is the game everyone will be watching. At 7:00 PM ET, the Philadelphia 76ers travel to Madison Square Garden in a matchup that is layered with storylines before a single tip-off occurs.

The Knicks will raise their first championship banner since 1973, ending a 53-year drought for a franchise that spent decades as the league's most tortured fan base. The ceremony before tip-off at MSG will be one of the most emotional moments in NBA arena history. Then the game begins.

LeBron James — wearing a Philadelphia Sixers uniform — will walk onto the Madison Square Garden floor, facing one of the most notoriously demanding crowds in sports. James, who signed with Philadelphia this offseason to join Joel Embiid and Jaylen Brown, makes his debut as a Sixer in perhaps the most pressure-filled environment available. The 76ers saw their championship odds move from 20-1 to 10-1 following the LeBron signing. Philadelphia wants to make a statement on night one.

Game 3: Oklahoma City Thunder at San Antonio Spurs (9:30 PM ET)

The night closes with the Western Conference Finals rematch: OKC Thunder traveling to San Antonio to face the Spurs. Shai Gilgeous-Alexander and the Thunder faced Victor Wembanyama and the Spurs in last season's WCF, with OKC advancing before falling in the NBA Finals. Both franchises enter 2026-27 hungry, young, and convinced they have the formula to go further this year.

Jalen Williams is pushing the Thunder organization to retire Russell Westbrook's No. 0 jersey on Opening Night, a tribute to the franchise legend who retired on August 12. Whether that ceremony happens before the Spurs game or separately is still being determined, but the emotional weight of honoring Westbrook in Oklahoma City — where he is beloved — adds another layer to an already packed night.

The Historic NBC Return

October 20 marks the first NBA Opening Night broadcast on NBC since the 1990s, part of the league's landmark new media rights deal. For a generation of younger fans, seeing the NBA on NBC with that iconic theme music will be a new experience. For older fans, it is a return to the Saturday afternoon games and primetime playoffs that defined the league's golden era. Either way, the broadcast represents a significant moment for the league's cultural footprint.

What's Next

Training camps open September 29. Preseason games begin October 3 and run through approximately October 18. Opening Night on October 20 is the culmination. Set your reminder now: three games, NBC, one historic night.
