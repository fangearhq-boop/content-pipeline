Wembanyama's France Closes FIBA Window 7-1: The World Cup Path Is Wide Open

By Marcus Cole

September 2, 2026

Victor Wembanyama spent his NBA offseason doing what he has always done best: dominating. France closed out the latest FIBA World Cup qualifying window with a 7-1 record in Group L, and Wembanyama — wearing the captain's armband for the first time — was the most dominant player on the floor in every game that mattered. If there were any questions about whether the San Antonio Spurs center could carry the weight of national leadership alongside his franchise responsibilities, this window answered them definitively.

A Statement Window

France played two games in the window's final leg, and Wembanyama was spectacular in both. Against Slovenia on August 27 in Paris, he posted 22 points and 9 rebounds while shooting 60 percent from the floor. France won convincingly, 92-67. Three days later, France traveled to Stockholm for a road test against Sweden at the Avicii Arena, where 14,750 fans turned out — a Swedish basketball all-time attendance record. Wembanyama scored 18 points, grabbed 8 rebounds, blocked 3 shots, and shot 6-of-7 from the field. Final score: France 84-69.

That is efficiency at a level that does not come with a degree of difficulty caveat. Wembanyama was not hunting shots; he was simply the most unguardable player every time he touched the ball.

The Captain's Burden, Embraced

Wembanyama was named France captain ahead of this qualifying window, taking over the leadership role that had previously belonged to veterans like Rudy Gobert. For a player who turned 22 in January, assuming that mantle at the international level is not a routine step. Wembanyama said before the window that "I miss FIBA basketball," a statement that reflected genuine enthusiasm rather than obligation. His play matched the sentiment.

France's roster also includes Gobert, who plays a complementary role alongside his San Antonio counterpart, and veterans like Nicolas Fournier who provide depth. But the window left no ambiguity about who the team is built around or who drives the outcomes. New head coach Frédéric Fauthoux, who replaced Vincent Collet after France's 2024 Paris Olympics campaign, now has a clear picture of what his star can do in a leadership context.

The World Cup Path

At 7-1 in Group L, France is in an excellent position to qualify for the 2027 FIBA World Cup. The exact qualification format involves the top finishers from each European qualifying group, with additional spots through secondary qualifying rounds. France's record puts them well ahead of the cutline, barring an extraordinary collapse in future windows — an outcome that seems implausible given the talent on their roster.

The next qualifying window will come later in the fall, giving Wembanyama a brief window to transition back to Spurs training camp (which opens September 29) before returning to FIBA duties later this season. Managing that schedule — NBA season plus international qualifying — is the challenge ahead. Based on what he showed in this window, the schedule does not appear to concern him much.

What's Next

Wembanyama and the Spurs report to training camp on September 28, with camp officially opening September 29. San Antonio hosts Oklahoma City in one of the most anticipated games on Opening Night — the WCF rematch on October 20. For France, the next FIBA qualifying window will be scheduled later this fall. Watch for Wembanyama's first preseason numbers as an early indicator of his NBA readiness heading into 2026-27.
