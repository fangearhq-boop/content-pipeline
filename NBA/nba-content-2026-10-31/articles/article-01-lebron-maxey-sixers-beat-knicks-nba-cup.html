LeBron and Maxey Lead 76ers Past Defending Cup Champs Knicks 98-93

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes

October 31, 2026

The defending NBA Cup champions came into Friday night's opener riding a wave of expectations. They left with a loss. Tyrese Maxey scored 27 points and LeBron James added 20 points, 9 rebounds, and 8 assists as the Philadelphia 76ers defeated the New York Knicks 98-93 in the NBA Cup group opener at Xfinity Mobile Arena.

Philadelphia is 5-1 on the season and opens Cup group play at 1-0. New York, looking to defend the Cup title it won last season, falls to 2-4 overall and 0-1 in group play — a rocky start the Knicks will need to address quickly.

Maxey's Night

Tyrese Maxey has established himself as one of the Eastern Conference's most dynamic scorers, and he reminded everyone of that on Friday. His 27 points on 10-of-19 shooting were efficient, timely, and decisive. Whether attacking the rim off the dribble or pulling up from mid-range, Maxey made plays that kept the Sixers in front when it mattered most.

Maxey and LeBron combined for 47 of Philadelphia's 98 points — a number that undersells the completeness of their impact. LeBron was not just a scorer. His 9 rebounds and 8 assists put him at the center of virtually everything Philadelphia ran offensively, and his decision-making in the fourth quarter was immaculate.

After the game, LeBron made clear that this partnership has a larger goal in mind. "I'm going to help this kid become legendary," he said of Maxey, a statement that suggests James views this as more than a short-term arrangement.

Brunson's Step-Back Wasn't Enough

Jalen Brunson gave the Knicks a chance. With 1:32 remaining and the game in the balance, Brunson pulled up for a step-back three that found the bottom of the net and pushed New York ahead 90-89. The Garden faithful — watching on every available broadcast — collectively exhaled.

Philadelphia didn't blink. LeBron answered with the go-ahead basket, converting through contact and swinging momentum back to the Sixers. V.J. Edgecombe then hit an insurance three that effectively sealed the win, leaving the Knicks unable to overcome the deficit in the final seconds.

It was the kind of closing sequence that separates teams built to win close games from those still figuring out how to finish. On Friday, the Sixers proved they belong in the first category.

The Defending Champions Stumble

New York enters the Cup as the reigning champion, and that label carries weight. But the Knicks at 2-4 are not playing like a team defending anything at the moment. Friday's loss was the latest in a string of results that raise legitimate questions about whether this group has the cohesion and health necessary to make another run.

Brunson's presence keeps the Knicks dangerous, and they will have multiple Cup group-play games to recover. The quarterfinals don't arrive until December 4-5, giving New York time to right the ship. But the defending champion's stumble on opening night sent a message to the rest of the field: the Sixers are a legitimate Cup contender.

What's Next

Philadelphia returns to action Saturday with a road game at the Charlotte Hornets, its second game in as many nights. The Sixers will look to build on a confident Cup performance and stay sharp heading into the weekend. LeBron's usage and conditioning will bear watching given the back-to-back.

For the Knicks, Saturday is a rest day before they resume Cup group play. The title defense has started poorly, and the margin for error in group play is thin. New York's next Cup game will carry real urgency — a second loss could jeopardize their path to December.
