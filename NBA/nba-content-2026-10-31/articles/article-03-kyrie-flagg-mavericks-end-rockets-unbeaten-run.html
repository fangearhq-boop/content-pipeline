Kyrie 35, Flagg 33: Mavericks Hand Rockets Their First Loss of the Season

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes

October 31, 2026

The Houston Rockets entered Friday's NBA Cup opener as the league's last perfect team. They exited American Airlines Center with their first loss. Kyrie Irving poured in 35 points with 9 assists, Cooper Flagg added 33 points, and the Dallas Mavericks outscored Houston 40-27 in the fourth quarter to win 116-111 and end the Rockets' unbeaten start in memorable fashion.

Dallas wins its Cup opener and improves to 4-2 on the season. Houston, which had been the NBA's most compelling early story, falls to 5-1 and faces its first serious test of character heading into the weekend.

Kyrie and Flagg Put on a Show

Irving's 35 points came with the flair and efficiency that makes him one of the most captivating players in the league. His 9 assists ensured that Flagg and the rest of the Mavericks' supporting cast stayed involved throughout, and his ability to create shots in the fourth quarter — when Houston was pressing and desperate — was the difference.

Cooper Flagg has been one of the season's best stories, a rookie who looks nothing like a first-year player. His 33 points on Friday were a statement. Flagg attacked the basket, hit big shots, and showed the poise of a seasoned veteran when Dallas needed him most. Together, Kyrie and Flagg scored 68 of the Mavericks' 116 points — a co-starring performance that announced Dallas as a legitimate Western Conference Cup contender.

Daniel Gafford provided interior depth with 18 points of his own, giving Dallas a three-headed offensive threat that Houston could not contain.

The Fourth Quarter That Decided Everything

The game was close heading into the fourth. Houston, led by Kevin Durant's 28 points, had played well enough to stay within striking distance throughout. But the Mavericks' fourth quarter was a different level entirely: 40 points, relentless execution, and clutch shot after clutch shot from Irving in the final minutes.

Dallas outscored Houston 40-27 in that final frame — a 13-point edge that transformed a competitive game into a Dallas win. The Rockets could not match the Mavericks' firepower when the stakes were highest, and that is a data point Houston's coaching staff will study carefully.

Durant, for his part, was excellent. His 28 points showed why the Rockets are still a team to be taken seriously. But in a fourth-quarter battle against Kyrie Irving, even an elite 28-point performance can fall short.

Houston's Perfect Record Gone

Losing a perfect record is a moment of reckoning for any team. For Houston, the question is how they respond. The Rockets' 5-0 start had generated genuine excitement — they were playing with an edge, defending at a high level, and getting contributions from multiple players each night.

Friday's loss was not a disaster. One defeat to a quality Dallas team, in an environment where Irving was simply unguardable in the fourth quarter, is not an indictment of the season. But it does reset the narrative. The Rockets are no longer undefeated. They are a team that just discovered what it looks like when a great player decides a game against them — and they have to figure out how to answer that in future Cup games.

What's Next

Dallas heads into the weekend with strong Cup momentum and a talented roster that can compete with anyone in the West. Irving and Flagg as a co-starring duo is a matchup problem for every team remaining in Cup group play.

Houston has Saturday off before returning to action. The Rockets will take stock of a loss that stings but does not derail a season. Kevin Durant's continued excellence, paired with the team's defensive foundation, gives Houston every reason to believe this was a one-game lesson rather than a structural problem. The Cup's group phase leaves room to recover — the question is whether Houston has the resilience to do it.
