Warriors Erase 25-Point Lakers Deficit; Curry Scores 31 in 112-95 NBA Cup Win

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes

October 31, 2026

It was the kind of collapse that will follow a team for months. The Los Angeles Lakers led Golden State by as many as 25 points at Chase Center on Friday night. They lost 112-95. Stephen Curry scored 31 points to lead the Warriors' stunning reversal, while Luka Dončić's brilliant 37-point effort for the Lakers turned into a footnote in one of the most dramatic NBA Cup openers in recent memory.

The Warriors open Cup group play at 1-0. The Lakers are 0-1 in Cup play and must regroup before a challenge only gets more difficult.

How 25 Points Disappeared

The Lakers arrived at Chase Center playing arguably their best basketball of the young season. Dončić was in command, attacking the Warriors' defense at will and building a lead that seemed insurmountable. Walker Kessler was dominant on the boards, contributing 13 rebounds, and the entire Lakers unit was executing with precision.

Then the second half arrived, and everything unraveled.

The Warriors, led by Curry, began dismantling the Lakers' lead with methodical efficiency. Golden State tightened defensively, took better shots, and rode Curry's ability to create offense from anywhere on the floor. The 25-point advantage became 20, then 10, then gone. Brandin Podziemski chipped in 21 points off the bench — another weapon the Lakers could not neutralize — and Golden State completed one of the season's most remarkable comebacks.

By the final buzzer, the 17-point losing margin made the Lakers' early dominance feel like a distant memory.

Dončić and the Painful What-If

Luka Dončić scored 37 points in a losing effort. Under almost any other circumstances, 37 points from your best player is a performance that wins basketball games. On Friday, it was simply not enough.

Dončić was at his best in the first half — pulling up from distance, drawing fouls, and orchestrating the Lakers' offense with the kind of floor vision that makes him one of the league's premier players. The second half told a different story, one where the Warriors made every adjustment necessary and the Lakers answered none of them.

This is the kind of game that defines teams early in a season. The Lakers will need to answer hard questions about their ability to hold leads under pressure, and those questions don't disappear simply because the losses come in October.

Curry's Milestone Amid the Comeback

Amid the chaos of erasing a 25-point deficit, Stephen Curry quietly accomplished something historic: he passed Dominique Wilkins to move into 18th place on the NBA's all-time scoring list. It was the kind of milestone that arrives naturally when a player of Curry's caliber stays healthy and keeps playing at an elite level — and it landed on one of the biggest nights of the young NBA Cup season.

Curry is still in the prime of his career and has years of basketball ahead. The all-time scoring list, once a distant aspiration, is now a realistic target for him to continue climbing, one game at a time.

What's Next

Golden State takes the Cup opener momentum and faces a lighter schedule over the coming days. The Warriors are built for the Cup format — Curry's ability to elevate in high-stakes environments makes them dangerous in every group game remaining.

For the Lakers, the fallout from Friday's collapse demands an immediate response. No. 1 on the agenda: figure out why a 25-point lead evaporated and make sure it never happens again. Cup group play provides a path back to relevance, but the Lakers cannot afford another performance like this if they want to reach December.
