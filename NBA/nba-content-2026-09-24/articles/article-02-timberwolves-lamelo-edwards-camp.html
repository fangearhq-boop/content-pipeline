Four Picks for a Backcourt: Timberwolves Open Camp With LaMelo-Edwards Partnership on Trial

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | September 24, 2026

The Minnesota Timberwolves entered the offseason willing to pay a steep price for a championship window — four first-round draft picks and Naz Reid — and today, with training camp officially opening, the audition begins.

September 24 marks the earliest date NBA teams with international preseason commitments can open camp, and the Wolves are among those getting an early start. The question hanging over everything isn't about talent. Minnesota has plenty of that. The question is chemistry.

The Ball-Edwards Experiment

LaMelo Ball and Anthony Edwards are two of the most gifted offensive players in the NBA. They're also two players who have spent their careers as the primary ball-handler on their respective teams. Ball built his reputation in Charlotte as a flashy playmaker with elite court vision and an NBA-ready three-point stroke. Edwards developed into the league's most explosive two-way guard in Minnesota, earning first-team All-NBA honors last season.

Fitting them together isn't impossible — great backcourts can be built around two playmakers — but it requires both players to adjust. The Wolves' projected starting lineup of Edwards, Ball, Rudy Gobert, Jaden McDaniels, and Jonathan Kuminga has size, length, and offensive firepower. What remains to be seen is whether the spacing works and whether both stars can thrive without monopolizing possessions.

The Trade Cost Creates Pressure

Trading four first-round picks is a massive bet. It accelerates Minnesota's timeline, depletes future flexibility, and signals that ownership and management believe this window is now. That belief creates pressure for players and coaches to deliver.

The Timberwolves enter camp with internal questions as well — reports indicate two players are on the "hottest trade candidate seats" as the front office looks to continue reshaping the roster. That reality means the opening of camp isn't just about running drills and getting in shape. It's about establishing roles and answering questions that will define whether this team can compete for a title.

What's Next

The Timberwolves head to Macao for NBA China Games in October before the regular season opens. That international trip gives the coaching staff extra time with the full roster — unusual early-camp reps that could accelerate the Ball-Edwards partnership. How those first practices look will matter. Keep an eye on spacing lineups and who plays with whom in the early scrimmages.
