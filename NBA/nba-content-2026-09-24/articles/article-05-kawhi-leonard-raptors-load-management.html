Kawhi Leonard Is Home in Toronto. The Real Question Is How Much Home He Can Give.

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | September 24, 2026

Kawhi Leonard left approximately $18.5 million on the table to return to Toronto. He waived a contract kicker and accepted a figure below maximum value to reunite with a city that treated him like royalty during the 2019 championship run. The Raptors gave him a 2-year deal with a player option for 2028-29.

That financial sacrifice was meaningful. But as training camp begins this week, the question that matters most isn't how much Kawhi gave up in salary. It's how much Kawhi can give on the court.

The Load Management Reality

Leonard is 35 years old. His career has been defined almost as much by the games he hasn't played as the ones he has. The Clippers era produced spectacular playoff performances alongside regular-season absences that frustrated fans and front offices. Managing his workload isn't a criticism — it's how he has stayed effective into his mid-thirties.

For the Raptors, who are still in a rebuild and unlikely to be immediate championship contenders, the load management question takes on a different shape. They aren't counting on Kawhi to play 70+ games. They need him to be available enough that his presence matters — to his teammates' development, to the franchise's identity, and to whatever competitive aspirations Toronto holds for this season.

What the Return Represents

Kawhi's decision to sign below market value reflects something real about his attachment to Toronto. He won his only championship here. He made one of the greatest shots in playoff history here. The city treated him as a legend. Coming back — at any price — was about more than basketball.

But basketball reality still applies. The Raptors are building around younger players. Kawhi's role is likely to be a veteran anchor, a mentor to developing pieces, and a capable scorer on nights when his body cooperates. Getting the most out of his remaining prime means protecting his health while extracting the maximum value from the games he does play.

What's Next

Training camp begins this week for the Raptors, with media day on September 28. How the coaching staff describes Kawhi's role and usage plan will be the first real window into how they intend to manage the reunion. His availability in the early preseason will be the next signal. For now, he's home again — and the NBA will be watching to see what that means.
