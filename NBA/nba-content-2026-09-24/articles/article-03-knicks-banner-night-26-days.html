26 Days: Knicks Banner Night Has LeBron, Brown, and a 53-Year Wait Coming to MSG

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | September 24, 2026

October 20 is 26 days away, and the storylines for the most anticipated opening night in recent NBA memory just keep multiplying.

The New York Knicks will raise their first championship banner since 1973 — a 53-year drought finally ended last June — and they'll do it in front of a crowd that will include LeBron James in a Philadelphia 76ers jersey, alongside a Sixers superteam that looks tailor-made for revenge narratives.

A Night Stacked With Storylines

Start with the banner. The Knicks championship is still fresh, the city is still buzzing, and Madison Square Garden will be electric from the moment the lights go down for the ceremony at 6:30 PM ET. That alone would make it must-see television on NBC and Peacock.

Then add this: LeBron James, 41 years old and in his second decade of being the best player on any team he joins, making his debut as a 76er. Jaylen Brown arriving from Boston in one of the offseason's biggest moves. Joel Embiid and Tyrese Maxey still anchoring a franchise that the Knicks swept 4-0 in last year's playoffs.

The Sixers aren't just a good team. They're a loaded, motivated team that watched the Knicks close out their series on the way to a title. And now they have two new co-stars. The first meeting of this rivalry in the 2026-27 season will happen in the building where the Knicks raised their first banner in 53 years.

Tickets Reflect the Moment

The secondary market has priced this game accordingly. Average resale tickets are running north of $2,500, with the get-in price still above $1,000. By comparison, this is the most expensive regular-season game of the week — by a significant margin. Fans are willing to pay for history.

What's Next

The ceremony starts at 6:30 PM ET on October 20. Tipoff follows at 7:00 PM on NBC/Peacock. Twenty-six days. If you're a basketball fan, this is the night you've been waiting for — whether you're a Knicks fan savoring the banner moment or a Sixers fan watching the league's newest super-team take the floor for the first time.
