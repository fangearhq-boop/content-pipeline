LeBron James Explains "Bunker Mentality" and Three Reasons He Chose the 76ers

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | September 27, 2026

LeBron James has never been shy about explaining his decisions — but his first extended comments as a Philadelphia 76er cut straight to the heart of what this team is trying to accomplish and why he signed on to be part of it.

In a candid appearance on "Mind the Game" with Steve Nash (released around September 25), LeBron laid out three explicit reasons for joining the 76ers on a two-year, $8 million deal this past July — and then delivered a message that immediately went viral: "We've got to have a bunker mentality."

Reason 1: Joel Embiid's Missing Piece

The first and most significant factor, according to LeBron, was Embiid himself.

"He's done everything in our league and the only thing he has not done is win a championship," LeBron said.

That single sentence sums up why this partnership carries so much weight. Embiid, one of the most dominant centers in NBA history, has won scoring titles, MVP awards, and defensive honors — but a championship has eluded him throughout his career with the 76ers. LeBron, who has won four titles and has positioned himself as a player who prioritizes winning above all else in the late stages of his career, sees Philadelphia as the place where he can finally help Embiid close that gap.

Reason 2: The Maxey Connection

The second factor was LeBron's friendship with Tyrese Maxey — a connection that goes beyond basketball and reflects the kind of chemistry that can only be built through genuine relationship.

Maxey, coming off a career year in 2025-26, is one of the league's fastest-rising stars. His energy, effort, and leadership presence made him a natural fit for what LeBron is trying to build in Philly. LeBron has spoken before about choosing teams where he feels an authentic connection with his teammates — and with Maxey, that connection was already there.

Reason 3: Respect for Jaylen Brown

The third reason raised some eyebrows: LeBron's respect for Jaylen Brown, who joined Philadelphia via trade earlier this summer.

"I love the way he approaches the game," LeBron said. "Kind of misunderstood, and that is absolutely okay."

Brown has often been a polarizing figure in NBA discourse — praised for his ability but sometimes criticized for his fit alongside other stars. LeBron's direct public endorsement sends a message to the league and to Brown himself: this partnership is intentional, and LeBron sees something in Brown that others have overlooked.

The "Bunker Mentality"

Perhaps the most significant part of LeBron's podcast appearance wasn't the explanations — it was the framing he chose for what lies ahead.

"We've got to have a bunker mentality," LeBron said when addressing how the 76ers will handle the relentless media scrutiny that will surround this team all season.

It's a phrase that speaks to experience. LeBron has navigated superteam expectations in Cleveland, Miami, and Los Angeles — he knows better than anyone that a roster loaded with stars does not automatically translate to a championship. The external noise, the expectations, the media spotlight — it can fracture a team that isn't mentally prepared for it.

At age 41, LeBron is choosing to address that dynamic head-on before the first training camp practice even takes place.

What's Next

The Philadelphia 76ers hold their official media day on Tuesday, September 29 — the last of the 30 teams to do so. When LeBron, Embiid, Brown, and Maxey step in front of cameras for the first time as a group, the questions will be pointed and the coverage will be massive.

Based on LeBron's comments, the 76ers are entering that moment with a clear philosophy: eyes down, tunnel vision, championship or bust. Whether they can execute that bunker mentality over the course of an 82-game season — and the playoffs that follow — is the most compelling question in the NBA heading into 2026-27.
