DeRozan to Denver, Kawhi Locks In Toronto: Two Legends, Two Paths

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | September 27, 2026

As the NBA prepares for media day on Monday and the official start of the 2026-27 season, two veterans made headline-grabbing decisions this week that offer a fascinating contrast in philosophy: DeMar DeRozan is chasing a ring, and Kawhi Leonard is building a legacy.

DeRozan to Denver: Ring Chasing at Its Most Transparent

Six-time All-Star DeMar DeRozan has agreed to a one-year veteran minimum deal with the Denver Nuggets, worth approximately $3.9 million. For a player of DeRozan's caliber and legacy, that number is almost insultingly low — and that's entirely the point.

DeRozan, one of the most accomplished mid-range scorers in modern NBA history, is making a conscious choice to sacrifice a larger payday for the chance to play alongside Nikola Jokic on a team that has the talent to win a championship. It is a calculation that more veterans are making as their careers enter their final chapters: a ring at the end justifies the financial sacrifice.

The fit makes obvious sense. Denver's offense runs through Jokic's playmaking, and the Nuggets have historically sought players who can score off the catch without requiring the ball in their hands for extended possessions. DeRozan's catch-and-shoot mid-range ability, combined with his veteran IQ and defensive reliability, fills a genuine need.

The question is whether Denver has enough depth and health around Jokic to survive the Western Conference gauntlet — but adding a player of DeRozan's quality on a minimum deal is exactly the kind of move that can push a contender over the edge.

Kawhi Leonard Stays in Toronto: $115M and a Legacy Move

Kawhi Leonard took the opposite path. The Toronto Raptors and Kawhi agreed to a two-year, $115 million extension with a player option in the 2028-29 season, giving Kawhi three total years and approximately $165 million remaining on his contract.

What makes this remarkable is what Kawhi left on the table. By the most common estimates, Kawhi took roughly $18.5 million below the maximum supermax he was eligible to receive. He accepted that discount to give the Raptors the cap flexibility to build a legitimate supporting cast around him — the same franchise thinking that has quietly made Toronto one of the more financially well-managed organizations in the league.

Kawhi returned to Toronto via trade from the LA Clippers this past July — a full-circle moment for the player who led the franchise to its only championship in 2019. His decision to extend at below-max value suggests he genuinely wants to finish his career in Toronto and add a second banner to the Scotiabank Arena rafters.

The Contrast in Philosophy

DeRozan and Kawhi shared the same backcourt in Toronto for years before the franchise-altering 2018 trade that sent Kawhi to San Antonio (and ultimately back to the Raptors via another trade). Now, heading into the twilight of their respective careers, they are making decisions that define what they each want their legacies to look like.

DeRozan is betting that a championship appearance — even in a reduced role — is worth more than another maximum payday on a team that probably cannot win a title. Kawhi is betting that Toronto, with financial flexibility to build around him, gives him the best shot at a second championship while cementing his status as the greatest Raptor of all time.

What's Next

DeRozan will be introduced as a Nugget at Denver's media day (September 28) and joins a roster that will enter 2026-27 as one of the Western Conference's most dangerous teams. Kawhi's Raptors media day appearance — his first since the extension became official — will be one of the most closely watched pressers of the week.

Two careers, two franchises, two very different bets on how to close out a legendary run. Both moves are fascinating for different reasons, and both deserve to be watched closely as the 2026-27 NBA season gets underway.
