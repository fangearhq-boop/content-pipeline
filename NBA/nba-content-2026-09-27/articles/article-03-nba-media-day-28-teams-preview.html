NBA Media Day: 28 Teams, One Day — The Storylines to Watch

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | September 27, 2026

The Philadelphia 76ers went first. The Houston Rockets and Dallas Mavericks held early sessions ahead of the NBA China Games. But tomorrow — September 28 — is the day the other 28 teams finally step in front of the cameras, and the storylines could not be more compelling.

Training camps officially open on September 29. Media day is the last quiet moment before the real work begins. Here is what to watch for as the league's biggest personalities face reporters for the first time this fall.

Detroit Pistons (1:30 PM ET) — The Duren Question

No media day appearance in the league tomorrow carries more stakes than Detroit's. With Jalen Duren's October 1 qualifying offer deadline just four days away, every word — and every absence — will be scrutinized.

GM Trajan Langdon and coach J.B. Bickerstaff are confirmed to speak. Whether Duren is present is an open question. After reportedly turning down a $200M+ extension and growing "increasingly willing" to accept the $9.6M qualifying offer, Duren's relationship with the organization is fragile. This media day could either signal a path toward resolution or confirm that the standoff is real.

Miami Heat (TBD) — Giannis Era Begins Officially

Giannis Antetokounmpo has been working in Miami for weeks, pulling Klay Thompson, Bam Adebayo, and Andrew Wiggins into unofficial California workouts. The Heat set their full 21-player training camp roster. The infrastructure is in place.

But tomorrow is when Giannis stands at a podium in a Miami Heat jersey for the first time and faces the media. His answers about learning Erik Spoelstra's system, his relationship with Bam in a twin-tower frontcourt, and his expectations for this team will set the tone for the entire Giannis era in South Florida.

Boston Celtics (12 PM ET) — Defending Champions Under Pressure

The Celtics are the defending champions, and the target on their backs has never been larger. With the 76ers' superteam now assembled and the Heat adding generational talent, Boston enters this media day needing to project the quiet confidence of a team that knows what it takes to win.

How the Celtics respond to questions about the new Eastern Conference landscape — specifically about LeBron, Embiid, Brown, and Maxey in Philadelphia — will be one of the most-watched moments of the day.

Oklahoma City Thunder (~12:30 PM CT) — Year 3 Expectations

The OKC Thunder are no longer a surprise. After two consecutive years of outperforming expectations, Shai Gilgeous-Alexander and company enter this season as legitimate Western Conference title contenders. The storyline at their media day will be expectations management: how does a team stay hungry when the element of surprise is gone?

Toronto Raptors — Kawhi's $115M Message

Kawhi Leonard just signed a two-year, $115M extension — taking roughly $18.5M below the max to stay in Toronto and give the Raptors cap flexibility to build around him. His first public comments on that decision will be a major moment for the franchise and its fanbase.

Kawhi is not known for extended media availability. Getting any substantive answer about his intentions and his health heading into the season will be a minor victory for reporters.

What's Next

After the cameras go away, the real work starts. Training camps open September 29, preseason games begin in early October, and the regular season tips off in mid-October.

Media day is the NBA's annual reminder that the sport is theater as much as competition — and tomorrow's performance from 28 franchises will set narratives that echo through the entire season. Watch closely. The opening lines of the 2026-27 NBA story are being written in real time.
