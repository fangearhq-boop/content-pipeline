Giannis Returns, Wins: Heat Beat Bucks 137-130 for Sixth Straight

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | November 19, 2026

Giannis Antetokounmpo walked back into the matchup against his former team, delivered a near triple-double, and left with a win. The Miami Heat defeated the Milwaukee Bucks 137-130 on Wednesday night, extending their winning streak to six games while handing Milwaukee its franchise-worst start to a season.

Antetokounmpo finished with 27 points, 14 rebounds, and 9 assists in his first game against the Bucks since the blockbuster trade that sent him to South Beach. Bam Adebayo added 26 points, Davion Mitchell contributed 24, and Miami drained a stunning 22 three-pointers on Vice City Night at Kaseya Center.

Giannis Is All Business

The anticipation surrounding this game was enormous. Antetokounmpo spent more than a decade in Milwaukee, won two MVP awards, and delivered the city its first NBA title in 50 years. His trade to Miami in the offseason sent shockwaves through the league. Wednesday's matchup was billed as the most emotionally loaded regular-season game of the young season.

Antetokounmpo didn't treat it that way.

"As special as every other night," Antetokounmpo said when asked if the win felt meaningful.

His performance backed up that nonchalance. He scored 27 on efficient shooting, dominated the glass with 14 rebounds, and nearly reached a triple-double with 9 assists — all while organizing Miami's offense and anchoring their defense against the team that knows him best.

The Poetic Subplot

The trade that sent Antetokounmpo to Miami brought Tyler Herro, Jaime Jaquez Jr., Kel'el Ware, and Kasparas Jakucionis to Milwaukee. All four played Wednesday. Combined, they scored 49 points for the Bucks — a respectable haul, but not nearly enough to overcome what they gave up.

Milwaukee now sits at 1-14, the worst record in the NBA, an extraordinary reversal for a franchise that reached the Eastern Conference Finals just three seasons ago.

Miami's Hot Streak

The Heat entered Wednesday's game with a 0-4 start that felt like a disaster. Nine wins in their last 11 games later, they are very much a playoff team at 9-6. The 22 three-pointers Wednesday were a season high — a collective explosion that overwhelmed Milwaukee's defense.

Adebayo's 26 points were a critical complement to Antetokounmpo's all-around performance, and Mitchell's 24 gave Miami a third reliable scorer. When three Heat players can eclipse 20 points on a given night, the team is very difficult to beat.

What's Next

Miami will look to extend the winning streak when they return to action later this week. The Bucks face a crossroads moment: at 1-14, coaching and roster decisions will come under intense scrutiny. For Antetokounmpo, Wednesday was just another night — which might be the most revealing statement about his level of focus heading into what he hopes is a championship run with his new team.
