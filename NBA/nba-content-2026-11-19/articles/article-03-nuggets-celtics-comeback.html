Nuggets Rally From 17 Down, Edge Celtics 105-101 Behind Jokic and Murray

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | November 19, 2026

Nikola Jokic (24 points, 8 rebounds, 9 assists) and Jamal Murray (21 points) engineered another remarkable Denver Nuggets comeback Wednesday night, as the Nuggets outscored the Boston Celtics 56-40 in the second half to win 105-101 at Ball Arena. DeMar DeRozan's clutch free throws with 49 seconds remaining gave Denver the lead it would not relinquish.

The Nuggets trailed by as many as 17 points in the first half. That they won at all is a testament to Denver's championship pedigree. That they won with that efficiency is a testament to Jokic.

The Second-Half Takeover

Denver was down at halftime, out-scored and seemingly overmatched. What followed was textbook Nuggets basketball. Jokic dictated the game's tempo — his combination of post scoring, perimeter passing, and rebounding created constant problems for a Boston defense that had no clean answers.

Murray attacked off pick-and-rolls and hit enough mid-range shots to keep Boston's attention split between two elite threats. By the fourth quarter, Denver had erased the deficit and taken control.

DeRozan, who scored 18 points on the night, delivered 13 of them in the fourth quarter. His two free throws with 49 seconds left came moments after Neemias Queta's putback had given Boston a 101-100 lead and briefly threatened to steal the win back for the Celtics.

Tatum Fades Down the Stretch

Jayson Tatum finished with 24 points, but the distribution was damning: 20 in the first half, just 4 in the second. As the Celtics' season-long offense runs through Tatum, his late-game disappearances are becoming a pattern worth monitoring.

Boston was also whistled for 30 fouls compared to Denver's 13, a disparity that made life difficult on the offensive end. Tatum drew a technical foul in the final seconds, compounding a frustrating night. Queta's 17-point, 10-rebound performance offered a bright spot for Boston's side, but it was not enough.

Standings Implications

Denver moved to 8-5 with the win, squarely in the Western Conference playoff picture. Boston dropped to 11-4 — still comfortably ahead in the East, but with questions about second-half reliability that will need answers before January.

What's Next

The Celtics return home to face the Indiana Pacers on Thursday, a chance to reset and respond. Denver continues a homestand before hitting the road. If Wednesday's performance is a blueprint for how the Nuggets navigate mid-season challenges, the West should take note: Jokic, Murray, and DeRozan are not going quietly.
