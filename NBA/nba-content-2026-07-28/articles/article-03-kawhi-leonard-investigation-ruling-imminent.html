NBA's Kawhi Investigation Moves Toward Ruling — Toronto Trade Still Frozen

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | July 28, 2026

The law firm is done. The NBA is reviewing. But "imminent" in the CBA process means there are still steps — and Toronto still can't plan.

The independent law firm hired by the NBA to investigate Kawhi Leonard's endorsement arrangement has completed its fact-finding phase. The league is now internally reviewing the findings.

That is the latest development in a probe that has stretched nearly a full year, frozen a significant trade between two franchises, and created an unprecedented limbo situation at the intersection of NBA discipline and free agency. Commissioner Adam Silver has publicly stated that the investigation must conclude before the 2026-27 season begins. That deadline is now less than three months away.

What does "imminent" actually mean, legally? Less than people might think. The CBA process requires that any proposed punishment be routed through an assistant arbitrator, and the National Basketball Players Association retains the right to appeal any decision. A league review, a proposed sanction, a CBA process, and a potential NBPA appeal could collectively take weeks — even after the initial ruling arrives.

What the Investigation Is About

At the center of the probe is a $28 million endorsement deal between Leonard and Aspiration Fund Adviser LLC, a financial services firm that has since filed for bankruptcy. The investigation is examining whether that deal — and potentially additional personal expense arrangements — violated NBA salary cap rules.

If the league concludes that violations occurred, the range of potential penalties includes voiding the endorsement contract or imposing a fine of up to $350,000 on Leonard or the Clippers organization. Owner Steve Ballmer could also face scrutiny depending on the findings.

The most consequential outcome, however, isn't the fine. It's whether the NBA ultimately approves or blocks the proposed trade that would send Leonard from the Clippers to the Toronto Raptors in exchange for Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, and draft picks.

Toronto Is in Limbo

The Raptors cannot finalize their roster construction around Kawhi's arrival until this investigation concludes. They can't know if the trade will be cleared or reopened for renegotiation. Players who might fit alongside Leonard — or who might be needed if he doesn't come — exist in planning uncertainty.

That situation has persisted since June 30, when the trade was first frozen at the league's direction. Every week of delay is a week the Raptors cannot operate with full information heading into what should be a pivotal offseason.

Heavy.com reported that a ruling was expected after the Summer League concluded. Summer League is now done. The league's review is underway. Silver's October deadline is the floor. But the actual timeline between "reviewing" and "ruling" is not publicly known.

What's Next

Watch for three sequential developments: first, a league ruling; second, a potential CBA arbitration process if the NBPA disputes any penalty; third, a trade approval or rejection from the league office once the investigation's final resolution is known. Each step has its own timeline.

Until all three clear, Kawhi Leonard remains a Clipper on paper, and Toronto's path to rebuilding around a cornerstone player remains on hold. Commissioner Silver's October deadline is the hard stop. What happens between now and then will shape both franchises' next several years.
