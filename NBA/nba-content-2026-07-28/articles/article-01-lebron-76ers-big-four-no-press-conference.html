LeBron's 76ers Big Four Is Official — And He's Not Holding a Press Conference

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | July 28, 2026

The Big 4 is assembled. Pennsylvania already declared LeBron James Day. The only thing missing is the podium — and LeBron doesn't do podiums.

LeBron James has not held an introductory press conference since he joined the Miami Heat in 2010. He didn't hold one in 2018 when he signed with the Los Angeles Lakers. And according to ESPN's Brian Windhorst, he won't be holding one in Philadelphia either.

That's fine. The 76ers don't need the ceremony. What they have is a roster that speaks for itself.

Philadelphia's Big 4 is now officially in place: Tyrese Maxey, VJ Edgecombe, Jaylen Brown, LeBron James, and Joel Embiid constitute a projected starting lineup that is unlike anything the Eastern Conference has seen assembled on a single roster. LeBron signed a two-year, $8 million deal — the veteran's minimum — and the 76ers completed their supporting trade by sending Paul George, two first-round picks, and two second-round picks to the Boston Celtics in exchange for Brown.

LeBron's agent, Rich Paul, has said that Tyrese Maxey was a primary reason his client chose Philadelphia over the competition. LeBron watched Maxey develop into one of the game's premier young guards and decided he wanted to play alongside him, not against him. The decision, Paul has noted, was the first time LeBron made a career choice without external pressure.

What Makes This Different

LeBron has won championships in Cleveland, Miami, and Los Angeles. He enters his 24th NBA season at 41 years old having signed for the veteran's minimum — a contract structure that reflects both his desire to win and his willingness to sacrifice earning power for roster flexibility. The $8 million he earns in Philadelphia is roughly 3 percent of what he could command on a max contract.

Pennsylvania Governor officially declared July 24 as "LeBron James Day" in the commonwealth. The City of Philadelphia prepared a welcome. The 76ers' front office — led by Josh Harris and Bob Myers — assembled the roster LeBron wanted. The one thing that hasn't happened, and won't happen: a press conference.

That's a LeBron signature. In Miami in 2010, his television special "The Decision" served as the announcement, but there was no post-signing podium moment. In Los Angeles, he let the ink dry without ceremony. In Philadelphia, it appears the same principle applies: the work is the announcement.

A Roster Built for Now

The 76ers have assembled a team with a clear championship window. Joel Embiid — who reportedly held a private two-hour meeting with LeBron in Los Angeles roughly a month before the announcement — told LeBron he had just completed his first surgery-free summer in years. That commitment to health reportedly played a significant role in LeBron's decision to choose Philadelphia.

Jaylen Brown adds a versatile two-way wing who can function as a primary scorer when needed. Maxey provides elite guard play and the kind of speed and shot-making that can drag defenses off center. LeBron, even at 41, remains a transformative presence — a player who makes every teammate better simply by reading the game at a level no one else can match.

The title odds responded immediately. The 76ers moved from 20-to-1 longshots to 10-to-1 contenders the moment the deal became official.

What's Next

The 76ers will continue to fill out their roster around the Big 4. Training camp opens in late September, and the 2026-27 schedule — which was reportedly held pending LeBron's decision — is expected to be released as soon as next week. Philadelphia's first game will be must-see television regardless of opponent.

And at some point, in some context, LeBron will speak. He'll appear on a podcast, or at a press availability during training camp, or in a media session before a preseason game. That will be the moment the Philadelphia press corps has been waiting for. But on the question of a formal introductory press conference with a podium, a microphone, and a prepared statement? Don't hold your breath. LeBron doesn't do those anymore.
