Bulls Reportedly Offer DeRozan $80 Million — But the Deal Isn't Done

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | July 28, 2026

Sacramento waived DeRozan. Chicago made an offer. Whether a 37-year-old All-Star fits a stated rebuild is the question the Bulls haven't answered yet.

The Sacramento Kings waived DeMar DeRozan this week, and the Chicago Bulls reportedly moved quickly. According to Yahoo Sports, Chicago has offered the six-time All-Star a two-year deal worth approximately $40 million per season — a total commitment of roughly $80 million for a player who turns 37 next year.

No deal has been confirmed as of July 28. This is a reported offer, not a signed contract. But the situation is live, and the outcome will say something significant about who the Bulls think they are right now.

Why DeRozan and Chicago Make Sense

DeRozan's connection to Chicago is more than professional. He spent three seasons with the Bulls beginning in 2021, and those years represented the most celebrated stretch of his career's second act — he averaged over 26 points per game in his first season in Chicago and earned three consecutive All-Star selections while there. The city embraced him, and the feeling was mutual. He has said publicly he would like to return.

On the court, he remains effective. With Sacramento last season, DeRozan averaged 18.4 points on nearly 50 percent shooting from the field, a mark that demonstrates his mid-range game and footwork have not eroded even as his age climbs. He isn't the player who scored 26 per night in Chicago, but he's still a legitimate contributor on a contending roster.

New Bulls head coach Tiago Splitter would inherit a veteran presence at the small-forward position who understands winning and who has operated in playoff environments. That kind of experience carries real value alongside younger pieces.

The Tension the Bulls Have to Resolve

The complication is what Chicago said it wanted this offseason. The Bulls spent the past several months framing their future around cap flexibility and youth development. The philosophy was build through young pieces, stay patient, let the roster grow. Signing DeRozan at $40 million per year is the opposite of that — it's a win-now move that consumes significant cap space for two seasons on a player in his late 30s.

That doesn't make it the wrong move. Splitter is a first-year head coach; veterans make first-year coaches better. DeRozan's leadership and professionalism are not in question. But the Bulls will have to be honest about which direction they're actually going, because committing $80 million to a 37-year-old veteran and simultaneously talking about youth development are not philosophies that coexist easily.

What's Next

Watch for a formal announcement from either side in the coming days. If the deal closes, DeRozan goes back to a city where he thrived, and the Bulls make a significant statement about their competitive timeline under Splitter. If it falls through, Chicago stays on its stated rebuild path — and DeRozan, still a productive NBA player, finds a different landing spot before training camp opens.

Either way, the offer itself is the story today. The Bulls blinked on their rebuild talk the moment a talent like DeRozan became available.
