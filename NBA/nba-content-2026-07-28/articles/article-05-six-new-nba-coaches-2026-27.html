Six New Head Coaches Are Coming to the NBA — Here's What Each Hire Means

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | July 28, 2026

One-fifth of the league enters 2026-27 under new leadership. From Dusty May coaching Cooper Flagg in Dallas to Taylor Jenkins starting over in Milwaukee, this is the biggest coaching overhaul in years.

The NBA enters the 2026-27 season with six new head coaches — six new offensive systems, six new voices in the locker room, and six new storylines that will unfold over an 82-game season and into the playoffs. It is the largest coaching turnover the league has seen in a single offseason cycle in recent memory.

Here is what each hire signals for the franchise that made it.

Dusty May — Dallas Mavericks

The most consequential hire of the cycle. Dusty May left the Michigan Wolverines immediately after winning an NCAA national championship to take over in Dallas, where the Mavericks hold the No. 1 overall pick in the 2026 draft — Cooper Flagg. May has built a reputation as one of college basketball's best offensive minds, and Dallas is betting that translates to the professional level when the prize pupil is the most celebrated prospect in years.

The pressure on May is enormous and immediate. He has no NBA head coaching experience. He is walking into one of the sport's most scrutinized markets. And his first season will be judged largely on how quickly Flagg develops under his guidance.

Taylor Jenkins — Milwaukee Bucks

Jenkins returns to head coaching after a season away from the sideline following his departure from Memphis. He inherits a Bucks roster that has been completely reconstructed after the Giannis Antetokounmpo trade. The challenge for Jenkins is establishing a team identity and a winning culture from scratch — a different assignment than the one he had in Memphis, where the culture was already built when he arrived.

Tiago Splitter — Chicago Bulls

Splitter steps into one of the more complex situations in the league. The Bulls have talked about a youth rebuild all offseason, but the reported $80 million offer to DeMar DeRozan suggests the front office may be pulling in a different direction. Splitter will need to be clear-eyed about what kind of team he's actually coaching before the season begins.

Sean Sweeney — Orlando Magic

Sweeney spent last season on the San Antonio Spurs staff that reached the NBA Finals. His elevation to head coach in Orlando is a rapid one, but Orlando has young talent — Franz Wagner, Paolo Banchero, and others — that could accelerate under a strong system builder. Sweeney's work with player development in San Antonio earned him this opportunity.

Jamahl Mosley — New Orleans Pelicans

Mosley served as head coach in Orlando before transitioning to New Orleans. He takes over a Pelicans roster with talented pieces but a recent history of injury-related setbacks. His familiarity with player development and his NBA head coaching experience make him a logical fit for a franchise that needs stability.

Micah Nori — Portland Trail Blazers

Nori arrives in Portland as the newest of the six coaches in terms of NBA head coaching experience, but he takes over one of the league's most interesting rosters: Damian Lillard, Deni Avdija, and now Ja Morant. Managing those personalities, keeping them healthy, and turning them into a coherent playoff team is a significant task for a first-year head coach.

What's Next

The 2026-27 NBA schedule is expected to drop as soon as next week, now that LeBron's landing spot has been settled. When it does, each of these six coaches will receive their first look at the 82-game road ahead. Training camps open in late September. By October, the rhetoric and the philosophy give way to the only thing that matters: results.

All six coaches enter their first season with something to prove. The NBA's coaching landscape has rarely offered more compelling opening chapters at once.
