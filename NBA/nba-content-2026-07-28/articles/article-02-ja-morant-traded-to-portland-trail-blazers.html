Grizzlies Trade Ja Morant to Portland — Fresh Start in the Pacific Northwest

By Marcus Cole | Hoop Heroes | July 28, 2026

Memphis gets Jerami Grant and Kris Murray. Portland gets a 26-year-old point guard who could be spectacular again — or might not stay healthy long enough to find out.

The Memphis Grizzlies have traded Ja Morant to the Portland Trail Blazers, ending the complicated chapter that defined the franchise for the past four years and beginning a new one for a player who has too often been defined by what kept him off the floor.

The trade package: Portland sends Jerami Grant, Kris Murray, and $1 million in cash to Memphis. No draft picks change hands. For the Grizzlies, the structure makes sense — they shed a player who appeared in just 79 games across the past three seasons and receive two proven rotation pieces without surrendering future flexibility.

For Portland, it's a gamble on upside. And by every observable measure, it might be a good one.

What Portland Is Getting

When Ja Morant is healthy and engaged, he is one of the most electrifying basketball players on the planet. His athleticism at 6-foot-2 is generational — a guard who attacks the basket with a combination of speed, hang time, and creativity that defenses simply cannot blueprint against.

The problems have been well-documented. Morant served a 25-game suspension in the 2022-23 season following social media incidents and missed additional time in the seasons that followed due to injuries. Across what should have been the prime stretch of his mid-20s, he managed 79 total games. The talent was never in question. The availability was.

Portland is built around pieces that can survive Morant's absences if they recur — Damian Lillard returned to the franchise on a three-year deal after the Milwaukee Bucks waived him, and Deni Avdija emerged as one of the West's most exciting young wings last season. But the goal is clearly to have Morant on the floor, healthy, and playing alongside both of them.

Memphis Turns the Page

From Memphis's perspective, this trade closes a turbulent era. The Grizzlies built their culture around toughness, collective play, and a scrappy identity that Morant embodied on his best days. When things went sideways, that culture paid a real price.

Jerami Grant brings 13-plus points per game of reliable scoring and wing defense. Kris Murray is a young forward still developing his two-way game. Neither player is a franchise cornerstone, but both represent real value — particularly in a trade that cost Memphis no draft capital.

New Portland head coach Micah Nori, a former Minnesota Timberwolves assistant, gets to write the Morant chapter from scratch. Nori has a reputation around the league for player development and offensive creativity. His fingerprints on a healthy Ja Morant would be worth watching.

What's Next

The Trail Blazers will begin training camp in late September with one of the more intriguing rosters in the Western Conference. Morant's first Portland preseason game will be circled on every schedule release. If he opens camp healthy and in rhythm, this trade will immediately look like a steal.

Memphis, meanwhile, begins its rebuild in earnest. The Grant and Murray additions give them two players with legitimate NBA futures. What they do next — in the draft, in free agency, in how they build their next culture — will define whether this trade was the right pivot moment or just the beginning of a longer rebuild than anyone anticipated.
