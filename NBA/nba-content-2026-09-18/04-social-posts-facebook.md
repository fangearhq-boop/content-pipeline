### STORY 1: Curry Extension — Long-Form Post

**Post Window:** 9:00 AM ET

Stephen Curry's extension decision is now 10 days from its natural soft deadline — Warriors media day on September 28.

Curry is eligible to sign a two-year, $136.7 million extension. If he does, he becomes the first player in NBA history to surpass $600 million in career on-court earnings. Both sides have confirmed mutual interest. The Warriors have reportedly given Curry full control over how the deal is structured — an extraordinary show of trust.

But there is no signed contract. And the gap between "mutual interest" and "ink on paper" is growing more visible by the day.

Here is why the structure matters as much as the number: if Curry takes the full maximum extension, the Warriors' projected cap space gets cut roughly in half. With Jimmy Butler III, Draymond Green, and potentially other key pieces headed toward free agency after 2026-27, how Curry structures this deal will shape what Golden State can do for years.

Training camp opens September 29. The regular season is October 20.

Every day between now and Sept. 28 without a deal is its own story.

### STORY 2: DOJ Clippers Probe — Long-Form Post

**Post Window:** 11:00 AM ET

The Department of Justice's criminal probe into the Los Angeles Clippers has entered its fifth week — and there is still no end in sight.

Federal investigators in the Eastern District of New York are examining whether the Clippers arranged secret sponsorship deals worth approximately $18 million for Kawhi Leonard through three separate companies — payments for no actual endorsement work. At least one subpoena has been issued. No charges have been filed.

The NBA has already delivered its punishment: a $30 million fine, five lost first-round draft picks (2029–2033), and suspensions for owner Steve Ballmer (one year), president Lawrence Frank (six months), executive Gillian Zucker (one year), and governor Peter Robertson (five years). Leonard received a $700,000 fine. His trade to the Toronto Raptors has been ratified.

The federal process is different — slower, more deliberate, and potentially much more consequential. A DOJ investigation at the "earliest stages" could mean months or years of work before any announcement. Or it could close quietly.

The 2026-27 season opens in 32 days. The Clippers' new chapter begins without Kawhi Leonard. But the shadow of the investigation will follow them into it.

### STORY 3: Jalen Duren QO — Long-Form Post

**Post Window:** 1:00 PM ET

The Jalen Duren standoff is nearing its end — and according to Marc Stein, the end is not what Detroit hoped for.

Stein reported Thursday that Duren is now "increasingly prepared" to accept his $9.6 million qualifying offer if the Pistons cannot deliver a deal by end of September. The Oct. 1 deadline is 13 days away. Detroit's last offer was five years at $190 million ($38 million per year average). Duren wants at least $200 million — closer to $40 million per year.

The stakes are enormous. If Duren accepts the qualifying offer, he plays the coming season for $9.6 million. He stays a Piston. But — critically — Detroit loses all matching rights. Next summer, Duren is an unrestricted free agent, and the Sacramento Kings are reportedly waiting with a max offer.

This is how franchise-altering decisions get made. Not in a dramatic press conference, but in a slow-moving negotiation where one side's final offer isn't enough and the other side calculates that patience is worth the risk.

The Pistons have 13 days to change the math.

### STORY 4: Knicks Banner Night — Long-Form Post

**Post Window:** 4:00 PM ET

Madison Square Garden paused ticket sales for Opening Night after a technical error sent prices soaring above $1,000 per ticket — before secondary market resale even began.

The demand makes sense. October 20 at MSG is shaping up to be one of the most culturally loaded nights in NBA history.

The New York Knicks will raise their first championship banner since 1973 at 6:30 PM ET, celebrating their 2026 NBA title — a five-game series win over the San Antonio Spurs — in front of a home crowd that waited 53 years for this moment. Finals MVP Jalen Brunson leads the ceremony.

Then the game tips off at 7:00 PM ET on NBC and Peacock. And the opponent makes it perfect: the Philadelphia 76ers, featuring LeBron James (41 years old) making his villain debut at MSG alongside Jaylen Brown, Joel Embiid, and Tyrese Maxey.

LeBron will watch a championship banner go up in the building before he can do anything about it. That is the script. The Knicks couldn't have written a better first act.
