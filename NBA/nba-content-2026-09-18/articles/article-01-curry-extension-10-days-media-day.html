10 Days to Media Day: Curry Extension Silence Gets Louder

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | September 18, 2026

Golden State Warriors

Ten days. The countdown clock ticked another number off Thursday, and Stephen Curry still has not signed the two-year, $136.7 million contract extension that would make him the first player in NBA history to eclipse $600 million in career on-court earnings. Warriors media day is September 28. The silence from both sides is not alarming — but it is getting louder.

ESPN's Anthony Slater confirmed weeks ago that both Curry and the Warriors share a mutual desire to keep the four-time champion in Golden State for the remainder of his career. The organization has given Curry an unusual level of deference over the deal's structure — effectively letting him decide how the contract affects the franchise's future flexibility. That is not nothing. That is trust, made financial.

What the Numbers Mean

The maximum available extension is two years, $136.7 million. Sign it in full and Curry clears $600 million in career earnings — a benchmark no NBA player has ever crossed. That milestone is significant but not the deciding factor. The structure matters as much as the total.

If Curry accepts the full maximum, the Warriors' projected cap space gets cut roughly in half. Golden State is projecting more than $130 million in space next offseason — before any Curry extension. A full deal drops that to somewhere between $45 million and $65 million. That number is the difference between transformational free agency and incremental improvement.

The variable is who else is hitting free agency. Draymond Green and Jimmy Butler III are both entering the final year of their contracts in 2026-27. The Warriors face a decision cascade — one in which how Curry structures his extension sets the parameters for everything that follows.

The Media Day Factor

September 28 is not a hard deadline. Curry can technically sign an extension at any point before the regular season tip-off on October 20. But media day has always carried a different weight.

If Curry walks into the Warriors' facility on September 28 without a deal in place, the first question every reporter asks is the obvious one. The extension story consumes media day entirely — not the season preview, not the training camp schedule, not the team's goals for a title run. The extension. Only the extension.

Warriors brass understands this. Beat reporters understand this. Curry's camp understands this. The gravitational pull of media day as a soft deadline is real even if no contract language makes it binding.

Where Things Stand

No deal announced. No deadline issued within negotiations. The public signals from both sides remain warm — mutual interest, Curry choosing his structure, a franchise at peace with however he decides. Warm signals, however, are not contracts.

Curry is 38. He has four championships, two MVPs, the all-time three-point record, and a legacy so secure that the extension is more closing ceremony than chapter opener. He is, by any reasonable accounting, deciding how he wants to end his career — not whether he will.

The 10-day window is not a crisis. It is a narrowing. The question — as it has been for two weeks — is whether Curry wants his opening media day soundbite to be about basketball, or about a contract that has not yet been signed.

What's Next

Warriors media day is September 28. Training camp opens September 29, with Golden State heading to Hawaii. Preseason begins October 3; the regular season tips off October 20 with the Knicks' banner ceremony and the 76ers' arrival at Madison Square Garden. If the extension is signed before media day, Golden State begins camp with its future anchored. If not, the question follows them to Hawaii. Either way, the clock is down to 10.
