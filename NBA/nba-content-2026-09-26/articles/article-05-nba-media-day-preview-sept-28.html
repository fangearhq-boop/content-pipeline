NBA Media Day Preview: 5 Storylines to Watch When 28 Teams Go Public Monday

By Damon Pierce | Hoop Heroes | September 26, 2026

The Philadelphia 76ers went today. The Mavericks and Rockets went last week. But the rest of the NBA — 28 teams — converges on Monday, September 28 for the league's mass media day, the annual event where months of trade rumors and offseason speculation give way to actual quotes, actual faces, and actual accountability.

Training camps open September 29. The regular season begins October 20. Monday is where it all starts to feel real. Here are the five storylines that will define media day coverage this year.

1. Will Jalen Duren Show Up in Detroit?

The Pistons hold their media day Monday, and whether their franchise center is in the building is the biggest yes/no question in the league right now. Duren reportedly turned down a "MEGA" offer this week. His attendance has been described as "highly questionable" for days. An absence would be a statement — not just about the contract, but about the relationship between a team and a player they've built their identity around.

Pistons coach J.B. Bickerstaff and teammates will face questions regardless. But the moment everyone is watching is whether Duren walks through the door.

2. Giannis + Bam: How Does Spoelstra Frame It?

Erik Spoelstra has a gift for managing complex roster dynamics with language that deflects rather than inflames. But on Monday in Miami, he'll be asked — repeatedly — how he plans to use two dominant frontcourt players who both need the ball to be effective. His answer will be the first public framing of the Heat's tactical identity for 2026-27.

Giannis will also be at a podium for the first time in a Heat uniform. Expect the questions to focus on his adjustment, his goals, and how he views Spoelstra's system after weeks of unofficial prep.

3. LaMelo and Edwards in Minnesota: Can They Coexist?

The Timberwolves made one of the summer's most dramatic acquisitions, trading Naz Reid and four first-round picks to bring LaMelo Ball alongside Anthony Edwards. Both are ball-dominant offensive players at their best. The Wolves haven't played a single game together — and the trade candidates looming over the roster (reports indicate Donte DiVincenzo could be gone before the season starts) add more uncertainty.

Camp media day gives LaMelo and Edwards their first joint press availability. The tone they set publicly matters.

4. Kawhi Leonard and Load Management in Toronto

Kawhi Leonard signed a two-year, $115 million extension to stay in Toronto and help rebuild the Raptors. He's 35, he has a history of managing his minutes carefully, and the Raptors are not expected to contend immediately. The question for media day: how does Kawhi describe his role? What does the Raptors' plan look like for managing a superstar in the early stages of a rebuild?

5. Warriors in Hawaii

Golden State holds a unique training camp this year — on the campus of BYU-Hawaii starting September 29. Media day Monday will feature the Warriors back in the Bay before they decamp to the islands. Whatever Stephen Curry has to say about his team's goals, it will happen in front of a Bay Area media contingent for the last time before the Hawaii session begins.

What's Next

Monday's media days run throughout the day across all 28 markets. Beat reporters will file quotes, coaches will outline visions, and players will face the questions that have been building all summer. Training camp begins September 29 across the league. The preseason tips off in early October. And on October 20, the 2026-27 NBA regular season opens with the Sixers at the Knicks — the first answers to the questions that form this Monday.
