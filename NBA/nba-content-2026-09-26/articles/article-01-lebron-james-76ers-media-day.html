LeBron James Officially a Sixer: 'I Want to Help Joel Embiid Win a Title'

By Jake Torres | Hoop Heroes | September 26, 2026

LeBron James walked into a room in Camden, New Jersey on Friday morning and officially became a Philadelphia 76er.

At the team's media day, James spoke publicly for the first time as a member of the Sixers — alongside Joel Embiid, Jaylen Brown, Tyrese Maxey, and rookie V.J. Edgecombe — and delivered the kind of clarity that had been swirling in speculation since July. His reasons for leaving Los Angeles were straightforward: a long-standing friendship with Maxey, and a singular desire to help Embiid finally win a championship.

"My friendship with Maxey was real," James said. "And the opportunity to help Joel win — that's a challenge I couldn't pass up."

The Contract That Surprised Everyone

The numbers raised eyebrows across the league. LeBron James, 41 years old and still playing at an All-Star level, signed a two-year, $8 million deal with Philadelphia. For context, a mid-level exception is worth more than twice that. James left substantial money on the table to be in a position to compete for one more title.

It is the kind of move that only a player with a certain security — financial, legacy-wise, and personally — can make. James has four championships, four MVP awards, and a case as the greatest player ever. What he doesn't have is a fifth ring. In Philly, he believes he can get it.

The Lineup on Paper

When LeBron takes the floor on October 20 for opening night, he will do so alongside one of the most decorated starting fives constructed in recent memory. Maxey runs the point and is already one of the league's best scorers. Embiid, when healthy, is a top-five player who has reached multiple conference finals but never captured a title. Brown, acquired from the Celtics in a trade that sent Paul George and four picks to Boston, is a two-time champion and elite two-way wing.

Adding LeBron to that group was the final piece of a summer that will be debated in NBA circles for years. Whether this team clicks — whether four stars with the ball in their hands can coexist — is the central question of the 2026-27 Eastern Conference.

The Opening Night Stakes

The Sixers' first game is no ordinary road trip. On October 20, they travel to Madison Square Garden to face the reigning champion New York Knicks — on the night the Knicks raise their first championship banner since 1973. It will be LeBron's debut, Brown's debut, and a full-on statement game for a 76ers franchise that was swept 4-0 by New York in last season's playoffs.

Embiid has spoken about that loss. The Sixers have talked about it. And now they have reinforcements. Media day is where the words start. October 20 is where the answers begin.

What's Next

Training camp opens September 29 at the Sixers' facility in Camden. The preseason schedule begins in early October before the regular season opener on October 20. With LeBron now formally in the fold, the storyline entering camp is less about roster construction and more about integration: how quickly can four stars find their rhythm? Spoelstra's Heat hold media day tomorrow, and the Knicks the same day. By Monday night, all 30 teams will have gone through their opening ceremonies. The 2026-27 season is no longer theoretical. It starts now.
