US Open 2026: Two Weeks Out, the Field Is Taking Shape

By Ryan Calloway | August 14, 2026

With both Canadian titles decided and Cincinnati underway, the final picture before the US Open begins to crystallize — and the questions around Sinner and Alcaraz loom over everything.

The US Open begins August 30 at Flushing Meadows. Two weeks out, the tennis world knows a lot more than it did a month ago — and some of the most important questions still have no answers.

Thursday's results clarified the picture considerably. Ben Shelton defended his Montreal title without dropping a set. Iga Swiatek ended a 10-month title drought with a dominant Toronto final victory. Both enter Cincinnati and then New York with the momentum of champions. The hard part for everyone else is figuring out how to beat them on fast hardcourts when they are playing this well.

The Men's Side: Shelton Surges, Sinner and Alcaraz Are Question Marks

Jannik Sinner remains the world No. 1, but the Italian has not competed since Wimbledon. He withdrew from Cincinnati with a right knee injury and is targeting the US Open as his return. His last Grand Slam appearance saw him win Wimbledon in July — but three weeks of rest and a knee concern entering one of the sport's biggest events creates uncertainty.

Carlos Alcaraz has been sidelined since April 14, when he sustained wrist tenosynovitis at the Barcelona Open. He missed Roland Garros, Wimbledon, and the Canadian Open and did not enter Cincinnati. His US Open participation remains unconfirmed, and the defending champion's status is the biggest variable in the men's draw.

In that vacuum, Shelton has seized the moment. The 24-year-old American is now a two-time Montreal champion, a title he won back-to-back in consecutive summers. He has the serve, the backhand, the firepower — and on this evidence, the mental resilience — to go deep in New York. Add in Frances Tiafoe, who is recovering from right-hand cyst surgery and targeting a US Open return, and the American men's story at Flushing Meadows is genuinely compelling.

Alexander Zverev (No. 1 seed Cincinnati) and Novak Djokovic (No. 3) are the other major factors. Zverev won Roland Garros in June; Djokovic is returning from Wimbledon and brings his remarkable record at the US Open with him.

The Women's Side: Swiatek in Form, Sabalenka the Constant

Iga Swiatek just won Toronto 6-2, 6-3 in a final that looked like something from a different era — baseline dominance that simply overwhelmed a quality opponent in Elena Rybakina. Her 12th WTA 1000 title and first of 2026 sends a clear message: the former No. 1 is operating near peak level when it matters most.

Aryna Sabalenka has been the most consistent player on the WTA hard-court swing for months, carrying a long winning streak into Toronto before falling to Alexandrova in an upset. She arrives at Cincinnati as the No. 1 seed and has deep US Open credentials — she won the title in 2023 and has consistently gone far at Flushing Meadows.

Rybakina, Gauff, and Svitolina round out the field worth watching. Rybakina was in the Toronto final and fired 13 aces in her quarterfinal win over Osaka earlier in the week. Gauff, the 2023 US Open champion, has been building momentum on the hard courts. Svitolina extended a 10-match WTA 1000 winning streak in earlier rounds of Toronto before losing to Swiatek in the semifinals.

What's Next

Cincinnati runs through August 23. After that, all roads lead to Flushing Meadows and the US Open, which begins August 30 with qualifying already underway. The main draw runs through September 13. The two weeks between now and the first round in New York will either confirm what Thursday's results suggest — or produce the kind of surprise that makes tennis unpredictable. Watch Cincinnati carefully. It is the last data point before the year's final Grand Slam.
