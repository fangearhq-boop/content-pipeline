Nakashima Reaches Career-High No. 22 After Historic Montreal Final Run

By Elena Voss | August 14, 2026

Despite losing the final to Shelton, Brandon Nakashima exits Montreal having reached his first-ever ATP Masters 1000 final and his highest-ever ATP ranking.

Brandon Nakashima walked off the court in Montreal on Thursday night as runner-up. He woke up Friday morning at No. 22 in the world — the highest ranking of his career.

That juxtaposition is what a first Masters 1000 final does for you. Nakashima, seeded 28th at the National Bank Open, produced one of the best weeks of his professional career before falling to defending champion Ben Shelton 6-3, 7-6(4) in an all-American final. The defeat is the least interesting part of the story.

A Week to Remember

Nakashima's Montreal run included some eye-catching individual performances. Against Lorenzo Darderi in the quarterfinals, he hit 11 aces in a 1-hour, 17-minute victory that required no breaks of serve. Against Rafael Jodar in the semifinals, he outlasted the 19-year-old Spaniard 7-6(3), 6-4 to book his place in the biggest final of his career.

The final itself was competitive until the end. Shelton broke in the third game of the first set and held the advantage comfortably. In the second set, Nakashima pushed back and leveled late, forcing a tiebreak — but Shelton's return game was too consistent when it mattered, and the American champion sealed it 7-4.

As the 28th seed making a Masters 1000 final, Nakashima created a piece of history simply by getting there. Masters 1000 finals are typically dominated by top-10 players; reaching one from outside the top 25 seedings is a notable achievement.

A UVA Cavaliers Final

One detail that will enter the trivia books: both Nakashima and Shelton played collegiate tennis at the University of Virginia. Their meeting in the Montreal final was the first time two former Cavaliers faced each other in an ATP Masters 1000 final. That kind of coincidence tends to become part of a tournament's lore, and this one will be remembered fondly by UVA fans for years.

Nakashima is 26 years old and from Carlsbad, California. The No. 22 ranking represents a jump from his pre-tournament position and will secure him a top seeding at the US Open later this month.

What's Next

Brandon Nakashima heads to Cincinnati and then to the US Open, which begins August 30 in New York. The career-high No. 22 ranking means he will receive a seeding at Flushing Meadows — his first time entering a Grand Slam with that kind of protection on the draw. After the week he just had in Montreal, the question is whether he can bring that form into the biggest stage in American tennis.
