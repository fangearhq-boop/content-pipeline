Cincinnati Day 2: Zverev and Djokovic Await as Western & Southern Open Builds Toward US Open

By Marcus Cole | August 14, 2026

The 125th edition of the Cincinnati Open enters its second day of first-round action, with the tournament's top seeds set to debut on Saturday.

The Western & Southern Open is getting underway in Mason, Ohio, but the players everyone wants to see most are not on the court yet.

Friday is Day 2 of the 125th men's edition of the Cincinnati Open at Lindner Family Tennis Centre. It features first-round matches for players who did not receive byes — including a notable contest between Sebastian Baez and Grigor Dimitrov on the ATP side. But all eyes are on Saturday, when the tournament's top seeds make their debuts.

No. 1 seed Alexander Zverev faces the winner of Cameron Norrie and Dino Prizmic in the first round. No. 3 seed Novak Djokovic — returning to competition for the first time since Wimbledon — will meet Thiago Agustin Tirante. Djokovic's participation here is one of the most anticipated storylines of the Cincinnati week, as the 39-year-old works to find his form heading into the US Open.

The Field Without Sinner and Alcaraz

The Cincinnati draw is shaped significantly by two major absences. Jannik Sinner, the world No. 1, withdrew before the tournament with a right knee injury and is targeting the US Open as his return event. Carlos Alcaraz, the defending Cincinnati champion, is still sidelined with the wrist tenosynovitis that has kept him out since April 14 and also skipped this event.

That leaves Zverev as the top seed and Djokovic as the third, with the field opened up considerably for others. Ben Shelton, the freshly-crowned two-time Montreal champion, was seeded No. 8 in the Cincinnati draw but will have to manage his recovery from the elbow injury he sustained in last week's semifinal. Cobolli (7th seed), Fritz (6th), and de Minaur (5th) round out a field that represents the best of those who are healthy.

WTA Picture

On the women's side, Aryna Sabalenka holds the No. 1 seed, with Rybakina as No. 2 and Gauff as No. 4. Iga Swiatek, the newly crowned Toronto champion, enters as a significant threat despite her seeding. Swiatek was the 2025 Cincinnati champion and brings immediate momentum into this week after her dominant 6-2, 6-3 final against Rybakina in Toronto on Thursday.

Both Sabalenka and Rybakina have had strong hard-court summers. Sabalenka carried a long hard-court winning streak into the Toronto week before Alexandrova stunned her in the round of 16. Rybakina reached the Toronto final. The WTA Cincinnati draw should produce quality tennis throughout.

What's Next

The Western & Southern Open runs through August 23, with the men's and women's finals on the same weekend. After Cincinnati, the entire field moves to Flushing Meadows for the US Open, which begins August 30. Saturday brings Zverev and Djokovic into action in Cincinnati — the first real test for the tournament's biggest names in what could be their final tune-up before New York.
