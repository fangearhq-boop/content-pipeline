Swiatek Wins Toronto, Ends 10-Month Title Drought with Dominant Final Victory

By Ryan Calloway | August 14, 2026

Iga Swiatek defeated Elena Rybakina 6-2, 6-3 to claim her first WTA title since 2025 and her 12th career WTA 1000 crown.

Iga Swiatek is a champion again — and for the first time in the 2026 season, the tennis world was reminded just how good she can be.

Swiatek defeated Elena Rybakina 6-2, 6-3 in Thursday's National Bank Open final in Toronto, claiming her 12th career WTA 1000 title and her first since roughly 10 months ago. As the No. 7 seed facing the No. 2 seed in the final, the scoreline tells the story: this was a controlled, dominant performance from a player who appeared to be operating at a different speed than her opponent.

Multiple sources described Swiatek as dictating from the baseline from the opening game. Rybakina, who had produced three set-down comebacks earlier in the Toronto draw, had no answer this time. The first set was over in a hurry at 6-2, and the second followed the same pattern.

Career Sweep of the North American Swing

According to Tennis Majors, Thursday's victory completes "a career sweep of the North American swing" for Swiatek — she has now won titles at all three North American hard-court venues on the WTA calendar. It is her first title at Toronto specifically.

The win is also her first WTA 1000 final appearance in 2026. At Roland Garros, Swiatek was eliminated in a surprising defeat to Mirra Kostyuk. Her performance in Toronto represented a return to the baseline authority that has defined her career at the sport's highest level.

Entering the final, Swiatek had beaten former No. 1s and top seeds throughout the week despite carrying only the No. 7 seed. The semifinals victory over Svitolina — their third meeting of 2026, with Svitolina having won the previous two — demonstrated her ability to solve tactical problems that had frustrated her earlier in the year.

Rybakina's Week

For Elena Rybakina, the runner-up result still represents a strong Toronto week. The Kazakhstani No. 2 seed beat Gauff in the semifinals — coming from a set down — and produced 13 aces in her quarterfinal win over Osaka. But in the final against a Swiatek playing at this level, the margin was simply too wide.

Rybakina enters Cincinnati as the No. 2 seed and will be one of the key factors in any US Open conversation after a consistent North American summer.

What's Next

Iga Swiatek heads to Cincinnati for the Western & Southern Open, where she was the defending 2025 champion. With the US Open beginning August 30 at Flushing Meadows, Swiatek enters the final stretch of the season as the player with the most momentum on the WTA side. Her 12th WTA 1000 title and first of 2026 cement her position as the frontrunner heading into New York.
