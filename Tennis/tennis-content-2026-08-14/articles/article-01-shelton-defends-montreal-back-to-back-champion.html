Shelton Defends Montreal Title, Joins Murray and Djokovic in Canadian Masters History

By Marcus Cole | August 14, 2026

Ben Shelton defeated Brandon Nakashima 6-3, 7-6(4) to become the first man since Rafael Nadal to defend the National Bank Open crown.

Ben Shelton is a back-to-back Canadian Masters champion, and tennis history is paying attention.

The American No. 5 seed defeated compatriot Brandon Nakashima 6-3, 7-6(4) in Thursday's National Bank Open final in Montreal, successfully defending the title he won in 2025. In doing so, Shelton became the first man since Rafael Nadal — who won consecutive editions in 2018 and 2019 — to repeat as champion in Canada. He joined Andy Murray (2009-10) and Novak Djokovic (2011-12) as the only men in the Open Era to hold that distinction.

The performance was all the more striking given the context. Shelton had entered the tournament 1-4 in Masters 1000 events this year. He leaves without having dropped a set across five matches — a level of dominance that would be impressive in any week, and becomes remarkable in the week after his semifinal collision with an LCD advertising panel left his left elbow and hand bleeding.

"One match can change everything," Shelton said afterward.

A Final Between Cavaliers

Thursday's final carried its own subplot: both Shelton and Nakashima played college tennis at the University of Virginia, making it an all-UVA Cavaliers final at a Masters 1000 event — a combination no one had ever seen before.

Nakashima, the 28th seed, had earned his place in the final through a week of exceptional tennis. He fired 11 aces in a 1-hour, 17-minute quarterfinal victory over Darderi and then outlasted Jodar in the semifinals. Against Shelton, he pushed the second set to a tiebreak but fell 7-4 as Shelton's return game proved too consistent at the key moments.

The loss does not diminish what Nakashima accomplished. As a 28th seed reaching a Masters 1000 final, he will rise to a career-high No. 22 in the ATP rankings — the biggest ranking achievement of his career.

The Bigger Picture

For Shelton, the timing of the Montreal title could not be better. The US Open begins August 30, and Shelton arrives at Cincinnati this week and then Flushing Meadows with a trophy, a confidence boost, and a physical test already behind him. His left arm, despite Thursday's bandaging, held up through a full match.

The men's draw at the US Open shapes up around significant absences. Jannik Sinner, the world No. 1, withdrew from Cincinnati with a right knee injury and is targeting the Open as his return. Carlos Alcaraz has been sidelined since April 14 with wrist tenosynovitis and also skipped Cincinnati. Shelton's form fills part of the void left by those absences — the defending Montreal champion now enters as one of the headliners of the American hard-court summer.

In Monday's doubles final, Orlando Luz and Rafael Matos of Brazil won the Canadian Masters doubles title, becoming the first Brazilian pair to win an ATP Masters 1000 doubles event.

What's Next

Ben Shelton heads to the Western & Southern Open in Cincinnati, where he was seeded No. 8. The tournament runs through August 23, serving as the final tuneup before the US Open. For Nakashima, the new career-high ranking of No. 22 opens the door to direct entry at major events and should secure a high seeding at Flushing Meadows. His Montreal run is the foundation on which the rest of his 2026 season will be measured.
