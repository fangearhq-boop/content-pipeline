### STORY 2: Rybakina Injury

**Post 2 — Long-Form** — 12:30 PM ET
Every day counts, and Elena Rybakina is running out of them.

The world No. 2 and US Open's second seed has not stepped on a practice court since retiring from her Cincinnati quarterfinal on August 21 with a left ankle injury. That's seven days of recovery — and the main draw begins this Sunday.

On Thursday, Rybakina told reporters she doesn't want to rush her return but is focused on being ready. She stopped short of confirming 100% participation. For a player who has won two Grand Slam titles and was considered a serious contender for this year's title, her status is one of the biggest questions hanging over Flushing Meadows.

If she can't go, her first-round opponent gets a walkover — and the women's draw reshuffles around her absence. If she plays through pain, she does so against players who've been match-sharp for weeks.

Tennis fans: are you rooting for Rybakina to make it? Or does her absence open the door for someone else?

### STORY 5: Arthur Fils

**Post 5 — Long-Form** — 3:00 PM ET
He just won the biggest title of his career. Now Arthur Fils gets a Round 1 draw that tests everything.

Last week in Cincinnati, the 21-year-old Frenchman defeated Frances Tiafoe 6-3, 1-6, 6-0 to claim his first Masters 1000 title — the most prestigious trophy below a Grand Slam. In doing so, Fils became the first Frenchman to win in Cincinnati since Guy Forget in 1991 and the first French winner of a Masters title since Jo-Wilfried Tsonga in 2014. He and Tiafoe also made history as the first two Black men to contest a Masters 1000 final.

At the US Open, seeded 14th, Fils opens against Stefanos Tsitsipas — a two-time Grand Slam finalist who knows how to play in New York. If Fils wins, he faces a draw that could bring him face-to-face with some of the sport's biggest names.

Momentum is real in tennis. And right now, Arthur Fils has as much of it as anyone.

Can he carry Cincinnati form into Flushing Meadows?
