Arthur Fils Won Cincinnati. His Reward at the US Open: Tsitsipas in Round 1.

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap

August 28, 2026

There is no easy path for Arthur Fils. He wouldn't have it any other way.

Last week in Cincinnati, the 21-year-old Frenchman won the biggest title of his career — the Masters 1000 championship at the Western and Southern Open, defeating fellow American Frances Tiafoe 6-3, 1-6, 6-0 in the final. It was his first Masters 1000 title and his fifth career ATP Tour trophy. He enters the US Open as the No. 14 seed, a Cincinnati champion, and the hottest player in men's tennis.

His first-round opponent at Flushing Meadows: Stefanos Tsitsipas, a two-time Grand Slam finalist.

A Historic Cincinnati Title

The numbers surrounding Fils's Cincinnati win carry historical weight. He became the first Frenchman to win the Cincinnati Open since Guy Forget claimed the title in 1991 — a 35-year gap in French champions at one of the tour's most prestigious Masters events. He is also the first French player to win any Masters 1000 title since Jo-Wilfried Tsonga won the Canadian Open in 2014.

In the final itself, Fils and Frances Tiafoe made history together: they were the first two Black men to meet in an ATP Masters 1000 final. The match drew significant attention beyond the tennis world, celebrated as a milestone for representation at the sport's highest levels.

Fils won the first set 6-3 before Tiafoe took the second 6-1. In a decisive third set, Fils was ruthless — closing it 6-0, allowing just one game in the set that put him in the record books.

Into the US Open

The Cincinnati title elevated Fils to No. 14 in the US Open seedings — a ranking that earns him structural protection in the early rounds but doesn't spare him from a tough first-round opponent. Tsitsipas, a former world No. 3 and a veteran of two Grand Slam finals, is precisely the kind of player who can end a momentum run if not taken seriously.

In the broader draw, Fils's path is characteristically demanding. The French star would need to navigate through the top half of the bracket, where Zverev and Auger-Aliassime are seeded, to make a serious run. That's the US Open: you earn it or you go home.

A Player on the Rise

Fils has been widely projected as a future Grand Slam contender — athletic, with a powerful forehand and the ability to dictate from the baseline. The Cincinnati title provides the first major evidence that the projection is becoming reality. Masters 1000 events are won by elite players. Fils is now among them.

Whether his Cincinnati form translates to a major — where the field is larger, the rounds longer, and the pressure unmatched — remains the defining question. The US Open is where those answers get tested.

What's Next

Arthur Fils opens his 2026 US Open campaign in Round 1 Sunday, August 30 against Stefanos Tsitsipas. A Fils victory would set up a second-round match and continued momentum through the draw. A loss to Tsitsipas would end the Cincinnati champion's tenure before the second week of the tournament.
