US Open 2026 Preview: Zverev, Alcaraz, and the Draw That Has Everyone Talking

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap

August 28, 2026

Two days. One of the sport's grandest stages. And a draw loaded with the kind of storylines that make the US Open the most dramatic Grand Slam on the calendar.

The 2026 US Open begins Sunday, August 30, at the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center in Flushing Meadows, New York — and this year's field arrives with more questions than answers. That's not a complaint. That's what makes the next two weeks unmissable tennis.

Men's Draw: Zverev Inherits No. 1

The biggest structural change to the men's draw comes before the first ball is struck. Jannik Sinner — the world No. 1 and reigning Australian Open champion — has withdrawn from the tournament due to a knee injury. That elevated Alexander Zverev to the top seed, a position the German has long been projected to hold when his time comes.

Zverev's time appears to be now. He won Roland Garros earlier this year and reached the Wimbledon final, going 18-2 at Grand Slam level in 2026. He begins his title run against Lorenzo Sonego, with a projected quarterfinal against No. 6 Alex de Minaur if seeds hold.

Carlos Alcaraz, the defending champion, is the No. 2 seed — and the most compelling figure in the draw. Alcaraz has not played a singles match since April's Barcelona Open, where he was diagnosed with tenosynovitis in his right wrist. He missed the French Open, Wimbledon, Toronto, and Cincinnati. He skipped the Winston-Salem tune-up as well, opting to go directly to Flushing Meadows. He is returning cold into a Grand Slam, and he and Novak Djokovic land on the same side of the bracket.

Djokovic — seeded fourth — is chasing his 25th Grand Slam title, a number that would stand alone in the record books. At 39, he continues to push forward with a singular focus. Felix Auger-Aliassime is seeded third and opens against Rinky Hijikata in what projects as a competitive first-round test.

Among the intrigue in the lower seeds: Arthur Fils, the No. 14 seed who won the Cincinnati Masters 1000 last week, opens against Stefanos Tsitsipas in a match that would be a quarterfinal-level clash at most tournaments. Fils arrives with momentum; Tsitsipas arrives with experience and two Grand Slam final appearances.

Other first-round highlights: Wawrinka vs. Berrettini (two players with serious serve-and-power games) and Rublev vs. Cilic.

Women's Draw: Sabalenka Hunts History

Aryna Sabalenka is the No. 1 seed and the two-time defending US Open champion. A third consecutive title would put her in the rarest company in the sport — and she opens against Camila Osorio in a first-round match that, on paper, presents little danger.

The bigger drama in the women's draw centers on No. 2 seed Elena Rybakina, who retired from her Cincinnati quarterfinal with a left ankle injury and has not practiced since. As of Thursday, Rybakina had not confirmed she would play, leaving one of the draw's most significant unknowns unresolved. If she withdraws, someone moves up in the bracket and Rybakina's first-round opponent receives a walkover.

Iga Swiatek enters as the No. 8 seed — a ranking that understates her threat level. On the other side of the draw, Swiatek could meet No. 2 Rybakina in the quarterfinals if both advance.

No. 4 Coco Gauff leads a quarter that also includes No. 17 Alex Eala, whose seeding at the US Open marks the highest Grand Slam seeding ever by a Filipino player. No. 6 Linda Noskova, the 2026 Wimbledon champion, rounds out a women's draw with multiple plausible paths to the title.

What to Watch

Can Alcaraz — rusty and returning from months off — defend his title? Or does the rust cost him in an early round? Can Rybakina compete at full fitness, or does injury force her out of one of the year's final major events? And will Djokovic, at 39, find one more Grand Slam run in him?

The 2026 US Open has the ingredients for a memorable two weeks. The first serve lands Sunday.
