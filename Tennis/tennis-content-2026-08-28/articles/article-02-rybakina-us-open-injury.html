Rybakina's US Open Status in Doubt: No. 2 Seed Has Not Practiced After Cincinnati Ankle Injury

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap

August 28, 2026

The 2026 US Open main draw begins Sunday, August 30 — and one of the biggest names in the women's field cannot yet confirm she will be there.

Elena Rybakina, the world No. 2 and the tournament's second seed, retired from her Cincinnati Open quarterfinal on August 21 with a left ankle injury after an awkward landing at the net while trailing 1-4 in the opening set against Iga Swiatek. Since then, the Kazakh star has not returned to the practice court and, as of Thursday, declined to confirm 100% participation in the US Open.

"I don't want to rush," Rybakina told reporters, according to Puntodebreak.com. "I want to make sure that everything is okay."

A Seven-Day Gap With No Practice

The math is stark. Rybakina has had seven days since her Cincinnati retirement with no match play and no confirmed practice sessions. First-round matches begin Sunday — which means she would have at most one or two days to test the ankle under court conditions before being called to compete in a Grand Slam.

Tennis365.com reported that Rybakina has not hit at the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center, where US Open players typically begin arriving for pre-tournament practice sessions this week. That absence is notable for a player who is considered among the two or three best in the world on hard courts.

The injury to Rybakina's left ankle reportedly had been bothering her through consecutive weeks of competition in Toronto and Cincinnati. The awkward landing in her Cincinnati quarterfinal worsened the existing issue to the point where she could not continue.

What Withdrawal Would Mean

If Rybakina cannot compete, the US Open field absorbs a meaningful blow. The two-time Grand Slam champion — and 2022 Wimbledon winner — had been considered a legitimate contender at Flushing Meadows, where her powerful serve and aggressive baseline game are well-suited to the hard-court surface.

Her scheduled first-round opponent would receive a walkover, and the bracket would reshape slightly around her absence. Beyond the immediate matchup, Rybakina's withdrawal would also affect the competition for the world No. 1 ranking, where she and Aryna Sabalenka remain within striking distance of each other.

Earlier in Cincinnati, Rybakina had needed a finals appearance to overtake Sabalenka for the top ranking — a window that closed when Sabalenka lost in the fourth round. If Rybakina withdraws from the US Open, defending points from last year's run becomes impossible and Sabalenka can widen her ranking lead.

Playing Through the Question

If Rybakina clears the ankle and takes the court Sunday, she does so as a player who has stayed off her feet for over a week — match-sharp preparation she simply won't have had. Her opponents, by contrast, will have had Cincinnati final-week matches or, in some cases, the Winston-Salem tune-up. The question of fitness isn't just whether she plays — it's whether she can be at full intensity from the first set of the first round.

The tennis world is watching for an update, which is expected to come no later than the eve of first-round play.

What's Next

Rybakina is expected to provide a fitness update before first-round play begins Sunday, August 30. Her status is officially day-to-day. Tennis Fanrecap will update this story as information becomes available.
