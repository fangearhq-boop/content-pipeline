Federer Returns to Arthur Ashe: The Exhibition That Stopped the Tennis World

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | August 25, 2026

Roger Federer walks back onto Arthur Ashe Stadium tonight. Four years after his retirement, four years after the last chapter of one of sport's greatest careers was written in London, the Swiss legend returns to the court where he won five US Open titles and made some of the most famous moments in tennis history.

The occasion is the 2026 US Open Fan Week exhibition. The format is straightforward: Federer faces Andy Roddick in a singles set (no-ad scoring), then the two rivals join forces with their former opponents — Federer alongside John McEnroe, Roddick alongside Andre Agassi — for a doubles set. The match begins at 8:00 PM ET at Arthur Ashe Stadium, and every seat has been sold.

The Rivalry Revisited

The Federer–Roddick head-to-head stands at 21-3 in Federer's favor — one of the most lopsided matchups among contemporaries at the top of the sport. Yet few rivalries in tennis history produced so many memorable matches in one direction. Roddick pushed Federer harder at Wimbledon than almost anyone: most famously in the 2009 final, when Federer won 5-7, 7-6(6), 7-6(5), 3-6, 16-14 in what many consider the finest final ever played on Centre Court.

Tonight's singles set carries none of that weight — no ranking points, no Grand Slam title on the line, no-ad scoring so it moves quickly. But the chemistry of competition that made their rivalry compelling never fully goes away, and the crowd at Arthur Ashe will likely remind both men of that.

Four Legends on One Court

The doubles set adds another dimension. John McEnroe and Andre Agassi — each a multiple US Open champion, each deeply associated with this particular stadium — will be on opposite sides of the net from one another, divided between a Federer team and a Roddick team.

Agassi won the US Open in 1994 and 1999. McEnroe won it in 1979, 1980, and 1981. Federer won here in 2004, 2005, 2006, 2007, and 2008. Roddick won in 2003. Between the four players on that court tonight, there are twelve US Open singles titles.

No-ad doubles between legends in their 40s and 50s will be lighter in tone than anything these four produced in their playing primes. That's exactly the point. This is Fan Week — a celebration of the sport before the serious business of the main draw begins August 30.

The Significance of Arthur Ashe

There is a specific gravity to this stadium. It holds more than 23,000 people and is the largest tennis venue in the world. When the lights are on and the stands are full, it creates a kind of pressure that does not exist anywhere else in the sport.

Federer knew that stadium for two decades. He knew how to close out a match under those lights. Tonight, for the first time in years, he will hear his name announced there again — and for a packed house that paid significant sums for that experience, it will be worth every dollar.

What's Next

After tonight's exhibition, US Open qualifying continues through the week. The draw ceremony takes place Thursday, August 27 at noon ET. The main draw begins August 30 — headlined by Zverev, Sabalenka, Alcaraz, Gauff, and a field shaped by more than fourteen withdrawals including Sinner.

Tonight belongs to nostalgia and to history. Starting Monday, the work of the 2026 US Open begins.
