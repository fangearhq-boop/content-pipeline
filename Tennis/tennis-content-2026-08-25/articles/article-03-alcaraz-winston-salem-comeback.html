Alcaraz at Winston-Salem: His First Competitive Tennis Since the April Wrist Injury

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | August 25, 2026

Carlos Alcaraz is playing competitive tennis again. For the first time since suffering a right wrist injury that removed him from the tour in April, the Spanish No. 2 seed has entered the Winston-Salem Open — an ATP 250 event at Wake Forest University — through a wildcard request. The tournament runs August 23–29, placing its final days just before the US Open main draw opens August 30.

An Unusual Comeback Route

Alcaraz's decision to play Winston-Salem is notable for several reasons. It is a tournament he has never previously entered, and ATP 250 events typically represent a step down in prestige from the Masters 1000 and Grand Slam events where Alcaraz has made his name. But those considerations are secondary to his primary objective: get competitive matches into his body before walking onto the court at Flushing Meadows.

Coming back from a months-long injury layoff — particularly a wrist injury for a player whose game depends as much on hand speed and racket-head acceleration as Alcaraz's does — requires competitive matches that cannot be replicated in practice. The rhythm of actual tournament play, the adrenaline, the shot-making under real pressure: those cannot be simulated. Winston-Salem is where Alcaraz finds out how close to match fit he actually is.

What's at Stake

Alcaraz arrives at the US Open as the No. 2 seed, behind Alexander Zverev. He is also confirmed for the mixed doubles alongside Serena Williams, making his first Grand Slam since the injury a double commitment. The question of how his wrist responds to the volume of competitive play — singles and doubles in the same week — is one the tennis world is watching carefully.

In a year where Sinner is absent and the men's draw has been weakened by a significant withdrawal list, Alcaraz represents one of the tournament's most legitimate title threats. A fit Alcaraz reaching the second week of a Grand Slam is a dangerous proposition for anyone in the field. Whether Winston-Salem delivers that fitness answer quickly enough is the variable no one outside his camp currently knows.

The Long Road Back

Alcaraz's 2026 season was interrupted at a moment when he appeared to be building toward one of the better years of his career. The right wrist injury in April ended that momentum and forced months of rehabilitation. He withdrew from Cincinnati as planned — protecting the wrist rather than rushing back before the most important tournament of the American hard-court swing.

Winston-Salem is the calculated risk: enough matches to find his game, not so many that he exhausts himself before the draw begins. It is the kind of decision that requires confidence in your own body and a clear understanding of what your game needs. Alcaraz has always demonstrated both.

What's Next

The US Open draw ceremony is Thursday, August 27 at noon ET. Alcaraz's first-round opponent will be known then. Main draw action begins August 30 at the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center. If his run at Winston-Salem provides the sharpness he is looking for, the draw will reveal a player capable of reaching — and winning — the second week.
