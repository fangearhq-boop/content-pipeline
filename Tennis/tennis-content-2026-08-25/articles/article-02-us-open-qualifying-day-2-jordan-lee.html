US Open Qualifying Day 2: Jordan Lee, Fearnley, and the Most Star-Studded Field in Years

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | August 25, 2026

While the spotlight tonight turns to Roger Federer's return at Arthur Ashe Stadium, US Open qualifying continues its own remarkable story at the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center in Flushing. Day 2 of first-round qualifying matches takes place Tuesday, completing the 128 combined men's and women's matches split over Monday and Tuesday before the competition moves into subsequent rounds.

The Headliners From Day 1

Monday's first day of qualifying produced a moment that will be talked about for years. Jordan Lee — 16 years old, playing on a wildcard — advanced through the first round. The teenager is among the youngest players ever to compete in US Open qualifying, and a successful run through the draw would write one of the most compelling stories of the fortnight.

Jacob Fearnley of Great Britain and Coleman Wong of Hong Kong were also among the Day 1 notables who advanced. The qualifying field at this year's US Open has been described by World Tennis Magazine as one of the most star-studded in recent memory — a sign of how deep professional tennis has become and how many ranked players find themselves needing a qualifying berth into major draws.

The Stakes

US Open qualifying is a three-round tournament. Winning all three rounds earns a spot in the main draw — 128 players in each singles draw — that begins August 30. Losing any round ends the tournament for that player entirely.

For players on wildcards and outside the main draw cut-off, qualifying is the only route in. For ranked players who might have expected a direct acceptance, a qualifying run can be a confidence-builder or a morale-sapper in the final days before the Grand Slam itself. The mental side of qualifying — playing competitive matches under pressure right before the main draw — is a distinct challenge that regular main draw entrants do not face.

The Context

The 2026 US Open has seen more than fourteen player withdrawals from the main draw, including Jannik Sinner, Emma Raducanu, and Bianca Andreescu. That means qualifying players who make it through will enter a draw with meaningful gaps — opportunities that might not have existed in a fully healthy field.

The admissions are free for the public at the USTA BJKNC during qualifying days. That free access helps make qualifying a legitimate spectator event and contributes to the atmosphere that makes this particular qualifying tournament stand out.

What's Next

Qualifying continues through the week. The draw ceremony for the main draw is Thursday, August 27 at noon ET. Main draw action begins August 30 with first-round matches across all disciplines. The women's singles final is scheduled for Saturday, September 12, with the men's final the following day.
