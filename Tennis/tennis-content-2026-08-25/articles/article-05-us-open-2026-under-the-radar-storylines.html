Five Under-the-Radar Storylines at the 2026 US Open

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | August 25, 2026

The top storylines at the 2026 US Open are obvious: Zverev defending his standing as No. 1 seed, Gauff defending her title, Alcaraz returning from injury. But the 128-player singles draws carry depth that goes beyond the names most casual viewers will recognize. Here are five storylines worth following once the draw drops Thursday at noon ET.

1. Alexandra Eala Makes Grand Slam Seeding History

Alexandra Eala of the Philippines enters the 2026 US Open as a seeded player — the first Filipino in history to receive a Grand Slam seeding. The 21-year-old has been one of the more compelling stories on the WTA Tour in the past two seasons, and her progress has been steady enough to reach a seeded position at the sport's hardest Grand Slam to enter seeded.

A first-round loss as a seeded player would be a setback. A run deep into the draw would be the kind of breakthrough that changes a career's trajectory. Either way, her presence in the seedings is already historic.

2. Djokovic's Pursuit of 25 Grand Slam Titles

Novak Djokovic arrives in New York seeded No. 4. At 39, he carries a record 24 Grand Slam titles and the appetite to extend it. The math works in his favor at this particular tournament: with Sinner absent and the draw reshaped by withdrawals, a favorable quarter could give Djokovic a path to the semifinal without encountering the top two seeds until that stage.

Djokovic at Flushing Meadows is always a serious proposition. He has won the US Open three times. He knows how to navigate a fortnight when the conditions are in his favor.

3. The Withdrawal Gap and Open Pathways

More than fourteen players have withdrawn from the 2026 US Open. That is a substantial attrition — the kind that reshapes entire sections of the draw and creates realistic paths for players seeded outside the top eight. In a typical year, a No. 12 seed might expect to face a top-four player in the quarterfinal. In this field, the arithmetic is different.

The draw ceremony on Thursday will make those pathways visible. Players and coaches will be calculating immediately. The beneficiaries of favorable placements may not be known until the third round.

4. Arthur Fils Arrives as Masters 1000 Champion

Arthur Fils won the Cincinnati Open on Sunday — his first Masters 1000 title, and France's first since Jo-Wilfried Tsonga in 2014. The 22-year-old finished the Cincinnati tournament without losing a three-set match (10-0 in such matches in 2026). He also has not lost to an American opponent since the start of 2025.

A first Masters 1000 title five days before a Grand Slam is the kind of form that makes a player dangerous. Fils will arrive at Flushing Meadows with a level of confidence that is difficult to manufacture and impossible to replicate. If the draw places him in a quarter without any of the top four seeds, he is a genuine contender to reach the second week.

5. Gauff Defending on the Biggest Stage

Coco Gauff is the defending US Open champion, the No. 3 seed, and the two-time Cincinnati Open champion after her win on Sunday over Jessica Pegula. She is playing the best tennis of her career heading into her home Grand Slam. Defending a Grand Slam title is historically one of the most difficult tasks in tennis — the weight of expectation, the elevated seeding that brings tougher opponents earlier, and the psychological challenge of being the hunter hunted.

Gauff has shown she can handle pressure in New York. The question is whether she can handle the specific pressure of walking back in as the champion and being expected to do it again. The history of defending Grand Slam champions suggests it is never easy. Gauff's trajectory suggests she may be the exception.

What's Next

The 2026 US Open draw ceremony is Thursday, August 27 at noon ET. First-round main draw action begins August 30 at the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center, Flushing, Queens. The record $108 million prize pool will be distributed across the entire tournament. Women's final: September 12. Men's final: September 13.
