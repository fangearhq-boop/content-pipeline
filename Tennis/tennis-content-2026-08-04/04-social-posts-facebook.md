### STORY 1: Eala Wins WTA DC Open — First Filipino WTA Champion

#### Long-Form Post — 8:00 AM ET

The DC Open WTA final was supposed to finish Sunday. A thunderstorm in Washington had other ideas.

When play resumed Monday at noon, Pegula led one set to none. Eala trailed two games to one in the second. Everything in the scoreline said this match was Pegula's to close.

Eala had a different plan. She won the second set 6-4. Then she won the third set 6-0. Fifteen of the final 18 games went to the 21-year-old from the Philippines — and when it was over, she had done something no Filipino tennis player had ever done before.

Alexandra Eala is a WTA Tour singles champion.

Her week at Rock Creek Park Tennis Center was one of the most memorable runs in recent WTA history. She came back from a set down against Qinwen Zheng in the first round. She upset defending champion Leylah Fernandez in the second round, rallying from 5-1 down. She beat Elina Svitolina and Naomi Osaka to reach the final. And then she came back from a rain-suspended first-set deficit to claim the title.

She leaves Washington at a career-high ranking of approximately No. 24. The Philippines has its first WTA singles champion.

### STORY 2: Fritz Wins DC Open ATP 500

#### Long-Form Post — 10:30 AM ET

Taylor Fritz has been one of the best players in the world for the past three years. He has the results, the ranking, the game to back it up. The one thing that kept slipping away was finishing. In 2026, he had reached three finals without winning any of them.

Monday in Washington, he finally finished.

Fritz defeated 19-year-old Rafael Jódar 7-6(2), 6-4 to win the DC Open ATP 500 title — his 11th career ATP title and first at this event. The first set went to a tiebreak, and Fritz was clean in it, winning 7-2. The second set was tighter but Fritz converted his opportunities and closed it out 6-4.

Jódar, who is 19 years old and had never been in an ATP 500 final before this week, makes his ATP Top 20 debut regardless of Monday's result. His run in Washington is its own story — but this week belongs to Fritz.

He now heads to Montreal, where he is seeded in a draw that is wide open without Sinner, Alcaraz, or Djokovic.

### STORY 3: ATP Montreal Masters 1000 — Day 1

#### Long-Form Post — 12:30 PM ET

The big summer starts today. The ATP Masters 1000 in Montreal opens its main draw at IGA Stadium — and the draw is genuinely wide open.

Jannik Sinner is absent. Carlos Alcaraz is absent. Novak Djokovic is absent. That removes the top three favorites from any tournament they enter and opens the door for the next tier to define this summer.

Alexander Zverev enters as the No. 1 seed. Felix Auger-Aliassime is seeded No. 2 — a career milestone, and one that comes in front of his home Montreal crowd. Ben Shelton, the defending champion, is No. 3. Alex de Minaur, who reached the DC Open quarterfinals before falling to Nakashima, is No. 4.

Jack Draper of Great Britain received a wildcard entry for his first professional match since a 2025 injury — one of the week's most compelling subplots.

Rain cancelled Sunday's first-round matches and compressed the schedule, meaning more tennis comes faster. For those who like drama in July and August, this is the week to tune in.

### STORY 4: WTA Toronto Day 1

#### Long-Form Post — 3:00 PM ET

Canada was ready to celebrate Bianca Andreescu on Monday. The former US Open champion, playing as a wild card in her home city, stepped onto Sobeys Stadium's main court. The crowd was with her.

Nikola Bartunkova was not impressed.

The Czech player defeated Andreescu 7-5, 6-4 in the WTA National Bank Open first round — one of the signature moments of Day 1 in Toronto. It was the kind of result that reminds everyone how relentless the WTA tour is: no homecoming, no narrative exemptions.

Elsewhere on Day 1, Alina Korneeva defeated Polina Kudermetova with a retirement, Karolina Pliskova moved through in straight sets, and Kayla Cross upset British qualifier Katie Boulter 7-6(3), 7-5.

Top seeds Aryna Sabalenka and Elena Rybakina have first-round byes and have not yet played. The defending champion bracket and the seeded half of the draw remain largely intact. Day 2 brings them into the mix.

### STORY 5: Alcaraz Targets Cincinnati — Sinner Rivalry Looms

#### Long-Form Post — 6:00 PM ET

For five months, Jannik Sinner has played without his most dangerous rival. The result has been arguably the most dominant stretch of tennis anyone has produced in the ATP's modern era — five Masters 1000 titles, Wimbledon defended, no sign of stopping.

Carlos Alcaraz is coming back.

The Spaniard, who has been out since April with a right wrist injury, has confirmed he is targeting the Cincinnati Open (August 13–23) for his return. Alcaraz is the defending Cincinnati champion — he won the title in 2025 when Sinner was forced to retire from the final due to illness.

The timing is almost symmetrical. Sinner built his reputation, in part, by stepping up when Alcaraz was unavailable. Now Alcaraz returns to the exact event where the rivalry's 2025 chapter ended abruptly — and with the US Open (main draw August 24) three weeks away, Cincinnati takes on the feel of a preview.

Sinner is also expected in Cincinnati. So is Novak Djokovic. So is Coco Gauff.

The summer of 2026 was Sinner's. The fall belongs to everyone who shows up ready.
