Taylor Fritz Wins DC Open ATP 500, Ending 2026 Final Drought with 11th Career Title

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | August 4, 2026

Taylor Fritz finally got his 2026 title. Three times this year he had reached the final of an ATP event and left without lifting the trophy. Monday at the Mubadala DC Open, that changed. Fritz defeated 19-year-old Rafael Jódar of Spain 7-6(2), 6-4 to claim his 11th career ATP title and his first at the Washington, D.C. ATP 500.

The final had been scheduled for Sunday but was pushed to Monday along with the WTA championship after heavy rain swept through the nation's capital and made play impossible. Fritz and Jódar returned to the William H.G. FitzGerald Tennis Center on Monday with the full weight of the final intact. Fritz handled it with the same efficiency that has defined him at his best — clean from the baseline, composed in the big moments, and clinical in the tiebreak.

Fritz Claims the Tiebreak, the Set, the Match

The first set went to a tiebreak, which is where finals are often decided. Fritz won it 7-2, losing only two points on his serve throughout the breaker. Jódar, who had shown considerable poise in beating Alejandro Tabilo in the semifinals after saving a set in that match, could not find the same rhythm against a player who had been on the tour for nearly a decade longer.

The second set followed a similar pattern. Fritz broke in the fourth game, held firm, and closed out 6-4 without facing a break point in the deciding stages. The match was over in under an hour and a half — efficient, professional, and exactly the kind of performance Fritz needed to snap his run of final setbacks.

Jódar Enters the ATP Top 20

For Jódar, the loss does not erase what he accomplished in Washington. At 19, he advanced through the draw of an ATP 500 event and made the final — a result that guarantees him a debut inside the ATP Top 20 this week regardless of Monday's outcome. He reached the final by defeating Tabilo in three sets after falling behind in the first. He had also beaten Nakashima earlier in the tournament. Jódar was not playing scared, and Fritz had to earn his tiebreak.

But Fritz's 11th title is a significant moment in the context of this season. He had reached finals in 2026 without converting, and the question of whether he could close at a major event was beginning to take shape. He answered it in Washington.

What's Next

Fritz now moves to the ATP Masters 1000 in Montreal, which opened its main draw Tuesday at IGA Stadium. He is seeded in a draw that does not include Sinner, Alcaraz, or Djokovic — all three of whom are absent due to injury or rest. Fritz and Zverev are reportedly in the same quarter of the draw, setting up a potential quarterfinal meeting. After Montreal, the tour turns to Cincinnati (August 13–23) and then the US Open, which begins its main draw August 24 in New York.
