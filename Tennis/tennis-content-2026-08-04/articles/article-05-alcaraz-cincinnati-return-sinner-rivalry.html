Alcaraz Targets Cincinnati Return After Five Months Out — Facing a Sinner Who Has Won Everything

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | August 4, 2026

Carlos Alcaraz has confirmed his target: the Cincinnati Open, beginning August 13. That will be his first professional tennis match since April, when a right wrist injury ended his season and removed him from both the French Open and Wimbledon. For five months, the question of what men's tennis would look like when he returned has been theoretical. It is about to have an answer.

In Alcaraz's absence, Jannik Sinner has answered every question. The world No. 1 has won all five ATP Masters 1000 events in 2026 and defended his Wimbledon title against Alexander Zverev. He has played without his most dangerous rival for the better part of the summer and produced the kind of sustained dominance that even the most historically dominant players rarely manage over a full season.

The Defending Champion Returns to the Scene

Alcaraz's return at Cincinnati carries its own historical weight. He is the defending champion at this event. In 2025, he won the Cincinnati title when Sinner was forced to retire from the final due to illness — a result that denied the match its proper conclusion. The two rivals have not played a complete match since before the Cincinnati 2025 final.

Now Alcaraz comes back to the same tournament. Sinner is expected to enter. The US Open begins its main draw August 24, three weeks after Cincinnati concludes. Every match either player plays between now and then functions as a preview of the season's final major.

The Challenge of Coming Back

A five-month absence creates specific challenges. Match sharpness takes time to rebuild, and even players of Alcaraz's caliber need competitive repetitions before they are operating at peak level. The Cincinnati draw will include Sinner, Novak Djokovic, Coco Gauff on the women's side, and a full field of players who have been competing continuously since April. Alcaraz will be the one who has not.

What Alcaraz has on his side: he is 23 years old, has won multiple Grand Slams, and has shown throughout his career that he can perform at the highest level regardless of circumstances. His game does not require a long runway. If he is healthy — and his team has said the wrist has healed — he arrives in Cincinnati capable of winning.

What's Next

The Cincinnati Open runs August 13–23 at the Lindner Family Tennis Center in Mason, Ohio. The ATP and WTA both play at the same facility during the same week, making it one of the few joint events on the calendar. Alcaraz's return there, combined with Sinner's expected entry and the proximity to the US Open (main draw begins August 24), makes the week one of the most anticipated on the 2026 calendar. The summer belongs to the first major match between these two players since the rivalry was interrupted.
