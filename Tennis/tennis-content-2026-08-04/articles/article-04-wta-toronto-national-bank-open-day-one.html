WTA Toronto Day 1: Bartunkova Upsets Andreescu on Home Soil as National Bank Open Main Draw Opens

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | August 4, 2026

The WTA National Bank Open in Toronto opened its main draw Monday at Sobeys Stadium, and the first day produced the kind of result that the city had not hoped for. Bianca Andreescu, the Canadian wild card and former US Open champion who had drawn home crowd support throughout her career, fell in the first round to Nikola Bartunkova of the Czech Republic, 7-5, 6-4.

Andreescu's homecoming — she is among the most recognized women's tennis players in Canadian sports history — had been one of the tournament's anticipated storylines. Bartunkova had a different agenda. The Czech player was competitive throughout, taking the first set in a contest that went to games and converting her opportunities when they came in the second.

Day 1 Results Across the Draw

Beyond the marquee upset, Monday's slate produced a mix of straightforward results and tight matches. Alina Korneeva defeated Polina Kudermetova with a retirement at 6-0, 3-0 — Kudermetova appearing to be dealing with physical discomfort before conceding. Karolina Pliskova moved through 6-4, 6-4 against Oleksandra Oliynykova. Kayla Cross knocked out British qualifier Katie Boulter 7-6(3), 7-5 in a tight two-set match.

In another three-setter on Day 1, Kayla Day outlasted Diane Parry 3-6, 6-3, 7-5 to advance. Anna Bondar defeated Anhelina Kalinina 6-4, 7-5. Zhang Shuai beat Yulia Putintseva 6-4, 6-1 in routine fashion. Elena-Gabriela Ruse dominated Peyton Stearns 6-0, 7-5, and McCartney Kessler was efficient against Cadence Brace, winning 6-3, 6-3.

Sabalenka and Rybakina Enter in Round Two

No. 1 seed Aryna Sabalenka and No. 2 seed Elena Rybakina both have first-round byes and have not yet played. Both enter the tournament in the second round, meaning they will meet the winners from Monday's matches. Their eventual paths through the draw represent two of the most dangerous threats in the field — Sabalenka enters as the form player in the WTA's top tier, and Rybakina's game translates particularly well to hard courts.

Jessica Pegula, who lost the DC Open final to Alexandra Eala on Monday morning, is expected to compete in Toronto this week as well. Her position in the seeding order and her entry into the second round will add another significant name to a draw that has already shown it will not produce easy matches for anyone.

What's Next

Day 2 in Toronto brings seeded players into action and sets the bracket toward the weekend. The WTA National Bank Open runs through August 13 at Sobeys Stadium. After Toronto, the women's tour moves to Cincinnati (August 13–23) and then the US Open, which begins its main draw August 24 in New York. Toronto is a Masters 1000 level event and carries substantial points — every result this week will matter.
