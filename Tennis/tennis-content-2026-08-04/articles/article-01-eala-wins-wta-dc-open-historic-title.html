Alexandra Eala Wins DC Open WTA 500 — Becomes First Filipino WTA Singles Champion

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | August 4, 2026

Alexandra Eala of the Philippines is a WTA Tour singles champion. On Monday at Rock Creek Park Tennis Center in Washington, D.C., the 21-year-old defeated top seed Jessica Pegula 4-6, 6-4, 6-0 to win the Mubadala DC Open WTA 500 title — becoming the first tennis player from the Philippines to win a professional singles title on the WTA Tour.

The final had begun Sunday afternoon and was suspended when thunderstorms moved through Washington with Pegula leading 6-4 and Eala ahead 2-1 in the second set. When play resumed Monday at noon Eastern time, nothing about what followed resembled the contest that had gone before. Eala won the second set 6-4. She won the third set 6-0. She won 15 of the final 18 games. The player who had trailed a set and looked like a runner-up finished the day as the champion.

The Week That Rewrote Philippine Tennis

Eala's path through the DC Open was as significant as the title itself. She opened against Qinwen Zheng, a former Olympic champion ranked in the world's top ten, and came back from a set down to win 4-6, 6-4, 6-1. In the second round, she faced Leylah Fernandez — the defending DC Open champion — and rallied from 5-1 down in the second set to win 6-2, 7-6(1). In the quarterfinals, she defeated Elina Svitolina 6-3, 6-4, the same score by which she had beaten the Ukrainian in Berlin earlier this season. In the semifinals, she handled No. 3 seed Naomi Osaka 6-4, 6-2.

By the time she stepped onto the court for Sunday's final, Eala had beaten a former Olympic champion, the defending tournament winner, the world's No. 10, and one of the biggest names in women's tennis. The rain gave her one more night to prepare. She came back ready.

The Final Set Said Everything

The 6-0 third set is the detail that will be remembered longest. Pegula is one of the most consistent closers on the WTA tour — she has won Dubai, Charleston, and other titles by finding her level under pressure. On Monday, Eala did not give her the chance. After converting her service games efficiently throughout the set, Eala broke twice and held twice, completing the bagel in under 30 minutes.

Eala ends the tournament with a career-high live ranking of approximately No. 24, up from No. 28 at the start of the week. More than the ranking, though, she carries a title that no Filipino player had ever held before. Tennis has been played in the Philippines for decades. Monday afternoon, the country got its first WTA champion.

What's Next

Eala will now travel to Toronto for the WTA National Bank Open, a Masters 1000 event where her DC Open run may earn her a direct placement or favorable seeding. The Toronto draw has already begun, with top seeds Aryna Sabalenka and Elena Rybakina holding first-round byes. For Pegula, also heading to Toronto and seeded in the top half, the loss is the first since her Charleston title in April — a reminder that even the best can lose when someone else comes back stronger from a rain delay. The WTA summer now turns toward Toronto, Cincinnati (August 13–23), and the US Open, which begins August 24 in New York.
