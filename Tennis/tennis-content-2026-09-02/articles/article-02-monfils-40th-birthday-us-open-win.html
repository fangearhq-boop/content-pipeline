Monfils Wins on His 40th Birthday at the US Open — Historic Night at Arthur Ashe

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | September 2, 2026

Gael Monfils beat Adolfo Daniel Vallejo on his 40th birthday, becoming the oldest man to win a US Open match since Jimmy Connors in 1992 and recording his 34th US Open win — the most by any Frenchman in history.

Gael Monfils turned 40 on Tuesday night. Then he won a match at the US Open. Then he took a bow at Arthur Ashe Stadium for what may be the last time.

The Frenchman defeated Argentine qualifier Adolfo Daniel Vallejo 2-6, 6-1, 6-2, 6-0 in his first-round match, firing 37 winners and growing stronger as the match progressed. The scoreline was almost secondary to the occasion: a living tennis legend celebrating a milestone birthday with a victory on the sport's biggest stage.

History Revisited

With the win, Monfils became the oldest man to win a US Open match since Jimmy Connors, who also won a first-round match at the age of 40 — on September 2, 1992. Connors made one of the most memorable US Open runs in history as a wild card, reaching the quarterfinals at Flushing Meadows before the tournament was done.

Monfils' victory was also his 34th career US Open win, the most by any French player in the tournament's history. For a career spanning two decades and 18 appearances at Flushing Meadows, that record feels fitting.

A Final Chapter

This is Monfils' 18th and final US Open. He has announced he will retire from professional tennis at the end of the 2026 season, concluding a career that has produced six ATP titles, two Grand Slam quarterfinal appearances, and a reputation as one of the sport's most electrifying entertainers.

He has not come to New York simply to say goodbye. He is competing in mixed doubles alongside his wife, Elina Svitolina — the pair competing together at a Grand Slam for the first time and advancing to the mixed doubles semifinals. Before his singles match Tuesday, Monfils revealed that Svitolina had given him a "special gift." He did not elaborate. He did not need to.

The New York crowd — familiar with Monfils for years and aware this is his farewell — gave him an atmosphere worthy of the night. He gave them 37 winners in return.

What's Next

Monfils advances to the second round at the US Open and continues what he has described as a farewell run at Flushing Meadows. Win or lose from here, he has already secured his place in the tournament's history — and his 40th birthday win will be remembered long after the 2026 US Open is decided.

The final chapter of one of tennis's great careers is being written in New York. Tuesday night was a very good page.
