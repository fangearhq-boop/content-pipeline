Rybakina Shakes Off Injury Concerns With Composed Win Over Frodin at US Open

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | September 2, 2026

The No. 2 seed, who had been unable to walk without pain before the tournament, delivered a 6-3, 6-2 performance in 74 minutes that strongly signals she is ready to compete for the title.

There were genuine questions about whether Elena Rybakina would be able to compete at the 2026 US Open. Those questions looked very different after Tuesday's first-round match at Flushing Meadows.

The No. 2 seed dismissed American teenager Thea Frodin 6-3, 6-2 in one hour and 14 minutes, advancing to the second round with a controlled and assured performance that showed minimal sign of the ankle and heel injury that had cast her tournament participation in doubt.

The Injury That Nearly Kept Her Out

Rybakina retired from her Cincinnati Open quarterfinal against Iga Swiatek on August 21, trailing 1-4 in the first set, due to a left ankle and heel injury. In the days that followed, she confirmed she had been unable to walk without pain and that she was undergoing assessment to determine whether she would be fit to play in New York.

She passed her fitness tests and flew to New York. What followed on Tuesday was a reminder of why she remains one of the most complete players on the WTA Tour.

Controlled From the Start

Rybakina took the first set 6-3, never facing serious resistance. She was sharper in the second, moving through 6-2 without drama. Her serve, always one of the most effective weapons in the women's game, held up throughout. Her footwork on the Flushing Meadows hard courts looked unimpaired.

Sunday Guardian reported that Rybakina "shrugged off injury scare" — and that characterization is accurate. She did not limp, did not call a medical timeout, and did not appear to be managing discomfort at any point during the match.

What's Next

Rybakina advances to the US Open second round on Thursday, September 3, where she faces Spain's Jessica Bouzas Maneiro. A second consecutive straight-sets win against Bouzas Maneiro would all but confirm she is fully fit and operating without restriction.

In a women's draw that lost Sabalenka's three-peat rival to injuries in the run-up, Rybakina fully healthy is a significant factor in determining who wins the title in New York. Tuesday was a very promising start.
