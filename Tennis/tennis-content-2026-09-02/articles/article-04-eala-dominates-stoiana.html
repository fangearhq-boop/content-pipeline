Eala Dominates Stoiana for Emphatic US Open First-Round Win

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | September 2, 2026

The Philippines' No. 17 seed dropped just three games in 78 minutes at Louis Armstrong Stadium, advancing to the second round of the US Open for the second consecutive year.

Alex Eala had the crowd at Louis Armstrong Stadium in the palm of her hand within minutes. By the time she walked off the court on Tuesday — 78 minutes after the first ball was struck — the result had never been in doubt.

The No. 17 seed from the Philippines defeated American qualifier Mary Stoiana 6-1, 6-2 in a first-round performance that was controlled, confident, and thoroughly dominant from the very first game.

Statement First Round

Eala conceded just three games across two sets — one in the opener and two in the second. She was aggressive on her first serve, made clean decisions from the baseline, and gave Stoiana very few opportunities to establish any rhythm. The qualifier, playing her first US Open main draw match, was unable to find a foothold at any stage of the contest.

For Eala, a player who has spent the last two years steadily climbing the WTA rankings and proving her seedings are earned, not granted, Tuesday was an ideal statement match. She entered as the highest-seeded Filipino player in Grand Slam history and responded with one of the more emphatic Day 3 results of the entire first round.

Back to the Second Round

This is Eala's second consecutive US Open second-round appearance. She has established herself as a genuine hard-court threat in 2026, with a run to the DC Open final earlier this summer — defeating Elina Svitolina and Naomi Osaka before losing to Jessica Pegula in the title match — cementing her reputation on the surface.

Her 2026 hard-court season had already produced a 22-9 record before the US Open began. Tuesday's win continues that momentum at the year's final Grand Slam.

What's Next

Eala advances to the US Open second round and will next face Ukraine's Oleksandra Oliynykova. A second-round victory would equal her deepest Grand Slam run and continue one of the most compelling individual stories of the 2026 US Open.

She has the seeding. She has the form. Tuesday showed she also has the composure for the biggest stages. The second round against Oliynykova will tell more — but right now, Eala is playing some of the best tennis of her career.
