### STORY 2: Monfils 40th Birthday Win

**Long-Form Post**
Window: 10:30 AM ET

Gael Monfils turned 40 years old on Tuesday — and he celebrated the way only Gael Monfils can.

The Frenchman walked out to Arthur Ashe Stadium for his 18th and final US Open appearance and defeated Adolfo Daniel Vallejo 2-6, 6-1, 6-2, 6-0. He fired 37 winners and grew stronger as the night progressed, losing only the opening set before taking complete control.

With the victory, Monfils became the oldest man to win a US Open match since Jimmy Connors, who also won on his 40th birthday — September 2, 1992. Connors that year made a legendary run to the quarterfinals as a wild card at age 39 and then returned at 40. Now Monfils joins that exclusive club.

The win was also his 34th career US Open victory, the most by any French player in the history of the tournament.

What made the night even more special: wife Elina Svitolina, the Ukrainian star, was there to share it. The two have been playing mixed doubles together at this US Open, reaching the semifinals — competing as a couple at a Grand Slam for the first time. Monfils revealed after the match that Svitolina had given him a "special gift" before he took the court.

Monfils will retire at the end of the 2026 season. He is playing for the memories now. Tuesday night's birthday win gave him one he will never forget.

### STORY 3: Rybakina Beats Frodin

**Long-Form Post**
Window: 12:30 PM ET

Elena Rybakina had been unable to walk without pain just days before the US Open began. On Tuesday, she stepped onto the court and barely looked bothered.

The No. 2 seed dismissed American teenager Thea Frodin 6-3, 6-2 in one hour and 14 minutes to cruise into the second round at Flushing Meadows.

Rybakina had retired from her Cincinnati quarterfinal against Iga Swiatek at 1-4 in the first set due to a left ankle and heel injury. In the days that followed, her participation at the US Open was genuinely in doubt. She confirmed she was in significant pain but cleared fitness tests before flying to New York.

Nothing about her performance Tuesday suggested she is working through any physical limitation. Her serve was sharp, her footwork assured, and she closed the match without drama.

She next faces Spain's Jessica Bouzas Maneiro in R2. If Rybakina is fully fit — and Tuesday's performance suggests she may be — she remains one of the most dangerous players in the women's draw.

### STORY 4: Eala Dominates Stoiana

**Long-Form Post**
Window: 3:00 PM ET

Alex Eala had something to prove on Day 3 at the 2026 US Open. She delivered.

The Philippines' No. 17 seed walked onto Louis Armstrong Stadium and dismantled American qualifier Mary Stoiana 6-1, 6-2 in just 78 minutes — one of the most emphatic performances of the first round.

Eala was aggressive from the very first game, conceding just three games across the entire match. She controlled rallies, moved well, and showed none of the nerves that can derail early Grand Slam matches. The crowd, appreciating her sharp play, gave her a warm reception throughout.

The result sends her into the US Open second round for the second consecutive year. She will face Ukraine's Oleksandra Oliynykova next.

Entering the tournament as the 17th seed — the highest Grand Slam seeding in Philippine tennis history — Eala has backed it up with a performance that matched expectations from the very first match. Her 2026 hard court season has been exceptional, and this win is a continuation of that run.
