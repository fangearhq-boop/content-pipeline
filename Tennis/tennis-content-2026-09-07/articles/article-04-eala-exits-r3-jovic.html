Alex Eala's US Open Campaign Ends in R3, But Historic Achievement Stands

By Marcus Cole | September 7, 2026

Iva Jovic defeated Eala 7-5, 3-6, 7-5 in three hours at Arthur Ashe Stadium. The third round is the deepest Grand Slam run by a Filipino woman in history.

Alex Eala's 2026 US Open campaign has come to an end, but what she did in Flushing Meadows this week will not be forgotten quickly in Philippine tennis.

Iva Jovic defeated Eala 7-5, 3-6, 7-5 in three hours and three minutes at Arthur Ashe Stadium Saturday night, bringing Eala's run to a close in the third round. Jovic won five of the last six games to take the match, extending her head-to-head advantage over Eala to 3-0.

A Match Worth Remembering

Eala won the second set to force a deciding set, and held serve late into the third. Jovic found her best tennis in the final games, but Eala made her work for every point. The match had the atmosphere of a tournament-defining moment — Arthur Ashe's night session crowd was fully invested, and Eala gave them reason to be.

Jovic, the No. 14 seed from the United States, played the kind of match that sends a message. She faces No. 4 seed Coco Gauff in the fourth round Monday — a bigger stage still, and she's earned it.

Eala's Legacy From This Tournament

Eala entered the 2026 US Open as the No. 17 seed — the highest Grand Slam seeding in Philippine tennis history. She reached the third round at Flushing Meadows, further than any Filipino woman has ever gone at a major. She played her biggest match at Arthur Ashe Stadium in primetime on the sport's biggest stage.

The record books will show a third-round exit. The context shows something different: a historic first, a seeding no Filipino player had achieved before, and a performance that proved Eala belongs at this level. This was not a ceiling. It was a floor.

What's Next

Eala's next Grand Slam opportunity is the Australian Open in January 2027. Given the seeding she has earned, she will enter Melbourne as one of the higher seeds in the women's draw — and with the experience of an Arthur Ashe night session match behind her.

For Jovic, the run continues Monday against Gauff. A win there would put her in the women's quarterfinals as one of the tournament's breakout stories.
