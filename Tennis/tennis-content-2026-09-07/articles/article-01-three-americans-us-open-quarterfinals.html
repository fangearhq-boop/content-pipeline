Three American Men Reach US Open Quarterfinals for First Time Since 1995

By Marcus Cole | September 7, 2026

Ben Shelton, Frances Tiafoe, and Alex Michelsen all won in straight sets Sunday, giving the United States its deepest men's presence at a major in three decades.

Something significant is happening at Flushing Meadows. Ben Shelton, Frances Tiafoe, and Alex Michelsen all won their fourth-round matches at the US Open on Sunday, giving the United States three men in the quarterfinals of a major for the first time since 1995.

The last time American men made this kind of statement at a Grand Slam, Pete Sampras was collecting trophies and a 23-year-old from Georgia named Andre Agassi was still in his prime. This US Open is shaping up to be the most meaningful chapter in American men's tennis in a generation.

Sunday's Results

Shelton, the No. 8 seed, was in complete control against Stefanos Tsitsipas, winning 6-2, 6-3, 6-4. It was the kind of dominant performance that announced he belongs on this stage — his serve was overpowering, his baseline game was locked in, and Tsitsipas had no answer. Next up for Shelton: defending champion Carlos Alcaraz.

Tiafoe, seeded No. 11, outlasted former champion Daniil Medvedev in a three-set battle that required back-to-back tiebreaks — 7-6(1), 6-4, 7-6(6). Tiafoe has been to the US Open semifinals before (2022), and this run has the same energy. He faces 20-year-old Michelsen in an all-American quarterfinal that guarantees at least one U.S. man in the semifinals.

Michelsen, at age 20, has been the tournament's surprise quarterfinalist. He beat No. 27 seed Tomas Etcheverry 7-6, 6-4, 6-4 without ever looking overwhelmed by the moment. According to the ATP Tour, Michelsen is the youngest American man to reach the US Open fourth round since Andy Roddick in 2002.

The Weight of 2003

The last American man to win a Grand Slam was Andy Roddick, right here at the US Open, in 2003. That is 23 years without a U.S. men's major champion. The drought has outlasted careers, retirements, and entire generations of players.

Now, with five Americans in the round of 16 — the most since 1995 — and three confirmed in the quarterfinals, the question that American tennis has been waiting years to ask is suddenly urgent: could this be the year?

What's Next

The men's quarterfinals begin Tuesday, September 8. Shelton faces Alcaraz in what shapes up as a generational clash — the hardest-serving American in the draw against the reigning champion who returned from a four-month injury and hasn't lost a set in the tournament. On the other side, Tiafoe and Michelsen play each other, making one American a guaranteed semifinalist.

Also in the men's half: No. 1 seed Alexander Zverev and 20-year-old American Learner Tien are among those still playing fourth-round matches Monday. If Tien wins, the American presence in the quarterfinals could grow to four.

The US Open men's quarterfinals are on ESPN and Tennis Channel beginning Tuesday. This is the week American tennis has been building toward for two decades.
