US Open 2026 QF Preview: Alcaraz vs. Shelton, Sabalenka's Three-Peat Bid, and an All-American Wave

By Ryan Calloway | September 7, 2026

The US Open quarterfinals begin Tuesday with the most compelling second-week field in years. Here is everything you need to know about the matchups, the stakes, and what to watch.

The US Open's second week begins Tuesday with quarterfinal matchups that could generate some of the most memorable moments in recent tennis history. Four men's and four women's QF matches will be played Tuesday through Thursday, with the semifinals to follow Friday and Saturday.

Men's Quarterfinals

No. 2 Carlos Alcaraz vs. No. 8 Ben Shelton

This is the match the draw produced that most fans circled when the bracket was revealed. Alcaraz, the defending US Open champion, returned from a four-month absence due to wrist injury and has looked sharp in every match. Shelton is playing arguably the best tennis of his life — he routed Tsitsipas without dropping serve in the fourth round.

The matchup pits Alcaraz's all-court brilliance against Shelton's power and athleticism. Shelton's serve is the great equalizer; Alcaraz's footwork and shot variety are his edge. Whoever wins will be considered the men's title favorite from that half of the draw.

No. 11 Frances Tiafoe vs. Alex Michelsen

This all-American quarterfinal guarantees one U.S. man in the men's semifinal for the first time since 2005. Tiafoe has been to the US Open semifinal before — in 2022, he took out Rafael Nadal in the fourth round before falling in four sets. Michelsen, at 20, is the tournament's surprise quarterfinalist and has dispatched every opponent in straight sets.

The veteran vs. the young gun. Tiafoe's game is built for big moments; Michelsen is fearless. This may be the men's QF most worth watching for pure tennis quality.

Women's Quarterfinals

No. 1 Aryna Sabalenka vs. Linda Noskova

Sabalenka is chasing a third consecutive US Open title, which would make her only the second woman in the modern era to accomplish that feat. Her path runs through Wimbledon champion Linda Noskova, who won her first Grand Slam title at the All England Club this summer and has the game to push Sabalenka. This is the highest-stakes women's QF at the US Open in years.

No. 6 Jessica Pegula vs. Emma Navarro

An all-American quarterfinal that guarantees a U.S. woman in the women's semifinal. Pegula has been one of the most consistent players in the draw; Navarro's run has been quieter but equally impressive. One of them will face the Sabalenka-Noskova winner in the semifinals.

What's Next

Men's QF matches begin Tuesday, September 8. Women's QF matches are scheduled for Wednesday, September 9, and Thursday, September 10. All matches air on ESPN and Tennis Channel.

The bigger picture: American tennis has not produced a men's Grand Slam champion since Andy Roddick at the US Open in 2003. With Shelton, Tiafoe, and Michelsen all in the quarterfinals, that 23-year drought has its best chance to end yet. On the women's side, if Pegula or Navarro wins and Sabalenka falls, an American could reach the final at her home major for the first time in years.

The US Open's second week starts Tuesday. It should be worth watching every day.
