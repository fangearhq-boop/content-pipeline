### STORY 1: Three Americans in US Open QF

**Long-Form Post**
Posting Window: 10:30 AM ET
Tier: 1

Something historic happened at Flushing Meadows on Sunday — and it hasn't happened in three decades.

Ben Shelton. Frances Tiafoe. Alex Michelsen. Three American men are in the US Open quarterfinals, giving the United States its deepest men's run at a major since 1995. Five Americans were in the round of 16 this year — the most since that same era.

Shelton was dominant. He took out Stefanos Tsitsipas 6-2, 6-3, 6-4 in an hour and a half — next up, he faces defending champion Carlos Alcaraz. Tiafoe overcame former US Open champion Daniil Medvedev in a tough three-setter, saving two tiebreaks along the way. And 20-year-old Alex Michelsen — the tournament's surprise quarterfinalist — kept rolling past Tomas Etcheverry.

The last American man to win a Grand Slam was Andy Roddick right here at the US Open in 2003. Could this be the year that drought ends?

Tiafoe and Michelsen face each other in an all-American QF — so at minimum, one American reaches the semifinal. Then there's Shelton, who has the power to take down Alcaraz. 🎾🇺🇸

What a moment for U.S. tennis. Are you watching?

### STORY 4: Eala R3 Exit

**Long-Form Post**
Posting Window: 8:00 AM ET
Tier: 2

Alex Eala's historic US Open run came to an end Saturday night at Arthur Ashe Stadium — but what a run it was.

Iva Jovic defeated Eala 7-5, 3-6, 7-5 in three hours and three minutes in one of the most intense matches of the tournament. Jovic won five of the last six games to close it out and extended her head-to-head advantage over Eala to 3-0.

Eala entered this tournament as the No. 17 seed — the highest Grand Slam seeding in Philippine tennis history. She reached the third round at Flushing Meadows, further than any Filipino woman has ever gone at a Grand Slam. She played at Arthur Ashe Stadium in primetime. She gave Jovic everything she had for three hours.

That's not a loss to hang your head about. That's the start of something. 🎾🇵🇭

What a statement from Eala at this US Open.
