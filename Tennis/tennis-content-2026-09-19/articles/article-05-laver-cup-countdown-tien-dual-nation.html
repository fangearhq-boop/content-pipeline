Six Days to Laver Cup: Tien's Dual-Nation Week and a Reshuffled Team World

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | September 19, 2026

On the same Saturday that the Laver Cup is six days away, Learner Tien is in Prague competing for the United States in the Davis Cup. He defeated Jakub Mensik of Czech Republic 6-2, 6-4 on Friday. On Saturday, he is back on court for doubles and potential reverse singles. Then, seven days from now, he will walk into The O2 in London wearing Team World colours.

Welcome to the final stretch of the 2026 tennis calendar.

Tien and Mensik: Two Flags, One Weekend Apart

The Tien-Mensik subplot is one of the more compelling threads in tennis right now. On Friday in Prague, they played each other for their national teams — USA vs. Czech Republic — in a Davis Cup qualifier with a Bologna Finals berth on the line. Tien won. It was not particularly close.

Seven days later, Tien will stand on Team World's side at the Laver Cup while Mensik takes his place with Team Europe. They will be in the same arena, competing for the same teams that will face each other across the weekend of September 25–27 at The O2. The overlap of national team commitments and the Laver Cup's pan-continental rosters is a feature of this stretch of the calendar that creates exactly this kind of cross-cutting drama.

Team World's Reshuffled Roster

Team World heads into the Laver Cup without Ben Shelton — who chose Davis Cup for the United States over the event — and without Tommy Paul, who withdrew from Laver Cup on September 12 due to an elbow injury. Their replacements tell their own stories: Brandon Nakashima, who stepped in for Shelton, is No. 16 in the world and making his Laver Cup debut. Francisco Cerundolo, replacing Paul, is competing in his fourth consecutive Laver Cup — a veteran of the format who brings quality and experience to a reshuffled team.

The full Team World roster: Taylor Fritz, Alex de Minaur, Alexander Bublik, Learner Tien, Brandon Nakashima, Francisco Cerundolo. Captain Andre Agassi returns for his second year leading the side. Vice-captain Pat Rafter is alongside him.

Team Europe's Depth

Team Europe enters as the heavy favourites based on rankings and recent form. The roster — Carlos Alcaraz, Alexander Zverev, Casper Ruud, Flavio Cobolli, Jakub Mensik, Rafael Jodar — is led by captain Yannick Noah and vice-captain Tim Henman. Zverev comes in as the freshest Grand Slam champion on either team, having won the US Open twelve days ago. Alcaraz, returning from his wrist injury that cost him most of the summer, reached the US Open fourth round and is building his match fitness ahead of the Asian swing.

Europe leads the all-time Laver Cup series 8–1. Team World's only victory came in 2025, making them the defending champions heading into this 10th edition of the event.

What's Next

The Laver Cup runs September 25–27 at The O2 in London. The format awards one point per match win on Day 1, two per win on Day 2, and three per win on Day 3. The first team to 13 points wins. Sky Sports has exclusive UK broadcast rights. Coverage is available internationally through the official Laver Cup channels.

After the Laver Cup, the tennis calendar moves into the Asian swing in full — China Open in Beijing beginning September 30, followed by the Tokyo ATP 500 and the Shanghai Masters in October. For players like Tien, Nakashima, and de Minaur, the Laver Cup is a moment to build confidence and momentum before that stretch of high-stakes ranking points.

First, though: six more days. And for Tien, a very busy weekend still ahead in Prague.
