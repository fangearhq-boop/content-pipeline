Shelton Stumbles, Tien Shines: USA and Czechia Tied 1-1 in Davis Cup

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | September 19, 2026

It was the kind of Davis Cup Friday that contained multitudes. In Prague's O2 Arena, on indoor hard courts that favored aggressive serve-and-volley tennis, the United States and Czech Republic opened their Davis Cup Qualifier Round 2 tie with a dramatic pair of singles matches — and the Americans head into Saturday's doubles and reverse singles with the tie delicately poised at 1-1.

The story of the day had two very different chapters.

Tien: A Debut for the Ages

Learner Tien made his Davis Cup debut on Friday, and he made it count. Facing Jakub Mensik — the Czech Republic's star and the No. 11 player in the world — Tien was composed, controlled, and clinical. He won 6-2, 6-4, breaking Mensik's serve three times across the match and never giving the Czech anything to build momentum with. It was a statement performance from a 20-year-old player who has been growing into one of the more complete players in American tennis.

To put it in context: Mensik is a player who had just competed in the Laver Cup and has beaten players ranked in the top five this year. Tien dispatched him in straight sets in his first ever Davis Cup rubber. U.S. captain Bob Bryan had reason to be delighted.

Shelton's Difficult Return

Ben Shelton's first competitive match since the US Open final did not go the way he or the American camp would have hoped. Playing Jiri Lehecka — the Czech's top-ranked player heading into the tie — Shelton lost 6-4, 6-4 in a match where Lehecka simply played better from the opening game.

The Czech broke Shelton's serve early in each set, which neutralized one of the American's biggest weapons and forced him to chase the match from behind. Shelton's serve, normally a powerful asset, was not at its usual standard — perhaps a reflection of where he is mentally and physically after the emotional weight of his first Grand Slam final just five days ago. He fought, as he always does, but Lehecka was sharper and more focused on the day.

Shelton's post-match reaction was characteristically honest. He acknowledged the loss, gave Lehecka credit, and pointed to Saturday's reverse singles as a chance to respond. The same rematch could happen: if the tie is still alive after doubles, Shelton vs. Lehecka goes again. Davis Cup does not give players long to dwell.

The Missing Men

USA went into this tie without Taylor Fritz and Tommy Paul, both of whom withdrew before the tournament. That left the team thinner than ideal, with Christian Harrison and Austin Krajicek providing depth in doubles and as fifth players. The absences added pressure to Shelton and Tien, and Friday showed both how much Tien has grown and how much the team misses Fritz in particular.

Czech Republic, meanwhile, has Mensik and Lehecka as a formidable singles tandem, and their doubles game will be tested Saturday when Harrison and Krajicek line up against the Czech pair.

What's Next

Saturday's order of play begins with the doubles rubber, then moves to reverse singles if needed: Mensik vs. Tien, and Lehecka vs. Shelton. First to three rubbers wins the tie and earns a spot at the Davis Cup Finals in Bologna, Italy, November 24–29.

For the USA, it is a genuine opportunity to show depth and resilience without some of their biggest names. For Shelton, Saturday offers what Davis Cup has always offered: the chance to right a wrong, quickly, in front of teammates who need him.

Tien's dual allegiance makes for an interesting subplot: after this weekend in Prague, he heads to London as part of Team World for the Laver Cup starting September 25. A busy week is about to get busier.
