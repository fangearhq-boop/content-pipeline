All-American WTA Final: Jovic Defends Title Against Stearns in Guadalajara

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | September 19, 2026

The 2026 Guadalajara Open has delivered a finale worth the wait. On Saturday, defending champion Iva Jovic faces fellow American Peyton Stearns in an all-American WTA 500 final — a matchup that would have seemed unlikely a week ago, but now sits as one of the most compelling stories of the fall swing.

Jovic arrived in Guadalajara as the No. 2 seed and the player everyone expected to be in this spot. The 22-year-old American has been one of the most consistent performers on hardcourt in 2026, and she showed exactly why in Friday's semifinal — a commanding 6-4, 6-1 victory over No. 6 seed Cristina Bucsa of Spain. Jovic broke Bucsa's serve four times in the match, mixing her heavy forehand with disciplined baseline positioning and a serve that found the lines when it mattered most.

Entering the final, Jovic is ranked a career-high No. 13 in the world — a number that reflects the steady improvement she has made across the 2026 season. A title today would push her further up the rankings and cement her position as one of the faces of American women's tennis heading into the Asian swing.

Stearns Delivers the Upset Run of the Tournament

Peyton Stearns came to Guadalajara unseeded and has been arguably the story of the week. The 23-year-old American was not expected to reach the final, but she has been relentless. Her semifinal performance against No. 8 seed Liudmila Samsonova was a masterclass in composure under pressure. Stearns dropped the first-set tiebreak 7–5 after fighting off multiple break points, then flipped the match entirely and ran away with it 7-6(7), 6-2.

It was a quietly methodical performance — Stearns is not flashy, but she is consistent, tactical and difficult to put away. Her game is built on steady groundstrokes and reading her opponent's patterns well, and against Samsonova she did exactly that once she found her footing in the second set.

For Stearns, reaching a WTA 500 final is the biggest achievement of her career so far. She has been working her way up the rankings through results and this week could be the breakthrough moment that accelerates that climb.

First Career Meeting

Jovic and Stearns have never played each other on the WTA tour before Saturday's final. That fact alone adds an element of uncertainty to a match where Jovic's pedigree and match-day experience would otherwise make her a clear favourite. Stearns has shown all week that she is more than capable of winning regardless of the opponent's ranking or seed — she beats the players in front of her and is not overwhelmed by the occasion.

The key question is whether Jovic can impose her own game quickly enough. When she gets her forehand going early and controls the baseline rally, she is difficult to beat. But Stearns will force Jovic to earn every point and will look to extend rallies and wait for her spots.

What's Next

Whoever lifts the Guadalajara trophy on Saturday will carry momentum into the WTA Asian swing. The China Open in Beijing begins September 30, followed by the Wuhan Open on October 12 — two high-value WTA 500 and 1000 events that will shape the end-of-season rankings.

For Jovic, a second straight Guadalajara title would make her a genuine Watch List contender for the WTA Finals in Cancun. For Stearns, even a runner-up finish here will push her deeper into the seedings for Beijing and set up a strong autumn campaign.

The WTA 500 Guadalajara Open final tips off Saturday afternoon. Coverage is available via WTA Live and regional broadcast partners.
