Canada One Point Away: Davis Cup Qualifier Day 2 vs. France

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | September 19, 2026

Canada woke up on Saturday morning holding a 2-0 lead over France in their Davis Cup Qualifier Round 2 tie — and standing just one point from a return to the Davis Cup Finals in Bologna, Italy.

Friday's Day 1 at the Centre Vidéotron in Quebec City was a near-perfect performance for the home side. Liam Draxl opened the tie with what he described as the best match of his career, defeating French No. 1 Arthur Rinderknech 6-2, 7-5 in a confident, assertive showing that set the tone. Then Félix Auger-Aliassime closed the day with a three-set battle — 7-6(5), 4-6, 6-3 over Quentin Halys — that showed FAA's mental fortitude in a tight, contested match.

With the tie at 2-0 in Canada's favour, the mathematics are simple: one more rubber win from Saturday's three remaining matches puts Canada through to the Davis Cup Finals.

Draxl's Career-Defining Day

It is worth taking a moment to appreciate what Liam Draxl did on Friday. Defeating Arthur Rinderknech — France's top-ranked singles player and a seasoned Davis Cup performer — in straight sets, on the first day of a high-pressure home tie, is no small feat. Draxl played with poise and attacked early, refusing to let Rinderknech dictate from the baseline. His net game was particularly effective, and his serve gave him free points when he needed them most.

At 24 years old, Draxl is maturing as a Davis Cup competitor, and this week in Quebec City could prove to be a significant moment in his career trajectory.

FAA Delivers in Three Sets

Félix Auger-Aliassime's match against Halys was trickier. France came back to win the second set and kept the match competitive into the third, but FAA found his best tennis when it mattered. His serve throughout the tie has been a major weapon, and in the decisive third set he controlled the match from the first game.

Currently ranked No. 4 in the world, FAA is in the form of his life heading into the fall Asian swing. His Davis Cup record for Canada remains outstanding, and this week he is reminding everyone why he is indispensable to the national team's ambitions.

Day 2 Schedule

Saturday begins with the doubles rubber at 2 PM ET. If Canada takes that point, the tie is over and the qualification secured. If France wins the doubles, the tie continues with reverse singles: Rinderknech vs. Draxl, then Halys vs. FAA. France would need to win all three remaining rubbers — doubles and both reverse singles — to pull off a dramatic comeback.

For France, it is a deeply unlikely scenario. But Davis Cup has produced stranger turnarounds, and Rinderknech and Halys are capable players who will not simply fold under pressure. France's pride is on the line.

What's Next

If Canada wins Saturday, they advance to the Davis Cup Finals in Bologna, Italy, scheduled for November 24–29. The Final 8 will feature seven qualified nations plus defending champions Italy, who receive a bye into the Finals.

For FAA, the path then leads to Beijing and the China Open starting September 30 — where he is one of seven top-10 players confirmed in the field, alongside defending champion Sinner and returning six-time champion Djokovic. After a season defined by consistency, the fall stretch could deliver the big title FAA is still seeking.

Centre Vidéotron will be rocking on Saturday afternoon. Canada is one point from Bologna.
