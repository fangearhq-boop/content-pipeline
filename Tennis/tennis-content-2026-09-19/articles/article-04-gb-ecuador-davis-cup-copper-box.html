Great Britain vs. Ecuador: Davis Cup Qualifier Opens at Copper Box London

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | September 19, 2026

Great Britain's Davis Cup campaign gets underway on Saturday at the Copper Box Arena in London, where captain Andy Murray has named a squad with a blend of experience and rising form. The two-day tie against Ecuador — Days 1 and 2 running September 19–20 — is Great Britain's path to the Davis Cup Finals in Bologna, Italy, November 24–29.

GB enters the tie as clear favourites. They are playing on home soil on a surface and in conditions that suit their game, and they have the personnel to close out a best-of-five tie efficiently. Ecuador, however, has earned their place in this round and will not come to London to be tourists.

The Great Britain Squad

The GB team is well constructed for this tie. Cameron Norrie remains a reliable anchor in singles — his game travels across surfaces, and his composure under Davis Cup pressure has improved year on year. Alongside him, Arthur Fery brings form and confidence after reaching the Winston-Salem final in August, a result that announced him as a genuine contender at ATP 250 and beyond. Fery has been one of the more exciting British tennis stories of 2026.

In doubles, Henry Patten is currently ranked world No. 1 — that in itself is a significant advantage. Patten's reading of the game, net presence, and experience with a range of doubles partners makes him a near-certain point for GB in the doubles rubber. Neal Skupski completes the doubles rotation with his own considerable pedigree in the format. Toby Samuel rounds out the five-man team as a versatile option.

Ecuador's Challenge

Ecuador's presence in this round of the Davis Cup Qualifiers is itself a notable achievement for South American tennis. The Ecuadorian team has historically punched above its weight in team competition, with a tradition of passionate, committed Davis Cup play stretching back decades. On a neutral surface against a stronger opponent, their best path is to make life uncomfortable early and hope the tie extends to five rubbers.

On indoor hard court in London, the mathematical advantage sits firmly with Great Britain. But Davis Cup has produced upsets before, and Ecuador will give every point everything they have.

The Copper Box Advantage

The Copper Box Arena, built for the 2012 London Olympics and a venue synonymous with British sport, will give GB a vocal, passionate home crowd. Home advantage in Davis Cup is real and significant — the crowd, the familiar conditions, the reduced travel burden — and GB will lean into every element of it. A full Copper Box behind Norrie and Fery in singles should give the home side a meaningful edge.

Day 1 begins Saturday. Day 2 follows Sunday. The order of play is singles-doubles-reverse singles.

What's Next

A GB victory sends them to Bologna for the Davis Cup Finals, where they would join the likes of Canada (if they close out France), Italy (hosts, automatic), and other qualifiers across the fourteen second-round ties being played this weekend around the world.

Fery, in particular, will be eager to build on his strong late-season form. After Winston-Salem and now Davis Cup, the young Briton is positioning himself well for a strong finish to 2026 and an aggressive ranking push heading into the new season. Davis Cup often becomes a launchpad — Fery has the tools to make the most of this platform.
