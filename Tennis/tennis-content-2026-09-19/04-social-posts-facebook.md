### STORY 2: Canada vs. France Davis Cup Day 2

**FB Long-Form** (post 8:00 AM ET)

Canada woke up Saturday morning in an enviable position: two points from the Davis Cup qualification they've been chasing.

On Friday in Quebec City, Liam Draxl delivered one of the best performances of his young career, defeating French No. 1 Arthur Rinderknech 6-2, 7-5 in a controlled, aggressive effort. Then Félix Auger-Aliassime closed out the day in style, battling past Quentin Halys 7-6(5), 4-6, 6-3 in a match that showed exactly why FAA remains one of Canada's most important players in this format.

The tie stands at 2-0 in Canada's favour. In this best-of-five format, they need just one more point from Saturday's three remaining rubbers — the doubles plus two reverse singles — to secure their spot at the Davis Cup Finals in Bologna, Italy (November 24–29).

France isn't out of it mathematically. They would need to win all three remaining rubbers to take the tie 3-2. But Canada is in the driver's seat.

The doubles rubber goes first at 2 PM ET. After that, it'll be Rinderknech vs. Draxl and Halys vs. FAA in the reverse singles.

A huge day of Canadian tennis awaits at the Centre Vidéotron.

Will Canada seal the deal today?

### STORY 3: Czechia vs. USA Davis Cup — Tied 1-1, Day 2 Incoming

**FB Long-Form** (post 10:30 AM ET)

Ben Shelton played his first competitive match since the US Open final on Friday — and it did not go to plan.

In Prague at the O2 Arena, Shelton ran into Jiri Lehecka on Czech indoor hard courts, and Lehecka was simply better. The Czech No. 24 won 6-4, 6-4, breaking Shelton's serve early in both sets and never letting the American settle into a rhythm. Five days after the emotional US Open final loss to Alexander Zverev, Shelton wasn't quite himself — and Lehecka made him pay.

The good news for the USA: Learner Tien had already given them a 1-0 lead with a brilliant Davis Cup debut. Tien defeated Jakub Mensik 6-2, 6-4 in a match that showcased exactly the kind of cool, composed tennis that has been turning heads on the ATP tour. Tien's Davis Cup debut: flawless.

So the United States and Czech Republic head into Day 2 level at 1-1. Today brings the doubles first — Christian Harrison and Austin Krajicek representing the Stars and Stripes — then the two reverse singles. If the tie goes the distance, Shelton gets a chance at redemption against Lehecka.

Only one team advances to the Davis Cup Finals in Bologna. For USA, it's about pride, momentum, and proving they can compete without Fritz and Paul (both withdrew pre-tournament).

### STORY 4: Great Britain vs. Ecuador Davis Cup

**FB Long-Form** (post 6:00 PM ET)

Great Britain's Davis Cup campaign gets underway today at the Copper Box Arena in London, and the squad captain Andy Murray has put together looks very capable.

The GB team features Cam Norrie, Arthur Fery — who made his mark with a memorable run to the Winston-Salem final in August — Henry Patten (currently ranked World No. 1 in doubles), Toby Samuel and Neal Skupski. It's a well-rounded group with experience and some emerging talent.

Their opponents Ecuador are making their second-round qualifier appearance, but on indoor hard court in London, the odds heavily favour the home side. GB will play Day 2 on Sunday, September 20. Whoever wins the best-of-five tie earns a spot at the Davis Cup Finals in Bologna, Italy, November 24–29.

Fery's form in particular has been exciting to watch. The 24-year-old Brit has made significant strides this year, and a Davis Cup run under pressure will only add to his growing experience.

With Patten's doubles dominance and Norrie's clay-to-hard versatility, GB should have the tools to get through.

### STORY 5: Laver Cup Countdown — Tien's Wild Week

**FB Long-Form** (post 9:00 PM ET)

Here's a fun tennis story for your Saturday night.

Learner Tien spent this weekend in Prague competing for the United States in the Davis Cup. He beat Jakub Mensik 6-2, 6-4 on Friday in a stellar Davis Cup debut. Then on Saturday, he suited up again for the doubles and potential reverse singles, all while the USA and Czechia battled for a spot in the Bologna Finals.

Next week? Tien will be in London, wearing Team World colours at the Laver Cup.

His Davis Cup opponent Mensik, meanwhile, played for Czech Republic this weekend — and will play for Team Europe at the Laver Cup starting September 25. Two players on opposite sides of the net this weekend, together in the same building seven days from now.

The Laver Cup — which runs September 25–27 at The O2 in London — features Team Europe (Alcaraz, Zverev, Ruud, Cobolli, Mensik, Jodar) against a reshuffled Team World (Fritz, de Minaur, Bublik, Tien, Nakashima, Cerundolo). Nakashima replaced Shelton (who chose Davis Cup instead) and Cerundolo replaced Paul (elbow injury).

Team Europe leads the all-time series 8–1, after Team World finally broke through with a win in 2025. This edition, the 10th in Laver Cup history, promises electric tennis and another compelling chapter in the rivalry.

Six days away. Start getting excited.
