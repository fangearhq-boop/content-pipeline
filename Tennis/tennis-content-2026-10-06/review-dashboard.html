### Long-Form Post The China Open semifinals produced one of the strangest, most dramatic moments of the 2026 tennis season. With Novak Djokovic leading Daniil Medvedev 7-5, 5-3 on Monday, something snapped. After losing a point, Medvedev struck a ball in frustration — and it sailed into the front row of stands, striking a spectator near the eye. The crowd went silent. Officials came to court. Medvedev didn't argue. He was disqualified. The Russian loses all ranking points and prize money from the tournament. It's a brutal outcome for Medvedev, who had beaten the top seed Zverev in the quarterfinals just days earlier. In the other semifinal, Alex de Minaur advanced when Hubert Hurkacz retired due to injury with the Australian leading 6-4, 3-2. That sets up Tuesday's final: Novak Djokovic — 33-0 all-time in Beijing — against Alex de Minaur. Djokovic has won the China Open every single time he has played in the final: 2009, 2010, 2012, 2013, 2014, 2015. That's six titles in six attempts. He's going for No. 7 today. De Minaur has never won in Beijing. But with a crowd cheering for an upset... can the Australian pull it off? Have you ever seen a disqualification like this in a major tennis semifinal? ### Image Post Caption Beijing Final day! Djokovic (33-0, 6 titles) vs. de Minaur. ### Long-Form Post World No. 1 Jannik Sinner has withdrawn from next week's Rolex Shanghai Masters, extending his longest absence from professional tennis. Sinner issued a statement on Saturday: "Unfortunately, I won't be able to play at the Rolex Shanghai Masters this year as I had hoped. I always enjoy playing in front of the Shanghai fans and I'm very sorry that I won't be able to do so next week." The Italian has not played since winning Wimbledon in July — a period that has now stretched to seven consecutive missed events. A knee injury sustained around that time has proven more stubborn than initially hoped, with Italian media now reporting that his appearance at Vienna (October 26) and the Paris Masters in November also remain uncertain. Sinner still leads the ATP rankings and has qualified for the year-end ATP Finals in Turin. But in the Race to the Finals standings, Alexander Zverev leads him by around 800 points — and Sinner's absence from Shanghai means he loses approximately 3,550 Race points he earned there last year. That gap could balloon significantly by the time Shanghai concludes on October 18. For fans who had hoped to see the defending Beijing champion return in Shanghai, this is disappointing news.
