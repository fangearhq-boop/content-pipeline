Jannik Sinner Withdraws from Shanghai Masters, Extending Longest Absence of His Career

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap

October 6, 2026

World No. 1 Jannik Sinner has officially withdrawn from the Rolex Shanghai Masters, ATP Tour officials confirmed, extending what has become the longest competitive absence of his career. The Italian has not played since winning Wimbledon in July, a stretch that now covers seven consecutive missed events. His statement, issued Saturday, offered little comfort to fans hoping for a return: "Unfortunately, I won't be able to play at the Rolex Shanghai Masters this year as I had hoped. I always enjoy playing in front of the Shanghai fans and I'm very sorry that I won't be able to do so next week."

A Knee Injury That Won't Resolve

Sinner's knee injury, which emerged around the time of Wimbledon, was initially described as precautionary. But event after event has slipped by — the Canadian Open, the Cincinnati Open, the US Open, the China Open — and now Shanghai joins the list. Italian media reports indicate that even Vienna (October 26) and the Paris Masters in November are not guaranteed.

The Italian has been working with specialists, including a reported consultation with a Lyon-based knee specialist earlier in October, but has not yet reached the fitness level required to compete at the top of the ATP Tour. What remains unclear is the specific nature of the injury and its expected timeline to resolution.

Race to Turin Implications

The points arithmetic is stark. Sinner won the Shanghai Masters in 2025 and is defending approximately 1,000 ranking points (Masters 1000 events carry significant point allocations). More critically, in the Race to the ATP Finals standings, he stands to drop roughly 3,550 Race points through the Shanghai, Vienna, and Paris events he may miss — points accumulated during last year's autumn run that will no longer be defended.

Alexander Zverev, who currently leads Sinner in the Race standings by approximately 800 points, is expected to add to his tally in Shanghai. Conservative projections suggest the gap between the two could exceed 4,000 Race points by the time the Shanghai final is played on October 18. Zverev, who won the French Open and US Open in 2026, may be on course for his first Year-End No. 1 ranking.

Sinner's ATP Finals spot in Turin is already secure — he qualified as World No. 1 well before this injury period. But if he does not return before the Finals begin in November, he would enter the event with minimal match practice after a months-long absence.

What's Next

The next realistic window for Sinner's return is the Vienna Open (October 26). Italian media reports describe that timeline as uncertain. If Vienna is also missed, Paris-Bercy (late October/early November) becomes the last significant event before the ATP Finals in Turin. Sinner's team has not confirmed a return date. Fans and rivals alike are watching closely — the Tour misses the world No. 1's presence, and the Race to Turin has become significantly clearer in his absence.
