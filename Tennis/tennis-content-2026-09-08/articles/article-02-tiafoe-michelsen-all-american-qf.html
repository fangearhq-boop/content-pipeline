Tiafoe vs. Michelsen: A U.S. Man Is Going to the US Open Semifinals for the First Time Since Roddick

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | September 8, 2026

US Open 2026 | Frances Tiafoe | Alex Michelsen | Quarterfinals | American Tennis

American tennis fans have waited 23 years for this moment. On Tuesday afternoon at Louis Armstrong Stadium, Frances Tiafoe and Alex Michelsen walk onto the court for their US Open quarterfinal — and before a single ball is struck, history is already made. A U.S. man will be in the semifinals of the US Open for the first time since Andy Roddick won the title in 2003.

The drought is over. The only remaining question is which American carries the banner into the final four.

The Road to This Moment

Frances Tiafoe, the No. 11 seed, has been building toward this his entire career. The 26-year-old from Maryland reached the US Open semifinals in 2022 and has been chasing that moment ever since. He earned this quarterfinal the hard way, defeating Daniil Medvedev in four competitive sets, 7-6(1), 6-4, 7-6(6), in the fourth round — outlasting one of the best hardcourt players on tour.

Alex Michelsen took a different path. The 22-year-old from California entered the US Open unseeded and has simply refused to lose, dispatching Tomas Martin Etcheverry in a composed straight-sets display, 7-6(6), 6-4, 6-4. He is the youngest American man to reach the US Open quarterfinals since Roddick himself in 2002.

The Weight of History

The last American man to win the US Open was Andy Roddick, in 2003. In the years since, American men have made occasional deep runs — but never claimed the title. Tiafoe's 2022 semifinal run, which captured the imagination of the country, ended one match short of the final.

Now two Americans stand on the cusp of something genuinely significant. The winner will be the first U.S. man in the US Open final four in over two decades. That is not a small thing. Arthur Ashe Stadium will be loud on Tuesday afternoon regardless of which American wins — the crowd simply wants to witness the moment.

Contrasting Styles, Matching Hunger

Tiafoe brings athleticism, flair, and veteran experience. He knows what Ashe Stadium sounds like when the crowd is fully behind you — he has lived that moment before. His two-handed backhand is one of the best on tour, and his ability to raise his level in the biggest points has defined his career.

Michelsen brings something different: precision, calmness under pressure, and a serve that has steadily improved. He has not dropped a set in this tournament. At 22, he plays with the composure of someone who does not yet fully grasp how big this stage is — which may actually be an advantage.

What's Next

Tuesday's match at Louis Armstrong Stadium is scheduled to begin at 1:00 PM ET. The winner advances to the semifinals and faces the winner of the Alcaraz vs. Shelton night match at Arthur Ashe Stadium. An all-American semifinal remains a possibility depending on results.

For American tennis, the important milestone has already been achieved. But both Tiafoe and Michelsen have made clear through their play this tournament that they are not here for a consolation prize. One of them wants to go further.
