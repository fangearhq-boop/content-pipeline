Gauff vs. Andreeva at Arthur Ashe: Two Champions, One Semifinal Spot

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | September 8, 2026

US Open 2026 | Coco Gauff | Mirra Andreeva | Quarterfinals | WTA

The US Open quarterfinal between Coco Gauff and Mirra Andreeva on Tuesday evening carries layers that reward attention. Both players are 21 years old. Both have already won Grand Slam titles. And earlier this year at the French Open, Andreeva beat Gauff in the semifinals on the way to winning Roland Garros.

Tonight at Arthur Ashe Stadium, they meet again — with a place in the US Open semifinals and a potential date with Aryna Sabalenka as the prize.

The Road Here

Gauff has been efficient and sharp throughout this tournament. She dismantled Iva Jovic in the fourth round 6-1, 6-4, losing just five games and looking very much like the player who won this title in 2023. The New York crowd has always responded to Gauff — she grew up in front of tennis audiences who watched her as a teenager, and she has grown into the sport's biggest American star.

Andreeva's path to the quarterfinals was more contested. She defeated Anastasia Potapova 5-7, 6-4, 6-3 in the fourth round — dropping the opening set before regrouping to win the last two convincingly. It was a reminder that Andreeva can find solutions when plans go wrong, a quality that defines champions.

The French Open Rematch

The subplot here is hard to ignore. Andreeva beat Gauff at the 2026 French Open semifinals before going on to win the title. It was a match that underscored how much Andreeva's game had grown — her baseline consistency, her shot-making under pressure, and her ability to absorb pace and redirect it.

Gauff took note. The weeks since have shown her refining exactly the areas that Andreeva exploited in Paris. The hardcourt surface at the US Open suits Gauff more naturally than clay suits Andreeva, which shifts the dynamics considerably. This rematch is far from a foregone conclusion.

Contrasting Strengths

Gauff's power and serve have elevated considerably this season. Her first-serve percentage and return statistics at this tournament rank among the best in the draw. She is also a superior athlete at the net — a dimension she has developed deliberately over the past 18 months.

Andreeva brings exceptional baseline consistency and an ability to stay in rallies that should not be winnable. She reads ball trajectory unusually well and redirects pace without seeming to generate it — a style that frustrates big hitters and rewards patience. On a fast hardcourt like Ashe, her margins are tighter, but she has shown repeatedly that margins do not rattle her.

What's Next

The winner faces Aryna Sabalenka in the women's semifinals, scheduled for Thursday. Sabalenka defeated Wimbledon champion Noskova 6-4, 6-3 in Tuesday's earlier quarterfinal and is operating at an intimidating level. Any player who wants to stop the three-peat will need to be at their absolute best.

Tuesday evening at Arthur Ashe Stadium, two of the sport's brightest young champions will settle which of them gets that chance. Both have already proven they can win a Grand Slam. The question tonight is which one wants this title more.
