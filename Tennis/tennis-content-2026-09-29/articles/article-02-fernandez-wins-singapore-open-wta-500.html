Leylah Fernandez Wins Singapore Open — Drops Gibson 7-5, 6-0 to Claim 6th WTA Title

By Marcus Cole | September 29, 2026

WTA | Singapore Open

Leylah Fernandez is a WTA 500 champion again. The Canadian defeated Talia Gibson 7-5, 6-0 in the Singapore Open final on Sunday, completing a dominant run through a draw that included the upset of top seed and Roland Garros champion Mirra Andreeva.

Fernandez won the final nine games of the match, turning a competitive first set into a dominant afternoon and claiming her 6th WTA Tour singles title in the process — her second at the prestigious 500 level. The victory lands at exactly the right moment: after an inconsistent 2026 season, Fernandez heads into the Asian swing as a title holder, with significant WTA Race to Singapore points in her column.

The Final

Gibson, an Australian qualifier who had the tournament of her life in Singapore, made Fernandez work in the opening set. The first set went with serve deep into the games before Fernandez found an opening, winning 7-5 to take control. What followed was one-way traffic. Fernandez won the second set 6-0 in clinical fashion, the scoreline reflecting the quality of her tennis from the moment she settled.

The match lasted approximately one hour and 38 minutes in total. Fernandez hit clean from the baseline, moved well, and — critically — closed efficiently once she had momentum. That ability to finish matches, rather than letting opponents back in, had been a weakness in earlier parts of her 2026 season. Sunday suggested she has found it again.

The Road to the Final

The defining result of Fernandez's Singapore week came in the quarterfinals. Mirra Andreeva, the No. 5-ranked player in the world and the top seed in the draw, was dispatched by Fernandez in what became the tournament's defining upset. It was Fernandez's first semifinal appearance of 2026 — a signal that she had recaptured some of what had made her dangerous at her best.

The semifinal tested her further. Fernandez dropped the first set 1-6 to Maja Chwalinska before mounting a comeback, winning 6-4, 6-2 to advance. That resilience under pressure — coming back from losing badly in the opening set — carried the psychological edge into the final.

Andreeva and Gibson

Andreeva's quarterfinal exit was the story that shaped the back end of the tournament. The Roland Garros champion arrived as the clear favourite and left earlier than anyone expected. Her 2026 WTA swing ends without the Singapore title she had been projected to contend for. She remains firmly in the top tier of the WTA Race, but Fernandez's upset added an asterisk to the week.

Gibson's run to the final was remarkable in its own right. An Australian qualifier reaching a WTA 500 final takes navigating a draw of far more experienced and highly ranked opponents. She showed composure and quality all week before Fernandez's form in the final exceeded her.

What's Next

Fernandez will carry her Singapore title into the Asian swing, where the WTA calendar continues with events in Beijing, Wuhan, and the season-ending WTA Finals in Singapore later this fall. The Singapore points bonus reshuffles the Race to Singapore leaderboard meaningfully — her position in the top 8 conversation comes into sharper focus heading into October. Watch for her draw assignment in Beijing.
