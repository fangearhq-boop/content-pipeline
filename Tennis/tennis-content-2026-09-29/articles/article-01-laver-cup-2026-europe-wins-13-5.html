Team Europe Wins Laver Cup 2026 — Zverev Seals 13-5 Victory Over Team World at The O2

By Elena Voss | September 29, 2026

ATP | Laver Cup

Team Europe is the 2026 Laver Cup champion. Captained by Yannick Noah, Europe defeated Team World 13-5 at The O2 Arena in London across three days of competition, ending the defending champions' title defense in comprehensive fashion.

Alexander Zverev closed it out on Sunday with a 7-6(3), 6-3 win over Learner Tien — a fitting conclusion for the man who has won the Roland Garros title, the US Open, and now the Laver Cup in a single calendar year. It is the most decorated season any European player has put together in years.

How the Weekend Unfolded

Europe came out with purpose on Friday, taking a 3-1 lead on Day 1 as Casper Ruud opened with a comeback win over Francisco Cerúndolo, Carlos Alcaraz and Jakub Menšík won their doubles, and Rafael Jódar secured another point for the blue side.

Saturday brought the event's most compelling tennis. Team World rallied in the afternoon session to pull ahead 5-3, with Alex de Minaur upsetting Zverev in a comeback and Learner Tien defeating Flavio Cobolli in a Laver Breaker to swing momentum. But Europe answered hard in the evening session. Alcaraz saved two match points against Taylor Fritz before winning in an extended deciding set. Ruud and Zverev then combined to win the doubles, restoring Europe's lead at 7-5 going into Sunday.

By Sunday's end there was no drama remaining. Cobolli and Menšík won their doubles to push Europe close, and Zverev's defeat of Tien — efficient, controlled, clinical — brought the score to 13-5 and sent Noah's squad to the trophy.

Zverev's Year in Full

It is difficult to overstate how complete Zverev's 2026 has been. He won Roland Garros in May. He won the US Open in September. And on Sunday at The O2, he sealed the Laver Cup for Team Europe. Three titles across three different surfaces and formats in the same season.

The clinching win over Tien, combined with his earlier defeat to de Minaur on Saturday, showed both sides of Zverev's character this week. He absorbed a loss, returned Sunday focused, and delivered when Europe needed it most. That composure — the ability to respond without panic — is part of what makes him the most complete player in the world right now.

Alcaraz and De Minaur — The Supporting Cast

Two other performances defined the weekend. Alcaraz's Saturday night singles win against Fritz was the match Europe most needed. Down 5-3 after the afternoon session, trailing in the third set and facing match points, Alcaraz's refusal to yield turned the day. It was a moment of individual brilliance in a team setting — the kind that Laver Cup manufactures at its best.

For Team World, de Minaur's defeat of Zverev was the high point of what was, ultimately, a difficult weekend. The Australian was arguably the best individual performer across the three days, winning in compelling fashion and giving his side something real to build on. But with Zverev, Alcaraz, Ruud, and the rest of Europe matching and exceeding on almost every other court, de Minaur's efforts could only do so much.

What's Next

The Laver Cup now moves into the offseason record books, and the focus of world tennis shifts east. The China Open in Beijing begins September 30, bringing with it the return of World No. 1 Jannik Sinner after an 11-week absence. Zverev, Djokovic, and de Minaur are all in the draw.

For Team Europe, the 2026 Laver Cup adds another chapter to a rivalry that now stands 9-1 in Europe's favour all-time. Team World will have to build toward a 2027 redemption in a different city. For Yannick Noah, the win represents his first Laver Cup title as captain — a perfect footnote to tennis's most theatrical event.
