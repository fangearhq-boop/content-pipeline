Sinner Confirmed for China Open — World No. 1 Returns to Competition After 11-Week Absence

By Ryan Calloway | September 29, 2026

ATP | China Open

The wait is over. Jannik Sinner is returning to competitive tennis at the China Open in Beijing, beginning September 30, ending an 11-week absence that began when he won Wimbledon in July.

Italian sports outlet Sportmediaset reported that Sinner has traveled to Beijing and plans to enter the draw at the ATP 500 event. Sinner addressed questions about his fitness directly in a conversation with La Repubblica: "The knee? It's all fine." Earlier reports had suggested he might skip the Asian swing entirely and not return until Vienna in late October — those fears have been set aside.

The Injury and the Absence

Sinner won Wimbledon on July 12, defeating Alexander Zverev in the final in what stands as one of the most anticipated matches of the 2026 season. Shortly after the title celebration, reports emerged that his right knee — which had been managed carefully for several months — had flared significantly. He withdrew from the US Open before the tournament began, and with each passing week, the timeline for his return remained uncertain.

The 11-week absence is the longest competitive break of Sinner's career. He has not played since the Wimbledon final. His ATP Rankings No. 1 position has been maintained throughout — he holds 11,500 points in the Race to Turin, with Zverev No. 2 at 9,690 — but with the Asian swing delivering significant points for results and Zverev in outstanding form, the gap needed defending.

The China Open Draw

Sinner's return at Beijing carries immediate stakes beyond the physical. He is the defending China Open champion, which means 500 Race to Turin points are on the line against the seeded draw. A deep run maintains his lead. An early exit, while not catastrophic, would invite Zverev to close the gap meaningfully before the Shanghai Masters and the ATP Finals.

The draw in Beijing is competitive. Zverev is present and in arguably the best form of any player in the world right now. Novak Djokovic is also entered in Beijing — the Serb famously withdrew from the China Open for over a decade before returning this fall, and arrives having dealt with a health scare at the US Open that generated significant discussion. De Minaur rounds out a draw that would test a fully-fit player, let alone one returning from a lengthy layoff.

Sinner's first match is expected no earlier than Wednesday. How he moves, how his serve holds up, and how his timing feels against tour-level opponents will tell a great deal about what the final months of 2026 look like for the world No. 1.

The Road Ahead

Beyond Beijing, the Asian swing includes the Shanghai Masters — an ATP Masters 1000 event where both Sinner and Zverev will be looking for title points. The season ends at the ATP Finals in Turin in November, where Sinner will be defending home-territory status as Italy's leading player.

For the first time since July, the world No. 1 is back. The China Open begins Monday.

What's Next

The China Open runs in Beijing from September 30 through October 9. Sinner is expected to play his first match on Wednesday. Zverev and Djokovic are both in the draw. Watch for the first-round schedule confirmation and Sinner's bracket assignment when the Beijing draw is released.
