Hangzhou Open Final Set — Medvedev Faces Rublev in All-Russian ATP 250 Showdown

By Elena Voss | September 29, 2026

ATP | Hangzhou Open

The 2026 Hangzhou Open will be decided by an all-Russian final. Daniil Medvedev, the No. 1 seed, meets Andrey Rublev in Monday's title match — a rivalry that has produced some of the more compelling tennis between two players who know each other exceptionally well.

Both players earned their places through strong semifinal performances on Sunday. Rublev defeated qualifier Kyrian Jacquet 7-6(7), 6-1 in a match that took on historical significance: it was Rublev's 400th career ATP Tour win, a milestone for a player who has been one of the most durable competitors on tour since his breakthrough seasons.

Semifinals in Full

Rublev's victory over Jacquet was tighter than the final score suggests. Jacquet, who had been one of the stories of the week after defeating 6th seed Fabian Marozsan to reach his first ATP Tour semifinal, pushed the Russian to a tiebreak in the opening set before Rublev settled in the second set to close it out. The 400th win came with appropriate difficulty for a milestone moment.

Medvedev had a more straightforward afternoon against Roman Safiullin, winning 7-5, 6-4 in one hour and 42 minutes. As the No. 1 seed who received a first-round bye, Medvedev has managed his energy well through the week — a factor that could matter in a final played at pace against another Russian's intensity.

The Rivalry

Medvedev and Rublev are more than rivals. They were training partners in earlier stages of their careers, have known each other since their junior days, and share a court history that has produced matches of genuine quality across multiple surfaces. Their head-to-head record has swung over the years — Medvedev holds the edge overall, but Rublev's victories have often come in meaningful moments.

The contrast in their paths to this final makes the match compelling. Medvedev: clinical, seeded No. 1, handled a smoother path. Rublev: grinding, emotional, celebrating a career milestone along the way. Both have reasons to want this particular title.

Stakes and Context

With Sinner, Zverev, Alcaraz, and most of the Laver Cup contingent absent from Hangzhou, the ATP 250 draw opened meaningfully. The prize fund is $1,039,090, and with the Asian swing continuing into October, the Race to Turin points from a 250 title provide useful consolidation for both players.

Medvedev is ranked inside the top 6 in the Race and would benefit from a title here heading into Shanghai's Masters 1000 draw. Rublev, similarly positioned, knows that strong Asian swing results have historically been where he builds the second half of his season.

What's Next

The Hangzhou Open final is scheduled for Monday, September 29. Following the conclusion of both Hangzhou and Chengdu this week, the Asian swing continues with the Japan Open in Tokyo (concurrent with Beijing) and then Shanghai's Masters 1000 event in mid-October. The Medvedev-Rublev result will set the tone for both players entering that phase of the fall calendar.
