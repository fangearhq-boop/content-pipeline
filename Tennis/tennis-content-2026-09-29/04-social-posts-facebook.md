### STORY 1: Team Europe Wins Laver Cup 2026

**Long-Form Post [8:00 AM ET]**
Team Europe is the 2026 Laver Cup champion, defeating Team World 13-5 in a dominant weekend at The O2 Arena in London.

Alexander Zverev sealed the title with a 7-6, 6-3 win over Learner Tien on Sunday — a fitting close to a year in which he has won the Roland Garros title, the US Open, and now the Laver Cup. Carlos Alcaraz delivered the key swing moment on Saturday, surviving two match points against Taylor Fritz to keep Europe ahead when the match was most in the balance.

Team World's only real highlight was Alex de Minaur, who beat Zverev in a comeback on Saturday to pull his side within striking distance. But Europe had too much depth. They won nine of their twelve singles matches across the three days and wrapped it up without drama on Sunday.

Captain Yannick Noah won his first Laver Cup title. Team Europe's all-time record now stands at 9-1. Team World will have to wait until 2027 to reclaim what they won in San Francisco last year.

It was a terrific week of tennis — and a fitting way to close out the post-US Open phase before the Asian swing gets underway.

### STORY 2: Fernandez Wins Singapore Open

**Long-Form Post [10:30 AM ET]**
Leylah Fernandez is a WTA 500 champion again.

The Canadian claimed the Singapore Open title with a 7-5, 6-0 final victory over Talia Gibson on Sunday, winning the final nine games of the match in dominant fashion. It is Fernandez's 6th WTA Tour title and her 2nd at the prestigious 500 level.

What makes this run even more impressive: she had to get through top seed Mirra Andreeva in the quarterfinals — the Roland Garros champion who was the heavy favourite coming into the week. Fernandez then came from a set down in the semifinal against Maja Chwalinska before closing out the title against Gibson.

After a difficult and inconsistent 2026 season, this is exactly the result Fernandez needed heading into the Asian swing. The Singapore title delivers significant points in the WTA Race to Singapore and puts her name firmly back in the conversation.

Gibson, an Australian qualifier making her first WTA 500 final, had an outstanding week of her own.

Great tennis from both players — what a week in Singapore.

### STORY 3: Sinner Confirmed for China Open

**Long-Form Post [12:30 PM ET]**
Jannik Sinner is coming back.

The world No. 1 has confirmed his entry to the China Open in Beijing, which begins September 30. Italian sports outlet Sportmediaset reported that Sinner is traveling to Beijing, and Sinner himself told La Repubblica: "The knee? It's all fine."

Just a week ago, reports were suggesting Sinner might skip the entire Asian swing and not return until Vienna in late October. Those fears appear to have been unfounded.

Sinner has been out since winning Wimbledon on July 12 — a right knee injury that also forced him to withdraw from the US Open. It has been 11 weeks since his last competitive match.

His return is significant for several reasons: he is the defending China Open champion with 500 Race to Turin points to defend, and the draw in Beijing also includes Alexander Zverev (who has had an extraordinary 2026) and a returning Novak Djokovic.

The Asian swing just became the most interesting stretch of the tennis season.

Welcome back, Jannik.
