Race to Turin: Djokovic's Shanghai Exit Reshapes the Bubble, Zverev Remains in Command

By Marcus Cole | October 9, 2026

Shanghai, China

The Race to Turin math shifted on Friday in a way that was not on the scorecards at the start of the week. Novak Djokovic's second-round exit from the Shanghai Masters — a 6-4, 6-3 loss to Hubert Hurkacz — removes from the draw a player who had been a realistic contender for a deep run and all the ATP Race points that come with it.

It also opens a lane for Hurkacz that did not exist 48 hours ago.

For Alexander Zverev, who advances as top seed despite an ankle scare against Wu Yibing, the picture is increasingly clear. He entered Shanghai at approximately 8,740 Race to Turin points. Jannik Sinner, the only player who could theoretically challenge him for Year-End No. 1, is absent for the entire tournament — and with that absence, Sinner loses roughly 3,550 points he defended last year. Even if Zverev exits Shanghai early, the gap between the two players projects to exceed 4,500 points after this week.

The Bubble Fight

The more pressing drama sits three spots below the top two. The Race to Turin allocates eight spots to the ATP Finals field. Zverev and Sinner are qualified. Spots three through eight are genuinely contested.

Entering Shanghai, the bubble picture included Ben Shelton at approximately 4,430 points, Carlos Alcaraz at around 4,100, Daniil Medvedev, Arthur Fils, Alex de Minaur, and Jakub Menšík — all within range of each other and all within range of being pushed out by a player beneath them having a great week.

Hurkacz is now that player. His win over Djokovic on Friday was not just a career milestone — it was a Race to Turin calculation. Every round Hurkacz advances in Shanghai adds points that could push him inside the top eight bubble, potentially at the expense of a player who exits the tournament earlier.

De Minaur's status is also relevant. The Australian retired from the Beijing final with a groin injury and his Shanghai availability is uncertain. If he cannot compete or exits early, his Race points from this week's tournament will reflect that absence.

What's Next

The Shanghai Masters runs through October 18. After Shanghai, Vienna (October 26) and Paris-Bercy follow before the ATP Finals in Turin in November. For the players in the bubble, every tournament remaining in the calendar is a chance to move up or fall back. The math is unforgiving: this is not a race where finishing ninth is close enough.

Zverev will monitor his ankle over the weekend. If he stays healthy and goes deep, he locks up Year-End No. 1 and gives himself the kind of late-season momentum that can carry into Turin. If the ankle becomes a problem, the conversation about whether any player can catch him in the Race becomes more active — though the arithmetic still strongly favors the German regardless.

The Shanghai bubble fight is the one to watch. Five or six players are realistically competing for three or four spots, with Hurkacz newly inserted into the mix after Friday's result. The ATP Finals field will look different by October 18 than it does today.
