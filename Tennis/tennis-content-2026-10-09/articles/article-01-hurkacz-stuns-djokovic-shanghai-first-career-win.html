Hurkacz Stuns Djokovic at Shanghai for First Career Win, Ending Four-Time Champion's Run

By Elena Voss | October 9, 2026

Shanghai, China

Hubert Hurkacz had faced Novak Djokovic eight times before Friday. He had lost all eight. He came to Shanghai carrying a groin injury he had suffered in Beijing just days earlier. The match was supposed to be another data point on an uncomfortable ledger.

Instead, Hurkacz delivered one of the signature upsets of the 2026 Asian swing, defeating Djokovic 6-4, 6-3 in the second round of the Rolex Shanghai Masters to record his first career victory over the 39-year-old Serb in nine career meetings.

Djokovic's exit is not just a loss — it is his earliest-ever departure from a tournament he has won four times. The four-time Shanghai champion entered the draw as a former champion looking to add another deep run. He left after a little more than an hour and a half against a Pole who was simply sharper on the day.

The Match

Hurkacz came out with his serve locked in from the first game. He finished the match with 13 aces against just 2 double faults and kept 70 percent of his first serves in — numbers that made him almost impossible to break. He saved both break points he faced over the course of the match.

The first set turned in the final game. Djokovic served at 4-5 and Hurkacz stepped in on the return at set point, firing a forehand return winner to claim the opening set in 53 minutes. The second set was more straightforward. Hurkacz broke in the fourth game and never surrendered the lead, serving out the match at 5-3 when Djokovic's return sailed long.

The atmosphere in the Shanghai arena was charged. Djokovic, who had won the Beijing title on Monday — his 102nd career ATP title — showed the visible fatigue that comes with deep runs at back-to-back tournaments. The Beijing week had demanded everything: five matches, including an extraordinary semifinal circumstance involving Daniil Medvedev's disqualification, before a final that ended in de Minaur's retirement. All of it in less than a week.

A First for Hurkacz

Hurkacz was gracious about the moment. "It's a special night," he said afterward. That is the measured way to describe what is, for the 29-year-old Pole, a genuine career milestone. Djokovic had been one of those opponents Hurkacz simply hadn't been able to figure out — his previous 0-8 record against the Serb made this one of the more one-sided ongoing rivalries among the game's current top players.

The fact that it came in Shanghai adds another dimension. This is a court Hurkacz knows well; he won the Shanghai title in 2023, beating Andrey Rublev in the final. He had to skip the 2024 defense through injury. Returning here for 2026 with a significant win over the game's all-time champion in the second round is precisely the kind of statement that can shift how a season is remembered.

There is also the small matter of the Race to Turin. Hurkacz entered Shanghai in the crowded bubble of spots three through eight for the year-end championship. Every win in Shanghai now carries direct value. A Djokovic scalp in R2 is the kind of result that reminds the tennis world a player is here to do more than advance one round at a time.

What's Next

Hurkacz will next face either Yi Zhou or Lorenzo Musetti in the third round. With Djokovic and Jannik Sinner — who withdrew from Shanghai entirely due to injury — already absent from the draw, the path through the bracket looks considerably different than it did at the start of the week.

For Djokovic, the early exit means a quick pivot. The calendar still holds Vienna and Paris-Bercy before the ATP Finals in Turin — provided his health cooperates after what has been a demanding stretch. His Beijing title was a triumph. Shanghai was something else. The ATP calendar rarely offers time to process either outcome before the next one arrives.
