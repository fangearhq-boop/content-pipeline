Zverev Advances at Shanghai Despite Ankle Scare, Year-End No. 1 Race Now His to Lose

By Marcus Cole | October 9, 2026

Shanghai, China

Alexander Zverev moved into the third round of the Rolex Shanghai Masters on Friday, but the scoreline — 6-4, 7-6(4) over Chinese wildcard Wu Yibing — was not the headline that tennis watchers carried out of Qi Zhong Stadium.

In the second set, Zverev landed awkwardly on a dive and grabbed his left ankle. Play halted. Zverev walked off, received attention, returned to the court, and eventually fought his way through a tiebreak he won when Wu fired long on match point. He advanced. But the ankle question followed him off the court.

The concern is real, because the stakes are real. Zverev is chasing the Year-End No. 1 ranking — a title he has never held and one that would make him the first German in the Open Era to reach the top of the men's game at season's end. With Jannik Sinner absent for the entire season due to injury, and with Novak Djokovic eliminated from Shanghai just hours earlier by Hubert Hurkacz, Zverev's path has never been clearer. He needs to stay healthy enough to walk it.

The Match

Wu Yibing, the 113th-ranked Chinese wildcard, gave the home crowd reasons to cheer throughout a competitive second set. Zverev controlled the first set by the numbers, breaking to lead and closing it out 6-4. The second set was tighter. Wu pushed Zverev into a tiebreak, and even after the ankle incident, the German kept competing.

In the tiebreak, Zverev converted on his second match point when Wu made an unforced error. The serve stats held up — Zverev is among the tour's most reliable servers and that reliability was part of how he managed to win while managing the ankle.

Afterward, Zverev's words about the ankle were cautious. He acknowledged the discomfort but declined to characterize the severity. That is the appropriate response for a player who knows another inflammatory news cycle helps no one heading into a match this week.

The Race Picture

Entering Shanghai, Zverev held approximately 8,740 Race to Turin points against Sinner's 7,950. With Sinner confirmed absent for the full tournament — his absence means he will lose roughly 3,550 of those points as he fails to defend his Shanghai points from last year — the gap between first and second widens with each passing day.

The math now strongly favors Zverev regardless of what happens in Shanghai. Even an early exit by Zverev would leave him ahead of Sinner by more than 4,500 points after this week. The question of who finishes No. 1 is functionally settled. The question of whether Zverev can go on a deep Shanghai run and add to his already impressive 2026 body of work is the live one.

The bubble fight for spots three through eight at the ATP Finals in Turin remains furiously competitive. Ben Shelton, Carlos Alcaraz, Daniil Medvedev, Arthur Fils, Alex de Minaur, Jakub Menšík, and Hurkacz are all in play. With de Minaur's health uncertain after his Beijing final retirement and with Djokovic now out of Shanghai, the Race picture for spots beyond the top two continues to evolve by the hour.

What's Next

Zverev will next face an opponent to be determined from the third-round bracket. If the ankle allows him to play his best tennis, the potential remains for a very deep Shanghai run. No Sinner. Djokovic gone. Alcaraz and Medvedev still in the draw, but both entering in the third round after byes — meaning they have no match sharpness yet.

The ankle will be monitored through the weekend. Any setback would reshape the Shanghai story entirely, from a coronation-in-progress into something more unpredictable. That is exactly the uncertainty no team inside the Zverev camp wants to carry into a Friday night in Shanghai.
