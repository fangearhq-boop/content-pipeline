### STORY 1: Winston-Salem Final — Fery vs. Buse

**Long-Form Post (10:30 AM ET)**

It's a final nobody saw coming — and that's exactly what makes it so good.

Arthur Fery and Ignacio Buse will play for the Winston-Salem Open title today at 4 PM ET, completing a week that featured two of tennis' most exciting young players at their breakout best.

Fery, the 24-year-old from Britain who turned heads worldwide with his Wimbledon semifinal run, advanced to his first-ever ATP Tour final after James Duckworth was forced to retire injured during their semifinal. Buse, representing Peru, earned his spot the hard way — defeating Benjamin Bonzi 6-2, 4-6, 6-1 in a match that showcased his growing comfort on hard courts. Buse's appearance marks the first time a Peruvian player has reached an ATP final in 22 years.

Neither player has won an ATP title. One of them will end today with their name on a trophy, and the other will carry the experience into tomorrow's US Open main draw knowing they belong at tennis' highest level.

### STORY 3: Rybakina Fitness Update

**Long-Form Post (12:30 PM ET)**

Elena Rybakina hasn't been on a tennis court since she retired from her Cincinnati quarterfinal eight days ago — and the US Open main draw begins tomorrow.

The world No. 2 and No. 2 seed in Flushing twisted her left ankle while trailing Iga Swiatek 1-4 in what had seemed like a routine quarterfinal. She left the court and hasn't returned since. "I haven't been able to practice yet," Rybakina told reporters this week. "I want to make sure that everything is okay. We will try it in the next few days."

The 2022 Wimbledon champion has been through MRI scans and daily treatment. The prognosis isn't dire — she says recovery is going well — but the timeline is tight. Players need weeks, not days, to prep mentally and physically for a Grand Slam. Rybakina is trying to compress that into hours.

If she withdraws, the draw reshuffles and a qualifier moves into the bottom half of the bracket. But Rybakina hasn't withdrawn. She's being cautious, and that itself is encouraging.

We'll know more by Sunday morning.

### STORY 5: Eala US Open Debut

**Long-Form Post (10:30 AM ET)**

Alex Eala has made history before — and tomorrow, she makes it again.

The 22-year-old from the Philippines enters the 2026 US Open as the No. 17 seed, the highest Grand Slam seeding in Philippine tennis history. When the main draw begins Sunday at Flushing Meadows, Eala will walk onto Arthur Ashe Stadium or one of the show courts not as a qualifier, not as a lucky loser, but as a seeded player with expectations.

She's earned it. Eala has gone 22-9 on hard courts in 2026, reached the DC Open final (a WTA 500 event), and defeated Jelena Svitolina, Naomi Osaka, and other top-20 players during her breakout summer. In Cincinnati, she beat Giulio Zeppieri and Irina-Camelia Begu before a tough loss to Amanda Anisimova.

She opens her US Open campaign against a qualifier in Round 1. On paper, it's a manageable draw. In practice, a Grand Slam debut as a seeded player is its own kind of pressure.

The Philippines has waited a long time for a moment like this. Eala's going to deliver it.
