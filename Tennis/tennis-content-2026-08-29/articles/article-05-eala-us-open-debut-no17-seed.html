Eala Makes US Open History as No. 17 Seed — Philippine Tennis' Biggest Stage Yet

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | August 29, 2026

Alex Eala has built her season one milestone at a time. On Sunday, she steps onto the court for the biggest one yet.

The 22-year-old from the Philippines enters the 2026 US Open as the No. 17 seed — the highest Grand Slam seeding ever for a Filipino player, in any sport's equivalent draw. When she walks onto her assigned court for Round 1, she will face a qualifier in what should be a favorable opening match. But the significance of the moment extends far beyond the scoreline.

Building a Summer

Eala's path to a seeded Grand Slam appearance began taking shape in early August. At the DC Open (WTA 500) in Washington, she ran through the draw without losing a set — defeating Zheng Qinwen in the first round, defending champion Leylah Fernandez in the second, world No. 10 Jelena Svitolina in the quarterfinals, and Naomi Osaka in the semifinals. In the final, she met No. 1 seed Jessica Pegula and fell 6-4, 6-3, but had already established herself as one of the hardest players to beat on hard courts in the summer of 2026.

Her 2026 hard-court record stands at 22-9 entering the US Open. In Cincinnati, she advanced through the first two rounds before falling to Amanda Anisimova. The consistency has been the story: not a single dominant tournament, but sustained competitiveness at the highest level of the game across an entire swing.

The No. 17 Seed

Seedings at Grand Slams are assigned based on computer rankings at the time of the draw. Eala's No. 17 ranking heading into the US Open draw ceremony reflects a year-long body of work that has placed her among the top-20 women's players in the world. It is not a ranking she has held briefly — it represents sustained performance across months of competition.

For the Philippines, the milestone carries real weight. Tennis in the country has grown significantly over the past decade, and Eala's development — initially supported by the Rafa Nadal Academy before she charted her own path — has given Philippine tennis its most visible international figure. Her social media following, a significant presence across Southeast Asia, grows with each match she wins at a major venue.

What the Draw Means

As the No. 17 seed, Eala cannot face any of the top 16 seeds until the third round. Her first-round opponent is a qualifier — a player who has fought through three qualifying rounds just to reach the main draw. The expectation is that Eala advances.

A Round 2 opponent would likely be another unseeded player. A deep run, into the second week, would require defeating players of increasing quality. But Eala has beaten top-10 players this summer, and she arrives at Flushing in form.

What's Next

Eala opens her 2026 US Open campaign on Sunday August 30 in Round 1. Order of play for Day 1 will be released Saturday evening. The women's singles final is scheduled for Saturday September 12. Full draws, schedules, and streaming options are available at usopen.org. Eala's matches on center courts will also be broadcast on ESPN.
