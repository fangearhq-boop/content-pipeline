Rybakina Still Off the Court — US Open No. 2 Seed's Fitness Unresolved

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | August 29, 2026

The US Open main draw begins tomorrow. Elena Rybakina still has not been on a tennis court.

The world No. 2 and No. 2 seed in Flushing Meadows sustained a left ankle injury during her Cincinnati Open quarterfinal on August 21, retiring from the match while trailing Iga Swiatek 1-4 in the first set. In the eight days since, the 2022 Wimbledon champion has undergone MRI scans, followed a daily treatment regimen, and declined to confirm whether she will play in the Grand Slam that begins Sunday.

"I haven't been able to practice yet," Rybakina told reporters earlier this week. "I want to make sure that everything is okay. We will try it in the next few days."

The Timeline

The injury occurred on August 21 when Rybakina's left ankle buckled during a Cincinnati quarterfinal that had appeared to be proceeding normally. The discomfort had reportedly been building over several matches, with the accumulated strain finally giving way at a critical moment. Rybakina confirmed that at its worst, "it was painful to walk."

By the end of last week, she had completed an MRI and begun intensive conservative treatment. Her team indicated that the injury was not structurally severe — no ligament tears were reported — but that she needed sufficient recovery time before returning to court. That window is now essentially closed, with the main draw opening in fewer than 24 hours.

In a further update on August 26, Rybakina said recovery "has been going well" but reiterated she had still not returned to practice. She added: "I don't want to rush. I want to be completely okay." Her latest available statements do not constitute a withdrawal or a confirmed entry.

What Withdrawal Would Mean

If Rybakina withdraws, the No. 2 seeding position effectively shifts the bracket, and a lucky loser or alternate enters the draw. Rybakina's projected quarterfinal match — possibly against Iga Swiatek, the No. 8 seed who defeated her in Cincinnati and who has been in strong form — would be affected.

For the tournament itself, losing the No. 2 seed the day before the draw begins would be a significant storyline in an already eventful lead-up period. The women's draw without Rybakina becomes more open for players in the bottom half of the bracket.

Historical Context

Rybakina has been one of the most consistent players in the WTA field over the past two seasons. She won the 2022 Wimbledon title, reached the 2023 Australian Open final, and has established herself as a genuine Grand Slam contender. A US Open appearance carries weight in her calendar regardless of form.

She entered Cincinnati in strong shape — defeating multiple opponents before the ankle issue forced her hand. The injury is a contrast to the sustained consistency she had shown since the hard-court season began.

What's Next

Player withdrawal deadlines for the US Open main draw are typically finalized in the hours before play begins. Rybakina's situation will become clearer Saturday evening or early Sunday morning. If she plays, she will face a difficult path given limited practice. If she withdraws, her next expected return to the tour would likely be the Asian hard-court swing in September. The US Open main draw begins Sunday August 30 at the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center.
