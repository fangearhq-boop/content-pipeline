Roger Federer Inducted into Tennis Hall of Fame — Class of 2026

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | August 29, 2026

Tonight, a legacy that needed no formal recognition finally receives it.

Roger Federer — 20-time Grand Slam champion, five-time US Open champion, and by any measure one of the greatest athletes in the history of tennis — is being inducted into the International Tennis Hall of Fame as part of the Class of 2026. The ceremony takes place tonight at 6:30 PM ET in Newport, Rhode Island, and will be broadcast exclusively on the Tennis Channel and via the Tennis Channel app. The event is hosted by Tom Rinaldi of FOX Sports, with approximately 30 Hall of Famers in attendance.

Class of 2026

Federer is inducted in the Player Category. Joining him in the Class of 2026 is Mary Carillo, the legendary broadcaster and commentator whose decades of tennis coverage — from CBS to HBO to NBC — helped shape how millions of Americans came to understand and love the game. Carillo is inducted in the Contributor Category.

The pairing reflects something fitting about tennis' reach: a Swiss player who won everything there was to win, and an American voice who helped tell his story and the stories of everyone who came before and after.

A Career Worth Enshrining

Federer retired from professional tennis in September 2022, closing his competitive career at the Laver Cup in London. His record speaks without embellishment: 20 Grand Slam titles, 310 weeks ranked world No. 1, eight Wimbledon titles, five US Open crowns (2004-2008 consecutive), six Australian Open titles, and one Roland Garros.

Beyond the statistics, Federer spent two decades playing tennis in a way that made the game feel effortless. The one-handed backhand, the serve-and-volley approach, the movement across all surfaces — he produced points that replayed across a generation's collective memory. The only debate was the degree to which he was unmatched.

His final competitive appearances at the 2022 Laver Cup drew some of the most emotional scenes in the sport's modern era, including a tearful retirement speech alongside his long-time rival Rafael Nadal that made global news.

The Timing

The induction ceremony falls one day before the US Open main draw begins on August 30. Federer won the US Open five straight times from 2004 to 2008 — still the longest men's singles winning streak in the tournament's Open Era. The timing connects his legacy directly to the event that once defined his calendar year.

The International Tennis Hall of Fame Celebrity Pro Classic, a doubles exhibition featuring Federer and other tennis luminaries, took place at the Newport facility on Friday August 28. Tonight's formal induction is the culmination of a weekend-long celebration.

What's Next

The full induction ceremony broadcasts live tonight on Tennis Channel at 6:30 PM ET. The Hall of Fame in Newport, Rhode Island is open to visitors year-round. Following the ceremony weekend, the tennis world shifts focus to the US Open main draw beginning Sunday August 30 — where Federer's legacy will once again be present in the fabric of Arthur Ashe Stadium.
