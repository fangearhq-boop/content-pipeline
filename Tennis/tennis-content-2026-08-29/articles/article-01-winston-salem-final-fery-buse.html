Fery and Buse Clash for Winston-Salem Title in All-Breakthrough Final

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | August 29, 2026

When Arthur Fery and Ignacio Buse walk onto the court in Winston-Salem, North Carolina this afternoon, neither will have won an ATP Tour title. By the time the last ball is struck, one of them will.

The 2026 Winston-Salem Open final — scheduled for 4 PM ET today, Saturday August 29, with broadcast on Tennis TV and ESPN — is the kind of match that defines young careers. It also serves as the last competitive warm-up before the US Open main draw begins Sunday in Flushing Meadows, giving both players a chance to enter the year's final Grand Slam with a title and a surge of momentum behind them.

Fery's Journey to His First Final

Arthur Fery, 24, is no stranger to high-stakes tennis. The British player announced himself to the world this summer with a remarkable run to the Wimbledon semifinal — a result that rocketed him into the global conversation and made him one of the most-watched young players on tour.

Fery advanced to the Winston-Salem final when his semifinal opponent, Australian veteran James Duckworth, was forced to retire through injury. It was not the most glamorous path to a final, but Fery had earned his position in the tournament through consistent performances across the week, deploying a serve-and-volley game that has separated him from the baseline-heavy norm of the modern tour.

This is Fery's first career ATP Tour final. A title today would make him one of the fastest-rising stories in British tennis since Andy Murray's early breakthroughs two decades ago.

Buse Makes Peruvian History

Ignacio Buse arrived at this final through a more direct route. The Peruvian, ranked World No. 36, defeated French veteran Benjamin Bonzi 6-2, 4-6, 6-1 in Friday's semifinal — a result that showed his ability to refocus after dropping the second set and close with authority.

Buse's presence in the final carries weight beyond the scoreline. He is the first Peruvian player to reach an ATP Tour final since Luis Horna in 2004 — a gap of 22 years. In a country where tennis occupies a devoted but smaller corner of the sporting landscape, Buse's run has generated significant attention at home.

Raised in Peru and trained extensively in Spain, Buse has steadily built his game on clay and has invested heavily in improving on hard courts. Winston-Salem represents a milestone in that development.

What to Watch

The matchup pits two contrasting styles against each other. Fery's net-attack game and aggressive serve can suffocate opponents in short bursts, while Buse prefers to extend rallies and win with consistency from the baseline. On a fast hard court, Fery's serve should be a weapon — but Buse's ability to reset after dropping a set, as he showed against Bonzi, cannot be underestimated.

Both players are also thinking beyond today. The US Open first round begins Sunday. Whoever wins this title will carry confidence into New York; whoever loses must process a near-miss and shift focus within 24 hours.

What's Next

Following the Winston-Salem final, both Fery and Buse move directly to the US Open main draw beginning Sunday August 30 at the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center in Flushing Meadows, Queens. The men's singles final is scheduled for Sunday, September 13. With $108 million in total prize money on offer — the largest in tennis history — the stakes only grow from here.
