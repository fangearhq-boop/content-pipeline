US Open 2026: Main Draw Starts Sunday — Everything You Need to Know

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | August 29, 2026

Fan Week ends today. The US Open begins tomorrow.

After seven days of qualifying matches, player practices, and festivities at the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center in Flushing Meadows, Queens, the 2026 US Open main draw officially kicks off on Sunday August 30. The tournament runs through September 13, with the women's singles final scheduled for Saturday September 12 and the men's final on Sunday September 13.

With a record $108 million in total prize money — a 21 percent increase from 2025, and the largest financial commitment in the history of the sport — the stakes this year match the occasion.

Men's Draw: The Alcaraz Question

Alexander Zverev enters as the No. 1 seed after Jannik Sinner's withdrawal due to right knee tendon inflammation. Zverev has been the most consistent player on the ATP Tour this season, accumulating 46 tour-level wins by the time he arrives in New York, and he begins his campaign as the man to beat.

But the storyline everyone is watching belongs to Carlos Alcaraz. The defending champion — and No. 2 seed — has not played a competitive match since April 14 in Barcelona. A right wrist injury (tenosynovitis) cost him Roland Garros, Wimbledon, and the entire North American hard-court swing. Alcaraz skipped Winston-Salem and arrives in Flushing with limited match practice, an uncertain physical condition, and the weight of being the title defender. If he reaches his peak form, he is the most dangerous player in the draw. If the rust lingers, any player in the top half could capitalize.

Novak Djokovic, 39, is the third storyline to watch in the men's draw. The Serbian legend is chasing his 25th Grand Slam title, and at an age where most players have retired, he continues to arrive as a genuine contender.

Women's Draw: Sabalenka's Three-Peat Bid

Aryna Sabalenka is the No. 1 seed and the clear favorite — at least on paper. The Belarusian is bidding for her third consecutive US Open title, which would make her the first woman to accomplish that feat since Serena Williams won from 2012 to 2014. Sabalenka opens against Camila Osorio, with a projected path that could include Barbora Krejcikova in Round 3, Diana Shnaider in Round 4, and Jessica Pegula in the semifinals.

The challenge is that Sabalenka's recent form has been inconsistent. Since her Miami title in March, she has gone 17-7 and lost six of her ten three-set matches. A first-round exit in Cincinnati to Sara Bejlek — ranked outside the top 30 — served as a reminder that form at the top of the game can shift quickly.

The No. 2 seed, Elena Rybakina, remains a question mark heading into Sunday. The 2022 Wimbledon champion has not practiced since retiring from her Cincinnati quarterfinal eight days ago with a left ankle injury. She has declined to confirm her participation.

Coco Gauff, the 2025 US Open champion, is seeded No. 4 and will be looking to defend her title. Jessica Pegula (No. 3) had a strong summer after winning the DC Open in August.

Names to Watch

Alex Eala of the Philippines enters as the No. 17 seed — the highest Grand Slam seeding ever for a Filipino player — and opens against a qualifier. Arthur Fils, the French player who won the Cincinnati title last week, enters Flushing with momentum and draws Stefanos Tsitsipas in his opening round. Coco Gauff opens her defense as the defending champion.

What's Next

The 2026 US Open main draw begins Sunday August 30. Order of play for the opening round will be released Saturday evening. The women's singles final is September 12; men's singles final is September 13. Full schedule and streaming details are available at usopen.org.
