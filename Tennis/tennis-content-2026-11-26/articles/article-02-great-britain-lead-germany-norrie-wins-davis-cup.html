Davis Cup Finals: Norrie Wins Opening Rubber as Great Britain Lead Germany 1-0

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | November 26, 2026

Great Britain made the perfect start to their 2026 Davis Cup quarterfinal against Germany in Bologna on Thursday, with Cameron Norrie defeating Jan-Lennard Struff 6-3, 3-6, 6-2 to give the Brits a 1-0 lead in the tie.

Norrie was composed and efficient across the three sets, winning the first with controlled baseline play before Struff fought back to take the second. The British No. 1 — who has been a consistent performer for Great Britain in Davis Cup over the years — reapplied pressure in the third set and won it convincingly to give his country the early advantage.

The focus now turns to Rubber 2, where 20-year-old Arthur Fery faces world No. 1 Alexander Zverev — the most anticipated individual match of Great Britain's campaign.

Fery vs Zverev: The Defining Match

This is the moment that will define Great Britain's quarter-final. Arthur Fery, a 20-year-old in his first Davis Cup Finals, finds himself on court against the best player in the world at this moment in time — Alexander Zverev, who ended 2026 as ATP year-end No. 1 after winning Roland Garros and the US Open.

The matchup has everything. Fery is young, attacking and unafraid of the moment. Zverev is experienced, proven in the biggest matches, and desperate to give Germany the momentum they need to level the tie and force a deciding doubles rubber.

Germany's squad includes Zverev alongside Jan-Lennard Struff, Tim Puetz and Kevin Krawietz — a solid team with both singles and doubles firepower. For Great Britain, the squad of Fery, Norrie, Henry Patten and Neal Skupski knows the doubles is their strength. Getting to it level at 1-1 would suit them.

Britain's First Finals Since 2023

Great Britain qualified for the Finals by beating Ecuador 3-0, earning their first Finals appearance since 2023. For a nation with a proud Davis Cup history, returning to Bologna with a genuine chance of a semifinal is cause for optimism.

If Norrie's win holds and Fery can produce something special against Zverev — or if the tie goes to doubles and Patten and Skupski, one of the best doubles partnerships in the world, can deliver — Great Britain could find themselves in Saturday's semifinal for the first time in three years.

The stakes couldn't be higher. Fery vs Zverev is live now.

What's Next

The result of the Great Britain vs Germany quarterfinal will determine Saturday's semifinal opponent for either Spain or Austria. If Great Britain advance, they face the winner of that afternoon tie. The Davis Cup semifinals are scheduled for Friday, Nov. 27 (Italy vs Czech Republic) and Saturday, Nov. 28 (the second tie), with the final on Sunday, Nov. 29. Follow live scores at daviscup.com.
