Sinner Confident of Australian Open Return After Tough Recovery: 'Won't Rush It'

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | November 26, 2026

Jannik Sinner has given tennis fans what they have been waiting to hear: he expects to be ready for the 2027 Australian Open — but he will not rush his comeback at the expense of his long-term health.

Speaking to media on Nov. 10, the Italian former world No. 1 expressed cautious optimism about his return, saying he is positive about being fit in time for the season's first Grand Slam but that rushing the process is not something he is willing to do. "If you rush things, it can lead to more serious problems," he said.

Sinner's 2026 season came to a premature end after one of its finest moments. Having won his fifth Grand Slam title at Wimbledon in July — a straight-set victory that added more silverware to an already remarkable year — a knee injury forced his US Open withdrawal in August. His season ended in October, and the recovery process has been complicated further by tonsil surgery performed earlier this month.

Rebuilding After Surgery

The tonsil operation added another setback to what was already a challenging rehabilitation. Sinner disclosed that he lost significant weight and muscle mass following the procedure, and that his medical team has advised him to rebuild his physical condition before returning to competitive tennis.

"Not even close to returning," was his assessment at the time of the surgery disclosure — but the Nov. 10 update represented a more optimistic tone. He is now actively working on rebuilding and has expressed belief that the January timeline is achievable.

The 2027 Australian Open begins Jan. 17 in Melbourne. That gives Sinner roughly seven weeks from now — not a lot of time, but potentially enough for a player of his physical gifts and professional dedication if the recovery continues on its current trajectory.

Year-End Rankings and Context

With Sinner absent since August, Alexander Zverev clinched the year-end ATP No. 1 ranking — the first German player in ATP history to hold the top spot at year's end. Sinner's absence from the year-end rankings battle was stark: in a full season, he would have been among the leading contenders.

The tennis world has noticed the gap. No player has been able to fully fill the void left by Sinner at the top of the sport. His return to the Australian Open — where he has been a perennial contender — would immediately reshape the narrative of the 2027 season's opening slam.

What's Next

Sinner's next confirmed appearance will likely be a limited pre-season schedule designed to ease him back into match fitness. His team has indicated he plans to play few tournaments before the Australian Open to avoid overloading a body still in recovery. The AO field, already featuring Alcaraz, Zverev and others in strong form, would only get more compelling if Sinner makes it back. His return is not guaranteed — but for the first time since his injury, the signs are genuinely encouraging.
