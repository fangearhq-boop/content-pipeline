Spain vs Austria Davis Cup QF: Alcaraz Expected to Rest as Afternoon Tie Approaches

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | November 26, 2026

The final quarterfinal of the 2026 Davis Cup Finals gets underway Thursday afternoon in Bologna — and it comes with one of the most intriguing storylines of the entire tournament. Carlos Alcaraz, Spain's world No. 4 and reigning ATP Finals champion, is expected to sit out singles against Austria.

Reports suggest Spain may rest Alcaraz — whose left thigh has been a persistent concern throughout the year-end swing — with the possibility that he features in doubles if the tie needs a third rubber. It is a calculated risk from the Spanish camp, banking on the depth of their singles options to get through without their headline act.

That means the singles duties fall to Rafael Jodar, who is set to open the tie against Austria's No. 1 Jurij Rodionov.

Austria's Historic Opportunity

Austria are in uncharted territory at the Davis Cup Finals. Making their first-ever appearance at the Final 8, they have already written history just by being here — and the possibility of facing a Spain side without Alcaraz in the singles lineup has only added to the belief that an upset could be on the cards.

Jurij Rodionov, the Austrian No. 1, has had a quietly impressive year. While he may not have the name recognition of his opponent, he will carry the full weight of Austrian expectation at the SuperTennis Arena on Thursday afternoon.

Austria's doubles pairing will also be pivotal if the tie goes the distance. Spain are well-equipped in that department — particularly if Alcaraz is added to the mix — but nothing is guaranteed on a Davis Cup court.

Spain's Davis Cup Pedigree

Spain are one of the most decorated nations in Davis Cup history, with five titles, and they arrive in Bologna with serious intentions of adding to that tally. Even without Alcaraz carrying the singles burden, Spain have the experience and collective quality to manage a tie like this.

What makes the matchup fascinating is the contrast in experience. Spain know how to win here. Austria are discovering what this stage feels like for the first time. At the Davis Cup Finals, that gap can matter — but it can also inspire the underdog.

What's Next

The winner of Spain vs Austria faces the Great Britain vs Germany winner in Saturday's semifinal. The final is on Sunday, Nov. 29. With Italy already through on the other side of the bracket, the picture of a potential Spain or Great Britain vs Italy final is beginning to take shape. Live updates are available at daviscup.com.
