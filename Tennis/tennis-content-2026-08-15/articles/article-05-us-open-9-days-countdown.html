9 Days to the US Open: Sinner and Alcaraz Fitness Crisis Reshapes Who Can Win Flushing Meadows

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap

August 15, 2026 — Cincinnati, Ohio

The US Open begins August 24. Nine days from today. And right now, the two players who have done more than anyone to define the men's game over the past two seasons are watching the Cincinnati Open from a distance, not playing in it.

Jannik Sinner, the reigning Wimbledon champion, withdrew from Cincinnati with a knee injury. Carlos Alcaraz, the defending US Open champion, withdrew with the wrist tenosynovitis that has kept him away from competitive tennis since April. He also missed Roland Garros and Wimbledon. His participation at Flushing Meadows is described diplomatically as uncertain — which is a polite way of saying the ATP's defending US Open champion may not be there to defend.

The absence of two top-five players from the field changes everything about how this week reads as US Open preparation.

Who Steps Into the Vacuum

Alexander Zverev has spent 2026 making the case that he is the best player in the world when he is healthy and focused. He won the Roland Garros title this year. He enters Cincinnati as the No. 1 seed. His half of the draw includes Novak Djokovic — a three-time Cincinnati champion with a 10-match win streak at this tournament — which means even before he gets to the final, Zverev may need to go through one of the most accomplished hard-court players of the Open Era.

Ben Shelton arrives in Cincinnati off the best week of his career. He defended his Canadian Open title in Montreal last week — the first man since Rafael Nadal in 2018-19 to win back-to-back at the Canadian Masters. That kind of momentum, on the same hard-court surface as the US Open, makes him one of the most dangerous players in the draw when the tournament reaches the second week.

And then there is Djokovic. His return to Cincinnati after a two-year absence, in a draw thinned out by injury, represents exactly the kind of opportunity that veteran champions are designed to capitalise on. He has won 10 consecutive matches at this tournament. If that streak continues deep into this week, he arrives at Flushing Meadows as something that rarely exists in modern tennis: a legitimate Djokovic who is also the favourite.

The Women's Picture

On the WTA side, Iga Swiatek enters as the player who appears to be peaking at exactly the right time. She won the Toronto WTA 1000 last week — her 12th career WTA 1000 title and her first of 2026, ending a 10-month title drought. She is the defending Cincinnati champion. She also beat Elena Rybakina in the Toronto final 6-2, 6-3, which means the world No. 2 has now felt what Swiatek looks like when she is confident and playing at that level.

Aryna Sabalenka is the top seed in Cincinnati and the current world No. 1. She has not won a Grand Slam title at Flushing Meadows, but her hard-court game translates well to the US Open conditions. A deep Cincinnati run would build on what has been a strong season.

Frances Tiafoe — who had right-hand cyst surgery around August 8 — joins the men's injury watch list alongside Alcaraz and Sinner. His availability for the US Open also remains in question.

What's Next

Cincinnati ends August 23. The US Open begins August 24. There is no recovery window — players who go deep here will step directly onto the New York courts with minimal rest.

That physical reality shapes how each player manages their week in Ohio. Some will conserve energy. Some will play to build confidence. For those who are fit and playing well, these nine days are the most important stretch of the hard-court season.

The Alcaraz and Sinner situations will continue to develop — if either confirms withdrawal from Flushing Meadows before the end of this week, the seedings and the narratives shift again. For now, the sport watches Cincinnati and waits for news from doctors' offices and practice sessions.

Nine days. The biggest hard-court Grand Slam of the year is nine days away — and the picture of who is fit enough to win it has never looked more open.
