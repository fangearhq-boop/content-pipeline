Djokovic Makes His Cincinnati Return — Three Titles, a 10-Match Streak, and a Wide-Open Draw

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap

August 15, 2026 — Cincinnati, Ohio

Novak Djokovic has not played at the Cincinnati Open in two years. On Saturday, he returned to the Lindner Family Tennis Center as something that does not arrive often in professional tennis: a legitimate favourite in a wide-open draw.

Djokovic, the third seed, faced Thiago Agustin Tirante of Argentina in his opening match of the tournament — a second-round assignment that, on paper, pits the experience of a three-time Cincinnati champion against an Argentine who arrived in Ohio off the back of one of Montreal's bigger upsets. Tirante beat Taylor Fritz, fresh off his Washington title, in the second round of the Canadian Open last week.

Context matters when sizing up a first-round opponent. But context matters even more when assessing Djokovic's overall position in Cincinnati this week.

The Numbers Behind the Return

The last time Djokovic played Cincinnati, he left with a win streak intact. That streak now stands at 10 consecutive matches won at this tournament. He is, by any measure, the most successful active player at the Lindner Family Tennis Center — and his three titles here (the most recent coming in 2015) remain a high-water mark that no current player has matched at this event.

He returns this year as the No. 3 seed — a ranking that reflects his recent form while acknowledging the gap between himself and Alexander Zverev, who holds the No. 1 position and the 2026 Roland Garros title. Djokovic is in the same half of the draw as Zverev, setting up what could be the most compelling semifinal of the summer: a matchup between two of the sport's most decorated hard-court performers, nine days before the US Open begins.

The Absences That Shape This Week

Djokovic's path to the title has been significantly cleared by injuries. Jannik Sinner — the reigning Wimbledon champion — withdrew from Cincinnati with a knee problem. Carlos Alcaraz, the defending Cincinnati and US Open champion, withdrew citing the wrist tenosynovitis that has kept him off tour since April. Neither of those withdrawals was Djokovic's doing, but both make the trophy considerably more accessible.

In any given year, a Masters 1000 field featuring Sinner and Alcaraz in form is effectively a three-way conversation for the title. Without both of them, the conversation narrows — and Djokovic and Zverev sit at the centre of it.

What's Next

Djokovic's path through the draw will tell us a great deal about what to expect from him at the US Open, which begins August 24. Cincinnati serves as the final competitive preparation before Flushing Meadows — a chance to sharpen groundstrokes, test serve mechanics, and rebuild match sharpness after whatever time off Djokovic has had.

If the streak holds and the three-time champion continues through the draw, a Djokovic-Zverev semifinal looms as the defining moment of the tournament. For tennis fans who have spent the summer watching the sport's next generation make noise, this Cincinnati week offers something different: the return of a player who has won more than anyone, on a court that has treated him very well indeed.
