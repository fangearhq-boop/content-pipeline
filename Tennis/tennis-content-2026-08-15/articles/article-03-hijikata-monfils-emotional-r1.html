Hijikata Rallies Past Monfils 2-6, 7-6, 6-3 in What May Be Monfils' Final Cincinnati Match

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap

August 13, 2026 — Cincinnati, Ohio

The Cincinnati crowd gave Gaël Monfils everything it had. And for one set, Monfils gave it right back.

The French veteran — who has announced he will retire at the end of the 2026 season — took the first set against Australian Rinky Hijikata with the kind of play that has made him one of the most beloved performers in the sport for nearly two decades. He moved. He dove. He celebrated. He turned the Lindner Family Tennis Center into, briefly, something that felt like a Monfils greatest-hits show.

Then Hijikata — 25 years old, from Australia, a player who graduated through the US college system at North Carolina — settled in. He won the second set in a tiebreak, claimed the third 6-3, and walked away with a 2-6, 7-6, 6-3 result in 2 hours and 31 minutes.

What Makes This Result Land Differently

First-round losses are routine at Masters 1000 events. What is not routine is the context Monfils carries into every match this season.

He is retiring at year's end. At 39, he is playing out the final chapter of a career that has included 12 ATP titles and appearances at all four Grand Slams. He has been a presence on tour for so long that some of the players he is now losing to were teenagers when he was winning titles.

Cincinnati has been one of the stops where Monfils built his reputation for crowd interaction and on-court theatrics. Whether this was his final appearance here depends on what else the schedule holds before October — but given the late-summer calendar, it seems likely that Thursday was the last time the Ohio faithful will see him live.

Hijikata's Hard-Court Breakthrough

For Hijikata, the moment carries different weight. This was, by multiple accounts, his first ATP win on hard courts. He had shown promise on the tour previously, but converting that promise against a player of Monfils' profile — even in the final stages of his career — is a meaningful step.

He played the match without blinking at the crowd noise or the emotional atmosphere. When Monfils took the first set and the Cincinnati faithful roared, Hijikata went back to his chair, reset, and came out a different player in the second.

His reward is a second-round match against Italian Luciano Darderi — another young, hard-court threat who beat Hijikata-equivalent opponents in the qualifying rounds. Another difficult test on a surface where Hijikata is now, at least, on the board.

What's Next

For Monfils, the focus turns to what remains of the season. The US Open begins August 24, and whatever decisions he makes about participation in New York will carry the same emotional weight that his Cincinnati appearance did. For those who have followed his career across the years, every remaining match is worth watching.

For Hijikata, Cincinnati continues — and with it, the chance to build on the first hard-court win that will now be permanently attached to his career record.
