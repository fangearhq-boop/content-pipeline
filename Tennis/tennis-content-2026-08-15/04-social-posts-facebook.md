**Long-Form Post** *(Posting Window: 8:00 AM ET)*

Two years is a long time in tennis.

The last time Novak Djokovic played at the Cincinnati Open, Alexander Zverev was a different player, Carlos Alcaraz was still finding his hard-court identity, and Jannik Sinner had yet to hit his peak.

Now — Alcaraz is out with a wrist injury. Sinner is out with a bad knee. And Djokovic is back in Cincinnati, chasing a fourth title at one of the stops on tour that has been most kind to him.

He enters with a 10-match win streak at this tournament. He enters as the third seed. And today, he plays Thiago Agustin Tirante — the Argentine who upset Taylor Fritz in Montreal last week — in his opening match at the Lindner Family Tennis Center.

If Djokovic can navigate his half of the draw, he'd likely face No. 1 seed Alexander Zverev in the semifinals — a match that has Grand Slam final energy written all over it.

With the US Open just nine days away, everything happening in Cincinnati carries extra weight. The king of Cincy is back — and the path in front of him is as open as it's been in years.

### STORY 3: Hijikata def. Monfils

**Long-Form Post** *(Posting Window: 9:00 PM ET)*

There's a reason Cincinnati has always loved Gaël Monfils.

He is the kind of player who makes tennis feel like a spectacle — diving for balls others wouldn't chase, celebrating with the crowd, turning an afternoon in Ohio into something that doesn't quite feel like tennis.

Thursday's match against Rinky Hijikata was no different. Monfils won the first set. The crowd was with him. At 39 years old, playing what is expected to be his final season on tour before retirement, he gave them what they came for.

But Hijikata — 25, Australian, and still building his reputation as a hard-court fighter — had enough to come back. 2-6, 7-6, 6-3 over 2 hours and 31 minutes. The result goes to the younger man.

What makes the result sting more is the context: Monfils announced he will retire at the end of the 2026 season. This was almost certainly his final match at Cincinnati.

Hijikata moves on to face Luciano Darderi in the second round, having claimed one of the most emotionally charged wins of his career.

As for Monfils — the story at Flushing Meadows, and at whatever other stops he plays before the season ends, will be worth following.

### STORY 5: US Open -9 Days

**Long-Form Post** *(Posting Window: 9:00 PM ET)*

Nine days. That's how long until the 2026 US Open begins at Flushing Meadows.

And right now, two of the biggest names in tennis are sitting in a medical room instead of a practice court.

Jannik Sinner withdrew from Cincinnati with a knee injury. He's the reigning Wimbledon champion — and whether he shows up at Flushing Meadows at full fitness is an open question that will define the men's draw.

Carlos Alcaraz hasn't played a competitive match since April. He withdrew from Cincinnati (as he did from Roland Garros and Wimbledon) with tenosynovitis in his wrist. He's the defending US Open champion — and his availability for New York is in serious doubt.

That opens the door wide for Alexander Zverev, Novak Djokovic, and Ben Shelton. Zverev is the top seed here, the Roland Garros 2026 champion, and the world No. 2. Djokovic, a 3-time Cincinnati champion, is back after a 2-year absence. Shelton just defended his Canadian Open title in Montreal — the first man since Nadal to win back-to-back in Canada.

On the women's side, Iga Swiatek is the frontrunner. She just won the Toronto WTA 1000 last week, ending a 10-month title drought. She's the defending Cincinnati champion. And with nine days to sharpen her game, she arrives at Flushing Meadows in the best form she's shown all year.

Cincinnati this week isn't just a Masters 1000. It's the final statement before the biggest Grand Slam of the North American swing.
