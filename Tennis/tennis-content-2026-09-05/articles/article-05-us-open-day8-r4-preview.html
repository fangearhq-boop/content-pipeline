US Open Round of 16 Preview: Alcaraz vs. Paul, Tiafoe vs. Medvedev, Shelton vs. Tsitsipas Lead Sunday

By Elena Voss | September 5, 2026

The 2026 US Open round of 16 arrives Sunday with three star-studded men's matches and continued women's action as the tournament's second week comes into focus at Flushing Meadows.

The round of 16 at the US Open is the moment the tournament changes. The casual fans from opening week are replaced by the deeply committed. The order of play gets shorter and more consequential. Every match from here on is a big match.

Sunday at Flushing Meadows is going to be a very good day of tennis.

Alcaraz vs. Paul (Men's)

The headline match on Arthur Ashe Stadium is the defending champion versus a player who had to claw his way through a five-set match just to earn the right to face him.

Carlos Alcaraz comes in with a .900 win percentage at Flushing Meadows — the best in the Open Era. He is 3-0 this tournament without dropping a set. He leads the head-to-head against Tommy Paul 6-2 and has not lost to him in their last five meetings.

Paul has New York in his corner. He survived Alexander Bublik in five sets on Saturday, showing the grit and resilience that the crowd here rewards. If there is a path to an upset, it runs through Paul's ability to keep the match close in the early sets and feed off the energy that Ashe provides when the underdog is hanging in.

On paper: Alcaraz. On the day: bring snacks and stay ready.

Tiafoe vs. Medvedev (Men's)

This matchup is a tennis fan's delight — two elite players whose games sit at opposite ends of the style spectrum. Frances Tiafoe is energy, aggression, and athleticism. Daniil Medvedev is consistency, control, and defensive precision that can make the best shots in tennis look insufficient.

Tiafoe beat Vacherot with authority on Saturday and is clearly playing well. Medvedev, who won his third-round match in straight sets, has the kind of baseline game that can neutralize Tiafoe's power and force him to make the errors. But Arthur Ashe at full noise favors Tiafoe in a way that almost no venue on Tour does. This could go five sets.

Shelton vs. Tsitsipas (Men's)

Ben Shelton finished his third-round match at 2 AM and is now set to face Stefanos Tsitsipas in the round of 16. Shelton's serve — which reached 147 mph on Saturday night — is one of the few weapons that can consistently win free points against anyone. Tsitsipas is experienced and versatile, but he has shown vulnerability against pure power servers at this tournament.

The US crowd will be firmly behind Shelton. If his first serve is on, this match could be surprisingly straightforward for the American eighth seed.

Women's Draw

Sabalenka, Swiatek, Gauff, and Rybakina have all been advancing on their respective sides of the women's draw. The round of 16 will see the full picture of the second week emerge — any of the four top seeds remaining would be in contention for a semifinal appearance. Rybakina's ankle, which caused concern earlier in the tournament, has not been a visible factor through her first three rounds.

How to Watch

Coverage begins on ESPN at 11 AM ET on Sunday, September 6. ABC picks up the afternoon session from 3 PM ET. Primetime coverage on ESPN starts at 7 PM ET. Spanish-language coverage is on ESPN Deportes throughout the day. The US Open app provides streaming access with a valid TV subscription.

What's Next

Sunday's round of 16 sets the quarterfinal bracket. The men's quarterfinals are scheduled for Tuesday, September 8, with the women's on Wednesday, September 9. The US Open women's final is Saturday, September 12, and the men's final is Sunday, September 13. A week of tennis remains — and based on the quality of play so far, it should be exceptional.
