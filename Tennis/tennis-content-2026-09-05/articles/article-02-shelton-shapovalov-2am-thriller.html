Shelton Outlasts Shapovalov in 2 AM US Open Thriller: 14 Aces, 147 MPH, R4 Secured

By Elena Voss | September 5, 2026

No. 8 seed Ben Shelton defeated Denis Shapovalov 7-6(3), 6-7(5), 6-2, 6-4 in a 3-hour, 22-minute battle at Arthur Ashe Stadium that finished at 2:08 AM — then advanced to face Stefanos Tsitsipas in the round of 16.

At some point past midnight, with Arthur Ashe Stadium still buzzing and the match locked in its fourth set, Ben Shelton unleashed a 147 mph serve down the T.

That is what US Open night sessions sometimes become. By the time Denis Shapovalov's return attempt fell short and the match was over, the clock read 2:08 AM. The match had lasted 3 hours and 22 minutes. Shelton had hit 14 aces. He had survived a set in which Shapovalov played brilliantly. And he had done all of this at Arthur Ashe Stadium, where the noise and the atmosphere are unlike anywhere else in tennis.

The final score: 7-6(3), 6-7(5), 6-2, 6-4. Shelton advances to the US Open round of 16.

A Match That Demanded Everything

Shelton won the first set in a tiebreak, taking it 7-3 with his serve dominating. But Shapovalov is a player who can produce remarkable tennis in bursts, and the second set was his — the Canadian won it 7-5 in the tiebreak, evening the contest and silencing the crowd for the first time all evening.

The third and fourth sets, however, belonged to Shelton entirely. After the match was leveled, the American eighth seed played with remarkable authority, winning 6-2 and 6-4 to close it out well past what any reasonable viewer expected to be bedtime.

The 14 aces were a defining feature of the night. Shelton's serve is one of the few genuine weapons in tennis that can simply end a point before it starts — and against Shapovalov, who can be inconsistent on return, it functioned exactly as designed. The 147 mph thunderbolt was the peak, but there were multiple serves in the 130s that were equally unreturnable.

R4: Shelton vs. Tsitsipas

Stefanos Tsitsipas defeated No. 18 seed Jiri Lehecka to advance to the round of 16, setting up a stylistic clash that is as interesting on paper as it will be on court.

Tsitsipas is a craftsman — a player who uses variety, spin, and court positioning to construct points methodically. Shelton is the opposite: he wants the point decided quickly and loudly, with a serve or a forehand that gives the opponent no time to think. Arthur Ashe at full volume will suit Shelton. But Tsitsipas has experience, and his ability to neutralize serving power by staying deep and absorbing pace makes him a genuine threat.

For American tennis fans, this is the dream US Open draw: Shelton on Ashe, deep in the tournament, with a chance to reach the quarterfinal.

What's Next

Ben Shelton faces Stefanos Tsitsipas in the US Open round of 16 on Sunday, September 6. The match order will be announced Saturday night. ESPN carries the full day of coverage from 11 AM ET, with primetime at 7 PM. For Shelton, a win over Tsitsipas would put him in his first US Open quarterfinal and confirm what his serve has been saying all week: he belongs in this conversation.
