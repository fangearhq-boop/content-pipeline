Wawrinka's Final Match: Tuesday Evening in Basel, Against Lehečká

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | October 27, 2026

Monday night at the Swiss Indoors belonged to tribute and memory. Tuesday night belongs to the match. Stan Wawrinka, 41, three-time Grand Slam champion, steps onto a Basel court Tuesday evening for the final professional match of his career, against eighth seed Jiří Lehečká. The result will be what it will be. The occasion is already settled.

Monday: The Tribute

The Swiss Indoors held a formal tribute ceremony Monday evening — a structured goodbye that Basel has been preparing for weeks. Among those who attended was Novak Djokovic, who had been personally asked to come. The gesture carried weight: Djokovic and Wawrinka were rivals through a decade of Grand Slam tennis, players whose careers overlapped at the highest level during the peak years of both. That Djokovic traveled to Basel, where he is also playing this week as a wildcard, to honor his contemporary said something about what Wawrinka's career means within the sport.

The ceremony covered the three Swiss Indoors titles — 2006, 2015, 2016 — the three Grand Slams, the Olympic gold with Federer in Beijing in 2008, and the career-high ranking of No. 3. It covered the backhand, which needs no introduction among tennis followers. And it covered the long second half of a career that most players do not survive intact, the years when Wawrinka kept competing after his major windows had passed because, by his own account, he still wanted to play tennis.

Tuesday: The Match

Jiří Lehečká is 24 years old and currently ranked No. 21. He reached the Miami Masters 1000 final in 2026 and, more recently, the final at Tokyo. He is the eighth seed in Basel and one of the faster-rising young players on tour. He and Wawrinka have never played each other on the ATP Tour — this is their first meeting, under circumstances neither could have imagined when Lehečká turned professional.

Wawrinka enters as a wildcard, ranked approximately No. 151. His 2026 season record against top-50 opponents was 1-11. By any competitive measure, the outcome Tuesday should favor Lehečká. But Wawrinka has built an entire career on performing in situations that forecasts said he shouldn't. The Australian Open final in 2014. The Roland Garros final in 2015, against Djokovic when Djokovic was the best player in the world. The US Open final in 2016, when he produced arguably the finest Grand Slam final performance of the modern era.

None of that guarantees anything Tuesday evening. It simply establishes that the man taking the court is not someone who has ever particularly respected probability.

What Basel Means

The Swiss Indoors was the site of Wawrinka's first ATP title in 2006, when he was 21 and the tournament was still heavily defined by Roger Federer's presence and dominance. Winning in Basel that year was a signal that something real was developing in Swiss tennis beyond the obvious. He won it again in 2015 and 2016 at the height of his powers, giving him three titles at one event spread across a decade — a record of consistency that reflects both his indoor aptitude and his attachment to the city.

Ending his career in Basel is not incidental. It is chosen. The crowd tonight will know it, and they will give him what Basel crowds have always given him — a reception that belongs to someone who has represented the place with distinction for a long time.

What's Next

After Tuesday's match, Stan Wawrinka's professional career is finished. The next chapter is whatever he decides comes after — commentary, coaching, family time, or simply not being a professional tennis player for the first time since his early twenties. He leaves with a résumé that will occupy a specific and well-defined place in tennis history: three Slam titles across three different surfaces, an Olympic gold medal, and a backhand that a generation of players grew up watching and trying to replicate. Basel gives him the proper goodbye. The rest is up to him.
