Djokovic Returns to Basel for the First Time Since 2011

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | October 27, 2026

When Novak Djokovic enters St. Jakobshalle on Tuesday for his opening match against Ugo Humbert, it will be his first time competing in Basel since 2011 — a gap of fifteen years. The circumstances this time are different from anything that has brought him to a tournament before: he enters as a wildcard, he is outside the top eight in the ATP Race to Turin, and he needs meaningful results to qualify for the ATP Finals next month.

Fifteen Years Away

Djokovic's absence from Basel across more than a decade was not the result of any particular falling-out with the tournament. It was scheduling, preference, and the mechanics of a career in which he was one of the three or four best players in the world for most of that stretch. He simply chose other events, or was not available, or did not need the points that Basel would offer.

In 2026, the calculus is different. Djokovic has confirmed he is targeting the ATP Finals in Turin — a tournament he has won six times — but his Race position entering the European indoor swing was precarious. Reports from earlier in October suggested he had originally planned only Paris Masters before the Finals, but the Race standings made Basel a more attractive option. A wildcard entry at a 500-point event is a meaningful opportunity when the margin between qualification and missing Turin is potentially small.

The Humbert Test

Ugo Humbert is not an easy first-round draw. The Frenchman is left-handed, a style that historically has caused problems even for elite players because of the spin angles and service direction it produces. Humbert has won four ATP titles on indoor hard courts — the exact surface where Djokovic needs to perform. He is ranked outside the top 20 this season but has demonstrated repeatedly that he can trouble any player in this environment.

Djokovic's record on indoor hard courts remains among the best in the open era. The concern is competitive rust. He has not played frequently this autumn, and his Race position reflects accumulated absences rather than any single dramatic result. A first-round win over Humbert would be reassuring. A loss would complicate the Race picture significantly.

Basel's History with Champions

The Swiss Indoors has a particular place in the sport's history as the tournament most associated with Roger Federer — who won the event ten times and effectively built it into one of the most watched indoor tournaments on the ATP calendar. Djokovic won in Basel in 2009 and 2010, two of his earlier major titles. Returning in 2026, for the final stretch of a career that has produced more Grand Slam titles than any player in history, carries its own weight.

This week, Basel is hosting Wawrinka's farewell, Shelton's title defense, a full Race drama across the draw, and Djokovic's first appearance in fifteen years. For a 500-point indoor event, it rarely gets more loaded than this.

What's Next

If Djokovic advances past Humbert, his second-round opponent would come from another section of the draw. A quarterfinal run at Basel would earn him 180 Race points; a semifinal 300; the title 500. Given where he sits in the standings, a title would likely confirm his qualification for Turin. For now, the focus is Humbert on Tuesday evening, and a tournament he last entered during a completely different chapter of his career.
