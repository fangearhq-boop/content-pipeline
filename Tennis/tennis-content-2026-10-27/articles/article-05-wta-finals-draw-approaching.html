WTA Finals Draw One Week Away — What to Watch When the Groups Are Set

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | October 27, 2026

The 2026 WTA Finals draw is expected around November 5 — three days before play begins at the Indian Wells Tennis Garden on November 8. With one week to go, the field is set, the venue is confirmed, and the conversations are shifting toward one central question: how will the two groups break down, and what does each potential grouping mean for the eight players who qualified?

The Format

The WTA Finals uses a round-robin structure: eight players split into two groups of four, each player facing the three others in her group. The top two players from each group advance to the semifinals, with the group winners crossing over to face the other group's runners-up. This format typically means four or five matches before the final, with almost no margin for a slow start.

Players are generally seeded by ranking for group assignment, with the top seed and No. 2 seed placed in separate groups to ensure they cannot meet before the final. That means Elena Rybakina and Aryna Sabalenka will be in different groups — but who else joins each of them shapes the difficulty of the path to the semifinal.

The Field

Rybakina arrives as both World No. 1 and the defending 2025 WTA Finals champion — a combination only a handful of players have held entering the year-end event. She won the Ningbo Open earlier this month and has been the most consistent player on tour in 2026. She enters Indian Wells as the logical favorite.

Aryna Sabalenka, the No. 2 seed, has won two Grand Slams this season and will be the most dangerous threat to Rybakina in the title race. A group featuring either of them also includes two other top-eight players, and the combinations matter. Coco Gauff and Mirra Andreeva are both capable of beating anyone in the field on a given day. Jessica Pegula, Elina Svitolina, Linda Noskova, and Karolina Muchova complete the eight.

Noskova in particular bears watching. She has been one of the year's better performers and secured her Finals spot with results that surprised some observers. Muchova, who qualified at the expense of Marta Kostyuk in the final week of the race, arrives as a player capable of doing damage in a round-robin format.

Indian Wells for the First Time

This is the first WTA Finals at Indian Wells, following the relocation from Riyadh after the Saudi Arabia contract ended. The Indian Wells Tennis Garden is a hard-court facility that hosted the BNP Paribas Open for decades and remains one of the finest tennis venues in North America. Its outdoor setting is a change from the indoor arenas that have typically hosted the year-end event, and the hard-court surface should produce fast, baseline-driven tennis — a format that suits Rybakina and Sabalenka well.

What's Next

The draw announcement is expected around November 5. Once groups are confirmed, the schedule for the group-stage round-robin matches will be published.
