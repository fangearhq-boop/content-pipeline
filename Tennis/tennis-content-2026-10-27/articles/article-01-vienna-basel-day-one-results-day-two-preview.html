Berrettini, De Minaur, Rublev Open With Wins — Vienna and Basel Day 2 Begins

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | October 27, 2026

Monday's first-round action at the Erste Bank Open in Vienna and the Swiss Indoors in Basel gave the European indoor season a confident start. Three players of particular interest in the ATP Race to Turin — Matteo Berrettini, Alex de Minaur, and Andrey Rublev — all advanced, setting up a loaded Tuesday that puts the tournament's biggest names on court for the first time.

Vienna: Berrettini Steady, De Minaur Resilient

Matteo Berrettini opened his Vienna campaign with a clean 6-3, 6-4 win over Diego Schwartzman's compatriot Tomás Martín Etcheverry, an efficient performance on indoor hard courts that suit his heavy serve. The Italian, who has spent much of the past two seasons managing injury absences, showed no such concerns Monday.

Alex de Minaur's opening match required considerably more drama. He was outplayed in the opening set, dropping it 1-6 to Czech qualifier Tomas Machac before refocusing and winning 6-4, 6-3 over the next two sets. De Minaur is currently outside the top eight in the ATP Race to Turin, and every win this week keeps the conversation alive. A Vienna title would be transformative for his Race position.

Jakub Mensik also advanced, defeating local wild card Lukas Neumayer in straight sets.

Basel: Rublev Makes a Statement

Andrey Rublev did not ease into Basel — he announced himself. A 6-3, 6-2 win over Portugal's Nuno Borges took 64 minutes and produced a first-serve stat line that would have turned heads at any level: Rublev lost only one point on his first serve across the entire match, compiling 21 winners to Borges's 11. TSN described the win as boosting his chances of qualifying for a fifth consecutive ATP Finals.

Like de Minaur, Rublev enters Basel outside the top eight in the Race. The five open spots are currently led by Medvedev (No. 5), Cobolli (No. 6), Fils (No. 7), and Tiafoe (No. 8), with Rublev, de Minaur, Djokovic, and others in pursuit. Both 500-point events this week represent the last major opportunity before the Paris Masters to shift the standings.

Day 2: The Headliners Arrive

Tuesday raises the level significantly. In Vienna, No. 1 seed Alexander Zverev opens his campaign against wild card Martin Landaluce. Carlos Alcaraz, the No. 2 seed and two-time Grand Slam champion, faces Karen Khachanov — a player he leads 6-0 in their head-to-head series. Zverev has confirmed his 2026 Year-End No. 1 ranking and enters Vienna already qualified for Turin; he is now targeting the title.

In Basel, Novak Djokovic plays his opening match against Ugo Humbert, and Stan Wawrinka faces Jiří Lehečká in what will be the final professional match of his career. The Vienna order of play puts Alcaraz on Centre Court first Tuesday afternoon, with Zverev to follow. The Basel evening session with Wawrinka is the emotional centerpiece of the day.

What's Next

Both tournaments run through November 1. Vienna and Basel each award 500 Race points to their champion and 300 to the finalist — enough movement to significantly change the qualification picture for the ATP Finals in Turin on November 15. Second-round matches at both events will begin Wednesday. Zverev and Alcaraz are expected to open as heavy favorites if they come through Day 2.
