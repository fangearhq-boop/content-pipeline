Rublev Dominant, De Minaur Resilient as Both Advance and Keep Race Hopes Alive

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | October 27, 2026

Two players chasing ATP Finals qualification from outside the automatic spots opened their weeks in opposite fashion on Monday — and both came away with wins. Andrey Rublev was overpowering at Basel. Alex de Minaur was struggling, then wasn't, in Vienna. The outcomes mattered because both players are in a tight race where every point counts.

Rublev: Only One First-Serve Point Lost

Rublev's 6-3, 6-2 win over Nuno Borges at the Swiss Indoors was remarkable for a specific reason: he won 25 of his 26 first-serve points across the entire match. That single dropped point aside, Borges essentially could not return Rublev's first serve. The match lasted 64 minutes, and Rublev finished with 21 winners to Borges's 11.

TSN noted that the win boosted Rublev's chances of qualifying for a fifth consecutive ATP Finals. The framing is accurate: Rublev needs a significant result in Basel — at minimum a quarterfinal, more likely a final or title run — to overtake players currently above him in the standings. The Race is structured so that early-round victories add 45 points per win in an ATP 500, which moves the needle modestly. Deep runs are what change the math.

De Minaur: Back From a Set Down

Alex de Minaur's path through Monday was less tidy. He dropped the opening set to Tomas Machac 1-6, a deficit that would have rattled many players considering the context — a Race match, on the road, against an opponent playing well. De Minaur came back to win 6-4, 6-3. That recovery is characteristic of a player who has repeatedly shown the mental capacity to reset between sets.

Like Rublev, de Minaur enters Vienna outside the top eight in the Race. The confirmed qualifiers — Zverev, Alcaraz, and Shelton — leave five spots contested among approximately seven players, with the point totals close enough that a Vienna title would likely be sufficient to qualify. Vienna awards 500 Race points to the champion, 300 to the finalist, and 180 to semifinalists. De Minaur reached the Vienna quarterfinals in 2024 before losing to Berrettini; a longer run this week would be meaningful.

The Larger Race Picture

As of the Oct 26 standings, Medvedev holds No. 5 (3,660 pts), Cobolli No. 6 (3,640), Fils No. 7 (3,425), and Tiafoe No. 8 (3,380). Rublev, de Minaur, Djokovic, and others are positioned just behind that group, separated by a margin that two good weeks could erase. With Vienna, Basel, and Paris Masters still to be played, there are approximately 2,000 points available to a player who reaches the finals of all three — a scenario that is unlikely but illustrates how volatile the standings remain.

The more realistic framing: a player who wins one of these 500-point events and runs deep in another has a credible path to Turin. Rublev and de Minaur gave themselves the simplest version of that path Monday by avoiding first-round exits.

What's Next

Rublev's second-round opponent at Basel will come from the Tabilo-Cilic match. De Minaur's next opponent in Vienna will be determined from Tuesday's draw. Both players have demonstrated the quality to go deep in 500-level events on indoor hard courts, which is the precise setting where the Race will be decided over the next two weeks. Monday was the first step. The rest of the week will tell a more definitive story.
