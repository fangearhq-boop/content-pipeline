### Long-Form Post Tonight in Basel, Stan Wawrinka plays the last professional tennis match of his career. Monday was his tribute night — a ceremony that honored 24 years of ATP Tour tennis, three Grand Slam titles, and three Swiss Indoors championships in the very building where he plays tonight. Novak Djokovic was personally invited and showed up to pay his respects. Now comes the match. He faces Jiří Lehečká, the 8th seed and one of the better young players in the game. They have never played each other before. Lehečká reached the Miami Masters 1000 final this year. This will not be easy. But that has never been the point of this week for Wawrinka. The career speaks for itself. The 2014 Australian Open. The 2015 Roland Garros. The 2016 US Open. An Olympic gold medal in 2008 with Federer. A backhand that could end a rally without warning from any corner of the court. The Basel crowd will give him something tonight that no ranking or title can produce. That's what farewells are for. What do you remember most about Stan Wawrinka's career?

Djokovic Returns to Basel After 15-Year Absence
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Novak Djokovic is at the Swiss Indoors in Basel for the first time since 2011. That is not a typo. Fifteen years. The tournament has taken place every year while Djokovic was one of the best players in the world, and he was never in the draw — until this week. He enters as a wildcard and he enters with a purpose. Djokovic currently sits outside the top eight in the ATP Race to Turin, which means he needs results — not just participation — from Vienna and Basel and Paris to secure his spot at the ATP Finals next month. A Basel wildcard gets him into a 500-point event where a champion collects major Race points. His first-round opponent is Ugo Humbert, the French lefty who has four ATP titles on indoor hard courts. This is not a gimme. Djokovic hasn't played Basel in 15 years, but he has played indoors his entire career. If he makes a deep run here, the Race picture changes significantly. Do you think Djokovic qualifies for Turin?

Rublev and De Minaur Win, Boost ATP Finals Hopes
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Two players on the ATP Finals bubble opened their weeks with wins on Monday — and both made a statement. In Basel, Andrey Rublev beat Nuno Borges 6-3, 6-2 in 64 minutes. He won 25 of 26 points on his first serve. For context: that is basically as dominant as serving gets. TSN noted the win "boosted his hopes of qualifying for a fifth consecutive ATP Finals." In Vienna, Alex de Minaur dropped the opening set 1-6 against Tomas Machac before grinding out a 6-4, 6-3 comeback. That kind of mental toughness matters when the Race comes down to a handful of points. Both players are currently on the outside looking in at the top eight. Three spots are locked up: Zverev, Alcaraz, and Shelton are qualified. Five spots remain for seven players, with only Vienna, Basel, and Paris Masters left to play. A title in either city this week would dramatically improve their odds. Monday was a start.

WTA Finals Draw Arrives in One Week
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post The 2026 WTA Finals draw is approximately one week away — expected around November 5, three days before play begins on November 8 in Indian Wells. This is the moment the format takes shape. Eight players, two groups of four, round-robin play with the top two from each group advancing to the semifinals. The composition of those groups determines everything — whether Rybakina and Sabalenka meet in the round-robin stage or wait for a potential final, whether Gauff gets drawn against the two toughest opponents or gets a slightly more forgiving path. The field is set: Rybakina (No. 1, defending champion), Sabalenka, Andreeva, Pegula, Gauff, Svitolina, Noskova, Muchova. Rybakina arrives as both the top seed and the reigning champion — a double distinction that only a handful of players have held going into the year-end event. The draw will drop around 6 a.m. ET on November 5. Mark your calendars. Who do you think wins the 2026 WTA Finals?
