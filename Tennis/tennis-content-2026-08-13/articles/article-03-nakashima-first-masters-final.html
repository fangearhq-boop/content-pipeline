Brandon Nakashima Reaches His First Masters Final — and His Path Through Montreal Was Remarkable

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | August 13, 2026

Brandon Nakashima entered the 2026 National Bank Open in Montreal as the 28th seed. On Wednesday night, he beat Rafael Jodar 7-6(3), 6-4 to reach the Masters 1000 final for the first time in his career.

That distance — from 28th seed to finalist — took less than two weeks and four matches. His reward is a date tonight with defending champion Ben Shelton. His accomplishment is a final that no one predicted when the draw was released.

The Run

Nakashima's Montreal campaign built quietly and then announced itself with force.

In the quarterfinals, he defeated Luciano Darderi in 1 hour and 17 minutes, firing 11 aces and never facing a break point. It was the kind of performance that generates little attention in the moment — a seeded player beating an unseeded opponent efficiently — but it signaled that Nakashima's serve was operating at its peak.

In the semifinals, the opponent was more formidable. Rafael Jodar had been one of the most talked-about players of the summer — a 19-year-old Spaniard who entered 2026 ranked No. 168 and has risen to No. 11 in the live ATP rankings. Jodar had beaten Arthur Fils in the quarterfinals to become the first player born in 2006 or later to reach an ATP Masters 1000 semifinal. He pushed Nakashima to a tiebreak in the first set.

Nakashima won the tiebreak 7-3, then closed the second set 6-4 to advance. The scoreline was clean. The path was not easy.

The UVA Connection

There was a quiet subplot to the Nakashima-Jodar semifinal that did not go unnoticed: both players attended the University of Virginia, where they played college tennis for the Cavaliers program.

Nakashima played at Virginia before turning professional and climbing the ATP rankings. Jodar's UVA path has contributed to his development as well. Their semifinal in Montreal was, among other things, a reunion of two former teammates competing at the highest level of their sport — on a court in front of a Masters crowd.

For Nakashima, the older of the two, the win was a professional statement. For Jodar, 19, the loss was still a landmark. Neither will look back on this week with any regret.

What Nakashima Needs Tonight

Ben Shelton is a significant challenge. He is the defending champion in Montreal. He has been the most dominant player in the tournament — with the exception of the injury detour during his semifinal, his tennis has been close to untouchable. He is also famously aggressive on his serve, which means Nakashima cannot simply trade with him from the back of the court.

Nakashima's best path to the title runs through his own serve. He has been virtually unbreakable in Montreal; if he can replicate the 11-ace performance he put up against Darderi and stay clean on his first serve, he forces Shelton into a game of chess rather than firepower. Nakashima is patient enough to play that game.

The match begins not before 8:00 PM ET in Montreal. By the end of the night, someone will have the title — and one player will have won his first.

What's Next

Nakashima is in the Cincinnati draw regardless of tonight's outcome. The Western and Southern Open, which opened today (August 13), is the final Masters event before the US Open (August 30). A Montreal final appearance will do nothing but improve Nakashima's seeding and confidence heading into the hardest stretch of the American summer.

Whether tonight ends in a title or a runner-up finish, Nakashima's Montreal run will stand on its own. For a 28th seed in a field this deep, reaching the final is the kind of result careers are built on.
