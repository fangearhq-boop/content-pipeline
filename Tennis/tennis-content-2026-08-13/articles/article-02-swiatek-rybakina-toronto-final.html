Swiatek Beats Svitolina; Rybakina Tops Gauff — Toronto Final Pits 6-6 Rivals Tonight at 6 PM

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | August 13, 2026

The Toronto WTA final is set, and it could not be more evenly matched.

Iga Swiatek and Elena Rybakina are meeting tonight at 6:00 PM ET on Centre Court at the National Bank Open in Toronto. Their head-to-head record going into the final stands at exactly 6-6 — six wins apiece, no edge for either player. What happens tonight will break that tie and determine who wins the biggest women's tennis title of the summer.

Swiatek got here by beating Elina Svitolina 6-3, 1-6, 6-3 in a rain-delayed semifinal on Wednesday. Rybakina advanced past Coco Gauff 5-7, 6-2, 6-2 — the third time in this Toronto tournament she has come from a set down to win.

Swiatek Over Svitolina: Beating Her 2026 Tormentor

The Swiatek-Svitolina semifinal was delayed more than an hour by rain before it finally began, and Swiatek came out looking like she hadn't lost a step. She broke Svitolina's serve in the first three service games of the match and built a commanding lead before closing out the opener 6-3. Svitolina had not been able to hold serve until she was already down 5-2.

The second set was different. Svitolina leveled her footwork, improved on return, and took the set 6-1 with a commanding run. The match reset. Swiatek steadied in the decider, broke at a key moment, and won 6-3.

The significance of the win was larger than the scoreline suggests. Svitolina had beaten Swiatek in both of their previous 2026 meetings — Swiatek entered the semifinal with an 0-2 record against her this season. Wednesday's win reversed that head-to-head trend at the highest possible moment: a Masters 1000 semifinal, with a final spot on the line.

It is Swiatek's 14th career WTA 1000 final. It is her first WTA 1000 final of 2026. She has not won a WTA 1000 title this season. Tonight is her clearest chance to change that.

Rybakina's Tournament: Three Times From a Set Down

Elena Rybakina's Toronto campaign has been defined by one characteristic: she keeps finding herself a set down, and she keeps winning anyway.

Against Coco Gauff in the semifinals, it happened again. Gauff won the first set 7-5, saving a set point against her at 5-4 to extend it before claiming the opener. Rybakina responded by winning the next two sets 6-2, 6-2. Gauff had the set in hand and let it slip; Rybakina then ran away from her.

It was the same pattern Rybakina used to beat Naomi Osaka in the quarterfinals — down a set, fought back, won. Same in an earlier round. Three times this week, she's been behind. Three times, she's come back.

Rybakina is also the defending Cincinnati champion. She transitions from Toronto to Cincinnati immediately after tonight's final — win or lose, she'll be heading to Ohio to defend that title as the No. 2 seed. Her serve has been decisive throughout Toronto: 13 aces in the Osaka quarterfinal, consistent first-serve dominance in every match.

Tonight's Final: What to Watch

With a 6-6 head-to-head, there are no reliable patterns to lean on. Each of their previous 12 matches has been a genuine contest. Their styles are complementary rather than matching — Swiatek's relentless baseline energy versus Rybakina's devastating serve and clean ball-striking.

The key question is which version of each player shows up. Swiatek at her best is the finest mover and competitor in women's tennis. Rybakina at her best serves so effectively that she removes entire game plans from her opponent's options. Both players are in form, both have earned their place here, and both know how to close a match at this level.

The final is on Centre Court at 6:00 PM ET.

What's Next

The winner of tonight's Toronto final heads to Cincinnati for the Western and Southern Open, which opened today. Sabalenka is the top seed in Cincinnati; Rybakina is the No. 2 seed and defending champion. Swiatek is also in the Cincinnati draw. The WTA's hard court season rolls directly into the US Open, which begins August 30 in New York.

Tonight's result will carry real momentum into the final weeks before Flushing Meadows. The winner of this final arrives in Cincinnati and at the US Open with a title in their legs.
