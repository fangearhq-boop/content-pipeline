Shelton and Nakashima Make Montreal an All-American Final — Defending Champ Plays Through Bleeding Elbow

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | August 13, 2026

Ben Shelton is going to defend his National Bank Open title tonight — and he had to bleed for the right to try.

The defending champion from Atlanta defeated Learner Tien 6-2, 6-3 in Wednesday's semifinals at IGA Stadium in Montreal, but not before a frightening moment in the second set when Shelton collided with an LED advertising panel placed near the baseline. He needed a medical timeout while trainers treated cuts on his left elbow and in the webbing between his fingers. His coach, Erin Stubbs, was furious about the panel's placement and demanded it be removed from the court before play resumed.

Shelton came back out and won the next game. He didn't flinch.

"It was payback," Shelton said of the win over Tien, who had defeated him in both of their previous head-to-head meetings. The 6-2, 6-3 scoreline was dominant, injury and all. On the other side of the draw, Brandon Nakashima handled 19-year-old Rafael Jodar 7-6(3), 6-4 to reach his first career ATP Masters 1000 final.

Tonight — not before 8:00 PM ET — it's Shelton vs. Nakashima for the Montreal title. Two Americans. One trophy.

Shelton's Semifinal: Dominant Until the Collision

Shelton had been in control from the start. He broke Tien early in the first set and never relinquished the advantage, closing the opener 6-2 with the clean, aggressive tennis that has made him one of the most dangerous players on hard courts. The second set was similarly comfortable until the moment Shelton ran into the advertising board during the sixth game.

The collision left Shelton visibly shaken. Trainers worked on his left elbow and hand while Stubbs made her frustration known to tournament officials about the panel's proximity to the playing surface. After the timeout, Shelton returned to the court, held serve, and finished the match without losing another game.

For Shelton, the win over Tien carried extra meaning. Their two previous meetings had gone to Tien, making this semifinal a chance to reverse a personal head-to-head trend at the worst possible moment — against his countryman on the day before an all-American final. Shelton reversed it emphatically.

Nakashima Holds Off Jodar's Tiebreak Push

Nakashima's path to the final was less dramatic but no less impressive. He faced Jodar — the 19-year-old Spaniard who entered 2026 ranked No. 168 and has become one of the summer's best stories — and had to work through a first-set tiebreak before taking control. The 7-6(3), 6-4 scoreline understates how well Nakashima served and managed the match.

It was Nakashima's second consecutive strong performance in Montreal. He had fired 11 aces in just 1 hour and 17 minutes against Luciano Darderi in the quarterfinals. Against Jodar, he was more measured, using his consistency and serve to prevent the teenager from settling into his rhythm.

For Jodar, the loss ends a remarkable run through the Montreal draw. He became the first player born in 2006 or later to reach an ATP Masters 1000 semifinal, going 34-13 on the season and climbing to No. 11 in the live ATP rankings. He had beaten Arthur Fils in the quarterfinals. His summer is not over — he transitions to Cincinnati, where he is also in the draw.

Tonight: All-American Final

The Shelton-Nakashima final is the most American the Canadian Masters has looked in decades. Shelton enters as the clear favorite — he is the defending champion, he has been the most dominant player in the tournament, and he has just beaten Tien with an injury. Nakashima enters having dropped just one set in Montreal (to Jodar in the tiebreak) and with his serve firing.

There is no bad outcome tonight for American tennis. One of them is going to win the Montreal Masters. The question is whether it's Shelton's back-to-back title or Nakashima's first at this level.

The match begins not before 8:00 PM ET at IGA Stadium.

What's Next

The Montreal finalist — whichever American wins — will head to Cincinnati for the Western and Southern Open, which opened today (August 13). The Cincinnati draw includes Zverev (No. 1 seed), Djokovic (No. 3 seed), and a host of players looking to finalize their US Open preparations. The US Open begins August 30 in New York.

Shelton will need to have his elbow evaluated before Cincinnati. Nakashima is in the draw regardless of tonight's result. Jodar, who lost to Nakashima in the semis, is also heading to Cincinnati — his summer schedule is as grueling as his results suggest.
