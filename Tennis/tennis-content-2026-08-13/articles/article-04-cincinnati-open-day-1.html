Cincinnati Open Day 1: Djokovic Returns, Zverev Leads Wide-Open Draw

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | August 13, 2026

The 2026 Western and Southern Open — the final ATP Masters 1000 and WTA 1000 event before the US Open — opened Thursday at the Lindner Family Tennis Centre in Mason, Ohio. It is the 125th edition of the Cincinnati men's event, and the draw looks genuinely wide open.

Alexander Zverev is the top seed on the men's side following the pre-tournament withdrawals of Jannik Sinner (knee) and Carlos Alcaraz (wrist). Novak Djokovic, returning to the ATP Tour for the first time since Wimbledon, is seeded third. In a draw quirk that immediately caught attention, Zverev and Djokovic are in the same half — meaning a potential quarterfinal or semifinal collision between two of the sport's biggest names is possible before the championship round.

Djokovic's Return

Novak Djokovic has not played a competitive match since Wimbledon. His return to Cincinnati marks his first ATP Tour appearance of the hard-court swing, and he arrives as the No. 3 seed in a draw that has been thinned at the top by injury.

Djokovic's likely second-round opponent will be the winner of Jan Choinski vs. Thiago Agustin Tirante, both of whom play their first-round match Thursday. Top seeds receive first-round byes in Cincinnati, so Djokovic does not play on Day 1 — his tournament begins in the second round.

The last time Djokovic played on hard courts in a significant event was before the summer clay season. His readiness is the most interesting question in the Cincinnati men's draw. Zverev, who won Cincinnati in 2021 and has been the No. 1 player on tour since Sinner and Alcaraz have been absent, is the clear form favorite. Djokovic's match rust is a real factor.

A Draw Shaped by Absences

The withdrawals of Sinner and Alcaraz have given this Cincinnati draw a different character than usual. Both players were expected to compete here as part of their US Open preparation; without them, the bracket opens up for players ranked No. 3 through No. 10 who rarely get this kind of space at the top of a Masters draw.

Taylor Fritz, Ben Shelton (if his elbow permits following tonight's Montreal final), and Medvedev are all in the draw and could realistically compete for the title. Rafael Jodar — who just made the Montreal semifinals — is also in Cincinnati, meaning he will be transitioning from Canada to Ohio with virtually no rest between events.

On the women's side, Aryna Sabalenka is the top seed. Defending champion Elena Rybakina is seeded second; she plays the Toronto WTA final tonight before heading to Cincinnati. Iga Swiatek is also in the Cincinnati draw as the 2025 champion.

Day 1 Results

Thursday's first-round action featured mostly qualifier-level and early-draw matches, as the seeded players hold their byes. Results included wins for Henrique Rocha, Aleksandar Vukic, Luca Van Assche, Quentin Halys, Jaime Faria, Aleksandr Shevchenko, Marco Trungelliti, Trevor Svajda, Coleman Wong, and Sho Shimabukuro. Cincinnati qualifying was delayed at least one day by rain before the main draw began.

Action picks up significantly on Day 2 (Friday, August 14), when first-round matches involving seeded players begin. Jack Draper, Denis Shapovalov, and Cameron Norrie are among the players expected to compete Friday.

What's Next

The Western and Southern Open runs through August 23, with the men's and women's finals both on the final weekend. The US Open follows immediately, beginning August 30 in New York. Cincinnati is the last major tune-up for every player in the draw — form and fitness here will shape expectations at Flushing Meadows.

The most significant story to watch: whether Djokovic can shake off his match rust in time to be a factor by the quarterfinals, and whether the clear form leader — Zverev — can hold his game together all the way through a compressed schedule.
