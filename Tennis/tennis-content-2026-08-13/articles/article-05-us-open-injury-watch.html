Alcaraz, Sinner, Raducanu, Tiafoe: The US Open Injury List Keeps Growing

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | August 13, 2026

The US Open begins August 30. The hard court swing — the weeks of Masters and WTA 1000 events designed to prepare players for Flushing Meadows — is supposed to sharpen the favorites and sort out the contenders. This year, it has mostly produced an injury ledger.

As Cincinnati opens on Thursday, the list of notable absences and compromised players is longer than it has been in recent memory. Some of the sport's biggest names are either out of Cincinnati entirely, dealing with nagging injuries, or — in at least one case — playing through blood on a court in Montreal.

The Withdrawn

Carlos Alcaraz withdrew from Cincinnati before the draw. The reigning US Open champion hasn't played a competitive match since April due to a wrist injury. That's roughly four months off the tour for the player defending the biggest title in American tennis. How Alcaraz arrives at the US Open — in terms of match fitness and confidence — is the most significant individual question heading into the tournament.

Jannik Sinner also withdrew from Cincinnati with a knee injury. Sinner was the No. 1 player in the world entering 2026; his Cincinnati absence drops him out of the No. 1 seed picture for the draw and limits his hard-court match time before the Open. He joins Alcaraz on the injury list at the worst possible time of the season.

Emma Raducanu withdrew from Cincinnati with an injury, continuing a pattern of fitness challenges that have interrupted what should be an emerging run of results for the British No. 1.

Jasmine Paolini, who made the Roland Garros champion run and has been one of the WTA's best stories in recent seasons, also withdrew from Cincinnati and Toronto before the main draw.

Frances Tiafoe had surgery to remove a cyst from his right hand around August 8. His US Open timeline is unclear, and with the tournament less than three weeks away, the margin for recovery is narrow.

The Compromised

Ben Shelton is in the Montreal final tonight — but he is playing with a bleeding elbow and hand after crashing into an LED advertising panel in his semifinal against Learner Tien. He needed a medical timeout and had the area taped before returning to win. He'll need to have the injury evaluated before Cincinnati, where he is also in the draw.

Shelton's ability to compete in Cincinnati after playing — and being injured — in the Montreal final tonight is an open question. The timeline is aggressive: the final is not before 8:00 PM ET Thursday; Cincinnati round-of-64 matches are already underway.

Who Benefits

With Alcaraz and Sinner absent from Cincinnati, Alexander Zverev is the unambiguous top seed and form favorite. He won Cincinnati in 2021 and has been the steadiest player in the men's game since the injuries mounted. Novak Djokovic's return as the No. 3 seed gives the draw its biggest storyline, but Djokovic's match rust is a real variable. Daniil Medvedev, Alex de Minaur, and Taylor Fritz are all capable of winning a title this wide open.

On the women's side, Aryna Sabalenka lost in the Toronto round of 16 to Alexandrova and will be motivated in Cincinnati. She is the No. 1 seed. Rybakina (No. 2) and Swiatek (the 2025 defending champion) are also strong options. The injury-thinned field creates real opportunities for players who can stay healthy through a grueling schedule.

What's Next

The US Open begins August 30. Players in both draws have approximately 17 days from Thursday to finalize their preparations. Cincinnati runs through August 23, leaving roughly one week between the Cincinnati final and the first round at Flushing Meadows.

For the injured and withdrawn players — Alcaraz especially — that window is both too short to build match fitness from scratch and long enough to make a decision about how to approach the Open. One thing is certain: the 2026 US Open will look different at the top of the draw than anyone expected at the start of the summer.
