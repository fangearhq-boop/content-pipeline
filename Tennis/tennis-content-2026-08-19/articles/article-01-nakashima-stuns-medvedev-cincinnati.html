Nakashima Saves Three Match Points to Stun Medvedev in Cincinnati Thriller

By Elena Voss | August 19, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

Brandon Nakashima was one point from elimination — three times over.

Down 0-40 on his own serve at 4-5 in the second set against Daniil Medvedev, the American saved all three match points, held serve, broke in the next game, and then dismantled the Russian 6-1 in the third set. Final score: 6-7(3), 7-6(4), 6-1 in 2 hours and 51 minutes. The Cincinnati crowd, which had come to watch a former world No. 1 cruise through the Round of 16, witnessed something different — a 22-year-old American refusing to let the match end.

The Turning Point

The first two sets were tight and competitive, with Medvedev taking the opener in a tiebreak. Nakashima appeared in serious trouble at 4-5, 0-40 in the second — three consecutive match points for Medvedev. But Nakashima's serve, which had been his most reliable weapon all match, came alive at the critical moment. He held, went on to win the second set in another tiebreak, and never looked back.

The third set was one-sided in the most emphatic way. Nakashima broke Medvedev twice and dropped only one game. Medvedev, who had been the clear favorite entering the match, had no answers as the American's ball-striking hit another level entirely.

A Summer to Remember

This win does not exist in isolation. Nakashima arrived in Cincinnati off the back of a runner-up finish at the Canadian Open in Montreal, where he fell to Ben Shelton 6-3, 7-6(4) in the final. That result pushed him to a career-high ranking of No. 22 in the world — already a milestone. Beating Medvedev in Cincinnati now puts him in the Round of 16 at back-to-back Masters 1000 events for the first time in his career.

Nakashima's career was once defined by early exits and unfulfilled potential. That narrative is over. He is playing the tennis of his life, and he is doing it at exactly the right time — 11 days before the US Open begins.

What's Next

Nakashima advances to the Cincinnati quarterfinals, scheduled for August 20. The US Open main draw begins August 30, with the draw announced August 27. After this summer, he enters Flushing Meadows as one of the American players opponents will want to avoid in the early rounds.

As for Medvedev — his Cincinnati exit continues what has been a surprisingly quiet hard-court summer for the Russian, who remains one of the sport's best hard-court players but has not found his best form in this swing.
