US Open 2026: 11 Days Out, Sinner and Alcaraz Fitness Still in Question

By Elena Voss | August 19, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

Eleven days separate the tennis world from the start of the US Open main draw. Two questions that have defined the hard-court summer remain unanswered: Is Jannik Sinner healthy enough to defend his position as the world's best player? And can Carlos Alcaraz return from four months of inactivity at the sport's highest level?

Sinner: Monitoring Day by Day

Jannik Sinner (ITA, World No. 1) has not competed since Wimbledon. He withdrew from both the Canadian Open and the Cincinnati Open with a right knee injury. The Australian Open champion and Roland-Garros runner-up has been undergoing treatment and is monitoring his knee's response on a daily basis.

Reports suggest Sinner is still targeting a return for the US Open, which begins its main draw on August 30. One reported option is a wildcard entry at the Winston-Salem Open — the ATP 250 event that runs the week before the US Open, finishing August 29 — which would give him match fitness before the Grand Slam. Nothing has been officially confirmed. Prediction markets have continued to list him as the runaway favorite despite his absence from the draw, a testament to the gap in class his form earlier in 2026 established.

Alcaraz: Four Months of Inactivity

Carlos Alcaraz (ESP, World No. 2, defending US Open champion) last played a professional match on April 14 in Barcelona. The tenosynovitis — inflammation of the tendon sheath — in his right wrist has kept him out of Roland-Garros, Wimbledon, the Canadian Open in Montreal, and Cincinnati. His US Open title defence is under serious threat.

A return to match play directly at Flushing Meadows, in best-of-five sets against high-quality opponents, would require extraordinary physical readiness from a player who has not competed in four months. Multiple outlets have described his situation as the biggest question mark of the summer.

The Men's Draw Without Them

If both Sinner and Alcaraz are absent or operating on limited preparation, the 2026 US Open men's draw becomes "unusually open," in the words of several analysts. Alexander Zverev, who is performing well in Cincinnati and has already won 46 tour matches in 2026, enters as the likely top seed. Alex de Minaur, Taylor Fritz, and surging younger players like Brandon Nakashima would all see their paths to a first Grand Slam title improve dramatically.

What's Next

The US Open draw is announced on August 27. The main draw begins August 30 at the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center in Flushing Meadows, New York. Any official fitness updates from the Sinner or Alcaraz camps are expected in the days between now and the draw announcement.
