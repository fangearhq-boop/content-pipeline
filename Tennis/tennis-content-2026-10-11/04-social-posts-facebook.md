### STORY 1: WTA Beijing Final

**Long-Form — 8:00 AM ET**
Sunday in Beijing is going to be special.

Qinwen Zheng stepped onto the court at the China Open as a wildcard — unseeded, playing in front of her home crowd, and knowing the odds weren't in her favor against one of the tour's best. But she dismantled Elise Mertens 7-5, 6-2 in the second semifinal to become the first Chinese player ever to reach the women's singles final at the China Open.

Her opponent? Mirra Andreeva, the 18-year-old Russian who beat Nikola Bartunkova 6-2, 6-2 in her semifinal. Andreeva is going to be World No. 3 on Monday regardless of the result today — she earned that guarantee by reaching the final. But she's hunting much more: a fifth title of 2026 and a third WTA 1000 trophy.

The one time these two have played before? Zheng won. In the 2024 China Open quarterfinals. Right here in Beijing. She's comfortable in this building, she's in form, and the crowd will be completely behind her.

For Andreeva, this is a chance to prove she belongs at the absolute top of the sport. For Zheng, this is about giving China a home champion in its own tournament.

Who do you have taking the title?

### STORY 2: Shanghai Day 5

**Long-Form — 12:30 PM ET**
The Rolex Shanghai Masters is delivering everything you could want from a Masters 1000 event — and Sunday might be the biggest day yet.

Three of the tournament's headliners are all in action in the third round. No. 1 seed Alexander Zverev, who had a scare with his ankle in Round 2 but pushed through to beat Wu Yibing, faces Quentin Halys. If all three win today, the quarterfinals start taking shape. The draw is holding and the biggest names are on a collision course.

### STORY 3: Zverev Practice Court Controversy

**Long-Form — 3:00 PM ET**
Off-court drama is never far from Alexander Zverev, it seems.

Reports surfacing on Rednote — the Chinese social media app — claim Zverev showed up significantly late to his allotted practice court on Saturday, holding Court 5 well beyond his booking time and forcing Ben Shelton and Cruz Hewitt (son of tennis legend Lleyton Hewitt) to move to a different court.

One spectator account describes waiting near the fence for about an hour and a half and watching Zverev arrive late, hit for roughly 30 minutes, then leave. Another user described the situation as "bullying," though it's worth noting that's a term from social media, not from any official involved in the incident.

As of Sunday, none of the parties involved — not Zverev, not Shelton, not the Hewitt camp, and not the tournament — have confirmed or denied the account.

What makes the story notable is the backdrop: Zverev's Round 2 opponent Wu Yibing separately called out his on-court behavior during their match, accusing him of repeatedly falling down, complaining about the court lights, and messing with the net tape.

Zverev is on course for a potential deep run here. Whether this noise follows him into the quarterfinals remains to be seen.

### STORY 4: Alcaraz Returns to Shanghai

**Long-Form — 6:00 PM ET**
730 days. That's how long Carlos Alcaraz had been away from the Shanghai Masters.

He missed the entire 2025 edition due to injury, and when he finally walked back onto these courts on Saturday, there was something to prove — to himself, to the Shanghai crowd, and maybe to the rest of the field.

His response? A 6-3, 6-3 win over Juan Manuel Cerundolo in 66 minutes. Clinical. Dominant. Twenty-four winners to Cerundolo's eight. First-serve percentage at 78%. Win rate behind first serve at 86%. The only talking point anyone seemed to want to discuss was the new mustache.

After the match, Alcaraz delivered a message to the Chinese fans: he missed this place, and he's glad to be back. That kind of connection with a crowd matters at a tournament where the atmosphere is among the best on the ATP Tour.

He arrives in Shanghai on a seven-match winning streak, having just defended his Japan Open title over Lehecka. The question now is how far he can go this week.

### STORY 5: Race to Turin

**Long-Form — 9:00 PM ET**
Four spots. A handful of players. A week of tennis that could change everything.

Alexander Zverev and Carlos Alcaraz have already qualified for the ATP Finals in Turin (Nov 15-22). With Jannik Sinner sidelined for the rest of the season, the remaining four berths are wide open — and Sunday's third-round results at the Rolex Shanghai Masters just shifted the math.

Here's where the Race stands heading into the final stretch:

- Zverev: ~8,800 pts (QUALIFIED)
- Alcaraz: 4,560 pts (QUALIFIED)
- Shelton: 4th — ~4,440 pts
- Cobolli: 5th — ~3,540 pts
- Tiafoe: 6th — ~3,340 pts
- Fils, Medvedev, Djokovic: chasing hard

Every R3 win is 90 Race points — meaningful, but not decisive. The real points come in the quarterfinals (180), semifinals (360), and finals (600/1,000). Shanghai still has the potential to qualify or eliminate several bubble players.

After Shanghai: Vienna (Oct 20-26) and Paris Bercy (Oct 27-Nov 2) are the final opportunities before the cutoff.
