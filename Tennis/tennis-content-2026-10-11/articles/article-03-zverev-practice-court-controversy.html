Zverev's Shanghai Masters Stays On-Court — and Off — After Practice Court Controversy

By Elena Voss | October 11, 2026

ATP | Rolex Shanghai Masters

Alexander Zverev arrived in Shanghai as the top seed and the world's No. 1 player. He is also, once again, the center of a controversy that has nothing to do with his tennis.

Reports circulating on Rednote — a Chinese social media platform — claim Zverev held a practice court at the Rolex Shanghai Masters well beyond his allotted time on Saturday, effectively blocking Ben Shelton and Cruz Hewitt (son of former World No. 1 Lleyton Hewitt) from using the facility during their scheduled slot.

What the Reports Say

According to multiple accounts shared on Rednote, Zverev booked Court 5 for a 10:30 a.m. practice session but arrived significantly late — around 11:50 a.m. by some accounts. Despite the delay, he reportedly continued hitting for an additional 30 minutes or so, extending well past the time Shelton and Cruz Hewitt were due on the same court at noon. The pair were then moved to Court 10.

One spectator who described watching the scene said they waited near the fence for approximately an hour and a half. Another user's characterization of the situation as "bullying" spread widely on the platform, though that description reflects one person's interpretation of events, not an official finding.

As of Sunday, none of the principals involved have commented publicly. Zverev's camp has not addressed the reports. Shelton has said nothing about the incident. The Hewitt family has not issued a statement. The Rolex Shanghai Masters organization has not confirmed or denied that any scheduling disruption took place.

Not the Only Criticism This Week

The practice court reports come on the heels of separate criticism from Zverev's Round 2 opponent, Chinese wildcard Wu Yibing. In comments after their match — which Zverev won 6-4, 7-6(4) — Wu said he did not appreciate Zverev's on-court conduct. Specifically, he accused Zverev of repeatedly falling down, complaining to the chair umpire about the court lighting, and messing with the net tape during the match.

Neither complaint has resulted in any formal action from tournament officials or the ATP.

The Context Around Zverev

Zverev is no stranger to off-court scrutiny. His year on court has been exceptional — he came into Shanghai near-certain to claim the Year-End No. 1 ranking, with Jannik Sinner out for the rest of the season. His form has been dominant. His results have been consistent. But each time a controversy arises around his behavior, it draws attention to a persistent tension between how he performs on the court and how he is perceived off it.

The practice court allegations, even unconfirmed, have generated significant discussion among tennis fans on social media. Whether the story develops further depends largely on whether any of the parties involved decide to address it publicly.

What's Next

Zverev faces Quentin Halys in the third round on Sunday. His focus will need to be entirely on-court if he wants to continue his Shanghai campaign. The controversy, for now, remains an unresolved background story. Tennis Fanrecap will update this coverage if any of the parties issue statements or if tournament officials address the situation.
