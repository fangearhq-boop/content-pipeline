Andreeva vs. Zheng: History Meets Destiny in the WTA Beijing Final

By Marcus Cole | October 11, 2026

WTA | China Open

For 38 years, the China Open women's singles draw had come and gone without a Chinese player lifting the trophy on home soil. On Sunday, Qinwen Zheng has a chance to change that forever.

Zheng became the first Chinese player to reach the Beijing women's singles final after dismantling 13th-seeded Elise Mertens 7-5, 6-2 in Saturday's second semifinal — a victory that grew more dominant by the game, as she won eight of the final 10 to close out the match. She entered the draw as a wildcard, unseeded, playing in front of tens of thousands of fans who showed up hoping for exactly this kind of run.

Her opponent on Sunday is No. 4 seed Mirra Andreeva, the 18-year-old Russian who has been one of the year's most consistent performers. Andreeva advanced with a commanding 6-2, 6-2 defeat of Nikola Bartunkova in the first semifinal, reaching her fourth WTA 1000 final of 2026.

The Stakes for Andreeva

Whatever happens on Sunday, Andreeva will rise to World No. 3 on Monday — a career high she secured by reaching the final. That milestone is guaranteed. But her ambitions go further. A win here would give her five WTA Tour titles in 2026 and a third WTA 1000 crown, cementing a season that has already announced her as a genuine contender at the sport's highest level.

Andreeva's path through the draw has been dominant. She has not dropped a set this week, and her 50th singles win of the 2026 season came against Bartunkova — a player who had herself pulled off a significant upset in the quarterfinals to reach the last four.

Zheng's Remarkable Run

Context matters for Zheng's achievement. She entered the draw as a wildcard — the lowest possible seeding position. To reach a WTA 1000 final from that starting point requires navigating a field that includes the tour's top players without the benefit of a favorable draw.

Her semifinal win over Mertens was especially noteworthy given what Mertens had already accomplished this week. The Belgian had beaten Coco Gauff and Iga Swiatek on consecutive days to reach the last four — a run that had many following this draw closely. Zheng was undeterred, taking control of the second set and not looking back.

The home crowd support has been a visible factor throughout Zheng's run. Beijing National Tennis Center has been packed for her matches, and the energy when she wins points has been unlike anything typically seen at WTA events.

A History That Favors Zheng — Narrowly

The two players have met once before. That match came in the quarterfinals of the 2024 China Open — the same tournament, the same venue. Zheng won, though Andreeva made her work for it, taking the first set before Zheng came back to seal it. On Sunday, Zheng will carry both the head-to-head record and the home advantage into their second meeting.

Andreeva is projected as a slight favorite entering the final, a reflection of her current form and ranking. But the conditions — the crowd, the history, the meaning of the moment for Chinese tennis — create an unpredictable atmosphere.

What's Next

The WTA China Open final takes place on Sunday. For Andreeva, the journey continues to the WTA Finals in Riyadh later in the season, where her No. 3 ranking will earn a spot among the year's eight best performers. For Zheng, a win on home soil would represent one of the most meaningful moments in recent WTA history — a Chinese champion at the tournament that carries China's name.
