Alcaraz Returns to Shanghai After 730 Days: 'I Missed This Place'

By Marcus Cole | October 11, 2026

ATP | Rolex Shanghai Masters

Carlos Alcaraz had not played at the Rolex Shanghai Masters in 730 days. He missed the entire 2025 edition due to injury — the kind of absence that builds anticipation among tournament organizers, local fans, and the global audience that follows the sport's biggest draws.

On Saturday, the wait ended. Alcaraz walked onto court against Juan Manuel Cerundolo and played the kind of tennis that reminds you why his return to any tournament is an event.

A Statement Opening Round

The numbers from his 6-3, 6-3 first-round win were striking. He finished with 24 winners to Cerundolo's 8 — a margin that illustrates how completely he controlled the points he chose to attack. His first-serve percentage sat at 78%, and he won 86% of first-serve points, making it nearly impossible for Cerundolo to find angles into the rallies.

The match lasted 66 minutes. It was not a grind. It was a demonstration.

The win extended Alcaraz's current winning streak to seven matches, built on the back of his Japan Open title in Tokyo the week before — a defense of his 2025 crown, completed with a 7-6(4), 6-4 win over Jiří Lehečka in the final.

The Message to Shanghai

After his match, Alcaraz addressed the crowd and the cameras with something that went beyond the standard post-match interview. He talked about how much he had missed Shanghai — the tournament, the city, the fans — and what it meant to be back after more than two years away.

That kind of connection between a player and a specific tournament crowd is difficult to manufacture and easy to recognize. Shanghai's atmosphere is known throughout the ATP Tour as something distinct — large, loud, genuinely engaged — and Alcaraz's relationship with Chinese fans has been warm since his earliest visits to the country.

A New Look for a Familiar Feel

One detail from Alcaraz's first match back at Shanghai generated its own discussion. He debuted a new mustache — a departure from his clean-shaven look that prompted significant commentary across tennis social media. Whether it stays through the tournament remains to be seen, but it suggests a player comfortable enough in his return to have a little fun with the moment.

What's Next

Alcaraz plays his third-round match at the Rolex Shanghai Masters on Sunday. The draw sets up the possibility of a quarterfinal or semifinal meeting with Zverev or Shelton as the week progresses. If his first-round level holds, he will be among the favorites to win the title in the tournament's second week. Tennis Fanrecap will have full coverage of his progress through the draw.
