Race to Turin: How Shanghai's Third-Round Results Shift the Bubble Math

By Ryan Calloway | October 11, 2026

ATP | Race to Turin

Two players have already qualified for the ATP Finals in Turin. The rest of the field is in a fight that will not be resolved until the final points are distributed at Paris Bercy in early November.

Alexander Zverev and Carlos Alcaraz are in. Everyone else is watching the standings update match by match, tournament by tournament, trying to hold their position or close a gap that keeps shifting.

Where the Race Stands

Heading into Sunday's third-round matches at the Rolex Shanghai Masters, the Race to Turin standings tell a story of separation at the top and compression in the middle:

  • Zverev: ~8,800 points (QUALIFIED)
  • Alcaraz: ~4,560 points (QUALIFIED)
  • Shelton: 4th — ~4,440 points
  • Cobolli: 5th — ~3,540 points
  • Tiafoe: 6th — ~3,340 points
  • Fils: 7th
  • Medvedev: 8th
  • Djokovic: 9th (climbed six places after winning the Beijing ATP 500)

Jannik Sinner, who held the No. 2 spot in the Race with approximately 7,950 points, is out for the remainder of the season with an injury. He will not defend his points, meaning his eventual drop-off only opens more opportunity for the players behind him.

What Each Win Means

The ATP points structure for Masters 1000 events breaks down as follows: a third-round win earns 90 Race points. Quarterfinals earn 180 points. Semifinals are worth 360. The finalist collects 600, and the winner receives 1,000.

At these stakes, Sunday's third-round results are meaningful but not decisive. Shelton, Cobolli, and Tiafoe all need wins today just to maintain their current trajectories. A player who exits in the third round and watches a rival reach the quarterfinals can see a 90-point gap widen to 270 within 48 hours.

The Danger of a Deep Zverev Run

One scenario worth watching: Zverev making a run to the final or title in Shanghai. He is already the near-certain Year-End No. 1 — his gap over Sinner makes that almost impossible to lose. But a Shanghai title would not only add 1,000 points to his total; it would also deepen the separation between himself and the bubble players, potentially locking in the order of qualifications even before Paris Bercy begins.

Djokovic's climb is the other story of the past week. His Beijing title — his seventh at that event and his 102nd career title — added significant Race points and moved him from outside the top eight into realistic contention for Turin.

Two Events Remain After Shanghai

The final two events before the Turin cutoff are the Vienna Open (October 20-26) and the Paris Masters (October 27-November 2). Both are indoor hardcourt events that typically see high-level fields. Vienna is an ATP 500, offering up to 500 Race points for the winner. Paris Bercy is a Masters 1000, the last of the season, with a full 1,000 points for the champion.

The ATP Finals in Turin run from November 15-22. The eight qualifiers will be confirmed after Paris Bercy concludes.
