Alcaraz Survives Tiebreak Scare, Rolls Into Japan Open Quarterfinals

By Ryan Calloway October 3, 2026

The Comeback Tiebreak

There was a moment in the first-set tiebreak when Matteo Arnaldi stood at the precipice of an upset. He led 6-2 with four set points against Carlos Alcaraz, the defending champion and world No. 3. The Ariake Coliseum crowd sensed something. Arnaldi likely sensed something. And then Alcaraz, as he tends to do in these moments, took it all away.

The Spaniard won six consecutive points to close the tiebreak 8-6. The second set was never in doubt: 6-1 in 45 minutes. Final score: 7-6(6), 6-1 over 1 hour and 45 minutes. Alcaraz admitted after the match that the tiebreak "frustrated" him — the exact kind of moment that separates champions from pretenders.

A Perfect Record in Tokyo

Saturday's win improved Alcaraz to 7-0 at the Japan Open across his entire career. He won the title in 2025 and has never lost a match at the Ariake Coliseum. There is something about Tokyo that suits his game — the hard courts, the crowd, the late-season timing when he's fully into the indoor season rhythm.

With Novak Djokovic and Alexander Zverev both committed to Beijing this week, the Japan Open field opened considerably at the top. Alcaraz entered as the favorite and has responded like one. Reaching the quarterfinals as the defending champion without dropping a set is exactly the form he needed heading into Saturday's match.

Japan Open Quarterfinal Bracket

Alcaraz's quarterfinal opponent is Denis Shapovalov, the Canadian who has been one of the circuit's most dangerous hard-court players when healthy. Shapovalov has the weapons — serve, forehand, speed — to challenge Alcaraz on any surface. Their previous head-to-head encounters have been competitive, and this setting, with Alcaraz's Tokyo record on the line, promises to be no different.

The rest of the quarterfinal bracket tells its own story. Arthur Fils and Valentin Vacherot — both Frenchmen who have been sharp in Tokyo — face off on one side. Jaume Munar and Kyrian Jacquet, both qualifiers who navigated multiple rounds to earn their spots, round out the draw. The presence of two qualifiers in the quarterfinals reflects how the Beijing tournament's pull on the top-ranked field left the Japan Open draw slightly shallower at the upper seeds, creating the kind of bracket chaos that makes for memorable upsets.

What's Next

Alcaraz faces Shapovalov in the Japan Open quarterfinals on Sunday. A win there would put the defending champion in the semifinals for a chance at back-to-back Tokyo titles. For Alcaraz, the Japan Open matters beyond the trophy — ATP Race points toward the Nitto ATP Finals in Turin are still very much on the line, and a deep run in Tokyo would strengthen his case significantly. The tiebreak drama against Arnaldi was a blip. At 7-0 in this city, the defending champion looks very much like the favorite to make it 8-0.
