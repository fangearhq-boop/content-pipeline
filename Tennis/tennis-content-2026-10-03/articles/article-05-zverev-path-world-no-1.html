Sinner Out of Shanghai: Zverev Can Become World No. 1 for the First Time

By Ryan Calloway October 3, 2026

The Numbers Have Changed

For years, the path to World No. 1 for Alexander Zverev always involved some version of the same obstacle: Jannik Sinner playing well enough to keep the gap insurmountable. On Saturday, that changed. With Sinner officially withdrawing from the Rolex Shanghai Masters due to right knee ITB syndrome, the rankings math suddenly works in Zverev's favor for the first time all season.

The current state of the ATP official rankings is straightforward: Sinner holds 11,500 points, Zverev has 9,690. That 1,810-point gap is significant but no longer permanent. Sinner won the Shanghai Masters in 2024, meaning he was defending approximately 1,000 ranking points at this year's event. By withdrawing, those points drop out of his 52-week rolling total on the day the tournament concludes — he earns nothing to replace them. Zverev exited Shanghai early in 2025, so he defends almost nothing there either.

The Path: Win Both, Become No. 1

The scenario has been confirmed by multiple analysts and ATP tracking services: if Alexander Zverev wins the China Open in Beijing (500 points) and the Rolex Shanghai Masters (1,000 points), he becomes World No. 1 by October 19. The last time Germany produced a World No. 1 in any Grand Slam-era ranking was never — no German man has ever held the top ATP ranking in the Open era. Zverev has been No. 2 twice in his career, most recently this season following his US Open title in August.

Zverev addressed the possibility directly on Saturday after Sinner's announcement, acknowledging the door is open and confirming his immediate focus remains on the match in front of him — his second-round contest against Juncheng Shang in Beijing. That is the right instinct. History will take care of itself if the wins come.

Why 2026 Is Zverev's Best Chance

The 29-year-old German has assembled the strongest season of his career in 2026. He won the US Open in August, defeating Ben Shelton in the final for his second Grand Slam title. He secured the Laver Cup clinch for Team Europe in September. He entered the Asian swing in the form of his life, and now faces the final piece — the ranking — with Sinner unable to defend it in the field.

Historically, Zverev's late-season form has been his strongest. The indoor hard courts that define October and November suit his aggressive serve-and-forehand game. In 2025, he lost early in Shanghai — a result he will want to correct. In 2026, he has every reason to play with urgency. The Race to Turin is effectively won (he is already qualified for the ATP Finals). This is about something bigger: becoming the best player in the world, on the record that matters most.

What's Next

Zverev faces Shang in Beijing on Saturday, with a potential Djokovic quarterfinal to follow if both advance. The China Open final is Sunday, October 7. Shanghai begins October 7 and runs through the 18th. If Zverev is to claim No. 1 on the October 19 rankings update, he would need to win both events in back-to-back weeks — a demanding task but one he is fully capable of achieving. The narrative of the 2026 tennis season — Sinner's dominance, Zverev's patience, and the race to the top — is reaching its conclusion. Zverev has the next two weeks to write the ending.
