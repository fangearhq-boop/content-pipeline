Sinner Withdraws from Shanghai Masters, Opening Door for Zverev to Reach No. 1

By Marcus Cole October 3, 2026

The Official Withdrawal

Jannik Sinner confirmed his withdrawal from the Rolex Shanghai Masters on Saturday via Instagram Stories, delivering the news many had anticipated but no one wanted to hear. "Unfortunately I won't be able to play at the Rolex Shanghai Masters this year as I had hoped," the world No. 1 wrote. The statement was short and apologetic — a pattern that has become painfully familiar to tennis fans over the past three months.

The Shanghai withdrawal marks Sinner's fifth consecutive missed tournament since he won the Wimbledon title in July. The Italian has sat out the Canadian Open, the Western & Southern Open in Cincinnati, the US Open, the China Open in Beijing, and now the Masters 1000 event in Shanghai. He has not struck a competitive ball since his final at the All England Club.

The Injury and What Comes Next

The diagnosis is iliotibial band syndrome — a condition affecting the connective tissue running along the outside of the knee and thigh. ITBS is a well-documented overuse injury common in endurance athletes, but its presence in Sinner at age 25 at the peak of his career has sent shockwaves through the sport. Earlier this week, his team traveled to Lyon, France, to consult Dr. Bertrand Sonnery-Cottet at the Hôpital Privé Jean Mermoz. The specialist's recommendation was clear: no surgery, continue conservative treatment.

The surgical option remains available if conservative therapies fail, but sources close to the situation describe it as an extreme, last-resort scenario. For now, Sinner continues with physical therapy and rest. No timeline for his return has been given. The Vienna Open (late October) and the Nitto ATP Finals in Turin (November) remain theoretically possible, but neither is confirmed. The 2026 season may be over for the man who dominated it.

What Sinner's Absence Means for the No. 1 Race

Sinner currently holds 11,500 ATP ranking points. Alexander Zverev sits at 9,690 — a gap of 1,810. In isolation, that looks comfortable. But the Shanghai withdrawal changes the arithmetic significantly. Sinner won the Shanghai Masters in 2024, which means he was defending approximately 1,000 ranking points at this year's event. By withdrawing without playing, those points expire from his 52-week rolling total immediately. Zverev, who exited early in Shanghai a year ago, defends almost nothing there.

The scenario, confirmed by analysts and ATP tour tracking sites, is straightforward: if Zverev wins in Beijing (500 points) and wins in Shanghai (1,000 points), he becomes World No. 1 by October 19. That would make the German the first player from his country to hold the top ATP ranking in the Open era — a mark he has been within sight of multiple times without quite reaching it.

Zverev addressed the possibility directly Saturday, acknowledging the door is open while remaining focused on the task immediately in front of him: completing his quarterfinal run in Beijing.

What's Next

The tennis world now watches in two directions. For Sinner, the hope is a healthy return before the season ends — Vienna or Turin remain the targets, but there is no certainty. For Zverev, the momentum of a US Open title, a Laver Cup clinch, and now a wide-open Beijing draw converge into one of the most compelling final stretches of any tennis season in years. Whether Sinner can return to defend his No. 1 position or whether Zverev rewrites the record books in the next two weeks is the defining story of the 2026 ATP season.
