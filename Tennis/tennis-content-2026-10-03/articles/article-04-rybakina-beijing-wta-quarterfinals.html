Rybakina Into Beijing QF in First WTA 1000 Campaign as World No. 1

By Marcus Cole October 3, 2026

A Dominant Run Continues

Elena Rybakina has looked every bit the World No. 1 since taking the ranking in September, and Saturday at the China Open was no exception. The Kazakh player swept past Xinyu Wang 6-2, 6-4 in 67 minutes to book her spot in the Beijing quarterfinals. Wang, playing in front of a home crowd, had the support of thousands of Chinese fans — but Rybakina's serve and groundstroke dominance left little room for the crowd to matter.

The win extends Rybakina's record at the tournament as she looks to add a WTA 1000 title to her 2026 Grand Slam collection. She won the US Open last month, defeating Aryna Sabalenka in the final 6-4, 5-7, 6-2. The China Open has been a happy hunting ground for top seeds in recent years, and Rybakina is playing exactly the kind of authoritative tennis that befits the No. 1 ranking.

A Historic Milestone

Rybakina's China Open campaign carries a particular significance. This is her first WTA 1000 quarterfinal appearance since becoming the world No. 1 on September 14 — the day she became the first Kazakh player in history, male or female, to hold a top ATP or WTA ranking. The milestone had been celebrated across Kazakhstan and the broader tennis world, and the Beijing quarterfinal is the first real test of whether she can perform to that standard at the highest non-Grand Slam level.

Her record as No. 1 has been encouraging. She has not dropped a set since taking the ranking. Her serve remains one of the most difficult in the women's game to break down — 183 km/h average first serve speed this week, with 73% first-serve percentage in the Wang match.

The Quarterfinal Draw

The China Open draw is full of familiar names heading into the last eight. Aryna Sabalenka, the No. 2 seed and Rybakina's recent US Open final opponent, is also in the tournament. A potential Rybakina-Sabalenka semifinal would be the WTA event of the week — a rematch of one of the best finals of 2026. Iga Swiatek, who has won the Beijing WTA title three times, is also advancing. Coco Gauff, who will reportedly make the semifinals, provides another blockbuster matchup possibility in the upper half of the draw.

Rybakina will learn her quarterfinal opponent once other Saturday matches conclude. The draw suggests a challenging path to the final — but nothing in her game this week suggests she is rattled by the names in the opposite corners of the bracket.

What's Next

Rybakina's quarterfinal is scheduled for Monday, October 5. A win would put her in the Beijing WTA semifinal with a potential Sabalenka or Swiatek encounter waiting. The China Open title carries significant WTA ranking points and would cement her position at No. 1 heading into the final stretch of the 2026 season. After a US Open title and this dominant start to the Asian swing, Rybakina looks capable of ending the year as the most dominant player in women's tennis in 2026.
