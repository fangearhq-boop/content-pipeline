Djokovic 32-0 in Beijing and Into the QF; Zverev Tests Home Hero Shang

By Elena Voss October 3, 2026

Djokovic Extends a Perfect Record

Novak Djokovic has played 32 matches at the China Open in Beijing. He has won all of them. Friday's second-round match against Chinese wild card Yunchaokete Bu pushed that mark to 32-0 — the kind of statistic that belongs more in mythology than sports history. The match itself required a comeback: Djokovic dropped the first set 4-6 and found himself in a tight tiebreak before closing out 7-6(2) and then running away with the third 6-2.

"What a match to be part of," Djokovic said afterwards — a quote that captured both the difficulty and the satisfaction of extending an already-improbable perfect record. Bu, ranked No. 104, was playing on home soil with a raucous crowd support. He deserved more from the set he took. But Djokovic at the China Open is a different animal than Djokovic anywhere else, and the Serbian six-time champion found his gear when it mattered.

Zverev Faces the Home Crowd Factor

Saturday brings another layer to the Beijing story. Alexander Zverev, the No. 1 seed and world No. 2, faces Juncheng Shang in the second round at Diamond Court. Shang is the kind of opponent who is difficult to game-plan against on neutral ground — and nearly impossible in front of a home crowd. The young Chinese player commands the kind of fervent home support that rattles visitors who aren't prepared for it.

Zverev is prepared. The German has been focused and efficient all week, knowing the stakes extend well beyond this tournament title. With Jannik Sinner confirming his Shanghai withdrawal on Saturday morning, Zverev now knows that winning Beijing and Shanghai back-to-back would make him World No. 1. Every match matters more than it did 24 hours ago. Shang is a test. Djokovic, potentially, is the test after that.

The Potential Quarterfinal

The China Open draw, set up before the tournament began, placed Djokovic and Zverev in the same half. Their potential quarterfinal — if both advance through their respective second-round matches — would be the marquee clash of the week in Beijing. They have met before on the tour, but rarely with this much riding on both sides.

For Djokovic, a quarterfinal win would put him three wins from a seventh Beijing title — a record extension in a city where he has never lost. For Zverev, a quarterfinal win over Djokovic would be a statement victory that makes the path to No. 1 feel very real. The quarterfinals are scheduled for Sunday, October 4, and Monday, October 5. The China Open does not lack for drama this weekend.

What's Next

Zverev's match against Shang on Saturday afternoon is the immediate focus. If the No. 1 seed advances, the Beijing quarterfinal bracket will be confirmed and the Djokovic-Zverev collision course becomes official. A Djokovic win over Zverev would put the returning Serbian champion into a semifinal against a player from the lower half — potentially Medvedev, Hurkacz, or Rublev, all of whom are alive in the draw. The China Open quarterfinal weekend is shaping up to be one of the best tennis days of the autumn season.
