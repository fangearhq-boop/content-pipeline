Federer Wins: Exhibition Night at Arthur Ashe Delivers Every Moment It Promised

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | August 26, 2026

Roger Federer beat Andy Roddick 6-3 in their singles set. Then he and John McEnroe beat Roddick and Andre Agassi 7-5 in doubles. Arthur Ashe Stadium was sold out. Anna Wintour performed the coin toss. Matthew McConaughey narrated the pre-match hype reel on the video board. Serena Williams, Felix Auger-Aliassime, Casper Ruud, and Alexandra Eala were in the crowd.

Tuesday night's exhibition — the centerpiece of 2026 US Open Fan Week — was billed as "An Icon Returns to New York," and it delivered. For two sets on the same stage where Federer won five US Open titles, the sport's retired greatest champion was back in competition gear, and the crowd at the largest tennis venue in the world was entirely present for every point.

The Singles Set: One More Win Over Roddick

The Federer–Roddick head-to-head was already 21-3 in Federer's favor heading into Tuesday night. It is now 22-3.

The singles set used no-ad scoring, meaning each game was decided on a single point at deuce. The format keeps things moving and prevents the extended baseline exchanges that defined their actual rivalries in the 2000s. Federer took the set 6-3, which — given the exhibition context and Roddick's age — represents exactly the kind of result that sends a crowd home satisfied.

The larger context of their rivalry was well understood in the arena. Roddick pushed Federer harder than almost anyone at Wimbledon: the 2009 final, where Federer won 16-14 in the fifth, is still cited among the finest Grand Slam finals ever played. None of that was on the line Tuesday night. The crowd did not need it to be.

The Doubles Set: Federer and McEnroe Prevail

The exhibition's second chapter was the doubles set: Federer partnering with John McEnroe against Roddick and Andre Agassi. The result was Federer/McEnroe 7-5.

The pairing of McEnroe alongside Federer — two players who dominated different eras at this exact venue — created combinations that produced genuine moments. McEnroe won three US Open singles titles here (1979, 1980, 1981). Federer won five (2004–2008). Agassi won two (1994, 1999). Roddick won one (2003). Between the four of them, twelve US Open titles shared the same court on a warm August night.

The Atmosphere

Before the match began, Matthew McConaughey narrated a video tribute shown on the Arthur Ashe scoreboard — a compilation of moments from Federer's career set against the backdrop of the stadium's history. When Federer walked onto the court, the standing ovation was immediate.

Anna Wintour, editor-in-chief of Vogue and a fixture in the celebrity row at Arthur Ashe, came onto the court to perform the coin toss. In the stands, several current tour players watched: Serena Williams, who had played her own mixed doubles earlier in the day; Felix Auger-Aliassime; Casper Ruud; and Alexandra Eala, who is seeded at the US Open for the first time this year.

What's Next

US Open Fan Week continues, but the exhibition is behind us now. The 2026 US Open singles draw is released today, Thursday August 27, at noon ET. The main draw begins August 30, headlined by No. 1 seed Alexander Zverev, No. 2 seed Carlos Alcaraz, and defending champion Coco Gauff. Federer's return to Arthur Ashe was a one-night event. The tournament that made his legacy begins in four days.
