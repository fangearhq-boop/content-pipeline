Sinner's Australian Open 2027 Return in Doubt as Surgery Option Remains Open

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | November 24, 2026

Tier 2 | New angle: 2026-11-23 Sinner season review — medical update

When Jannik Sinner ended his 2026 season in October, the message from his team was consistent: target the Australian Open in January, rest the right knee, and return healthy. That plan is now under pressure.

Fresh medical consultations have not ruled out surgery on Sinner's knee. His doctors — including a Lyon specialist initially consulted — originally recommended against an operation, preferring conservative treatment. But with roughly four months of non-surgical management behind him and the January 17 Australian Open start date approaching, the situation remains uncertain.

What Surgery Would Mean

If Sinner proceeds with surgery, the recovery timeline creates a serious problem for his Australian Open participation. A knee operation of the kind typically required for this type of inflammation and structural concern generally demands at minimum six to eight weeks of initial recovery before any on-court work can begin. That would take him past the AO start date with no match preparation.

The 2025 Australian Open was Sinner's first Grand Slam title. He won Wimbledon 2026 before the right knee deteriorated through the North American summer, ultimately forcing him out of the US Open in August and ending his season entirely in October.

The Ranking Implications

Sinner currently sits outside the year-end top 3 due to his abbreviated season, but his points from the 2026 Australian Open — which he reached the final of — remain on his record. If he misses AO 2027, he would forfeit the chance to defend those points, potentially affecting his ranking for the first half of the season.

At 25, Sinner is still in his prime. Missing one or even two Slams due to an injury managed carefully is preferable to rushing back and risking a more serious setback. His team has made clear they understand this. The question is not whether he can play through pain — it is whether playing in January is worth the medical risk.

What's Next

The timeline for a final medical decision is not public. Australian Open entry cutoffs mean Sinner will need to make a call in December. If he withdraws, he joins Rybakina (who missed the WTA Finals with illness) in a growing list of elite players managing health carefully through the off-season.

If he is cleared and plays, the AO draw becomes one of the most compelling in years — Sinner, Alcaraz, and Zverev would form a top three with legitimate title claims. That is a matchup tennis fans are hoping for. Whether the knee cooperates is not up to them.
