Mirra Andreeva Wins the 2026 WTA Finals — Youngest Champion Since Sharapova in 2004

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | November 24, 2026

Tier 2 | New Story — WTA Finals recap, Nov 8–15

When Mirra Andreeva lifted the 2026 WTA Finals trophy at Indian Wells on November 15, she did so without having dropped a single set all week. The 19-year-old Russian defeated Elina Svitolina 7-5, 6-3 in the final, capping a week of dominant tennis that confirmed what many had suspected was coming: Andreeva is ready to lead the next chapter of women's tennis.

The victory made Andreeva the youngest WTA Finals champion since Maria Sharapova won the event in 2004 — a 22-year gap. She is also the first player since Iga Świątek in 2023 to win the year-end championship without losing a set.

A Week Without Weakness

Andreeva moved through the Indian Wells field with the controlled aggression that has defined her season. Her fourth title of 2026, the WTA Finals win came against a difficult draw — including Svitolina, who had reached the final by defeating higher seeds.

The 7-5, 6-3 scoreline in the final was straightforward but it understated the quality of Andreeva's performance across the week. She served with unusual efficiency, converted breakpoints at a high rate, and showed the ability to raise her level in the moments that mattered — a characteristic that separates champions from contenders.

Year-End Rankings: Rybakina Holds No. 1

The year-end rankings offer a fuller picture of the 2026 women's season. Elena Rybakina finishes as world No. 1 with 8,099 points, despite withdrawing from the WTA Finals with illness — a measure of how dominant her regular-season results were. Andreeva ends the year at a career-high No. 2 with 7,806 points, just 293 behind. Aryna Sabalenka is No. 3 with 6,685 points.

The top three encapsulates the state of women's tennis: an established No. 1 in Rybakina, an ascendant No. 2 in Andreeva who may already be the more dangerous player in head-to-head matches, and Sabalenka — who won the US Open in 2025 and reached the Australian Open final in 2026 — refusing to cede ground.

Sharapova Comparisons

The Sharapova comparison is unavoidable given its historical weight, and Andreeva has handled it with maturity. Both are Russian, both broke through at a young age, and both displayed a mental toughness under pressure unusual for their years. Where Sharapova was defined by her serve and competitive ferocity, Andreeva's game is built around a flatter, penetrating groundstroke game — a modern baseline power player who can also construct points.

At 19, Andreeva has time to develop into something beyond any comparison. The WTA Finals, won without dropping a set, is a compelling argument that the development is already well advanced.

What's Next

Andreeva, Rybakina, and Sabalenka will all begin their 2027 seasons in January, with the Australian Open starting on January 17. Rybakina returns as the defending AO champion — having won the 2026 edition 6-4, 4-6, 6-4 over Andreeva in that final — which sets up a rematch dynamic that promises to run through the season.

Women's tennis enters 2027 with a genuinely open field at the top. Andreeva's WTA Finals performance showed she belongs among the favorites for every major. The question is whether this was the year she arrived or just the beginning.
