Davis Cup Finals 2026: Czech Republic Opens Against Injury-Hit Canada in Bologna

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | November 24, 2026

Tier 1 | Follow-Up: 2026-11-23 Davis Cup Finals preview

The 2026 Davis Cup Finals have arrived, and they opened Monday in Bologna with one of the week's most intriguing quarterfinal matchups: a Czech Republic side stacked with two top-15 players against a Canadian team missing two of its most recognizable stars.

The first quarterfinal at BolognaFiere pits Jiří Lehečká (ATP No. 14) and Jakub Menšík (ATP No. 15) against a Canadian squad depleted by injury. Denis Shapovalov and Gabriel Diallo are both sidelined, leaving Félix Auger-Aliassime to carry the load for a nation that has been among the sport's most dangerous Davis Cup nations in recent years.

Czech Republic's Loaded Lineup

Czech Republic enters the week as one of the dark horses for the title. Captain Tomáš Berdych — himself a former top-5 player and Davis Cup stalwart — has two of the ATP's most improved performers at his disposal.

Lehečká has established himself as one of the hardest servers on tour, capable of winning big matches on indoor hard courts, which is exactly what BolognaFiere provides. Menšík, still only 20, has completed a remarkable year, breaking into the top 15 and earning widespread recognition as the sport's most exciting emerging talent.

Together, they give the Czech Republic a formidable top two. The question is whether Auger-Aliassime, without his usual supporting cast, can find enough to prevent an upset.

Canada's Challenge Without Its Stars

Canada has been a Davis Cup force, reaching the Finals three times in the past four years. But Shapovalov's absence removes one of the most dynamic two-handed backhands in the sport, while Diallo had been in some of his best form before the injury ended his season prematurely.

Auger-Aliassime leads 3-1 in his head-to-head record against Lehečká, which offers Canada a realistic path in the singles rubbers. But covering both Day 1 singles matches without depth behind him is a significant ask, and Czech Republic's doubles pairing adds another layer of pressure.

The Bigger Picture: Italy's Championship Pursuit

While the Czech-Canada quarterfinal opens the week, the event's dominant storyline is Italy's chase for a fourth consecutive Davis Cup title — a feat that would be unprecedented in the modern format. The host nation faces South Korea in their quarterfinal on Tuesday, with the semifinal and final to follow over the weekend.

Jannik Sinner's absence through injury means Italy will be without their world No. 3, but the depth of the Italian squad has been tested in prior rounds and has held firm. Home court advantage in front of a passionate Bologna crowd will play its part.

Germany faces Great Britain and Spain meets Austria in the other two quarterfinals on Wednesday, completing a bracket that sets up potential heavyweight semifinal clashes.

What's Next

The Davis Cup Finals continue through Sunday, November 29. Semifinals take place November 27 and 28, with the final on November 29. If Italy advance, they will play their semifinal at home in front of what promises to be a full house at BolognaFiere.

Czech Republic's Czech fans will be watching closely: a win today would put them in the semifinals for only the second time in the past decade. Canada, however, has shown the ability to overperform when depleted — and Auger-Aliassime in must-win situations is never an easy opponent.
