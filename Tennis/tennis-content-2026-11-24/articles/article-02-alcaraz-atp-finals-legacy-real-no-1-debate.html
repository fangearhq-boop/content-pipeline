Alcaraz Joins Federer, Agassi, and Djokovic — but Zverev Still Keeps No. 1

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | November 24, 2026

Tier 1 | Follow-Up: 2026-11-23 Alcaraz wins ATP Finals + Historic season review

When Carlos Alcaraz hit the final ball of Sunday's ATP Finals championship match in Turin — and then anxiously checked whether it was in — it capped more than just a title. It completed one of professional tennis's most exclusive career résumés.

Alcaraz is now the fourth player in ATP history to hold a career Grand Slam and the ATP Finals title simultaneously. The list before him: Andre Agassi (1999), Roger Federer (2003), and Novak Djokovic (2015). Rafael Nadal, with 22 Grand Slams to his name, never won the ATP Finals. Alcaraz has done it at 23.

The Trophy vs. the Ranking

The day after the win, the tennis world found itself in a philosophical debate the sport occasionally generates and rarely resolves neatly.

Alexander Zverev is the year-end world No. 1. He won Roland Garros and the US Open — two Grand Slams in one season — and accumulated enough points to comfortably top the year-end standings. By every statistical measure the ATP uses to determine its champion, Zverev was the best player of 2026.

Alcaraz ends the year ranked No. 4. He also won six titles this season, went undefeated in Turin with a 5-0 record — not dropping a single set until the final — and beat Zverev in that final 6-2, 7-6(9-7), saving three set points in the second-set tiebreak before closing it out.

When asked at his post-match press conference whether he considered himself the "real No. 1," Alcaraz was gracious but clear: the win was "special," he said, describing it as redemption after losing to Sinner in last year's final. The question lingered longer than his answer did.

A Historic Four

The significance of Alcaraz's entry into the Agassi-Federer-Djokovic club deserves a moment's consideration. The ATP Finals is not a Grand Slam — it carries no ranking points from the standard calendar — but it is widely regarded as the sport's fifth major, the definitive test of who performed best across the entire season.

To win it and also hold victories at all four Grand Slams places a player in a category defined entirely by sustained excellence across surfaces, formats, and pressure levels. That the club now has four members after Alcaraz's Turin victory — and that Alcaraz is the youngest of them — adds a generational dimension to the achievement.

Alcaraz won the French Open in 2024, Wimbledon in 2024, the US Open in 2025, and the Australian Open in 2026, completing the career Grand Slam. He added the ATP Finals title on Sunday.

Into the Off-Season

Alcaraz mentioned managing a left leg concern during the tournament, acknowledging post-final that he "felt it a little" but felt "just okay." The absence of Sinner — out since October with a knee injury — meant the question of who truly dominates the sport heading into 2027 is unresolved.

Zverev, Alcaraz, and a returning Sinner (health permitting) represent the most compelling top three in men's tennis in years. With the Australian Open starting January 17, the off-season will be short.

What's Next

The Australian Open 2027 begins January 17 in Melbourne. Alcaraz will be the defending champion. Zverev will carry the year-end No. 1 ranking into the draw. Sinner's participation remains uncertain pending a decision on his right knee. If all three are healthy and present, the men's draw will be one of the most anticipated in years.

For now, Alcaraz rests with his ATP Finals trophy and his place in a four-man club that none of tennis's greatest names could enter. That is not nothing — whatever the rankings say.
