Korpatsch Ends 34-Year Wait — First German to Win Hamburg Since Steffi Graf

By Elena Voss July 27, 2026

A Title 34 Years in the Making

Tamara Korpatsch won the MSC Hamburg Ladies Open on Sunday, defeating Anna Bondar 6-3, 6-3 in one hour and 28 minutes at the Rothenbaum clay. The result carries a piece of history that makes it stand apart from a standard WTA 500 title. Korpatsch became the first German player to win the Hamburg title since Steffi Graf in 1992 — a gap of 34 years.

Graf won Hamburg six consecutive times between 1987 and 1992, and after she moved on, no German player added to that count. It stayed that way for more than three decades. On Sunday, in front of her home crowd, Korpatsch ended the wait.

A Wildcard Win at Home

The circumstances surrounding the title amplify its meaning. Korpatsch, 31 and born in Hamburg, entered the tournament as a wildcard. She was ranked approximately No. 95 heading into the week — a number that would typically make a run through a WTA 500 draw improbable. She did not find it improbable.

Her path to the final included a semifinal that came through injury default — top contender Mayar Sherif, who had dominated the week and beaten Paula Badosa in the quarterfinals, retired against Korpatsch at 6-1, 2-0 with an injury. The retirement handed Korpatsch the match without a full contest. But she still had to win the final against Bondar, and she did so cleanly, dropping a total of six games across two sets in 88 minutes.

The Ranking Impact

The Hamburg title takes Korpatsch into the WTA top 50 for the first time in her career. Her previous career high was No. 71, set in October 2023. She now has two career WTA titles — the first came at the 2023 Transylvania Open — and she enters the North American hard-court swing with a singles record of 5-0 against Bondar and a ranking she has never previously reached.

For Bondar, Sunday was her first WTA Tour singles final appearance. The Hungarian, ranked around No. 58, had survived a three-hour-and-29-minute semifinal against Elina Avanesyan to reach the title match. She could not find the same level against Korpatsch.

What's Next

The Hamburg title is the last clay-court result of the 2026 European season. Korpatsch transitions to hard courts with the North American swing now underway. The DC Open in Washington D.C. runs July 27–August 2, followed by the Canadian Open in Toronto (Aug 1–11) and Cincinnati before the US Open. Whether Korpatsch's clay-court form carries across surfaces remains to be seen, but a WTA 500 title — the first German Hamburg victory in 34 years — gives her a result that resets what is expected of her wherever she plays next.
