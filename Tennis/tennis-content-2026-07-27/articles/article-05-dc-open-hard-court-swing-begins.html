DC Open Begins Monday — North American Hard Court Swing Opens in Washington

By Elena Voss July 27, 2026

The Season Pivots

Sunday produced the final clay-court results of the European summer season. Luca Van Assche won in Estoril; Tamara Korpatsch won in Hamburg; Lilli Tagger won in Prague. Four champions were crowned across four countries over a day and a half of tennis. Then Monday arrived, and the surface changed.

The Mubadala Citi DC Open — a combined ATP 500 and WTA 500 event at the William H.G. FitzGerald Tennis Center in Washington D.C. — opened its first round on Monday. The tournament runs through August 2. It is the first hard-court event of the North American swing, beginning a sequence that leads into the Canadian Open and eventually the US Open, which begins August 24.

The Field in Washington

On the men's side, Taylor Fritz enters as one of the top seeds at the ATP 500 level. Frances Tiafoe — a Washington area native who has drawn large home crowds at this tournament in previous years — is in the draw and is one of the most watched players as the American hard-court season begins.

On the women's side, Alex Eala of the Philippines comes into the tournament at a career-high ranking of No. 29, the result of a strong grass-court campaign earlier in the summer. The DC Open is an early opportunity for her to test whether that form carries onto hard courts.

Players who competed in the clay events that closed Sunday will not be in the Washington field this week — the schedule does not allow for it. DC draws largely from players who skipped the final clay week, giving them an extra week on hard courts before the Canadian Open.

The Surface Transition

The shift from clay to hard courts is one of the more meaningful transitions in the tennis calendar. Clay is slower, higher-bouncing, and rewards baseline patience; hard courts are faster and demand sharper footwork and a more aggressive first strike. Players who have dominated the clay swing often look for adjustments that do not always come immediately.

Van Assche, Korpatsch, and Tagger — three of Sunday's clay champions — will all face the hard-court surface at some point in the coming weeks. For Tagger at 18, the first hard-court events of her professional career as a top-50 player will be significant data points. For Van Assche at No. 48, the Canadian Open draw on July 31 will reveal what hard-court opponents and seedings he faces next.

What's Next

The Mubadala Citi DC Open runs through August 2. Quarterfinals are expected around Thursday; the finals are Sunday. Both the ATP and WTA Canadian Open draws are released the same day the DC Open finals are played — Friday, July 31 — which means the first week of hard courts in Washington overlaps directly with the buildup to the first Masters 1000 and WTA 1000 events of the summer. For players in both draws, this week is part preparation and part opportunity to earn ranking points before the bigger stages arrive.
