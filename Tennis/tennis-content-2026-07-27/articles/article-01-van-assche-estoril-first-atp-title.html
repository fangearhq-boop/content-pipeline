Van Assche Wins Estoril, Claims First ATP Title at 22

By Ryan Calloway July 27, 2026

A First Title in Three Sets

Luca Van Assche won his first ATP Tour title on Sunday, defeating Alexander Blockx 6-4, 4-6, 7-5 in the Millennium Estoril Open final in Portugal. The match lasted two hours and 24 minutes. Van Assche, 22, who entered the tournament ranked No. 65, rose to a new career high of No. 48 — his first time in the top 50.

Neither player had reached an ATP senior final before Sunday. Van Assche had never won a Tour title. Blockx, 21, from Antwerp, Belgium, was also competing in his first final. The Estoril draw closed with an all-unseeded championship match — a rarity at any Tour level, and a reflection of the week both players had assembled.

The Match

Van Assche took the opening set 6-4, dictating from the baseline in the style that carried him through the week. Blockx adjusted and won the second set by the same scoreline, leveling the match and forcing a deciding third. The final set tightened as both players held their ground, until Van Assche moved through 7-5 to close it out.

The road to the final was notable on its own. Van Assche beat top seed Andrey Rublev in the quarterfinals — a win he described as the biggest of his career — before defeating fellow Frenchman Hugo Gaston in an all-French semifinal. Blockx earned his own place in the final by coming back from 5-7, 0-5 in the third set against Luciano Darderi in the semifinals, winning seven straight games to force and win two tiebreaks.

Blockx: The Runner-Up With a Long Runway

Blockx reached his first ATP final at 21 having already built a notable career resume. He won the 2023 Australian Open junior title and reached world No. 1 in the ITF junior rankings before turning professional. In May 2026, he reached the Madrid Masters 1000 semifinals — only the third Belgian player to reach an ATP Masters 1000 semi — and climbed to a career high of No. 36.

He entered Estoril ranked approximately No. 69, and he pushed Van Assche to three sets in a final where neither player was expected to be by many observers at the start of the week. He will have more finals in front of him.

What's Next

Van Assche carries his new career high of No. 48 into the North American hard-court swing. The DC Open in Washington D.C. is underway this week, followed by the Canadian Open (ATP 1000) in Montreal from August 4–13, and the Cincinnati Open before the US Open begins August 24. A first ATP 250 title does not automatically translate to results on a faster surface, but Van Assche enters the swing with something he did not have before Sunday: the confidence of a player who has won at Tour level when it mattered. Blockx will also begin his hard-court season in Washington or Montreal, depending on his schedule, carrying the experience of a first Tour final at 21.
