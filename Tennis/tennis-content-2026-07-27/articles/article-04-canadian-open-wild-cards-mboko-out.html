Canadian Open Wild Cards Set — Zhao Gets Farewell, Mboko Out With Knee Injury

By Ryan Calloway July 27, 2026

The Wild Card Decisions

Tennis Canada confirmed the final wild cards for the 2026 National Bank Open women's field in Toronto this weekend. Carol Zhao, 31, a Canadian ranked No. 406 in the world, received one of the remaining spots. Ariana Arseneault, from Richmond Hill, Ontario, received the other. The announcement completes a wild card group that also includes Bianca Andreescu, Venus Williams, Kayla Cross, Katherine Sebov, Cadence Brace, and Rebecca Marino.

Zhao's wild card carries a specific weight. She turned professional in 2016 and has spent a decade on the WTA Tour. She has confirmed this is her final season as a professional. The wild card gives her a home farewell at Sobeys Stadium in Toronto, in front of a Canadian tennis fan base that has watched Canadian women's tennis rise significantly over the past decade. For a player at No. 406 in the rankings who has given ten years to the sport, this is the kind of send-off that matters.

Mboko Out With Knee Injury

Victoria Mboko, 19, currently ranked around No. 10 in the world, will not play in Toronto. She withdrew with a medial collateral ligament injury in her left knee, suffered during a match against Karolina Pliskova at the HSBC Championships in London. Mboko is the defending champion — she won the 2025 National Bank Open — and her absence removes one of the most interesting storylines the tournament would have had.

She has confirmed she will attend the tournament in person despite being unable to compete. That means the crowd in Toronto will see her, just not on court. The injury is a significant blow for a 19-year-old who had established herself as one of the best players in women's tennis.

The Men's Field in Montreal

The men's draw in Montreal (Aug 4–13) continues to absorb the impact of Jannik Sinner's withdrawal, confirmed last week due to health concerns, joining Novak Djokovic and Carlos Alcaraz on the sidelines. Sinner is the world No. 1 and has been one of the dominant forces in ATP tennis in 2026. Alcaraz has been sidelined since April with a wrist injury. Djokovic cited a reduced schedule.

Alexander Zverev is expected to be the top seed in Montreal. Ben Shelton, the defending Montreal champion, is in the field. Felix Auger-Aliassime plays in his home tournament. The depth of the draw is strong even without the three withdrawals — 71 of the ATP's top 72 players have entered — but the absence of the top three names in the sport from a Masters 1000 event is a notable gap.

What's Next

Both the men's and women's Canadian Open draws are scheduled to be released on Friday, July 31. First matches for the women in Toronto begin Saturday, August 1; the men in Montreal begin Tuesday, August 4. The DC Open in Washington D.C. is underway this week as players warm up on hard courts before Canada. Whatever the draws produce on Friday will shape the first two weeks of the North American hard-court swing heading toward the US Open on August 24.
