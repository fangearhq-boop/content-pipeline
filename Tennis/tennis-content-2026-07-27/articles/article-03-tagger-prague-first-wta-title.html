Tagger Wins Prague at 18 — First WTA Title, Youngest Player in WTA Top 50

By Marcus Cole July 27, 2026

The Comeback Final

Lilli Tagger won her first WTA Tour singles title on Sunday at the Livesport Prague Open, defeating Daria Snigur 7-6(3), 6-2 in a final that had to be earned the hard way. Tagger, 18, from Lienz, Austria, fell behind 2-5 in the opening set before winning five straight games to level the match, then won the tiebreak 7-3 and ran through the second set 6-2.

The result makes Tagger the youngest player in the WTA top 50, rising to approximately No. 45. She is only the second teenager — after Mirra Andreeva — to win a WTA Tour singles title in 2026.

Who Tagger Is

The 18-year-old Austrian has drawn attention for her playing style as much as her results. She plays with a one-handed backhand, a technique that is uncommon at the professional level and vanishingly rare on the women's tour. She is coached by Francesca Schiavone — the Italian who won the 2010 French Open — at Schiavone's academy in Varese, Italy.

Tagger won the 2025 French Open junior title before turning professional. Earlier this week in Prague she upset No. 2 seed Barbora Krejcikova in the semifinals — coming back from a set down in a 2-hour-50-minute match — before Sunday's final against Snigur. The week's work confirms that her junior results were not a ceiling but a floor.

Snigur's Final

Daria Snigur, 24, of Ukraine, had reached the Prague final as the No. 8 seed after defeating Valentova in the semifinals. Snigur, who entered the week ranked around No. 77, built the 5-2 lead in the opening set that looked like it would carry her to the title. Tagger's recovery from that position — winning five straight games, controlling the tiebreak, and then outplaying Snigur entirely in the second set — showed what the Austrian is capable of when she finds her rhythm on clay. It was also a reminder that Snigur has been one of the consistent performers in the lower half of the WTA top 100 since her 2022 US Open upset of Halep, without yet breaking into a higher tier.

What's Next

Tagger enters the North American hard-court swing as a first-time WTA champion at 18. The DC Open in Washington D.C. runs through August 2; the Canadian Open in Toronto begins August 1. It is worth noting that Prague is a clay-court event, and Tagger's trajectory on hard courts is still being established at the Tour level. But the ability to come back from 2-5 down in a final against an experienced opponent, and to close out a tiebreak and a second set without losing momentum, suggests a player who can compete under pressure regardless of surface. The French Open in 2027 will be a significant test of just how far the junior champion and current Prague champion has developed.
