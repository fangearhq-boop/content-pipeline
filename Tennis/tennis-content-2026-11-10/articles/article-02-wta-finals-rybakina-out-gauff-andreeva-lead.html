WTA Finals Day 1–2: Rybakina Out, Gauff and Andreeva Lead Their Groups

By Marcus Cole | November 10, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

The 2026 FAGE WTA Finals in Indian Wells lost its headline act before a ball was struck. Elena Rybakina, the world No. 1 and defending champion, withdrew from the tournament for medical reasons on the eve of the event. What followed anyway was two days of compelling tennis that set up an open and unpredictable round-robin.

Rybakina Out, Kostyuk In

Rybakina said she was "not physically ready to compete" and withdrew from the event before her scheduled Day 1 match in Group 1. Marta Kostyuk stepped in as the first alternate, taking Rybakina's place in the draw.

The absence of the defending champion reshapes the Group 1 picture entirely. Rybakina had been seen as the most likely Group 1 winner, and her absence opens the door for Coco Gauff, Jessica Pegula, and Linda Noskova to control their own destinies. For Kostyuk, the opportunity is unexpected and welcome.

Day 1: Gauff Wins, Noskova Wins

Group 1 opened on Sunday, November 8, with Coco Gauff taking on fellow American Jessica Pegula. It was a tight, absorbing contest. Gauff took the first set 6-4, lost the second in a tiebreak 6-7(5), and then pulled clear in the decider, 6-2, to win her opening match.

The other Day 1 match in Group 1 saw Linda Noskova, the Czech 23-year-old who qualified for the Finals for the first time, dispatch Kostyuk 6-2, 6-4. Noskova has been one of the most improved players on tour in 2026, and she showed why with a commanding performance in her debut.

Day 2: Andreeva Steals the Show

Group 2 came to life on Monday, November 9. Aryna Sabalenka opened her campaign by beating Karolina Muchova 7-6(2), 7-5 in a match that required patience and precision. Sabalenka, the two-time US Open champion, is typically reliable at this stage of the season, and she delivered when it counted.

Then came Mirra Andreeva. The 20-year-old Russian, making her WTA Finals debut, beat Elina Svitolina 6-4, 6-0. The numbers do not quite capture how dominant she was. Andreeva won the final seven games of the match without dropping a single one. After a tight opening set, she simply ran through Svitolina in the second, closing it out with authority.

Andreeva qualified for the Finals for the first time in 2026 after a breakthrough year that included significant results at Slams and Masters events. Her performance in Indian Wells suggests the stage is not too big for her.

Group Standings After Two Days

Group 1 (after Day 1): Gauff — 1 win; Noskova — 1 win; Pegula — 0 wins; Kostyuk — 0 wins.

Group 2 (after Day 2): Sabalenka — 1 win; Andreeva — 1 win; Svitolina — 0 wins; Muchova — 0 wins.

Each player in the round-robin plays three matches, and the top two from each group advance to the semifinals. With two wins and two losses in Group 1 distributed after one round of play, every match from here matters.

What's Next

The round-robin continues through November 13, with semifinals scheduled for November 14 and the final on November 15. The WTA Finals prize pool stands at $15.5 million, and the title is up for grabs with the defending champion already watching from the sidelines. Any of the remaining seven players can make a case for the title. That is exactly the kind of wide-open field that makes the year-end Finals worth watching.
