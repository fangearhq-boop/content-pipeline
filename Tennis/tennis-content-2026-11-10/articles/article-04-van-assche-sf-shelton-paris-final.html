Van Assche's Paris Fairy Tale Ends in the Semifinals — but the Future Is Bright

By Elena Voss | November 10, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

The noise inside Accor Arena in Nanterre had been building all week. First the upset of the 15th seed. Then the win over the man who had shocked the world No. 1. Then the quarterfinal. By the time Luca Van Assche walked out for his Paris Masters semifinal against Ben Shelton, the crowd was operating on a frequency normally reserved for Grand Slam week. This was French tennis at its most alive.

The fairy tale ended there. Shelton beat Van Assche 5-7, 6-3, 6-4, rallying from a set down to reach the Paris Masters final — the first American to do so since Jack Sock won the title in 2017. But the numbers on a scoreboard miss the point. Van Assche's week in Paris was something far bigger than a semifinal exit.

Shelton's Rally

Van Assche took the first set, riding his serve and the energy of a home crowd that treated every winner as a national triumph. Shelton, the third seed and one of the most powerful players on tour, had to weather the storm before finding his level in the second set.

The American's serve — which produced 19 aces in his quarterfinal win over Tommy Paul — eventually became the deciding factor. Once Shelton began landing first serves consistently in the second and third sets, Van Assche had fewer opportunities to dictate from the baseline. Shelton closed the third set out 6-4 to advance to his sixth final of 2026.

The Run That Defined the Week

Van Assche came into the Paris Masters as a wildcard. He had a ranking that did not qualify him directly for the main draw, so he was given an invitation — a gesture of goodwill to a local player with potential.

What followed was not what anyone scripted. He beat Mariano Navone in the first round. Then he beat Learner Tien, the 15th seed, 7-6(4), 6-4, saving nine break points along the way. In the round of 16, he beat Lorenzo Musetti — the same Musetti who had just shocked the world No. 1 Alexander Zverev two days earlier. And in the quarterfinals, he edged Rinky Hijikata 6-4, 6-7(4), 7-5.

Four wins. All at Accor Arena. All in front of a crowd that grew louder with each round. Van Assche is 21 years old, and this was the greatest week of his career by any measure.

What It Means for French Tennis

The last French player to reach the Paris Masters singles final was Jo-Wilfried Tsonga. The last French player to win it was Yannick Noah, and that was in 1983. Van Assche did not win the title, but his run to the last four on a wildcard has done something important: it has given the French tennis public a name to believe in again.

At 21, he has time, talent, and now, a defining week in his memory. The Paris crowd gave him energy he had never experienced at this level. He gave them a reason to believe the next era of French tennis is not a distant promise but a present reality.

What's Next

Luca Van Assche has no immediate obligation with the Paris defeat. His ranking will climb significantly after this result, and he will return to the tour with a credibility he earned in the most visible arena in French tennis. The next French player to reach a Paris Masters final will need to earn it the same way Van Assche did — through results. He has shown it is possible. That matters for everyone who comes after him.
