ATP Finals Turin Preview: Alcaraz Arrives on Fire With Five Days to Go

By Ryan Calloway | November 10, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

Five days. That is all that separates the tennis world from the Nitto ATP Finals in Turin, and for the first time in years, the storylines heading into the year-end championship feel genuinely electric. The eight-player field is complete, the draw is imminent, and one player has separated himself from the conversation about favorites in a way that is hard to argue with.

The Man to Beat: Carlos Alcaraz

Carlos Alcaraz arrives in Turin having won five titles in 2026, the last two in consecutive weeks. He won in Vienna on November 2 and followed it with the Rolex Paris Masters title on November 8. Eleven wins across two tournaments. Zero sets lost over those two title runs. The form is unprecedented even by his own exceptional standards.

At 23, Alcaraz is on the cusp of winning the ATP Finals for the first time. He has the year-end No. 2 ranking, he is the class of the field on current form, and he has the serve and groundstroke combination to handle any surface. Turin's indoor hard courts suit his game. It would be a genuine surprise if he does not reach the final.

Zverev Needs Redemption

Alexander Zverev will enter the Inalpi Arena as the year-end world No. 1 — a distinction he has never held before. He clinched it in October, long before Paris. But what happened in Paris leaves a question mark.

Zverev lost in the second round to Lorenzo Musetti, 7-6(5), 6-2 — the fifth No. 1 seed in ATP history to lose his opening match as the top-ranked player, and the first to do so in straight sets. He is heading into Turin with a loss in his most recent match, which is not how the world No. 1 wants to arrive at the year's biggest indoor event.

That said, Zverev won the ATP Finals in 2021 and finished as runner-up in 2022. He knows this stage, and he knows he is capable of winning it. If he is at his best — powerful serve, aggressive baseline play — he is a genuine threat to Alcaraz.

Shelton, Medvedev, and the Rest

Ben Shelton goes directly from Sunday's Paris Masters final — where he lost to Alcaraz 7-6(5), 7-5 — to Turin. That is a remarkable week, with minimal rest between events. He is the first American to reach the Paris final since Jack Sock won the title in 2017, and he arrives in Italy with his profile at an all-time high. Whether the quick turnaround affects him physically remains to be seen.

Daniil Medvedev is the dark horse the field will not overlook. He won the ATP Finals in 2020 and reached the final in 2024. Indoor hard courts remain his best surface, and when Medvedev is focused and moving well, he is capable of upsetting anyone in the draw.

Rounding out the eight: Flavio Cobolli, Arthur Fils, Frances Tiafoe, and Rafael Jodar. The last two carry their own narratives heading into Turin.

Tiafoe and Jodar: The Debutants

Frances Tiafoe has been knocking on the door of the ATP Finals for years. At 29, he makes his first appearance at the year-end championship in 2026, and the American crowd — even in Italy — will be rooting for him. Tiafoe's game, built on athleticism and shot-making, can produce magic on any given day.

Rafael Jodar wrote history just by getting to Turin. He became the first player in ATP history to qualify for the ATP Finals in his debut year on tour, a feat that required a remarkable consistency across 2026 that few expected from a first-year player. At 22, he is the youngest player in the field.

What's Next

The Nitto ATP Finals draw is expected shortly. The event runs November 15–22 at the Inalpi Arena in Turin. As always, the round-robin format means every match matters from day one — a loss can be survived, but two puts a player in serious jeopardy. With Alcaraz in the field and in this form, every other player in Turin knows they will need to produce something special.
