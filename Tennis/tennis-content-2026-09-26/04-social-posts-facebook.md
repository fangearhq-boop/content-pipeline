### STORY 2: Singapore Open — Prozorova Controversy

**Long-Form Post — 8:00 AM ET**
Talia Gibson is into the Singapore Open final — but the way she got there has sparked a major controversy involving the WTA's concussion protocol.

Gibson's semifinal opponent, Tatiana Prozorova, was forced to withdraw on Saturday after the WTA physio applied the concussion protocol. The trigger: Prozorova was struck in the face by a ball during a doubles quarterfinal match on Friday. Her balance test results came back poor, and the WTA ruled she could not play Saturday's singles semifinal.

But Prozorova is fighting back. She posted publicly that the ball grazed her cheek and hit her shoulder — and that her poor balance-test results were the result of playing three matches of three or more hours on three consecutive days, not a head injury. She says she was pressured into withdrawing against her will. The WTA issued a statement defending the protocol.

This puts the WTA in a difficult position: the protocol exists to protect player safety, but when a player publicly disputes the diagnosis and says she felt fine, the optics get complicated.

Meanwhile, Gibson — a qualifier from Australia who beat Sakkari to get to the semis — is in her first-ever WTA final on Sunday. She faces the winner of Leylah Fernandez vs. Maja Chwalińska. A victory would be a remarkable story for a player who arrived in Singapore unseeded.

What do you think — was the WTA right to enforce the concussion protocol, or should a player have more say in that decision?

### STORY 3: Hangzhou Open QF

No. 1 seed Daniil Medvedev's Round of 16 result against Valentin Royer was not fully confirmed at time of writing. With most top-10 ATP players in London for Laver Cup, the Hangzhou title is genuinely up for grabs — and Jacquet looks like a real darkhorse.

### STORY 5: China Open Preview

**Long-Form Post — 6:00 PM ET**
Four days from now, the China Open gets underway in Beijing — and for the first time in a while, the ATP 500 title race is genuinely wide open.

Jannik Sinner, who was the defending champion having beaten Learner Tien 6-2, 6-2 in last year's final, officially withdrew this week citing his ongoing knee issue. He's the world No. 1, but he hasn't played competitively since Wimbledon in July.

With Sinner out, the door opens for players like Daniil Medvedev (who could be fresh from Hangzhou) and Alexander Zverev (who'll be finishing up Laver Cup duties in London). The draw drops on September 28.

On the women's side, the China Open is a WTA 1000 event, and newly crowned World No. 1 Elena Rybakina leads the field. Rybakina took the top ranking from Sabalenka earlier this month and has confirmed her Beijing entry. The China Open is also a huge points event in the Race to the WTA Finals in Riyadh.

Beijing is always one of the most competitive weeks of the Asian swing. This year, with Sinner absent, it might also be the most dramatic. Who are you backing to win in Beijing? 🎾
