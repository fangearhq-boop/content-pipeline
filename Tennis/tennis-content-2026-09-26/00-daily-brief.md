### STORY 2: Singapore Open — Prozorova Walkover Controversy Puts Gibson in Final
**Type:** FOLLOW-UP (Sep 25 S3: QF results → dramatic SF development: Prozorova WD via concussion protocol, controversy erupts)
**Tier:** 1 (Major WTA controversy involving player welfare protocol; qualifier Gibson reaches her first WTA final)
**Byline:** Elena Voss
**Angle:** Australian qualifier Talia Gibson is into the WTA 500 Singapore Open final after semifinal opponent Tatiana Prozorova was forced to withdraw under the concussion protocol, triggering a public dispute between the Russian and the WTA. Prozorova was struck in the face by a ball during her doubles quarterfinal match on Friday. The WTA physio applied the Concussion Protocol, and after assessing Prozorova's balance test results, ruled she could not play Saturday's singles semifinal. Prozorova publicly blasted the decision, posting that the ball had only grazed her cheek and hit her shoulder, and that her poor balance-test results were due to fatigue from three-plus-hour matches across three consecutive days — not a head injury. She claims the WTA physio pressured her into withdrawing against her will. The WTA issued a statement defending the protocol. Gibson — an Australian qualifier who had already beaten Maria Sakkari in the quarterfinals — now waits for Sunday's final against the winner of the other SF: Leylah Fernandez (Canada) vs. Maja Chwalińska (Poland), which was scheduled for Saturday evening. Gibson would be a first-time WTA titlist if she wins Sunday.
