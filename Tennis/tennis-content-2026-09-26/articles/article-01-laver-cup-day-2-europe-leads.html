Team Europe Leads 3-1 After Laver Cup Day 1, Day 2 High-Stakes Matches Loom

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | September 26, 2026

Team Europe arrived at Day 2 of the 2026 Laver Cup in control, holding a 3-1 lead over Team World following Friday's opening session at The O2 in London. But the match is far from decided — Saturday's Day 2 assigns two points for each win, meaning the entire event can flip in a single afternoon.

Europe's lead was built on a combination of grit and star power. Casper Ruud opened the day session with a hard-fought victory over Francisco Cerúndolo, dropping the first set in a tiebreak before rallying to win 6-7(4), 6-4, [10-8] in a super-tiebreak. Rafael Jódar was far more clinical in the night session's opening match, dismantling Alexander Bublik 6-2, 6-3 in a Laver Cup debut that will be remembered for its dominance. The doubles pairing of Carlos Alcaraz and Jakub Menšík then delivered for Europe, defeating Taylor Fritz and Bublik 6-4, 6-4 to wrap up Day 1 with three of the four available points.

Team World's lone bright spot was Brandon Nakashima. The American arrived in London as something of an unknown commodity on the Laver Cup stage, but he delivered a composed, authoritative performance against Menšík — winning 6-1, 3-6, [10-7] — to at least give World a foothold heading into Day 2.

What Day 2 Means — and What's at Stake

With the format shifting to double points on Saturday, World's path back into the match runs through this session. Four singles and doubles matchups are scheduled, and winning all four would give Team World 8 more points, bringing the overall score to 9-11 in Europe's favor heading into Day 3, where each win is worth 3 points.

The Day 2 lineup presents some of the marquee matchups of the entire event. Alexander Zverev, who completed the greatest season of his career with the Roland Garros and US Open double, faces Alex de Minaur (Australia) — an opponent with plenty of motivation to perform for a Team World side that needs wins now. Later, Carlos Alcaraz meets Taylor Fritz in a match that carries enormous weight for both teams.

The day opens with Flavio Cobolli (Italy, Europe) taking on Learner Tien (United States, World) — a clash of two players whose Laver Cup records will be shaped significantly by this week. And the evening session doubles pits Ruud and Zverev (Europe) against Bublik and Nakashima (World), in what promises to be a high-energy closer.

Europe's Tradition vs. World's Hunger

Team Europe has won the Laver Cup eight of the nine times it has been held since the inaugural event in 2017. Last year in San Francisco, Team World claimed a 15-9 victory — their first Cup triumph — led by Ben Shelton, who has since missed this edition due to injury. That win made Worlds' defending status all the more significant heading into a home-crowd advantage weekend in London, which historically favors Europe.

The team compositions reflect the current balance of power on tour. Europe's roster includes Zverev and Alcaraz, who hold the top two spots in the Race to the ATP Finals. World features de Minaur, Fritz, Tien, Nakashima, and Bublik — a group hungry to prove the 2025 triumph was no fluke.

What's Next

Day 2 matches take place Saturday at The O2 in London. Day 3 — the Cup's final session — is scheduled for Sunday, September 27. Each Day 3 win is worth 3 points. The first team to reach 13 points wins the Cup. Following the event, key players in the ATP field turn their attention to the China Open (Beijing, starting September 30), where Sinner's withdrawal creates an open field for the ATP 500 title.
