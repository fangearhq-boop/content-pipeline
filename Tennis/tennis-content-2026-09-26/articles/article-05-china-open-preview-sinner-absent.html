China Open Preview: Sinner Out, Rybakina Leads, and a Wide-Open ATP Field in Beijing

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | September 26, 2026

The 2026 China Open arrives in four days — a dual ATP 500 and WTA 1000 event that annually draws one of the strongest combined fields of the autumn swing. This year, it does so without the most significant name in men's tennis: world No. 1 Jannik Sinner, who officially withdrew Friday citing his knee issue, will not be in Beijing to defend his title.

Sinner won in Beijing in 2025, defeating Learner Tien 6-2, 6-2 in the final to claim the ATP 500 crown. He hasn't played competitively since Wimbledon in July, and his withdrawal from China is now the second consecutive Asian swing event he'll miss, following his earlier pull-out from preliminary consideration. His team has not confirmed a return date, though the Shanghai Masters (starting October 5) and Vienna Open (October 26) have been cited as possible timelines.

Who Fills the Void on the ATP Side?

With Sinner absent, the ATP 500 title opens up meaningfully. Daniil Medvedev is one of the strongest candidates — he's currently in Hangzhou's quarterfinals and could arrive in Beijing with match sharpness and momentum. Alexander Zverev, fresh from Laver Cup duties that conclude Sunday, is also expected to be in the Beijing draw, though his fitness entering the week will need monitoring after a demanding weekend in London.

The draw is expected to be released on Sunday, September 28. Until then, the seedings remain speculative, though the field is expected to feature several top-15 ATP players motivated by the Race to the ATP Finals in Turin.

Sinner currently sits second in the Race to Turin at 9,630 points, comfortably in the field for the season-ending event. His absence from Beijing does not threaten that standing, but it does mean he arrives in November with months of missed match play to overcome if he is to compete for the end-of-year No. 1 ranking.

Rybakina Leads a WTA 1000 Field

On the women's side, Elena Rybakina enters the China Open as the newly installed world No. 1. Rybakina took the top ranking from Aryna Sabalenka earlier this month after her US Open victory, and confirmed her Beijing entry weeks ago. The WTA 1000 designation makes this event one of the highest-value of the Asian swing in terms of points, and Rybakina's first appearance as world No. 1 in Beijing carries symbolic weight.

The full WTA field for Beijing includes multiple top-10 players competing for points in the Race to the WTA Finals in Riyadh. The draw will be released Sunday alongside the ATP draw.

What's Next

The China Open draw is released Sunday, September 28. The tournament begins Tuesday, September 30, at the National Tennis Center in Beijing. The men's ATP 500 final is expected Saturday, October 4, with the women's WTA 1000 final to follow Sunday, October 5 (approximate dates subject to schedule). Eyes will be on the Beijing entry list for confirmation of key names following their Laver Cup and Hangzhou/Chengdu commitments over this weekend.
