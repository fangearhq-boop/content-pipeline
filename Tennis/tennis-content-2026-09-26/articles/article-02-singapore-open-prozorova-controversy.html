Gibson Advances to Singapore Final as Prozorova Concussion Walkover Sparks WTA Dispute

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | September 26, 2026

Talia Gibson will play in her first WTA final on Sunday after opponent Tatiana Prozorova was forced to withdraw from their Singapore Open semifinal under the WTA's concussion protocol — a decision that triggered an immediate and very public dispute between the Russian and the governing body.

The sequence of events began Friday, when Prozorova was struck in the face by a ball during a doubles quarterfinal match. The WTA physio applied the concussion protocol, and after conducting a balance assessment, ruled that Prozorova's test results were insufficient to allow her to compete in Saturday's singles semifinal.

What happened next was unusual even by the standards of high-stakes professional tennis. Rather than accept the ruling quietly, Prozorova took to social media and issued a statement directly challenging the WTA's assessment. She argued the ball had only grazed her cheek and hit her shoulder, and that any poor performance on the balance test was attributable to cumulative physical fatigue — she had played three matches of three hours or more on three consecutive days — not to a head injury. She claimed the physio pressured her into withdrawing against her wishes.

The WTA responded with its own statement defending the protocol, saying it had been followed correctly and that player safety is paramount.

A Debate the WTA Did Not Want

The controversy puts the WTA in a position it will have to navigate carefully. Concussion protocols exist in nearly every major professional sport precisely because the long-term risks of competing through a head injury are severe — and because athletes are notoriously poor judges of their own concussion symptoms, especially in a competitive moment. Prozorova disputing her diagnosis is not unprecedented in sport, but it is rare for that dispute to go public so quickly and so forcefully.

Prozorova's argument — that a balance test after three consecutive long matches might naturally produce poor results regardless of a head injury — is a legitimate scientific question, and one the sport may need to address if its protocols do not currently account for cumulative fatigue. Whether the WTA's existing tools can differentiate between concussion symptoms and extreme exhaustion will likely become a point of discussion in the days ahead.

Gibson's Moment

For Gibson, the controversy is an unfortunate backdrop to what should be a celebrated achievement. The Australian qualifier arrived in Singapore unseeded and without a WTA title to her name. She defeated Maria Sakkari in the quarterfinals in a three-set match that showcased her resilience — a 6-1, 4-6, 6-2 result that was more convincing than the scoreline suggested.

Now she has a chance at a first WTA title in Sunday's final. Her opponent will be the winner of Saturday evening's other semifinal: Leylah Fernandez (Canada) versus Maja Chwalińska (Poland). Chwalińska came through the quarterfinals by defeating Elise Mertens, the defending Singapore champion, 6-2, 6-2.

What's Next

The Singapore Open final is Sunday, September 27. Gibson awaits the Fernandez-Chwalińska winner. The walkover controversy will continue to develop as Prozorova's public statement and the WTA's response attract further scrutiny. The WTA has not confirmed whether a formal review of the protocol application is planned. Meanwhile, top WTA players including Fernandez will be shifting attention to the WTA 1000 China Open in Beijing, which begins September 30.
