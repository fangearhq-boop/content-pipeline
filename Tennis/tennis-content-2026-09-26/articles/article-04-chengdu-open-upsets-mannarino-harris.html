No. 1 Seed and Defending Champion Both Fall at Chengdu Open as Mannarino and Harris Make Their Runs

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | September 26, 2026

In a single session of quarterfinal tennis Friday in Chengdu, the ATP 250 tournament lost its top seed and its defending champion. The remaining bracket is now genuinely open, and two unlikely names are at the center of it: Adrian Mannarino and Lloyd Harris.

Mannarino, the 38-year-old Frenchman, arrived in Chengdu having won just one of his previous six tour-level matches. He went on to stun home favourite Shang Juncheng earlier in the week, and on Friday he backed it up with a 6-4, 6-4 dismissal of No. 3 seed Alejandro Tabilo — the man who won this title in 2025, and who came into this match as one of the pre-tournament picks to go deep. Mannarino won it in straight sets and didn't look like he was struggling. At 38, in what many assumed would be the twilight of his career, he's playing some of the best tennis he's shown in years.

Separately, qualifier Lloyd Harris (South Africa) produced the other headline of the day by defeating No. 1 seed Valentin Vacherot 6-4, 7-6(5). Vacherot had been the tournament favorite, seeded first with an inside-the-top-50 ranking and a clean run through the early rounds. Harris, who arrived as a qualifier without seeding protection, ended that campaign with a composed two-set performance.

Mannarino vs. Shapovalov: Saturday's Quarterfinal to Watch

Saturday's quarterfinal draws Mannarino against No. 7 seed Denis Shapovalov (Canada). Shapovalov reached this stage by claiming his 250th career tour-level victory earlier in the week — a milestone moment for the Canadian, who made his professional debut nearly a decade ago as a teenage sensation. He'll be heavily motivated to convert that momentum into a semifinal berth, and a match against the in-form Mannarino will test precisely that.

Shapovalov's game — explosive serving, aggressive baseline play, lefty advantage — is the kind that can silence an opponent in full flow. Whether Mannarino's current run is a genuine resurgence or a small tournament's worth of everything going right will become clearer after Saturday's match.

Harris Waiting on the Other Side

Harris awaits in the other half of the draw. A qualifier who played his way into Chengdu through the qualifying rounds, he's now two wins away from a title that many assumed would go to one of the tournament's top seeds. His next opponent depends on the results of other quarterfinals still to come.

What's Next

Chengdu Open quarterfinals and semifinals run Saturday and Sunday, September 26-27. Mannarino vs. Shapovalov is the marquee QF on Saturday. The final is expected Sunday. Players who exit early in Chengdu will pivot to the China Open in Beijing, starting September 30.
