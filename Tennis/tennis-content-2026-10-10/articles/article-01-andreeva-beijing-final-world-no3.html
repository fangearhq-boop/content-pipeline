Andreeva Beats Bartunkova 6-2, 6-2 to Reach WTA Beijing Final, Rise to World No. 3

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | October 10, 2026

WTA | China Open | Beijing | Mirra Andreeva | Nikola Bartunkova

Mirra Andreeva stormed into the WTA China Open final on Saturday, defeating Czech qualifier Nikola Bartunkova 6-2, 6-2 in 62 minutes to book her spot in Beijing's title match. The 18-year-old Russian did not just win — she dominated, barely letting Bartunkova breathe in a performance that announced her arrival at the top of the women's game in unmistakable terms.

The result guarantees Andreeva a rise to World No. 3 in the WTA rankings on Monday, the highest of her career, and marks her fourth WTA 1000 final. The win also pushed her to 50 victories in the 2026 season.

Clinical From the Start

Andreeva broke Bartunkova twice in each set to produce the clean scoreline. She never allowed her opponent to settle, mixing aggression from the baseline with sharp net approaches that Bartunkova — ranked No. 130 at the start of the week — had no answers for.

Bartunkova had been the story of the tournament's earlier rounds, upsetting fellow Czech Karolina Muchova in the quarterfinals to reach her first WTA 1000 semifinal and guarantee a top-30 debut. Against Andreeva, however, the gap in class was simply too large.

The Russian's movement, depth on both wings, and ability to accelerate through the ball were on full display throughout. It was the kind of performance that leaves little doubt about trajectory.

A 2026 Season to Remember

Andreeva's 2026 numbers tell the story clearly. Fifty wins before the calendar reaches mid-October. Four WTA 1000 finals. A career-high ranking of No. 3 still to come. She has turned the transition from teenage prodigy to genuine title contender — a transition that often trips up young players — into a seamless progression.

She turns 19 in October, meaning most players her age are still navigating the early rounds of smaller events. Andreeva is shaping the narrative of the WTA's biggest hard-court tournaments.

What's Next

Andreeva will face the winner of Saturday's second semifinal between Qinwen Zheng and Elise Mertens in Sunday's final. Zheng, a home wild card, defeated Elina Svitolina in the quarterfinals; Mertens eliminated both Coco Gauff and Iga Swiatek in consecutive rounds. Whoever advances, Andreeva goes into Sunday's final as the form player in the draw.

The WTA China Open final is scheduled for Sunday in Beijing.
