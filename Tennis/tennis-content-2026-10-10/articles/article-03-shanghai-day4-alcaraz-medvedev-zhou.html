Shanghai Masters Day 4: Alcaraz Opens, Medvedev Fires 21 Aces, Zhou Yi Stuns Musetti

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | October 10, 2026

ATP | Shanghai Masters | Carlos Alcaraz | Daniil Medvedev | Zhou Yi | Lorenzo Musetti

Day 4 of the Rolex Shanghai Masters brings the first glimpse of No. 2 seed Carlos Alcaraz, the tournament's most anticipated entrant after his Japan Open triumph last week. Alcaraz received a first-round bye and makes his tournament debut against Argentina's Juan Manuel Cerundolo, who advanced to the second round by dispatching Colombia's Nicolás Mejía 6-4, 6-4.

With the upper half of the draw still loading up, Saturday also features the fallout from Daniil Medvedev's dominant advance into the third round and the stunning upset that Chinese wild card Zhou Yi delivered to the crowd's delight.

Medvedev: 21 Aces, Problem Solved

Daniil Medvedev wasted no time establishing himself as a serious Shanghai contender, overwhelming Jan-Lennard Struff with a serving exhibition that produced 21 aces. The No. 8 seed has been inconsistent in stretches this season, but this performance — clinical serving, limited errors, controlled aggression — was a reminder of why he belongs among the tournament favorites.

Medvedev advances to the third round, where the draw will begin to clarify potential paths toward the quarterfinals.

Zhou Yi: Home Crowd Goes Wild

The moment of Day 3 — and arguably the upset of the tournament — arrived when Chinese wild card Zhou Yi, ranked No. 277 in the world, defeated No. 5 seed Lorenzo Musetti of Italy 7-6(7), 3-6, 7-6(1) in a three-set battle. Zhou claimed the final tiebreak convincingly after the match had been in the balance throughout. The Qizhong Tennis Center erupted.

For Shanghai, a home-crowd story like this carries enormous significance. Zhou's run has galvanized local interest and gives Chinese fans a name to follow deeper into the draw. Whether he can continue against higher-level opposition will be the next chapter.

Alcaraz: The Main Event

Arriving in Shanghai after defending his Japan Open title — he beat Jiří Lehečka 7-6(4), 6-4 in last week's final — Carlos Alcaraz enters as the player the tournament most wants to see succeed. At No. 2 in the world and fresh off a hardcourt title, he brings momentum.

His opening match against Cerundolo is not a gimme. The Argentine has form on hardcourts and will look to make the opening uncomfortable. But Alcaraz's tools — serve, return, heavy forehand, and net presence — give him natural advantages against most draws. His performance on Saturday will set the tone for his Shanghai campaign.

What's Next

With Day 4 wrapping up in Shanghai, attention will turn to the third round, where Alcaraz, Medvedev, and Zverev will each face the next test. Alexander Zverev — the No. 1 seed managing a left ankle concern — is also expected in action this weekend. The full picture of the draw will become clearer by Sunday evening. The Rolex Shanghai Masters runs through October 18.
