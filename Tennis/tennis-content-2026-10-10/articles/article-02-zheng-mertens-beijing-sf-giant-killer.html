WTA Beijing SF: Mertens Has Beaten Gauff and Swiatek — Can Zheng Stop the Giant-Killer?

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | October 10, 2026

WTA | China Open | Beijing | Elise Mertens | Qinwen Zheng | Semifinal

Elise Mertens has been building toward this moment all week. The Belgian veteran came into the WTA China Open seeded 22nd and left few expecting fireworks. Then she beat No. 3 seed Coco Gauff 6-3, 6-3 in the fourth round. Then she beat No. 8 seed Iga Swiatek 7-6(0), 6-3 in the quarterfinals, without facing a single break point. Now she stands one win away from the Beijing final — and Qinwen Zheng is the obstacle in her path.

Mertens: Flawless Execution

The numbers from Mertens's quarterfinal win over Swiatek were remarkable. She won 87 percent of her first-service points — 33 of 38 — and hit 24 winners against just 11 unforced errors. She did not face a single break point against the former world No. 1 and two-time China Open finalist. The Belgian's controlled aggression, particularly on her first strike, left Swiatek with no rhythm and no footholds.

It added up to a result that confirmed Mertens as one of the tournament's most dangerous players, regardless of seeding. Her first WTA 1000 singles semifinal since 2021 carries real weight — she is not here by accident.

Zheng: Playing for Beijing

Qinwen Zheng entered the tournament as a wild card rather than through her ranking, but she has played like anything but. The Chinese star battled past Elina Svitolina 6-3, 7-6(6) in the quarterfinals, holding steady in a close second set to earn her semifinal berth. The crowd has lifted her throughout the week — Beijing is her city, and the atmosphere inside the venue when she plays is unlike anything else at this tournament.

Zheng's power game on hard courts gives her a natural path against Mertens. The question is whether she can execute under pressure against an opponent who, this week, has shown no signs of cracking.

Who Has the Edge?

Mertens's first-serve numbers make her difficult to break, but Zheng's return game has the pace to disrupt that foundation. The key may come down to the second set, when tournaments often turn: Mertens claimed hers against Swiatek from a tiebreak; Zheng won hers against Svitolina in the same fashion. A third-set scenario seems entirely possible.

What is less in doubt: this is the semifinal the draw deserved. Two players in form, playing for the right to face Andreeva in Sunday's final.

What's Next

The Zheng–Mertens semifinal is scheduled for Saturday in Beijing. The winner will face Mirra Andreeva — who beat Bartunkova 6-2, 6-2 in the first semifinal — in Sunday's WTA China Open final.
