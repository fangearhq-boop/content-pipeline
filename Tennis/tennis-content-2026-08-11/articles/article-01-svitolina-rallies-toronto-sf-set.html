Svitolina Rallies from a Set Down to Beat Alexandrova, Sets Up Swiatek Showdown in Toronto

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | August 11, 2026

Ekaterina Alexandrova had beaten the world No. 1 on Saturday. She had gone into the quarterfinal without dropping a set since. She was the tournament's most dangerous player, and she won the first set 6-3.

None of it was enough.

Elina Svitolina won 11 of the next 12 games to defeat Alexandrova 3-6, 6-0, 6-3 in 93 minutes and reach the Toronto WTA 1000 semifinal. The Ukrainian who had every reason to struggle — facing the player of the tournament on a day when the crowd expected another upset — delivered one of the more methodical comebacks of the week instead.

Earlier in the day, Iga Swiatek had dispatched Diana Shnaider 6-2, 6-1 in 64 minutes without facing a single break point. The Toronto SF is set: Swiatek meets Svitolina on Wednesday August 12.

The Comeback

Alexandrova had built real momentum coming into this match. Her 7-6(3), 4-6, 6-4 win over Aryna Sabalenka on Saturday was the biggest upset of the Canadian swing; her subsequent four straight sets against Svitolina were supposed to validate it. Instead, Svitolina took the match apart methodically from the moment she went down a set.

The second set was 6-0. Not a competitive 6-2 or a scrappy 6-3 — a clean double bagel on a player who had just eliminated the world No. 1. Svitolina won 11 of 12 games across the second and third sets. She built a 5-1 lead in the decider before closing 6-3.

With the win, Svitolina's WTA 1000 winning streak reaches 10 matches: six from her Rome title run and four in Toronto. It is the longest active WTA 1000 streak on the tour. She is in her fourth WTA 1000 semifinal of 2026, which is more than any other player this season. It is the 16th WTA 1000 semifinal of her career.

Swiatek's Clinical Afternoon

While Svitolina was working through her comeback, Swiatek's afternoon looked nothing like it. The seventh seed played 64 minutes and won 6-2, 6-1. She did not face a single break point. She won 88 percent of her first-serve points and created nine break point opportunities of her own, converting five of them.

This was the most dominant individual performance in the Toronto draw since the tournament began. Shnaider, who had beaten Jessica Pegula 6-3, 6-2 in the quarterfinals, had no answer for Swiatek's pace and placement.

For Swiatek, the win is notable for a different reason: it is her first WTA 1000 semifinal of a 2026 season that has been inconsistent by her historical standards. The path to the SF was harder than the result suggests — she required three sets to beat Kostyuk in the previous round — but the form she showed against Shnaider is what coaches and fans knew was there.

What's Next

The Toronto WTA 1000 semifinal between Swiatek and Svitolina is scheduled for Wednesday August 12. The career head-to-head favours Swiatek 4-3, but Svitolina has won both 2026 meetings — at Indian Wells in March and in Rome in May — on two different surfaces. Whoever advances meets the winner of the other semifinal in the Toronto final.

The US Open main draw begins August 25. Toronto ranking points and momentum matter significantly heading into Flushing. Both players arrive at this semifinal with a genuine case to win, and nothing from Tuesday's results clearly settles the question of who is the favourite.
