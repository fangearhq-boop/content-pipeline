Shelton vs. Mensik in Montreal: The Defending Champion Meets His Clearest Test Yet

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | August 11, 2026

Ben Shelton has not dropped a set in four matches in Montreal. He is the defending champion. He is the only top-10 player remaining in the draw after first-round exits, upsets, and a field that was already weakened by three of the world's top five withdrawing before the tournament began.

He meets Jakub Mensik in the quarterfinal — a 20-year-old Czech who has beaten Shelton twice in three career meetings and who dispatched Botic van de Zandschulp 6-4, 7-5 to reach this point. The defending champion's clearest test of the week is the quarterfinal.

Shelton's Montreal Run

Shelton arrived in Montreal as the tournament's third seed and defending champion after titles in Dallas, Munich, and Stuttgart earlier in 2026. He has handled each of his four matches cleanly. The round of 16 against Joao Fonseca — the Brazilian teenager who had beaten Tsitsipas and Ruud in consecutive matches to reach that point — was the biggest challenge. Shelton won 6-3, 7-6(3).

What stands out about Shelton's Montreal is the efficiency rather than the drama. He has not required three sets. He has broken when he needed to. His serve has been a consistent weapon. The profile of a defending champion who is comfortable at this venue comes through in how he has played rather than just in the results.

Mensik's Advantage and What It Means

Jakub Mensik is 20 years old and has won against Shelton in two of their three career meetings. A 2-1 head-to-head advantage is not a decisive factor — the conditions change, the draw changes, and both players are different from match to match. But it is worth noting because it is the clearest counter-argument to treating this match as straightforward for Shelton.

Mensik is ranked approximately No. 17 and has won titles in 2026. He is not a player who arrived in Montreal looking to upset a higher seed; he arrived as a legitimate contender to win matches at this level. His path to the quarterfinal involved beating Van de Zandschulp in straight sets, which is a competent result against a capable opponent.

The challenge for Mensik is that Shelton's form and comfort in Montreal — and the specific conditions and court that suit his game — are factors that do not change match to match the way head-to-head numbers do. Shelton on this surface, in this tournament, in this sequence of form, is a different opponent from the one Mensik has beaten previously.

What's Next

The quarterfinal is scheduled for Tuesday August 11 as part of a four-match QF day in Montreal. If Shelton wins, he advances to the semifinal where he would likely meet either Learner Tien or Daniel Merida — putting two Americans or an American and a Spaniard in the last four. If Mensik wins, the defending champion exits in the quarterfinal and Mensik enters the SF as its top-5 seed result. The Montreal final is scheduled for Thursday August 13. The US Open main draw begins August 25.
