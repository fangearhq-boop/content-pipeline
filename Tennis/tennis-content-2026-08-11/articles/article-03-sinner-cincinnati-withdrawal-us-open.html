Sinner Joins Alcaraz in Withdrawing from Cincinnati; US Open Preparation Takes New Shape

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | August 11, 2026

Jannik Sinner has withdrawn from the 2026 Western and Southern Open in Cincinnati with a right knee injury, joining Carlos Alcaraz — who pulled out with a wrist injury — as the tournament's most significant pre-event withdrawal. The world No. 1 and the defending champion of the event are both absent. The US Open main draw begins August 25.

The draw ceremony for Cincinnati was held Tuesday afternoon. Alexander Zverev is the new top seed. Novak Djokovic is entered in the draw and competing for the first time since Wimbledon.

Sinner's Withdrawal

Sinner has not competed since Wimbledon, where he won the title. A right knee problem has prevented his return, keeping him out of the Canadian Open in Montreal as well as Cincinnati. He has been receiving treatment in Turin and is expected to travel to New York in the coming days to continue preparation for the US Open.

In a statement on his withdrawal, Sinner said his right knee has been bothering him and that despite working hard with his medical team, he is not ready to compete. He is targeting the US Open but that participation is not guaranteed. He remains the world No. 1 and the favourite in the US Open odds, but his physical readiness for the opening week in Flushing is genuinely uncertain.

This is the second consecutive Masters 1000 event Sinner has missed. In 2025, he reached the Cincinnati final. In 2026, he will not compete there.

Alcaraz's Situation and the Wider Picture

Carlos Alcaraz withdrew from Cincinnati several days before Sinner, citing the wrist injury that has sidelined him since April. Alcaraz is the defending Cincinnati champion and has not competed since April 14 in Barcelona. He has previously stated Cincinnati is off his schedule, with the US Open as his target. The defending US Open champion's title defence preparation has effectively been limited to training and recovery for four months.

Together, their absences create a Cincinnati draw that features Alexander Zverev as the top seed — coming off an opening-round exit in Montreal — and Novak Djokovic as the headline returnee. Djokovic, who skipped the entire Canadian swing, makes his first competitive appearance since Wimbledon. His form going into Cincinnati is unknown; his ability to peak for a major is historically not.

What's Next

The Western and Southern Open runs August 13-23. The US Open main draw begins August 25 at the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center in Flushing. Sinner's knee status will determine whether he arrives in New York having played competitive tennis in August or having managed the injury through treatment and training alone. The next 14 days will settle what shape the world No. 1 is actually in when the most important event of the American hard-court season begins.

For the wider US Open picture, the field without confirmed Sinner and Alcaraz is wide open in ways it has not been since before the two established themselves at the top of the sport. That opening creates opportunities for the Montreal generation — Shelton, Mensik, Tien, Fils — and for the re-established names in the Cincinnati draw: Zverev, Djokovic, and whoever emerges from the Canadian swing with form and confidence.
