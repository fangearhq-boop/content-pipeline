Tiafoe vs. Shelton: An All-American US Open Semifinal and a Promise to American Tennis

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | September 9, 2026

US Open 2026 | Frances Tiafoe | Ben Shelton | Semifinals | ATP

For the first time in a generation, a U.S. man is guaranteed to play in the US Open final. Frances Tiafoe and Ben Shelton have both punched their tickets to the men's semifinals, and on Friday at Arthur Ashe Stadium, they will play each other for the right to go even further.

The all-American semifinal is historic in its implications. One of these two players — one 26, one 24 — will stand in the US Open final on Sunday. American men's tennis has been waiting for this kind of moment for over two decades.

Two Different Paths to Friday

Tiafoe and Shelton arrived at the semifinals through very different kinds of matches. Tiafoe came from behind — down two sets against Alex Michelsen before winning three in a row in a display of mental toughness and tactical adjustment. It was Tiafoe at his most resilient, playing the kind of tennis that gets better as a match gets harder.

Shelton came through the tournament's most electric night. His five-set win over Carlos Alcaraz finished at approximately 3:30 AM ET, the latest finish in US Open history. He closed it with an ace in a 10-point match tiebreak. The crowd was still buzzing when the final point landed, and it will still be talking about it on Friday when the two Americans take the court.

Friends Who Will Have to Compete

Tiafoe and Shelton have a well-known friendship on the ATP Tour. They have practiced together, supported each other, and been part of the same generation working to restore American tennis to the highest level of the sport. Now they have to try to beat each other for a spot in a Grand Slam final.

The matchup is fascinating on a technical level. Shelton's serve is one of the best in the game, capable of ending points before they begin. Tiafoe has one of the most complete offensive arsenals in the sport — forehand, backhand, movement, and a serve that can also take over at key moments. Both players play well under pressure, and both have shown this week that they can handle five sets.

The Larger Picture

Andy Roddick won the US Open in 2003. No American man has won it since. There have been deep runs, semifinal appearances, and near-misses. But the final has remained out of reach. On Friday, the semifinal itself guarantees that changes.

This match carries the weight of that history without either player needing to acknowledge it explicitly. Tiafoe and Shelton are playing for themselves, for their careers, and for Friday's result. The record books will handle the rest.

What's Next

Tiafoe vs. Shelton is scheduled for Friday, September 11 at Arthur Ashe Stadium. The winner will face whoever emerges from the other half of the men's draw — currently featuring the Zverev vs. Van de Zandschulp quarterfinal on Wednesday evening. On the women's side, Sabalenka awaits in the semifinals on Thursday, where she will face the winner of Gauff vs. Andreeva.

Friday's match is the matchup American tennis has dreamed about. Two of its best players, on its biggest stage, with a Grand Slam final at stake. Don't miss it.
