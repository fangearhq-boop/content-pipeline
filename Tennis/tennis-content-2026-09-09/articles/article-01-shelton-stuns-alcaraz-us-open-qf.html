Ben Shelton Stuns Alcaraz 6-7, 6-1, 6-3, 1-6, 7-6 in Historic 3:30 AM Finish at US Open

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | September 9, 2026

US Open 2026 | Ben Shelton | Carlos Alcaraz | Quarterfinals | ATP

The 2026 US Open delivered one of its most extraordinary nights in tournament history on Tuesday evening. Ben Shelton defeated Carlos Alcaraz 6-7(5), 6-1, 6-3, 1-6, 7-6(10-6) in 4 hours and 28 minutes, with the final point landing at approximately 3:30 AM ET — the latest finish in US Open history. Shelton sealed the match with an ace in the 10-point match tiebreak, sending a sold-out Arthur Ashe Stadium into a late-night frenzy.

It was the biggest win of Shelton's career. He entered the match with a career record of 0-3 against the two-time US Open champion. He leaves it with his spot in the semifinals confirmed and an entire city buzzing.

A Five-Set Battle That Refused to End

The match swung wildly from the opening set. Alcaraz claimed the first set in a tiebreak, showing the kind of precision and shot-making that has made him one of the most feared players in the game. Shelton responded with two dominant sets in a row, winning 6-1 and 6-3 to take a 2-1 lead. Then Alcaraz stormed back, taking the fourth set 6-1 and forcing a deciding fifth.

The fifth set went with serve until the two players entered the 10-point match tiebreak. Shelton, who built his game around a massive serve and relentless court coverage, was in his element. He led 8-7 in the tiebreak before serving it out — the final point a booming ace that silenced any remaining doubt.

Shelton's Defining Moment

Shelton has always been one of the most dynamic presences on the ATP Tour. His serve regularly tops 135 miles per hour, and his energy on court feeds off crowd noise in a way few players can match. New York has adopted him as its own, and on Tuesday night, the city stayed awake to see if he could do the impossible.

He could. The 24-year-old has now reached three Grand Slam semifinals, but none came at a moment like this. Alcaraz was the tournament favorite, a player who had been 15-1 in five-set matches and who had won 13 straight against American opponents. Shelton ended both streaks in the same night.

Alcaraz's Difficult Return

For Alcaraz, the exit is a difficult one. The Spaniard had been sidelined for 139 days with a wrist injury and returned to the tour only weeks before the US Open. To reach the quarterfinals in his second event back represented a significant achievement. But Shelton was simply too good on the night — too physical, too determined, and far too loud.

Alcaraz will leave New York without the title, but his return from injury has already demonstrated that his level remains world-class. The questions about his wrist are answered. He will be back.

What's Next

Shelton advances to the US Open men's semifinals, where he will face fellow American Frances Tiafoe on Friday at Arthur Ashe Stadium. Tiafoe also won his quarterfinal on Tuesday, defeating Alex Michelsen in five sets after coming back from two sets down. The all-American semifinal is set — and one of these two players will play in the US Open final.

New York has been waiting for an American man in a Grand Slam final for over two decades. On Tuesday night, Ben Shelton made sure someone will be there. He also made sure nobody slept.
