Tiafoe Completes Dramatic Comeback, Defeats Michelsen in Five Sets to Reach US Open Semifinals

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | September 9, 2026

US Open 2026 | Frances Tiafoe | Alex Michelsen | Quarterfinals | ATP

It looked bleak for Frances Tiafoe. Two sets down to a 22-year-old American who had been one of the breakout stories of the tournament. The Louis Armstrong Stadium crowd was split, caught between two players they had reason to cheer for. Then Tiafoe did what Tiafoe does — he decided the match wasn't over.

Tiafoe defeated Alex Michelsen 5-7, 3-6, 7-5, 6-3, 7-6 on Tuesday to advance to the US Open men's semifinals. Three sets in a row to close out the match, all won in different ways — power, tactics, and finally sheer nerve in a fifth-set tiebreak. This is now Tiafoe's second US Open semifinal.

The Turning Point

Michelsen started the match in complete control. The unseeded 22-year-old had already eliminated higher-ranked opponents throughout the tournament, and his ball-striking in the first two sets was precise and aggressive. Tiafoe seemed unable to find a foothold. Down 5-7, 3-6, the match appeared to be slipping away.

The third set was the hinge. Tiafoe began finding the lines more consistently and started working Michelsen around the court instead of going toe-to-toe in baseline exchanges. He won 7-5. From that point, the momentum clearly shifted. The fourth set, 6-3, saw Tiafoe play some of his best tennis of the tournament. And by the time the fifth set arrived, Michelsen — who had been brilliant for two sets — was working to recapture a rhythm that Tiafoe had methodically taken apart.

An All-American Stage

The quarterfinal was always going to produce an American in the final four, but the all-American nature of the match gave it extra weight. Tiafoe and Michelsen are both part of the generation that American tennis has counted on to end a long drought at the Grand Slam level.

Tiafoe, at 26, is the veteran of the two. He has been in this moment before, has absorbed big losses on big stages, and has come back stronger each time. His semifinal appearance is not a surprise to anyone who has watched him closely. It is, however, validation of the work he has put in.

Michelsen, meanwhile, can take significant positives from a run that brought him to the quarterfinals of his home Grand Slam. He played exceptional tennis and pushed the more experienced Tiafoe to five sets. The future is bright.

What's Next

Tiafoe will face Ben Shelton in the US Open men's semifinals on Friday at Arthur Ashe Stadium. Shelton delivered his own remarkable performance on Tuesday night, defeating Carlos Alcaraz in five sets in a match that finished at approximately 3:30 AM ET. The two Americans are friends on the ATP Tour — but on Friday, friendship ends at the baseline.

An American man is going to the US Open final. Now the question is which one. For Tiafoe, the answer has to come from the same place it always does: the belief that no situation is beyond recovery, and no opponent is out of reach.
