Zverev vs. Shelton: The US Open Men's Final That Will Rewrite History

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | September 12, 2026

Sunday's US Open men's final is not just a tennis match. It is a referendum on two of the most compelling storylines in the sport: Alexander Zverev's bid to win two Grand Slams in a single calendar year, and Ben Shelton's chance to end the longest championship drought in American men's tennis in more than two decades. Both players earned their final spot on Friday. Both understand what is at stake. The match begins at 4 PM ET at Arthur Ashe Stadium and will be broadcast on ESPN.

The Case for Zverev

Alexander Zverev, 29 and the world No. 1, has had the best Grand Slam season of his career in 2026. He won Roland Garros in June — his first major title, secured after years of reaching late-round disappointments. He reached the Wimbledon final on grass. Now he has survived a dangerous Khachanov in three tight sets to reach the US Open final on hard courts.

He is only the 12th man since 1978 to reach Grand Slam finals on three different surfaces in a single season. The historical company he is keeping is rarefied. If he wins Sunday, he joins a very short list of players who have won multiple Grand Slams in the same calendar year in the post-Federer, post-Nadal, post-Djokovic era of men's tennis.

Zverev leads their career head-to-head and brings experience — six Grand Slam finals, including the Roland Garros win — that Shelton does not yet have.

The Case for Shelton

Ben Shelton is 23 years old and playing in his first Grand Slam final. He reached it by defeating No. 2 seed Carlos Alcaraz in the quarterfinals — a 3:33 AM finish that was the latest in US Open history — before defeating his compatriot Frances Tiafoe in four sets in the semis. None of this was supposed to happen this easily. Shelton was ranked No. 8 heading in. He is leaving as a Grand Slam finalist.

If Shelton wins, he becomes the first American man to lift a Grand Slam trophy since Andy Roddick won this very tournament in 2003. The 23-year drought has never been more tangible than it will be Sunday afternoon at Flushing Meadows. The crowd at Arthur Ashe, in New York, will be behind him completely.

He also has the intangible that matters most: a big serve that works anywhere, no fear, and a tournament draw that has already tested him in ways a first-time finalist rarely experiences.

What to Watch

The serve will be the defining weapon. Shelton's serve is one of the most powerful on the ATP Tour — he generates pace and angles that neutralize baseline rallies before they begin. Zverev's serve is excellent but more reliant on placement than raw speed. In tiebreaks, which the Zverev-Khachanov semifinal showed are likely in a tight match, Shelton's serve becomes an even larger equalizer.

Zverev's edge is in baseline consistency. He wins long rallies at a higher rate and can absorb pace without losing control. If he makes Shelton play extended exchanges, the experience gap could show in a five-set match.

What's Next

Zverev vs. Shelton, Sunday September 13 at 4 PM ET on ESPN at Arthur Ashe Stadium. One match. Two kinds of history. The US Open's closing act is set.
