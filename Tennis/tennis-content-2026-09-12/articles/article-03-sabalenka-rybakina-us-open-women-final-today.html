Sabalenka vs. Rybakina: The US Open Women's Final Is Here — Today at 4 PM ET

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | September 12, 2026

Saturday afternoon at Arthur Ashe Stadium, Aryna Sabalenka and Elena Rybakina meet for the US Open women's singles title — their 18th career meeting and their third Grand Slam final. The match begins at 4 PM ET and will be broadcast live on ESPN and ESPN Deportes. Both players have had brilliant weeks. The question of who wins has been the most interesting open question in women's tennis for the past year.

What Sabalenka Is Chasing

Aryna Sabalenka, the No. 1 seed, won the US Open in 2023 and 2024. A victory today would make her the third woman in the Open Era to win three consecutive US Open titles — joining a list that most recently includes Serena Williams, who accomplished the feat from 2012 to 2014.

Sabalenka has been the dominant force in the draw all week, defeating No. 3 Jessica Pegula 7-5, 6-2 in Thursday's semifinal. She has dropped only one set through five matches and enters Saturday's final with her best US Open form in three years of defending this title.

There is a caveat, however. In today's head-to-head, Sabalenka leads 10-7 — but Rybakina won the last time they met in a Grand Slam final. January's Australian Open ended with Rybakina winning 6-4, 4-6, 6-4, even after trailing 3-0 in the third set. Sabalenka has finished 2026 with wins over Rybakina at Indian Wells and Miami. They are genuinely close.

What Rybakina Is Chasing

Elena Rybakina, the No. 2 seed, will become the world No. 1 ranked player regardless of today's result — the new rankings release Monday, and Rybakina's US Open run has already guaranteed her the top position, ending Sabalenka's 99-week reign at the top of the WTA.

But Rybakina wants more than a ranking. She won the 2026 Australian Open in January, making her the only player on the Tour to win a hardcourt major this year. A victory at the US Open would give her both hardcourt Grand Slams in 2026 — an achievement that would define her season as exceptional by any measure.

She defeated No. 4 Coco Gauff 3-6, 6-4, 6-4 in Thursday's semifinal, rallying after dropping the opening set to close it out in three.

The Key Factor

In their 17 career matches, the player who wins the first set has gone on to win 14 times. First-set momentum between Sabalenka and Rybakina is not just important — it is historically decisive. Both players know this. The opening games of the first set are likely to set the tone for the entire afternoon.

What's Next

The US Open women's final begins at 4 PM ET today on ESPN at Arthur Ashe Stadium. It is their third Grand Slam final meeting; the series is split 1-1. Sabalenka is favored, but Rybakina has beaten her before at this stage and on this surface. This is the last time Sabalenka is guaranteed to face the world No. 1 ranking holder as the defending champion — after today, Rybakina holds it. One title. Two champions. Afternoon in New York.
