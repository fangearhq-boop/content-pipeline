Zverev Survives Khachanov Comeback to Reach Third Grand Slam Final of 2026

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | September 12, 2026

The scoreline will read 6-3, 7-6(7), 7-6(6). It will suggest a controlled, comfortable progression from the top seed. The truth is more interesting. Alexander Zverev came within two points of losing in the semifinals of the US Open on Friday, and he still found a way through. That is what the best players do at the biggest moments, and Zverev has been doing it all year.

The victory puts Zverev into the US Open final for the second time in his career — and into his third consecutive Grand Slam final of the 2026 season.

How Close It Got

Zverev won the first set 6-3 without drama, controlling the rally exchanges that define his game at his best. The second set went to a tiebreak. Khachanov pushed it but Zverev closed it out 7-5 in the breaker. The third set followed a similar pattern — tight, defensive, neither player giving ground easily — until Khachanov led the tiebreak 4-1 and had two set points that would have forced a fourth set.

Zverev erased both. He won the tiebreak 8-6 and the match was over. His sixth career Grand Slam final was confirmed. His third of 2026 was confirmed.

A Season for the History Books

It bears repeating what Zverev has accomplished this year. He won Roland Garros in June — his first Grand Slam title, secured on clay after years of near-misses in the sport's biggest matches. He then reached the Wimbledon final, contending on grass. Now, in New York, on hard courts, he is in the final again.

According to ATP Tour records, he is only the 12th man since 1978 to reach Grand Slam finals on three different surfaces in a single calendar year. The group that has achieved that feat is short and distinguished. Zverev is now among them.

Sunday's final against Ben Shelton offers Zverev the chance to win two Grand Slam titles in 2026 — something few players have managed in the modern era.

What's Next

Zverev plays Ben Shelton in Sunday's US Open men's final at 4 PM ET on ESPN. Shelton, 23 years old and in his first Grand Slam final, enters the match as the underdog on paper — but with the weight of American tennis history behind him. Zverev leads their career head-to-head. The difference may come down to serve dominance and who wins the big points in the tiebreaks that this final, based on the semifinal patterns, seems likely to produce.
