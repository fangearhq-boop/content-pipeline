Ben Shelton Defeats Frances Tiafoe to Reach First Grand Slam Final at US Open

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | September 12, 2026

Ben Shelton is going to a Grand Slam final. He is 23 years old, and on Friday night at Arthur Ashe Stadium, he showed every reason why this moment was inevitable. Shelton defeated Frances Tiafoe 4-6, 6-3, 6-3, 7-5 in an all-American semifinal that was tense, physical, and ultimately decided by the player who refused to let the occasion shrink him. Sunday, he faces No. 1 Alexander Zverev for the US Open title.

How It Unfolded

Tiafoe started the better player, winning the first set 6-4 behind clean ball-striking and the kind of aggressive-court energy that has powered him through this tournament. But Shelton found his footing in the second set, leaning on his massive serve and heavy groundstrokes to take it 6-3. From that point, the momentum never shifted back.

Shelton won the third set 6-3 as Tiafoe's forehand — one of the best in the game when it is working — began to struggle under the pace. The fourth set went to Shelton 7-5, and when the match ended, the roar at Arthur Ashe Stadium was a complicated sound: part celebration for the winner, part recognition of what both players had accomplished just by being on that court.

Tiafoe, 28, finishes his 2026 US Open in the semifinals for the third time in his career. He was gracious in defeat. The two Americans embraced at the net, and the crowd in New York understood the significance of the scene.

The Weight of History

Shelton's appearance in Sunday's final carries a layer of history beyond the match itself. He is the first Black American man to reach a US Open final since Arthur Ashe in 1972 — 54 years ago. He is also the first Black American man to reach any Grand Slam final since MaliVai Washington played at Wimbledon in 1996. The court where Shelton will compete on Sunday is named after Ashe.

He is also one win away from becoming the first American man to win a Grand Slam title since Andy Roddick lifted the trophy at the 2003 US Open. The 23-year drought in American men's tennis at the majors can end this weekend in the most American fashion imaginable — in New York, at the US Open, against a worthy opponent.

What's Next

Shelton faces No. 1 seed Alexander Zverev in Sunday's US Open men's final, scheduled for 4 PM ET on ESPN. Zverev, who defeated Karen Khachanov on Friday, enters Sunday's final having won Roland Garros in June and reached the Wimbledon final — the most complete Grand Slam season of his career. Zverev leads their career head-to-head. But Shelton has already shown this tournament that reputation means very little once the match begins. Whatever happens Sunday, American tennis has a landmark weekend in the books.
