The Last One Was Arthur Ashe: The Weight of What Ben Shelton Is Walking Into

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | September 12, 2026

The last Black American man to reach a US Open final was Arthur Ashe, in 1972. The court where Ben Shelton will play on Sunday is named after him. That is the distance — 54 years — between what happened then and what is about to happen now.

Shelton is 23 years old. He grew up in Atlanta, the son of Bryan Shelton, a former professional tennis player. He went to the University of Florida, turned professional in 2022, and has spent the past four years building the kind of game that generates this moment. He did not arrive at this Grand Slam final by accident.

What the History Looks Like

The last Black American man to reach any Grand Slam final was MaliVai Washington at Wimbledon in 1996, three decades ago. Before him, it was Ashe — who won Wimbledon in 1975, reached the US Open final in 1972, and won the inaugural US Open in 1968. Ashe was not just a tennis player. He was the person who built the argument, over years, that this sport could belong to anyone who wanted it enough.

Tennis named its most important court — the stadium that holds 23,000 people in New York — after him. On Sunday, a 23-year-old from Atlanta will play there for the US Open title.

The weight of that is not a burden Shelton carries reluctantly. When he speaks about tennis history, he speaks about it with the fluency of someone who studied it. He knows the names. He knows what they did. He knows what it means that he is here.

Not Just Shelton

Frances Tiafoe, who lost to Shelton in Friday's semifinal, was also in this story. The 28-year-old from College Park, Maryland — the son of Sierra Leonean immigrants who cleaned the facilities at a tennis center in exchange for court time for their son — reached his third US Open semifinal in five years. Two Black American men in the same US Open men's draw, both in the final four. That has not happened before.

Tiafoe's loss on Friday was real and felt across New York's tennis community. But his presence in this draw was not incidental. Tiafoe has made himself one of the five or six best players in the world on hard courts. That took years of work that intersects with, but is ultimately independent of, the history both men are writing this week.

What's Next

Ben Shelton plays Alexander Zverev in the US Open men's final on Sunday at 4 PM ET at Arthur Ashe Stadium. Before any tennis is played, the walk onto that court will be something. The last man who looked like him to walk onto that court for a final was Arthur Ashe. The game does not forget those things. Neither do the people who love it.
