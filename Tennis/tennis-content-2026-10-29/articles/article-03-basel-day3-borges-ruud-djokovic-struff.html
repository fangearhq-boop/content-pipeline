Basel Day 3: Borges Upsets Ruud, Djokovic Faces Struff in Thursday R16

By Marcus Cole | October 29, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

Wednesday's play at the Swiss Indoors produced one of the early upsets of the indoor hard court season: Joao Borges defeated No. 9 seed Casper Ruud 1-6, 6-3, 6-4 in a match that swung completely after a first set that Borges barely contested. Holger Rune also advanced, maintaining his position in a draw that is becoming more open by the day.

Thursday's second round features matches with significant implications. Novak Djokovic, who came from behind to beat Ugo Humbert on Wednesday, faces Jan-Lennard Struff — a physical, experienced indoor player who beat Yannick Hanfmann in the first round. No. 1 seed Ben Shelton is scheduled against wildcard Kym, and No. 2 seed Fils faces American Brandon Michelsen.

Borges Dismantles Ruud After a Slow Start

Joao Borges's win over Casper Ruud is a result that rewards a closer look. The first set was not competitive — Borges lost it 1-6 and looked overmatched. What followed was a complete reversal of form: a 6-3 second set that put the match on serve, and a decisive 6-4 third that closed it out.

Ruud, who has been one of the most consistent indoor hard court performers on the ATP Tour in recent seasons, exits Basel before the quarterfinals. Borges, meanwhile, advances to a second-round matchup that will be a stiffer test — but his ability to reset after a poor first set is a quality worth noting.

Thursday's Second Round

The marquee match Thursday at Basel from a Race-to-Turin perspective is Djokovic vs. Struff. Djokovic needs points — around 2,840 entering the week — and a quarterfinal run in Basel would put him back in conversation for an ATP Finals spot. Struff is dangerous on indoor hard courts, particularly with his big serve, and this will be a genuine test of Djokovic's ability to win back-to-back matches at pace.

Shelton and Fils, seeded first and second respectively, are expected to advance, but both opponents give them more than nothing. Shelton has been dominant this week — he fired 44 aces in his first-round win — and continues to look like the player to beat in Basel's top half.

What's Next

Basel quarterfinals are scheduled for later in the week, with the draw continuing to narrow. The Paris Masters draw drops Friday, adding context to every remaining Basel and Vienna result. The overlap of these three tournaments — Vienna, Basel, and Paris — in the space of two weeks represents the most compressed stretch of major ATP points available since the indoor swing began in October.
