Djokovic Beats Humbert 4-6, 6-3, 6-4 in First Basel Win Since 2011

By Elena Voss | October 29, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

Novak Djokovic kept his Race to Turin alive Wednesday night in Basel, coming from behind to defeat Ugo Humbert 4-6, 6-3, 6-4 in 2 hours and 28 minutes at the Swiss Indoors — his first Basel win since 2011.

The match followed a pattern Djokovic has become accustomed to in this stage of his career: lose the first set, absorb the pressure, then find the level that has made him the most decorated player in professional tennis history. Humbert, ranked inside the top 15 and playing confidently, took the opener by the same margin. When he called for a medical timeout for a back issue while trailing 4-1 in the second set, the match's momentum had already swung decisively.

Djokovic saved 5 of 6 break points he faced throughout the match. He broke Humbert once in each of the final two sets and closed out the victory cleanly. The result returns him to the top 10 in the ATP Rankings — a meaningful threshold as the Race to Turin enters its final stretch.

Race to Turin Watch

Djokovic entered Basel sitting approximately 11th in the Race to Turin with around 2,840 points. That leaves him well outside the top eight who qualify for the ATP Finals in Turin, but the remaining points on offer are significant: Basel and Vienna are each worth 500 points to the champion, and the Rolex Paris Masters — draw ceremony scheduled for Friday, main draw beginning November 2 — offers 1,000 points.

A deep run in Basel, starting with Thursday's second-round match against Jan-Lennard Struff, could push Djokovic well inside the bubble. Struff eliminated Yannick Hanfmann in the first round to set up this match. Djokovic is 15 years removed from his last Basel win; the 2026 field gives him a path, but the margin for error is gone.

What Humbert Showed

The match was closer than the final score suggests. Humbert is a dangerous player on indoor hard courts — his flat groundstrokes and aggressive service game are well-suited to the conditions at St. Jakobshalle in Basel. He won the first set cleanly and was competitive in the third until Djokovic broke at the right moment.

The back issue and subsequent medical timeout in the second set disrupted what had been a well-organized tactical plan, but Humbert finished the match and pushed Djokovic to work for every break. He exits Basel in the first round despite playing at a level that would beat most opponents in this field.

What's Next

Djokovic faces Jan-Lennard Struff in the second round Thursday. A win would set up a potential quarterfinal run — and every quarterfinal at an ATP 500 event adds Race points that matter in this final stretch of the season. The Paris Masters draw drops Friday evening, revealing the bracket that could decide whether Djokovic, Medvedev, Cobolli, and others secure their spots in Turin.
