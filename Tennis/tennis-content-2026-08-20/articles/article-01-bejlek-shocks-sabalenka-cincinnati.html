Bejlek Shocks World No. 1 Sabalenka to Reach First WTA 1000 Quarterfinal

By Marcus Cole | August 20, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

Sara Bejlek was 0-3 against top-10 opponents before Thursday. She was ranked No. 35 in the world. She was facing the player who has dominated WTA hard-court tennis for the better part of two years.

And then she beat her.

Bejlek defeated world No. 1 Aryna Sabalenka 7-6(7), 6-4 in the Cincinnati Open Round of 16, reaching her first-ever WTA 1000 quarterfinal with one of the most commanding performances of the summer. The result is the biggest upset of the hard-court swing and changes the landscape of the Cincinnati draw entirely.

The Tiebreak That Turned the Match

The match was on serve through most of the first set, and the tiebreak appeared to be going Sabalenka's way. The world No. 1 built a 5-1 lead — one point from taking the set. That's when Bejlek found something.

The 20-year-old Czech reeled off six consecutive points to steal the tiebreak 9-7. The momentum swing was total. Sabalenka, whose first serve had already been unreliable — she hit four double faults and got only 50 percent of first serves in — had no answer in the second set. Bejlek won it 6-4 to complete the upset.

Bejlek's serve stats told a different story: three aces, zero double faults, and 67 percent of first-serve points won. On the day when she needed her serve to hold under pressure, it did. Sabalenka's, characteristically one of the best in the game, did not.

A Profile in Composure

What made Bejlek's performance striking was how little the occasion appeared to affect her. Facing the world No. 1 at a hard-court Masters 1000 event, she maintained her movement, her shot selection, and her nerve through the tiebreak crisis and into the second set.

At 20 years old, Bejlek has been climbing the WTA rankings steadily without generating major headlines. Thursday's result changes that. A win over the world No. 1 — from 1-5 down in a first-set tiebreak — is the kind of performance that defines careers.

What's Next

Bejlek advances to face No. 20 seed Madison Keys in the quarterfinals on Friday, August 21. A win would put her in her first WTA 1000 semifinal, with the US Open Fan Week starting Saturday and the main draw beginning August 30.

As for Sabalenka — this exit adds to a puzzling hard-court summer for a player who arrived in Cincinnati as the overwhelming favourite. She remains No. 1 in the world, and the US Open is the goal. But Thursday's result is a reminder that rankings don't win matches.
