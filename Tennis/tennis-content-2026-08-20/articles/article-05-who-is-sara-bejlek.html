Who Is Sara Bejlek? The 20-Year-Old Who Just Beat the World No. 1

By Ryan Calloway | August 20, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

If Thursday's match in Cincinnati was the first time you heard the name Sara Bejlek, you are not alone. That's about to change.

The 20-year-old Czech player, ranked No. 35 in the world, beat Aryna Sabalenka — the world No. 1 — 7-6(7), 6-4 in the Cincinnati Open Round of 16. It is the biggest win of her career. It puts her in her first-ever WTA 1000 quarterfinal. And it happened in a manner that was, frankly, hard to believe in real time.

The Record Before Today

Bejlek arrived in Cincinnati ranked No. 35 and 0-3 against top-10 opponents. The sample size was small, but the pattern was consistent: she had never beaten a player inside the world's top 10.

On paper, Thursday's match looked like a straightforward assignment for Sabalenka. The world No. 1 had cruised through her earlier matches and was expected to do the same here. Sabalenka's serve, her game's foundational weapon, had been working well. Nothing about the pre-match picture suggested Bejlek would be the answer.

How She Did It

Bejlek's game is built on solid fundamentals rather than overpowering weapons — consistent movement, a reliable serve, and the ability to absorb pace from the baseline. Against Sabalenka, those qualities became the entire match.

When Sabalenka built a 5-1 lead in the first-set tiebreak, the logical conclusion of the match seemed clear. Bejlek disagreed. She won six consecutive points to take the tiebreak 9-7 — a sequence that visibly rattled Sabalenka and settled Bejlek. The second set followed the same template: Bejlek steady, Sabalenka error-prone, the result moving steadily in one direction.

The final stats reinforced the story. Bejlek: three aces, zero double faults, 67 percent first-serve points won. Sabalenka: four double faults, zero aces, 50 percent first serves made.

What's Next

Bejlek faces No. 20 seed Madison Keys in the quarterfinals on Friday, August 21. Keys is a seasoned hard-court player with a powerful game — this will not be an easy match. But Bejlek has already demonstrated this week that she can handle pressure from the biggest opponents in the world.

A win on Friday would put her in a WTA 1000 semifinal, two days before US Open Fan Week begins. From 0-3 against top-10 opponents to a potential Cincinnati semifinal — that's the Bejlek story this week, and it's still being written.
