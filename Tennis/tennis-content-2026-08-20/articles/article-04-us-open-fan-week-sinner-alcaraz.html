US Open Fan Week Starts Saturday — And the Two Biggest Questions in Tennis Remain Unanswered

By Marcus Cole | August 20, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

Three days. That's how long until US Open Fan Week begins at the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center in Flushing Meadows. The main draw follows on August 30.

And the two players who have defined men's tennis in recent years — Jannik Sinner and Carlos Alcaraz — still haven't confirmed they'll be there.

Sinner: Knee, Caution, and a Race Against the Calendar

Jannik Sinner, the world No. 1, has not competed since Wimbledon. A right knee problem surfaced after Wimbledon, prompting visits to medical clinics in Italy and a withdrawal from the North American hard-court swing that included both Montreal and Cincinnati. He did not play either Masters 1000 event.

The current status is cautiously optimistic: Sinner is believed to be on course for the US Open, and one report indicated he may enter Winston-Salem as a wildcard to get match time before Flushing Meadows. But "believed to be on course" is not the same as confirmed. Every day without an official statement adds another layer of uncertainty to the men's draw.

If Sinner misses the US Open, the top of the men's ranking — and the top seed slot at Flushing — would require significant reshuffling.

Alcaraz: Four Months, One Target

Carlos Alcaraz, the defending US Open champion, last competed on April 14 in Barcelona. A wrist injury has kept him out for nearly four months. He skipped Roland Garros, Wimbledon, Montreal, and Cincinnati — every major event of the spring and summer.

This week, reports emerged that Alcaraz is training in Spain with Danish star Holger Rune, working through fitness assessments to determine whether he can compete at Flushing Meadows. His camp has not confirmed a US Open entry as of August 20. Nine players have already withdrawn from the 2026 US Open main draw.

The defending champion showing up without match play since April would face enormous physical and mental challenges in a best-of-five format over two weeks. But Alcaraz has shown before that raw talent and competitive drive can overcome preparation deficits in short bursts.

What's Next

US Open Fan Week — which includes qualifying matches, the Mixed Doubles Championship, Arthur Ashe Kids' Day, and player practices — begins Saturday, August 23. The main draw commences August 30, with the men's singles final scheduled for Sunday, September 13.

With Sinner's and Alcaraz's statuses still unresolved, expect official entry confirmations (or withdrawals) in the coming days. The draw ceremony itself — which will reveal the bracket — is a closely watched event as a result. In the meantime, the hard-court players who have been competing all summer — Nakashima, Tommy Paul, Tiafoe, and others currently alive in Cincinnati — have one clear advantage: they're match-fit and they know it.
