First German to Finish No. 1 in ATP History — Now Zverev Eyes the Davis Cup

By Elena Voss | November 25, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

Alexander Zverev clinched the year-end No. 1 ranking on October 12, 2026, becoming the first German player in ATP history to finish a season at the top of the rankings. He received the trophy for it at the Nitto ATP Finals in Turin on November 16. Now he is in Bologna, leading Germany at the Davis Cup Finals, with an opportunity to close a year that already defies easy description.

No German had done this before. That is the context for everything that follows.

How He Got Here

The year-end No. 1 ranking was earned through a season that produced two Grand Slam titles. Zverev won Roland Garros in June and the US Open in September — a clay court major and a hard court major in the same calendar year, the kind of double that the ATP Tour's most decorated players achieve once or twice in a generation.

The ATP's own assessment, following the Roland Garros win, was that it represented the best season of his career. The US Open only deepened that case. By the time the year-end rankings were locked in on October 12, the question was not whether Zverev would finish No. 1 — it was how significant that finish would look in the context of German tennis history.

The answer is: more significant than anything that came before it. The historical No. 1 rankings list includes Djokovic, who has held the year-end position eight times, and Alcaraz, who held it in 2022 and 2025, and Sinner, who held it in 2024. Zverev is now on that list. First time for a German.

The Trophy in Turin

The year-end No. 1 trophy was presented at the Nitto ATP Finals in Turin on November 16. The ceremony added a formal punctuation to a season that had already been extraordinary. Zverev competed at the Finals in Turin as the top seed — the event's most prominent player — and received recognition that went beyond individual match results.

The trophy has been given to the best player in the world at the end of each season for decades. The list of recipients is a roster of the sport's most celebrated careers. Zverev's name is now on it.

What He Is After Next

Zverev is in Bologna because he wants the Davis Cup title. That much is not complicated. He has the Roland Garros and US Open trophies. He has the year-end No. 1 recognition. The one major team achievement of the tennis calendar — the Davis Cup title for Germany — would be a final piece of a 2026 season that is already historic.

Germany faces Great Britain in Thursday morning's quarterfinal. Zverev will play his first competitive match since the Turin ATP Finals. The transition from individual honors to team format is a different kind of pressure, one that some players handle better than others. Zverev has represented Germany in Davis Cup before. He knows what it asks of him.

The 2026 season has not ended yet. For Alexander Zverev, it will end when Germany's Davis Cup run ends — either in a final on Sunday, or somewhere before that. He came to Bologna to make sure it ends on Sunday.

What's Next

Alexander Zverev and Germany face Great Britain in the Davis Cup quarterfinal on Thursday, November 26 at BolognaFiere. The winner advances to the semifinals on Saturday, November 28.
