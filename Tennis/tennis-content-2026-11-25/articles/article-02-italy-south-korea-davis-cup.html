Italy Seek Historic Fourth Straight Davis Cup Title — South Korea Stand in the Way

By Elena Voss | November 25, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

Italy have won the last three Davis Cup Finals. No nation has won four consecutive titles in the modern format. On Tuesday in Bologna, they will attempt to become the first. The team standing in their way is South Korea — a squad making their first-ever appearance in the Davis Cup Finals quarterfinal.

The gap between these two programs, measured by recent results and head-to-head record, is significant. Italy lead the all-time H2H 3-0. They are playing at home. They have three consecutive titles as proof of concept. The numbers point one direction.

But South Korea earned their place here, and that matters.

What Italy Are Chasing

Italy won the Davis Cup title in 2023, 2024, and 2025. Each win came with the expectation that it would be harder than the last, and each time the Azzurri found a way. The 2026 edition represents something different: a chance at history that no team in the current format has reached before.

They do so without Jannik Sinner, who ended his 2026 season on October 6 with a knee injury. The No. 3 player in the world will not be suiting up in Bologna. His absence removes the most dominant serving force in Italian tennis, but the squad David Ferrer has assembled around him is deep enough to remain the heavy favorites in every match they play.

Flavio Cobolli, Matteo Berrettini, and Lorenzo Musetti form the core of a lineup that has won at this level before. The question is whether they can do it without the safety net that Sinner provides.

South Korea's Historic Moment

Regardless of the final score on Tuesday, South Korea have already done something worth acknowledging. Reaching the Davis Cup Finals quarterfinal for the first time in the program's history is not an accident. It is the product of sustained investment and a generation of Korean players who have competed on the ATP Tour at the highest level.

Kwon Soon-woo is the most recognizable name on the roster, a player who has reached No. 40 in the world and competed at every major event. The team around him has built enough depth to win a qualifying round and emerge from their Finals group. That is real.

What they face in Italy, in Bologna, with the crowd entirely against them, is a different level of challenge. But the storyline is genuine: the first-ever quarterfinal appearance, against the three-time defending champions, in the most watched Davis Cup event in a decade.

The Home Crowd Factor

Bologna has become Italy's fortress. The BolognaFiere arena fills for Davis Cup in a way that few indoor venues do in tennis, and the atmosphere when Italy play is among the loudest in the sport. For South Korea, navigating that environment without Sinner on the other side of the net is the ask — but the crowd will still be overwhelmingly Italian, and that matters in a team format where momentum between rubbers shapes everything.

Italy's experience in this environment, playing in front of this crowd, is itself an advantage that does not appear in the head-to-head statistics.

What's Next

The Italy vs. South Korea quarterfinal is scheduled Tuesday, November 25 at BolognaFiere. The winner advances to face Czech Republic in the semifinals on Friday, November 27. If Italy win, they will play for a record fourth straight Davis Cup title this weekend in Bologna.
