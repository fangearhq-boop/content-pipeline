Sinner Reveals Secret Tonsil Surgery: Significant Weight Loss, 'Not Even Close to Returning'

By Marcus Cole | November 25, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

Jannik Sinner disclosed in a YouTube series this week that he had his tonsils removed in November — a surgery that had not been publicly announced until now. Combined with the knee injury that ended his 2026 season and the significant muscle mass loss he described in the same interview, the picture of Sinner's current health situation is significantly more complicated than previously reported.

His own assessment of his timeline: "I'm not even close to returning."

What He Said

The disclosure came through a YouTube series that Sinner has been releasing during his recovery period. In discussing the tonsil surgery, he said his face had been "quite dry" in the aftermath — a description that hints at the discomfort of the procedure and the recovery period that followed.

He confirmed that the surgery resulted in significant muscle mass loss. Doctors advised him to focus entirely on rebuilding his body before returning to any tennis activity. That directive — rebuild first, play later — means he is not in any kind of court-side preparation phase. He is earlier in the process than that.

"I'm not even close to returning." That is a direct quote. It is not spin, and it is not managed messaging. It is a player describing where he actually is.

The Full Picture of His Injuries

The tonsil surgery is new information. The knee is not. Sinner ended his 2026 season on October 6, with the knee issue cited as the reason. At the time, the question was whether he would recover in time for the Australian Open, which begins January 17. That question has not been answered, and the tonsil surgery — with its associated muscle loss and additional recovery timeline — makes it harder to answer optimistically.

Italian media is now reporting that his Australian Open 2027 participation is "not yet secure." That framing is careful and accurate. It is not a withdrawal. It is not a confirmed entry. It is a question mark that has grown larger in the past week.

What Was Not Disclosed Until Now

The timing of the tonsil surgery — in November, shortly after the season ended — is worth noting. The procedure was not announced when it happened. It was not disclosed during the ATP Finals in Turin, an event Sinner would typically have competed at. It surfaced this week through the YouTube series, with no prior indication that it had occurred.

The reason for the non-disclosure is not explained. What matters now is the totality of his situation: a knee requiring conservative treatment, a tonsil removal with muscle mass consequences, and doctors who have told him the priority is rebuilding his body, not returning to the court.

The Australian Open Question

January 17 is the Australian Open start date. That is roughly seven and a half weeks from now. For a player who has significant muscle mass to rebuild and is not close to court activity, that timeline is tight. Not impossible — recovery trajectories can accelerate — but tight enough that the "not yet secure" language from Italian media is not alarmist. It is realistic.

Sinner was the dominant force in tennis in 2024 and 2025. He will be a central story of 2027, whether he plays or whether he does not. The tennis world wants him healthy and competitive. Right now, he is working toward that. How long it takes is a question that remains genuinely open.

What's Next

Sinner's Australian Open 2027 status remains uncertain. Italian media reports his participation "not yet secure." The Australian Open begins January 17. Any further update on his recovery timeline is expected to come through the same YouTube series or his official channels.
