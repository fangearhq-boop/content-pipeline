Winston-Salem Open Reaches Semifinals — Last ATP Tune-Up Before US Open

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | August 27, 2026

The 2026 Winston-Salem Open is down to four. The ATP 250 at Wake Forest University in North Carolina plays its semifinals on Thursday, with the final scheduled for Saturday, August 29. One day after the champion lifts the trophy, the US Open main draw begins at Flushing Meadows.

This is the last ATP event of the summer hard-court swing before the year's final Grand Slam — and this year, more than most, the timing matters. The US Open draw has been shaped by a significant number of withdrawals, and the players who arrive in New York having competed recently in match play will carry a different kind of preparation than those coming off rest or practice only.

What Winston-Salem Means at This Point in the Season

For players ranked between 30 and 100 in the world, Winston-Salem is a legitimate title opportunity. An ATP 250 trophy in late August, heading into the final Grand Slam, can lift a ranking, restore confidence, and provide the competitive edge that practice alone does not replicate. Players who compete well in Winston-Salem this week carry a specific advantage: their first week at the US Open will not be their first competitive matches on hard court in August.

The tournament field does not include the top seeds from the US Open draw. Those players — Zverev, Djokovic, Gauff, Swiatek — typically bypass Winston-Salem in favor of rest or practice blocks. But the mid-tier of the US Open draw is well represented in Wake Forest this week, and what happens today in the semifinals will ripple into the early rounds at Flushing.

Alcaraz Is Not Here

Carlos Alcaraz confirmed Thursday that he is going directly to the US Open, skipping Winston-Salem entirely. Reports earlier in August had suggested he might request a wildcard into the event as a return-to-play warm-up after his four-month injury absence. He chose otherwise — trusting his practice preparation and the two mixed doubles matches he played at Flushing Meadows earlier this week (won R1, lost QF with Serena Williams) as sufficient match preparation.

It is a different bet than most players in his situation would make. Coming off a four-month absence and going directly into a Grand Slam as the No. 2 seed is the kind of entry point that tests not just the wrist but everything — timing, footwork, decision-making, the competitive instincts that only switch on in real matches. The Winston-Salem field will arrive in New York with those instincts freshened. Alcaraz will not.

What's Next

The Winston-Salem final takes place Saturday, August 29. The champion earns ATP ranking points, a title, and — given the timing — the best possible mental state heading into the US Open the following day. For the runners-up and semifinalists, the experience still provides valuable preparation.

The US Open main draw begins Sunday, August 30. The Winston-Salem finalists will know their first-round opponents in New York before they leave Wake Forest, making Saturday's final an unusual double event: a tournament conclusion and an opening preview of the hardest two weeks in hard-court tennis.
