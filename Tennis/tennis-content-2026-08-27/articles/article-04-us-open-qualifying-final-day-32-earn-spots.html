US Open Qualifying Final Day: 32 Players Earn Main Draw Spots

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | August 27, 2026

Thursday is the last day of qualifying at the 2026 US Open. By the end of today's matches at the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center, 32 players — 16 men and 16 women — will have earned spots in the main draw. The qualifying tournament began Monday, August 24, and runs four rounds over four days, concluding today with a final round that determines the last entries into the bracket.

How Qualifying Works

The US Open qualifying draw places 128 men and 128 women into a three-round competition (played across four days, with first-round matches split over Monday and Tuesday). Players must win three consecutive matches against professional-level opponents to earn a main draw spot. The qualifying field is drawn from players ranked outside the direct acceptance cutoff — typically between approximately No. 100 and No. 200 in the world — along with wild card entries who received special invitations.

This year's qualifying field was described as one of the strongest in recent memory, with several established tour-level players working their way back from injury, suspension, or ranking drops competing against rising young professionals. Winning three rounds in this field is a genuine accomplishment before a single main draw match is played.

What the 32 Qualifiers Face Next

The 32 players who advance today will be placed into the main draw in the spots designated for qualifiers. In most cases, this means opening-round matches against seeded opponents or high-ranked direct acceptances — the draw is structured so that qualifiers do not face each other in the first round. The path for a qualifier to reach the second week of a Grand Slam runs through top-100 players from the first match.

That difficulty is part of what makes qualifying survival meaningful. Players who come through three rounds of qualifying and then advance in the main draw are operating at an exceptional level. Most years, one or two qualifiers make a notable run — the fourth round, a quarterfinal — and those runs become the surprise stories of the tournament.

Notable Qualifying Story: American Teens Exit

Among the stories that closed in the qualifying second round: all three American teenagers who had reached Round 2 — Jordan Lee, Michael Antonius, and Andrew Johnson — were eliminated on the same day. Lee, the Wimbledon junior champion, fell to David Rincon of Spain, 6-3, 4-6, 6-1. None of the three will appear in today's final round or earn a main draw spot.

Their absence from the final qualifying round is a reminder of the gap between junior success and professional qualifying — a gap that is real, measurable, and temporary for players with Lee's trajectory.

What's Next

After today's final qualifying round concludes, the 32 qualifiers will be announced and placed into the main draw bracket. The full order of play for Sunday, August 30 — the opening day of the main draw — will be released in the coming days. Main draw matches begin across the outer courts and show courts, with Arthur Ashe Stadium and Louis Armstrong Stadium hosting the highest-profile Day 1 matchups.

The 2026 US Open main draw features top seeds Alexander Zverev and Aryna Sabalenka, along with the highly anticipated return of Carlos Alcaraz after a four-month injury absence. The 32 qualifiers who earn spots today step into that field on Sunday and begin their own chapter of the tournament.
