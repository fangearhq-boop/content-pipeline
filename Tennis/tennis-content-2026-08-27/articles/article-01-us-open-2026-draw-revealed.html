US Open 2026 Draw Revealed: Zverev No. 1, Alcaraz No. 2, Eala Makes History

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | August 27, 2026

The 2026 US Open draw is set. The ceremony took place Thursday morning at noon ET in New York, and the bracket is now locked ahead of Sunday's main draw opening. The final Grand Slam of the year begins August 30 at the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center in Flushing Meadows, New York.

Alexander Zverev enters as the No. 1 men's seed — the highest seeding of his career at a Grand Slam. Carlos Alcaraz, returning from a four-month absence due to a right wrist injury, is slotted in as the No. 2 seed. Novak Djokovic holds the No. 4 position, with a chance to claim his 25th major title if the draw falls his way.

Women's Seeds: Sabalenka Leads a Strong Field

On the women's side, Aryna Sabalenka is the No. 1 seed, looking to defend her US Open title and extend her run at Flushing Meadows. Behind her, Iga Swiatek holds the No. 2 position, Coco Gauff is No. 3, and Jessica Pegula carries the No. 4 seed — a notable American presence in the upper bracket.

The women's draw is among the deepest in recent years. Sabalenka arrives as the clear favorite, but Gauff, Swiatek, and Pegula all have the game to challenge for the title at this surface. Hard courts suit this group. The final should deliver a match worth watching.

Eala Makes History

Alex Eala has earned a Grand Slam seeding for the first time in her career, making her the first Filipino player ever to receive a seeding at a major. The 20-year-old has been one of the most compelling storylines on the WTA Tour in 2026, and her seeding reflects a season that has turned heads across the tour.

Coming to New York as a seeded player changes the draw dynamics for Eala. She will be protected from the top seeds in early rounds, giving her a cleaner path through the first week if she plays to her seeding. What she does with that opportunity will be one of the defining subplots of this US Open.

A Draw Shaped by Withdrawals

Jannik Sinner — who would have been among the top seeds — is absent from this year's US Open. He joins more than 14 other notable players who have withdrawn from the draw, creating gaps throughout the bracket that reshuffle the paths for those who are competing.

A draw with this many open slots creates opportunities. Lower seeds and qualifiers who earn entry will face paths that, in a normal year, would have run through top-30 opponents in the second and third rounds. The withdrawal count makes this one of the more unpredictable US Opens in recent memory.

What's Next

First-round matches begin Sunday, August 30, with the draw split across three days (August 30, 31, and September 1) to accommodate 128 men's and 128 women's matches. The women's final is set for Saturday, September 12, and the men's final follows on Sunday, September 13.

For Zverev and Sabalenka, the No. 1 seedings mean entry into the main draw from the top of their respective halves. For Alcaraz, the question is not the seeding but the rust — Sunday will be his first competitive singles match since April. For Eala, it is history in motion. And for the 32 qualifiers who earned spots through the qualifying rounds, it is the biggest stage they have competed on.

The 2026 US Open starts Sunday.
