Jordan Lee and American Teens Fall in US Open Qualifying Second Round

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | August 27, 2026

The American teenage storyline at 2026 US Open qualifying came to a close in the second round, with Jordan Lee, Michael Antonius, and Andrew Johnson all eliminated on the same day. None of the three will advance to the main draw through the qualifying route. Their exits ended one of the more watched subplots of the pre-tournament week at Flushing Meadows.

Jordan Lee's Week

Lee, 16 years old and a resident of Orlando, Florida, arrived at Flushing Meadows carrying significant weight for a teenager. He won the Wimbledon boys' singles title this summer — a result that fast-tracked his profile and earned him a wildcard into the qualifying draw.

He used it well in the first round. Lee defeated Mark Lajal 4-6, 6-3, 6-3 in a match that required him to absorb a lost first set and find another level in the second and third. He did. The win was watched closely by everyone paying attention to American tennis development.

In the second round, Lee faced David Rincon of Spain — a 23-year-old with considerably more professional match experience. Rincon controlled the opener and held his ground in the third when Lee pushed the match to a deciding set. Final score: 6-3, 4-6, 6-1. The result was honest. Lee came close enough to get a look at the gap; Rincon was simply more equipped for the moment.

Antonius and Johnson

Michael Antonius and Andrew Johnson, two other American teenagers who had advanced into the qualifying second round, also lost. All three exits happened within the same qualifying window, ending any possibility of an American teenager making the 2026 US Open main draw through qualifying.

The three-player contingent had been described as part of the deepest American qualifying presence in several years — a reflection of investment in junior development and the pipeline of young players reaching professional age simultaneously. Getting three American teens to the second round of qualifying at a major is not a failure. Getting none of them through to the main draw is the honest accounting of how difficult qualifying actually is.

What the Exit Means and Doesn't Mean

Losing in the second round of US Open qualifying at 16 is a different result than it appears. Lee played two matches at a major venue under real professional conditions and performed well enough to win one of them. He is not yet ready for the main draw — that is the simple read — but the gap between where he is and where he needs to be is not enormous. It is measurable in months and training, not years.

The Wimbledon junior title established him as one of the best juniors in the world. The qualifying run confirmed that the professional game is harder and that Lee has the tools to grow into it. At 16, both of those conclusions are entirely fine.

What's Next

Lee, Antonius, and Johnson return to the junior circuit and the lower levels of the professional game to continue building their rankings and match experience. For Lee specifically, the next year of professional exposure will be closely watched. Wimbledon junior champions have a mixed track record in the transition to the senior tour — some arrive within a year, some take longer. Lee's aggressive baseline game and competitive instincts suggest a faster transition is possible.

Thursday is the final day of qualifying. The 32 players who earn main draw spots today will be announced and placed in the bracket ahead of Sunday's opening round. Lee will not be among them — but his name will be worth tracking over the next 12 months.
