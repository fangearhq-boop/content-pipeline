Alcaraz Confirms US Open Return — No Tune-Up, Straight to Flushing Meadows

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | August 27, 2026

Carlos Alcaraz is returning to professional tennis. And he is doing it on the biggest stage the sport offers.

The 23-year-old confirmed Thursday that he will compete in the 2026 US Open, beginning August 30, without playing any tune-up event first. He passed on Winston-Salem, the ATP 250 at Wake Forest University that runs through August 29, choosing instead to prepare on the practice court and enter Flushing Meadows directly. His last competitive singles match was in April at the Barcelona Open, where he withdrew after his opening match due to a right wrist injury diagnosed as tenosynovitis.

What He Missed

The months since Barcelona have been a long, quiet chapter for the No. 2 seed. Alcaraz was absent from the French Open, Wimbledon, the Canadian Open, and the Cincinnati Open — four events that defined the spring and summer calendar for everyone else in the men's draw. In a normal year, those four tournaments would have accumulated thousands of ranking points, match experience, and momentum for whoever performed well. Alcaraz watched from the sideline.

Tenosynovitis is an inflammation of the tendon sheath — manageable with rest and treatment, but a slow recovery for a player who relies on wrist load across all of his groundstrokes. Alcaraz has a heavily loaded backhand and a serve that generates significant wrist stress. Getting the wrist healthy enough to compete at Grand Slam intensity required patience that the schedule did not make easy.

The Only Match Play He Has

His only competitive match time at Flushing Meadows came in the US Open mixed doubles, where he partnered Serena Williams on a wildcard entry. The pair won their opening match over Erin Routliffe and Lloyd Glasspool 5-3, 4-1, then fell in the quarterfinals to Flavio Cobolli and Belinda Bencic, 5-4(4), 4-1. It was two matches under pressure in a competitive setting, not the equivalent of a singles tournament run — but it gave Alcaraz real information about how the wrist responds to match conditions.

What he does not have is the kind of singles rhythm that comes from a week of main-draw competition. Going directly from practice to a Grand Slam first round — as the second seed, with the entire tennis world watching — is an unusual entry point even for an elite player.

The Decision to Skip Winston-Salem

Earlier in August, there were reports that Alcaraz was considering a wildcard entry into the Winston-Salem Open as a final tune-up before New York. He ultimately decided against it. The calculus likely involved weighing the risk of re-aggravating the wrist in a competitive setting against the benefit of additional match time. For a player with seven Grand Slam titles and the competitive experience Alcaraz carries, trusting the body and going into Flushing fresh may have been the more conservative choice — despite how it looks from the outside.

The mixed doubles appearances appear to have confirmed enough. The wrist held up. Now the question is the rest of his game.

What's Next

Alcaraz draws his first-round opponent from today's reveal and begins singles play on Sunday, August 30. As the No. 2 seed, he opens in the bottom half of the draw — on a potential collision course with Zverev in the final rather than an earlier round. If the bracket opens up as seedings suggest, the first real test may not come until the quarterfinals or semifinals.

Four months off. No warm-up. No clarity on timing, rust, or match sharpness. Those are the unknowns. The known is that Alcaraz, when healthy, is one of the best hard-court players in the world. Sunday will start to answer the question of how much has changed.
