### STORY 1: US Open 2026 Draw Revealed

**Long-Form Post**
The 2026 US Open draw has been released, and the field is set for the year's final Grand Slam.

On the men's side, Alexander Zverev enters as the No. 1 seed — his best Grand Slam seeding to date. Carlos Alcaraz is No. 2, returning after a four-month absence due to a right wrist injury. Novak Djokovic is seeded No. 4, still chasing his 25th major title.

For the women, Aryna Sabalenka is the No. 1 seed, looking to defend her US Open title. Iga Swiatek is No. 2, Coco Gauff is No. 3, and Jessica Pegula is No. 4 — a strong American presence in the upper seeds.

The standout seeding story belongs to Alex Eala. The Filipino star has earned a Grand Slam seeding for the first time in her career — the first Filipino player ever to be seeded at a major. Flushing Meadows has been the stage for many historic firsts. Add this one to the list.

The tournament is missing Jannik Sinner, along with 14-plus other notable withdrawals that have reshaped the draw. The open field creates opportunities throughout the bracket.

Main draw play begins Sunday, August 30. Which match are you most excited about?

### STORY 2: Alcaraz Confirms US Open Return

**Long-Form Post**
Carlos Alcaraz is coming back. And he's going straight to the biggest stage.

Alcaraz confirmed Thursday that he will compete in the 2026 US Open — his first professional match since withdrawing from the Barcelona Open in April with a right wrist injury (tenosynovitis). He skipped the French Open, Wimbledon, the Canadian Open, Cincinnati, and Winston-Salem during his recovery. He returns as the No. 2 seed at Flushing Meadows.

This is a high-wire act. Four months off the tour, no tune-up event, stepping directly into a Grand Slam draw with the whole tennis world watching. His only competitive match play during this stretch was in the US Open mixed doubles — a run that lasted through the quarterfinals (alongside Serena Williams) before losing to Cobolli and Bencic.

The wrist is healthy. The rust is unknown. The draw has him seeded second, which means a potential semifinal against Zverev or Djokovic.

Sunday, August 30, will tell us a lot.

### STORY 3: Jordan Lee and American Teens Fall in Qualifying

**Long-Form Post**
The American teenage experiment in US Open qualifying came to an end Wednesday.

Jordan Lee, the 16-year-old Wimbledon junior champion from Orlando, Florida, fell to David Rincon of Spain in the second round of qualifying, 6-3, 4-6, 6-1. Lee had shown real fight in the first round — defeating Mark Lajal 4-6, 6-3, 6-3 in a three-set battle — but Rincon was the more experienced player and it showed in the third set.

Michael Antonius and Andrew Johnson, two other American teenagers who had advanced into the second round, also lost on the same day. It was a sweep in the wrong direction for the American youth contingent.

None of this diminishes what Lee accomplished this week. Coming to Flushing Meadows at 16, winning a round of qualifying at a major, and competing into the second round is exactly the kind of experience that builds professional players. The crowd will remember his name.

He'll be back.

### STORY 4: US Open Qualifying Final Day

**Long-Form Post**
The last day of US Open qualifying is underway.

By the time the courts go quiet tonight at Flushing Meadows, 32 players — 16 men and 16 women — will have earned the right to compete in the main draw. They'll join the direct acceptances and wild cards in one of the deepest Grand Slam fields of the year.

Qualifying at a major is a three-round gauntlet. Players who come through typically rank between 100 and 200 in the world — good enough to compete professionally but not good enough for a direct acceptance. Today's final round separates the ones who belong in this draw from the ones who came close.

The 32 qualifiers will be placed into the main draw, with first-round matches beginning Sunday, August 30. Most will face seeded opponents in the opening round — the reward for making it through is the challenge of facing the best players in the world.

That's what makes qualifying matter.

### STORY 5: Winston-Salem Open Semifinals

**Long-Form Post**
The 2026 Winston-Salem Open is down to four players.

The ATP 250 tournament at Wake Forest University reaches its semifinals today, with the final scheduled for Saturday, August 29 — one day before the US Open main draw begins at Flushing Meadows.

What makes this event particularly meaningful is the timing. Every player who competes in a Winston-Salem semifinal or final this week carries that match experience and court rhythm directly into the US Open. In a field full of players who have been on the practice court but not in match play, the Winston-Salem finalists arrive in New York with something valuable: recent results under pressure.

Carlos Alcaraz, notably, is not in the draw. He confirmed Thursday that he's going straight to the US Open without a tune-up event — a different kind of bet on his readiness.

The Winston-Salem title this year carries an unusual weight. The champion lifts the trophy Saturday, then boards a flight to New York with a head full of momentum and a calendar full of hard-court matches ahead.
