### Long-Form Post Carlos Alcaraz is coming back. And he's going straight to the biggest stage. Alcaraz confirmed Thursday that he will compete in the 2026 US Open — his first professional match since withdrawing from the Barcelona Open in April with a right wrist injury (tenosynovitis). He skipped the French Open, Wimbledon, the Canadian Open, Cincinnati, and Winston-Salem during his recovery. He returns as the No. 2 seed at Flushing Meadows. This is a high-wire act. Four months off the tour, no tune-up event, stepping directly into a Grand Slam draw with the whole tennis world watching. His only competitive match play during this stretch was in the US Open mixed doubles — a run that lasted through the quarterfinals (alongside Serena Williams) before losing to Cobolli and Bencic. The wrist is healthy. The rust is unknown. The draw has him seeded second, which means a potential semifinal against Zverev or Djokovic. Sunday, August 30, will tell us a lot. ### Image Post Caption Alcaraz is back. No tune-up. Straight to the US Open. Sunday, Aug 30. 🎾

Jordan Lee and American Teens Fall in Qualifying
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post The American teenage experiment in US Open qualifying came to an end Wednesday. Jordan Lee, the 16-year-old Wimbledon junior champion from Orlando, Florida, fell to David Rincon of Spain in the second round of qualifying, 6-3, 4-6, 6-1. Lee had shown real fight in the first round — defeating Mark Lajal 4-6, 6-3, 6-3 in a three-set battle — but Rincon was the more experienced player and it showed in the third set. Michael Antonius and Andrew Johnson, two other American teenagers who had advanced into the second round, also lost on the same day. It was a sweep in the wrong direction for the American youth contingent. None of this diminishes what Lee accomplished this week. Coming to Flushing Meadows at 16, winning a round of qualifying at a major, and competing into the second round is exactly the kind of experience that builds professional players. The crowd will remember his name. He'll be back. ### Image Post Caption Jordan Lee, 16, went the distance. R2 loss to Rincon, 6-3, 4-6, 6-1. Next time. 🎾
