Montreal Round 3 Preview: FAA at Home, De Minaur vs. Norrie, and a Draw Wide Open for the Taking

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | August 6, 2026

When Alexander Zverev, Daniil Medvedev, and Taylor Fritz all lost on the same Wednesday in Montreal, the National Bank Open's carefully seeded draw effectively ceased to exist. What remains heading into Thursday's Round 3 is something far more interesting: a genuinely open tournament where the player who wants it most has a real chance of winning it.

Thursday features a full slate of matches, and four in particular stand out.

FAA at Home

Felix Auger-Aliassime is playing the biggest Masters 1000 tournament of his career on home soil in Canada. His career-first seeding at this level — entering the draw as the second seed — places him in rare territory for a Canadian player in his country's biggest event.

The Montreal crowd will be behind him entirely, and FAA has the game to make a run in a draw that has lost so much of its depth already. If he can convert that home support into a strong Round 3 performance, Thursday could be one of the most electric days the National Bank Open has seen in years.

De Minaur vs. Norrie

Third seed Alex de Minaur faces British No. 1 Cameron Norrie in a match between two of the most consistent baseline competitors on the ATP Tour. De Minaur's speed and defensive excellence make him difficult to beat over three sets, and he is one of the genuine title contenders remaining with Zverev and Medvedev gone.

Norrie, who has had a solid summer campaign, will be a tough test. But de Minaur at his best on hard courts is capable of controlling matches from start to finish.

Fils vs. Navone

Arthur Fils faces Mariano Navone in what could be the most entertaining match on Thursday's schedule. Navone is in the middle of a career-best 2026 season and arrived in Montreal after beating Matteo Berrettini in a gruelling three-setter that required him to save 11 of 12 break points. He has won 19 matches this year — a career high.

Fils, a powerful young Frenchman with big-match experience, will not make it easy. This is a match between two players trending in the right direction who each have something to prove.

Shelton Defending

Ben Shelton, the defending Montreal champion, has already won his Round 2 match and brings the weight of a title defence into his Round 3 appearance. Defending a Masters 1000 title is never easy — the pressure is different when the trophy is yours to protect — but Shelton's game is built for hard courts and Montreal crowds, and he enters Thursday as one of the tournament's most dangerous remaining players.

What's Next

Thursday's Round 3 results at the National Bank Open in Montreal will set the quarterfinal lineup for Friday. With the top seed, fourth seed, and eighth seed all eliminated, the remainder of the tournament is genuinely unpredictable — which is exactly what makes it worth watching.
