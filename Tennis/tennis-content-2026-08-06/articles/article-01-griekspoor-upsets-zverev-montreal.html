Griekspoor Upsets No. 1 Zverev in Montreal — Biggest Shock of 2026 Hard-Court Swing

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | August 6, 2026

Tallon Griekspoor walked onto the court in Montreal trailing Alexander Zverev 3-9 in their career head-to-head and having lost their last four meetings. He left having done something no player had managed since 2022: knock out the National Bank Open's top seed in his opening match.

Griekspoor defeated No. 1 seed Zverev 6-7(3), 6-2, 6-4 on Wednesday in a performance that was as dominant as the scoreline suggests. The Dutchman struck 20 forehand winners throughout the match and was virtually untouchable at the net, winning 17 of 18 points when he advanced forward.

It stands as Griekspoor's eighth career win over a top-10 player, and it makes him the first to beat the No. 1 seed in their NBO opening match since Nick Kyrgios defeated Daniil Medvedev in 2022.

A Match of Two Halves

Zverev, the Roland Garros champion and world No. 2, won the opening set on a tiebreak and had every reason to feel comfortable. The German is one of the best players in the world on hard courts and had been the clear favourite entering the tournament.

But Griekspoor shifted gears in the second set. His forehand became a weapon rather than a tool — he was not just redirecting balls, he was dictating every exchange from deep in the court. The 30-year-old moved forward with purpose and refused to let Zverev settle into his usual rhythm from the baseline.

By the time the third set began, the momentum was entirely with Griekspoor. He served it out without drama, completing a win that stunned the Montreal crowd and immediately announced that this year's tournament would be unpredictable from start to finish.

Context in the Draw

Zverev was one of the three players who, along with Daniil Medvedev and Taylor Fritz, fell on the same Wednesday in Montreal. The top seed's early exit blows open the top half of the draw and hands genuine opportunity to players who would not ordinarily expect a clear path to the semifinals of a Masters 1000 event.

Griekspoor now advances to Round 3 as one of the more dangerous remaining players in that section of the bracket. The form he showed against Zverev — particularly his aggressive net approach and relentless forehand production — is the kind of tennis that can beat anyone on hard courts on a given day.

What's Next

Griekspoor moves into Round 3 at the National Bank Open in Montreal, where the draw has opened considerably following the upsets of Zverev, Medvedev, and Fritz. A quarterfinal run is within reach for the Dutchman if he can reproduce Wednesday's level.

For Zverev, attention turns to the Cincinnati Open (August 13-23) and the US Open (August 24), where he will remain one of the sport's most formidable competitors despite this early exit in Canada.
