Montreal's Day of Chaos: Van de Zandschulp Stuns Medvedev, Tirante Ousts Fritz as Three Seeds Fall

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | August 6, 2026

One upset would have been a story. Two would have been remarkable. Three top-eight seeds falling on the same afternoon in Montreal? That is something else entirely.

While Tallon Griekspoor was knocking out No. 1 seed Alexander Zverev on Wednesday at the National Bank Open, two equally dramatic results were playing out elsewhere in the draw. Botic van de Zandschulp defeated fourth seed Daniil Medvedev 6-3, 7-6(5), and Thiago Agustin Tirante eliminated eighth seed Taylor Fritz 7-5, 6-3. By the time the day's matches were complete, the Montreal draw had been turned upside down.

Van de Zandschulp Outlasts Medvedev

Van de Zandschulp's win over Medvedev came in one hour and 39 minutes and required the Dutchman to hold firm when his opponent mounted a comeback in the second set.

After winning the opening set 6-3, Van de Zandschulp raced out to a 3-0 lead in the second. Medvedev — known for his ability to fight back from seemingly impossible positions — steadied himself and clawed his way back to level, eventually forcing a tiebreak at 6-6. But Van de Zandschulp took the tiebreak 7-5 to seal a win that improved his head-to-head record against Medvedev to 1-4.

It is one of the defining results of Van de Zandschulp's career — a clean, composed win over a former world No. 1 and one of the hardest players to put away on any surface.

Tirante Exposes Fritz's Physical Struggles

The story of the Tirante-Fritz match was as much about what Fritz could not do as what his Argentine opponent did well.

Fritz arrived in Montreal as the reigning DC Open champion, having beaten Rafael Jodar in the final in Washington just days earlier. But the physical toll of that run — combined with the quick turnaround to Montreal — appeared to catch up with the American in the second set. Fritz converted zero break points throughout the match and struggled to generate the power that defines his game at his best.

Tirante, relentless from the baseline, won 7-5, 6-3 to record the third top-10 win of his career. It is the kind of breakthrough result that can reset a player's confidence for the rest of the season.

A Historic Afternoon

The significance of losing three top-eight seeds in one day at a Masters 1000 event cannot be overstated. Draws of this depth rely on their seeded players staying in the tournament to generate the marquee matchups that follow in the later rounds. When three fall simultaneously, everything changes.

For the remaining players in Montreal, Wednesday's results represent an opportunity. For the tournament's lower seeds and qualifiers, the path to the final has never looked clearer.

What's Next

Van de Zandschulp and Tirante advance to Round 3 on Thursday, joining Griekspoor in an unfamiliar role as potential quarterfinal threats. Medvedev and Fritz will look to reset and prepare for the Cincinnati Open, which begins August 13, and then the US Open starting August 24.

Montreal has already delivered more chaos in two days than many tournaments manage in a full week. There is every reason to expect more to follow.
