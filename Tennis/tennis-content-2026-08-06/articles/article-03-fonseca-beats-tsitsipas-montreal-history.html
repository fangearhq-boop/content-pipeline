Fonseca Beats Tsitsipas in Montreal — First Brazilian to Reach Canadian Masters R3 Since Kuerten

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | August 6, 2026

Gustavo Kuerten is one of the most beloved tennis players Brazil has ever produced — a three-time French Open champion and a man whose name still resonates decades after his prime. On Wednesday in Montreal, 19-year-old Joao Fonseca added a footnote to that legacy by becoming the first Brazilian to reach the Round of 16 at the Canadian ATP Masters 1000 since Kuerten himself.

Fonseca defeated Stefanos Tsitsipas 7-6(3), 7-5 in Round 2 of the National Bank Open, completing a straight-sets win that featured a memorable final sequence in which the teenager regrouped after dropping his serve while attempting to close the match.

The Drama at 5-4

Fonseca had the second set under control, serving for it at 5-4. What followed was the kind of moment that tests a player's mental composure at the highest level. He dropped his serve — handing Tsitsipas a lifeline and resetting the tension in the match.

Lesser players might have tightened. Fonseca went the other direction. He won eight of the next nine points, broke back immediately, and closed out the match to complete one of the standout wins of his young career. The mental quality he showed in that sequence will matter more in the long run than the scoreline.

Building a Head-to-Head Record

Fonseca's ATP head-to-head record against Tsitsipas now stands at 2-0. He also defeated the Greek star in Davis Cup action last year, which means he has beaten one of the top-20 players in the world three times across different competitions without losing once.

That consistency against a specific opponent at this early stage of a career suggests more than luck. There is something about Fonseca's game — likely his aggressive serve and ability to take the ball early — that creates problems for Tsitsipas in particular.

Back After Wimbledon

Fonseca had not played a tour-level match since losing to Roman Safiullin in the third round of Wimbledon last month. His return to competition could not have gone much better. His serve was sharp throughout, and his ability to construct points aggressively from the back of the court was exactly what Montreal needed to see from the Brazilian.

This is a player who entered the season with enormous expectations — and Wednesday's win is another reminder that those expectations are not misplaced.

What's Next

Fonseca advances to Round 3 at the National Bank Open in Montreal, where he joins a field that has already lost Zverev, Medvedev, and Fritz to upsets. The draw is wide open, and the 19-year-old from Brazil has as good a chance as anyone to make a genuine run at the title. His next match will be the true test of whether this is a one-week story or the beginning of a longer breakthrough.
