### STORY 1: Griekspoor Upsets Zverev

**Long-Form Post (8:00 AM ET):**
We knew Montreal was going to be exciting — but nobody saw this coming.

Tallon Griekspoor defeated No. 1 seed Alexander Zverev 6-7(3), 6-2, 6-4 on Wednesday, handing the Roland Garros champion one of the most surprising losses of the entire 2026 season.

Going into the match, Griekspoor had lost four straight meetings with Zverev and trailed their career head-to-head 3-9. None of that mattered once play started. The Dutchman was absolutely clinical from the baseline — 20 forehand winners — and ruthless at the net, winning 17 of 18 net points.

After dropping the first set in a tiebreak, Griekspoor took control and never looked back, taking the second 6-2 and the third 6-4 to complete the biggest upset of the Montreal tournament so far.

It is the eighth top-10 win of his career, and he becomes the first player to beat the top seed in their NBO opening match since Nick Kyrgios defeated Daniil Medvedev in 2022.

With Zverev gone, the top half of the draw is suddenly very different. Griekspoor now moves into Round 3 as one of the more dangerous players left in the field.

What a way to start the Rogers Cup week.

### STORY 2: Van de Zandschulp and Tirante Upsets

**Long-Form Post (10:30 AM ET):**
If you blinked, you missed the wildest day of the 2026 hard-court season.

On the same Wednesday that Zverev was being knocked out by Griekspoor, two more jaw-dropping results were playing out at the National Bank Open in Montreal.

Botic van de Zandschulp defeated fourth seed Daniil Medvedev 6-3, 7-6(5) in an hour and 39 minutes. Medvedev battled from 0-3 down in the second set to force a tiebreak — and still lost. Van de Zandschulp was simply too solid.

At the same time, Thiago Agustin Tirante took down eighth seed Taylor Fritz 7-5, 6-3. Fritz — who had just celebrated winning the DC Open title in Washington less than a week earlier — converted zero break points and struggled physically throughout the second set. He could not find a way to stop Tirante, who was relentless from the back of the court and recorded just the third top-10 win of his career.

In total: Zverev (No. 1 seed), Medvedev (No. 4 seed), and Fritz (No. 8 seed) all lost on the same day in Montreal. Three of the top eight seeds gone in one afternoon.

The draw is completely blown open.

### STORY 3: Fonseca Beats Tsitsipas

**Long-Form Post (12:30 PM ET):**
Joao Fonseca just made a little slice of Brazilian tennis history.

The 19-year-old defeated Stefanos Tsitsipas 7-6(3), 7-5 in Montreal to become the first Brazilian player to reach the Round of 16 at the Canadian ATP Masters 1000 since Gustavo Kuerten — one of the greatest clay-court players in the sport's history.

The match was not easy. Fonseca had the second set within his grasp, leading 5-4 and serving to close it out — only to drop his serve. What happened next was the defining moment. He won eight of the next nine points to seal the match and celebrate one of the standout wins of his young career.

His ATP head-to-head record against Tsitsipas now stands at 2-0. He also defeated the Greek star in Davis Cup action last year, which suggests there is something about how Fonseca matches up against the veteran that simply works in his favor.

Fonseca is back competing after losing to Safiullin in the third round of Wimbledon, and Montreal has reminded the tennis world why he is so exciting to watch. The next test comes in Round 3 — but this week, everything feels possible.

What do you think — can Fonseca make a run at the title?

### STORY 4: WTA Toronto R3

**Long-Form Post (3:00 PM ET):**
The US Open is just over two weeks away, and the WTA Toronto tournament is doing everything it can to sharpen the field's title contenders.

Thursday brings Round 3 action at the National Bank Open, and the two players most likely to be holding the trophy next week are both in action.

Aryna Sabalenka — who beat Moyuka Uchijima 6-3, 6-3 in her opener and has now won 13 of her last 14 hard-court matches in 2026 — faces Shuai Zhang, who advanced when Jelena Ostapenko retired from their match trailing 6-0, 4-0. Sabalenka leads their career head-to-head 4-1, so the numbers heavily favor the world No. 1.

Iga Swiatek, who dominated Sara Bejlek 6-0, 6-3 in her first match and is building momentum steadily, is also in action Thursday.

If both win, a Sabalenka-Swiatek quarterfinal or semifinal would be must-watch tennis heading into the US Open. It would give us a preview of what could be the biggest match in women's tennis this year.

Stay tuned — Thursday in Toronto could be a very big day.
