Can Shnaider Pull Another Toronto Upset? Swiatek Faces the Left-Hander Tonight

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | August 10, 2026

Tonight at Centre Court in Toronto, Iga Swiatek plays Diana Shnaider in the quarterfinals. On paper, Swiatek is the clear favourite — ranked No. 8, a former world No. 1, with a head-to-head record of 1-0 over her opponent. In practice, nothing about this tournament has followed the expected order.

Ekaterina Alexandrova just beat Sabalenka and Svitolina in back-to-back rounds. The world No. 1 has already packed her bags. Toronto is the kind of tournament, on this kind of week, where the expected order requires more scrutiny than usual.

Shnaider deserves to be taken seriously in this match. Her win over Jessica Pegula in the last round — 6-3, 6-2 in 73 minutes — was not close. Pegula is one of the most consistently solid players in women's tennis. Shnaider beat her without sustaining a competitive set, directing the match from the first game to the last with her left-handed serve and the angles that come naturally to a player constructed differently from most of her opponents.

Shnaider's Week in Toronto

Diana Shnaider is 22 years old and unseeded in this draw. She has beaten higher-ranked players before — her trajectory in 2025 and 2026 has included wins over top-20 opponents at various points — but Montreal 2026 represents something different in terms of the stage and the moment.

Left-handers create specific problems on hard courts. The serve angle, the forehand direction, the tendency to drag opponents wide on the deuce side before redirecting down the line — these are not disadvantages that training fully neutralises. The best players in the world have learned to manage them, but managing a problem is different from eliminating it.

Shnaider beat Pegula in 73 minutes. She won the first set 6-3 and the second 6-2. There was not a period in that match where Pegula appeared to find her game. That is meaningful context for what Swiatek faces tonight.

Swiatek's Road to the Quarterfinal

Swiatek's path to the Toronto quarterfinal included the most significant result of her North American swing: beating Marta Kostyuk 3-6, 6-1, 6-2 in the fourth round. The win was important beyond the scoreline — Kostyuk had beaten her at Roland Garros in May, and the ability to recover from that result and dispatch the same opponent on a different surface carries psychological weight.

Swiatek dropped the first set before dominating the next twelve games. That pattern — slow start, decisive recovery — has been a feature of her tournament. It has also been a feature of her season. She is not the player she was at the peak of her dominance in 2023 and 2024, but she is a dangerous quarterfinal opponent, and tonight's match gives her the chance to reach a Toronto semifinal.

The head-to-head with Shnaider is 1-0 in Swiatek's favour, from their meeting in Madrid in 2025. That match was on clay. The surfaces are different enough that the result cannot be treated as fully predictive.

What's Next

The Swiatek-Shnaider quarterfinal is scheduled for the evening session at approximately 7:00 PM ET. The winner advances to the Toronto semifinal, where Alexandrova is already waiting after her wins over Sabalenka and Svitolina.

If Swiatek wins, she sets up a semifinal that pits two players — herself and Alexandrova — who arrived in Toronto without being expected to reach this stage as a potential headline final. If Shnaider wins, she enters that same conversation as an unseeded player who would have beaten both Pegula and a former world No. 1 in consecutive rounds.

Toronto is producing its stories exactly as it tends to: without much concern for what the seedings suggested was supposed to happen.
