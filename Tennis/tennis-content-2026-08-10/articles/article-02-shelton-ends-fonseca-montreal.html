Shelton Ends Fonseca's Remarkable Montreal Run — Defending Champion Advances to QF

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | August 10, 2026

Ben Shelton defeated Joao Fonseca 6-3, 7-6(3) in the Montreal last-16 to advance to the quarterfinals. The most anticipated match of the Canadian swing lasted less time than the buildup suggested it would, and the defending champion was the reason.

Shelton broke twice in the match. He handled the tiebreak in the second set with the efficiency you expect from a player who has been here before. Fonseca, who had beaten Grigor Tsitsipas in the second round and Casper Ruud in the third, found in Shelton an opponent whose ceiling was higher than the run that brought him to the match.

The score does not fully capture what happened, but it does not distort it either. This was Shelton operating well within himself against the toughest opponent he had faced in the tournament. He did not need brilliance. He needed professionalism. He found it in two sets.

Fonseca's Montreal Story

Joao Fonseca arrived in Montreal having beaten Tsitsipas and Ruud in consecutive matches. That sequence — a former world No. 3 in round two, a two-time Grand Slam finalist in round three — is not a typical teenager's week at a Masters 1000 event. It earned him the crowd, the attention, and the expectation that the story would continue.

Shelton was the wall the story ran into. The 2-0 head-to-head between them (Shelton also beat Fonseca in Munich earlier in 2026) proved to be a reasonable guide to what happened. Fonseca played with the energy and intent that characterised his whole week. Shelton played with the steadiness of a defending champion who has been in these situations before.

Fonseca is 19 years old. His US Open is a fortnight away. Montreal was a significant statement for a player who is still adding competitive miles to his game at the highest level.

Shelton's Path to the QF

Shelton has not dropped a set this tournament. That detail matters for a defending champion who arrived in Montreal with the pressure of protecting a title he won in 2025. The draw has cleared significantly around him — Zverev, Medvedev, Fritz, Hurkacz, Ruud, and Paul were all eliminated before Monday — but Shelton's performances have not been the product of easy competition alone.

His quarterfinal opponent is Jakub Mensik, the Czech No. 13 seed who defeated Botic van de Zandschulp 6-4, 7-5 in the last round. Mensik has been one of the more impressive players in the draw, winning 81 percent of his first-serve points in that match and converting three of four break-point chances. Shelton will face something different on Tuesday than he found in any of his previous rounds.

What's Next

Shelton faces Mensik in the quarterfinals on Tuesday or Wednesday. The other side of his half-draw includes Tien, Merida, Fils, and Jodar — a bracket without a clear favourite beyond the defending champion. If Shelton wins his half of the draw, he arrives in the final with fewer familiar opponents than he might have expected when the week began.

Fonseca's summer continues. He is in the Cincinnati entry list and the US Open is three weeks away. Montreal ended when it had to end — against the defending champion, in a tiebreak, with the crowd on his side and Shelton unmoved by any of it.
