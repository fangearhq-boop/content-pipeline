Alexandrova Into the Toronto Semifinals — Two Wins Over Top Players in 48 Hours

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | August 10, 2026

Ekaterina Alexandrova did it again on Monday. Two days after ending Aryna Sabalenka's Toronto title defence, she defeated Elina Svitolina in the quarterfinals in two hours and 29 minutes to reach the tournament's last four.

The numbers say a great deal about how she did it: eight aces, five of thirteen break points converted, a clinical performance against an opponent who was 40-11 on the season before walking onto Centre Court. Svitolina won this Toronto tournament in 2017. She is one of the most experienced players in women's tennis. Alexandrova did not give her a path.

Forty-eight hours earlier, Alexandrova had beaten the world No. 1 Sabalenka 7-6(3), 4-6, 6-4. That was one of the most significant upsets of the Canadian swing. What has happened since has turned it into the opening chapter of something more.

The 48-Hour Arc

To understand what Alexandrova has accomplished at this tournament, consider the sequence. On Saturday, she faced Sabalenka — the top seed, the defending champion, the world No. 1 on the most dominant hard-court run in women's tennis. She won in three sets. The head-to-head, which was 4-5 in Sabalenka's favour, levelled at five wins apiece.

On Monday, she came back against a different kind of challenge. Svitolina does not overpower opponents the way Sabalenka does; she constructs points, moves well, and makes the kind of errors that look like tactical decisions rather than mistakes. Alexandrova handled her with aces and break-point efficiency — eight of the former, five converted from thirteen opportunities on the latter.

She has not dropped a set in either of these two matches. That is not a detail. Against Sabalenka and Svitolina in back-to-back rounds, not dropping a set represents a level of performance that very few players in the draw could have matched.

What This Run Means

Alexandrova's win over Sabalenka on Saturday was already her third career victory over a world No. 1 — she previously beat Swiatek at Miami in 2024 and Sabalenka at Doha in 2025. Adding Svitolina in a quarterfinal the same week extends a story that is no longer about a single result.

There is a way to characterise these wins as flukes — draws lined up, confidence carried over, the world No. 1 had an off week. That explanation has limits. Alexandrova has beaten three different players of the highest quality in three separate tournaments, twice at the most consequential points of the week. At some stage, the pattern becomes more interesting than the exceptions that supposedly explain it.

She is now in the Toronto semifinals. Players still left in the draw include Coco Gauff and Elena Rybakina. The bracket has opened in significant ways since Sabalenka's exit, and Alexandrova is the reason.

What's Next

Alexandrova plays her semifinal on Tuesday or Wednesday. The opponent will be determined by the other quarterfinals completing Monday. The WTA Toronto title carries significant ranking points and could be the biggest result of her career if she reaches and wins the final.

The US Open begins on August 30. Whatever happens in Toronto this week, Alexandrova arrives at Flushing Meadows with wins over two of the most prominent players in the draw on her recent record. Nobody entering this tournament expected that sentence to be written on Monday afternoon. It is.
