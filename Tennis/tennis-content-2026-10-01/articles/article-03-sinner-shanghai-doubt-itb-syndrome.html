Sinner Unlikely to Play Shanghai — Iliotibial Band Syndrome Identified, Five Events and Counting

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | October 1, 2026

The Jannik Sinner injury story keeps getting harder to read. Italian media outlet tennisnow.com reported Thursday that Sinner is "unlikely to play Shanghai" — the Masters 1000 event beginning October 7 — which would extend the world No. 1's match absence to five consecutive events since winning Wimbledon in July.

The injury has been identified as iliotibial band syndrome: a thick fibrous band of tissue running along the outer thigh to the tibia. It is a common overuse injury, particularly in athletes who perform repetitive lateral movements. Conservative treatment — rest, physical therapy, and gradual rehabilitation — is ongoing. Surgery has been mentioned as a contingency if progress stalls, but Sinner's team has not indicated it is under active consideration.

The Absence Grows

Sinner's last competitive match was his Wimbledon final victory on July 13, where he defeated Carlos Alcaraz to claim the title. Since then, he has withdrawn from the Canadian Open, Cincinnati Open, U.S. Open, and China Open. If Shanghai is also missed, the count reaches five — a stretch approaching 90 consecutive days without a competitive match for the player who entered 2026 as the world's top-ranked player and defending Wimbledon champion.

The Australian Open champion made his position clear after the China Open withdrawal: "My knee is not where I would like it to be and I'm not ready to compete yet. I will do everything possible to be there on court as soon as I can." The Shanghai development suggests the knee remains a barrier.

ATP Race Implications

Sinner remains No. 1 in the official ATP Rankings — he holds a 1,810-point lead over Alexander Zverev — but the Race to Turin picture is more complicated. Zverev leads the Race at 8,650 points to Sinner's 7,950. Crucially, Sinner is projected to drop an additional 3,550 Race points through year-end due to results he is defending from 2025 but will not repeat. Zverev, by contrast, is projected to drop only around 1,080 points.

That arithmetic makes Zverev the heavy favorite to finish the year as Race No. 1 — and potentially to overtake Sinner in the official ATP Rankings as well — if the Italian's absence continues through the Asian swing.

What's Next

No official withdrawal from Shanghai has been filed as of Thursday. The next realistic return window for Sinner, based on the current injury timeline, would be the Vienna Open in late October or the Paris Masters in November. Whether he can compete at the ATP Finals in Turin — which he has already qualified for on points — would depend on his recovery over the next six weeks. His team has not set a public timeline for return.
