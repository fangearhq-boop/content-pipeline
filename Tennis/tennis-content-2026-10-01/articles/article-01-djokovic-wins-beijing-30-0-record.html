Djokovic Beats Borges in Beijing, Extends Perfect Record to 30-0 in First Match Since Wimbledon

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | October 1, 2026

Novak Djokovic walked back onto a tennis court Thursday for the first time since July 7 — and produced the kind of performance that makes you wonder why anyone ever doubted him. The Serbian champion defeated Nuno Borges 6-3, 7-6(2) at the China Open in Beijing, extending his all-time record in the Chinese capital to 30-0 and answering the loudest question hovering over his fall season.

His last match before today was a Wimbledon quarterfinal loss. Nearly three months later, Djokovic showed no rust in the first set, racing through it in dominant fashion. A medical timeout at 2-3 in the second set paused the match and briefly raised concerns, but he regrouped and closed the tiebreak 7-2 with the kind of efficiency that 20-time Grand Slam champions tend to summon.

30-0 and Six Titles

The number tells the story. Djokovic has played 30 matches in Beijing in his career. He has won all 30. He has also won the China Open six times — the most of any player in the tournament's history. Thursday was his first appearance at the event since 2015, and the 11-year gap between visits didn't seem to affect his connection with the crowd or the court.

His opponent, Portugal's Nuno Borges, had beaten Djokovic once before — in Athens in 2024 — but there was little of that competitive fire on display here. Djokovic controlled the match from the baseline, dictating rallies and giving Borges few clean looks at his return games.

Context: The Injury Layoff

Djokovic has been sidelined since his Wimbledon quarterfinal, where he fell to Carlos Alcaraz in five sets. The nature and timeline of his recovery were not publicly disclosed in detail, but his withdrawal from the U.S. Open and a quiet absence from the tour through September created uncertainty about when — and how well — he would return.

Thursday provided the first real data point. A single match is not a verdict, but 6-3, 7-6(2) over a quality opponent is a good opening statement. The medical timeout may require monitoring as the tournament progresses, but Djokovic has historically managed his body through long events.

What's Next

Djokovic's second-round opponent will be determined by the bracket as the China Open continues through October 6. Top seed Alexander Zverev — who defeated Cam Norrie in his first-round match — sits in the same quarter of the draw as Djokovic, making a potential quarterfinal meeting between the two the match to watch. The ATP 500 title in Beijing is the kind of result that could reinvigorate Djokovic's late-season push before the Shanghai Masters the following week.
