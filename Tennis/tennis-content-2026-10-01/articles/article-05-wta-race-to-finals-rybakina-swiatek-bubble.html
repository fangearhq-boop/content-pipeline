WTA Race to the Finals: Rybakina Leads, Swiatek Fights from 10th as Beijing Looms Large

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | October 1, 2026

Elena Rybakina enters the China Open as the WTA World No. 1 and leads the Race to Finals with 7,492 points — but the standings behind her tell a story of pressure, recalibration, and at least one stunning fall from grace. Iga Swiatek, who won the WTA Finals four consecutive times from 2020 to 2023 and held the No. 1 ranking for most of the past three years, enters Beijing this week ranked 10th in the Race with 3,584 points. She is 650 points outside the top-8 qualifying bubble.

The China Open — a WTA 1000 event running through October 11 — may be Swiatek's best remaining chance to reverse her year's trajectory.

The Standings

Rybakina leads comfortably. Aryna Sabalenka is second at 6,485, sitting 1,007 points back. Below them, the standings compress quickly: Jessica Pegula (5,825), Mirra Andreeva (5,722), Coco Gauff (5,654), Elina Svitolina (4,809), Karolina Muchova (4,400), and Linda Noskova (4,234) currently occupy the eight qualifying spots. Noskova, in eighth, has just 650 points on Swiatek in ninth-place Marta Kostyuk (3,910) and 10th-place Swiatek.

For Swiatek, the math is achievable but urgent. A China Open title awards 1,000 WTA Finals qualification points. A semifinal run delivers 600. Either result would lift her into or very near the bubble. An early exit, by contrast, could effectively close the door.

Rybakina's Historic Context

The woman who holds No. 1 did so for the first time just weeks ago. Rybakina — who reached the top of the WTA Rankings on September 14 after winning the 2026 U.S. Open over Sabalenka — is the first Kazakh player in history, male or female, to hold the top ranking in professional tennis. She carries three Grand Slam titles: Wimbledon 2022, Australian Open 2026, and U.S. Open 2026. Her first WTA 1000 tournament as No. 1 is this week in Beijing.

The top of the WTA Rankings is now a legitimate three-way contest. Rybakina leads. Sabalenka, who held No. 1 for 99 consecutive weeks before the U.S. Open, is still one good run away from retaking the top spot. Gauff, fresh off a strong fall calendar, continues to rise. Beijing could reshuffle the hierarchy again.

What's Next

The WTA China Open continues through October 11, making it one of the final major opportunities for bubble players to accumulate points before the WTA Finals field is set. Rybakina, Sabalenka, Gauff, and Andreeva — all top seeds — received byes into the second round. Swiatek will need to navigate a loaded field in what is, by every measure, a must-run week in her 2026 season.
