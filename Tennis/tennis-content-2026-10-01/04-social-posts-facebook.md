### STORY 1: Djokovic Extends Beijing Record to 30-0

#### Long-Form Post -- 8:00 AM ET

Novak Djokovic walked onto a Beijing hard court for the first time in nearly three months — and he looked like he'd never left.

Playing his first tour match since the Wimbledon quarterfinals on July 7, Djokovic defeated Nuno Borges 6-3, 7-6(2) at the China Open on Thursday. A medical timeout at 2-3 in the second set raised eyebrows, but the Serbian legend closed the tiebreak 7-2 without drama. His Beijing record is now 30-0. He has won six titles there. He hasn't lost a match in that city in his entire career.

The win settles an important question hanging over the back half of his season: is Djokovic fit enough to compete at the top level? Thursday suggests yes. He'll next face a tougher test as the tournament deepens — a potential quarterfinal against top seed Alexander Zverev looms as one of the week's marquee matchups.

### STORY 3: Sinner Shanghai Now in Doubt

#### Long-Form Post -- 12:30 PM ET

Every week brings a new chapter in the Jannik Sinner injury saga — and this week's is the hardest yet.

Italian media are now reporting that Sinner is "unlikely to play Shanghai," the Masters 1000 event beginning October 7. The injury has been identified as iliotibial band syndrome — a thick fibrous band running along the outer thigh to the tibia — and conservative treatment is ongoing. Surgery remains a last resort if progress stalls.

If Shanghai falls, that would be five consecutive events missed since Wimbledon: Canadian Open, Cincinnati, U.S. Open, China Open, Shanghai. No match since July 7. The world No. 1 is watching the Asian hard-court swing from the sideline while his Race to Turin lead over Zverev shrinks with each day that passes.

The next realistic return window — Vienna in late October — is still three weeks away.
