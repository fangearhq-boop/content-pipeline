### STORY 1: Montreal QF Blitz — Three Americans in the Semis

#### Long-Form Post

Montreal handed American tennis a moment it hasn't had in 34 years.

Ben Shelton was first. He defeated Jakub Mensik 6-3, 6-1 — not just in scoreline but in every single stat. Shelton hit 18 winners and only 3 unforced errors. He won 89% of first-serve points. It was the most complete performance by any player in the Montreal draw this week.

Then Learner Tien beat Daniel Merida 6-3, 6-2. Then Brandon Nakashima dismantled Luciano Darderi 6-2, 6-3 in just 1 hour and 17 minutes, firing 11 aces and never facing a break point.

Three Americans in the semifinal of a Masters 1000. The last time this happened was 1992 — Agassi, Lendl, and MaliVai Washington in Montreal. Before Djokovic was a professional. Before Federer held a racket at tour level.

Tonight's SFs: Shelton vs. Tien on one side. Jodar vs. Nakashima on the other.

Who are you backing to win the whole thing?

### STORY 2: Jodar Makes Masters SF History

#### Long-Form Post

Rafael Jodar walked into 2026 ranked No. 168 in the world. He was 18 years old. Barely on tour.

Today, the 19-year-old Spaniard became the first player born in 2006 or later to reach a Masters 1000 semifinal after defeating Arthur Fils 7-6(5), 6-3 in Montreal. His live ATP ranking sits at No. 11. He has won 34 matches this season against just 13 losses.

This is his fourth semifinal of 2026. He was the runner-up at the DC Open ATP 500 two weeks ago, losing to Taylor Fritz in the final. He has SFs at Marrakech, Barcelona, Washington, and now Montreal.

Tonight he plays Brandon Nakashima in the first Montreal SF. If Jodar wins, he will become one of the youngest Masters finalists in the sport's modern era.

The player everyone is talking about in Montreal is not the defending champion. It's the 19-year-old from Spain who wasn't supposed to be here yet.

What do you think — can Jodar go all the way tonight?

### STORY 3: Rybakina Beats Osaka; Gauff Walkover

#### Long-Form Post

Elena Rybakina was down a set and a break in the second set against Naomi Osaka. Most players don't come back from there. Rybakina did.

She won the second set in a tiebreak, 7-5. Then she won the third 6-4, serving 13 aces across the match including one to seal it. In the third set alone, she won all 18 points on her first serve. That's the Rybakina that wins Grand Slams.

Osaka played well for long stretches — the first set was genuine — but the serve was too much in the end. The match lasted 2 hours and 33 minutes.

On the other side of the draw, Coco Gauff received a walkover after Belinda Bencic withdrew with a hip injury. Gauff is rested. Rybakina has just played a 2h33m battle.

Tomorrow's Toronto SF: Gauff vs. Rybakina. Their only previous meeting was also in Toronto — in 2022, when Gauff edged it in a third-set tiebreak. Four years later, same city.

Who wins the rematch?

### STORY 4: Cincinnati ATP Draw

#### Long-Form Post

The Cincinnati draw is out. Here's what you need to know.

Alexander Zverev is the top seed after Jannik Sinner (right knee) and Carlos Alcaraz (wrist) both withdrew before the tournament. Novak Djokovic returns to the tour as No. 3 seed, his first event since Wimbledon.

The big story in the draw: Zverev and Djokovic are in the same half. They could meet before the final — potentially as early as the quarterfinals. Also in that half: Rafael Jodar at seed No. 12, who is still playing tonight in Montreal.

The full top eight: Zverev, FAA, Djokovic, Medvedev, de Minaur, Fritz, Cobolli, Shelton.

Cincinnati has been the last Masters tune-up before the US Open for decades. With Sinner and Alcaraz both absent, this is one of the most open Cincinnati draws in years. Someone is going to emerge from this draw with real momentum heading into Flushing.

Who do you think wins Cincinnati?

### STORY 5: Cincinnati WTA Draw

#### Long-Form Post

The Cincinnati WTA draw is also out, and it's as loaded as you'd expect this close to the US Open.

Aryna Sabalenka is the top seed, coming in motivated after her Toronto round of 16 exit. Elena Rybakina is second. Jessica Pegula is third, Coco Gauff fourth. Iga Swiatek enters as the defending champion after winning Cincinnati in 2025.

Ten Grand Slam champions are in the field. The prize money is $7.4 million. This is one of the best fields on the women's tour all year, concentrated into one week before the US Open.

Sabalenka vs. Rybakina in the final would be the matchup everyone has circled. Swiatek arrives as champion but will need to get through whoever survives Toronto first. And Gauff enters having just received a walkover — rested but potentially match-rusty.

Who comes out of Cincinnati with the best US Open momentum?
