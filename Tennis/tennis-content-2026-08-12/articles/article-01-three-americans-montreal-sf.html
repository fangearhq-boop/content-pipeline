Shelton Dominates Mensik as Three Americans Reach Montreal Semis for First Time Since 1992

By Marcus Cole August 12, 2026

A Day of American Tennis That Hasn't Happened in 34 Years

Ben Shelton walked off the IGA Stadium court having beaten Jakub Mensik 6-3, 6-1. Not close in scoreline, and not close in any of the underlying numbers either. Shelton fired 18 winners against 3 unforced errors and won 89% of points on his first serve. It was the most one-sided performance in the Montreal draw since the first round.

Then Learner Tien beat Daniel Merida 6-3, 6-2. Then Brandon Nakashima dismantled Luciano Darderi 6-2, 6-3 in 1 hour and 17 minutes, serving 11 aces without losing a service game. Three Americans in four quarterfinals. Three Americans in the Montreal semifinals.

It last happened in Canada in 1992. Andre Agassi, Ivan Lendl, and MaliVai Washington were the three that year. Djokovic and Federer were not yet professionals. The current generation of European dominance had not started. And now, 34 years later, Shelton, Tien, and Nakashima have put American men back in that conversation at a Masters 1000.

Shelton's Numbers Tell the Story

The defending champion was supposed to face his stiffest test of the week against Mensik. The Czech 13th seed led the career head-to-head 2-1 and had beaten the kind of player — Van de Zandschulp — who typically tests Shelton's movement. Instead, Shelton won the match in 62 minutes and left Mensik almost nothing to work with.

Eighteen winners, three unforced errors, 89% first-serve points. Those are the numbers of a player who is not just in form but in the kind of locked-in form that wins tournaments. His winning run in Canada now stands at 10 matches, stretching back to his title run in 2025. No player in the Montreal draw has been more consistent, more dominant, or more convincing.

Tonight he faces Learner Tien, another American, in the second semifinal. Tien defeated Merida 6-3, 6-2 in a straightforward win that shows he too has not been troubled much this week. Two Americans on the same side of a Masters draw at the semifinal stage.

Nakashima Makes His Case in the Other Half

Brandon Nakashima's win over Darderi was different in texture from Shelton's but equally emphatic in its result. Nakashima served 11 aces, never faced a break point, and won the match in under an hour and 20 minutes. Darderi had been one of the draw's most dangerous players on clay this season but couldn't make anything stick against Nakashima's serve.

Nakashima faces Rafael Jodar of Spain in the first semifinal, starting at 6 PM ET. Jodar is 19 and has risen from No. 168 in the world to No. 11 in the live rankings this season. He is the only non-American in the final four, and the only player in the draw under 20 years old. A Jodar-Nakashima match between two former University of Virginia players adds another subplot to what is already an unusually compelling evening of tennis.

What's Next

The Montreal semifinals run tonight, August 12. Jodar vs. Nakashima goes first, starting at 6 PM ET, followed by Shelton vs. Tien. The winner of each match plays in Wednesday's final. For anyone tracking American men's tennis, tonight's two matches matter beyond Montreal: the US Open main draw begins August 24, and whoever performs well here arrives in Flushing with form and ranking points intact. Cincinnati, starting tomorrow, is the last Masters tune-up. Tonight is a preview of who arrives there with confidence.
