Rafael Jodar at 19: The First Player Born in 2006 to Reach a Masters 1000 Semifinal

By Elena Voss August 12, 2026

From No. 168 to No. 11 Live in One Season

Rafael Jodar started 2026 ranked No. 168 in the world. He was 18 years old and had won his first ATP-level match just months before. On Tuesday night in Montreal, he beat Arthur Fils 7-6(5), 6-3 to become the first player born in 2006 or later to reach a Masters 1000 semifinal in ATP history.

He is now No. 11 in the live ATP rankings. He has gone 34-13 on the season. His fourth semifinal of the year came at the biggest stage he has played on, and he won it against Fils, a player ranked inside the top 20 and in the semifinal of this very tournament one year ago.

The 2026 season has produced a generation of young ATP players who are ahead of schedule. Mensik won titles. Jodar has done something more: he has climbed from the fringes of the tour to the top 15 in nine months, while reaching the semis of a clay tournament in Marrakech, a clay title week in Barcelona, a hard-court ATP 500 final in Washington, and now a Masters 1000 semifinal in Montreal. None of those venues are the same. All of those results point in the same direction.

How He Beat Fils

The Jodar-Fils quarterfinal required a first-set tiebreak. Fils pushed hard and made Jodar work through the breaker, but Jodar took it 7-5. Once he had the first set, the second came more freely, 6-3. Fils is a natural hard-court player with big serving, and Jodar managed to neutralize that by keeping the rallies under control and breaking once at the right moment in the second.

What is consistent about Jodar's wins this season is that they don't usually look spectacular. He wins the right moments. He controls what he can control. For a 19-year-old in his first full tour season, that kind of match management is not common. The DC Open final against Fritz, which Jodar lost, was competitive. His Montreal run through a draw loaded with players in the top 20 was not a lucky draw. He beat real opponents.

Tonight: Jodar vs. Nakashima

The first Montreal SF, starting at 6 PM ET, pairs Jodar against Brandon Nakashima. Both players attended the University of Virginia. Nakashima won his QF against Darderi without dropping a service game and served 11 aces in under 80 minutes. He is 24, ranked higher than Jodar in the official standings, and playing in his first Masters SF as well.

If Jodar wins tonight, he will play in his first Masters final on Wednesday. He would be 19 years old, in a final against either Ben Shelton or Learner Tien. A Jodar win tonight would also mean he needs to be in Cincinnati for the Western and Southern Open, which starts tomorrow.

What's Next

Jodar vs. Nakashima begins at 6 PM ET tonight in Montreal, followed by Shelton vs. Tien. If Jodar wins tonight's SF, his summer only gets more intense: the Montreal final is Wednesday, and Cincinnati starts on the same day. The US Open main draw begins August 24. For a 19-year-old who started the year ranked No. 168, these are not small stakes. They are the biggest weeks of his career, arriving before most players his age have reached a first tour-level quarterfinal.
