Rybakina Fires 13 Aces to Beat Osaka in 2h33m Thriller; Gauff Gets Walkover into Toronto SF

By Ryan Calloway August 12, 2026

Down a Set and a Break — Rybakina Came Back Anyway

Naomi Osaka was a set up and a break up in the second set. Elena Rybakina was in a position that should have produced a straight-set victory for Japan's four-time Grand Slam winner. Rybakina responded with 13 aces and one of the better serving performances of the Toronto tournament.

The final scoreline was 4-6, 7-6(5), 6-4 in 2 hours and 33 minutes. But the shape of the match tells a more specific story. Osaka played well enough to win the first set. The second set went to a tiebreak at 7-5. In the third, Rybakina won all 18 points on her first serve and sealed the match with an ace. That is not a player who scraped through. That is a player who found the gear she needed when she needed it.

Gauff Advances Without Playing

On the opposite side of the quarterfinal draw, Coco Gauff received a walkover after Belinda Bencic withdrew with a hip injury. Gauff didn't need to play a point. She arrives in Friday's semifinal rested, while Rybakina has 2 hours and 33 minutes of work in her legs.

The last time Gauff and Rybakina met in a tournament match was at this same event in 2022. Gauff won that one in a third-set tiebreak. Four years later, same city, same surface, same tournament. Their second meeting is scheduled for August 13.

Toronto SFs and What They Mean for the US Open

The full semifinal picture for WTA Toronto: Iga Swiatek (POL, No. 7 seed) vs. Elina Svitolina (UKR, No. 9 seed) today, August 12. Coco Gauff (USA, No. 4 seed) vs. Elena Rybakina (KAZ, No. 2 seed) on August 13.

Every player in the final four is a current or potential US Open contender. Swiatek is the defending champion at Flushing. Svitolina has her best WTA 1000 form of the season with a 10-match winning streak. Gauff won the 2023 US Open. Rybakina won Wimbledon in 2022 and the Australian Open in 2023. The Toronto SFs are not just about Toronto points. They are a direct preview of who arrives at the US Open (main draw August 24) with momentum, fitness, and confidence.

Rybakina's 13 aces against Osaka were a reminder that when she is serving well, she is the most difficult player to break down in the women's game. The question now is whether Gauff's return game, one of the best in the WTA, can neutralize that on a hard court in front of a Toronto crowd that will be fully packed for a Friday evening match.

What's Next

Swiatek vs. Svitolina happens today on Centre Court in Toronto. Gauff vs. Rybakina follows on August 13. The Toronto final is scheduled for August 14. All four players move to Cincinnati the following week, where the Western and Southern Open begins August 13. That is two weeks before the US Open starts in New York on August 24.
