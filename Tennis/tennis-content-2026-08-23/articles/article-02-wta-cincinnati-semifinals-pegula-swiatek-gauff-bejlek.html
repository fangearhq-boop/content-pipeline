Pegula Ends Swiatek's Cincinnati Run; Gauff Eases Past Bejlek Into Final

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | August 23, 2026

Saturday's WTA Cincinnati semifinals produced exactly what was promised — drama and clarity in equal measure. Jessica Pegula spent 2 hours and 47 minutes outlasting defending champion Iga Swiatek in a three-set battle that required a comeback in the deciding set. A few courts later, Coco Gauff was economical and decisive, wrapping up Sara Bejlek in 76 minutes. Sunday's final: an all-American showdown, the first in Cincinnati in 56 years.

Pegula's Comeback Win Over Swiatek

Iga Swiatek arrived at the Cincinnati semifinals with a 10-match winning streak on the North American hard-court swing and the defending title. She left with neither.

Pegula took the opening set 7-5 through chaos — five of the first seven games went against serve, with the match looking like neither player wanted to hold. Pegula eventually broke the pattern, pulling away to close it out. Swiatek regrouped in the second, breaking twice to level the match 6-4.

The third set turned on a single sequence. Swiatek broke Pegula early to lead 3-1, and for a moment the momentum appeared to have shifted decisively. But Pegula broke back immediately. From that point, she held every service game to close out 6-4. The match ended 7-5, 4-6, 6-4.

Pegula described it afterward as a match she had to fight for from start to finish. She reached her fourth WTA final of 2026 — previous titles this year came in Dubai, Charleston, and at the DC Open. Adding Cincinnati would give her a fifth title of the season.

Gauff Without Sympathy for Bejlek

Sara Bejlek's week in Cincinnati was one of the more unlikely runs the WTA has seen this season. The 20-year-old Czech, ranked No. 35, arrived without a win over a top-10 player and left having beaten world No. 1 Aryna Sabalenka in the round of 16. Her path to the semifinal carried genuine underdog appeal.

Gauff did not extend the story. The No. 4 seed won 6-4, 6-1 in 76 minutes, losing just five games across the match. Bejlek managed to stay with Gauff through the opening set — the Czech leads 4-3 in the opener at one point — but Gauff found an extra gear from there and did not look back.

It is Gauff's first Cincinnati final since 2023, the year she won the title. The US Open defending champion is in form heading into the final week before her Flushing Meadows title defense begins August 30.

Context: Rybakina's Absence and the US Open Picture

A notable shadow over the WTA Cincinnati draw was Elena Rybakina's ankle injury retirement in the quarterfinals against Swiatek. Rybakina, the No. 2 seed, left the court trailing 4-1 in the first set. Her US Open participation remains uncertain, and her fitness will be closely watched through the week.

Swiatek, despite her Cincinnati exit, remains a formidable US Open contender. Her 10-match streak on hard courts this summer showed she is playing well. A three-set loss to a top-3 player does not change that calculus significantly. What Sunday's Cincinnati final does do is confirm that Gauff and Pegula are equally dangerous entering the season's final major.

What's Next

Gauff and Pegula meet in the Cincinnati women's final on Sunday, August 23. The winner claims their first or latest WTA 1000 title of 2026. Following Cincinnati, the US Open main draw begins August 30 at the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center. Draw ceremony is Thursday, August 27.
