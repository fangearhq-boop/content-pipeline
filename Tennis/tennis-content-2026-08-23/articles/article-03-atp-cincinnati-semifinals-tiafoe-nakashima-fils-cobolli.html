Tiafoe Saves Set Points, Fils Stays Composed: The Making of a Historic Final

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | August 23, 2026

Every final has a story behind it. Sunday's Cincinnati ATP final between Frances Tiafoe and Arthur Fils arrived via two very different routes on Saturday: one tense and fought to the last point, the other relatively composed and efficient. Together they produced a matchup that the ATP is calling historic — the first Masters 1000 final between two Black players in the tour's history.

Tiafoe's Three-Set-Point Save

Frances Tiafoe does not always make things easy on himself. His semifinal against Brandon Nakashima offered fresh evidence. Tiafoe won the match 7-5, 6-3 in 1 hour and 42 minutes, but the scoreline does not fully capture what he had to survive to get there.

Tiafoe saved three set points during the contest — moments where Nakashima could have tilted the match in a completely different direction. Each time, Tiafoe held. His first-serve percentage told a more complete story of the match: Tiafoe won 30 of 39 first-serve points, a 77% conversion rate. Nakashima managed 62%. When Tiafoe was on, Nakashima had almost no way through.

Once Tiafoe won the opening set, he was comfortable. The second went 6-3, businesslike by comparison to the opening. Nakashima, who had advanced to back-to-back Masters 1000 semifinals for the first time in his career, exits with a 2026 season that stands as his best yet — Tour-leading 22 hard-court wins, a career-high ranking, and a Montreal finalist run. He goes to the US Open with credentials.

Fils Does It the Efficient Way

Arthur Fils had a different semifinal experience. The 21-year-old Frenchman took apart No. 7 seed Flavio Cobolli 6-3, 6-4, never facing a serious crisis and converting when he needed to. For a player in his first Masters 1000 final, the composure was notable.

Fils had already reached three Masters 1000 semifinals in 2026 before this Cincinnati run, so the format was not new to him. He had not broken through to a final yet. Dispatching a seeded opponent in straights to get there is the kind of statement win that carries confidence forward.

Cobolli's run at Cincinnati — he had reached his first Masters 1000 semifinal this week — also deserves acknowledgment. He goes to the US Open having shown he can compete deep into the biggest tournaments on tour.

What's at Stake Sunday

Neither Tiafoe nor Fils has won a Masters 1000 title. One of them will on Sunday afternoon. Beyond the individual prize, the final carries the weight of being an ATP first — confirmed by the tour ahead of the match.

Tiafoe's pre-final comment was direct: "It's going to be a great show. I'm very motivated." After the set-point drama of his semifinal, he sounded not shaken but energized. Fils, for his part, has shown all week that he is here to compete, not to be part of the occasion.

Both finalists head from Cincinnati to New York. The US Open main draw begins August 30. A Masters 1000 title — or even a strong final showing — carries momentum and seeding implications that matter in New York.
