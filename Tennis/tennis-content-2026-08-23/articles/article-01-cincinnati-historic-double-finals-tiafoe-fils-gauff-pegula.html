Cincinnati's Historic Finals Day: Two Landmark Matches, One Sunday

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | August 23, 2026

The 2026 Cincinnati Open closes with a finals day that will be remembered long after the trophies are handed over. On Sunday afternoon, two separate pieces of history are being made at the Lindner Family Tennis Centre — one on the men's side, one on the women's, both in the same building on the same afternoon.

In the ATP final, Frances Tiafoe and Arthur Fils are set to contest the first Masters 1000 final between two Black players in ATP history. In the WTA final, Coco Gauff and Jessica Pegula will play the first all-American women's singles final at the Cincinnati Open in 56 years. Neither pairing was inevitable. Both arrived through dramatic semifinal wins. Neither player in either final has ever won a Masters 1000 title at Cincinnati before.

Tiafoe vs. Fils: The ATP's First

Frances Tiafoe has been a major tennis name for years, but Masters 1000 finals have remained elusive. He reaches Sunday's final after a tense semifinal win over countryman Brandon Nakashima — saving three set points along the way before closing it 7-5, 6-3. It is his second career Masters 1000 final, both of them at Cincinnati.

His opponent, Arthur Fils of France, has arrived at his first Masters 1000 final with impressive composure. The No. 21 seed defeated No. 7 seed Flavio Cobolli 6-3, 6-4 in the semifinals, efficient and unhurried. Fils had already reached three Masters 1000 semifinals in 2026 before this run. Now he has reached the final, and one of those wins will stand as his first.

The ATP confirmed that Sunday's final is the first Masters 1000 singles title match between two Black players in the tour's history. Tiafoe addressed the moment before the match: "It's going to be a great show. I'm very motivated." History has a way of raising stakes that already feel high.

Gauff vs. Pegula: Fifty-Six Years in the Making

Coco Gauff and Jessica Pegula have been American tennis's most prominent women for several years now. They are Olympic doubles partners. They travel the same tour. Sunday, they meet across the net in Cincinnati with the title at stake — the first time two American women have contested this final since 1970.

Gauff reached the final by dispatching Sara Bejlek 6-4, 6-1 in a clinical 76-minute semifinal. Bejlek's story this week was extraordinary — the 20-year-old Czech had upset world No. 1 Aryna Sabalenka earlier in the tournament — but Gauff did not allow the Cinderella run to extend to the final. The defending US Open champion is in form at exactly the right time of year.

Pegula's route was harder. She defeated defending champion and 10-match winning-streak holder Iga Swiatek 7-5, 4-6, 6-4 in a 2-hour, 47-minute semifinal filled with service breaks and momentum swings. Pegula fell behind a break in the third set and recovered. That kind of finish — fighting out of trouble at the end of a grueling match — tends to carry mental weight into the next contest.

A Tournament That Delivered

Before reaching Sunday, the 2026 Cincinnati Open already gave fans Bejlek over Sabalenka, an all-American men's semifinal between Tiafoe and Nakashima, and Pegula's epic three-set win over Swiatek. The draw of top seeds Sinner and Djokovic meant a wide-open bracket entering the week. What filled that vacuum was drama, upsets, and four players who earned their finals spots the hard way.

The US Open main draw begins one week from today, on August 30. Several of these players — Tiafoe, Fils, Gauff, Pegula, and Nakashima — will head to New York carrying momentum regardless of Sunday's outcomes. Cincinnati this week served as the last major rehearsal.
