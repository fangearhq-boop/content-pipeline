US Open Draw Ceremony Preview: The Most Wide-Open Draw in Years

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | August 23, 2026

The 2026 US Open draw ceremony is Thursday, August 27, four days away. Seedings will be calculated using the ATP and WTA rankings from Monday, August 24. Whatever bracket emerges from that ceremony will be the most discussed draw in years — because the men's field, for the first time in recent memory, does not have Jannik Sinner in it.

Sinner Out, Alcaraz the Last Man Standing

Jannik Sinner, the World No. 1, officially withdrew from the US Open last week with a right knee tendon injury. It is his first career Grand Slam withdrawal. His 2026 season — 44 wins, three losses, six titles, five consecutive Masters 1000 crowns — ended when the knee would not cooperate.

Carlos Alcaraz, the defending champion, is in the field. He confirmed his participation weeks ago after recovering from the right wrist injury that kept him away from the clay and grass seasons. Alcaraz has not played a competitive match since April 14 in Barcelona — a span of more than four months — and he arrives with no warm-up events behind him. He will be the highest-profile name in the draw and the default favorite on prediction markets, despite the obvious question of match sharpness.

Fourteen-plus players had already withdrawn from the US Open by the end of the Cincinnati tournament. The final withdrawal count will not be known until the draw closes, but the number has been unusually high throughout the summer.

Who's Coming In Hot

The North American hard-court swing produced a clear group of players heading to New York with momentum. From Cincinnati: Tiafoe and Fils, the men's finalists, with whoever wins the title arriving as perhaps the hottest player in the men's draw. Flavio Cobolli, who reached his first Masters 1000 semifinal. Jessica Pegula and Coco Gauff, who will play for the women's title Sunday. Brandon Nakashima, who reached back-to-back Masters 1000 semifinals before losing to Tiafoe.

How those players are seeded will depend on Monday's rankings. The seedings set the bracket positions, determine who gets byes, and ultimately shape which side of the draw each player occupies. A player who enters the US Open with Cincinnati momentum and lands in a favorable quarter has a different path than one who faces a more difficult half.

The Women's Side

On the women's side, Aryna Sabalenka enters as world No. 1 despite her early Cincinnati exit — she lost to Bejlek in the round of 16, one of the tournament's most striking results. Sabalenka remains the top seed. Behind her, Elena Rybakina's ankle injury from the Cincinnati quarterfinals introduces uncertainty about her US Open fitness.

The women's draw is expected to be led by Sabalenka and Rybakina at the top, with Pegula and Gauff immediately behind. Any of those four would be unsurprising US Open champions. The same is true for Iga Swiatek, who despite her Cincinnati semifinal exit has been playing at a high level throughout the hard-court summer.

Prize Money: $108 Million

This year's US Open offers $108 million in total prize money — an increase of 21% from 2025 and the largest prize pool in the history of professional tennis. The winner of each singles final will receive the largest individual payout in Grand Slam history.

The women's final is scheduled for Saturday, September 12. The men's final follows on Sunday, September 13.
