Stan Wawrinka's Career Is Over After Basel First-Round Loss to Lehečká

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap

October 30, 2026

Stan Wawrinka played his last professional tennis match on Tuesday evening at the Swiss Indoors in Basel. Eighth-seeded Jiří Lehečká defeated him 7-5, 7-6(2) in the first round, ending a career that spanned 22 years and produced three Grand Slam titles.

It ended quietly, on the same Swiss soil where it all began. Wawrinka, 41, entered as a wildcard ranked No. 151 in the world. He had announced in December 2025 that the 2026 season would be his last. He had won a tournament at home. Now he would say goodbye at home.

Lehečká, ranked No. 21 on the ATP Tour, broke Wawrinka while he was serving at 5-5 in the opening set. The Czech dropped the first set 7-5, then dominated the second-set tiebreak, winning it 7-2. It was Lehečká's first career win at the Basel tournament, on his third attempt.

Three Majors, Three Upsets

Wawrinka's Grand Slam story is unlike any other player of his generation. He won three Majors, and all three came when he was the underdog. At the 2014 Australian Open, he defeated Djokovic in the final 6-3, 6-2, 3-6, 6-3. At the 2015 French Open, he beat Djokovic again in four sets on clay. At the 2016 US Open, he dismantled Andy Murray — then the world No. 1 — in straight sets.

Each title felt implausible before the final. Each one happened anyway. That combination of improbability and execution defined his career more than any single statistic.

He was not the player with the most titles or the most weeks at No. 1. He was the player who showed up in the biggest moments and beat whoever was standing across the net — including the two best players of his era, repeatedly, at Majors.

A Fitting Farewell in Switzerland

The Basel tribute this week was fitting. On Monday night, before Wawrinka played his opening match, the Swiss Indoors held a ceremony in his honor. Novak Djokovic — who lost to Wawrinka in two of those Grand Slam finals — was personally invited and attended. The gesture spoke to the mutual respect between two players who defined a generation of men's tennis.

Wawrinka's career was not without its valleys. He spent extended periods outside the top 100 due to injury. He worked his way back multiple times, winning titles in 2023 and 2024 at the age of 38 and 39. His persistence became part of his legend.

In 2026, his final season, he went 1-11 against top-50 opponents. He reached the final round of his career ranked No. 151. He went down in three sets, in his home country, to a player 20 years his junior. That is how it ends for almost everyone. It ended that way for Stan, too, and it was still worth watching.

What's Next

There is nothing next for Wawrinka on the ATP Tour — only whatever comes after tennis. Lehečká advances to the Basel round of 16, where the broader tournament draws attention to the ATP Finals race and the remaining qualifier spots for Turin. But Tuesday in Basel, the focus was on one last match for a three-time Grand Slam champion.

Stan Wawrinka finishes his career with three Grand Slams, a career-high ranking of No. 3 in the world, and a record against Novak Djokovic in Major finals of 2-0. Not bad for someone who was never supposed to win any of them.
