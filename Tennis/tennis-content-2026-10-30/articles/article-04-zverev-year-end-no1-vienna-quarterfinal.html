Zverev Clinches Year-End No. 1 Ranking, Advances to Vienna Quarterfinals

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap

October 30, 2026

Alexander Zverev has done something he had not done before: he will end a calendar year as the ATP's No. 1-ranked player. A 6-4, 7-5 win over Tallon Griekspoor in the Vienna round of 16 on Wednesday officially clinched the year-end top ranking. He will hold the No. 1 position when the ATP publishes its November 2 rankings.

Zverev is already one of three confirmed qualifiers for the Nitto ATP Finals in Turin, alongside Carlos Alcaraz and Ben Shelton. The Vienna win and the year-end ranking clinch arrive in the same week, a stretch that cements the German's position as the dominant figure in men's tennis during the second half of 2026.

Alcaraz Also Advances to Vienna QF

Carlos Alcaraz, entering Vienna as a wild card and seeded second in the draw, also advanced to the quarterfinals. He beat Nikoloz Basilashvili 6-1, 7-6(11) — saving four set points in the second-set tiebreak, which stretched to 13 games of tiebreak play before Alcaraz closed it out. The Spaniard's wild card entry came after his recovery from a wrist injury that cost him several months earlier in 2026.

Alcaraz and Zverev are on the same side of the Vienna draw. If they both advance, a semifinal meeting is possible.

Cobolli Injury Adds an Unwanted Subplot

One note from Wednesday's Vienna results that carries real consequences: Flavio Cobolli retired against Matteo Berrettini in tears, apparently with a muscle injury, while trailing 5-2 in the opening set. Berrettini advanced to the quarterfinals.

Cobolli's injury adds to the already-compressed ATP Finals race. He had been among the players competing for the final qualifying spots in Turin. An injury withdrawal from Vienna limits his points total at this event, and the Paris Masters — which begins Friday — becomes essential for his qualification hopes, assuming he is fit enough to compete.

Vienna Quarterfinal Lineup

The Vienna quarterfinals feature Zverev against Arthur Fery, Alcaraz against a to-be-determined opponent, Berrettini in the mix after Cobolli's retirement, and De Minaur — who also advanced in the round of 16 — continuing his push in the Race to Turin. Daniil Medvedev also won his round-of-16 match in Vienna and remains in contention both in the tournament and in the Race standings.

Vienna and Paris run simultaneously, which means the autumn calendar is now fully split: players in Vienna are still alive in an ATP 500, while those who lost in Vienna or Basel are already looking at Paris as the final qualifying opportunity before the Turin deadline.

What's Next

The Vienna quarterfinals will be played Friday and Saturday. The tournament final is scheduled for Sunday, November 1. With Zverev's year-end No. 1 already clinched, the Vienna title is the remaining prize this week. The winner earns 500 Race points — meaningful for those in contention for the five open ATP Finals spots.

Zverev's immediate objective is the Vienna title. His larger legacy objective — ending the year at the top — is already secured.
