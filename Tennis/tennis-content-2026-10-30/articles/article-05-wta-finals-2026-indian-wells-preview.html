WTA Finals Open in 9 Days: Full Field Set, Draw Arrives November 5

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap

October 30, 2026

The 2026 WTA Finals begin November 8 at Indian Wells, California, and the draw will be released November 5. With all eight participants confirmed, the anticipation centers on one central question: when do Rybakina and Sabalenka meet?

The WTA Finals uses a round-robin format. Eight players are divided into two groups of four. Each player faces the other three in their group, with the top two from each group advancing to the semifinals. The draw composition determines whether the sport's two most dominant players collide in the group stage — where one might be eliminated before the knockout rounds — or reach each other in a potential semifinal or final.

The Field

The eight qualified players are: Elena Rybakina, Aryna Sabalenka, Mirra Andreeva, Jessica Pegula, Coco Gauff, Elina Svitolina, Linda Noskova, and Markéta Vondroušová.

Rybakina arrives as the defending champion and heavy favorite. She won the 2025 WTA Finals and backed it up in 2026 with victories at the Australian Open and the Ningbo Open. The Kazakh player has been the most consistent force in women's tennis over the past two seasons, and her serve-dominated game plays effectively on the Indian Wells hard courts.

Sabalenka is the year's other major force. She has won at least one Grand Slam in each of the last three seasons, and her baseline power game gives her the profile to beat anyone in the field on any given day. The Rybakina-Sabalenka head-to-head, currently 10-7 in Sabalenka's favor, has produced several of the season's best matches.

The Rest of the Field

Mirra Andreeva, 19, has had a breakout 2026 season that pushed her into the top five and earned her a Finals spot for the first time. Coco Gauff and Jessica Pegula represent the American challenge, with both players having reached Grand Slam finals in the last two years. Elina Svitolina's qualification is another story of return and resilience. Linda Noskova and Markéta Vondroušová round out a field with no obvious weak link.

The round-robin format rewards consistency over a week, which tends to favor Rybakina and Sabalenka, both of whom have proven they can play five matches of high-level tennis in quick succession.

The Venue

The 2026 WTA Finals have moved from Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, where they were held the previous two years, to Indian Wells. The move is a one-year arrangement; the Finals relocate again to Charlotte, North Carolina, from 2027 through 2029. Indian Wells gives the event an established tennis infrastructure and a well-known hard-court surface that suits several players in the field.

What's Next

The draw release on November 5 will set the group pairings and the week's schedule. Play begins November 8 with round-robin matches, semifinals are slated for November 14, and the final is November 15. The WTA Finals title is the last major prize of the 2026 women's tennis season.

All eyes will be on the November 5 draw to see which group Elena Rybakina and Aryna Sabalenka land in — and whether the sport's two best players are headed for a potential final, or a group-stage collision that could eliminate one of them before the weekend.
