Djokovic Falls to Struff for First Time in Basel — ATP Finals Bid Now Depends on Paris

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap

October 30, 2026

Novak Djokovic's attempt to qualify for the Nitto ATP Finals ended Thursday in Basel when Jan-Lennard Struff became the first player to beat the former world No. 1 in their ATP head-to-head. Struff won in three sets at the Swiss Indoors, taking the second and third sets after Djokovic claimed the first. Djokovic's Basel campaign is over.

For Djokovic, the loss is more than a tournament exit. It is a significant blow to his chances of qualifying for the year-end championships in Turin, which run November 15–22 at the Inalpi Arena. He entered this week sitting outside the top eight in the live ATP Race to Turin standings, needing a deep run in either Basel or the upcoming Paris Masters to close the gap. Basel is now gone.

The Race to Turin: What Djokovic Needs

Only three players have officially clinched spots at the ATP Finals: Alexander Zverev, Carlos Alcaraz, and Ben Shelton. Five places remain open, and the competition for them has intensified in the late-autumn swing. Djokovic sits approximately 9th–11th in the live Race standings, depending on the source and the timing of updates.

The context that matters: world No. 1 Jannik Sinner ended his season on October 6 due to persistent knee inflammation, removing the top-ranked player from the qualification picture. That opened a path for players further down the Race, but it also meant fewer guaranteed points at the top affecting relative gaps.

The Paris Masters — which begins Friday, October 31, and runs through November 8 — is a Masters 1000 event, offering up to 1,000 Race points to the champion and significant points at each round. For Djokovic to realistically reach the top eight, he likely needs to reach at minimum the semifinals in Paris, and quite possibly the final.

First Career Loss to Struff

Struff, ranked well outside the top 10, entered his match against Djokovic with an 0-8 career record against the Serbian. The win Thursday was the first in their ATP head-to-head. It also extended Struff's run of strong form in the indoor autumn season, where he has won several matches against higher-ranked opponents.

Djokovic had won his first-round Basel match — his first appearance at the Swiss Indoors since 2011 — by defeating Ugo Humbert in three sets. That win briefly returned him to the ATP top ten. Thursday's loss means his Basel points tally remains at the round-of-16 level.

What's Next

Djokovic's 2026 season comes down to one last major tournament. The Paris Masters is a historically strong event for him — he has won there multiple times over his career. Whether his form, and the Race standings, align for a run deep enough to qualify remains to be seen.

The five open spots at Turin will likely be decided in Paris, which ends November 8 — just one week before the Finals begin. Djokovic is not eliminated from qualification, but the margin is thin and the opponents remaining in Paris are formidable. His path still exists. It is just narrower after Thursday in Basel.
