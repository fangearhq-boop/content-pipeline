### STORY 1: Stan Wawrinka Career Ends

**Long-Form (9:00 AM ET)**

It ended the way it had to — in Switzerland, in front of his people, at the Swiss Indoors in Basel.

Stan Wawrinka played the final match of his 22-year professional career on Tuesday night. Jiří Lehečká, seeded eighth at this week's tournament, beat the Swiss legend 7-5, 7-6(2) in the first round.

There were no complaints. No drama. Just a great champion stepping off the court for the last time.

Three Grand Slam titles define Stan's career: the 2014 Australian Open, the 2015 French Open, and the 2016 US Open. Each came as a remarkable upset. He beat Djokovic in the 2014 and 2015 finals, and Djokovic came to Basel this week just to attend Wawrinka's tribute ceremony. That says everything.

He was never the favorite. He was never supposed to win. He won anyway — three times.

Tennis gave Stan Wawrinka a lot of grief: injuries, long stretches outside the top 100, critics who wrote him off too many times to count. He gave the sport a career worth celebrating.

Thank you, Stan.

### STORY 2: Djokovic Falls to Struff

**Long-Form (10:00 AM ET)**

Novak Djokovic's chances of playing the ATP Finals in Turin this November took a serious hit Thursday in Basel.

Jan-Lennard Struff — ranked well outside the top 10, a consistent presence on tour but never a regular Djokovic problem — beat the former world No. 1 in three sets. It was the first time in their career head-to-head that Struff had beaten Djokovic. Not a fluke win. A real match, three sets, earned.

Djokovic entered this Basel week already on the outside looking in. He's sitting somewhere between 9th and 11th in the live Race to Turin standings. With only three of eight spots in the field locked up — Zverev, Alcaraz, and Shelton — five places are still open. But the competition is fierce.

Now Djokovic must go to Paris. The Rolex Paris Masters starts tomorrow and runs through November 8. It's his last realistic chance to put together the points needed to qualify. He'll need a deep run — probably at least the semifinals, possibly the final.

The Paris Masters has been kind to Djokovic over his career. Whether that history matters in 2026 is another question.

### STORY 3: Basel Upsets

**Long-Form (11:00 AM ET)**

If you were watching Basel on Thursday, you saw one of those days that makes tennis so compelling.

The top seed fell. The third seed fell. Wild cards and low-ranked players went out and beat people they weren't supposed to beat.

Jérôme Kym — a Swiss player ranked No. 222 in the world, playing in his home country on a wild card — defeated top seed Ben Shelton. Shelton arrived at Basel as one of only three players to have already clinched a spot at the ATP Finals. His tournament is over, though his season plans are fine. Kym's win is the biggest of his career.

In the same session, Valentin Vacherot upset third seed Rafael Jódar, continuing a week that has shuffled the lower half of the ATP Finals race. Jódar, unlike Shelton, was not yet qualified — so his early exit matters for the Race standings.

What does the QF look like now? Kym faces Lehečká. Struff (who beat Djokovic) faces Borges. Fritz meets Fils in what could be a preview of the ATP Finals. Vacherot faces the remaining bracket winner.

It's not the bracket anyone predicted. That's the beauty of it.

Who do you think wins Basel?

With Jannik Sinner out for the rest of the season due to a knee injury, and Carlos Alcaraz missing several months earlier this year, Zverev has been the most consistent player on tour over the second half of 2026.

The Vienna-Basel double week rarely produces both men's titles for the same player, but it's produced a compelling set of quarterfinals on both fronts.
