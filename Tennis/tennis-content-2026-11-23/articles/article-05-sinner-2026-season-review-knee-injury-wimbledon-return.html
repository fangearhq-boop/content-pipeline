Wimbledon Champion, Then Gone: Jannik Sinner's 2026 Season in Review

By Ryan Calloway November 23, 2026

The Season That Started as a Championship Run

Jannik Sinner entered 2026 as the world's No. 1 player, the defending ATP Finals champion, and one of three men — alongside Alcaraz and Zverev — who had reasonable claims to being the best on the planet. He had won the 2025 Australian Open and the 2025 ATP Finals and arrived in January with a ranking that reflected one of the most dominant 12-month stretches any player had produced in years. The first half of 2026 continued that standard. He went deep at tournaments, defended points efficiently, and in July climbed to Wimbledon's final.

He won it. His match against Alexander Zverev on Centre Court was the kind of match that reminds you why grass-court tennis, at its best, is unlike anything else in sport. Sinner claimed his second Grand Slam title of his career and, at that point, led the year-end race. For a few weeks in July 2026, Sinner looked like the player most likely to end the year on top of the men's game again.

The Injury Timeline

Right knee inflammation had been building before Wimbledon, and after his Centre Court triumph it became impossible to ignore. He withdrew from the US Open — the announcement came on August 21 — citing the right knee as the reason. It was the first Grand Slam he had missed in several years, and the withdrawal was accompanied by cautious language about recovery timelines and ongoing treatment.

On October 6, Sinner confirmed what had been suspected for weeks: his 2026 season was over. He said he would take all necessary steps to reach full fitness before returning to match play. Conservative treatment — without surgery — was the chosen path. His team expressed optimism about recovery but acknowledged the uncertainty that always accompanies a knee injury in a sport that demands lateral movement and explosive stops on hard courts.

What He Still Accomplished

It is worth taking a step back from the injury narrative to register what Sinner did complete in 2026. He won Wimbledon. He led the ATP race for months. He defended his No. 1 ranking through the summer against two of the best players in the game. Some players spend their entire careers without winning a Grand Slam. Sinner added a second before his season was two-thirds over.

The question of what 2026 might have been without the injury — a Roland Garros, a US Open, a Davis Cup title — is unanswerable, and there is no point dwelling in it. The season he did have included a Wimbledon trophy, and the conversation about his ceiling is not closed. His return, whenever it comes, will carry the weight of months of silence and the expectation of a player who was No. 1 heading into the year.

What's Next

Sinner is targeting the 2027 Australian Open as his return event. The tournament begins in January. Conservative treatment will determine whether that timeline holds — if the knee responds as hoped, he arrives in Melbourne with accumulated ranking points, a healthy body, and an enormous motivation to show that the second half of 2026 was a temporary interruption. The Alcaraz-Sinner rivalry, one of the defining matchups of this era, has been on pause since July. When it restarts, it will be the most anticipated match in tennis. Melbourne, in the heat of January, is as good a place as any for a rivalry to resume.
