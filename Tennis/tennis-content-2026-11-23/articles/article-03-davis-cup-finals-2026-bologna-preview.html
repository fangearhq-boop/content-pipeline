Davis Cup Final 8 Bologna Preview: Can Italy Make It Four in a Row?

By Marcus Cole November 23, 2026

The Field and the Stakes

Twenty-four hours after the ATP Finals ended in Turin, the Davis Cup Final 8 opens in Bologna. Eight nations arrive on Tuesday, November 24, with one goal: end Italy's run. The Azzurri have won the Davis Cup in 2023, 2024, and 2025 — three consecutive titles in the modern Final 8 format. A fourth straight would take them into territory no nation has claimed in the current era. The last comparable dynasty was the United States in the early 1970s, when they won four consecutive titles between 1968 and 1972.

The field they must navigate includes Spain, Germany, Great Britain, Czechia, Canada, Austria, and South Korea. Italy received the host wild card as defending champion and plays their quarterfinal against South Korea on Wednesday. Beyond the host nation, the most compelling stories in the draw involve players who were in Turin just days ago: Alexander Zverev leads Germany into a quarterfinal against Great Britain, while Carlos Alcaraz — fresh from Sunday's ATP Finals win — anchors Spain against Austria.

The Draw in Full

The quarterfinal schedule runs Tuesday through Thursday. Czechia meets Canada on Tuesday, November 24. Italy face South Korea on Wednesday, November 25. Thursday brings the two most anticipated ties: Great Britain versus Germany, and Austria versus Spain. The semifinals follow on Friday and Saturday, with the final on Sunday, November 29.

Great Britain versus Germany is the tie to circle. Zverev, who played six matches in Turin last week, will be the central figure for Germany — a player who has shown repeatedly that he raises his level in team competition. Great Britain have produced competitive Davis Cup squads in recent years, and home support, even at a neutral venue, tends to travel for these occasions. Spain versus Austria looks like a favorable draw for Alcaraz's side, giving them a potential path to the semifinal without facing Germany or Italy before the final four.

Italy's Case for a Fourth Title

Italy's depth across singles and doubles makes them the clear favorites. They have won three consecutive titles on the strength of a team structure that maximizes every tie — players who have played in multiple Davis Cup Finals and understand the pressure that comes with them. Home court in Bologna adds another layer. The crowd, the familiarity of the conditions, and the weight of expectation all favor the host nation.

But the Davis Cup Final 8 format — best of three ties, two singles and one doubles — is designed to produce upsets. A single bad day from a team's No. 1 player, or a doubles pair that underperforms, can end any campaign. Spain in 2026, with Alcaraz operating at the level he showed this week, is the team best positioned to replicate that kind of unbeatable singles performance. If Alcaraz is healthy and plays both his singles, Spain may be as difficult to stop as Italy.

What's Next

The first ties on Tuesday will give an early read on the form of the field. Watch the Zverev-Germany matchup against Great Britain closely on Thursday — it may be the quarterfinal that defines the draw's other half. For fans who followed the ATP Finals all week, the Davis Cup is a chance to see the sport's best players compete in a format that removes the individual and raises the national stakes. Full schedule: quarterfinals November 24-27, semifinals November 27-28, final Sunday November 29.
