Alcaraz Wins First ATP Finals Title, Beats Zverev in Tiebreak Thriller

By Elena Voss November 23, 2026

Three Set Points Down, One Trophy Up

Carlos Alcaraz is the 2026 ATP Finals champion. The 23-year-old Spaniard beat world No. 1 Alexander Zverev 6-2, 7-6(9-7) on Sunday at the Inalpi Arena in Turin, claiming the one title that had been missing from his trophy case. It was not, in the end, a straightforward afternoon. The first set went exactly as Alcaraz's best tennis promises: crisp, dominant, nearly faultless. He broke early, held comfortably, and took the opener 6-2 in a statement that seemed to suggest this would be over quickly.

The second set told a different story. Zverev found his level, the match tightened, and they went to a tiebreak. At 6-3, Zverev had three consecutive set points — three separate chances to extend the match to a deciding third set. Alcaraz saved every single one. Then, at 9-7, he found a backhand down the line on match point. The ball landed in. Alcaraz dropped to the court.

"This one means the world to me, to my team, to my family," he said afterward. "I couldn't have ended the week in a better way." For a player who has won four Grand Slams, the ATP Finals represented unfinished business — and Sunday was the night it finally got done.

A Perfect Week and a Historic Title

Alcaraz finished the tournament 5-0, dropping zero sets across five matches — a perfect campaign at the season-ending event. He walked away with $5,171,000, the largest prize in the history of the ATP Finals, plus 1,500 ranking points as the unbeaten champion. Throughout the week he played with heavy strapping on his left leg, managing a thigh issue that had shadowed his entire autumn. It never showed in his movement. If anything, he played his most composed tennis under those circumstances.

The win also made him the fourth man in Open Era history to hold the career Grand Slam and the ATP Finals title simultaneously, joining Andre Agassi, Roger Federer, and Novak Djokovic. And it ended a 28-year wait for Spanish tennis. The last champion from Spain at the year-end event was Alex Corretja, who won in 1998. Alcaraz was not yet born.

Zverev's Year — and What Sunday Leaves Open

For Zverev, the defeat stings in a particular way. He arrived in Turin having won Roland Garros and the US Open in 2026, the year-end No. 1 ranking already secured. He went 5-0 in his matches heading into the final, dropping zero sets across the week. He played extraordinary tennis for six days. And then in the second-set tiebreak of the most important match of the tournament, he held three set points and could not convert a single one.

Sunday does not undo Zverev's season — winning two Grand Slams and finishing No. 1 is an achievement that stands on its own. But the ATP Finals title remains the one piece of his record that Alcaraz now has and he does not. That gap will be the story heading into 2027.

What's Next

For Alcaraz, the question is what comes next in a season that already rewrote the record books. The Davis Cup Final 8 opens in Bologna on Tuesday, and Spain — with Alcaraz available if he recovers from Sunday — is among the contenders. The 2027 Australian Open will follow in January, where Alcaraz will defend the title he won in January 2026 to complete his career Grand Slam. For Zverev, the Davis Cup also looms — he leads Germany into the quarterfinals against Great Britain on Thursday. For tennis, the rivalry between these two is only just getting started, and after a week like this one, the appetite for it has never been higher.
