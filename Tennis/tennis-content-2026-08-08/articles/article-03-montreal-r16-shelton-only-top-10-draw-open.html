Montreal R16 Set — Shelton Is the Last Top-10 Player Standing After Historic Upset Week

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | August 8, 2026

The Montreal Round of 16 is confirmed, and it looks nothing like anyone predicted when the draw was made at the start of the week.

Ben Shelton, the defending champion and No. 2 seed, is the only player ranked inside the world's top 10 still competing at the National Bank Open. Six seeded players in the top 17 — Alexander Zverev, Daniil Medvedev, Taylor Fritz, Hubert Hurkacz, Casper Ruud, and Tommy Paul — have all been eliminated. In their place, a Round of 16 that features players from Brazil, the Netherlands, the United States, the Czech Republic, and Argentina, many of whom would have been longer-priced title options before a ball was struck.

It is the most seed-depleted Round of 16 at the Canadian Masters in recent memory, and it has created something genuinely rare in a Masters 1000 event: the very real possibility that the title goes to a first-time champion at this level.

The R16 Bracket

The Round of 16 is set with four confirmed matches. Joao Fonseca, who has beaten both Tsitsipas and Ruud in succession this week, faces defending champion Shelton in what has immediately become the standout fixture. Fonseca is 19; Shelton is also 19 — two teenagers competing in the same R16 at Montreal for the first time since Denis Shapovalov and Tsitsipas did so in 2018.

In the other matches: Jakub Mensik (CZE, No. 13 seed) faces Botic van de Zandschulp (NED), who has now defeated Medvedev and Hurkacz in this same tournament. Learner Tien (USA) takes on Thiago Agustin Tirante (ARG), who upset Fritz in Round 2. Tallon Griekspoor, who started the week's chaos by eliminating No. 1 seed Zverev, meets Daniel Merida.

Two Dutch players — Griekspoor and Van de Zandschulp — are in the last 16, along with multiple players who were not favoured to reach this stage. No single dominant player has emerged from the wreckage of the upsets to position themselves as the obvious title favourite.

What the Chaos Means

The elimination of so many top seeds reflects in part the variance that makes hard-court tennis in August unpredictable. Players are fatigued after a long season, many are managing minor injuries with the US Open approaching in less than three weeks, and the conditions in Montreal reward serving and clean ball-striking over the grinding baseline exchanges that favour the sport's established elite.

Van de Zandschulp's wins over Medvedev and Hurkacz, Griekspoor's demolition of Zverev, and Tirante's defeat of Fritz are not purely shock results — they are wins by players who have been building their level across the season and found that level in the right week at the right tournament. Several of the players remaining have shown genuine hard-court quality in 2026.

Shelton remains the player to beat. As defending champion, he is comfortable at the facility, knows the courts, and has already demonstrated this week that his game is in good shape. But the bracket no longer offers him an obvious route to the final.

What's Next

The Round of 16 matches are scheduled to begin Saturday, August 8, with the quarterfinals expected Sunday and the semifinals and final extending into the coming week. The US Open starts August 24, meaning Montreal's final week overlaps with the final preparation window for the year's last Grand Slam.

Several players still in the draw — including Fonseca, Shelton, Griekspoor, and Van de Zandschulp — could enter the US Open with significant momentum if they continue their runs. A maiden Masters 1000 title this week in Montreal would represent one of the tournaments' more remarkable stories in recent years.
