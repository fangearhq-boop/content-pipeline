Alcaraz Withdraws From Cincinnati — US Open Title Defence in Serious Doubt

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | August 8, 2026

Carlos Alcaraz will not play at the Cincinnati Open, and with the US Open beginning on August 24, his defence of the title he has held for the past year is now in genuine jeopardy.

Alcaraz has been sidelined since April due to tenosynovitis in his right wrist — an inflammation of the connective tissue that protects the tendons. He missed Roland Garros. He missed Wimbledon. Cincinnati (August 13-23) was the tournament where he was projected to return, the last significant hard-court event before the season's final Grand Slam.

That return will not happen. Multiple reports have confirmed his withdrawal from Cincinnati, and several outlets have described his US Open participation as uncertain. The timeline makes the concerns understandable: the US Open begins just one day after Cincinnati concludes. A player returning from four months without a competitive match would typically require weeks of gradual preparation to return to Grand Slam-level form and intensity.

The Significance of the Absence

Alcaraz had been expected in both Montreal and Cincinnati as the player who could challenge Jannik Sinner's dominance of the 2026 hard-court season. Sinner has won every Masters 1000 event he has entered this year — five consecutive titles — and he has also defended his Wimbledon title. With Alcaraz absent throughout the entire North American swing, those challengers have not materialized.

Alcaraz is also the defending Cincinnati champion. His withdrawal means he surrenders that title as well, and it is not yet known whether he will be medically cleared to travel to New York for the US Open.

The gap between his last competitive match and the start of the US Open is now four months. Even in the most optimistic scenario — where Alcaraz is fit enough to enter the draw on August 24 — the question of match fitness and physical readiness after such a long absence would surround his run from the first round.

What It Means for the US Open Draw

Jannik Sinner stands as the dominant favourite for the 2026 US Open in both outright odds and on-court form. The Italian world No. 1 has been the most consistent player in men's tennis this year across all surfaces, and the hard courts of Flushing Meadows suit his style particularly well.

The competition to be regarded as Sinner's primary rival has broadened in Alcaraz's absence. Alexander Zverev, who won Roland Garros 2026 in what was his first Grand Slam title, arrives as a genuine contender. The Canadian hard-court tournaments have produced several players with strong confidence heading into New York — Ben Shelton, Joao Fonseca, and others — though Sinner remains well clear in terms of consistent top-level results in 2026.

On the women's side, Aryna Sabalenka is bidding for a third consecutive US Open title, which would make her one of the tournament's great modern champions. Iga Swiatek, Mirra Andreeva, and a strong WTA field complete the picture.

What's Next

Alcaraz's team has not ruled out his US Open participation, but there has been no confirmation of a return date or a timetable for him to resume competitive play. The next significant update is expected when the US Open entry list closes and the draw is made, in the days immediately before August 24.

If Alcaraz does not play, the 2026 US Open will be the third consecutive Grand Slam he has missed. The absence of one of the sport's biggest names — a three-time Grand Slam champion and the defending title holder — from the final major of the year would be a significant moment in the season's narrative.
