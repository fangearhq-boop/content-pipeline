Fernandez Stuns Roland Garros Champion Andreeva 6-1, 6-4 — Last Canadian Standing in Toronto

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | August 8, 2026

Mirra Andreeva came to Toronto as the reigning Roland Garros champion, a 19-year-old Russian who had just become the youngest women's Grand Slam winner since 1992. On Friday at the National Bank Open, she ran into Leylah Fernandez on home soil.

Fernandez defeated Andreeva 6-1, 6-4 in the third round — a comprehensive, clinical dismantling of the No. 5 seed that has been described as her most significant win since her run to the 2021 US Open final. It was not close. The first set was over almost before it started, and the second set told the same story.

With the win, Fernandez becomes the last Canadian player standing in the entire National Bank Open field, carrying the hope of a home crowd that packed Court Centrale to witness one of the upsets of the North American hard-court season.

Andreeva Had No Answer

The weight of what Fernandez accomplished only becomes clear in context. Andreeva did not arrive in Toronto as a random seed. She won Roland Garros six weeks ago, defeating Maja Chwalinska 6-3, 6-2 in the final to claim her first Grand Slam title. She was the player of the clay-court season, and her ranking of No. 5 in the world understated how dangerous she had looked all summer.

None of that mattered on Friday. Fernandez built pressure from the first game and never released it. She won the opening set in a manner that gave Andreeva no foothold — no rally to lean into, no extended point to change the momentum, no tactical adjustment that worked. The French Open champion was given a 6-1 first set by a Canadian playing the tennis of her life in front of her home country.

The second set was tighter but the result was never truly in doubt. Fernandez maintained her level throughout, closed out the match at 6-4, and the reaction in the stadium reflected the magnitude of what she had done.

A Statement Performance

Fernandez was a finalist at the 2021 US Open — a run that announced her as one of the most tenacious competitors in women's tennis. The years since have included strong results but not that same headline level of performance at the biggest events.

This win is a reminder that the talent and the competitive instinct that took her to that US Open final have not gone anywhere. She is playing in the round of 16 at a WTA 1000 event, on her home country's soil, having just beaten the player who won the year's second Grand Slam.

Elsewhere in the WTA Toronto draw, Aryna Sabalenka and Iga Swiatek are both advancing. But on Friday evening, the tournament belonged to Leylah Fernandez.

What's Next

Fernandez advances into the Round of 16 at WTA Toronto, where she will face a quarterfinal berth if she can produce another strong performance in front of the Toronto crowd.

For Andreeva, the loss is an early exit from a tournament she was expected to go deep in, but she remains one of the most promising players in women's tennis at 19. Her season has been defined by Roland Garros, and the hard-court swing — with the US Open on August 24 — gives her an immediate chance at another deep run.
