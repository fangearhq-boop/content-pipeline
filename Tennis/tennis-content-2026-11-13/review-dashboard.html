### Long-Form Post If you stayed up for Thursday night's WTA Finals action, you got your money's worth. Jessica Pegula found herself serving with a match point against her — down in the third set against Linda Noskova — and somehow fought her way out. She went on to win 7-6(2), 4-6, 7-5 in 2 hours and 46 minutes. Sixteen aces. Noskova, the Wimbledon champion, hit 15 double faults. It was that kind of match. The result knocked Coco Gauff out of the tournament. That is the brutal part of WTA Finals math: Gauff beat Marta Kostyuk 6-4, 7-5 on the same day, won her match, did everything right — and still got eliminated. Pegula's win over Noskova sealed Group 1 on the head-to-head tiebreak. Noskova had already secured her semifinal spot at 2-0. She finished the round robin 2-1. Pegula advances as Group 1 runner-up and faces Mirra Andreeva in Saturday's first semifinal. The two have never played each other on tour before. Meanwhile, Group 2 is still live. Aryna Sabalenka and Elina Svitolina are both 1-1, and they play today at noon PT. That match decides the last semifinal spot — and what happens in Sabalenka's year-end ranking picture. Semis Saturday. Final Sunday. This WTA Finals has delivered. Who wins Saturday — Andreeva or Pegula?

### Long-Form Post The 2026 Nitto ATP Finals is two days away, and the opening schedule is confirmed. Sunday, November 15 at the Inalpi Arena in Turin opens with Alexander Zverev against Rafael Jodar not before 2:00 PM CET. The evening session, beginning at 8:30 PM CET, is Arthur Fils against Ben Shelton. Both matches are in the John McEnroe Group. Carlos Alcaraz, the second seed and perhaps the tournament's biggest draw, does not play until Monday. He opens his Ivan Lendl Group campaign against Frances Tiafoe, also not before 2:00 PM CET on November 16. A few things to note about Day 1. Zverev, who is trying to win a third ATP Finals title, opens against Jodar — the debutant who made history this season as the first player to qualify for the tournament in his debut year on the ATP Tour. Fils and Shelton are two of the most watchable young players in the game, and their McEnroe Group opener will likely be one of the loudest atmospheres of the week. Turin runs through November 22. The draw keeps Zverev and Alcaraz apart until the semifinals at the earliest. Are you watching Sunday?

### Long-Form Post The 2026 Nitto ATP Finals starts Sunday, and the head-to-head records between the eight players reveal a lot about where the drama will come from. In the John McEnroe Group, the most revealing number is Zverev vs. Shelton: 4-0. The year-end No. 1 has never lost to the American, and their most recent match was the 2026 US Open final — a five-set battle Zverev won in a tiebreak. That is a significant head-to-head advantage going into their round-robin encounter. Arthur Fils and Rafael Jodar are debutants who are talented enough to push Zverev, but the numbers favor the top seed to advance. The Ivan Lendl Group is tighter. Alcaraz and Medvedev have played each other in Grand Slam finals — including the 2023 US Open — and there is no dominant H2H between the two. Alcaraz arrives with five titles this year and a 10-match winning streak. Medvedev has the indoor hard-court game and the experience of winning at this event before. Cobolli and Tiafoe are both first-timers with the weapons to disrupt the favorites. Indoor hard courts in Turin favor big servers and aggressive players. That suits Shelton, Fils, and Tiafoe as much as it suits the top seeds. Which player surprises you at the 2026 ATP Finals?
