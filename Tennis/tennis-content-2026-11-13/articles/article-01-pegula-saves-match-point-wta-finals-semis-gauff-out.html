Pegula Saves Match Point to Reach WTA Finals Semis, Eliminating Gauff on Tiebreak

By Ryan Calloway | November 13, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

Jessica Pegula was one point from elimination. With a match point against her in the third set of Thursday's WTA Finals group decider, the American dug in against Linda Noskova and found a way. She won 7-6(2), 4-6, 7-5 in 2 hours and 46 minutes, delivering the most dramatic result of the round robin and ending Coco Gauff's tournament in the cruelest possible way — by winning her own match while Gauff also won hers.

Noskova, the 2026 Wimbledon champion, had already secured her semifinal place heading into Thursday. She finished 2-1. Pegula advances as the Group 1 runner-up and faces Mirra Andreeva in Saturday's semifinals — a first career meeting between the two.

The Match Point and What Followed

The numbers from this match were unusual. Pegula served 16 aces. Noskova hit 15 double faults. Those totals pointed to a match defined by big swings and high variance — a contest neither player could fully control, where the difference came down to who steadied at the most important moment.

When Noskova earned match point in the third set, the Group 1 picture was about to change. Gauff had already beaten Marta Kostyuk 6-4, 7-5 in the day's other match. If Noskova had converted, Gauff would have advanced on head-to-head record. But Pegula saved the point and held on to close the third set 7-5.

The first set tiebreak went to Pegula 7-2, suggesting her serve was working well on the day. The second set, 4-6 for Noskova, showed the Czech was not done and could match the American shot for shot when her double faults stopped stacking up. The third set was tense from the start, resolved only by the kind of late-match nerve that decides WTA Finals group stages.

Gauff Out Despite Winning

Coco Gauff handled her end of the bargain. She beat Marta Kostyuk 6-4, 7-5 on the same Thursday card to finish 2-1 in Group 1 — the same record as Pegula. But the head-to-head tiebreak, which factors in results between the tied players, went against her: Gauff lost to Noskova on Day 3; Pegula beat Noskova on Thursday. The tiebreak settled the standings in Pegula's favor.

It is the kind of result that feels unfair but is entirely consistent with the round-robin format. Gauff went 2-1, won three times over the course of the week if you count the opening victory over Pegula, and still exits before the knockouts. That is the WTA Finals group stage at its most unforgiving.

What's Next

Pegula faces Mirra Andreeva in Saturday's first WTA Finals semifinal, with play scheduled to begin at 12:00 PM PT in Indian Wells. Andreeva went 2-0 in Group 2, beating Svitolina and Sabalenka without dropping a set, and enters the knockout stage as the form player of the round robin.

Noskova will learn her semifinal opponent after Friday's decisive Group 2 match between Aryna Sabalenka and Elina Svitolina. The final is Sunday, November 15, completing the season-ending event in Indian Wells.
