ATP Finals Head-to-Head Preview: Zverev Owns Shelton, Alcaraz vs. Medvedev Is the Rematch to Watch

By Elena Voss | November 13, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

Two days before the 2026 Nitto ATP Finals opens in Turin, the head-to-head records between the eight players reveal where the round-robin drama is most likely to come from — and which favorites have history on their side. The tournament starts Sunday, November 15 at the Inalpi Arena. The draw keeps Alexander Zverev and Carlos Alcaraz in separate groups until at least the semifinals.

John McEnroe Group: Zverev 4-0 vs. Shelton

Alexander Zverev has a 4-0 career record against Ben Shelton, which is as one-sided a head-to-head as exists among the eight qualified players. Their most recent meeting was the 2026 US Open final, a five-set match Zverev won in a tiebreak. That Shelton pushed him to five sets in a Grand Slam final shows the American's potential; that Zverev has never lost to him, across four different matches and surfaces, shows the top seed's consistency.

Arthur Fils and Rafael Jodar are both making their ATP Finals debuts and do not yet have established head-to-head records against Zverev at this event. Fils, a top-10 presence all year, has the serve and forehand to cause problems on indoor hard courts. Jodar qualified in his debut ATP Tour year — a first in the tournament's history — and cannot be dismissed as a no-name placeholder. First-time qualifiers who made it on results, not reputation, have beaten favorites in Turin before.

Fils and Shelton also have no dominant H2H with each other. Their match Sunday evening at 8:30 PM CET will give the McEnroe Group its opening shape.

Ivan Lendl Group: Alcaraz vs. Medvedev Is the Tournament's Headline Matchup

The Ivan Lendl Group's marquee match is Alcaraz against Medvedev. The two have played each other in Grand Slam finals before — their 2023 US Open final, won by Medvedev, is the most prominent — and there is no dominant career edge between them. Alcaraz arrives with five 2026 titles and a 10-match winning streak through Vienna and Paris. Medvedev is a former world No. 1 who has won at the ATP Finals and whose indoor hard-court game is among the best in the sport.

The indoor surface at the Inalpi Arena removes wind and bounce variation, narrowing the margins to serve, return, and baseline patterns. That suits both players. Medvedev's flat-hitting and long-match consistency are suited to indoor conditions; Alcaraz's current form and momentum favor him regardless of surface.

Flavio Cobolli and Frances Tiafoe are debutants who complete the Lendl Group. Tiafoe's big serve and baseline power make him a difficult draw for anyone on a good day; Cobolli has been consistent enough this year to earn his first Finals appearance. Neither is a favorite to advance, but both are capable of a result that reshapes the group standings.

What's Next

The round-robin stage runs from November 15 to 20. The semifinals are November 21 and the final is November 22. Because Zverev and Alcaraz are in separate groups, the earliest they could meet is the semifinal stage — and only if both advance as group winners or runners-up. The 2026 ATP Finals field is one of the more generationally diverse in the tournament's history, with four debutants and two former world No. 1s shaping both groups.
