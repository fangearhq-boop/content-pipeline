Sabalenka vs. Svitolina: The Last WTA Finals Semi Spot — and Sabalenka's Ranking — Are Decided Today

By Elena Voss | November 13, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

The final semifinal spot at the 2026 WTA Finals comes down to one match. Aryna Sabalenka and Elina Svitolina are both 1-1 in Group 2 and will play Friday afternoon at noon PT in Indian Wells. The winner joins Mirra Andreeva, Jessica Pegula, and Linda Noskova in Saturday's semifinals. The loser goes home.

For Sabalenka, this match carries a second layer of pressure beyond just the tournament. Her year-end No. 2 ranking is now in play. Andreeva, ranked No. 3, went 2-0 in the group round and is in the semifinals. The more Sabalenka loses ground, the closer Andreeva gets to the year-end ranking she currently holds.

How Sabalenka Got Here

Sabalenka opened the WTA Finals with a 7-6(2), 7-5 win over Karolina Muchova, a measured performance against a tricky left-hander. That result suggested she had arrived in Indian Wells ready to compete at the highest level.

Wednesday changed that picture. Mirra Andreeva beat her 6-4, 6-0 — the same 6-0 set that dominated post-match discussion and raised questions about the gap between the two players at this moment. Sabalenka fell behind in the first set and never recovered. The match lasted 62 minutes. It was not close after the halfway point.

She arrives at Friday's match needing to demonstrate that result was an outlier. Her record against Svitolina historically favors the Belarusian, but this week's form suggests Svitolina is capable of competing at this level.

Svitolina's Week in Context

Elina Svitolina opened Group 2 play by beating Karolina Muchova in three sets — 3-6, 6-2, 6-2 — which required coming back from a set down and finding a consistent level in the second and third. That kind of resilience defines Svitolina's career: she is a player who finds her best tennis when the match demands it, not always at the outset.

She then lost her second match, leaving her at 1-1 heading into Friday. The Sabalenka meeting is effectively a one-set scenario with the best of three tiebreaks — both players know what the stakes are from the first game.

What's Next

The match begins at noon PT on Friday, November 13 in Indian Wells. If Sabalenka wins, she faces Linda Noskova in Saturday's second semifinal. If Svitolina wins, she faces Noskova instead — and Sabalenka exits the tournament having won only one of three matches at this year's season-ending event.

Either way, Mirra Andreeva's path to the WTA Finals title goes through the other half of the draw. The final is Sunday, November 15. The ranking implications for Sabalenka's year-end position are still developing and may not be fully clear until the final results are in.
