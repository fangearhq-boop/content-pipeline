WTA Finals Semifinals Set: Andreeva vs. Pegula Headlines a Saturday That Could Define the Season's End

By Ryan Calloway | November 13, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

The WTA Finals semifinals are set on the Group 1 side — and Friday's decisive Group 2 match between Aryna Sabalenka and Elina Svitolina will fill in the final bracket spot before the day is out. Play starts at 12:00 PM PT on Saturday, November 14 in Indian Wells. The final is Sunday, November 15.

The confirmed semifinal: Mirra Andreeva against Jessica Pegula. It will be the first career WTA meeting between the two. Noskova awaits the winner of the Sabalenka-Svitolina match in Saturday's second semifinal.

Andreeva vs. Pegula: What to Watch

Mirra Andreeva arrives at this match having been the most dominant player of the round robin. She went 2-0, beat both Svitolina (6-4, 6-0 on Day 2) and Sabalenka (6-4, 6-0 on Day 4), and did not play a third set all week. The form she has shown is genuine — she is not winning on margins. She is winning by controlling matches from the first game and not giving opponents the footholds they need to find momentum.

Pegula got to this point by surviving. Her 7-6(2), 4-6, 7-5 win over Noskova on Thursday — which included saving a match point — was the opposite of a clean performance. It was a player finding a way when nothing came easily. That kind of resilience is not nothing at a season-ending event, but it is a different profile than Andreeva's current form.

The two have never played each other on tour. Scouting reports go only so far. Pegula's left-handed baseline game presents angles that many right-handed opponents have trouble with; Andreeva's ability to redirect heavy balls with compact, low-risk groundstrokes has proven effective against bigger hitters this week. The first set may well define the match.

Noskova Waits for Her Opponent

Linda Noskova enters the semifinals as the Group 1 winner. She went 2-0 through the round robin before Thursday's loss to Pegula, which came after her semifinal place was already secured. The Czech, who won Wimbledon this year in her first Grand Slam final, has shown across the week that her game travels well to hard courts.

Her opponent will be known by Friday night. If Sabalenka beats Svitolina, Noskova faces the two-time Grand Slam champion who entered the tournament ranked No. 2 in the world. If Svitolina wins, Noskova faces a player who has been in multiple WTA Finals before and tends to raise her level in knockout tennis.

What's Next

Semifinals begin at 12:00 PM PT on Saturday in Indian Wells. The final is Sunday, November 15, completing the WTA Finals. The result will carry year-end ranking implications for all four remaining players — Andreeva's place in the top three, Sabalenka's position at No. 2, and what the final ranking points look like for Pegula and Noskova after their semifinal runs.
