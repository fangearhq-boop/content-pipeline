Sonay Kartal: From No. 170 to the Wuhan Final — A Comeback for the Ages

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | October 19, 2026

WUHAN, China

Sonay Kartal did not win the Wuhan Open. She lost the final to Maja Chwalinska 6-2, 4-6, 2-6 after taking the first set. But the story of her week in Wuhan — ranked No. 170, six months removed from a back injury, beating three elite opponents to reach a first WTA 1000 final — stands as one of 2026's most compelling tennis narratives.

Kartal, 24, becomes the first British woman to reach a WTA 1000 final in the Open Era. The runner-up points push her firmly into the WTA top 50, an extraordinary turnaround for a player whose ranking tumbled from the mid-50s to No. 170 during her injury layoff.

The Road to the Final

Kartal's Wuhan draw was not forgiving. After opening with a win over Jasmine Paolini in the quarterfinals, she shocked No. 6 Elina Svitolina 6-2, 2-6, 6-4 — her first career win over a top-10 player. In the semifinals, she outlasted former French Open champion Jelena Ostapenko 7-6(11), 7-6(5) in a two-hour-plus battle.

Three consecutive wins over quality opponents. At No. 170, Kartal became the third-lowest-ranked player to reach a WTA 1000 semifinal since the format was introduced in 2009.

In the final, she edged the first set against Chwalinska — the tournament's breakthrough story — before the Pole took control in sets two and three. Kartal served well throughout but could not match Chwalinska's groundstroke depth in the deciding set.

The Injury Context

Kartal's absence from tour began in spring. A back injury that initially seemed manageable became a prolonged ordeal, costing her months of court time and sending her ranking plummeting. She returned to tour with no ranking protection that would keep her in major draw main draws.

Her Wuhan campaign started in qualifying — the route that brings its own psychological weight. She won through to the main draw and then kept winning, eventually against players ranked hundreds of spots above her.

What's Next

Kartal's new top-50 ranking opens doors. She will enter upcoming draws as a seeded or protected player, removing the qualifier grind that defined her Wuhan week. The Paris Rolex Masters (October 31–November 8) does not feature women's competition, but the WTA Finals field (November 8–15, Indian Wells) will soon be confirmed, and Kartal's ranking puts her within striking distance for late-season events.

More than titles or rankings, however, Sunday's result demonstrated that Kartal belongs at the highest level of women's tennis. That is a foundation she can build on.
