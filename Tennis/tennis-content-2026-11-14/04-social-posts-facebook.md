**Semifinal 1 — Mirra Andreeva vs. Jessica Pegula (noon PT)**
Andreeva enters without having dropped a single set all week. She beat Svitolina 6-4, 6-0. She beat Sabalenka 6-4, 6-0. She beat Shnaider 6-2, 6-1. Their career head-to-head favors Noskova 2-1, with Svitolina's most recent win coming at Stuttgart earlier this year.

The final is Sunday. Someone becomes the 2026 WTA Finals champion tomorrow. Who are you rooting for?

### STORY 4: Alcaraz Form Feature

**Long-Form Post (3:00 PM ET)**

Carlos Alcaraz described his form heading into the ATP Finals with unusual directness: "Probably the best indoor tennis of my career."

That's not a small statement from someone who reached the 2025 ATP Finals final and has five 2026 titles to his name. But the numbers support it. He went through Vienna and Paris without losing a match — nine consecutive wins, two titles, and a Paris Masters final victory over Ben Shelton that felt one-sided even though the scoreline (7-6, 7-5) doesn't quite show it.

What's changed? Alcaraz has historically been a clay-court and grass-court player, with skeptics pointing to his indoor hard-court record as the place where the game's bigger servers could level the playing field. Vienna and Paris have silenced that argument, at least for now. He is hitting the ball cleaner on hard indoors, and his movement is as sharp as it's been all year.

His first ATP Finals match is Monday, November 16 against Frances Tiafoe in the Ivan Lendl Group. Zverev is the year-end No. 1 and the two-time champion, but Alcaraz is the form player. This is going to be the storyline of the week.

### STORY 5: Andreeva Group Stage Masterclass

**Long-Form Post (6:00 PM ET)**

Take a moment to appreciate what Mirra Andreeva has done this week at the WTA Finals.

She beat Elina Svitolina 6-4, 6-0. She beat Aryna Sabalenka 6-4, 6-0. She beat Diana Shnaider 6-2, 6-1. Three matches, zero sets dropped, six sets won. At 19 years old, in what is her first WTA Finals appearance.

The last time a teenager played the WTA Finals was Coco Gauff in 2023. What Andreeva has done in the group stage goes beyond just the age. Sabalenka is a Grand Slam champion and the world No. 2. Svitolina has won 20 WTA titles. Shnaider is a top-20 player. Andreeva defeated all three in straight sets. She gave up a combined total of only three games per set on average.

The 6-0 set against Sabalenka on Wednesday was the moment people started paying attention. The complete 3-0 record is what makes it historically significant. If she wins today's semifinal against Pegula — a first career meeting — she plays in the final on Sunday.

The 2026 WTA Finals might be the moment Andreeva announces herself not as a future star, but as the best player in the world right now.

Who is your pick — Andreeva or the field?
