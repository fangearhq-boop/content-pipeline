WTA Finals Semifinals: Andreeva vs. Pegula, Noskova vs. Svitolina — What to Watch

By Marcus Cole | November 14, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

The 2026 WTA Finals semifinals are here, and Saturday's card at the Indian Wells Tennis Garden delivers two matchups that couldn't be more different in texture. First up: Mirra Andreeva, who has not dropped a single set in three group-stage matches, faces Jessica Pegula in a first career meeting on tour. Then Linda Noskova, the 2026 Wimbledon champion, takes on Elina Svitolina, who arrives on the momentum of Friday's upset win over Aryna Sabalenka. Play starts at noon PT. The final is Sunday.

Semifinal 1: Mirra Andreeva vs. Jessica Pegula

Andreeva has been the defining performance of this WTA Finals. She beat Svitolina 6-4, 6-0. She beat Sabalenka 6-4, 6-0. She beat Shnaider 6-2, 6-1. She has not been in a tiebreak, not been pushed to a third set, and not let an opponent find any rhythm once she establishes control. At 19, she is playing tennis with the assurance of a veteran and the energy of someone who has nothing to lose.

Pegula's path here was different. She beat Linda Noskova 7-6(2), 4-6, 7-5 in a 2-hour-46-minute match on Thursday that required saving a match point in the third set and landing 16 aces to outlast a player who had already clinched her semifinal spot. Pegula was not the best player of the week in the group stage — she lost to Gauff in the opener — but she is experienced, athletic, and capable of raising her level in elimination moments.

These two have never played each other. Andreeva's current form makes her a clear favorite, but Pegula is not a player who folds. Watch for how the first few games of the opening set set the tone. If Pegula can disrupt Andreeva's rhythm early, this could be a longer match than the group stage suggested.

Semifinal 2: Linda Noskova vs. Elina Svitolina

Noskova went 2-1 in Group 1 — a record that looked more impressive when you consider she had already clinched her semifinal spot before Thursday's loss to Pegula mattered in the standings. She is the Wimbledon champion, a player who has demonstrated she can win on every surface, and she arrives with more rest than her opponent.

Svitolina came through Friday's group decider against Sabalenka 6-3, 3-6, 6-1 — a result that required clinical break-point conversion and a third set she dominated. She is playing with confidence. The career head-to-head favors Noskova 2-1, with Svitolina's win at Stuttgart earlier this season representing the most recent evidence that she can compete with the Czech player on hard courts.

This is the more physically demanding semi for Svitolina given the Friday timing. How her legs hold up in the second and third set — if the match goes that far — could determine the outcome.

What's Next

The WTA Finals champion will be crowned Sunday, November 15, in Indian Wells. The prize money for the event is $10 million — the largest in WTA history. With Sabalenka and Gauff eliminated, the final four represent the breadth of the tour: a 19-year-old Russian making her Finals debut, an American serving veteran, a Czech Wimbledon champion, and a Ukrainian who has spent years making every result feel like it carries something beyond the sport itself.

Whoever wins on Sunday will have earned it in one of the most competitive WTA Finals in recent memory.
