Svitolina Shocks Sabalenka to Reach WTA Finals Semifinals

By Elena Voss | November 14, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

Elina Svitolina eliminated Aryna Sabalenka from the 2026 WTA Finals on Friday with a 6-3, 3-6, 6-1 victory that completed the semifinal bracket and sent the world No. 2 home without winning a single knockout match in Indian Wells. Svitolina converted her fourth match point in a third set that exposed the worst of Sabalenka's week, as 35 unforced errors accumulated and what looked like a potential comeback in the second set became a distant memory by mid-third.

The victory improved Svitolina's career record against Sabalenka to 2-6 — a lopsided head-to-head in Sabalenka's favor that makes the result even more striking. Svitolina won 6 of 8 break points on the day, a clinical efficiency that overwhelmed a Sabalenka who was already managing the memory of Wednesday's 6-4, 6-0 loss to Mirra Andreeva.

How the Match Was Won

Svitolina took control in the first set, breaking early and then holding firm. Sabalenka's power game arrived in the second set — she broke, leveled, and briefly looked like the player who had beaten Karolina Muchova in the tournament opener. At one set apiece, it felt like the match had turned.

Then the third set started. Sabalenka fell behind 1-4 and could not stop the bleeding. The error count climbed. Svitolina's movement and consistency made every exchange feel like attrition, and Sabalenka had no attrition left. When Svitolina hit her fourth match point, the match was over in a moment that summarized a week gone badly wrong for the top seed.

What It Means for the Rankings

Sabalenka arrived in Indian Wells ranked world No. 2. Andreeva is currently ranked No. 3. After a week in which Sabalenka won only one match — the Group 2 opener against Muchova — the gap has narrowed. Andreeva went 3-0 through the group stage without dropping a set, and plays in today's semifinals against Jessica Pegula. A deep run by Andreeva would put her year-end ranking in the conversation.

Sabalenka is now watching from outside the draw. The ranking picture will not fully resolve until after the final on Sunday, but Friday's result has made the year-end No. 2 position genuinely uncertain for the first time since the summer.

What's Next

Svitolina plays Linda Noskova in today's second semifinal after the conclusion of the first semi between Andreeva and Pegula. Noskova, who won the 2026 Wimbledon title and went 2-1 in Group 1, is the more rested player entering the matchup. Their career head-to-head favors Noskova 2-1, though Svitolina's win at the Stuttgart Open earlier in the year suggests the gap is not as wide as the record implies.

The WTA Finals final is Sunday, November 15 in Indian Wells. For Svitolina — a player who has bounced back from some of the most difficult circumstances in tennis over the last several years — a final appearance would be one of the most resonant storylines of the season.
