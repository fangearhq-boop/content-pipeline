3-0, Zero Sets Dropped: Andreeva's WTA Finals Group Stage Was Historically Dominant

By Marcus Cole | November 14, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

Mirra Andreeva has not lost a set at the 2026 WTA Finals. She has won six sets. She has beaten the No. 2 player in the world twice — once in the group stage — and two of her three opponents are Grand Slam-level competitors. She is 19 years old, in her first WTA Finals appearance, and she is the last player anyone who entered Indian Wells last week would have called the dominant figure of the tournament.

That is exactly what she is.

The Three Matches

Andreeva opened against Elina Svitolina and won the final seven games to take the match 6-4, 6-0 in 70 minutes. It was a signal — a performance from a player who had already found her footing at this level before most people had caught up with the story. Svitolina is a 20-time WTA title winner and a former top-3 player in the world. The 6-0 second set was not a fluke of the scoreline; Andreeva won it cleanly, with purpose.

Then came Aryna Sabalenka, the world No. 2 and one of the most powerful players the tour has seen in a decade. Andreeva beat her 6-4, 6-0 in 62 minutes. The second-set bagel in that match became the most-discussed moment of the week — not because it was ugly, but because it was a statement. Andreeva executed a game plan against Sabalenka that exposed exactly the moments when Sabalenka's errors multiply under precise pressure.

She completed the group stage against Diana Shnaider, ranked in the top 20, and won 6-2, 6-1. At that point the record was 3-0 with zero sets dropped — a group stage that made it impossible to describe her as anything other than the best player at the 2026 WTA Finals so far.

The Historical Context

Andreeva is the first teenager to play the WTA Finals since Coco Gauff in 2023. Her age makes the numbers more striking, but age alone does not explain what she has done here. The standard for group stage dominance — completing three matches without dropping a set in a field that includes Grand Slam champions — is rare at any age.

She is also approaching a year-end ranking that could challenge Sabalenka's No. 2 spot. With Sabalenka exiting at 1-2 and Andreeva still in the draw, the ranking implications of this week may not resolve until the final on Sunday.

What's Next

Andreeva plays Jessica Pegula today in the first semifinal, starting at noon PT in Indian Wells. It is their first career meeting. Pegula has been tested and battle-hardened through the week. She saved a match point. She played nearly three hours on Thursday. She will not be an easy opponent.

But Andreeva has not shown a single sign that difficulty is arriving. She is playing the best tennis of her career in the biggest event of the season, and Saturday's semifinal is the latest test of whether this run is what it looks like — or whether it's about to become something the sport has not seen in a long time.
