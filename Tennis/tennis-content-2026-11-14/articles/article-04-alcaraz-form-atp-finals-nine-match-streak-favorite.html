Nine Wins in a Row: Alcaraz Enters ATP Finals as the Season's Form Player

By Elena Voss | November 14, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

When Carlos Alcaraz left Paris last week with his first Paris Masters title and a 7-6(5), 7-5 win over Ben Shelton in the final, he had something to say. He called it "probably the best indoor tennis of my career." For a player who has long been characterized as a clay and grass specialist, that is a significant statement — and after nine consecutive wins and two back-to-back titles in Vienna and Paris, the evidence supports the claim.

What's Changed on Indoor Hard

Alcaraz's indoor hard-court record was always the asterisk on his dominance. He had won five titles in 2026 — Australian Open, Doha, Tokyo, Vienna, Paris — but skeptics pointed to the spring and summer results on clay and grass as his true home. Indoor courts, where ball speed is consistent and big servers can dominate the return game, seemed like territory where others could neutralize him.

Vienna and Paris changed that argument in real time. In Vienna, he moved through the draw with the precision of a player who had solved the indoor puzzle. In Paris, he defeated one of the world's most dangerous servers in Shelton, in straight sets, in a Masters 1000 final. The key is not simply that he won — it's how he won. He dictated terms from the baseline, found angles that neutralized the serve, and moved with speed that is unusual for the conditions.

The ATP Finals Prize

Alcaraz has reached the ATP Finals before but has not won it. He finished as runner-up in 2025 — a result that left him with unfinished business at the event. This year, he is the second seed behind year-end No. 1 Alexander Zverev, a two-time champion who has dominated this event in a way that most players never approach.

The head-to-head between Alcaraz and Zverev is one of the most significant in tennis. They have both won Grand Slam titles in 2026, and their rivalry at the top of the men's game has been defined by consistency, competitiveness, and an inability for either player to establish a clear edge. At the ATP Finals, on indoor hard courts, the advantage could go to either player.

What's Next

Alcaraz's first match is Monday, November 16 at not before 2:00 PM CET against Frances Tiafoe in the Ivan Lendl Group. Tiafoe is an ATP Finals debutant who has spoken openly about how much it means to him to compete at this level. He will not be an easy opponent. Alcaraz will then face Flavio Cobolli and Daniil Medvedev to complete the group stage.

If form holds, an Alcaraz-Zverev final on November 22 would complete one of the great rivalries of the decade. The ATP Finals has a history of delivering exactly the final it promises. Alcaraz arrives in Turin as the player nobody wants to face, and his white whale — the year-end championship — is in front of him again.
