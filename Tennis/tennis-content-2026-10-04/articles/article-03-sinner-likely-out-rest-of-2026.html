Sinner's Injury Worsens in Scope: Reports Say World No. 1 May Miss Vienna and Paris Too

By Marcus Cole October 4, 2026

From Shanghai to Everything

When Jannik Sinner posted his Shanghai Masters withdrawal on Instagram Stories on Saturday, it read as the latest in a painful but sequential string of absences. One more tournament crossed off a list that has been growing since Wimbledon. On Sunday, the picture darkened further.

Reports from Tennis365 and multiple Italian outlets now indicate that Sinner is likely to miss not only Shanghai but the Vienna Open (October 26) and the Paris Masters (early November) as well. His team has not issued a statement confirming this, and Sinner's representatives have not put a formal end date on his absence. But the language from those close to the situation has shifted — from the optimism of "targeting a return" to something closer to an acknowledgment that the timeline is genuinely uncertain.

The Injury and the Season It Has Cost

Sinner's diagnosis is iliotibial band syndrome — a connective tissue overuse injury running along the outside of the knee and thigh. After his Wimbledon win in July, a procedure to address it was considered but ultimately set aside in favor of conservative treatment. His team traveled to Lyon for a consultation with specialist Dr. Bertrand Sonnery-Cottet, who recommended continued rest and physical therapy rather than surgical intervention.

Surgery remains a last resort if conservative therapy fails. But that option — with its own recovery timeline — would almost certainly end any chance of returning in 2026. The Italian's season record before the injury was 44-3, including his second consecutive Wimbledon title and a dominant first half of the year on clay. To have that season end in mid-July without a competitive ball struck since would be a brutal conclusion to what started as a historic year.

The ATP Finals Remains the One Fixed Point

Sinner is already qualified for the Nitto ATP Finals, which takes place in Turin from November 15 to 22. His team has been careful to keep that door open in every communication about his status — noting that the goal remains competing in his home country at the season's most prestigious end-of-year event. Whether that is still achievable after five months without competitive match play is a question his doctors and his body will have to answer in the coming weeks.

The ATP Finals would require Sinner to be match-ready following an extended layoff, competing immediately against the top eight players in the world. His preparation options are narrowing. Vienna would have provided three weeks before Paris, which would have provided two weeks before Turin. If both fall away, Sinner would arrive in Turin without a competitive match since July — an extraordinary ask even for a player of his talent and resilience.

What's Next

The tennis world is watching Sinner's situation carefully as the final stretch of the 2026 season unfolds. For now, no official timeline has been provided — each week will bring new updates as his team assesses his recovery. The hope is that conservative treatment eventually resolves the injury without surgery. The concern is that the 2026 season has already effectively ended for one of the most dominant players the ATP Tour has seen in years.
