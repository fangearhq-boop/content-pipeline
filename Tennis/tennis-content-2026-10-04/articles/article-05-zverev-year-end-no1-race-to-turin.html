Zverev Can Make History: First German Year-End World No. 1 if Sinner's Season Is Over

By Elena Voss October 4, 2026

The Math After Shanghai

Alexander Zverev has spent most of the last three years within reach of the ATP's top ranking without quite getting there. The 2026 season has changed the equation entirely — and Jannik Sinner's extended absence has changed it further.

In the Race to Turin, the points tracking used to determine the season's top eight, Zverev leads with 8,650 to Sinner's 7,950 — a 700-point gap that sounds manageable for Sinner but has one critical feature: Sinner is no longer earning points, and he is about to start losing them. He won the Shanghai Masters in 2024, which means he was defending approximately 1,000 ranking points at this year's event. His withdrawal without playing a match causes those points to drop off his rolling total immediately. Zverev, who exited Shanghai early in 2024, defends almost nothing there.

The combined swing: a Zverev title in Beijing (500 points) and a title in Shanghai (1,000 points) could put him atop the Race and the ATP world ranking simultaneously — surpassing Sinner in both simultaneously — by October 19. It is the scenario that has been simmering all week, and it is now more achievable than it has been at any point this season.

The Season That Built This Moment

Zverev's case for Year-End No. 1 was built long before Sinner's injury entered the picture. He won Roland Garros in June — his first Grand Slam title, the one that had eluded him through multiple major final appearances — and then backed it up with a US Open title in September. Two Slam titles in a calendar year. His season record stands at 58-14. In a different year, with a healthy Sinner, this would still be a compelling Race. With Sinner out, it has become Zverev's to lose.

This is the context around the Beijing quarterfinal on Sunday evening: not just a match, but one more potential step on the path to history. No German player has ever held the ATP's top ranking in the Open era — not Becker, not Stich, not anyone. The German tennis public has not had a player at the absolute pinnacle of the sport, and Zverev has carried that weight without the reward for years. This year, with two Grand Slams and a Race to Turin lead, the reward feels within grasp.

Who Else Is in the Race

The Race to Turin is not only about the top two. The final six qualifying spots are still contested, and the Asian swing is the last major opportunity for players on the bubble to secure their places at the ATP Finals in Turin.

Ben Shelton (4,430 points) and Carlos Alcaraz (approximately 4,050) are third and fourth. Alcaraz, who is contesting the Japan Open semifinals on Monday, missed Roland Garros with a wrist injury and has been grinding his way back up the Race standings since. Every match in Tokyo adds to his total. Novak Djokovic sits at approximately 2,310 Race points — a deep Beijing run, particularly reaching the final or winning the title, could dramatically improve his position.

The remaining spots (roughly 5th through 8th) are still in flux, with players including Flavio Cobolli, Frances Tiafoe, and Felix Auger-Aliassime all within range depending on results over the next two weeks.

What's Next

Zverev plays Beijing on Sunday evening, with the Djokovic quarterfinal the immediate focus. Beyond that, Shanghai begins on October 7. A Zverev run through both tournaments would rewrite the ATP record books. For the German No. 1 candidate, the window is open, the form is there, and the calendar is almost out of pages. This is the moment.
