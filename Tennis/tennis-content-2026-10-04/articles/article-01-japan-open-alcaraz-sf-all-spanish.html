Alcaraz Into Japan Open Semifinals as Shapovalov Retires; All-Spanish SF Set vs. Munar

By Ryan Calloway October 4, 2026

Shapovalov Retires, Alcaraz Advances

Carlos Alcaraz is through to the Japan Open semifinals for the second consecutive year, but the circumstances on Sunday were not what anyone anticipated. Denis Shapovalov, the Canadian veteran who had pushed the defending champion hard in a tense first set, was forced to retire with an upper right thigh injury while trailing 2-1 in the second. Alcaraz had just won the first-set tiebreak 7-3 when the injury brought the quarterfinal to an abrupt end.

Shapovalov, who has battled various physical issues throughout his career, showed obvious discomfort during the first game of the second set. Trainers were called before he ultimately made the difficult decision to withdraw rather than risk further damage. Alcaraz, gracious in his response, acknowledged the difficult circumstances while noting he was pleased to keep his unbeaten record in Tokyo intact. He is now 7-0 all-time at the Japan Open, a remarkable record that underscores his affinity for the hard courts at Ariake.

An All-Spanish Semifinal Takes Shape

While Alcaraz's quarterfinal ended prematurely, the match that followed delivered one of the more satisfying results of the tournament. Jaume Munar, the 27-year-old from Mallorca, dismissed Frenchman Kyrian Jacquet 6-4, 6-2 in a composed and controlled performance that belied his position in the draw. Munar had worked through qualifying to reach the main draw, and Sunday's win — his fourth of the week — confirmed that his run has been built on genuine quality, not bracket luck.

Alcaraz vs. Munar is a rare pairing on the ATP Tour. The head-to-head stands 3-1 in Alcaraz's favor, but those meetings stretch back several years, and the Munar who arrived in Tokyo this week is playing some of the most consistent tennis of his career. The all-Spanish semifinal on Monday will be their first meeting on tour in some time, and the hometown crowd in Tokyo will be watching closely.

Fils Falls, Vacherot Through to the Other Half

The other quarterfinal development of Sunday involved a significant upset. Arthur Fils, the No. 2 seed who had impressed in his earlier rounds, was eliminated by Monégasque qualifier Valentin Vacherot. The French teenager had been among the tournament favorites heading into his QF slot, but Vacherot — playing with nothing to lose as a qualifier — was the better player on the day and advances to the semifinal against the fourth qualifier from the opposite half of the draw.

Fils's exit leaves the top half of the draw entirely in the hands of players who arrived in Tokyo without seedings — a testament to the depth and unpredictability of the current ATP Tour. Vacherot's run has been one of the stories of the week.

What's Next

The Japan Open semifinals are scheduled for Monday, October 5. Alcaraz will face Munar in the headline match, with Vacherot going against the other semifinalist in the lower half. Alcaraz, as defending champion and the top seed, is the clear favorite — but Munar's form demands respect. A final berth would put Alcaraz in position to claim back-to-back Japan Open titles, an outcome that would further cement his status as the dominant force on the Asian swing this fall.
