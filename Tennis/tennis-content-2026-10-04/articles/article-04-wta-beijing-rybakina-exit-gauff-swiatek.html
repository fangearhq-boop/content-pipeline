Rybakina Stunned in Round 2; Gauff and Swiatek Eye Beijing QF Showdown

By Ryan Calloway October 4, 2026

The Top Seed Falls

Elena Rybakina's first China Open as World No. 1 ended almost before it started. Alina Charaeva, a 22-year-old from Armenia ranked No. 118 in the world, defeated the new WTA top dog 3-6, 6-4, 6-3 in 2 hours and 11 minutes on Saturday in the second round. Rybakina, who claimed the No. 1 ranking when she won the US Open in September, won the first set comfortably. Then Charaeva found her level, held her nerve in the second set, and took full control in the third.

It is the kind of upset that defines the WTA's current unpredictability. Rybakina entered Beijing as the clear favorite and the highest-profile player in the draw. She leaves without a single quarterfinal point from the Chinese capital, at a tournament she hoped would extend her momentum as the new No. 1. Charaeva, meanwhile, advances to the third round and has already created the story of her career.

A Draw That Suddenly Looks Wide Open

Without Rybakina, the China Open field has lost its clearest hierarchy. What remains is a draw full of contenders and a genuinely exciting setup in the upper half. No. 3 seed Coco Gauff and No. 8 seed Iga Swiatek — the two-time Beijing champion who has now slipped to eighth in the world rankings — are positioned in the same quarter of the draw, on a direct path to meet in the quarterfinals if both advance through the third round.

Their head-to-head is one of the most-watched rivalries in women's tennis. Swiatek leads 11-5 overall, a margin that reflects years of dominance. But in 2026, Gauff turned the tables in their sole meeting at the United Cup, and the 21-year-old American has looked more physically imposing and technically rounded than at any point in her career. A Gauff-Swiatek QF in Beijing — without the weight of either player carrying the top seed — would be one of the most compelling WTA matches of the fall.

The WTA Year-End No. 1 Subtext

Beyond the match itself, there is a ranking subplot threading through every point in Beijing. Swiatek fell from her long-time perch atop the WTA rankings when Rybakina's US Open title pushed the Kazakh to No. 1. The Pole is now fighting to keep her Year-End No. 1 hopes alive — a deep run in Beijing would be meaningful to that chase. Gauff, for her part, is looking to consolidate a strong season and add another WTA 1000 title to her credentials.

Sabalenka (No. 2 seed) and the other half of the draw remain in the picture, but for now, all eyes are on whether Gauff and Swiatek can navigate their respective paths to a quarterfinal — and whether Beijing will deliver the showdown that the draw currently promises.

What's Next

The WTA China Open continues with the third round on Sunday and Monday, October 4-5. Quarterfinals are expected around October 8-9. The Gauff-Swiatek potential matchup remains the story to watch — and without Rybakina in the picture, whoever claims the Beijing title this week will do so having beaten the very best that remains in the draw.
