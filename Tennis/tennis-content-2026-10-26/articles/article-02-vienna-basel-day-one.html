Vienna and Basel Both Open Monday — Two ATP 500s, One Massive Week

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | October 26, 2026

The ATP fall indoor season reaches its most concentrated point this week. The Erste Bank Open in Vienna and the Swiss Indoors in Basel both launched their main draws on Monday — two ATP 500 events running simultaneously, each carrying 500 Race to Turin points for the champion. Between them, they feature most of the players who will determine who fills the final spots at the ATP Finals in November.

Vienna: Zverev, Alcaraz, and a Familiar Draw

In Vienna, Alexander Zverev opens as the No. 1 seed against wildcard Martin Landaluce. A quarterfinal result or better this week would clinch the year-end ATP No. 1 ranking for Zverev — a milestone he has been chasing all season.

The more watched first-round match may be Carlos Alcaraz against Karen Khachanov. Alcaraz, here on a wildcard as the No. 2 seed, leads his head-to-head with Khachanov 6-0. It is the kind of match-up where Alcaraz is expected to advance, but Khachanov is a capable indoor player and the scoreline does not fully reflect the physical quality of those meetings.

Defending champion Jannik Sinner is absent from the draw with a knee injury, leaving a path slightly more open than it would otherwise be. The draw will still be contested fiercely — this is the penultimate 500 before Paris.

Basel: Shelton, Djokovic, and Wawrinka's Night

The Swiss Indoors has a different texture this week. Ben Shelton is the top seed and opens against Casper Ruud. Shelton and Ruud are in the same half as Novak Djokovic, who enters on a wildcard against Ugo Humbert — a draw that sets up a potential Shelton-Djokovic semifinal if both advance.

Djokovic is playing Basel with Race points squarely in mind. He sits outside the automatic qualification line and needs results from Vienna, Basel, and Paris to secure his spot in Turin.

The emotional center of Monday's Basel schedule, however, belongs to Stan Wawrinka. The 41-year-old Swiss player plays his tribute night match against Jiří Lehečká — a formal farewell at the event he has won three times. Basel has been one of the defining venues of Wawrinka's career, and Monday night gives the hometown crowd a proper sendoff.

The Week in Context

Running two ATP 500 events simultaneously is unusual, and the scheduling creates a week of genuine complexity. Players who entered both events are separated by geography. The Race standings after this week will look significantly different — with two champions earning 500 points each and a wave of semifinalists and quarterfinalists picking up Race-relevant points throughout the bracket.

Vienna and Basel together account for 1,000 potential Race points, then Paris adds another 1,000 the following week. For the bubble players — Medvedev, Cobolli, Fils, Tiafoe, Jódar, de Minaur, and Djokovic — the next 14 days are as consequential as any stretch of the season.

What's Next

Both tournaments run through November 1. Second-round matches begin midweek, and quarterfinals are expected Thursday and Friday. Paris Masters follows immediately, beginning October 31. The ATP Finals in Turin are set for November 15–22, and the final Race picture will not be settled until Paris wraps up on November 8. This is the final fortnight that decides everything.
