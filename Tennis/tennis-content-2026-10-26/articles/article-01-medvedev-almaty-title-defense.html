Medvedev Finally Defends a Title, Wins Almaty for the 25th Time

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | October 26, 2026

Daniil Medvedev has now won 25 ATP Tour singles titles. More importantly, on Sunday in Almaty, he finally won one for the second time. The Russian came back from a set down to defeat qualifier Nikoloz Basilashvili 6-7(6), 6-1, 6-3 in the Almaty Open final — notching his first career title defense at the fourth attempt.

A Tiebreak That Told a Story

For a while, it looked like the underdog's moment. Basilashvili, ranked No. 128 in the world and in the draw as a qualifier, pushed the opening set all the way to a tiebreak and won it 8-6. It was the kind of early lead that invites a different kind of match — one defined by belief and momentum rather than ranking logic.

Medvedev, however, did not flinch. Once the tiebreak was over, he was a different player. He broke Basilashvili's serve four times across the second and third sets and never faced a break point himself after that opening set. The 6-1, 6-3 conclusion was not so much a collapse by Basilashvili as it was a display of Medvedev at his most methodical and controlled.

The Title Defense That Never Happened — Until Now

It is a strange footnote in a career as accomplished as Medvedev's that he had never successfully defended a title. In three previous attempts, the Russian had returned to defend a championship and fallen short each time. Sunday changed that.

The win gives him his 25th career ATP Tour singles title and adds a layer of personal significance that the raw number alone does not capture. A player of his caliber coming to Almaty as defending champion and leaving with the trophy again says something about his focus and his level of play in the Central Asian swing.

For Basilashvili, the run was still extraordinary. A qualifier ranked outside the top 100 reaching an ATP final is rare. He came within one set of becoming the first qualifier to win this event. He will leave Kazakhstan with considerably more ranking points than he arrived with.

Race Implications

Beyond the personal milestone, the Almaty title carries immediate strategic significance. The victory moves Medvedev to No. 5 in the ATP Race to Turin with 3,660 points — just 20 points ahead of Flavio Cobolli, who sits at No. 6. Three spots remain contested among seven players, and the margins are razor-thin.

The timing is notable. Vienna and Basel both opened their main draws on Monday, offering 500 ATP Race points to each champion. Paris Masters follows immediately with 1,000. Medvedev has positioned himself on the right side of the bubble with a title, but the next two weeks will determine whether that is enough.

What's Next

Medvedev is in the Vienna Erste Bank Open draw as the No. 3 seed, entering later in the week. The tournament runs through November 1. After that, Paris Masters begins October 31 — a 1,000-point event that could reshape the Race standings entirely before the ATP Finals cutoff. With five spots still open, Medvedev's first-ever title defense may prove to be his most important win of the fall.
