### STORY 2: Sinner China Open — No Withdrawal Filed

**Long-Form Post [12:30 PM ET]**

Still no word from Jannik Sinner's camp.

The world No. 1 remains on the China Open entry list as of today — no withdrawal filed, no official confirmation he'll play. The China Open draw is expected around September 28, and tournament play begins September 30. That means the window for a final decision is closing fast.

His team's stated position is that he is targeting Beijing, where he is the defending champion. But former Italian ATP professional Stefano Pescosolido told Tuttosport this week that Sinner is taking a cautious approach: "I think he's a little more careful from a physical standpoint. He wants to avoid relapses."

Sinner has not played since winning Wimbledon in July. His right-knee injury has kept him out of the entire North American summer swing, including the US Open. With his ATP Finals spot already locked up as No. 2 in the Race to Turin, the pressure to rush back is less than it might otherwise be.

A Beijing comeback would be a huge moment. A skip to Vienna in late October would be the smart play — but not the one fans are hoping for.

### STORY 3: Singapore Open — QF Day / Eala

**Long-Form Post [8:00 AM ET]**

The Singapore Open quarterfinals are today — and the draw looks very different from what was planned.

Amanda Anisimova, the No. 2 seed, has withdrawn from the tournament with a left wrist injury, joining a list of 11 players who have pulled out from this WTA 500 event. It has been a difficult week for the field in terms of health, but the silver lining is that it has opened the draw significantly.

The biggest beneficiary is Alexandra Eala of the Philippines. The No. 17-ranked player is the highest remaining seed in the bottom half of the draw, and she has proven this season that she belongs at this level — she won the DC Open WTA 500 earlier in 2026, her first title at that tier.

Top seed Mirra Andreeva (Roland Garros champion) is on the other side of the draw and remains the clear favourite for the title. She rolled through her second-round match against Sasnovich without dropping a set.

Elise Mertens, Barbora Krejčíková, and Olena Oliynykova are also alive in the QFs. It is a good eight. Despite all the chaos, the Singapore Open is delivering.

### STORY 5: Chengdu Open — Brooksby Upsets Báez

**Long-Form Post [6:00 PM ET]**

Jenson Brooksby just announced his presence at the Chengdu Open.

The American defeated No. 8 seed Sebastián Báez of Argentina 7-5, 7-5 on Day 1, knocking out one of the higher seeds in the draw in straight sets. It is the kind of result that signals how open this tournament could be.

Top seeds Valentin Vacherot and defending champion Alejandro Tabilo have both received byes into the Round of 16 and will not play until later in the week. So the early rounds of the Chengdu Open are all about building narrative — and Brooksby has started one.

Local hero Shang Juncheng — the 2024 Chengdu champion — faces 38-year-old Adrian Mannarino in his first-round match. The home crowd will be electric for that one. Denis Shapovalov and Alejandro Davidovich Fokina are also in the draw, adding serious talent to the mix.

Two ATP 250s, both in China, both wide open. It is a good week for tennis fans who want results.
