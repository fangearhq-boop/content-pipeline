Singapore Open QF Day: Eala's Title Path Opens After Anisimova Withdraws

By Marcus Cole | September 24, 2026

WTA | Singapore Open

The WTA 500 Singapore Open quarterfinals are being played Thursday in what has become an unexpectedly reshaped tournament. Amanda Anisimova, the No. 2 seed, withdrew before completing her matches with a left wrist injury — the 11th player to pull out of the event — and her absence has fundamentally altered the bottom half of the draw.

The biggest beneficiary is Alexandra Eala of the Philippines. The No. 17-ranked Eala, who entered the event as the No. 3 seed after Jessica Pegula's pre-tournament withdrawal, is now the highest remaining seed in the bottom half. A path to the Singapore Open final, which would have looked difficult against a full-strength field, is now significantly clearer.

Eala's Season Context

Eala is not arriving at this opportunity without credentials. Earlier in 2026 she won the DC Open, a WTA 500 event, for her first title at that level. That result confirmed what those who had followed her closely already knew: she belongs at the top tier of women's tennis, not just as a future prospect but as a present competitive force.

The Singapore Open, held at the OCBC Arena in an indoor hard court setting, suits her game. A final here would represent her second WTA 500 final of the season and potentially her second title. The Philippines tennis community, and a broader Asian tennis fanbase, would make that result feel significant.

Andreeva the Favourite

On the other side of the draw, Mirra Andreeva is the clear tournament favourite and has shown no signs of vulnerability. The top seed, who won the Roland Garros title earlier in 2026, came through her second-round match against Aliaksandra Sasnovich 7-5, 6-0 without extended difficulty. The Russian teenager has been one of the most formidable players on tour since her Roland Garros breakthrough, and indoor hard courts are a surface she handles well.

A potential final between Andreeva and Eala would be a compelling story on multiple levels — champion vs. champion, European Grand Slam winner vs. Southeast Asian representative.

The QF Field

Beyond Andreeva and Eala, the quarterfinal lineup includes Elise Mertens, Barbora Krejčíková, and Olena Oliynykova — all credible threats to go deep. The Singapore Open has been chaotic by withdrawal standards (11 players out), but the remaining field retains genuine quality. Someone among that group will represent a serious challenge regardless of draw positioning.

What's Next

Semifinal results from Thursday's QF play will determine Friday's semifinal pairings. The Singapore Open WTA 500 final is scheduled for Sunday, September 27 — the same day as the Laver Cup final day in London. Watch wtatennis.com for live scores and draw updates throughout Thursday play.
