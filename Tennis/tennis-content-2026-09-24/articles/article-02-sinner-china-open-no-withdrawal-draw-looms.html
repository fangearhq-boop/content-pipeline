Sinner Still on China Open Entry List — No Withdrawal Filed as Draw Date Approaches

By Elena Voss | September 24, 2026

ATP | Player Update

The tennis world is still waiting. As of Thursday, September 24, Jannik Sinner remains listed as an entrant in the China Open field with no official withdrawal filed. The ATP 500 event in Beijing begins September 30 — six days from now — and the draw is expected around September 28. That means the world No. 1's window to make a final decision is closing.

Sinner has not played a competitive match since winning Wimbledon on July 12. A right-knee tendon injury has sidelined him through the entire North American summer swing, including the US Open, where he submitted an official withdrawal. He completed treatment at JMedical in Turin and has resumed high-intensity training — but the question of when to return to competition remains live.

What His Camp Has Said

Sinner's team's stated position, reported by multiple outlets, is that he is targeting the Asian swing and intends to play in Beijing and Shanghai. That framing has remained consistent. No member of his team has publicly walked it back.

But the background noise has grown louder. Former Italian ATP professional Stefano Pescosolido, speaking to Tuttosport, offered a more cautious assessment: "I think he's a little more careful from a physical standpoint. He wants to avoid relapses." Pescosolido floated the possibility that Sinner skips the entire Asian swing and returns to competition at the Vienna Open in late October instead.

The Calculation

The strategic case for waiting is clearer than it might appear. Sinner's ATP Finals spot in Turin is already secured — he sits No. 2 in the Race to Turin. Missing the China Open means forfeiting the 500 points he earned from his 2025 title defense, which will affect his ranking. But his long-term fitness going into the year-end events is arguably more important than any points calculus in September.

Beijing in late September and early October also presents environmental challenges — heat, humidity, and travel fatigue for a player returning from a multi-month layoff. Vienna indoors in late October offers a gentler re-entry into match play.

The smart money says Vienna. But Sinner has not confirmed it, and until the withdrawal deadline passes, China remains officially on the table.

What Changes If He Skips

Alexander Zverev, the reigning US Open champion, becomes the clear favourite in Beijing — a title he has never won. Novak Djokovic, who has a 29-0 all-time record at the China Open and holds six titles there, also becomes an even bigger story if Sinner is absent. Djokovic is targeting a return to the top 10 after dropping to No. 12 following his US Open first-round exit.

The China Open field is strong regardless — but Sinner's absence would fundamentally change the narrative around the event.

What's Next

The China Open draw is expected around September 28. Any withdrawal filed before that date would resolve the speculation. If Sinner's name appears in the draw on Saturday, expect him to play. Watch atptour.com and the China Open official channels for updates. Tournament play in Beijing begins September 30 for both the ATP 500 (men) and WTA events running concurrently.
