Laver Cup 2026: Day 1 Lineup Confirmed — Ruud Opens, Jódar Debuts, Alcaraz in Doubles

By Ryan Calloway | September 24, 2026

ATP | Laver Cup

The Laver Cup 2026 Day 1 lineup is official, and it has been shaped to deliver maximum drama from the opening match. When play begins at The O2 Arena in London on Friday, September 25, Casper Ruud of Team Europe will face Francisco Cerúndolo of Team World in the tournament opener — a rematch that carries stakes neither player will have forgotten.

Cerúndolo beat Ruud at the 2024 Laver Cup in Berlin, the only singles loss of Ruud's Laver Cup career. That result, combined with Ruud's standing as one of Europe's most reliable performers in team events, gives Match 1 an immediate story line.

Day Session (1 PM Local)

Two matches are scheduled for the afternoon session at The O2:

Match 1 sees Ruud (EUR) open against Cerúndolo (World). Cerúndolo is a late addition to Team World, called in after Tommy Paul withdrew with an elbow injury. He has been one of Team World's steadiest contributors in recent editions and arrives in London with a Laver Cup point on his résumé against Ruud specifically.

Match 2 follows with Jakub Menšík (EUR) against Brandon Nakashima (World). Nakashima also joined Team World late as a replacement for Ben Shelton, who withdrew for Davis Cup duty with the United States. Both Menšík and Nakashima are among the more promising young players on their respective tours, and this is a matchup of genuine quality.

Night Session (7 PM Local)

The night session opens with one of the most anticipated match-ups of the entire event: Rafael Jódar of Spain against Alexander Bublik of Kazakhstan. Both players are making their Laver Cup debuts. Jódar, 20, is one of the most exciting young talents in Spanish tennis and steps into the spotlight at one of the sport's biggest stages for the first time. Bublik brings an entirely different energy — unpredictable, dangerous, and capable of producing tennis that looks nothing like anyone else's game.

The closing match of Day 1 is doubles: Carlos Alcaraz and Menšík for Team Europe against Taylor Fritz and Bublik for Team World. It is a high-profile pairing for Europe, placing two of the team's most dynamic players together on opening night. Alcaraz does not play singles on Day 1. Alexander Zverev, the reigning US Open champion, is expected to be held back for Day 2 singles.

The Stakes

Team Europe enters the weekend holding an 8-1 all-time series advantage. But the record is misleading in terms of current trajectory. Team World won convincingly in San Francisco in 2025, 15-9, ending a long run of European dominance. Europe wants to reclaim the trophy at home in London, a city where the Laver Cup was last held in 2022. Team World wants to show that last year was not a fluke.

The format means Day 1 results, each worth one point, are foundational. Going into the weekend 0-4 or 1-3 in Day 1 play is a recoverable deficit — points are worth more on Day 2 and Day 3 — but momentum and energy in this event are real factors. Ruud knows that better than anyone.

What's Next

Day 1 play begins Friday, September 25 at The O2 Arena in London, with the day session starting at 1 PM local time and the night session at 7 PM. Coverage is available on Sky Sports and TNT Sports in the UK, with international broadcast information at lavercup.com. Day 2 on Saturday brings the higher-stakes two-point matches, and the final three-point Sunday session closes the event. The first team to reach 13 points claims the 2026 Laver Cup.
