### STORY 4: What an Alcaraz Title Would Mean

**Long-Form Post (12:30 PM ET)**

Carlos Alcaraz has won almost everything.

Two Wimbledon titles. Roland Garros. The US Open. At 23, he has a Grand Slam on every surface that holds one. But the ATP Finals — the year-end championship for the eight best players in the world — has never been his.

Until today, he had never even played in the final. The tournament structure has worked against him, the draw has not been kind, and injury has ended runs before they began. Now he is here, with a 13-match winning streak, managing a thigh concern, and facing the most in-form player of the week in Alexander Zverev.

If he wins, he completes a career sweep of the sport's biggest titles. He also becomes the first Spanish champion at the ATP Finals since Álex Corretja lifted the trophy in 1998 — 28 years ago.

That is what today is about for Alcaraz. History. The last piece.

### STORY 5: Zverev's Third Title Bid

**Long-Form Post (3:00 PM ET)**

Alexander Zverev was already having a career-best year before this week. He finished as year-end world No. 1 for the first time in his career. Now he is in the ATP Finals final without dropping a set, with a chance to win the tournament for the third time.

The list of players who have won the ATP Finals three or more times is short. Ivan Lendl. Pete Sampras. Roger Federer. Novak Djokovic. Jimmy Connors. Ilie Nastase. John McEnroe. Lleyton Hewitt. Zverev already has two titles — 2018 and 2021. A third today would put him in that company.

He has looked the part all week. His serve is functioning at the highest level. His foot movement is sharp. The ankle that caused concern before the tournament has not cost him a single set, let alone a match.

The challenge is Alcaraz, who leads their head-to-head 7-6 and won their last meeting in a five-set Australian Open semifinal earlier this year. But hard-court finals often favor the big server, and Zverev's ball has been the best in Turin.

Can he finish it?
