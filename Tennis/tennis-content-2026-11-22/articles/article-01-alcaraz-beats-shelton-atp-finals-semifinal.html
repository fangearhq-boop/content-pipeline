Alcaraz Comes Back From a Set Down to Beat Shelton 3-6, 6-3, 7-6(1) — Advances to ATP Finals Final

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | November 22, 2026

Carlos Alcaraz will play in the ATP Finals final for the first time in his career after defeating Ben Shelton 3-6, 6-3, 7-6(1) in the first semifinal at the Inalpi Arena in Turin on Saturday evening.

It was not a comfortable path. Shelton won the first set, broke early, and played with the kind of heavy-serving aggression that had carried him through the group stage and into this semifinal. Alcaraz, who has been managing a left thigh issue throughout the tournament, looked briefly restricted — his movement not quite as free as it had been in the round-robin phase.

Alcaraz Resets, Then Closes

The second set told a different story. Alcaraz tightened his return, controlled the pace from the baseline, and broke when it mattered. He took the set 6-3 to level the match and set up a deciding third.

The tiebreak was one-sided. Alcaraz won it 7-1, losing only a single point. That margin — against a player who had won the first set and pushed him throughout — showed exactly what separates Alcaraz from even the most dangerous opponents on his best day. When the moment demands it, he is simply better.

The win extended Alcaraz's winning streak to 13 consecutive matches — a run that began before the Paris Masters earlier this month and has carried him through the most demanding stretch of the European indoor season.

Shelton's Week in Turin

For Shelton, this ATP Finals appearance was a step forward rather than a setback. He qualified for the event for the first time, beat both Jodar and Fils in the group stage, and reached a semifinal against the No. 2 seed. He pushed Alcaraz to three sets, won the first, and left with a 2-2 tournament record.

At 23, Shelton is developing into one of the tour's most dangerous big-match players. His US Open win over Alcaraz in September remains a marker of what he is capable of. The gap between that result and Saturday's outcome is not as large as the scoreline suggests.

What's Next

Alcaraz faces Alexander Zverev in the ATP Finals final on Sunday, with the match scheduled no earlier than 3:00 PM CET at the Inalpi Arena. Zverev reached the final with a 7-6(0), 6-4 victory over Daniil Medvedev in the night session on Saturday — a result built on five consecutive straight-set wins and not a single set dropped all tournament.

If Alcaraz wins the final, he will claim the ATP Finals title for the first time in his career and become the first Spanish champion at the year-end championships since Álex Corretja in 1998.
