Alcaraz vs Zverev: Everything You Need to Know About Today's ATP Finals Final

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | November 22, 2026

The 2026 Nitto ATP Finals final is set. Carlos Alcaraz and Alexander Zverev will contest the championship match on Sunday at the Inalpi Arena in Turin, with play scheduled to begin no earlier than 3:00 PM CET. It is the matchup most of the tennis world wanted from the moment the draw was made.

The Paths to the Final

Zverev has been flawless. He went 3-0 in group play and then dismantled Daniil Medvedev 7-6(0), 6-4 in the semifinal — winning the tiebreak without dropping a point. Five wins, zero sets lost. His serve has been a weapon throughout, and there is no sign of the ankle concern that he mentioned before the tournament began.

Alcaraz has been tested. He completed the group stage 3-0 but began managing a left thigh issue during his win over Cobolli. In the semifinal against Ben Shelton, he lost the first set before resetting, taking the second, and dominating the tiebreak 7-1. His winning streak now stands at 13 consecutive matches — a run that takes in Paris, the group stage, and a three-set semifinal.

Head-to-Head and Surface Context

The overall head-to-head stands at 7-6 to Alcaraz. Their most recent meeting was the 2026 Australian Open semifinal, where Alcaraz won a five-set battle that many called the match of the year. On hard courts specifically, Zverev holds the edge, and indoor hard — the surface at the Inalpi Arena — has historically favored big servers, which describes Zverev's game.

What History Is on the Line

Zverev is seeking his third ATP Finals title, having won the event in 2018 and 2021. Only eight players in the history of the tournament have won it three or more times, among them Ivan Lendl, Pete Sampras, Roger Federer, and Novak Djokovic.

Alcaraz is seeking his first. He has won Wimbledon twice, Roland Garros, and the US Open, but the ATP Finals has been elusive. If he wins today, he becomes the first Spanish champion at the year-end championships since Álex Corretja lifted the trophy in 1998 — 28 years ago.

What's Next

Sunday's final will close the 2026 season. Following the conclusion of the ATP Finals, the tour takes a full off-season break before the 2027 swing begins in January — opening in Brisbane, Doha, Adelaide, and Auckland before the Australian Open. For Alcaraz and Zverev, today is the last competitive match of the year. One of them ends it as champion.
