Zverev Beats Medvedev 7-6(0), 6-4 — Into ATP Finals Final Without Dropping a Set All Tournament

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | November 22, 2026

Alexander Zverev has been playing a different tournament to everyone else in Turin this week. On Saturday night, he proved it again: a 7-6(0), 6-4 defeat of Daniil Medvedev in the second ATP Finals semifinal at the Inalpi Arena, keeping alive a streak of five wins without conceding a single set.

The tiebreak defined the match. Medvedev, one of the most composed big-point players on tour and a former ATP Finals champion himself, did not win a point. Zverev took it 7-0 — a perfect tiebreak in a match of this magnitude, against a player of that caliber.

A Dominant Week at Inalpi Arena

Zverev has not been in a third set all week. He opened with straight-set wins in the round-robin, managed his ankle concern without it affecting his results, and has now gone through five of the tour's best players — including two former ATP Finals champions in Medvedev and, earlier, Ben Shelton — without dropping a set.

His serve has been the foundation. Medvedev, who entered with a 14-8 head-to-head lead over Zverev, found no way to disrupt the German's rhythm from the back of the court. The second set was tighter than the first, but Zverev broke once to close it out 6-4 and move into Sunday's final with the form of the best player in the building.

Medvedev's Week

Medvedev reached the semifinal after winning his Day 6 group match against Tiafoe. He has been consistent throughout the tournament but ran into Zverev at peak form. His head-to-head advantage heading in was real — 14-8 is a significant record — but Saturday's outcome suggests the margins between these players, on this surface, in this week, ran in one direction.

Medvedev exits without reaching the final for the second consecutive year. He remains one of the best indoor hard-court players in the world. This week simply belonged to Zverev.

What's Next

Zverev meets Carlos Alcaraz in the ATP Finals final on Sunday, with the match scheduled no earlier than 3:00 PM CET. Alcaraz won the first semifinal against Ben Shelton 3-6, 6-3, 7-6(1) — coming back from a set down, managing a thigh concern, and extending his winning streak to 13 matches.

If Zverev wins, he claims his third ATP Finals title — joining a group of eight players in history to have won the event at least three times — and completes the crowning achievement of his career year as world No. 1.
