World No. 1, Five Wins, No Sets Dropped: What a Third ATP Finals Title Would Mean for Zverev

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | November 22, 2026

Alexander Zverev arrived at the ATP Finals as the year-end world No. 1 for the first time in his career. He leaves the week — whatever happens on Sunday — having already validated that ranking. Five wins. Zero sets dropped. A tiebreak won 7-0 against one of the tour's best big-point players. A career year, capped in Turin.

But a third ATP Finals title would mean something beyond any of that.

Joining an Exclusive Group

Only eight players in the history of the event — which has run since 1970 under various names — have won the ATP Finals three or more times. The list includes Ivan Lendl, Pete Sampras, Roger Federer, Novak Djokovic, Jimmy Connors, Ilie Nastase, John McEnroe, and Lleyton Hewitt. These are some of the most decorated names in the history of the game.

Zverev won his first title here in 2018, beating Roger Federer in the final. In 2021, he defeated Daniil Medvedev. A third title today, against Alcaraz, would put him in that historical company and confirm that his wins were not isolated moments but a defining thread of his career.

The Complete Year for Zverev

Being year-end No. 1 is the highest recognition the ATP can give to a season's body of work. It means more points accumulated across more tournaments than any other player over 12 months. Zverev has achieved that for the first time in 2026, and the ATP Finals — the final event of the season — gives him a chance to close the year with the sport's most prestigious title attached to it.

His form in Turin has been consistent with his form throughout the year on hard courts. The serve is functioning at a high level. His movement, ankle concern aside, has been assured. The confidence of a player who is absolutely certain of his game has shown in every tiebreak, every closing game, every critical moment this week.

What's Next

The final is Sunday at the Inalpi Arena in Turin, beginning no earlier than 3:00 PM CET. Zverev faces Carlos Alcaraz, who has won 13 consecutive matches and leads their overall head-to-head 7-6. Their Australian Open semifinal earlier this year — a five-set Alcaraz win — is the most recent data point. But this is a final. Indoor hard court. A year-end No. 1 at peak form against a player managing a thigh concern and coming off a three-set semifinal.

The 2026 season ends tonight. For Zverev, it could end as the best year any tennis player has had in a generation.
