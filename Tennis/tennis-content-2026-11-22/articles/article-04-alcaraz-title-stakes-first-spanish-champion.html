First ATP Finals Title, First Spanish Champion in 28 Years: What Alcaraz Is Playing For Today

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | November 22, 2026

Carlos Alcaraz has built a career defined by collecting the titles no one expects him to win so young, and then collecting them again. Two Wimbledon championships. A French Open. A US Open. At 23, he has Grand Slam titles on grass, clay, and hard courts. And yet, the ATP Finals — the event that crowns the eight best players in the world at the end of every season — had never gone his way.

Until this week, he had never reached the final. This afternoon, in Turin, he has the chance to change that.

The One Major Title That Has Eluded Him

The ATP Finals carries a different kind of weight to a Grand Slam. It is a round-robin format, condensed into eight days, against only the year's top performers. There are no upset paths from a lucky draw, no early rounds to build rhythm, no weak opponents. Every match is a final-level test.

Alcaraz has had difficult draws, early losses, and injury interventions in previous appearances. This year, he went 3-0 in the group stage, managed a thigh issue through the semifinals, and reached the final on a 13-match winning streak. If the tournament has ever offered him a moment, it is this one.

The Spanish Legacy on the Line

Beyond Alcaraz's personal history, today's final carries a national footnote. Spain has produced some of the greatest players in the history of the sport — from Àlex Corretja, who won this tournament in 1998, to Rafael Nadal, who dominated clay courts for two decades. But no Spanish player has won the ATP Finals since Corretja did it in Hannover, 28 years ago.

Corretja beat Carlos Moyá in an all-Spanish final that year. Nadal, despite his dominance on tour, never won the year-end championships. Alcaraz, who carries the torch of Spanish tennis into a new era, has an opportunity today that no compatriot has had for nearly three decades.

What's Next

The final is at the Inalpi Arena in Turin, beginning no earlier than 3:00 PM CET. Alcaraz faces Alexander Zverev, the year-end world No. 1, who has not dropped a set in five tournament matches. The head-to-head stands at 7-6 to Alcaraz overall, with their most recent meeting — the 2026 Australian Open semifinal — going five sets in Alcaraz's favor. This is a different day, a different surface, and a different kind of pressure. By this evening, the last trophy of the season will have a name on it.
