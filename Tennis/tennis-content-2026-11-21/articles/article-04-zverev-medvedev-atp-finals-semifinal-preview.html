Zverev vs Medvedev: Two Former Champions, One Ankle Concern, and the ATP Finals Semifinal

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | November 21, 2026

Saturday evening's ATP Finals semifinal at the Inalpi Arena in Turin begins at 8:30 PM CET, and it is a match between two players who have already won this event. Alexander Zverev took the title in 2018 and again in 2021. Daniil Medvedev won it in 2020. Between them they have three of the last eight ATP Finals titles, and they know exactly what is required to win here. The difference on Saturday is that Zverev is managing a right ankle issue that he described in terms that suggested it may never fully go away.

The Head-to-Head

Medvedev leads their career head-to-head 14-8. That is a substantial advantage built over years of matches at the highest level. On indoor hard courts specifically, the conditions they both prefer and both know, Medvedev's ability to absorb pace, disrupt timing, and hit winners from defensive positions has caused Zverev problems repeatedly.

But Zverev has found solutions before. He is the year-end No. 1 — the best player in the world by ranking points across a full season — and he has beaten Medvedev in conditions similar to these. The most recent significant indoor match between the two went Zverev's way, and form heading into this week is relevant. Zverev went 3-0 in the group stage without dropping a set. Medvedev was equally efficient, with 15 aces in his win over Tiafoe on Friday.

Zverev's Ankle

The ankle has been the central question since Zverev visibly hurt it in the closing stages of his win over Fils on Thursday. He was asked about it ahead of the semifinal and gave an honest, if concerning, answer: he said the problem "will probably stay until the end of my career," referencing the history of his ankle since the ligament tear he suffered at the 2022 French Open.

He has managed the ankle throughout his career since that injury — winning titles, reaching Grand Slam finals, and clinching the year-end No. 1 ranking while dealing with it at various points. Getting treatment, managing pain levels, and competing through discomfort is not new territory for him. Whether the flare-up from Thursday's match has healed sufficiently for a full-intensity semifinal is the question that only the first few games of Saturday's match will answer.

The Case for Medvedev

Medvedev arrived in Turin in form. His three wins in the Ivan Lendl Group — including the clinical Tiafoe defeat — showed a player operating at a level close to his best. His serve has been outstanding all week. His return game, which is one of the tour's best at neutralizing big servers, will be tested against Zverev's delivery but is capable of competing effectively.

The 14-8 career head-to-head is not an accident. Medvedev understands how to disrupt Zverev's rhythm — keeping the ball deep, extending points, and forcing errors from a player who needs clean ball-striking to generate his best tennis. If Zverev is managing ankle discomfort, that disruption becomes easier to execute.

What's Next

Zverev and Medvedev meet at 8:30 PM CET Saturday in the second ATP Finals semifinal at the Inalpi Arena in Turin. The winner plays Sunday's final, not before 3:00 PM CET on November 22. The earlier semifinal — Alcaraz versus Shelton at 2:30 PM CET — will complete the final draw. A Zverev win sets up the most anticipated final of the week: Alcaraz against the year-end No. 1. A Medvedev win reopens the conversation about the 2020 champion's ability to still win the season's biggest prizes.
