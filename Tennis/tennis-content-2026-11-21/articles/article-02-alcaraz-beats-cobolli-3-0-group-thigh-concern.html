Alcaraz Completes 3-0 Group Stage but Left Thigh Concern Clouds Shelton Semifinal

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | November 21, 2026

Carlos Alcaraz finished the ATP Finals group stage without a loss, beating Flavio Cobolli 6-4, 6-1 on Friday to complete a perfect 3-0 record in the Ivan Lendl Group. But a left thigh issue that developed during the match — requiring strapping and visibly affecting his movement at points — has become the dominant story heading into his Saturday semifinal against Ben Shelton.

Reports from Turin indicated Alcaraz was managing the injury in the buildup to Saturday and limiting his movement during practice. Regardless, he is in the semifinals, and his opponent will be waiting regardless of what his physical condition looks like on the day.

The Cobolli Match

The score, 6-4, 6-1, flatters Alcaraz slightly in the first set and understates his dominance in the second. He dropped his serve once in the opening set before breaking back and taking control. The second set was near-dominant — Cobolli won only one game, and the Italian's energy and the support of the Turin crowd were not enough to shift the match's momentum.

Alcaraz had already secured his place in the semifinals before the final round-robin day, so the Cobolli match carried no standing implications for him. He played it as a competition, though — there was nothing casual about his approach — and the injury emerged in that context. That makes it more concerning, not less. He was not stretching himself beyond necessity.

Cobolli exits the tournament at 1-2. He beat Tiafoe in the group opener in what was one of the week's most significant wins for Italian tennis, but wins over Alcaraz or Medvedev were always going to be the obstacle. He was competitive throughout his two losses and will leave Turin having shown he belongs in this company even if the results did not go his way.

The Injury Concern

The thigh issue is the central question for Alcaraz heading into the semifinal. He has played through injury concerns before — the left thigh was strapped for some of his group matches — but the reports of limited movement in practice are a different category of concern. If his mobility is compromised on Saturday, Shelton's game becomes a significantly more dangerous proposition.

Shelton beat Alcaraz in a five-set US Open quarterfinal in September — a match that ended past midnight in New York. Alcaraz won their most recent meeting, the Paris Masters final earlier this month, in straight sets. The head-to-head is 4-1 to Alcaraz at tour level, but the US Open win showed that Shelton is capable of beating him when the conditions allow it. An injury-affected Alcaraz changes the conditions.

What's Next

Alcaraz faces Shelton in the ATP Finals semifinals on Saturday at 2:30 PM CET at the Inalpi Arena in Turin. It is the first of the two semifinals on Day 7. If Alcaraz wins, he will play Sunday's final — his chance to claim an ATP Finals title for the first time. He has never won the event. The final is scheduled for Sunday, November 22, not before 3:00 PM CET.
