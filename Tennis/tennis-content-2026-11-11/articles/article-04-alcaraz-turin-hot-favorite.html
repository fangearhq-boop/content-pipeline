Alcaraz Arrives in Turin Chasing His First ATP Finals Title — On the Hottest Winning Streak of 2026

By Elena Voss | November 11, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

By any honest assessment, Carlos Alcaraz is the form player in men's tennis right now. He has won 10 consecutive matches, taking back-to-back Masters 1000 titles in Vienna and Paris. He has five titles in 2026, nine career Masters 1000 crowns, and a game that is currently running at a frequency no one else in the field can match.

He is also the player who has never won the Nitto ATP Finals. And that is exactly why he is in Turin.

The Form That Brought Him Here

The Vienna title last week was impressive. The Paris title that immediately followed it was something else. Five wins in Paris, including a comeback from two match points down against Taylor Fritz in the semifinals, culminating in a measured, clinical final victory over Ben Shelton — that is not the resume of a player in his first good week of the year. That is a player who has worked out how to win at the highest level, repeatedly, against the best competition the tour offers.

The 10-match streak heading into Turin includes wins over three top-10 opponents. Alcaraz has not been tested by a slow or favorable bracket. He has beaten the players who were in front of him, and he has done it on indoor hard courts — which, historically, have been his slightly weaker surface compared to clay.

That last detail matters for Turin, where the Inalpi Arena's conditions typically favor big servers and players who can flatten the ball out aggressively. Alcaraz has, over the past two weeks, shown he can do both things when required.

The One Missing Trophy

For all of Alcaraz's achievements, the ATP Finals has remained out of reach. He has qualified for the event before but never won it, never reached the final, never converted his baseline excellence into the specific demands of a week-long indoor round-robin against an elite eight-man field.

That changes nothing about his current form. But it is context worth holding: being the best player on the tour for most of a year does not automatically translate to winning the ATP Finals. The format, the surface, the compressed schedule, the fact that every opponent is calibrated to the high-intensity indoor game — it all filters the result in ways that the standard tour doesn't.

Alcaraz knows this. He has spoken about wanting to win Turin before his career is done. This may be the best chance he has had to do it.

The Competition

Zverev won two Grand Slams this year and enters as the year-end No. 1. Shelton pushed Alcaraz to two tight sets in the Paris final on Sunday. Medvedev at this event, in past years, has been formidable. No one in this field arrived by accident.

But the gap between Alcaraz's current form and the rest of the field is visible. It does not mean he wins — the ATP Finals has produced enough surprises over the years to know that nothing is certain in a round-robin format. It does mean that going into the draw ceremony Thursday, the smart money is on the Spaniard with the momentum.

What's Next

The draw is Thursday, November 12 at 12:00 PM. First matches at the Inalpi Arena begin Saturday, November 15. The final is Sunday, November 22.

Alcaraz will want to know his group as quickly as possible — not to manage expectations, but to start preparing for the specific opponents in front of him. After Vienna and Paris, he is not here to feel out the week. He is here to win it.
