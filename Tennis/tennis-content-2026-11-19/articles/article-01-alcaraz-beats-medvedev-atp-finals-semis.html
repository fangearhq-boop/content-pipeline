Alcaraz Cruises Past Medvedev 6-2, 6-4 to Book ATP Finals Semifinal Spot

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | November 19, 2026

Carlos Alcaraz is into the ATP Finals semifinals, and he got there in a performance that left little room for doubt. His 6-2, 6-4 win over Daniil Medvedev on Wednesday at the Inalpi Arena in Turin was compact — approximately 72 minutes — and commanding throughout. Alcaraz faced zero break points, extended his head-to-head lead over Medvedev to 8-3, and confirmed his place in the last four as the Ivan Lendl Group winner at 2-0.

The only note of concern came from an injury. Alcaraz took a medical timeout for his left thigh mid-match, emerging with the leg visibly strapped. He completed the second set without any visible decline in level, which suggested the issue was precautionary rather than severe — but it is a detail that will be worth monitoring as the week continues.

How the Match Unfolded

The opening set was straightforward. Alcaraz moved through Medvedev's service games with controlled aggression, while holding his own serve comfortably. The 6-2 score was an accurate summary of the balance of play.

The second set was more even but never tilted Medvedev's way. The Russian, who reached the group stage having beaten Cobolli in his opener, could not create the break-point opportunities he needed to change the dynamic. Alcaraz closed out 6-4 and reached the bench with his record in the group stage spotless.

Zero break points conceded is the number that most clearly defines the match. It was not simply that Alcaraz won — it was that he won without letting the match become competitive at the critical moments. That level of service-game security, sustained across two sets and through a medical delay, points to a player in very good shape heading into the knockout rounds.

The H2H With Medvedev

The head-to-head between these two players is now 8-3 in Alcaraz's favour. They have met in multiple major finals and in both Masters 1000 and slam contexts. Medvedev's route to victories against Alcaraz typically involves attrition — long matches, extended baseline exchanges, and wearing the Spaniard down over five sets. In a best-of-three format on fast indoor courts, the conditions have consistently favoured Alcaraz, who can close points more directly.

Wednesday was another example of that pattern. Medvedev's ability to construct long points and shift the tempo of rallies could not get traction in a match that never extended to the kind of length where his best tennis tends to emerge.

What's Next

Alcaraz will play the McEnroe Group runner-up in the semifinals on Saturday at the Inalpi Arena. The McEnroe Group finale takes place on Thursday — Ben Shelton faces Rafael Jodar in the afternoon, and Alexander Zverev plays Arthur Fils in the evening. The winner of that group, alongside Zverev who is already through, will determine Alcaraz's semifinal opponent. As for his left thigh, any update from the Alcaraz camp before Thursday's semifinal schedule is confirmed will be closely watched. The form is there; the fitness question is the only open variable.
