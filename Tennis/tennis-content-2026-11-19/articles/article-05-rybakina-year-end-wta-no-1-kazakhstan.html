Rybakina Closes 2026 as WTA World No. 1 — First From Kazakhstan to Achieve It

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | November 19, 2026

Elena Rybakina ends 2026 at the top of the WTA rankings, cementing a historic year with a distinction no player representing Kazakhstan has held before. She is the 17th player in WTA history to close a season ranked No. 1 in the world. The ranking was clinched on October 29 at the Pan Pacific Open in Tokyo. The official year-end tally: 8,099 points. She receives the Chris Evert WTA No. 1 Trophy as the official acknowledgment of the achievement.

What the Year-End Rankings Show

Mirra Andreeva, who won the 2026 WTA Finals in Indian Wells without dropping a set, closes the season at No. 2 with 7,806 points. The gap between Rybakina and Andreeva at year-end is 293 points — close enough that the season, had it gone differently at a few key tournaments, could have produced a different final order.

Iga Swiatek, who held the year-end No. 1 position in 2022, 2023, and 2024, finishes 2026 at No. 9. That drop from the summit to ninth place over two seasons is the most significant shift in the top of the rankings, and it reflects a year in which Swiatek's dominance gave way to a broader field of contenders.

Rybakina's Year in Context

Rybakina has been building toward the No. 1 position for several seasons. She won Wimbledon in 2022, her first Grand Slam title, and has been a consistent presence in the later stages of majors and WTA 1000 events since. The pan Pacific Open in Tokyo was the moment the year-end calculations confirmed what her tournament results had been pointing toward across 2026.

Playing under the Kazakhstani flag — she was born in Moscow and represented Russia before obtaining Kazakhstani citizenship — Rybakina's achievement extends the year-end No. 1 to a nation that has never had a player in this position before. The 17th to reach the year-end summit is a figure that places her in historical company going back to the founding of the WTA rankings system.

What's Next

Rybakina will begin her 2027 season with the year-end No. 1 ranking and the target that comes with it: defending a position everyone on the tour is working to take. Andreeva, at No. 2 and 19 years old, will be the most closely watched name in terms of trajectory. Swiatek, at No. 9, is a former three-time year-end top player who will not accept a ranking in the lower reaches of the top 10 as a permanent state. The 2027 Australian Open will be the first major test of whether the year-end order holds or shifts quickly. The WTA's depth going into next season is as strong as it has been in years.
