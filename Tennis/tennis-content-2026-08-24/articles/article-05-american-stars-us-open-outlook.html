American Tennis After Cincinnati: Tiafoe, Nakashima, Gauff, and Pegula Head to Flushing

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | August 24, 2026

The Cincinnati Open is over. The results are in the books, and four American players are heading to the US Open carrying something valuable from this week — not just ranking points and prize money, but form, confidence, and in two cases, the particular motivation that comes from reaching a final and losing.

The 2026 US Open begins August 30 at Flushing Meadows. For Tiafoe, Nakashima, Gauff, and Pegula, the timing could not be better.

Frances Tiafoe: Two Finals, Still Waiting

Frances Tiafoe has now lost two Masters 1000 finals, both at the Cincinnati Open. In 2024, he fell to Jannik Sinner. On Sunday, he fell to Arthur Fils 6-3, 1-6, 6-0 — a match that began with Tiafoe in control and ended with Fils playing the best tennis of the afternoon in a ruthless third set.

There is no way to spin a 1-6, 6-0 final set as anything other than a difficult conclusion. But the path that brought Tiafoe to that point was remarkable: he saved three set points to defeat Brandon Nakashima in the semifinals, and he fought his way through the bracket with the composure of a player who has learned how to navigate the pressure of big matches.

His first Slam title still awaits. He enters the 2026 US Open with the form to make a deep run and the hunger that only close calls produce.

Brandon Nakashima: A Career-Best Season

Nakashima's 2026 has been quietly historic. He reached the semifinals at Montreal and then Cincinnati — back-to-back Masters 1000 semifinals that represent the highest level of sustained performance in his career. His Tour-leading 22 hard-court wins in 2026 tell the story of a player who has become genuinely difficult to beat on the surface that will define the next month.

His loss to Tiafoe on Friday was a competitive match decided by a single set point in the opening set. His trajectory is clear. Whether that trajectory produces a US Open quarterfinal, or something deeper, is a conversation the draw will start when it is released Thursday.

Coco Gauff: Two-Time Cincinnati Champion

Coco Gauff won this tournament. That is the full and complete summary of her Cincinnati week — she was the best player in the draw and she leaves with the trophy. She enters Flushing as the No. 3 women's seed and as someone who has already played and won Grand Slam-level tennis within the past 12 months.

The US Open has particular meaning for Gauff. She won the 2023 title at Flushing Meadows in one of the defining matches of her career. The hard-court game she is playing right now is the best argument for why she is one of the favorites to win again.

Jessica Pegula: Finalist and No. 4 Seed

Pegula lost Sunday's final to Gauff, 6-2, 6-4, but her week delivered something more lasting than a final score. She defeated Iga Swiatek — who had won 10 consecutive matches entering the match — in a 2-hour 47-minute semifinal that required her to fight back from a break down in the third set. That kind of win demonstrates the kind of mental endurance that Grand Slam runs are built on.

She enters Flushing as the No. 4 seed and as a player who has shown this summer that she can beat anyone on the tour on a given day. The US Open is her home Grand Slam. Her draw this week suggests she is going to give her home crowd plenty to cheer about.

What's Next

The 2026 US Open draw ceremony is Thursday, August 27. Main draw begins Sunday, August 30 at the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center. Gauff is seeded No. 3, Pegula No. 4. The men's bracket, with Zverev as No. 1 seed and Alcaraz at No. 2, will be closely watched for where Tiafoe and Nakashima land in the draw.
