Fils and Gauff Win Cincinnati: France's First Masters 1000 in 12 Years, Gauff's Second Crown

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | August 24, 2026

The 2026 Cincinnati Open produced its champions on Sunday, and both finals delivered the kind of clarity that only the scoreboard can offer. Arthur Fils defeated Frances Tiafoe 6-3, 1-6, 6-0 in 1 hour and 28 minutes to claim the biggest title of his career. Coco Gauff defeated Jessica Pegula 6-2, 6-4 to win her second Cincinnati Open crown. France had not produced a Masters 1000 champion since Jo-Wilfried Tsonga in 2014. Gauff had not won this tournament since 2023.

Both finals were also the latest chapters in a week that had already given tennis fans more than enough to talk about — the first ATP Masters 1000 final between two Black players, the first all-American WTA Cincinnati final in 56 years. The results lived up to the billing.

Fils: A Perfect Final Set

Arthur Fils had to solve a problem in this final. He solved it decisively.

The first set belonged to the 22-year-old Frenchman, who claimed it 6-3 with controlled aggression. The second belonged to Frances Tiafoe, who fought back to 6-1 and reset the match entirely. Tiafoe, who has lived in big moments throughout his career, appeared ready to extend this final into a longer story.

Fils did not allow it. The third set was as one-sided as professional tennis gets at this level — 6-0, six games, no breaks, no margin for debate. Fils finished the match 10-0 in three-set matches in 2026. He has also not lost to an American opponent since the start of 2025, a run that now extends to eight matches.

At 22 years old, Fils becomes the youngest-ever French Masters 1000 champion, and the first French player to reach three Masters 1000 semifinals in a season since Guy Forget in 1991. The Cincinnati title is his fifth on the ATP Tour and his second of 2026, adding to the Barcelona title he claimed earlier this year.

For Tiafoe, it is the second time he has reached a Masters 1000 final and come up short, with both defeats at Cincinnati. He saved three set points to reach Sunday's match, only to find Fils delivering a third set that left no room for a comeback. The loss stings, but his Cincinnati run confirmed what his ranking already suggested: he is playing the best tennis of his career heading into Flushing Meadows.

Gauff: Controlled from the Start

Coco Gauff's win over Jessica Pegula was not an easy afternoon — Pegula makes everything difficult — but Gauff held control throughout.

She opened the match with five consecutive games, building a 5-0 lead in the first set before Pegula steadied herself. The second set was competitive: Pegula pushed to 5-4, forcing Gauff to serve it out. Gauff did, closing the match 6-2, 6-4 with the kind of composure that has made her among the most reliable players in the game on hard courts.

The win gives Gauff her second Cincinnati Open title. It also confirms her standing heading into the US Open — she enters Flushing as the No. 3 seed, with the tournament's momentum behind her and the memory of the 2023 US Open title in her pocket.

Pegula can point to a difficult week's work — she defeated No. 1 Aryna Sabalenka's successor as defending champion, Iga Swiatek, in a 2-hour 47-minute semifinal, fighting back from a break down in the third set. That kind of win earns its own kind of respect, regardless of the final result. She enters the US Open as the No. 4 seed.

What's Next

The attention now shifts fully to Flushing Meadows. US Open qualifying began Monday at the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center, and the main draw starts August 30. The draw ceremony is Thursday, August 27, with seedings based on the August 24 rankings — which now reflect this week's Cincinnati results. Alexander Zverev is the No. 1 men's seed with Jannik Sinner absent; Aryna Sabalenka tops the women's draw.
