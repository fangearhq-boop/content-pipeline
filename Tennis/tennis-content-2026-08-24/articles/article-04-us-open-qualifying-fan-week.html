US Open Qualifying Begins: The Road to Flushing Starts Now

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | August 24, 2026

The 2026 US Open is officially underway. Not with the main draw — that begins August 30 — but with qualifying, which started Monday at the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center in Flushing, Queens. For many players, today's matches represent the most important competition of their season. A qualifying win earns a spot in a $108 million Grand Slam. A loss sends them home.

Qualifying at a Grand Slam operates on a simple principle: the field is full, and the only way in is through. Players ranked outside the main draw cutoff compete in a three-round qualifying event, with the winners earning positions in the 128-player bracket. It is not glamorous tennis. It is played on outer courts in front of small crowds, often in heat that makes concentration difficult. But for the players fighting through it, these matches carry everything.

Fan Week at Flushing

While qualifying matches run through the grounds, Fan Week at the US Open gives ticket holders access to the site. Arthur Ashe Kids' Day opened the week on August 23. Tomorrow, Tuesday August 25, brings one of the most anticipated non-competitive events in years: Roger Federer and Andy Roddick face John McEnroe and Andre Agassi in an exhibition on Arthur Ashe Stadium.

The event is sold out. It brings together four of the defining personalities in the history of the men's game — two from one generation, two from another — for a night that is less about competition than about celebration. Federer last played at the US Open in 2019. His return to Flushing, even in an exhibition context, is notable enough on its own. Paired with three other Hall of Famers on Arthur Ashe Stadium, it promises to be the kind of evening people remember.

Fan Week runs through August 29. A Free Fan Access Pass is required for grounds admission during the qualifying and Fan Week period. The main draw begins Sunday, August 30.

A Record Prize Pool

The 2026 US Open carries a $108 million prize pool — a record for the event and one of the largest in professional sports. The increase reflects the continued growth of tennis's commercial footprint, and it means the financial stakes at every level of the draw are higher than they have ever been.

For the players grinding through qualifying right now, that number is not abstract. A first-round loss in the main draw still earns a significant check. Making the third round changes a player's year. And for the players ranked well enough to be direct entries into the main draw — Zverev, Alcaraz, Sabalenka, Gauff, and the rest — the prize money is a backdrop to a competition that is already about more than money.

What's Next

US Open qualifying continues through Wednesday, August 26. The draw ceremony for the main draw is Thursday, August 27, when the full 128-player brackets for men's and women's singles will be revealed. The Fan Week Federer and Roddick vs. McEnroe and Agassi exhibition is tomorrow, August 25, at Arthur Ashe Stadium. Main draw begins Sunday, August 30.
