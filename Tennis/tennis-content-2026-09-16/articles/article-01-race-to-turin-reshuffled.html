Race to Turin Reshuffled: Zverev Leads, Shelton Surges to Third

By Elena Voss

September 16, 2026

The dust has barely settled on the 2026 US Open, and already the Race to Turin standings look dramatically different from where they stood two weeks ago. Alexander Zverev, fresh off his second Grand Slam title of the year, leads the PIF ATP Live Race to Turin with 8,650 points. Jannik Sinner, who missed the fortnight entirely with a right-knee injury, sits second with 7,950. Both have already clinched their spots at November's Nitto ATP Finals — the qualification cutoff currently sits at 5,070 points, and neither man is in danger of falling below it.

The genuinely interesting story is everything below them.

Shelton Storms Into Third

Ben Shelton's US Open final run — which took him through Carlos Alcaraz in the quarterfinals and Frances Tiafoe in the semifinals before a hard-fought four-set loss to Zverev — has vaulted him to third in the Race with 4,420 points. The 23-year-old American, who entered the tournament ranked No. 9 in the world and left it at a career-high No. 4, now sits within 650 points of Alcaraz in fourth (4,050 points).

For context on what that means: no American man has qualified for the Nitto ATP Finals since John Isner made it to London in 2018. That eight-year drought is now at genuine risk of ending. Shelton needs to stay consistent through the Asian swing and the fall indoor season to get there, but the trajectory is undeniable.

The Fight for Six Spots

Below Shelton and Alcaraz, the standings compress quickly. Flavio Cobolli, the Italian who reached the French Open final earlier this year, holds fifth with 3,530 points. Frances Tiafoe sits sixth at 3,330 — barely 200 points behind Cobolli. Arthur Fils, the young Frenchman who had a strong summer hard-court swing before a fourth-round US Open exit, is seventh with 3,150. Daniil Medvedev currently occupies the eighth and final qualifying position with 2,780 points.

The gap between Medvedev in eighth and players immediately outside the cutoff is the razor's edge of the season. Every match between now and the end of November carries weight for half a dozen players whose year-end legacy depends on which eight men stand in Turin.

The Asian Swing Sets the Table

The next major block of points is available at the China Open in Beijing, which begins September 30 and carries 500 ranking points for the champion. The Shanghai Masters follows October 7-18 — a Masters 1000 event worth 1,000 points to the winner — and represents the biggest single points opportunity remaining before the indoor season. Vienna and Paris-Bercy will round out the fall calendar before Turin.

For Zverev, the Race lead adds motivation to an already remarkable 2026 season. He has won 51 of 64 matches. Holding the Race lead at the ATP Finals would make him the year-end No. 1 — a title he has been chasing since finishing the 2021 season at No. 3.

What's Next

All eyes now turn to Beijing on September 30. Sinner's return from his knee injury, Shelton's form as a tour favorite rather than an underdog, and Zverev's ability to sustain a career-best season will define the final two months of tennis's year. Six spots at the ATP Finals remain to be decided. The Asian swing is where that fight begins in earnest.
