Sinner Returns to High-Intensity Training, Targets China Open Comeback

By Marcus Cole

September 16, 2026

The question hanging over men's tennis since Jannik Sinner walked off the Wimbledon court in July with a title and a knee that needed attention has finally started to answer itself. The world No. 1 has returned to high-intensity training in Turin after completing treatment for extra-articular tendinous inflammation in his right knee — and the China Open in Beijing, which begins September 30, is his confirmed comeback target.

The injury kept Sinner away from the Canadian Open, the Cincinnati Open, and the US Open, where his absence was felt throughout the draw. He won Wimbledon to retain his title, then essentially disappeared from competition for six weeks while the rest of the tour competed at Flushing Meadows. Now, with the Asian swing two weeks away, Sinner is training at the Circolo della Stampa Sporting in Turin and the reports coming out of that training block are encouraging.

What the Injury Was — and Why It Matters

Extra-articular tendinous inflammation is inflammation in the tissue surrounding the knee joint, rather than damage inside the joint itself. Medically, that distinction matters: inflammation in the connective tissue around a joint generally carries a better recovery prognosis than damage to the cartilage, meniscus, or ligaments inside it. Sinner completed his treatment at JMedical in Turin, a specialist facility, and his current training program has moved beyond the testing and strengthening phase into full practice intensity.

The Italian gave the tennis world a brief update at Formula 1's Italian Grand Prix — a typically understated assessment from a player who keeps his medical updates precise and emotion-free. The message was clear: the knee was responding, the timeline was on track.

What's at Stake in Beijing

The China Open carries 500 ranking points for the champion and 300 for the runner-up. For Sinner, who already holds 7,950 Race to Turin points and has qualified for the Nitto ATP Finals in November, the Beijing event is less about securing his place in Turin and more about competitive sharpness — returning to match conditions after six weeks off — and building momentum for Shanghai, where the bigger points are available.

There is also a competitive reality to manage. Zverev leads the Race with 8,650 points. Two Grand Slam wins this year, a season record of 51-13, and the Race lead: Zverev enters the Asian swing as the form player in men's tennis. Sinner will need to find his rhythm quickly to close that gap before the season ends. A rusty return in Beijing followed by a deep Shanghai run is probably the most likely scenario, but Sinner has shown throughout his career an ability to perform at the highest level even after extended time away from competition.

The Asian Swing Picture

Sinner's return also shapes the conversations around Beijing and Shanghai in ways that go beyond his own ranking. With the world No. 1 back in the draw, the upper half of every bracket gets harder. Players who were calculating their paths to the final without Sinner in the picture will need to reconsider. Zverev, Shelton, Alcaraz, and whoever else makes the Asian swing a competitive battleground will know that the man who held this ranking from the start of 2026 is coming back to defend it.

What's Next

Sinner has two weeks before Beijing. If the knee continues to respond to training as reported, expect him to travel east around September 26-27 and begin his pre-tournament preparation at the site. The 2026 season has been unusual for him — Wimbledon in July was the high-water mark before the knee injury that cost him three hard-court events. The Asian swing is where he reasserts himself. The question is whether six weeks away from competition changes his game — or whether Sinner simply picks up where he left off at Wimbledon.
