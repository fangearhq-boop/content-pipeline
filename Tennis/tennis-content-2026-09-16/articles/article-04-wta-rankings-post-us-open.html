WTA Rankings Shake-Up: Kostyuk Breaks Into Top 10, Raducanu Drops 19

By Elena Voss

September 16, 2026

Elena Rybakina leading the WTA rankings is no longer news — she secured that position when she won the US Open, and the first week of her reign at No. 1 has passed without incident. What deserves a closer look is everything happening below her, because the post-US Open rankings tell a broader story about where women's tennis is heading and which players are rising fast enough to matter.

Rybakina's 9,901 points put her comfortably clear at the top. Aryna Sabalenka, who lost the US Open final to Rybakina in three sets, holds second at 7,875 — more than 2,000 points behind. A 99-week reign at No. 1 ended with the rankings release on September 15, and Sabalenka will need a strong Asian swing to close the gap. Jessica Pegula is third with 7,265 points, and Coco Gauff fourth at 7,244 — separated by just 21 points, one of the tightest gaps in recent memory at the top of the rankings.

Kostyuk Makes History

The most significant mover this week is Marta Kostyuk. The Ukrainian, who reached the fourth round at the US Open and has been quietly consistent throughout the 2026 season, enters the top 10 for the first time in her career. She debuts at No. 10 with 3,980 points.

Kostyuk, who turns 22 in September, has built her game on the hard courts. She hits with pronounced topspin from the baseline and has shown an improving ability to handle pressure points in the latter rounds of major tournaments. Her fourth-round run at Flushing Meadows was a statement of intent. The question now is whether she can maintain top-10 consistency — a different challenge from reaching it for the first time.

Andreeva Consolidates Fifth

Mirra Andreeva, who won Roland Garros earlier this year and represents the leading edge of Russian women's tennis after the rankings dominance of Sabalenka in recent seasons, consolidates fifth with 5,743 points. Linda Noskova of the Czech Republic holds sixth (5,328), Elina Svitolina seventh (4,809), Karolina Muchova eighth (4,683), and Iga Swiatek ninth (4,619).

Swiatek at ninth deserves a note. The Polish player, who was ranked No. 1 for much of 2024 and 2025, has had a difficult 2026 by her own standards. A US Open quarterfinal exit was enough to keep her in the top 10, but she enters the Asian swing without the ranking pressure she has historically been able to apply. Whether Swiatek rediscovers her most dominant form on the hard courts will be one of the storylines to watch in Beijing and Shanghai.

Raducanu's Drop

The most notable fall belongs to Emma Raducanu, who dropped 19 positions following an early US Open exit. Raducanu's ranking had been built in large part on her 2021 US Open title — points that have long since cycled off — and her 2026 season has been interrupted enough times by injury that a consistent top-10 ranking has remained elusive. The -19 drop is jarring in a single-week context but reflects a broader challenge that the British player has been navigating for several years.

What's Next

The WTA Asian swing begins with the China Open in Beijing (September 30 - October 11) and the Wuhan Open (October 12-18), where Rybakina's first major tests as world No. 1 will come. Sabalenka, who has been Rybakina's primary rival, is confirmed in the fields for both events. The top of women's tennis is tightly competitive in a way it has not been since the height of the Williams era, and the Asian swing will begin to separate those who are building toward the season-end WTA Finals and those who are merely defending what they have.
