Laver Cup 2026 Preview: The Rematch Nobody Scheduled Could Be the Match Everyone Wants

By Ryan Calloway

September 16, 2026

Nine days after Alexander Zverev and Ben Shelton played a four-set US Open final at Arthur Ashe Stadium, they will walk into The O2 Arena in London and, if the Laver Cup format cooperates, possibly play each other again. This time in team colors. This time with a crowd that has had nine days to stoke the story.

The 2026 Laver Cup — the ninth edition of the event that Roger Federer co-founded and which now stands as one of the most watchable weeks in men's tennis — takes place September 25-27 at The O2, returning to London for the second time in the event's history. Team Europe faces Team World across five sessions over three days, with the first team to 13 points claiming the title.

Team Europe: Deep and Dominant

Team Europe, captained by Yannick Noah, lines up with the tournament's two highest-ranked players in Alexander Zverev (No. 2 in the world) and Carlos Alcaraz (No. 3). Around them: Casper Ruud, Jakub Mensik, and Flavio Cobolli — a lineup that gives Noah genuine options at every slot in the draw. Zverev comes in as the hottest player in the world, having won Roland Garros and the US Open in 2026, and having gone 51-13 for the season. If he is anything like he has been since June, he may be the single most difficult player on either side to beat.

Alcaraz adds another dimension. The Spaniard, who was recovering from a wrist injury for the first half of the 2026 season, has been building form through the hard courts and brings the kind of athletic baseline-to-net game that the Laver Cup's indoor hard surface suits well. Ruud, Mensik, and Cobolli each add further depth, ensuring that Team Europe has viable singles players at every slot, not just at the top.

Team World: Motivated and Loaded

Team World, captained by Andre Agassi, is arguably the deepest World squad in the event's nine-year history. Shelton, who reached the US Open final just nine days before the Laver Cup begins, brings momentum and an unmistakable edge — he has spoken about how his runner-up finish stings, and that kind of motivation tends to play out on a court. Alex de Minaur, Tommy Paul, Taylor Fritz, Learner Tien, and Alexander Bublik complete the roster.

De Minaur, one of the most reliable serve-and-baseline players in the game, gives Agassi a steady anchor. Tien, the 20-year-old who announced himself at the US Open by eliminating an emotional Gaël Monfils, brings youth and flat-ball hitting that functions well at any level. Bublik's serve-volleying, big-risk style is built for the one-set Laver Cup format, where variance is a feature rather than a bug.

What Makes This Year Different

The Laver Cup has always drawn compelling rosters, but the 2026 edition benefits from unusual narrative timing. The US Open ended just nine days before play begins. The sport is in a period of genuine generational transition — Sinner, Zverev, Shelton, Alcaraz, Mensik, Cobolli, and Tien have collectively made clear that the era of dominance by a single player is over, replaced by something more chaotic and more engaging. The Laver Cup, which matches players across different rankings in short-format matches, is designed to let that chaos express itself.

Singles matches are one set. Each day escalates the points per match — Day 1 singles are worth 1 point each, Day 2 worth 2 points, Day 3 worth 3 points. Doubles are played at lower point values. The design forces teams to save their best players for the later sessions, and the mathematics consistently produce final-session elimination scenarios that no other event in tennis replicates.

What's Next

Session 1 begins Friday, September 25, with doors opening at 11:30 AM. The event runs through Sunday, September 27. Tickets remain available for most sessions, though evening sessions at The O2 — the same venue that hosted historic Laver Cup moments in 2022 — are likely to sell out. For tennis fans in London, this is the last major live event of the season before the indoor hard-court swing takes over. For everyone else watching from afar, the only question worth asking is: do Zverev and Shelton meet again? And if they do, who wins this time?
