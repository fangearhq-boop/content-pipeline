### Long-Form Post *Window: 12:30 PM ET* The most watched comeback in tennis right now has nothing to do with a tournament. It is Jannik Sinner, world No. 1, returning to the practice court in Turin. Sinner missed the Canadian Open, Cincinnati, and the US Open with extra-articular tendinous inflammation in his right knee — inflammation in the tissue around the joint, not inside it, which is medically significant. He completed treatment at JMedical in Turin and has resumed high-intensity training at the Circolo della Stampa Sporting. His target: the China Open in Beijing, which begins September 30. That means two more weeks of preparation before he returns to competitive tennis for the first time since Wimbledon, where he retained his title. The timing matters. The Race to Turin still has Sinner at No. 2 (7,950 points), already qualified. But Zverev leads the Race with 8,650 points, and a prolonged absence would erode whatever edge Sinner has in the year-end championship picture. Two weeks at the practice court first, then Beijing. Tennis's biggest return is coming. --- ### Image Post Caption *Window: 12:30 PM ET* Sinner injury update: back to high-intensity training in Turin after knee treatment. The top five are now Rybakina (9,901), Sabalenka (7,875), Pegula (7,265), Gauff (7,244), and Mirra Andreeva (5,743). Four of the five are under 25 years old. The headline mover upward is Marta Kostyuk, who breaks into the top 10 for the first time in her career, debuting at No. 10 with 3,980 points. The Ukrainian, who reached the fourth round at Flushing Meadows, has quietly built one of the more consistent seasons on tour. ### Long-Form Post *Window: 9:00 PM ET* While the US Open dominates the last two weeks of conversation, a different kind of tennis competition is about to begin. The Davis Cup Qualifiers 2nd Round runs from September 18-20, and 14 nations will compete for the last seven spots in November's Finals. Italy, the defending champions, earned a direct bye to the Finals. Spain — five-time champions and perennially one of the most competitive nations in the competition — enter at this second-round stage. Seven ties spread across the globe, each decided on a home-and-away basis over two days. The tie that will draw the most attention in this part of the world: Great Britain vs. Ecuador at London's Copper Box Arena on September 19-20. The Copper Box, a compact indoor venue that creates an intense atmosphere, has hosted some of the most memorable Davis Cup moments in recent British tennis history. Davis Cup is different from individual tour events. National pride, a home crowd, team camaraderie — elements that can flip the form book entirely. The Finals in November will feature the Final 8, but first, seven nations need to earn their way in.
