The Big Three Era Is Over: No Federer, Nadal or Djokovic in the Top 10 for the First Time Since 2002

By Ryan Calloway

September 15, 2026

For more than two decades, three names defined professional tennis. They won 66 Grand Slam titles between them. They turned every major final into a global event. They were so dominant, so consistent, so relentlessly excellent, that the tennis world built its identity around the question of which one of them would win the next major — not whether one of them would.

On Monday, for the first time since October 2002, none of Roger Federer, Rafael Nadal or Novak Djokovic appears in the ATP top 10. The Big Three era is over.

How It Ended

The end came not with a dramatic final match, but with a quiet first-round exit. Djokovic, the last standing member of the Big Three after Federer retired in September 2022 and Nadal in November 2024, lost to Mariano Navone in the first round of the US Open. It was the kind of loss that happened more frequently as Djokovic turned 39 — a younger, fresher opponent outworking an aging champion over three sets.

The defeat cost Djokovic 790 ranking points and sent him from No. 5 to No. 12. He is not retired. He is not done. But for the first time in 24 years, he does not sit inside the top 10 of the rankings he once ruled so completely that he held the No. 1 ranking for 428 weeks — the most in ATP history.

What 2002 Looked Like

The last time the top 10 was completely free of the Big Three was October 2002. Federer was 21 years old and had just won his first major, Wimbledon, the previous summer. Nadal was 16 and still two years from his first Roland Garros title. Djokovic was 15. The rankings were dominated by names that subsequent generations would remember only as answers to trivia questions: Lleyton Hewitt, Thomas Johansson, Carlos Moya, Albert Costa.

What followed that moment was 24 years of extraordinary, uninterrupted excellence from three men who played in different styles, on different surfaces, in different decades of their careers, and yet somehow remained at the top of the sport long past the age when most professional athletes have declined.

The Numbers That Define an Era

Federer: 20 Grand Slam titles, a record 310 weeks at No. 1, six Wimbledon titles in a row. Nadal: 22 Grand Slam titles including 14 Roland Garros championships, a record that may never be broken on clay. Djokovic: 24 Grand Slam titles, 428 weeks at No. 1, the only man to win all four Grand Slams on three different surface conditions.

Together, they won 66 of the last 83 Grand Slam titles. No three players in the history of any sport have dominated their respective discipline so thoroughly for so long.

The New Rulers

The names atop the rankings now are different. Jannik Sinner leads at No. 1. Alexander Zverev, the 2026 Roland Garros and US Open champion, sits at No. 2. Carlos Alcaraz — already a three-time Grand Slam champion in his early twenties — is No. 3. Ben Shelton, 23, is No. 4 following his US Open finalist run.

The talent is real. The ambition is undeniable. Whether any of them will build a career that echoes the Big Three is a question that will take years — perhaps decades — to answer.

What's Next

Djokovic has not announced retirement. He is expected to compete at select events through the end of 2026 and into 2027. But the era of the Big Three dominating the rankings conversation is finished. The sport they shaped for a generation now belongs to the players they inspired — a passing of the torch that was always coming, even if no one was quite ready for it to arrive.
