Rybakina's First Week at No. 1: China Open and Asian Swing Test Her New Status

By Elena Voss

September 15, 2026

Elena Rybakina has been the WTA world No. 1 for exactly one day. There is no time to adjust to the view from the top. The China Open begins September 30 in Beijing, and the Wuhan Open follows on October 12. The Asian swing — two WTA 1000 events in two weeks — arrives as Rybakina's first meaningful test of whether she can hold the position she earned by defeating Aryna Sabalenka 6-4, 5-7, 6-2 in Saturday's US Open final.

Rybakina officially claimed the No. 1 ranking on September 14, 2026, becoming the 30th woman to reach the world's top position in women's tennis and the first player from Kazakhstan to do so. She ended Sabalenka's 99-week reign at the top — one of the longest sustained runs at No. 1 in recent WTA history — with a performance that showcased everything that makes her such a difficult matchup: a serve that rivals any in the women's game, groundstrokes that dictate rallies from the first ball, and a composure in finals that lesser players often lack.

The China Open Test

The China Open in Beijing is a premier event in the WTA calendar, drawing full fields and carrying 1,000 ranking points for the champion. As a newly minted No. 1, Rybakina will face the unique pressure of being the player everyone wants to beat — a psychological shift that the best players manage by focusing on process rather than protection of status.

Her previous results on the Asian swing have been solid without being dominant. The draw she receives in Beijing — and whether Sabalenka is on the other side of it — will shape how her first weeks as No. 1 are remembered.

Sabalenka's Response

Aryna Sabalenka will not accept the transition quietly. The Belarusian is one of the most competitive players in the world, and she has demonstrated throughout her career that ranking setbacks sharpen rather than diminish her focus. Her loss in the US Open final was her second consecutive defeat by Rybakina in a Grand Slam final — she also lost the 2026 Australian Open to the Kazakhstani in January.

The H2H between the two now stands at 8-11 in favor of Sabalenka entering the Asian swing, but the momentum clearly belongs to Rybakina. Three of their four most recent meetings have gone Rybakina's way, including both Grand Slam final encounters in 2026.

What's Next

After Beijing and Wuhan, the WTA calendar moves into the indoor hard-court season in Europe, culminating with the WTA Finals in Riyadh in November. Rybakina will need consistent results at every stop to hold off Sabalenka and the rest of a talented field hungry to displace her. The race for the year-end No. 1 ranking starts now, and Rybakina holds the lead. Whether she can extend it across five more weeks of competition is the story of the WTA's fall season.
