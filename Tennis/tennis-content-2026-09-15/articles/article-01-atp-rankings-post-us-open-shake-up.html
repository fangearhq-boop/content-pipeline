ATP Rankings Shake-Up: Shelton Soars to No. 4, Djokovic Falls Out of the Top 10

By Elena Voss

September 15, 2026

The 2026 US Open is over, the courts at Flushing Meadows are quiet, and the ATP rankings have been redrawn. The tournament that saw Alexander Zverev claim his second Grand Slam title and Ben Shelton become the first Black American man in a US Open final since Arthur Ashe in 1972 has left its mark on the rankings in ways that will shape the rest of the season.

The biggest mover up the board is Shelton. The 23-year-old American entered the fortnight ranked No. 9. He exits at a career-high No. 4, a jump of five places that came on the back of wins over No. 2 Holger Rune, quarterfinal conquests over defending champion Carlos Alcaraz, and an electric final against Zverev that lasted three hours and 33 minutes. As the first American man in a major final since Andy Roddick at the 2009 Wimbledon, Shelton's ranking reflects a performance that announced him as a genuine force in men's tennis.

His US Open run also makes him the highest-ranked former college player since 2006. It was Shelton's University of Florida Gators roots, after all, that gave the tennis world one of its most entertaining servers — a left-handed delivery that touches 145 mph and unsettles opponents at every level.

Tiafoe's Return and the American Top-10 Double

Shelton is not alone in the top 10. Frances Tiafoe, who reached the semifinal at Flushing Meadows before falling to Shelton in a historic all-American clash, climbed back into the top 10 at No. 8. It marks the first time two American men have occupied top-10 spots simultaneously in years, and it signals a meaningful shift in the balance of men's tennis away from Europe.

For American tennis fans who grew up watching the sport drift through a generation-long drought since Roddick's 2003 US Open title, this is an inflection point worth savoring.

Zverev Closes on Sinner

At the top of the rankings, the title chase is narrowing. Zverev's US Open title carried him to No. 2 with 9,690 points — just 1,810 points behind world No. 1 Jannik Sinner, who did not reach the final. Perhaps more significantly, Zverev now leads the PIF ATP Live Race to Turin, the season-long competition that determines qualification for November's Nitto ATP Finals. He enters the Asian swing as the form player in men's tennis.

Sinner, who has held the No. 1 ranking for most of 2026, will need to perform well in Shanghai and at the indoor hard-court events in the fall to defend his position. The race is genuinely on.

Djokovic Drops Out of the Top 10

The most consequential fall in the rankings belonged to Novak Djokovic. The 39-year-old Serb, owner of 24 Grand Slam titles and arguably the greatest tennis player in history, lost to Mariano Navone in the first round of the US Open. The defeat cost him 790 ranking points and sent him plummeting from No. 5 to No. 12 — out of the top 10 for the first time since 2018.

The full top-10 post-US Open: Sinner (No. 1), Zverev (No. 2), Carlos Alcaraz (No. 3, 5,560 pts), Shelton (No. 4), with Tiafoe at No. 8. Djokovic sits at No. 12.

What's Next

The attention turns immediately to the Asian swing. The China Open begins September 30 in Beijing, followed by the Shanghai Masters in mid-October. These hard-court events carry significant ranking points and will be decisive in determining who sits atop both the ATP rankings and the Race to Turin when the indoor season begins. Shelton, in particular, will look to build on his US Open breakthrough and turn a career-high ranking into a year-end milestone.
