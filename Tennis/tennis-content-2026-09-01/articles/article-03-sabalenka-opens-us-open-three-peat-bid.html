Sabalenka Opens US Open Three-Peat Bid with Straight-Sets Win Over Osorio

By Marcus Cole

September 1, 2026

US Open 2026

The quest for history began on Monday evening at Arthur Ashe Stadium. Aryna Sabalenka, the two-time defending US Open champion, stepped onto the court and did exactly what the world No. 1 was expected to do: win cleanly, quickly, and without drama.

Sabalenka defeated Camila Osorio (COL) 6-4, 6-4 in her opening match, taking the first step in what would be a remarkable achievement if completed. A third consecutive US Open title would make her the first player to accomplish that since Serena Williams won three in a row from 2012 to 2014.

It was a composed start for a player who has done everything at this tournament except lose in the first round in recent memory.

Clinical and in Control

Osorio, ranked 35th in the world, is a capable clay-court player who can make life difficult for even the best hard-court opponents. On Monday, she had little answer for Sabalenka's baseline power and serving efficiency.

Sabalenka won the majority of her first-serve points and broke Osorio twice in each set. The Colombian had her moments — a brief run of games in the first set when she threatened to level — but Sabalenka responded emphatically each time. The match was over in under 80 minutes.

It was, in many ways, a typical early-round performance from a player who has looked ominously consistent at this tournament over the past two years.

The Path to History

The women's draw at the 2026 US Open carries context that works in Sabalenka's favour. Second seed Elena Rybakina, the most credible threat in her half of the draw, entered the tournament having not fully practiced since suffering an ankle injury in the Cincinnati quarterfinals. Rybakina played her opening match on Day 1, but questions about her fitness remain throughout the fortnight.

Iga Swiatek, seeded seventh after a difficult Grand Slam stretch, is on the opposite side of the draw, with Coco Gauff and Jessica Pegula also presenting significant obstacles. But Sabalenka — healthy, motivated, and with the form of a defending champion — looks the clearest front-runner in the women's field.

To win three consecutive US Open titles would place her among the greatest US Open performers in the Open Era. Serena's three-peat from 2012 to 2014 remains the benchmark. Before that, Steffi Graf won three in a row from 1988 to 1990.

What's Next

Aryna Sabalenka advances to the second round of the 2026 US Open, where she will next play on Wednesday. The women's draw continues with Day 3 on Tuesday featuring Coco Gauff (No. 4 seed) and Australian Open champion Madison Keys in action. The women's final is scheduled for Saturday, September 12.
