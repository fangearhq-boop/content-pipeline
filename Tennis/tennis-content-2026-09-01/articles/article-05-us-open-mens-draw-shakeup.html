Three Top Contenders Gone Before Round 2: The 2026 US Open Men's Draw Is Wide Open

By Elena Voss

September 1, 2026

US Open 2026

In any other year, losing a single top-four seed before the second round would be considered a notable upset. At the 2026 US Open, two days in, the men's draw has already shed three of its biggest names — and one of them never even played a match.

Jannik Sinner, the world No. 1 and the clear pre-tournament favourite, withdrew before the draw was made citing knee tendon inflammation. Novak Djokovic, the No. 3 seed and four-time US Open champion, fell to qualifier Mariano Navone in five sets on Day 1 while visibly ill. And Arthur Fils, the No. 10 seed and freshly crowned Cincinnati Masters champion, was stunned by Stefanos Tsitsipas on Day 2 in four sets.

The result: a men's draw that, with two rounds of first-round matches still left to complete, is as open as it has been at Flushing Meadows in recent memory.

Alcaraz Emerges as the Clear Favourite

Into that vacuum stepped Carlos Alcaraz, the defending champion and No. 2 seed, who returned from a 139-day absence with a 6-4, 6-4, 6-4 win over Roman Safiullin on Monday. It was his first competitive match since April, and it was reassuringly dominant from start to finish.

Alcaraz faces Jaime Faria (Portugal) in the second round and, if he progresses, would be on course for a potential quarterfinal or semifinal meeting with No. 1 seed Alexander Zverev — who opens his own campaign on Tuesday against Sonego. On current form and fitness, Alcaraz looks the player most likely to lift the trophy on September 13.

Who Else Can Challenge?

Beyond Alcaraz and Zverev, the field presents several legitimate alternatives. Frances Tiafoe, who reached the Cincinnati semifinals just two weeks ago, arrived at the US Open with strong hard-court form and the added motivation of playing on home soil. Brandon Nakashima, who holds a career-high ranking and has more tour wins on hard courts in 2026 than almost anyone, is another American who could make a deep run in a draw this disrupted.

Tsitsipas's win over Fils — his first over the Frenchman in six attempts — serves as a reminder that momentum from an unexpected result can carry players well beyond what their form entering a tournament suggested. The Greek is now through to R2 against Harris and is playing with the kind of confidence that has been absent from his game for much of the past 18 months.

The dark-horse list extends further. Ben Shelton, the 2024 US Open finalist, is lurking in the draw. Taylor Fritz (No. 9) opens on Tuesday and has twice reached Grand Slam semifinals. Even players like Daniil Medvedev, if healthy and focused, can dismantle any opponent on this surface.
