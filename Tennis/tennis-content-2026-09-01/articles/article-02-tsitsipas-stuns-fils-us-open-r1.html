Tsitsipas Stuns No. 10 Seed Fils in Second Major Upset of the 2026 US Open

By Elena Voss

September 1, 2026

US Open 2026

Novak Djokovic on Sunday. Arthur Fils on Monday. In the space of 48 hours, the 2026 US Open men's draw has absorbed two of the most stunning first-round exits in recent memory — and it is only the beginning of the second week.

Stefanos Tsitsipas defeated No. 10 seed Fils 4-6, 7-6(3), 6-1, 6-4 on Monday night at Flushing Meadows, ending the reign of the Cincinnati Masters champion and handing the Greek his first win ever against the Frenchman. Tsitsipas had entered the match 0-and-5 against Fils. He left it with 50 winners and a second-round spot against South Africa's Lloyd Harris.

The Comeback That Defined the Match

Fils was the clear favourite entering the contest, and for much of the first two sets he looked it. The French No. 1 took the opening set with relative comfort and built a break lead in the second, leaving Tsitsipas — who had been struggling for consistency in his groundstrokes — facing the very real prospect of a straightforward defeat.

The turning point came in the second-set tiebreak. Tsitsipas, refusing to cede the set, won it 7-3 and carried that momentum immediately into the third set. What followed was the most dominant stretch of the match: Tsitsipas took the third set 6-1 with barely a stumble, and Fils — who had looked so composed just an hour earlier — was now fighting the pace and depth that Tsitsipas was generating from the baseline.

The fourth set followed the same pattern. Tsitsipas broke early and served out the match, finishing with 50 winners — 12 more than Fils — in a performance that bore no resemblance to the player who had entered the match as a heavy underdog.

Context: The Second Shock in Two Days

Tsitsipas's win comes just 24 hours after Djokovic's five-set loss to qualifier Mariano Navone — itself described as the most surprising result at the US Open in years. Together, the two exits have reshaped the men's draw before the first round has even concluded.

Fils had arrived at Flushing Meadows carrying momentum from his Cincinnati Masters title, which he won less than two weeks ago. Cincinnati champions have historically been among the favourites at the US Open. Not this year. The 22-year-old Frenchman, ranked No. 21, will return next week having fallen at the first hurdle at a Grand Slam he had been expected to go deep in.

For Tsitsipas, the win offers something different: a reminder that the 28-year-old Greek remains capable of producing his best tennis when the stage is biggest. He had not made a Grand Slam quarterfinal since the 2025 French Open. This result, at the very least, restores belief that he belongs.

What's Next

Stefanos Tsitsipas advances to the second round of the 2026 US Open and will face Lloyd Harris (RSA). The draw now features a men's field that has already lost three of its most prominent names in Sinner (withdrew), Djokovic (R1), and Fils (R1). Carlos Alcaraz and Alexander Zverev remain the top surviving seeds, with the tournament continuing through the men's final on Sunday, September 13.
