### STORY 1: Alcaraz Returns — Def. Safiullin 6-4, 6-4, 6-4 (Tier 1)

**FB Long-Form — 8:00 AM ET**

Carlos Alcaraz stepped back onto a Grand Slam court Monday night for the first time in 139 days — and he looked like he'd never left. 🎾

The defending US Open champion, returning from a right wrist injury that kept him sidelined since April's Barcelona Open, dispatched Roman Safiullin in straight sets, 6-4, 6-4, 6-4, in a match that lasted just over two hours and showed no signs of rust. He was sharp off both wings, controlled the baseline exchanges, and broke when it mattered.

"I'm just really happy I was able to compete again," Alcaraz said afterward. "I missed the crowd, the energy, the competition."

The win wasn't without some nerves — he admitted he had wondered whether he'd be able to hold up physically through a best-of-five match after so long away. He did, comfortably. Alcaraz now faces Portugal's Jaime Faria in the second round on Wednesday.

With No. 3 seed Djokovic already gone from the draw after his shock Day 1 exit, Alcaraz looks increasingly like the man to beat this fortnight.

### STORY 2: Tsitsipas Stuns No. 10 Fils (Tier 1)

**FB Long-Form — 10:30 AM ET**

Lightning struck twice in the opening rounds of the 2026 US Open. 🎾

Just 24 hours after Djokovic's stunning first-round exit against qualifier Mariano Navone, Stefanos Tsitsipas produced one of his best performances in years to eliminate No. 10 seed Arthur Fils — who entered Flushing Meadows on the back of winning the Cincinnati Masters title.

The match was a rollercoaster. Fils took the first set and built a break lead in the second, but Tsitsipas refused to go away. He took the second set on a tiebreak, then dominated the third and fourth sets almost without contest — finishing with 50 winners to Fils's 38.

What made the result even more remarkable: Tsitsipas had gone 0-and-5 against Fils before Monday night. He had never beaten the Frenchman in any of their previous meetings. He picked the biggest possible stage to end that run.

Tsitsipas now advances to face Lloyd Harris in the second round.

The question now: with Sinner, Djokovic, and Fils already out of the men's draw, who stops Alcaraz from getting back to the final?

### STORY 3: Sabalenka Opens Three-Peat Bid (Tier 2)

**FB Long-Form — 12:30 PM ET**

Aryna Sabalenka has won the US Open in 2024 and 2025. On Monday at Arthur Ashe Stadium, she took her first step toward making it three in a row. 🎾

Sabalenka defeated Colombia's Camila Osorio 6-4, 6-4 in a composed, efficient opening match. It was the kind of controlled performance you'd expect from the world No. 1 — no drama, minimal unforced errors, clinical when it mattered.

If Sabalenka wins the title this year, she'll become the first player to win three consecutive US Open crowns since Serena Williams did it from 2012 to 2014. That's the level of history she's chasing.

There's also an opportunity opening up. Second seed Elena Rybakina — the biggest threat in her half of the draw — entered the tournament still recovering from an ankle injury suffered at Cincinnati and reportedly not having practiced fully before arriving in New York. The path could get clearer with every passing day.
