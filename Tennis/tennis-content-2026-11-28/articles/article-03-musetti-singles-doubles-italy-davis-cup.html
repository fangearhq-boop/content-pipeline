Musetti Wins Singles and Doubles in One Day to Send Italy to Davis Cup Final

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | November 28, 2026

When Italy needed someone to step forward in the most important Davis Cup tie of their year, Lorenzo Musetti did it twice. First in singles, then in the doubles when the whole semifinal was on the line. Friday's 2-1 win over Czech Republic belonged to Musetti in a way that is rare in this team format, and it added another chapter to what is becoming one of the defining performances in Italian tennis history.

The day started with Musetti in singles against Jakub Mensik. It was a matchup that could have gone either way — Mensik is a gifted player who has shown the ability to beat top-20 opponents on the tour this season. Musetti did not let it become a contest. He won 6-3, 6-4 in one hour and 26 minutes. Clean, authoritative, efficient. Italy had the first point.

The Tie Turns

Jiri Lehecka then beat Flavio Cobolli 6-2, 1-6, 6-3 to level the tie. It was a reminder that Czech Republic had not beaten seven consecutive Davis Cup opponents by accident. Lehecka is one of the more dangerous players in this format — fast, aggressive, and experienced enough to close out a close match. Cobolli fought back after losing the first set, but it was not enough.

At 1-1, everything came down to the deciding doubles. Italy captain Filippo Volandri had a choice to make. He went back to Musetti.

Partnering Andrea Vavassori, Musetti won the decisive rubber. The details of exactly how the doubles played out were still filtering through as this article went to publication, but the outcome was clear: Italy won, they are in the final, and Musetti won both his rubbers.

What Musetti's Week Means

It is worth stepping back to appreciate the full picture of what Musetti has done during this tournament. With Jannik Sinner absent since August — and his Australian Open preparation in doubt — the question before the Finals began was whether Italy could find the same level without their best player. The assumption was they would struggle more than in previous years.

Musetti has not let that assumption stand. He has been Italy's most important singles player this week, contributing crucial wins at every stage. His performance against Mensik — controlled, quick, never allowing the Czech to get into a rhythm — showed a maturity that his best wins on the main tour this season also demonstrated. And then he came back for doubles.

At 24 years old, Musetti's best years on tour are still ahead of him. If Sinner returns healthy next year and Italy have Musetti alongside him at the peak of his powers, the prospect for Italian tennis over the next few years is genuinely exciting. But right now, in November 2026, what matters is that Musetti carried Italy to a Davis Cup final again, almost single-handedly.

What's Next

Italy await the winner of Saturday's Spain vs Great Britain semifinal in Sunday's Davis Cup final in Bologna. If Musetti is called upon to play singles in the final — and there is every reason to think he will be — he will walk out having won three consecutive rubbers in this tournament across two ties. Italy are looking for a fourth consecutive title. For Musetti, the week is not over yet.
