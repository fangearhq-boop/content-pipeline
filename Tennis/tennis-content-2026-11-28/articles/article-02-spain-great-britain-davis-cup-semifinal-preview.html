Spain vs Great Britain: Davis Cup Semifinal Preview — Can Fery and GB Reach the Final?

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | November 28, 2026

When the Davis Cup Finals bracket was set in September, most observers would have predicted a Spain vs Italy final in Bologna. Great Britain were an intriguing wildcard, talented enough in certain matchups but without the depth that the top nations typically bring to this format. That assessment has been tested over the last week. On Saturday, in the second semifinal in Bologna, it faces its ultimate examination.

Spain arrive with one enormous unknown hanging over their preparation. Carlos Alcaraz — the world No. 2 or No. 3, depending on the rankings week — sat out singles in the quarterfinal against Austria with a left thigh concern. Rafael Jodar and Daniel Merida handled business 2-0 in his absence. Whether Alcaraz is fit to play singles today is the defining question of this semifinal.

Spain's Depth Has Already Been Tested

The fact that Spain beat Austria without Alcaraz is both reassuring and misleading at the same time. Austria's draw — Sebastian Ofner and Alexander Erler — are competent Davis Cup players, but they are not Cameron Norrie, and they are not whatever version of Arthur Fery shows up on Saturday.

What Spain's win over Austria did demonstrate is that Jodar and Merida can be relied upon in this format. Jodar recovered from losing the opening set before winning the next two to defeat Ofner. Merida completed the tie against Erler. These are players who understand what Davis Cup demands, and they can win at the Finals level.

If Alcaraz plays and is close to full fitness, Spain are significant favorites in this semifinal. The world No. 2 on clay-influenced hard courts, in a Davis Cup format he has shown he can dominate, against a Great Britain team that is riding emotion rather than experience — that matchup tends to go one way.

Great Britain's Case for an Upset

Then again, Great Britain were supposed to lose to Germany too.

Arthur Fery is 20 years old. In the quarterfinal against Germany, he faced Alexander Zverev — the year-end ATP No. 1, one of the best players of his generation, a Grand Slam champion. And Fery beat him. That result reframed this Great Britain team entirely. It was not just an upset; it was a signal that Fery has the temperament and the game to perform at the highest level in this format.

Cameron Norrie has been a steady presence throughout the week. He beat Jan-Lennard Struff in the quarterfinal, playing the kind of consistent, grinding tennis that makes him difficult to beat on a Davis Cup surface. And the doubles partnership of Neal Skupski and Henry Patten — who beat Kevin Krawietz and Tim Puetz 6-2, 7-6(6) in the Germany tie — have established themselves as one of the better doubles combinations in this event.

Great Britain are a coherent team. They have belief. And against an Alcaraz who may not be at full fitness, belief and momentum can be enough.

What's Next

Spain and Great Britain get underway in Bologna on Saturday. The winner advances to Sunday's final against Italy, who beat Czech Republic 2-1 on Friday with Lorenzo Musetti winning singles and the decisive doubles.

If Spain win, it is a rematch of the 2025 Davis Cup final, which Italy won 2-0. If Great Britain win, it would be the most remarkable storyline of a remarkable tournament — a team facing Italy in a Davis Cup final for the first time since 1937. Either way, Sunday's final in Bologna promises to be one of the most compelling days in recent Davis Cup history.
