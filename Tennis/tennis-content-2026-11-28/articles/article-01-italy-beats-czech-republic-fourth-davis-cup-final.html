Italy Beat Czech Republic 2-1 to Reach Fourth Consecutive Davis Cup Final

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | November 28, 2026

Lorenzo Musetti won both his singles rubber and the decisive doubles match to send Italy into Sunday's Davis Cup final for the fourth consecutive year. The defending champions beat Czech Republic 2-1 on Friday in Bologna, extending a dynasty run that has now survived every challenge thrown at it — including the continuing absence of Jannik Sinner.

Musetti opened the tie with a dominant 6-3, 6-4 win over Jakub Mensik, completed in 1 hour and 26 minutes. It was an efficient, authoritative performance that immediately put Italy in control, and it underlined why Musetti has emerged as the team's most important player in this tournament in Sinner's absence.

Czech Republic responded through Jiri Lehecka, who beat Flavio Cobolli 6-2, 1-6, 6-3 in the second rubber to level the tie at 1-1 and send the semifinal to a deciding doubles match. Lehecka had won seven consecutive Davis Cup matches coming into the tie, and he backed that form up when it mattered most.

Musetti Returns to Win the Doubles

With the tie on the line in the third rubber, Italy captain Filippo Volandri turned back to Musetti — this time partnering Andrea Vavassori in the doubles. The pair had been part of Italy's squad throughout the tournament, and Vavassori in particular has become one of the more reliable doubles performers in this format over the past three years.

They won the decisive doubles rubber to put Italy through. For Musetti, it was an extraordinary day of tennis — singles in the afternoon, doubles in the evening, both crucial, both won. It is the kind of contribution that gets remembered long after the tournament is over, and it cemented his status as the central figure in Italy's campaign this week.

Czech Republic's campaign ended with their first loss of the tournament. They had come into the Finals in impressive form, having beaten Canada 2-0 in the quarterfinals with Lehecka defeating Felix Auger-Aliassime and Tomas Machac taking care of Liam Draxl. But Italy, even without Sinner, proved a step too far.

Italy's Remarkable Dynasty

It is easy to reach for superlatives when describing what Italy have done in Davis Cup over the last four years. Three consecutive titles. Now a fourth consecutive final appearance. And they have done it this year entirely without the player who has been the best in the world for much of 2025 and 2026.

Sinner has been absent from the squad since August following knee surgery that was later complicated by tonsil surgery. His recovery timeline remains uncertain ahead of the Australian Open. For Italy, managing without their most dominant player while still reaching Davis Cup finals says everything about the depth and quality of this group.

Cobolli, Musetti, Berrettini, Vavassori — these are players who have won at the highest level on tour. Under Volandri, they have developed a cohesion and a collective belief in this format that is difficult to replicate. The home advantage in Bologna has helped, but it does not fully explain what Italy have achieved.

What's Next

Italy now wait to find out who they face in Sunday's final. The second semifinal — Spain vs Great Britain — takes place on Saturday in Bologna, and the winner advances to meet Italy for the title. Italy beat Spain 2-0 in last year's final. Great Britain have not been in a Davis Cup final since 1937.

The final is scheduled for Sunday, November 29, in Bologna. If Italy win, they will have won four consecutive Davis Cup titles — a remarkable achievement for a squad that has had to reinvent itself in various forms across four years. For now, they wait. They have earned the right to.
