Great Britain's Davis Cup Journey: From Fery's Zverev Upset to the Spain Semifinal

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | November 28, 2026

When Great Britain qualified for the 2026 Davis Cup Finals by beating Ecuador 3-0, the expectation was that they would be competitive but ultimately short of the final few. They had Cameron Norrie, a proven Davis Cup performer. They had the experienced doubles pair of Neal Skupski and Henry Patten. And they had Arthur Fery, a 20-year-old wildcard who had made promising strides on the tour but had not yet faced anything close to what waited for him in Bologna.

What followed across the first week of the Finals was a demonstration that form, expectations, and rankings tell only part of the story when Davis Cup reduces tennis to its rawest, most competitive form.

The Fery-Zverev Moment

Everything changed on Thursday. Great Britain faced Germany in the quarterfinal, and Germany's player of the week was supposed to be Alexander Zverev — the year-end ATP No. 1, the best player in the world in 2026 by the most important measure. Zverev had come into the Finals as the heaviest favorite for individual rubber wins, the kind of player who is simply expected to win Davis Cup matches.

Arthur Fery beat him.

The details of exactly how will be analyzed and replayed for years in British tennis circles. Fery is 20 years old. This was his Davis Cup Finals debut. He beat the year-end world No. 1 in straight sets — not in a panic, not through Zverev playing badly, but through his own quality. It was not an upset that happened to him. He made it happen.

Cameron Norrie had already beaten Jan-Lennard Struff in the opening singles rubber to give Great Britain the first point. When Fery followed that up against Zverev, the tie was Great Britain's before the doubles even began. Neal Skupski and Henry Patten went out and beat Kevin Krawietz and Tim Puetz 6-2, 7-6(6) in the doubles to put the official 2-1 on the scoreboard, but the tie was decided when Fery walked off court having beaten the best player in the world.

Saturday: Spain Stands Between GB and History

Saturday's semifinal against Spain is a different challenge. Spain are deeper than Germany, and they may have Carlos Alcaraz back in the lineup — he sat out singles against Austria with a thigh concern but could return for Saturday. Great Britain's route to victory runs through managing Alcaraz, or hoping his fitness is not what it was before the thigh issue emerged.

What Great Britain have on their side is momentum of a kind that is hard to quantify but impossible to ignore. This is a team that knows they can beat anyone in the draw. Fery has beaten the No. 1. Norrie has been consistent all week. Skupski and Patten in doubles are a pair that any team would be happy to have on their side in a deciding rubber.

The last time Great Britain were in a Davis Cup final was 1937. The last time they reached a semifinal in the modern Finals era was years ago. What this team has done in Bologna is already extraordinary regardless of Saturday's result. But sport doesn't reward what has already happened — it rewards what is about to happen.

What's Next

Great Britain vs Spain gets underway on Saturday in Bologna. The winner advances to Sunday's Davis Cup final against Italy, the three-time defending champions who beat Czech Republic 2-1 on Friday with Lorenzo Musetti winning singles and the decisive doubles. For Great Britain, the question is simple: can they go one win further and reach a final for the first time since 1937? The answer depends, in part, on whether a 20-year-old from Surrey is ready to do it again.
