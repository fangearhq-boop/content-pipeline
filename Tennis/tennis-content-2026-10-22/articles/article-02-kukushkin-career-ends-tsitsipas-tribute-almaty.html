Kukushkin's Career Ends at Almaty — Tsitsipas Wins, Pays Tribute on Court

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | October 22, 2026

Mikhail Kukushkin's 20-year professional career ended on Wednesday in Almaty — the city and country he called home — when No. 7 seed Stefanos Tsitsipas beat him 6-1, 6-2 in the second round of the Almaty Open. The match lasted just over an hour. After the final point, Tsitsipas did not go to his bag or his chair. He walked over to Kukushkin instead.

Tsitsipas asked Kukushkin to sign his shirt. He described the experience afterward as a surreal feeling — a strange, eerie sense of playing someone whose final ATP Tour match it was, knowing that every rally was shortening a career that had begun two decades earlier.

A Career That Lasted Long Enough to Matter

Kukushkin turned professional in 2006, at 18 years old. He reached a career-high ranking of No. 39 in February 2019 — a number that reflects just how close he came to the upper tier of professional tennis without ever quite breaking through to it permanently. He won one ATP title, in St. Petersburg in 2010. He played at Grand Slams, competed against the best players in the world at his peak, and kept competing long after most players with similar trajectories had stepped away.

Earlier this week, he beat Stefanos Sakellaridis 7-6(4), 7-6(3) in the first round — his first ATP win since 2024, delivered in two tiebreaks. He described that match afterward as a summary of his whole career: never easy, never straightforward, fought for every point. He saved match points. He found a way. And then he came back on Wednesday to face a seeded player, which is exactly what his week at Almaty was always going to give him in the second round.

The Right Setting for a Final Act

There is something appropriate about Kukushkin ending his career in Kazakhstan, where he was born and where the crowd knew his name and his story. The Almaty Open is a small ATP 250 tournament, not a Grand Slam, not a major. But for a player who spent most of his career grinding through tournaments at every level, a home crowd farewell is more fitting than a first-round exit at a Masters 1000 would have been.

Tsitsipas, currently ranked in the world's top 10, was generous in his remarks afterward. The sign on the shirt was not a formality. It was acknowledgment from one professional to another — a recognition that 20 years at this level is not nothing, regardless of where the ranking peaked.

What's Next

For Tsitsipas, the Almaty quarterfinals are Thursday. He enters as the top remaining seed and the heavy favorite to advance to the semifinals and beyond. A first ATP title of 2026 would be a meaningful result going into the year's final stretch.

Kukushkin's career, by any measure, is complete. He leaves the tour having competed at the highest level for two full decades, with one title, a career-high ranking in the top 40, and a final match played in front of a home crowd that cheered every point. The tennis record books will show 6-1, 6-2. The real story is everything that came before it.
