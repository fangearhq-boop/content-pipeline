### STORY 2: Kukushkin Career Ends

**Long-Form Post (10:30 AM ET)**
Mikhail Kukushkin's tennis career is over — and it ended the way most of his career went: in a close, grinding, fighting match against a better opponent, on familiar ground in Kazakhstan.

Tsitsipas beat Kukushkin 6-1, 6-2 in the second round at Almaty on Wednesday, in what became the final match of Kukushkin's 20-year professional career. After the match, Tsitsipas stayed on court to pay tribute — describing the experience as a surreal feeling, playing someone whose career was ending with every rally. He asked Kukushkin to sign a shirt.

Earlier in the week, Kukushkin had beaten Stefanos Sakellaridis to win his first ATP match since 2024. He announced Almaty would be his final tournament. He got one farewell win in Kazakhstan, in front of a home crowd, and a generous send-off from one of the tour's best players.

Twenty years is a long time to compete at the highest level. Kukushkin reached a career-high ranking of No. 39 in February 2019, won his only ATP title in St. Petersburg in 2010, and was still grinding as a wildcard entry at age 38. He won his last match in tiebreaks — two of them. Of course he did.

### STORY 5: ATP Race to Turin

**Long-Form Post (6:00 PM ET)**
Four days. That's how long it is until Vienna and Basel both open their draws — and the ATP Race to Turin enters its most decisive stretch of the season.

Three players are already confirmed for the ATP Finals in Turin (November 15–22): Alexander Zverev, Carlos Alcaraz, and Ben Shelton. Five spots remain. And the list of players still in contention reads like a who's who of the ATP's elite: Flavio Cobolli, Frances Tiafoe, Arthur Fils, Rafael Jodar, Daniil Medvedev, Alex de Minaur, and Novak Djokovic are all fighting for places 4 through 8.

Vienna and Basel run from October 26 to November 1. Then Paris Masters runs from October 31 to November 8. That's three events — two of them ATP 500s, one a Masters 1000 — to decide the fate of five spots. The margins are tight, and a semifinal run in any of these events could swing the standings dramatically.

Players like Fils (currently projected in the top 8) have the most to gain from a title at Vienna or Basel. Djokovic, coming in as a wild card at Basel, will be motivated by more than just ranking points.

Which player do you think finishes 8th in the Race to Turin when all is said and done?
