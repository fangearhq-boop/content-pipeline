Basel Final: Lehecka vs. Vacherot — A Surprise Sunday in Switzerland

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap

November 1, 2026

When the Swiss Indoors draw was released last week, the expected finalists were somewhere in the top half of the bracket. Ben Shelton, the No. 3 seed, was there. Taylor Fritz was seeded sixth. Holger Rune was a name. Novak Djokovic entered as a wild card with a week to build momentum before Paris.

None of them are playing Sunday. Instead, the Basel final is Jiří Lehečká against Valentin Vacherot — and if you did not follow every match of the draw, you are not alone in finding that surprising.

How Vacherot Got Here

Valentin Vacherot is ranked No. 31 on the ATP Tour. He represents Monaco, a country with a long relationship with tennis but not with ATP singles trophies. He has one career title to his name — the Shanghai Masters in 2025, which remains one of the more quietly impressive wins of that season.

At Basel, he did not look like a wildcard story. He beat Holger Rune 6-3, 3-6, 6-3 in the quarterfinals and followed it by dismantling sixth seed Taylor Fritz 7-6(5), 6-3 in the semifinals. Fritz had been one of the more dangerous players in the draw, a potential deep run in his final tournament before Paris. Vacherot ended that possibility efficiently, and by doing so, became the first player from Monaco to reach the Swiss Indoors final.

That historical footnote matters to the people of Monaco. The principality has an ATP Masters 1000 event at Monte-Carlo each spring, but reaching a final in Basel — the city most associated with Roger Federer's legacy — as a representative of Monaco carries its own symbolic weight.

Lehečká's Comeback Week

Jiří Lehečká, seeded eighth and ranked No. 21 in the ATP, entered as a more recognized presence in the draw — but his path was not clean. In the quarterfinals, he beat Jérôme Kym, the Swiss wild card whose stunning upset of Ben Shelton in the previous round had briefly made him the story of the week. Then, in the semifinal against Jan-Lennard Struff, Lehečká dropped the first set and fell behind to a match point before turning the match around: 3-6, 7-5, 6-4.

That kind of win — saving match point, winning the third set — tends to stay with a player heading into a final. The belief that you can come back from a moment of near-elimination is a useful thing to carry into a Sunday title match.

Lehečká also carries a relevant piece of head-to-head history into the final. The two have played twice before, splitting the results: Lehečká won their meeting in Tokyo earlier this year; Vacherot won at Paris last year. A 1-1 head-to-head is as neutral a starting point as exists in tennis.

The Paris Subplot

There is one additional layer of context for Sunday's match in Basel, and it involves where both players go next. The Rolex Paris Masters begins Monday, November 2. The draw is already public. And Valentin Vacherot is slated to play Novak Djokovic in the first round.

Djokovic has never beaten Vacherot on the ATP Tour. That matchup is already one of the most-watched first-round contests on the Paris main draw. If Vacherot wins today in Basel — his second ATP title, achieved on a Sunday — he will fly to Paris and face Djokovic in a first-round contest on Monday or Tuesday. That is not an ideal recovery window.

It is also not Vacherot's problem to solve right now. One match at a time, and today's match is in Basel.

A Sunday for Tennis

The Swiss Indoors has a history of delivering in its final rounds. The late-October, early-November indoor swing traditionally produces tight, high-quality tennis. Basel's crowd, still processing the retirement of their greatest champion in Wawrinka this week, showed up all week. Sunday's final, regardless of the outcome, continues that tradition.

Lehečká is the slight favorite based on ranking and recent form. Vacherot has beaten higher-ranked players this week and has the experience of having previously won a title at this level. Both players know what a trophy feels like, or in Vacherot's case, what it felt like once.

By Sunday afternoon, one of them will have added another one.
