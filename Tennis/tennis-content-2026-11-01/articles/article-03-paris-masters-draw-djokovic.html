Paris Masters Draw: Djokovic Opens Against Vacherot — and Needs a Semifinal to Reach Turin

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap

November 1, 2026

The draw for the 2026 Rolex Paris Masters was released Friday evening, and within minutes the tennis world had focused on one specific line: Novak Djokovic, seeded 10th, opening against Valentin Vacherot.

Vacherot beat Djokovic at Shanghai last year. Djokovic has never beaten Vacherot in their ATP head-to-head. And Vacherot, as of Sunday, is playing a final at the Swiss Indoors in Basel — meaning that if he wins today's title, he will face Djokovic in Paris with essentially no recovery window between tournaments.

The Paris Masters begins Monday, November 2. The top eight seeds receive first-round byes and begin play Tuesday or Wednesday. Djokovic, as the No. 10 seed, plays in the first round — Monday or Tuesday, according to tournament director Gilles Moretton, who confirmed the order of play will be released Sunday evening.

The Draw in Full

Alexander Zverev is the No. 1 seed, Carlos Alcaraz No. 2, Ben Shelton No. 3, and Daniil Medvedev No. 4. All four are already qualified for the ATP Finals in Turin (November 15–22). The Paris Masters is the final Masters 1000 event of 2026, offering 1,000 Race to Turin points to the champion and significant points at every round.

Based on the draw, Djokovic's projected path looks like this: Vacherot in the first round, Luciano Darderi or a qualifier in the second round, Alex de Minaur in the third round, and Zverev in the quarterfinals. To qualify for Turin, he needs to reach the semifinals. That means winning four consecutive matches, including against de Minaur and Zverev.

That is not an impossible task. Djokovic has won Paris multiple times across his career. He performed at a high level at Wimbledon earlier this year. But the draw is not kind, and every player between Djokovic and the semifinals is capable of ending his run.

The Turin Stakes

The Nitto ATP Finals are November 15–22 at the Inalpi Arena in Turin. Eight players qualify based on Race points accumulated during 2026. Four have already locked in: Zverev, Alcaraz, Shelton, and Medvedev. Four spots remain. The candidates include Djokovic, de Minaur, Fritz, Arthur Fils (health permitting), and Frances Tiafoe.

Djokovic's position in the Race standings going into Paris is outside the top eight. To realistically enter the top eight and hold that position, he likely needs to reach at minimum the semifinals and possibly the final. A quarterfinal exit, even against a top-four seed, may not be enough depending on how other players perform.

Tournament director Pioline said last week that this Paris Masters may be Djokovic's last or second-to-last appearance at the event. That framing — whether accurate or not — adds a resonance to his matches in Paris that extends beyond tournament standings. This is a player who has shaped the culture of this event across two decades, and his presence in the draw this week carries its own weight regardless of the Race mathematics.

The Broader Draw

Beyond Djokovic, the draw delivers several compelling early-round matchups. Alcaraz, who may arrive in Paris having just won Vienna on Sunday, is seeded second and has path toward the final that does not meet Zverev until the championship match. Shelton, who qualified for Turin earlier in the season, enters as a legitimate title threat on indoor hard courts where his serve is maximally effective.

Medvedev, the defending champion at Paris-Bercy in several past seasons, is seeded fourth and will be looking to add points to a season that has delivered a Turin qualification but fewer titles than his peak years. He is always a threat on this surface — the indoor hard courts at La Défense Arena suit his game as well as any venue on tour.

Qualifying concludes Sunday. Main draw play begins Monday, November 2. The Paris Masters ends November 8 — one week before the ATP Finals in Turin begin. What happens this week in Paris will determine whether the year-end championships feature Djokovic for the first time in 2026, or whether the 23-time Grand Slam champion ends his season before the season's final chapter.
