ATP Race to Turin: Four Spots Left, De Minaur Leads by One Point as Paris Begins

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap

November 1, 2026

With the final Masters 1000 event of 2026 beginning Monday in Paris, the race to fill the last four spots at the Nitto ATP Finals has reached its most compressed and consequential moment. Four players have already qualified — Alexander Zverev, Carlos Alcaraz, Ben Shelton, and Daniil Medvedev — and four spots remain. Depending on how Paris unfolds over the next week, the group of contenders will be sorted one way or another by November 8.

The current snapshot is tight. Alex de Minaur holds ninth place in the PIF ATP Live Race to Turin with 3,210 points — exactly one point ahead of Rafael Jodar in eighth. That margin is not a typo. It is the livelihoods of the contenders below him expressed in a single point of separation.

De Minaur's Sunday Opportunity

Before Paris begins, there is the matter of today's Vienna final. De Minaur faces Carlos Alcaraz for the title at the Erste Bank Open — a match that, from a Race to Turin standpoint, could resolve some of the urgency he carries into next week. A title today would add significantly to his Race total and create breathing room over Jodar and the others chasing him.

A loss today keeps him at 3,210 points — ninth place by a single point — and sends him to Paris needing a solid run. The difference between arriving in Paris as the player who won Vienna and arriving as the player who came close is not only psychological. It is 170+ Race points that could decide whether he qualifies without playing a semifinal in Paris or needs deep results to hold off competitors below him.

Fritz Fading, Fils Uncertain

Taylor Fritz had entered the Basel week as one of the more realistic candidates for one of the final Turin spots. He reached the semifinal at the Swiss Indoors before losing to Valentin Vacherot 7-6(5), 6-3. The loss itself is not disqualifying, but the Tennis365 headline described his Turin hopes as "fading," and the math supports that characterization. Fritz will need results in Paris to stay in the conversation.

Arthur Fils, seeded fifth or thereabouts in Paris, remains an important variable — but he retired from his Basel quarterfinal against Fritz with lower back discomfort. His status for Paris is uncertain. If Fils cannot compete at full capacity, or withdraws, that reshuffles the contender picture both in terms of Race points and draw dynamics.

Frances Tiafoe is also in contention and will be in the Paris main draw. Like Fritz, he needs a deep run in Paris to make a realistic case for qualification.

Djokovic's All-or-Nothing Paris

Novak Djokovic enters Paris in the most unambiguous situation of any remaining contender. He needs to reach the semifinals. His draw — opening against Vacherot, then Darderi or a qualifier, then de Minaur, then Zverev — is not what anyone would describe as soft. Every match is competitive; the players standing between him and a semifinal are all capable of winning.

Djokovic is 39 years old and has been managing his schedule carefully in 2026. He took wild card entries at Vienna and Basel, built some match wins, and arrives in Paris carrying that momentum — but also the loss to Struff in Basel as a reminder that the results can turn quickly. Paris has historically been a strong event for him. The draw has not been historically favorable.

The ATP Finals in Turin begin November 15. By November 8, when Paris concludes, the final roster of eight qualifiers will be known. The math still gives Djokovic a path. It is narrower than anyone would have predicted at the start of the indoor autumn swing, and it runs directly through some of the best players in the world.

The Next Seven Days

Paris is worth 1,000 points to the champion, 600 to the finalist, 360 to each semifinalist. Those numbers matter enormously when the gaps between contenders are in the single or low double digits. A player who enters Paris 200 points outside the qualification line can vault inside it with a run to the quarterfinals.

For the first time all season, every remaining match this year has a clear and immediate consequence for which eight players make it to Turin. That is what the final week of a tennis season looks like when the Race has stayed genuinely open this deep. The draws are set. The points are on the table. The week begins Monday.
