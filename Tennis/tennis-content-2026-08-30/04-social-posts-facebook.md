### STORY 1: Buse Wins Winston-Salem

**FB Long-Form — 9:00 AM ET**

IGNACIO BUSE IS THE 2026 WINSTON-SALEM CHAMPION 🎾

On a warm Saturday afternoon in North Carolina, 22-year-old Ignacio Buse made history. The Peruvian — ranked World No. 36 — dismantled Great Britain's Arthur Fery 6-3, 6-2 to win the Winston-Salem Open, claiming his second ATP Tour title of 2026 and becoming the youngest champion in the tournament's history.

It was a dominant performance from a player who looked completely at home on the biggest stage. Buse never let Fery settle, using his baseline consistency and sharp crosscourt groundstrokes to keep the British serve-and-volleyer off balance throughout. After dropping his serve in the first set, Buse broke back immediately and never looked back.

The victory carries enormous historical weight for Peruvian tennis. Buse is the first Peruvian player to win an ATP Tour final since 2004 — a drought of over two decades. Back home in Lima, the reaction has been electric.

Now he gets no rest. The 2026 US Open main draw begins TODAY in Flushing Meadows, and Buse will be among those stepping into the biggest stage in hard-court tennis with the wind firmly at his back. His first ATP title came on clay in Hamburg in May. Winston-Salem proves he can win on hard courts too.

What a summer for Nacho Buse.

### STORY 4: Rybakina Enters US Open Despite Ankle Uncertainty

**FB Long-Form — 1:00 PM ET**

As of three days ago, Elena Rybakina had not set foot on a practice court.

The World No. 2 and No. 2 seed at the 2026 US Open has been nursing a left ankle injury since retiring from her Cincinnati quarterfinal against Iga Swiatek on August 21. She told reporters on August 27: "I haven't been able to practice yet... I want to make sure that everything is okay."

But today, Rybakina stepped into the draw.

The Kazakh withdrew from the mixed doubles event with Taylor Fritz — a sign that she is managing her workload carefully — but chose to enter the first round of singles, facing wild card Thea Frodin. It's a calculated gamble. Rybakina is the two-time Grand Slam champion (2022 Wimbledon) who could claim the world No. 1 ranking if she were to win this tournament.

The stakes for her showing up are enormous — and so is the risk of setting back her recovery.

Today's match against Frodin will tell us a great deal about what's left in Rybakina's ankle and what she can realistically achieve over the next two weeks.

### STORY 5: Alcaraz Returns Monday / Eala Opens

**FB Long-Form — 6:00 PM ET**

Two must-watch storylines are coming Monday at the 2026 US Open — and they could not be more different.

First: Carlos Alcaraz returns to the court.

The No. 2 seed from Spain hasn't played a singles match since April. A right-wrist tenosynovitis injury forced him to skip the French Open, Wimbledon, the Canadian Open, and the Cincinnati Masters. He enters the US Open with zero hard-court match play in 2026 and enormous questions about his readiness. His first-round opponent is Roman Safiullin (RUS). How Alcaraz moves and how his wrist holds up will be the most scrutinized tennis on Day 2.

Then there's Alex Eala.

The 22-year-old from the Philippines is the No. 17 seed at this US Open — the highest Grand Slam seeding ever by a Filipino player. She opens against American qualifier Mary Stoiana, who earned her Grand Slam main draw debut after beating China's Yue Yuan 6-3, 3-6, 6-4 in qualifying. It's a matchup between two players experiencing a first — and one that Philippine tennis fans have been waiting years to see.

Monday at Flushing Meadows is going to be appointment viewing.

Who are you more excited to watch — Alcaraz or Eala?
