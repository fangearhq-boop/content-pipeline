US Open 2026 Day 1 — Djokovic Chases 25, Rybakina Enters Despite Injury, Williams Returns

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | August 30, 2026

The year's final Grand Slam is underway. The 2026 US Open opened both its men's and women's singles draws on Sunday at USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center in Flushing Meadows, New York, with Day 1 delivering a schedule full of marquee names, compelling storylines, and $108 million in total prize money at stake.

The Day Session: Pegula, Rybakina, Medvedev Open Early

Arthur Ashe Stadium opened its gates for the day session at 11 AM ET, with No. 3 seed Jessica Pegula (USA) taking on Elena-Gabriela Ruse (ROU) in the first match on the showpiece court. Pegula, one of the most consistent players on the women's tour and a consistent US Open threat, entered the match as a heavy favorite in front of what is always among the most passionate home crowds in tennis.

Elsewhere in the day session, Daniil Medvedev — the 2021 US Open men's champion — stepped back onto the court where he famously outlasted Novak Djokovic in the final five years ago. The Russian, seeded No. 7, faces Frenchman Hugo Gaston in what is expected to be a warm-up exercise before his tougher tests in later rounds.

The most anticipated day session match belongs to No. 2 seed Elena Rybakina, whose participation at this tournament has been the defining question of the opening week. The Kazakhstani, who retired from her Cincinnati quarterfinal on August 21 with a left ankle injury, told reporters as recently as August 27 that she had not been able to practice. She withdrew from the mixed doubles event. But she entered the singles draw and faces wild card Thea Frodin in a match that will be watched as much for medical information as for tennis.

The Night Session: Djokovic's Historic Chase Begins

The headline act of Day 1 is reserved for the evening. At 7 PM ET on Arthur Ashe, four-time US Open champion Novak Djokovic begins his 2026 campaign against Argentina's Mariano Navone — and with it, his pursuit of a 25th Grand Slam singles title.

Djokovic arrives as the No. 4 seed in the men's draw, seeded behind Alexander Zverev (No. 1), Carlos Alcaraz (No. 2), and Felix Auger-Aliassime (No. 3). He has spent much of 2026 in an unusual position — no longer the dominant force he was at his peak, but still among the handful of players capable of winning majors when the moment calls for it. A 25th Slam would stand alone in history.

Also in Sunday night's session on Arthur Ashe: 46-year-old Venus Williams takes on Sofia Kenin. Williams, playing on a wild card, entered with a 14-match losing streak but with the full force of the US Open crowd behind her.

The Bigger Picture

The 2026 US Open's opening day carries the weight of several unresolved narratives. Rybakina's health will determine whether the WTA No. 1 ranking race remains a true three-way fight among her, Sabalenka, and the rest of the field. Carlos Alcaraz — not on court until Monday — arrives having not played a singles match since April and will be scrutinized from the moment he steps onto the court against Roman Safiullin. Aryna Sabalenka, the two-time defending champion, is bidding to be the first women's US Open three-peat winner since Serena Williams (2012-14).

Two weeks of tennis, a full field, and more questions than answers. That is Day 1 at the 2026 US Open. Everything begins now.

What's Next

Day 2 on Monday, August 31 brings two of the tournament's highest-profile first-round matches. No. 2 seed Carlos Alcaraz faces Roman Safiullin in his first singles match since April. And No. 17 seed Alex Eala, making history as the highest-seeded Filipino player at a Grand Slam, opens against American qualifier Mary Stoiana. Monday at Flushing Meadows is appointment viewing.
