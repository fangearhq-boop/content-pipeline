Ignacio Buse Wins Winston-Salem Open — Youngest Champion, Historic Win for Peru

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | August 30, 2026

Ignacio Buse walked off the court in Winston-Salem on Saturday with his second ATP Tour title of 2026 and a piece of tournament history. The 22-year-old Peruvian defeated Britain's Arthur Fery 6-3, 6-2 in the Winston-Salem Open final, becoming the youngest player ever to lift the trophy at the ATP 250 event and the first Peruvian player to win an ATP Tour final since 2004.

The scoreline told a clean story. Buse broke Fery's serve in the first set, withstood a brief push-back, and then closed out the second set with clinical authority. From beginning to end, the match lasted just over an hour — a dominant performance at the start of a US Open week.

A Historically Young Champion

At 22 years and five months, Buse now owns a record that Hubert Hurkacz held for seven years. The Polish star had been Winston-Salem's youngest champion since winning the title in 2019, when he was one month older than Buse is now. That record falls to a player from a country where ATP Tour titles do not come often.

The victory is Buse's second ATP title of 2026. His first came in May at the Hamburg Open — an ATP 500 on clay — marking one of the most promising young seasons on the tour. Winston-Salem is his first title on hard courts, the surface that matters most with the US Open starting the same day.

Buse's Path to the Title

Buse arrived at the final having knocked out several opponents with a combination of relentless baseline groundstrokes and mental toughness. In Saturday's semifinal, he defeated French veteran Benjamin Bonzi 6-2, 4-6, 6-1 — a performance that showed his ability to recover after dropping a set and close with conviction.

Against Fery, Buse found another gear entirely. The British player had reached the final after advancing when James Duckworth retired through injury in the semifinals, and while Fery's serve-and-volley approach is one of the most distinctive on the tour, Buse neutralized it by keeping the ball deep and giving Fery little time to set up at the net.

Fery had announced himself to the world earlier this summer with a run to the Wimbledon semifinals, but Saturday was Buse's day.

What It Means for Peru

The last Peruvian player to reach an ATP Tour final before Buse was Luis Horna, in 2004 — a gap of 22 years. Buse has spoken openly about the pride he takes in representing his country on the international stage. After the Winston-Salem victory, he told reporters: "Que se escuche en todo el mundo, Perú es clave" — roughly translated as "Let the world hear it: Peru is important."

From a ranking of No. 135 in the world just a year ago, Buse has transformed himself into a legitimate force on the ATP Tour. Two titles, two very different surfaces, one calendar year. The trajectory is remarkable.

What's Next

Buse heads directly to Flushing Meadows, where the 2026 US Open main draw began on Sunday. He enters as one of the hottest players in the field — and one of the few arriving at the final Grand Slam of the season having won a title in the week prior. His US Open first-round draw will be closely watched. For a player who has now proven he can win on hard courts, the question is how deep he can go at the biggest stage of the season.
