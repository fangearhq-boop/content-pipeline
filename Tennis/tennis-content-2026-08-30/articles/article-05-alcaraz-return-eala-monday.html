Day 2 Preview: Alcaraz's Return and Eala's Debut Are the Must-Watch Matches of Monday

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | August 30, 2026

Day 1 at the 2026 US Open belongs to Djokovic, Rybakina, and the Williams sisters. Day 2 belongs to two players stepping onto a Grand Slam court for very different reasons — one returning from an injury that kept him away for four months, the other making history as the first player from her country to be seeded at a major.

On Monday, August 31, Carlos Alcaraz and Alex Eala will share the spotlight at Flushing Meadows. Both matches are first rounds. Both carry weight far beyond any single point.

Alcaraz: A Return Four Months in the Making

The last time Carlos Alcaraz played a singles match, it was April in Barcelona. He won that match, but his right wrist had been bothering him for weeks. Shortly after Barcelona, he was diagnosed with tenosynovitis — an inflammation of the tendon sheaths in the wrist — and made the decision to shut down his season.

What followed was one of the most unusual absences in recent Grand Slam history. Alcaraz, the two-time former US Open champion and arguably the most gifted player of his generation, sat out the French Open, Wimbledon, the Canadian Open, and the Cincinnati Masters. While Alexander Zverev claimed the Roland Garros title and Wimbledon final, and while the hard-court summer unfolded without him, Alcaraz watched, recovered, and waited.

Now he is at the US Open, seeded No. 2, with zero match play on any hard court in 2026 and a first-round opponent in Roman Safiullin. The Russian is a capable player on hard courts — not a pushover — and how Alcaraz manages that match will reveal whether four months of rest has restored him or whether he needs match play to find his rhythm.

Watch his footwork. Watch how he moves on wide forehands. Watch the wrist on flat drives. The body language in the early service games will say more than any quote from the locker room ever could.

Eala: The Weight of History, the Joy of Tennis

Alex Eala was already the highest-seeded Filipino player in Grand Slam history before she ever walked onto the court at Flushing Meadows this week. The No. 17 seed opens against American qualifier Mary Stoiana — a player who earned her first-ever Grand Slam main draw spot with a grinding 6-3, 3-6, 6-4 qualifying win over China's Yue Yuan.

That is the matchup: the most historically significant player from the Philippines in her sport's biggest tournament, against a qualifier fighting for a breakthrough of her own. Two players who have earned their way here through hard work, long seasons, and results that no one can take from them.

Eala has had a remarkable 2026 on hard courts. Her 22-9 record on the surface coming into the US Open includes a final appearance at the DC Open, where she fell to Jessica Pegula 6-4, 6-3. She has won at least one match at every Grand Slam this year. Now she arrives as a seed — the first time her name appears in the tournament's official bracket section, not the qualifiers, not the wild cards.

For Filipino tennis fans, Monday morning at the US Open is appointment television regardless of the time zone difference.

What's Next

Both Alcaraz and Eala face first-round opponents who are winnable for healthy versions of each player. The results will be less interesting than the performances. For Alcaraz, a clean win with signs of wrist comfort would be the best-case scenario heading into a second round that promises to be more demanding. For Eala, a focused, composed win would set the tone for what could be a deep tournament run — her draw quarter is headed by No. 4 seed Coco Gauff, a potential fourth-round meeting. Monday at Flushing Meadows delivers answers to questions the tennis world has been asking for months.
