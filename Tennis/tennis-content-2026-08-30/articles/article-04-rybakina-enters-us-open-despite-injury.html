Rybakina Steps Onto the US Open Court, Ankle Injury Be Damned

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | August 30, 2026

As recently as three days ago, Elena Rybakina had not been able to practice. The World No. 2, nursing a left ankle injury that had forced her to retire from the Cincinnati quarterfinals on August 21, told reporters she was focused entirely on recovery and was not ready to confirm her presence at the 2026 US Open.

Then she entered the draw anyway.

Rybakina took the court on Sunday for her first-round match against American wild card Thea Frodin. It is the most carefully watched match of the US Open's opening day — not because Frodin is a significant opponent, but because every Rybakina step, pivot, and push-off will be analyzed for what it tells us about how the tournament's No. 2 seed can actually perform over the next two weeks.

The Injury Timeline

The ankle trouble began at the Western & Southern Open in Cincinnati. Rybakina had been managing the injury across back-to-back weeks in Toronto and Cincinnati when it buckled under the pressure of a quarterfinal against Iga Swiatek. Down 1-4 in the first set, she chose not to push further and retired from the match.

In the days that followed, she underwent MRI imaging and worked with the medical team traveling with the Kazakh tennis federation. She withdrew from the US Open mixed doubles event — the clearest signal that she was managing a load-sensitive situation. The mixed doubles withdrawal was announced August 24, leaving her partnership with Taylor Fritz for another year.

But singles was a different calculation. The stakes are simply too high to withdraw if there is any realistic chance of competing.

The Stakes: No. 1 Ranking, Three-Peat Prevention, Her Own Legacy

Rybakina is the No. 2 seed at this US Open. She is the two-time Grand Slam champion who won Wimbledon in 2022. She is also in the middle of a WTA No. 1 ranking race that has run across the entire 2026 season. Winning the US Open would almost certainly vault her to the top spot — a milestone she has been chasing for years.

On the other side of the draw, Aryna Sabalenka is chasing a third consecutive US Open title. No women's player has won three straight US Opens since Serena Williams did it from 2012 to 2014. The rivalry between Sabalenka and Rybakina is the defining women's tennis story of the current era, and it has the potential to define this tournament.

That is what Rybakina is weighing against her ankle recovery. That is why she entered the draw.

Who Is Thea Frodin?

Thea Frodin is an American wild card and one of the newer names in women's professional tennis. She does not represent a serious threat on paper for a healthy Rybakina — but that qualifier matters enormously right now. The question the opener answers is not whether Rybakina can beat Frodin. The question is what Rybakina looks like while doing it. How freely does she move? Does she protect the ankle on wide balls? Does she take anything off her serve?

The first round will be a medical scouting report as much as a tennis match.

What's Next

If Rybakina advances through her first-round match, the tournament field will be watching her second-round opponent draw with the same intensity as the match itself. A healthy Rybakina in the second week of the US Open is among the tournament's most formidable scenarios. An injured one still commands respect — but is also the kind of situation that, if pushed too hard, ends a season before it should. The next several days will tell us which version of Rybakina is here.
