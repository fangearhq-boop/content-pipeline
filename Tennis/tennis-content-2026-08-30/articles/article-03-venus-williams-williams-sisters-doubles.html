Venus Falls in R1, But the Williams Sisters Story at the US Open Is Just Beginning

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | August 30, 2026

Venus Williams walked off Arthur Ashe Stadium on Sunday evening having lost in the first round of the 2026 US Open to Sofia Kenin. She won an impressive set along the way, put up a fight in a match where very few expected her to compete, and then faced the reality that her singles run at this year's tournament is over.

She is 46 years old. She came in on a wild card, carrying a 14-match losing streak that stretched back into 2025. The outcome was anticipated by most observers. But Venus Williams has never cared much about what observers anticipate.

A Night to Remember, Even in Defeat

Kenin, a former Grand Slam champion herself (2020 Australian Open), had wins under her belt heading into Sunday's match and was the clear favorite. The result held — but Venus made her work for it. Before the final points were played, the Arthur Ashe crowd had already given Venus something that no result can take away: a standing ovation, the full weight of the stadium's affection, the recognition that she has earned her place here simply by stepping onto this court at this stage of her life.

She won seven Grand Slam singles titles across a career that began when most current players were in elementary school. She was the first Black woman ranked No. 1 in the Open Era. She won this tournament — singles — in 2000 and 2001. To see her on this court in 2026 is to understand how long and how fully she has given herself to the game.

The Reunion That Awaits

But the 2026 US Open chapter for Venus Williams is not finished. It has only just changed shape.

Venus and her sister Serena have been awarded a women's doubles wild card for the tournament — and with it comes one of the most emotionally significant moments in recent Grand Slam history. Serena Williams, 44, returned to competitive tennis in June 2026, more than three years after her retirement at the 2022 US Open — the same tournament where she and Venus last played doubles together at a Grand Slam. That day, they lost in the first round. This time, they arrive in different circumstances.

Together, the Williams sisters are 14-time Grand Slam doubles champions. They won the US Open doubles title in 1999 and again in 2009. They have won four Olympic gold medals playing alongside each other. The doubles partnership of Serena and Venus Williams is one of the most accomplished in the history of the sport — and it is back.

Serena's Return

Serena returning to a Grand Slam in 2026 would have seemed unthinkable when she played her farewell singles match here in Flushing Meadows four years ago. She announced she was "evolving away" from tennis, raised a daughter, and built a career beyond the sport. Then she chose to return — not to singles, which would be an entirely different challenge, but to doubles alongside her sister.

At 44, Serena is playing for reasons that have nothing to do with rankings or titles. She is here because this game shaped her life, because her sister is on the other side of the net beside her, and because there is something left to enjoy at a place she has called her greatest stage.

What's Next

The Williams sisters doubles draw at the 2026 US Open is one of the most anticipated matchups of the tournament's first week. Their first-round opponent and schedule will be announced soon. For Venus, whose singles run has ended, the doubles court is now the focus — and what a focus it is. Watch for Serena and Venus when they step out together in the doubles draw. The crowd at Arthur Ashe will be ready.
