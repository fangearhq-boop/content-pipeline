Great Britain Stun Zverev's Germany to Reach Davis Cup Semifinals

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | November 27, 2026

Great Britain are in the Davis Cup semifinals after one of the most compelling quarterfinal victories in the history of the Finals format. On Thursday in Bologna, they beat Germany 2-1, with 20-year-old Arthur Fery's defeat of world No. 1 Alexander Zverev providing the central moment of a tie that will be remembered long after the tournament concludes.

Cameron Norrie gave Britain the platform. In Rubber 1, the experienced British No. 1 beat Jan-Lennard Struff 6-3, 3-6, 6-2 in a physical, competitive match that swung in both directions before Norrie's greater consistency won out. Struff is a dangerous opponent on clay and hard courts alike, and Norrie's performance was not flashy — but it was exactly what Great Britain needed.

Then Fery walked onto court for Rubber 2 and changed the story entirely.

Fery Beats the World No. 1

Zverev came to Bologna as the ATP's year-end No. 1 — the first German in the history of the tour to claim that distinction. He had won multiple titles this season, and his record on fast indoor surfaces made him one of the most difficult opponents anyone in this draw could face. The expectation was straightforward: Germany would level the tie through their best player and force a deciding doubles.

Fery had other plans. The 20-year-old, ranked around No. 50 and making his first appearance at the Davis Cup Finals, played the match of his young career. He won in what witnesses described as a controlled, composed performance that belied both his age and the significance of the occasion. When the final ball landed, Great Britain led 2-0, and Zverev's 2026 season was over.

The result was not a fluke. Fery has been one of British tennis's most exciting emerging talents, and those who have followed him through the ranks knew he had the game for moments like this. But knowing and seeing it happen in a Davis Cup quarterfinal against the world's best player are two different things.

Skupski and Patten Seal It

With Britain already 2-0 up, the doubles became a formality in terms of result — but Skupski and Patten played it like it still mattered, beating Kevin Krawietz and Tim Puetz 6-2, 7-6(6) to complete the 2-1 victory. The pair are among the best Davis Cup doubles teams in the competition and showed exactly why throughout the week.

For Germany, the defeat ends what had been a historic 2026 season. Zverev's year-end No. 1 ranking was the first by a German in ATP history, a landmark achievement for a player who has spent years working toward the top. Davis Cup was the final piece he could not deliver. He will return in 2027 looking to change that.

What's Next

Great Britain face Spain in Saturday's semifinal in Bologna. If Alcaraz is fit to play, Spain will present a significant step up from Germany. If he is not, Britain's confidence after this result — and Fery's in particular — will make them dangerous regardless of opponent. For the first time in years, Great Britain have genuine momentum in team tennis. Saturday's match will tell us whether this week is a breakthrough or a blip.
