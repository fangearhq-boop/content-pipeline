Italy vs Czech Republic: Davis Cup Semifinal Preview — Can Italy Reach a Fourth Straight Final?

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | November 27, 2026

Italy's bid for a fourth consecutive Davis Cup title moves into its decisive phase on Friday. The defending champions face Czech Republic in the first semifinal of the 2026 Finals, with the match getting underway at 4:00 PM CET (10:00 AM ET) at BolognaFiere in Bologna. A place in Sunday's final is on the line, and both teams have strong reasons to believe they can claim it.

The context surrounding Italy is impossible to ignore. Jannik Sinner, the world No. 1 and winner of five Grand Slams, has been absent since August with a knee injury that was complicated by tonsil surgery in November. His recovery timeline puts his Australian Open preparation under significant question, let alone this week's Davis Cup. For a squad that has carried the expectations of Italian tennis on its collective shoulders for three years, his absence represents the most significant test of their depth.

Yet Italy have found ways to win without him. In Wednesday's quarterfinal, Lorenzo Musetti delivered a dominant 6-0, 7-6(5) performance against South Korea's Seongchan Hong, and the doubles pair of Matteo Berrettini and Andrea Vavassori survived an extraordinary deciding rubber, eventually winning 7-6(2), 7-6(11) to put Italy through 2-1. It was exactly the kind of resilience a team needs when it is trying to do something historically difficult.

The Czech Challenge

Czech Republic arrive in Friday's semifinal in commanding form. They dispatched Canada 2-0 in the quarterfinals, with Jiri Lehecka defeating Felix Auger-Aliassime 7-6(4), 6-2 and Tomas Machac following up against Liam Draxl. Both won without requiring the doubles rubber to decide the tie.

The Czechs have reportedly won seven consecutive Davis Cup matches heading into this semifinal, a run that reflects the quality and consistency they have shown across the tournament. Lehecka, ranked in the top 15, has established himself as one of the more dangerous players in this format over the last two years. Machac has been excellent as a secondary option.

Italy are likely to field Musetti and Flavio Cobolli as their singles players, with Berrettini and Vavassori on standby for doubles if needed. The head-to-head matchups — Cobolli and Musetti against Lehecka and Machac at the individual level — are genuinely close, which makes this tie difficult to call.

The Home Crowd Factor

Italy are, in a meaningful sense, the home team. The Finals are being held at BolognaFiere, and the Italian crowd has been a factor throughout the week. When Berrettini and Vavassori were grinding through that doubles tiebreak against South Korea on Wednesday, the noise in the arena was palpable. The Czechs will need to manage that environment, which is easier said than done in a Davis Cup knockoff match.

Berrettini in particular thrives in this setting. The former Wimbledon finalist has reinvented himself at this level after injuries disrupted much of his peak years on tour, and in Davis Cup he has become Italy's emotional anchor. If he is called on to play singles — and he might be, depending on how coach Filippo Volandri manages the rotation — his presence would change the dynamic of this tie entirely.

What's Next

Italy and Czech Republic get underway at 10:00 AM ET Friday morning in Bologna. The winner advances to Sunday's final, where they will face the winner of Saturday's Spain vs Great Britain semifinal. For Italy, a victory would put them within one match of a historic fourth consecutive Davis Cup title. For Czech Republic, it would represent the culmination of a tournament run that has quietly been one of the most impressive in the Finals. Watch this space.
