Davis Cup Finals 2026: Four Teams, One Trophy — Who Has the Edge This Weekend?

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | November 27, 2026

The 2026 Davis Cup Finals are down to four teams, with two semifinals set for Friday and Saturday in Bologna before Sunday's final. It is as open a competition as the event has produced in years — defending champions Italy face a Czech Republic side in compelling form, while Spain navigate Alcaraz fitness concerns against an inspired Great Britain squad. Here is how each team's chances look heading into the decisive weekend.

Italy — The Dynasty Under Pressure

Three consecutive titles. Twelve consecutive wins. Italy have been the dominant force in Davis Cup since the Finals format was established, and they are playing at home in Bologna. The crowd factor alone is worth something at this level.

But Jannik Sinner's absence changes the equation. The world No. 1, absent since August with a knee injury and tonsil surgery complications, would have made Italy the clear favorites in any draw. Without him, the pressure falls on Flavio Cobolli, Lorenzo Musetti, and Matteo Berrettini to deliver across a potential three-rubber tie against a Czech Republic team that has been utterly ruthless.

Italy's path to victory runs through Musetti's serving level and Cobolli's ability to stay competitive in tight matches. Berrettini in doubles with Vavassori is a known quantity. The question is whether the singles lineup can get to that point with at least one win.

Czech Republic — The Quiet Contenders

Czech Republic have won seven consecutive Davis Cup ties entering Friday's semifinal. They beat Canada 2-0 in the quarterfinals with both Lehecka and Machac winning in straight sets. No drama. No necessity for a deciding rubber.

Lehecka is ranked in the top 15 and plays with the kind of controlled aggression that makes him difficult on fast indoor courts. Machac has been his reliable partner. If Czech Republic can win their singles against an Itlay side playing without Sinner, they will face the favorites' tag in Sunday's final for the first time in this generation's Davis Cup history.

The challenge for the Czechs is the environment. Playing against Italy in Bologna, in front of an Italian crowd, is about as difficult a Davis Cup environment as exists in the sport. How Lehecka and Machac handle that pressure will define their campaign.

Spain — The Sleeping Giant

Spain arrive at the semifinal stage having barely broken sweat, which is both an asset and a question mark. Jodar and Merida beat Austria 2-0 without Alcaraz playing a point. That conserves Alcaraz's energy for Saturday — if his thigh allows him to play.

A fit Alcaraz changes Spain's ceiling dramatically. He is one of the best players in the world on any surface, and his competitive intensity in team formats has been a defining feature of his career. If he is available, Spain should be considered favorites for Saturday's semifinal against Great Britain.

The risk is that the thigh concern lingers and Spain enter another key tie without their best player. Great Britain have shown this week that they can beat anyone in the draw.

Great Britain — The Disruptors

Nobody expected this. After qualifying through Europe's regional stage with a 3-0 win over Ecuador, Great Britain came to Bologna as credible competitors but not favorites. After Thursday, the equation has changed.

Arthur Fery's win over Zverev — the year-end No. 1 — was not just an upset. It was a statement about what this young team is capable of. Norrie's consistent contribution and the Skupski-Patten doubles partnership add up to a balanced, dangerous squad.

Britain's ceiling this weekend depends on Fery sustaining his level. If he plays Saturday the way he played Thursday, there is no singles combination in Spain's draw that is guaranteed to beat him. That is a sentence nobody would have written coming into this week.

What's Next

Italy vs Czech Republic gets underway Friday at 10 AM ET. Spain vs Great Britain follows Saturday. The final is Sunday, November 29, in Bologna. This is the final weekend of competitive tennis in 2026, and it has everything.
