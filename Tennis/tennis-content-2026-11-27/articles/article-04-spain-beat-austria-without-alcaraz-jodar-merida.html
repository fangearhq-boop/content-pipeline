Spain Reach Davis Cup Semifinals Without Alcaraz as Jodar and Merida Step Up

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | November 27, 2026

Spain are in the Davis Cup Finals semifinals after a performance that said as much about their depth as it did about their star players. On Thursday in Bologna, they beat Austria 2-0 without Carlos Alcaraz — their world No. 2 and the player most opponents prepare their whole week around — with Rafael Jodar and Daniel Merida delivering the wins that put Spain into Saturday's semifinal against Great Britain.

Alcaraz sat out singles with a left thigh concern that the Spanish team has been managing carefully throughout the week. His absence from the lineup meant Spain's singles selections fell to two players with significantly fewer points in their respective columns — but both proved equal to the task.

Jodar and Merida Deliver

Jodar went first, beating Sebastian Ofner to give Spain the opening in Rubber 1. It was a composed performance from a player who could easily have been overwhelmed by the occasion and the pressure of filling in for the team's biggest name. Ofner, ranked comfortably inside the top 50 and a threat on hard courts, was managed without drama.

Merida completed the job in Rubber 2, beating Alexander Erler to give Spain a 2-0 lead and render doubles unnecessary. It was the cleaner, more decisive outcome Spain could have hoped for — no need to push Alcaraz back onto court, no unnecessary risk to his fitness, and two points banked for Saturday.

The result highlighted something that often gets lost in discussions about Spain's Davis Cup fortunes: they are not a one-man team. The federation has consistently developed players capable of competing at this level, and Thursday demonstrated that in the most emphatic terms.

The Alcaraz Question

Saturday is a different conversation. Great Britain — fresh off a stunning win over Germany in which 20-year-old Arthur Fery beat the world No. 1 Zverev — are playing with a confidence that makes them dangerous. Spain's coaching staff will need to decide whether to manage Alcaraz's thigh carefully or whether the semifinal demands his presence from the start.

If Alcaraz plays, he almost certainly starts in singles. His record against Norrie and Fery gives Spain a significant advantage in those rubbers, and his competitive instincts are such that he would not sit out a semifinal willingly. The question is whether his medical team is comfortable with the risk.

If he does not play, Jodar and Merida will need to replicate Thursday's performance against tougher opponents. Great Britain beat the world No. 1 yesterday. That is the standard Spain will be measured against on Saturday.

What's Next

Spain face Great Britain in Saturday's Davis Cup semifinal in Bologna. The winner advances to Sunday's final to face the winner of Friday's Italy vs Czech Republic tie. For Spain, this is familiar territory — they have been a fixture at the top of Davis Cup for years. The question is whether Thursday's comfortable win without their best player will give them added confidence going into what promises to be a much harder test.
