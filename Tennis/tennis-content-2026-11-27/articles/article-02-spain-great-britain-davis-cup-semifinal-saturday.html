Spain vs Great Britain: Saturday Davis Cup Semifinal Preview — Alcaraz Fitness and Fery's Breakthrough

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | November 27, 2026

Saturday's Davis Cup semifinal in Bologna has the makings of a classic. Spain, the perennial power of European team tennis, face a Great Britain side that has just produced the upset of the week. Both teams arrive at BolognaFiere off compelling quarterfinal victories, and the question of Carlos Alcaraz's fitness hangs over everything.

Spain beat Austria 2-0 on Thursday in a performance that raised more questions than it answered. Rafael Jodar and Daniel Merida won their singles rubbers, and the tie was concluded before doubles were needed. The result looked comfortable enough on paper — but Alcaraz, Spain's world No. 2 and arguably the most important player in the draw, did not play a single point. He sat out with a left thigh concern.

Spain's coaching staff have kept their cards close on his availability for Saturday. He trained on Friday and is listed as day-to-day. What is clear is that a Spain team with Alcaraz is a significantly different proposition to a Spain team without him — and Great Britain know that better than most.

Britain's Remarkable Week

Great Britain's path to Saturday's semifinal has been nothing short of remarkable. Cameron Norrie beat Jan-Lennard Struff in Rubber 1 of the Germany quarterfinal, winning 6-3, 3-6, 6-2 to give Britain the opening. Then came the moment that will define this week in British tennis.

Arthur Fery, 20 years old and in his first Davis Cup Finals, stepped onto court for Rubber 2 against Alexander Zverev. Zverev, the year-end ATP No. 1, the best player in the world by ranking in 2026, was supposed to be the wall Britain could not break. Fery broke it. The result sent the British contingent in the arena into celebrations that continued through the doubles, where Neal Skupski and Henry Patten beat Kevin Krawietz and Tim Puetz 6-2, 7-6(6) to seal the win.

Fery's win was not just about its result — it was about what it means for his trajectory. At 20, ranked around No. 50, he has just beaten the No. 1 player in the world in a Davis Cup semifinal environment. The game takes notice of performances like that.

Saturday's Matchup

If Alcaraz plays, Spain are heavy favorites. His head-to-head record against Norrie and Fery gives him a significant advantage on paper, and Spain's depth — already proven by Jodar and Merida's performance against Austria — means they can absorb a loss anywhere in the draw.

If Alcaraz does not play, the picture shifts. Jodar and Merida will need to replicate their quarterfinal performances against a stiffer test, and Britain will have an opening they will be desperate to exploit. Norrie is a proven Davis Cup performer. Fery is riding a confidence wave that makes him dangerous regardless of opponent.

Britain captain Leon Smith will make his selection decisions based on what he has seen in training. Skupski and Patten in doubles represent one of the best pairings at this event, and their Thursday performance showed exactly why.

What's Next

Spain vs Great Britain gets underway on Saturday, November 28, in Bologna. The winner faces the Italy-Czech Republic winner in Sunday's final. For Great Britain, it would be their first Davis Cup final in the modern Finals format. For Spain, it would be a chance to reclaim a trophy they have not won since the last iteration of this event's format. This is what the last week of the tennis year is for.
