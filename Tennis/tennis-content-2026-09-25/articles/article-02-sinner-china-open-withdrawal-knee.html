Sinner Officially Withdraws From China Open: "My Knee Is Not Where I Would Like It to Be"

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap

September 25, 2026

The news that tennis fans had been bracing for finally arrived on Friday. Jannik Sinner, the world No. 1, has officially withdrawn from the 2026 China Open in Beijing, releasing a personal statement that made clear his recovery from a right knee injury is not yet complete.

"I'm very sorry to announce that I won't be able to play in Beijing this year," Sinner wrote. "My knee is not where I would like it to be, and I'm not ready to compete, but I will do everything possible to be there on court as soon as I can."

The withdrawal ends any remaining uncertainty about Sinner's participation in the ATP 500 event (September 30–October 6), where he was the defending champion. He had remained on the entry list through Thursday, with no official withdrawal filed. As recently as this week, his team's position was that they were targeting Beijing. Now the door is closed on that chapter of the 2026 season for the Italian superstar.

A Title Defense That Will Not Happen

Sinner won the China Open in 2025 with a dominant 6-2, 6-2 victory over American Learner Tien in the final — a result that demonstrated the kind of clinical efficiency that has defined his game at its best. He was also the 2023 China Open champion, meaning he has back-to-back titles in Beijing that he will not have the chance to defend this cycle.

The right knee injury first emerged around the time of Wimbledon, where Sinner competed and won. Since then, he has not returned to competitive play. The Canadian Open, Cincinnati, and the US Open all passed without Sinner on court. With Friday's announcement, China joins that list.

The injury is described as extra-articular tendinous inflammation in the right knee. He completed treatment at the JMedical clinic in Turin earlier this month and resumed high-intensity training, but the decision was ultimately made that he is not yet ready to compete at the required level.

What Comes Next: Shanghai or Vienna?

The more pressing question now is where Sinner will return. Two possibilities remain on his schedule. The first is the Shanghai Masters, an ATP Masters 1000 event that runs from approximately October 5-18. A return there would preserve his presence in the Asian swing and allow him to begin accumulating points before the season's final push.

The second option is the Vienna Open, an ATP 500 event beginning October 26 — which would mean skipping the entire Asian swing and returning to competition with the indoor European leg instead. This is the more conservative option and one that former Italian ATP professional Stefano Pescosolido suggested earlier this week, noting that Sinner "wants to avoid relapses."

No timeline has been confirmed by Sinner or his team as of Friday. The decision will depend on how his knee responds over the coming days.

The Bigger Picture

Sinner's ATP Finals spot is already secured regardless of what happens in the coming months. He sits second in the Race to Turin with 9,630 points. That number is enough to guarantee his place in Turin even if he does not play another match this season. The priority, clearly, is arriving at the ATP Finals in November — and at the 2027 Australian Open — with a knee that is fully functional.

His absence from Beijing hands the China Open a different narrative. Zverev, who won the US Open last week, is the most prominent name now in the field. Without Sinner, the defending champion's spot at the top of the draw is vacated.

What's Next

Sinner is expected to make an announcement about his return timeline in the coming days or weeks. Tennis Fanrecap will continue tracking his recovery as updates become available. The Shanghai Masters draw will be made shortly after the China Open concludes, and whether Sinner's name appears in it will be the next significant marker in this story.
