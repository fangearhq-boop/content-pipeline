Laver Cup 2026 Day 1 Underway at The O2 as Europe and World Begin London Showdown

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap

September 25, 2026

The 2026 Laver Cup has officially opened its doors at The O2 Arena in London, and Day 1 is delivering a full slate of action as Team Europe and Team World begin their three-day battle for supremacy on one of tennis's most distinctive stages.

The afternoon session kicked off with a rematch that carries some history: Casper Ruud of Team Europe against Francisco Cerúndolo of Team World. The two last met at the 2024 Laver Cup in Berlin, where Cerúndolo won 6-4, 6-4 in the opening match of that edition. Whether the Argentine can replicate that performance on an indoor hard court in London — and in front of a crowd that will likely lean European — is one of the day's intriguing storylines.

Following Ruud and Cerúndolo, Czech star Jakub Menšík, 19, takes on Team World's Brandon Nakashima in the second singles match of the afternoon session. Menšík has been one of Europe's most exciting young players in 2026 and carries momentum into this week.

A Night Session Built for Debuts

The night session at 7 PM local time features one of the most anticipated storylines of Day 1: two Laver Cup debutants meeting on the biggest stage of their respective careers. Spain's Rafael Jódar and Kazakhstan's Alexander Bublik are making their Laver Cup debuts simultaneously, which gives the match an extra layer of unpredictability. Bublik is one of the most watchable players on tour — capable of brilliance and chaos in equal measure — while Jódar has been quietly building one of the more consistent seasons in Europe's camp.

The night session closes with the doubles match that has generated significant pre-event buzz: Carlos Alcaraz and Menšík for Team Europe take on Taylor Fritz and Bublik for Team World. It is the same pairing captain Yannick Noah deployed in 2025, and for good reason — Alcaraz's court instincts in doubles have been on full display in recent Laver Cup editions. Fritz, meanwhile, brings a calm precision that complements Bublik's explosive play.

Alcaraz will not play singles on Day 1. That debut is reserved for Day 2 or beyond. Alexander Zverev is also expected to open his singles account on Day 2.

The Stakes

Team Europe enter as the home-court favorites in every sense — The O2 has been their venue in spirit even if the competition travels. They hold an 8-1 lead in the all-time Laver Cup series. But Team World are the defending champions after a commanding 15-9 victory in San Francisco in 2025 — their first title since the inaugural edition — and captain Andre Agassi has assembled a side with serious depth.

The scoring system rewards patience and stamina: Day 1 wins are worth 1 point each, Day 2 wins are worth 2 points, and Day 3 wins are worth 3 points, with the last set replaced by a 10-point match tiebreak. First to 13 points wins the Laver Cup.

Team Europe (Capt. Yannick Noah): Carlos Alcaraz, Alexander Zverev, Casper Ruud, Jakub Menšík, Lorenzo Cobolli, Rafael Jódar (alt. Arthur Fery).

Team World (Capt. Andre Agassi): Taylor Fritz, Alex de Minaur, Alexander Bublik, Learner Tien, Francisco Cerúndolo, Brandon Nakashima.

What's Next

Day 1 results will shape the early psychological momentum of the 2026 Laver Cup. With a full session of singles and doubles ahead, the team that takes Day 1's points — four maximum — will set the tone heading into the higher-value matches of Saturday and Sunday. Day 2 coverage continues at Tennis Fanrecap, with Alcaraz and Zverev expected to enter the singles draw.
