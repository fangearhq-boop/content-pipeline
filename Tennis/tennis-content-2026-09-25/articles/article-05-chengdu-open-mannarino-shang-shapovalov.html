Chengdu Open: Mannarino Stuns Home Hero Shang, Shapovalov Earns 250th Career Win

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap

September 25, 2026

If the Chengdu Open crowd came to see local favourite Shang Juncheng extend his brilliant relationship with the city where he won his first ATP title in 2024, Thursday delivered the wrong ending. French veteran Adrian Mannarino, 38, silenced the stadium by defeating Shang 7-6(9), 6-2 in the round of 32 — a result that was entirely against the pre-match narrative but built entirely on Mannarino's gritty resilience.

The first set tiebreak ran to 11 points, with Mannarino eventually taking it 9-7 after a tension-filled sequence. Once he had that foothold, the second set followed in a different direction entirely: Mannarino was in command, taking it 6-2 to complete the upset in front of a stunned home crowd.

Mannarino's Unlikely Run Continues

The context makes the result even more striking. Mannarino had won just one of his six most recent tour-level matches before arriving in Chengdu. At 38, he is one of the oldest players still competing at this level with regularity, and expectations for his week were minimal. Shang, meanwhile, is a rising star making his return from injury — a player with home crowd support, ranking momentum, and a title memory in this exact city.

Mannarino now faces a different mountain in the Round of 16: No. 3 seed Alejandro Tabilo (Chile), the defending Chengdu champion. Tabilo received a first-round bye and enters Friday's match fresh. For Mannarino to reach the quarterfinals from here would be a significant story in Asian swing tennis.

Shapovalov's 250th Win

Denis Shapovalov (Canada, No. 7 seed) added his name to the week's results with a 6-4, 7-6(3) win over Dutch player Tallon Griekspoor. More meaningfully, the victory represented the 250th tour-level win of Shapovalov's career — a milestone for a player who has battled injuries at various points in recent years to remain a relevant force at this level.

Shapovalov, who reached No. 10 in the world rankings at his peak in 2021, has shown renewed consistency in 2026 and entering the Chengdu Open as a seeded player reflects that upswing in form. His path through the draw will test him against the Chengdu field as it thins out in the second half of the week.

The Bigger Picture in Chengdu

Top seed Valentin Vacherot (France) has not yet entered the competition, having received a bye through the first round. He will play his first match in the Round of 16, where he is expected to face Aleksandar Kovacevic or a qualifier. Vacherot, the best French player in the Chengdu draw, enters as the favourite to reach the final.

The 2026 Chengdu Open — like the concurrent Hangzhou Open — benefits from the Laver Cup vacuum: most of the ATP's top 10 are in London, leaving the Asian 250s as genuine opportunities for players further down the rankings.

What's Next

The Round of 16 is scheduled for Friday, September 25, with Mannarino vs. Tabilo and Shapovalov's match both on the agenda. Quarterfinals follow over the weekend. The Chengdu Open final is set for Monday, September 29. Full coverage at Tennis Fanrecap throughout the week.
