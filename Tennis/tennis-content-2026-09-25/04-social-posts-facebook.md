### STORY 2: Sinner China Open Withdrawal

**Long-Form Post — 12:30 PM ET**
Jannik Sinner made it official on Friday: he will not defend his China Open title in Beijing.

In a personal statement posted to social media, the world No. 1 wrote: "I'm very sorry to announce that I won't be able to play in Beijing this year. My knee is not where I would like it to be, and I'm not ready to compete, but I will do everything possible to be there on court as soon as I can."

Sinner has not played a competitive match since winning Wimbledon in July. A right knee injury has kept him sidelined through the Canadian Open, Cincinnati, and the US Open. He was the defending Beijing champion — he dismantled Learner Tien 6-2, 6-2 in last year's final — but that title defense is now off the table.

The bigger question is what comes next. His team hasn't confirmed a return date, but two events are in play: the Shanghai Masters (Oct 5-18) or the Vienna Open (Oct 26). Vienna would mean skipping the entire Asian swing. Given the team's cautious approach, Vienna may be the more likely destination.

The silver lining: Sinner's ATP Finals spot is already secured. He sits second in the Race to Turin with 9,630 points, well behind Zverev but comfortably ahead of the rest. The priority now is making it to Turin healthy.

### STORY 3: Singapore Open SF Set

**Long-Form Post — 8:00 AM ET**
The Singapore Open semifinals are set after a quarterfinal day full of contrasting drama.

Top seed Mirra Andreeva — the Roland Garros champion — left no doubt, defeating Leylah Fernandez 6-4, 6-3 to advance smoothly. Andreeva has been the standout player all week and enters Saturday's semifinal as the heavy favourite.

Elise Mertens reached the semifinals without playing a ball, receiving a walkover after Maja Chwalińska withdrew. The Belgian No. 4 seed will need to find her match rhythm against Andreeva.

On the other side of the draw, China's Xinyu Wang turned the tournament on its head by defeating Tatiana Prozorova 7-5, 6-4. Prozorova had herself been the surprise of the week, upsetting No. 3 seed Alexandra Eala in the round of 16. And Greek No. 7 seed Maria Sakkari also advanced, beating Australia's Talia Gibson in back-to-back tiebreaks, 7-6(2), 7-6(1).

Semifinals Saturday: Andreeva vs Mertens, and Wang Xinyu vs Sakkari. Final is Sunday.

### STORY 5: Chengdu Open Day 3

**Long-Form Post — 6:00 PM ET**
Adrian Mannarino refuses to be written off. The 38-year-old Frenchman was a massive underdog against Shang Juncheng on Thursday at the Chengdu Open — but he delivered one of the week's best results, defeating the former champion 7-6(9), 6-2.

Shang, the 2024 Chengdu title winner and a beloved home favourite, was making his comeback from injury. The sold-out crowd in Chengdu was behind him. But Mannarino — who had won just one of his last six tour-level matches — weathered an intense first-set tiebreak (7-9) and then dominated the second set.

Mannarino next faces No. 3 seed Alejandro Tabilo, the defending Chengdu champion, in the Round of 16. That's another tough draw, but at 38, Mannarino is playing with nothing to lose.

Meanwhile, Denis Shapovalov's win over Tallon Griekspoor 6-4, 7-6(3) was his 250th career tour-level victory — a milestone for the Canadian who has endured his share of injury setbacks in recent years.

Top seed Valentin Vacherot has yet to play. The quarterfinals loom.
