Race to Turin: Zverev's Year-End No. 1 All But Decided with Sinner Out of Shanghai

By Marcus Cole | October 8, 2026

Shanghai, China

When Alexander Zverev steps onto the court in Shanghai for his first match later this week, the ATP Race to Turin standings will already be telling a clear story: barring something extraordinary, 2026 will end with the first German player ever holding the year-end No. 1 ranking.

Jannik Sinner, the defending Shanghai champion, filed his official withdrawal on October 3. It was his seventh consecutive missed event, with his last match played at Wimbledon in July. The cause — an injury that has been managed conservatively throughout the fall — has kept the Italian away from the tour during the stretch when he traditionally accumulates the most points.

Sinner entered the Shanghai draw with approximately 7,950 Race points and is set to shed roughly 3,550 of those as his 2025 title defense expires. Zverev, with approximately 8,740 Race points entering the tournament, holds a gap that will widen to more than 4,500 points after Shanghai concludes October 18.

What Would Have to Happen

The math is unflinching. For Sinner to catch Zverev, he would need to win the Paris Masters and the ATP Finals in full — and Zverev would need to exit both events in the first round. Sinner has not confirmed he will play either tournament, having told reporters in the past that Vienna (October 26) was a target, with Paris uncertain.

Zverev, for his part, has been quietly consistent. His form on hardcourt has been strong all fall, and he arrives in Shanghai as the top seed with a draw that sets up an intriguing potential path through the quarters and semis.

The Bubble: Six Spots in Play

While the No. 1 picture is clearing, the fight for the remaining places in the eight-man Turin field is genuinely competitive. Spots 3 through 8 are all in contention with three weeks of the season remaining.

The current standings entering Shanghai show Ben Shelton at approximately 4,430 points, Carlos Alcaraz at approximately 4,100, Daniil Medvedev at approximately 2,520, Arthur Fils at approximately 1,860, Alex de Minaur at approximately 1,860, and Jiří Menšík at approximately 1,860. The tight cluster from positions five through eight means that one deep Shanghai run could shift the entire picture.

De Minaur, meanwhile, withdrew his retirement from the Beijing final with a groin injury and his status for Shanghai is uncertain. If he misses the event, his Race position could erode further as rivals earn points in his absence.

What's Next

Shanghai runs through October 18. Zverev and Djokovic are the two most-watched names in the draw, but the Race subplot will shape every match. Players on the bubble — Shelton, Medvedev, Fils — cannot afford early exits. For Zverev, the goal shifts from qualification to locking in No. 1 and arriving at Turin with full confidence in his ranking.

If Sinner does return for Vienna or Paris, the late-season drama would at least carry some intrigue. But the numbers, right now, point heavily to Zverev's year.
