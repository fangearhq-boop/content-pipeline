Djokovic Wins 7th Beijing Title and 102nd Career Crown as De Minaur Retires

By Marcus Cole | October 8, 2026

Beijing, China

Novak Djokovic added yet another page to his extraordinary biography on Monday, claiming his seventh ATP China Open title — and his 102nd career ATP title — after Alex de Minaur retired in the second set of their final with a groin injury.

Djokovic won the opening set in a tiebreak 7-6(3) before de Minaur, showing visible discomfort during a medical timeout, walked back onto the court and conceded the match at 0-1 in the second set. The final score: 7-6(3), 1-0 ret.

It was the latest in a series of extraordinary milestones for the 39-year-old Serb. With the victory, Djokovic became the oldest ATP 500 champion in the Open Era — a record that underscores just how far into uncharted territory his career has traveled.

34-0, Seven Titles

Djokovic has never lost a match at the China Open in Beijing. His all-time record now stands at 34-0, and his seven titles are the most any player has claimed at a single tournament in the Open Era. The numbers are simply without parallel.

Monday's final did not come without drama elsewhere in the draw. In Friday's semifinal, Daniil Medvedev was disqualified after a ball he hit into the stands in frustration struck a spectator near the left eye. Medvedev, who was trailing Djokovic 7-5, 5-3 at the time of the incident, lost all his points and prize money for the tournament. De Minaur advanced to his first Beijing final with a 6-4, 3-2 (ret.) victory over Hubert Hurkacz, who also retired due to injury.

The final ultimately lasted less than 90 minutes, but the context around it — Medvedev's DQ, two retirements inside 48 hours — made for one of the more unusual Beijing weeks in recent memory.

102 and Counting

The 102nd title is a number that stretches comprehension. Roger Federer retired with 103 tour titles; Djokovic is now one behind his longtime rival. At age 39, the idea of an even 100, let alone more, seemed improbable just a few years ago. Yet here Djokovic stands, the oldest man to win an ATP 500 event and still adding to a resume that has no obvious ceiling.

Djokovic arrived in Beijing having not played a final since Wimbledon in July. The China Open run — five matches, zero losses — was a reminder that, when healthy, he remains among the most dangerous players in the draw regardless of surface or tournament tier.

De Minaur, who had beaten Hurkacz in their semifinal before the retirement, was gracious despite the circumstances. The Australian No. 1 is 28 years old and still searching for his first Masters 1000 title; a groin injury in the biggest final of his season represents a painful setback.

What's Next

Djokovic advances to the Shanghai Masters, which is already underway (October 7-18), where he is seeded and scheduled to face Hubert Hurkacz in the second round. A deep Shanghai run would further cement his position in the ATP Race to Turin, where Alexander Zverev currently leads.

De Minaur's status for Shanghai is uncertain given the groin issue; his representatives have not confirmed whether he will compete or withdraw. The ATP Race picture, with Jannik Sinner also absent from the full Asian swing, leaves the No. 3-8 spots in the eight-man field genuinely in play heading into the final three weeks of the season.
