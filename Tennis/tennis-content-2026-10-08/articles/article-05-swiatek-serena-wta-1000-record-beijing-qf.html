One Win From History: Swiatek Chases Outright WTA 1000 Wins Record in Beijing QF

By Ryan Calloway | October 8, 2026

Beijing, China

A number that spent years in Serena Williams' name alone now has a co-holder: Iga Swiatek's Round of 16 victory over Iva Jovic at the WTA China Open gave her 148 WTA 1000 main-draw wins, matching the all-time record Williams set across her legendary career.

Swiatek will have the chance to claim sole possession of the mark when she faces Elise Mertens in the quarterfinals on October 9. It is a meeting that carries weight on multiple levels: Mertens is the player who just eliminated defending champion Coco Gauff in a major upset, and a Swiatek win would push her onto the short list of records that transcend a single era of the game.

Context: 148 Wins

To put the record in perspective: Swiatek, who turns 26 in May, has accumulated 148 WTA 1000 main-draw wins while also holding five Grand Slam titles. The WTA 1000 win total reflects consistency at the sport's second-biggest tier of events — not just Grand Slam success — making it a measure of sustained excellence across the full season.

Williams reached 148 across a career that spanned more than 20 years. Swiatek is doing it in her mid-20s, which raises the obvious question: how far beyond 148 could she go?

The win over Jovic was Swiatek's 31st WTA 1000 quarterfinal, and her sixth match at WTA 1000 level this season. She entered Beijing as the No. 8 seed, seeded outside the top three for the first time in years at a major event.

The Bigger Stakes: WTA Finals Bubble

Beyond the record, Swiatek has immediate competitive incentives. She sits 10th in the WTA Race to Finals with 3,584 points, approximately 650 points outside the bubble for the eight-player field in Riyadh. With only a handful of tournaments remaining before the qualifying window closes, every match in Beijing matters.

Mertens presents a genuine challenge. The Belgian veteran has already proven this week she can beat a top-three seed; she is serving well and generating errors from the opposition. Swiatek will need to be assertive from the first game to maintain control.

The rest of the quarterfinal bracket — featuring Andreeva (4), Svitolina (6), Muchova (7), Alexandrova (19), and Zheng — means that whichever player wins the Swiatek-Mertens match will likely face a different challenge in the semis. No top-three seed remains, so the path is open.

What's Next

The WTA Beijing quarterfinals are scheduled for October 8-9, with Swiatek vs. Mertens on October 9. The semifinals follow on October 10, the final on October 11. Swiatek's record pursuit is the story entering the back end of the tournament — but for a player who builds entire seasons around major titles, the record is secondary to the trophy. Both, ideally, come at the same time.
