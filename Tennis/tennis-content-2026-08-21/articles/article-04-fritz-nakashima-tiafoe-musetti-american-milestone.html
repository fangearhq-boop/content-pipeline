Four Americans in Cincinnati ATP Quarterfinals — First Time Since 1995 — as Fritz, Nakashima, Tiafoe Battle On

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | August 21, 2026

American tennis is making history at the Cincinnati Open this week, and Friday's quarterfinals are the stage. Four Americans — Taylor Fritz, Brandon Nakashima, Frances Tiafoe, and Tommy Paul — all reached the quarterfinals of the Cincinnati Open, the first time that has happened since 1995. Paul was eliminated Thursday by Flavio Cobolli. That leaves Fritz, Nakashima, and Tiafoe in action Friday, all with a place in the semifinals at stake.

Fritz vs. Nakashima: American vs. American

The highest-profile all-American matchup of the day is the No. 6 seed Taylor Fritz against Brandon Nakashima, who arrived in Cincinnati at a career-high No. 22. Both are American. Both have been playing excellent tennis this summer. And only one can advance.

Fritz leads the head-to-head 4-1, and their most recent meeting came just weeks ago in the Washington DC semifinals, where Fritz won in straight sets. Fritz brings the experience, the ranking advantage, and the recent H2H form into Friday's match. He also brings the weight of expectation as the higher seed and the player with more major wins on his resume.

Nakashima counters with momentum. He reached the Montreal Masters final before coming to Cincinnati, where he upset Medvedev in the round of 16 in a three-set thriller that required him to save three match points. The 24-year-old from San Diego is playing the best tennis of his life, and the career-high ranking reflects it. When a player is this hot, head-to-head records matter less than they usually do.

Tiafoe vs. Musetti: The Comeback Story

Frances Tiafoe's season has been defined by interruption and resilience. He dealt with a back injury earlier this year, then underwent surgery on a hand cyst in early August — just weeks before Cincinnati. His run this week, including the upset of No. 2 seed Felix Auger-Aliassime, has been one of the more quietly remarkable stories of the North American swing.

On Friday he faces No. 10 seed Lorenzo Musetti, the Italian who has translated his clay-court craft into a strong hard-court game this season. Musetti beat Jaime Faria 7-5, 6-2 in the round of 16 to advance. He brings controlled aggression and a one-handed backhand capable of producing winners at difficult moments.

Tiafoe's power, athleticism, and crowd-feeding energy make him dangerous at any time — and particularly dangerous as a story the crowd wants to support. The question is whether his conditioning is fully recovered from the hand procedure.

The 1995 Comparison

The ATP Tour highlighted the American milestone this week, noting it hadn't happened since 1995. That was the era of Pete Sampras, Andre Agassi, Michael Chang, and Jim Courier — the golden generation of American tennis. The fact that Fritz, Paul, Nakashima, and Tiafoe match that generation's depth at a single event underscores how American men's tennis has rebuilt itself over the past decade.

Fritz has been the steadiest American performer for several years. Paul's upset of No. 1 Zverev in the round of 16 was one of the week's signature moments. Nakashima's leap into the top 25 represents the new wave arriving. And Tiafoe — in the top 20, coming back from injury — provides the veteran edge and the crowd connection that every deep run needs.

What's Next

Friday's ATP quarterfinals — Fritz vs. Nakashima and Tiafoe vs. Musetti — take place on Stadium Court at the Lindner Family Tennis Centre in Mason, Ohio. Winners advance to Saturday's semifinals.
