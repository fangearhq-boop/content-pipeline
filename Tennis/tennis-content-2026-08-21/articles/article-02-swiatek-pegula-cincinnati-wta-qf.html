Swiatek Advances, Rybakina Retires as Pegula Battles Into Cincinnati Semifinals

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | August 21, 2026

Two WTA quarterfinals are settled at the Cincinnati Open, and the results carry weight well beyond this week's draws. Iga Swiatek is through to the semifinals after Elena Rybakina retired with a left ankle injury. Jessica Pegula survived a two-hour battle to beat Amanda Anisimova 6-4, 2-6, 7-6(4). The US Open storylines are multiplying fast.

Rybakina Retires — Ankle Injury Raises US Open Concern

Elena Rybakina walked off the court Friday before the first set was complete. The No. 2 seed retired while trailing Iga Swiatek 4-1 in the opening set, her left ankle having become too problematic to continue. Swiatek advances to the semifinals — her second consecutive Cincinnati SF — without needing to expend her full effort.

For Rybakina, the injury carries significance beyond Cincinnati. She came into this week not just as a title contender but as a player who could have displaced Aryna Sabalenka at the top of the WTA rankings. That bid is now over. More pressing is the question of her fitness ahead of the US Open, which begins August 30. Her ankle will be monitored closely over the coming days.

Rybakina had survived two rain stoppages in her earlier match against Elise Mertens. The extra court time and weather delays may have contributed to the ankle strain, though no official explanation was provided at the time of retirement.

Pegula Grinds Out Three-Set Win Over Anisimova

Jessica Pegula's quarterfinal against Amanda Anisimova was everything Swiatek's was not — a full, draining, back-and-forth battle that went the distance. Pegula won the opener 6-4, lost the second set 2-6, then gritted through a deciding tiebreak to advance 7-6(4).

The No. 3 seed's performance was a study in mental resilience. She dropped a set to a player who has the game to cause problems on hard courts, then held her nerve in the most pressure-heavy scenario — a match tiebreak — to come through. Pegula enters the semifinals with her baseline game intact and her mental game tested.

She now faces the winner of the Coco Gauff vs. Marta Kostyuk quarterfinal, scheduled for Saturday. Gauff leads the head-to-head 3-2 over her career; Kostyuk eliminated Gauff at Roland Garros earlier this year, making the matchup a genuine tossup.

WTA Semifinal Picture

With Swiatek and Pegula confirmed, the WTA semifinals on Saturday will feature two more finalists from Friday's remaining matches. Sara Bejlek — who shocked No. 1 Sabalenka in the round of 16 — faces Madison Keys in a quarterfinal that could produce another upset. The Gauff-Kostyuk quarterfinal, one of the most anticipated matches of the week, is scheduled for Saturday morning.

Swiatek, who is the defending Cincinnati champion from 2025, faces the winner of Bejlek and Keys. She enters the semifinal fresh, with minimal effort expended on Friday. That rest could prove critical over a demanding weekend that includes semis on Saturday and the final on Sunday.

What's Next

The remaining WTA quarterfinals — Bejlek vs. Keys (Friday) and Gauff vs. Kostyuk (Saturday) — will complete the semifinal draw. Both WTA and ATP semifinals are scheduled for Saturday, August 22. The Cincinnati finals are Sunday, August 23. The US Open begins one week later on August 30.
