Carlos Alcaraz Confirms US Open Return, Ending Four-Month Absence

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | August 21, 2026

Carlos Alcaraz is heading back to Flushing Meadows. The defending US Open champion officially confirmed his participation in the 2026 US Open on Thursday, ending four months away from competitive tennis due to a right wrist injury.

The announcement came through an Instagram post shared with sports journalist Fabrizio Romano. "I'm back. Here we go," Alcaraz said in the accompanying video. Two words. Four months of waiting. One of the most anticipated returns in recent tennis memory.

Four Months, Two Grand Slams, Zero Matches

Alcaraz last played competitively on April 14 at the Barcelona Open. Shortly after, his team revealed he was dealing with tenosynovitis — an inflammation of the tendon sheath in his right wrist — and that he would miss an extended period of competition.

That absence proved longer than most had hoped. He missed Roland Garros. He missed Wimbledon. As the North American hard court swing unfolded through July and August, Alcaraz watched from Spain, training with hitting partners including Holger Rune while his wrist healed.

His team took what the New York Times described as a "cautious approach" to recovery — a decision that likely felt frustrating in the short term but sets him up for a sustained return rather than a premature comeback that risks re-injury.

Cold Start at the Biggest Stage

What makes Alcaraz's return unusual is the method: no warm-up tournament. No tune-up event. No chance to shake off match rust before competing at a Grand Slam with 2,000 ranking points on the line.

It is, by any measure, a high-risk approach. Players returning from extended injury layoffs typically use a smaller tournament to find their rhythm before the major pressure of a Grand Slam. Alcaraz is skipping that step entirely, betting that four months of preparation work will compensate for the lack of competitive reps.

He won't be the only one arriving in New York with questions hanging over him. World No. 1 Jannik Sinner, who has been dealing with a right knee injury since Wimbledon, is also expected to skip any warm-up events before the US Open. The two top players in men's tennis will both be walking into the biggest tournament of the hard court swing cold.

The Title Defense Challenge

Alcaraz arrives at the US Open as the defending champion and with 2,000 ranking points tied to his result in New York. A poor showing — an early-round exit — would cost him significantly in the rankings and could open the door for rivals to move ahead.

The draw ceremony is scheduled for August 27. The main draw begins August 30. Alcaraz has roughly nine days from his announcement to step onto a Grand Slam court for the first time since winning the 2025 US Open title.

For all the uncertainty, the news of his return drew immediate excitement across the tennis world. A US Open without Alcaraz was always going to feel incomplete. A US Open with him — even a rusty, match-hungry version — is a different tournament entirely.

What's Next

Alcaraz will travel to New York ahead of the US Open draw on August 27. The main draw begins August 30 at the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center in Flushing, Queens. US Open Fan Week starts August 23, giving fans the opportunity to watch practice sessions before competition begins.
