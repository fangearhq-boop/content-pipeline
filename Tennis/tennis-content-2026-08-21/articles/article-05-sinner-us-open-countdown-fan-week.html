Sinner Completes Shockwave Therapy, Plans New York Arrival as US Open Fan Week Begins Tomorrow

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | August 21, 2026

US Open Fan Week starts tomorrow. The draw is August 27. The main draw begins August 30. And Jannik Sinner — World No. 1, defending champion, and the most-watched question mark in tennis — has completed his knee treatment in Turin and is preparing to fly to New York.

The Sinner Knee Update

Sinner has been working through a right knee injury at the J|Medical clinic in Turin since withdrawing from the Cincinnati Open earlier this month. The injury, described as tendon inflammation in the right knee, prompted him to miss both the Canadian Open and Cincinnati Open — his first extended absence since becoming World No. 1.

His treatment has included shockwave therapy, a technique that uses acoustic waves to stimulate healing in damaged tendons. It is not a conventional treatment, and Sinner's use of it drew attention from the tennis community. He has been working under physiotherapist Gianluca Melegati, and reports from Italy this week indicate the shockwave phase is complete.

The next step is New York. Sinner is reportedly planning to fly to the United States on Monday or Tuesday — August 25 or 26 — which would give him approximately 12 days of practice at Flushing Meadows before the main draw begins. It's a compressed timeline for a player returning from injury to a Grand Slam, but 12 days is more preparation time than some might have expected given the severity of his absence.

Confirmed vs. Expected

There is an important distinction between Sinner's status and that of Carlos Alcaraz. Alcaraz confirmed his US Open participation on Thursday via a social media announcement. Sinner has not made a public confirmation. His travel plans and the completion of his treatment timeline are encouraging signals, but his participation is not yet official.

Both players last played at Wimbledon — Sinner winning the title in July, Alcaraz having been absent since April. Both are arriving cold, without warm-up events. If both play, the US Open men's draw becomes significantly more unpredictable than it would be without either of them. If one or both miss the event, opportunities open for players like Zverev, Djokovic, Fritz, and Tiafoe who have match sharpness heading into New York.

The Bigger Picture: An Injury-Marked US Open Field

More than 11 players have already withdrawn from the 2026 US Open. Elena Rybakina retired from her Cincinnati quarterfinal Friday with a left ankle injury, adding another name to the concern list. The women's field has been reshaped by injury throughout the summer — Raducanu, Paolini, and others are already confirmed absent.

Sabalenka, the WTA No. 1 and defending US Open champion, was upset by Sara Bejlek in the Cincinnati round of 16. She exits Cincinnati early. Swiatek is alive in the Cincinnati draw. The women's field heading into the US Open is genuinely open in a way it hasn't been in several years.

On the men's side, Zverev and Djokovic head to New York with the most match sharpness. American players Fritz, Tiafoe, and Nakashima have all performed well in Cincinnati this week. The absence of Sinner and Alcaraz — or the rust-affected presence of either — creates a genuinely unpredictable men's field.

What's Next

US Open Fan Week begins Saturday, August 23, with practice sessions open to fans at the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center in Flushing, Queens. Qualifying rounds run August 25-29. The draw ceremony is August 27. The main draw begins August 30, with the men's and women's finals scheduled for the second week of September.
