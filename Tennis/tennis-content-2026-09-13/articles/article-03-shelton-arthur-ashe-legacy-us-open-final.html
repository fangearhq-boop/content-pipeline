The Weight of History: Ben Shelton's US Open Final and the Legacy of Arthur Ashe

By Ryan Calloway | September 13, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

When Ben Shelton walks out onto Arthur Ashe Stadium today for the 2026 US Open Men's Final, the moment carries more than a match score. He is the first Black American man to play in a US Open final since Arthur Ashe won the title on this very court in 1972. That is 54 years. The stadium was named in Ashe's honor. And today, one of his successors will play for the championship inside it.

A victory would also end the longest drought in American men's tennis: no American man has won a Grand Slam since Andy Roddick claimed the US Open in 2003. Twenty-three years. Shelton, 23, was not yet born when Roddick lifted that trophy.

A Path Through the All-American Draw

Shelton's route to the final was a story in itself. He beat four American men in the top half of the draw and, in the semifinals, defeated Frances Tiafoe 4-6, 6-3, 6-3, 7-5 in one of the most emotionally charged matches of the tournament. Tiafoe and Shelton are close friends, training partners, and the two Black American men who anchored the US Open's most celebrated week in a generation.

After the match, Shelton stood at the microphone on Arthur Ashe Stadium and spoke quietly about family. "My dad lost his mum earlier this year," he said. "She's the reason he was able to play tennis. Without her there's no Ben Shelton standing here today. This one is for her."

It was the kind of moment that makes sport something beyond sport.

The Ashe Parallel

Arthur Ashe won the 1968 US Open as an amateur, the first Black man to win a Grand Slam singles title. He won Wimbledon in 1975. The stadium at Flushing Meadows was renamed in his honor after his death in 1993. He remains one of the most significant figures in the history of the sport — not only as a champion but as a voice for justice and equality off the court.

Shelton has spoken in the past about the weight of representation and about Ashe's place in the game. Today, he plays inside the building that carries Ashe's name, in a final that carries Ashe's shadow.

The Challenge Ahead

Shelton faces Alexander Zverev, the world No. 1, who leads their head-to-head record 5-0 and is playing in his third consecutive Grand Slam final of 2026. Zverev is the most complete player in the world right now — a deep returner, a powerful baseline presence, and a champion who has found his best in the biggest moments this year.

The numbers say Zverev should win. But the 2026 US Open has been full of moments that numbers couldn't predict. Four of the top four women's seeds reached the semifinals for the first time since 1975. Djokovic and Sinner went out before the second week. And a 23-year-old American reached the final for the first time since a different era of the game.

What's Next

The match begins at 2 PM ET today on ESPN at Arthur Ashe Stadium. Whatever the result, Ben Shelton has already made this one of the most memorable US Opens in years. If he wins, the conversation becomes something larger — a new chapter in American tennis and in the legacy of the court where he will play for it.
