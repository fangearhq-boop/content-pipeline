2026 WTA Grand Slam Season: Three Champions, Record Prize Money, and the End of an Era

By Marcus Cole | September 13, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

The 2026 WTA Grand Slam season is complete. With Elena Rybakina winning the US Open on Saturday, the final chapter of what may be remembered as one of the most competitive women's years on record has been written. Three different players won Grand Slam titles. The world No. 1 changed hands. And the era of Sabalenka's dominance at the top of the rankings came to an end — not abruptly, but through the sustained excellence of a rival who is now at least her equal.

How the Four Slams Played Out

The Australian Open in January set the tone. Rybakina and Sabalenka met in the final, the same matchup from the 2023 Australian Open final, but this time Rybakina won. She took it in two close sets, collecting her second Grand Slam title and establishing herself as the most dangerous hard-court player in the women's game.

Roland Garros in May and June produced the year's biggest upset at a Slam level when Mirra Andreeva, the young Russian star, claimed the French Open title. Andreeva's win on clay was celebrated as the arrival of a new generation and gave the French Open a champion outside the established top tier for the first time in several years.

Wimbledon results will be reflected in the full-season records, but the year's story was largely told on hard courts — and the US Open delivered its expected conclusion: Rybakina over Sabalenka, for the second time in a year, in a three-set final that was closer than the scoreline in the third set suggests.

A Draw That Will Be Talked About

The 2026 US Open women's draw was historically deep. All four top seeds — Sabalenka (No. 1), Rybakina (No. 2), Jessica Pegula (No. 3), and Coco Gauff (No. 4) — reached the semifinals for the first time since 1975. That kind of result reflects a draw where the favorites justified their seedings and none of the upsets from the men's side — where Djokovic lost in round one and Sinner withdrew — materialized on the women's half.

The semifinals were compelling on their own terms: Sabalenka defeated Pegula, and Rybakina defeated Gauff in a three-set match that ended with the Kazakh player having won 12 of the final 14 games. By the final, Rybakina was in the form of her career.

Record Prize Money

The 2026 US Open offered $108 million in total prize money, a 20 percent increase over the previous year and the largest total purse in Grand Slam history. Rybakina's $5.5 million winner's check is the largest single prize ever paid out at a major. It reflects both the growth of the sport and a deliberate effort by the US Open to ensure prize money increases outpace inflation across all rounds.

What's Next

Rybakina heads into the Asian swing as WTA world No. 1 for the first time. The WTA Finals in November will serve as the final major event of the women's season. Sabalenka, Andreeva, Pegula, and Gauff are all likely to feature, and the rankings battle between the top four will run through the final weeks of October. This year's men's final between Zverev and Shelton concludes today at 2 PM ET on ESPN — the last match of the 2026 US Open and the final Grand Slam of the year.
