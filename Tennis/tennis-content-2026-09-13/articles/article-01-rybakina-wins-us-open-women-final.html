Rybakina Wins 2026 US Open Women's Title, Defeating Sabalenka 6-4, 5-7, 6-2

By Elena Voss | September 13, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

Elena Rybakina is the 2026 US Open champion. The Kazakh star defeated Aryna Sabalenka 6-4, 5-7, 6-2 on Saturday in the women's singles final at Arthur Ashe Stadium, claiming her third Grand Slam title and her first US Open crown in a match that lasted 2 hours and 21 minutes.

The victory was commanding in its structure. Rybakina raced through the first set in 44 minutes, relying on her heavy serve and flat groundstrokes to overwhelm Sabalenka before the Belarusian had time to settle. Sabalenka clawed back in the second set, winning it on a tiebreak to level the match, but Rybakina was sharp again in the third, breaking Sabalenka twice to close out the title in dominant fashion.

Third Slam, First US Open

The win makes Rybakina one of the defining players of this era. She also beat Sabalenka in the Australian Open final in January of this year, making Saturday's result her second Grand Slam victory over her chief rival in 2026 alone. Her three Grand Slam titles — Wimbledon 2022, Australian Open 2026, and now the US Open — are a testament to her consistency at the highest level.

Sabalenka arrived in New York attempting to win an unprecedented third consecutive US Open, a feat not achieved by any woman since Serena Williams won three straight from 2012 to 2014. It was not to be. Sabalenka reached two Grand Slam finals in 2026 and fell in both.

Record Prize Money and a New No. 1

Rybakina's prize for the title is $5.5 million — the largest payout in Grand Slam history, reflecting the 2026 US Open's record-breaking $108 million total prize pool, a 20 percent increase over the previous year. The financial record added another layer of significance to an already historic afternoon.

The biggest consequence of the result, however, may be on the rankings. Rybakina becomes the new WTA world No. 1 when the updated rankings are published Monday, ending Sabalenka's extraordinary 99-consecutive-week run at the top. She is also the first player from Kazakhstan — on either the ATP or WTA tour — to hold the No. 1 ranking.

A Season for the Record Books

The 2026 WTA Grand Slam season produced three different champions. Rybakina won the Australian Open in January, Mirra Andreeva claimed Roland Garros in the spring, and Rybakina closed the season at the US Open. It is one of the most competitive women's years in recent memory — four of the top four seeds reached the US Open semifinals for the first time since 1975.

Sabalenka, who had won three consecutive Grand Slams entering 2026, leaves New York without a title but with two finalist appearances that underline just how closely matched the top of the women's game remains.

What's Next

With the US Open title secured, Rybakina will carry the No. 1 ranking into the Asian swing and the WTA Finals in November. Sabalenka, now No. 2, will look to regain the top spot over the back half of the season. The men's final takes place today at 2 PM ET on ESPN, with Alexander Zverev facing Ben Shelton on Arthur Ashe Stadium.
