Rybakina Becomes New WTA World No. 1 Monday — Ending Sabalenka's 99-Week Reign

By Elena Voss | September 13, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

Elena Rybakina will become the new WTA world No. 1 on Monday, September 14, when updated rankings are published following the 2026 US Open. Her title victory over Aryna Sabalenka on Saturday brings to an end Sabalenka's record-breaking 99-consecutive-week reign at the top — the longest single run at No. 1 by any player since Serena Williams.

It is a milestone that extends well beyond the ranking itself. Rybakina is the first player from Kazakhstan — on either the ATP or WTA tour — to hold the world No. 1 ranking. She clinched the right to take the top spot mathematically when she beat Zheng Qinwen in the quarterfinals last week, but she needed the title to complete the journey, and she did it in definitive fashion.

A Season Built for This Moment

Rybakina's rise to No. 1 has been years in the making. She won Wimbledon in 2022, establishing herself as a genuine major champion. In January 2026, she beat Sabalenka in the Australian Open final, taking her second Grand Slam and putting herself firmly in contention for the top ranking. Now, with the US Open title added, she has claimed two of the four Grand Slams in a single calendar year.

The 2026 season has been defined by Rybakina's ability to perform at her best in the biggest environments. Her serve — arguably the most effective in the women's game — and her flat groundstrokes have proven difficult to handle on hard courts, and this US Open was no exception. She did not drop a set until the Women's Final itself, where Sabalenka pushed her to three before Rybakina closed it out in the third.

Sabalenka's Record Run Ends

Aryna Sabalenka's 99-week stretch at world No. 1 was one of the most dominant periods at the top of the women's rankings in the sport's modern era. She won three consecutive Grand Slams between 2024 and 2025, the kind of run that places players in the conversation about the all-time greats. Even in 2026, she reached the finals of two of the four Slams.

She finishes the year at world No. 2, still firmly inside a generation of players who have made this one of the most competitive periods in women's tennis. The rivalry between Rybakina and Sabalenka — three Grand Slam final meetings in two years, with Rybakina winning two — is likely to define the next chapter of the WTA.

What's Next

Rybakina will carry the No. 1 ranking into the Asian hard-court swing and then to the WTA Finals in November. With two Grand Slam titles in 2026 and the year's final major crown, she is the clear favorite heading into the end of season. Sabalenka, in pursuit of the No. 1 ranking again, has the Pan Pacific Open and the China Open ahead to close the gap. Monday's ranking change will be official, but the competition for who ends the year at the top is far from settled.
