Zverev vs. Shelton: 2026 US Open Men's Final Set for Today at 2 PM ET

By Marcus Cole | September 13, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

The 2026 US Open ends today at Arthur Ashe Stadium, where Alexander Zverev and Ben Shelton meet in the men's singles final starting at 2 PM ET on ESPN. Two very different careers arrive at the same moment: Zverev seeking his second Grand Slam title of the year in his third consecutive major final, Shelton playing in his first major final at age 23 and chasing a piece of American tennis history.

Zverev's Route to the Final

The world No. 1 has been a dominant presence throughout the draw, even if the path wasn't always smooth. He beat Karen Khachanov in the semifinals in three sets, with the final two decided by tight tiebreaks — 7-6(9) and 7-6(6). Those close finishes followed a quarterfinal against Tommy Paul van den Zandschulp that was more comfortable. Zverev has now reached three consecutive Grand Slam finals in 2026, winning Roland Garros in June and reaching the Australian Open final in January.

His head-to-head record against Shelton is 5-0. In previous meetings, Zverev's deep return game and ability to neutralize big serves have been a key factor against the American's aggressive, all-or-nothing style. The numbers strongly favor the German.

Shelton's Route to the Final

Ben Shelton has been one of the stories of the 2026 US Open from the first round. He reached the final by defeating fellow American Frances Tiafoe in four sets in an all-American semifinal — 4-6, 6-3, 6-3, 7-5 — in a match that packed Arthur Ashe Stadium with noise and energy from the first point. Before that, he won his quarterfinal in straight sets and navigated a difficult second week that took out several higher-ranked players.

Shelton's game is built on a 147 mph serve that can put any opponent on the back foot immediately, a fearless forehand, and an ability to raise his level in the big moments that has defined his run in New York.

What's at Stake

For Zverev, the title would make him a two-time Grand Slam champion and confirm him as the most dominant player of this phase of the game. The $7.67 million winner's check — the largest in men's tennis history — is also on the line.

For Shelton, the stakes are just as high but carry a different weight. An American man has not won a Grand Slam since Andy Roddick lifted the US Open trophy in 2003. That 23-year drought ends today if Shelton can find a way past Zverev. He is also making history as the first Black American man in a US Open final since Arthur Ashe in 1972, a significance that has not been lost on anyone in New York this week.

What's Next

Coverage begins at Arthur Ashe Stadium at 2 PM ET on ESPN. Whoever wins today claims the final Grand Slam title of the year and the No. 1 ranking context heading into the Asian swing. The women's champion Elena Rybakina became the new WTA world No. 1 yesterday after defeating Sabalenka. Today closes out one of the most memorable US Opens in recent memory.
