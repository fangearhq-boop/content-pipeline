### STORY 1: Rybakina Wins 2026 US Open Women's Title

**Long-Form (9:00 AM ET)**
Elena Rybakina is the 2026 US Open champion. 🎾

In one of the most anticipated women's finals in recent years, Rybakina defeated Aryna Sabalenka 6-4, 5-7, 6-2 in 2 hours and 21 minutes on Arthur Ashe Stadium. It's Rybakina's third Grand Slam title and her first US Open crown — bookending an extraordinary 2026 season that opened with her Australian Open win back in January.

The victory also ends Sabalenka's bid for an unprecedented third consecutive US Open title. Sabalenka, who entered the match as the defending champion and world No. 1, fought back in the second set but couldn't maintain it in the third. Her 99-consecutive-week reign at the top of the WTA rankings ends on Monday when Rybakina officially takes over.

Rybakina's $5.5 million prize is the largest in Grand Slam history, reflecting the US Open's record $108 million total prize pool this year.

The 2026 US Open comes down to this. 🎾

### STORY 3: Shelton Chases History

**Long-Form (12:00 PM ET)**
Ben Shelton is one match away from rewriting history.

When the 23-year-old walks out onto Arthur Ashe Stadium today at 2 PM ET, he will become the first Black American man in a US Open final since Arthur Ashe did it in 1972. A win today would also make him the first American man to win a Grand Slam since Andy Roddick claimed the 2003 US Open — a drought stretching 23 years.

After beating Frances Tiafoe in a charged all-American semifinal, Shelton took a moment at the podium to honor his late grandmother. "My dad lost his mum earlier this year," he said. "She's the reason he was able to play tennis. Without her there's no Ben Shelton standing here today. This one is for her."

He faces Alexander Zverev, the world No. 1 and Roland Garros champion, who leads their head-to-head 5-0. But numbers haven't told the full story at this US Open. Shelton has been as good as anyone in the draw, and Arthur Ashe Stadium has been his home all fortnight.

This is what major championship tennis is made of. 🎾

### STORY 4: Rybakina's New No. 1 Era

**Long-Form (3:30 PM ET)**
A new era begins in women's tennis starting Monday.

Elena Rybakina's US Open title means she will officially become the WTA world No. 1 when the new rankings are released. She ends Aryna Sabalenka's remarkable 99-consecutive-week run at the top — the longest stretch since Serena Williams held the position for extended periods.

It's a historic moment for Rybakina and for Kazakhstan. She is the first player from Kazakhstan — on either the ATP or WTA tour — to hold the No. 1 ranking. She actually clinched the top spot mathematically last week when she beat Zheng Qinwen in the quarterfinals.

With two Grand Slam titles in 2026 and now a career-high ranking, Rybakina heads into the Asian swing and the WTA Finals as the woman to beat.

### STORY 5: 2026 WTA Grand Slam Season Wrap

**Long-Form (5:00 PM ET)**
Three Grand Slam titles. Three different champions. One unforgettable WTA season.

The 2026 women's Grand Slam season is now complete, and it may be remembered as one of the most competitive on record. Rybakina won the Australian Open in January, defeating Sabalenka in the final. Mirra Andreeva claimed Roland Garros in the spring, becoming one of the youngest French Open champions in recent memory. And now Rybakina has closed the season at the US Open with her second Slam of the year.

Sabalenka, who entered 2026 having won three consecutive Grand Slams, reached two finals this year — the Australian Open and the US Open — without winning either. She finishes the season No. 2, her record-breaking No. 1 run at an end.

The women's game has never felt more wide open. Four of the top four seeds reached the US Open semifinals for the first time since 1975. The prize money topped $5.5 million per finalist for the first time.

What a year. 🎾
