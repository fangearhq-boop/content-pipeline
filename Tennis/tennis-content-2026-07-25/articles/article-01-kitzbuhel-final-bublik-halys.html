Bublik vs. Halys: Can the Defending Champion Do What No One Has Since 2013?

By Marcus Cole July 25, 2026

The Final Is Set

Alexander Bublik and Quentin Halys will contest the Generali Open Kitzbühel final on Saturday, and the stakes are clearer than most ATP 250 title matches. Bublik — the defending champion from Kazakhstan — is chasing something that has not happened at this tournament since 2013: a successful title defense. He beat Tomas Martin Etcheverry 6-3, 6-4 in Friday's semifinal to get here. Halys beat Yannick Hanfmann 6-4, 7-6(2) in the other half.

Bublik leads the head-to-head record against Halys 2-0. That matters on clay, where rhythm and confidence carry weight. But Halys has earned the right to stand across the net from a defending champion: he came through a tough second-set tiebreak against Hanfmann and has not been broken at a critical moment all week.

What Makes Kitzbühel Defense So Rare

The Austrian clay is not a surface that rewards defending champions easily. Since the 2013 title defense, more than a decade of Kitzbühel finalists have failed to return and win the following year. The draw structure, the grueling mid-summer schedule, and the fact that many top players skip the event entirely — or peak elsewhere — means the field can shift dramatically from one edition to the next.

Bublik won here in 2025 with the kind of tennis that makes him difficult to solve on a slow clay surface. His serve variety, willingness to attempt improbable shots, and the sheer range of pace he can produce all translate unusually well to the surface. The question is whether he is replicating that form this year. His 6-3, 6-4 dismissal of Etcheverry suggests he is close.

Halys's Case

Quentin Halys is not simply the final's supporting act. The Frenchman arrived in Kitzbühel ranked outside the top 90 and has beaten two opponents comfortably on the way to the final. His win over Hanfmann on Friday went the distance in the second set, but the 7-6(2) scoreline in the tiebreak tells you Halys handled the pressure when the points mattered most.

Halys has not won an ATP 250 title at this level. That's the gap between him and Bublik right now. But finals have a way of collapsing experience gaps when one player is nervous about protecting something and the other has nothing to lose. Halys is the underdog by every metric available. That can be useful.

What's Next

The Kitzbühel final is scheduled for Saturday. If Bublik wins, he joins a short list of players who successfully defended this title and adds another clay-court entry to his resume heading into the North American hard-court season, beginning with the National Bank Open in Montreal next week. If Halys wins, it's his first ATP 250 title and a result that reshapes how the tennis world thinks about the Frenchman's ceiling. Tennis Fanrecap will have the result as soon as it is confirmed.
