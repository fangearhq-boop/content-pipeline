The Montreal Exodus: Sinner, Djokovic, Alcaraz, Draper All Withdraw From National Bank Open

By Marcus Cole July 25, 2026

The Scope of the Withdrawals

The National Bank Open has confirmed four of the biggest names in men's tennis will not play in Montreal next week. Jannik Sinner, Novak Djokovic, Carlos Alcaraz, and Jack Draper are all out. Sinner cites an elbow injury sustained at Wimbledon. Djokovic is dealing with a separate injury. Alcaraz has been sidelined since April — he missed the French Open and Wimbledon as well. Draper's arm has not held up well enough for a return from his own Wimbledon withdrawal.

Sinner addressed his decision directly: "After carefully considering all the factors together with my team, we have made the difficult decision to withdraw from Montreal. It is never easy to miss such an important event, but we believe this is the right decision to prioritize my health." It is a measured statement for a situation that is genuinely frustrating for the world No. 1, who has won in Montreal before and would prefer to be playing.

Between the four of them, these are players who have won Grand Slams, Masters 1000 events, and have spent significant portions of 2026 at the top of the rankings. Their combined absence is not just a headline. It reshapes the entire tournament.

The Injury Epidemic Context

This does not happen in isolation. Wimbledon 2026 ended with 18 player withdrawals across the draws — a number that prompted Jack Draper, himself one of the withdrawals, to publicly call for structural change in how the tennis calendar is organized. The combination of surface transitions, a compressed schedule, and the demands of a 52-week professional season has produced an injury rate that is impossible to ignore.

The National Bank Open now inherits the aftermath. Tournament organizers in both Toronto (women, August 1) and Montreal (men, August 4) have confirmed player lists that look very different from what anyone expected when the season began. On the women's side, Italy's Jasmine Paolini has also withdrawn with a foot injury, and Canadian wildcard Victoria Mboko is out for the rest of the season following her knee injury at Queen's Club.

Who Benefits

Three names stand out as the immediate beneficiaries of the cleared path in Montreal. Alexander Zverev, the recent Roland Garros champion, now looks at a field where he is clearly the highest-ranked player available and a strong favorite to reach at least the final. Ben Shelton is the defending Canadian Open champion and will not need to face three of the four players most capable of beating him on a hard court. Daniil Medvedev won this event in 2021 and knows how to perform in Montreal regardless of the field's composition.

Beyond those three, the draw becomes genuinely interesting. A player sitting outside the top 20 who has never won a Masters 1000 could find themselves in a final that would define their career. It has happened before at this tournament, and the 2026 edition has set up the conditions for it to happen again.

What's Next

Draws for the National Bank Open are expected around July 29-31. The women's event in Toronto begins August 1; the men's event in Montreal begins August 4. Following the current clay-court tournaments wrapping up this weekend, the transition to hard courts begins for everyone healthy enough to make it.
