Prague Semifinals: Krejcikova on Seven Straight, Valentova Carries the Upset Story

By Elena Voss July 25, 2026

Krejcikova's Run

Barbora Krejcikova arrived in Prague having won her last six matches. She has now made it seven. The 2021 Roland Garros and 2024 Wimbledon champion is the kind of player who finds form in clusters rather than grinding through seasons uniformly, and right now she is in one of those clusters. Her semifinal opponent Saturday is Lilli Tagger, a player without the same Grand Slam pedigree but capable of extending rallies on clay in ways that can disrupt timing.

Seven consecutive wins at this stage of the summer is not a minor streak. Krejcikova built it through Athens and has continued it in Prague without dropping a match. If she keeps it going through the final, it will be one of the cleaner hot streaks any player has produced in the European clay swing's closing week.

Valentova's Quarterfinal Upset

Tereza Valentova took down defending champion Marie Bouzkova — the No. 1 seed — in Friday's quarterfinals, and it was not a straightforward result. The match went three sets and required a 7-6(8-6) final-set tiebreak to decide it. That kind of match, won under pressure by the lower-seeded player, tends to generate either exhaustion or momentum going into the next round. Valentova's semifinal against Daria Snigur on Saturday will tell you which.

Bouzkova had won this event before. The defending champion is generally the safest bet in any draw because they know the surface, the conditions, and the pressure of the week. Valentova beating her anyway — in a tiebreak that went to 8-6 — is one of the week's better quarterfinal stories.

The Czech Factor

Prague has always had a strong Czech flavor, and this week is no exception. Krejcikova and Valentova are both Czech, which means the Prague semifinals could — depending on results — produce an all-Czech final. The crowd has shown up for the locals throughout the week, which is exactly the kind of environment that can extend a run that might otherwise stall.

Snigur (Ukraine) in the other semifinal is a player with the game to disrupt Valentova's momentum, particularly if the Ukrainian finds her service rhythm early. The Krejcikova-Tagger match carries the higher profile, but the Valentova-Snigur result could be the more surprising one.

What's Next

Both Prague semifinals are scheduled for Saturday, with the final on Sunday July 26. A Krejcikova win would extend her streak to eight and set her up for a title run that would look extremely good heading into the North American hard-court season.
