Wawrinka's Career Is Over — Lehečká Wins 7-5, 7-6(2)
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post It ended in a tiebreak. Jiří Lehečká beat Stan Wawrinka 7-5, 7-6(2) in Basel Tuesday evening, and with that result, one of the greatest careers in tennis history is officially over. Wawrinka served at 2-6 in the tiebreak. Lehečká won the last five points from 2-2. That was it. The statistics from the match tell you what Wawrinka was working against: he won 17 of 39 second-serve points. Lehečká won 17 of 20. A 41-year-old playing his final match isn't going to overpower No. 21 in the world, and he didn't. But he was in it through most of the second set, and Basel gave him everything they had. The career: 590 wins, 985 matches, 16 ATP titles, three Grand Slams across three different surfaces (2014 Australian Open, 2015 Roland Garros, 2016 US Open), an Olympic gold medal with Roger Federer in Beijing. Career-high ranking No. 3. And a backhand that will be shown in highlight reels for as long as people talk about tennis. He chose Basel for a reason. He won his first title here in 2006, his third in 2016. This week, the city gave him a tribute night on Monday and a real match on Tuesday. That's what a proper goodbye looks like. What do you remember most about Stan Wawrinka's career? --- ### Image Post Caption Stan Wawrinka's career is over after a 7-5, 7-6(2) loss to Lehečká in Basel. Three Grand Slams. 590 wins. Zverev Survives Thriller in Vienna, Alcaraz Escapes Khachanov
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Vienna delivered two very different second-round matches Tuesday. Alexander Zverev needed everything he had to beat wildcard Martin Landaluce 7-5, 3-6, 7-6. Landaluce took the second set and pushed Zverev to four match points in the deciding tiebreak before the No. 1 seed finally closed it out. The win was Zverev's 63rd of the year — still leading the tour — and he's now one quarterfinal appearance away from mathematically locking up the ATP World No. 1 ranking for 2026. The fact that it was this hard against a wildcard ranked around No. 67 will be noted. Carlos Alcaraz had a cleaner night. He took care of Karen Khachanov 6-4, 7-6(5) after Khachanov served for the second set at 6-5. Alcaraz broke to love and won the tiebreak 7-5. Controlled, efficient, the kind of match you want from a top seed. Both men advance and keep their Vienna title hopes alive. The draw opens up nicely from here. Who do you see winning the Vienna title this week? --- ### Image Post Caption Zverev saved four match points last night. Alcaraz did what Alcaraz does. Vienna's still standing — for now. 🎾 #ATPVienna

### Long-Form Post The 2026 WTA Finals are now 11 days away — and the draw should be announced around November 5. Indian Wells Tennis Garden, November 8–15. Round-robin format: two groups of four, with the top two from each group advancing to the semifinals. The full eight-player field: Elena Rybakina (No. 1, defending champion), Aryna Sabalenka, Mirra Andreeva, Jessica Pegula, Coco Gauff, Elina Svitolina, Linda Noskova, and Karolína Muchová. Rybakina arrives as the defending champion and the No. 1 seed. The draw's defining question: does she land in the same group as Sabalenka, or are they on opposite sides? A same-group draw would mean one of the two best players in the world goes out before the semifinals. This is the first WTA Finals at Indian Wells — it was relocated from Riyadh following the 2026 geopolitical situation.
