Zverev Saves Four Match Points, Alcaraz Escapes: Vienna Day 2

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | October 28, 2026

Tuesday in Vienna produced two dramatically different matches from the top two seeds, and both survived. Alexander Zverev needed more than three hours and four saved match points to beat wildcard Martin Landaluce 7-5, 3-6, 7-6. Carlos Alcaraz was cooler, defeating Karen Khachanov 6-4, 7-6(5) after defusing a moment that could have changed the match entirely. Both advance to the second round. Zverev is now one quarterfinal appearance away from mathematically locking up the ATP Year-End No. 1 ranking.

Zverev vs. Landaluce: The Thriller

Martin Landaluce arrived at Vienna as a wildcard ranked around No. 67 in the world, and he nearly ended the title defense before it started. He took the second set after Zverev won the first 7-5, then pushed the decisive third set into a tiebreak that he came within two points of winning four separate times.

Zverev saved all four match points and won the tiebreak to advance. The match was his 63rd win of the 2026 season — still the ATP-leading total. That he had to fight this hard for it against a wildcard will be noted. Zverev has been the clear favorite to end the year at No. 1 since Jannik Sinner withdrew for the season with a right knee injury, but performances like Tuesday's are a reminder that the ranking and the form are not always the same thing.

The year-end No. 1 question is straightforward from here: Zverev needs one quarterfinal appearance at Vienna to close the gap mathematically. After Tuesday, he is positioned to get there.

Alcaraz vs. Khachanov: Control

Carlos Alcaraz entered with a 6-0 head-to-head record against Karen Khachanov, and while Tuesday's match was not a rout, he stayed in control when it mattered. Alcaraz took the first set 6-4, and the second set turned on a single game.

Khachanov served for the second set at 6-5. Alcaraz broke to love — four points, no debate — and then won the tiebreak 7-5 to take the match. The H2H is now 7-0. Alcaraz advances to the second round and keeps his Vienna draw looking manageable.

The contrast between the two matches is instructive. Zverev is fighting for every point and winning ugly. Alcaraz is finding the right moment to end it cleanly. Both approaches get the job done, and at this stage of the indoor season both men will take the win.

Vienna Draw Context

With both top seeds through, the Vienna draw is developing as expected at the top. The tournament runs through November 1, with the final on Sunday. Zverev's path to the quarterfinal will determine whether the year-end No. 1 is settled this week or carries over to Paris.

Both players will have an extra day of rest before their next matches later in the week. Given the length of Zverev's Tuesday match, that rest will be useful.

What's Next

Zverev and Alcaraz both return to action later this week in the Vienna second round. For Zverev, a quarterfinal appearance locks up the ATP Year-End No. 1 ranking, ending one of the season's longest-running storylines. Alcaraz will look to continue building toward a deep run at Vienna, where he entered as the second seed. The full results from Wednesday at both Vienna and Basel will clarify the draw picture across the indoor season's final stretch.
