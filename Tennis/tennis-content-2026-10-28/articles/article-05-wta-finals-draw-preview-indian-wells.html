WTA Finals Draw Expected This Week: Eight Players, Indian Wells, November 8

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | October 28, 2026

The 2026 WTA Finals open November 8 at the Indian Wells Tennis Garden, and the draw is expected to be announced around November 5. All eight players are confirmed. The field is strong and reasonably deep, and the draw composition — particularly whether the top two seeds land in the same group — will shape the event before it starts.

The Field

Elena Rybakina enters as the No. 1 seed and the defending champion. She won the Ningbo Open earlier this month, defeating Leylah Fernandez in the final for her 15th career title and her first as the world's top-ranked player. She arrives at Indian Wells in form and with the structural advantage that comes with being last year's winner.

Aryna Sabalenka is seeded second. The two players have been the defining rivalry of the 2026 women's season, and a draw that separates them into different groups would mean they could only meet in the final — while a same-group draw would eliminate one of them before the semifinals.

The remaining six players are Mirra Andreeva, Jessica Pegula, Coco Gauff, Elina Svitolina, Linda Noskova, and Karolína Muchová. Muchová secured the eighth spot after Marta Kostyuk retired from her match at Ningbo earlier in the month, closing out a Race that had been contested until the final days of the qualifying window.

Format and Venue

The WTA Finals uses a round-robin format: two groups of four players, with each player facing the other three in her group once. The top two finishers from each group advance to the semifinals. The format rewards consistency and penalizes early struggles — a player who drops their first match is not eliminated but carries a deficit into every subsequent match.

This is the first WTA Finals held at the Indian Wells Tennis Garden. The event was relocated from Riyadh, where it had been held in recent years, following the 2026 geopolitical situation. Indian Wells is one of the premier tennis facilities in the world and hosted a record combined ATP-WTA event earlier in the 2026 season.

The Draw Question

In a round-robin format, the draw matters more than it does in a standard knockout bracket. The group placement determines not just potential opponents but the scheduling of matches and, critically, whether the two best players in the field meet before the semifinals.

If Rybakina and Sabalenka are drawn into the same group, one of them will not reach the final — they will face each other in the group stage, and the loser of that match will need to win their remaining group matches to advance, or could be eliminated. If they are in separate groups, a final between them is the likely outcome if both perform to their seedings.

Below the top two, the seedings matter less in predicting outcomes. Andreeva, Gauff, and Pegula all have the capacity to take a set — or a match — off any player in the field on the right day.

What's Next

The draw announcement is expected around November 5. Once the groups are confirmed, preview coverage will focus on which group has the tougher path and whether the defending champion Rybakina and Sabalenka are on a collision course for the final or facing each other in the round-robin stage. The tournament opens November 8 with the first round-robin matches, and the semifinals are scheduled for November 13 with the final on November 15.
