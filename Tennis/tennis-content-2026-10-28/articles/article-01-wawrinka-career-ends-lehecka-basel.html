Wawrinka's Career Is Over: Lehečká Wins 7-5, 7-6(2) in Basel

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | October 28, 2026

It ended in a tiebreak. Jiří Lehečká defeated Stan Wawrinka 7-5, 7-6(2) in the first round of the 2026 Swiss Indoors on Tuesday evening, and Stan Wawrinka's professional tennis career is over. He walked off the Basel court for the last time at 41 years old, with a crowd that had given him everything it had and a scoreline that was, all things considered, honest.

How the Match Went

Lehečká, the eighth seed and No. 21 in the world, was the better player from the start. The second-serve statistics told the story plainly: Lehečká won 17 of 20 second-serve points, an 85 percent conversion rate. Wawrinka won 17 of 39, a 44 percent rate. Against a player who is 24 years old and still climbing, that differential is difficult to overcome even on good nights. This was not one of Wawrinka's good nights, and he probably knew it wouldn't be.

The first set went to Lehečká 7-5. The second set tightened, and Wawrinka stayed in it long enough to force a tiebreak. At 2-6 down in the tiebreak, Wawrinka served. Lehečká won the next five points from 2-2. The match was over.

Lehečká advances to the second round at Basel. Wawrinka will not play another professional match.

The Career He Leaves Behind

Stan Wawrinka retires with 590 wins from 985 matches, 16 ATP titles, and three Grand Slams. The titles came on three different surfaces: the 2014 Australian Open on hard court, the 2015 Roland Garros on clay, and the 2016 US Open on hard court. He reached a career-high ranking of No. 3 in 2014. He and Roger Federer won the Olympic doubles gold medal at the 2008 Beijing Games.

None of those numbers capture the particular quality of what Wawrinka's career meant. His Grand Slam finals were not incremental wins — they were performances. The 2015 Roland Garros final against Novak Djokovic, played when Djokovic was the best player in the world in perhaps the best stretch of Djokovic's career, produced one of the finest singles matches in the history of the tournament. The 2016 US Open final against Andy Murray was a destruction. The backhand, a single-handed shot that Wawrinka hit at a pace and angle that no one else in the modern era could replicate, is already part of the permanent record of what the game has looked like at its highest level.

He also survived. Career-threatening knee injuries, a difficult divorce, years when the question was not whether he would win another Slam but whether he would compete at all. He kept competing, because he wanted to play tennis, and because stubbornness at that level of professional sport is itself a form of achievement.

Why Basel

Wawrinka won his first ATP title in Basel in 2006, when he was 21 and the tournament was still, in large part, defined by Federer's presence. Winning here that year was a signal. He won again in 2015 and 2016, giving him three titles at the event across a decade. Ending the career in Basel was not incidental. It was chosen, and the Swiss Indoors gave him the tribute night he deserved on Monday before the real match Tuesday.

Novak Djokovic was among those who attended Monday's ceremony, having been personally asked. That Djokovic made the trip — he is also playing at Basel this week — said something about what the tennis world thinks of what Wawrinka built over 20 years.

What's Next

Jiří Lehečká advances to the second round at Basel and will face his next opponent on Thursday. For Wawrinka, the next chapter is whatever comes after professional tennis — the first time since his early twenties that his schedule will not be organized around match draws. He leaves the sport with a resume that occupies a specific and well-earned place in tennis history: three Slam titles, an Olympic gold, and a backhand that will be shown in highlight packages for as long as people talk about the sport. Basel gave him a proper goodbye. The rest belongs to him.
