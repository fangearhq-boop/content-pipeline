ATP Race to Turin: The Standings After Vienna and Basel Day 2

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | October 28, 2026

After two days of play at the Erste Bank Open in Vienna and the Swiss Indoors in Basel, the Race to Turin standings remain tight in the contested zone — and the picture will not clear meaningfully until the Paris Masters begins October 31. Here is where things stand, with the caveat that standings figures at this stage of the season shift with every round played.

The Confirmed Qualifiers

Four players have already secured their spots at the ATP Finals in Turin (November 15–22). Alexander Zverev leads the overall Race at approximately 9,150 points and will lock up the year-end No. 1 ranking with a quarterfinal appearance at Vienna this week. Carlos Alcaraz and Ben Shelton are also confirmed. Jannik Sinner is confirmed as well despite ending his season early with a right knee injury — the points he accumulated before withdrawing are sufficient.

That leaves five spots for seven or more serious contenders.

The Contested Zone

As of approximate standings from October 27, the five through eleven spots break down as follows: Daniil Medvedev sits fifth at around 3,660 points. Giulio Cobolli is immediately behind at approximately 3,640 — a gap of just 20 points between them. Arthur Fils is seventh at around 3,475, with Frances Tiafoe eighth at approximately 3,380.

The effective cutoff runs through Roberto Bautista Agut Jódar in ninth at roughly 3,159 points — the last spot before the gap widens to players who would need significant results just to reach the top eight. Alex de Minaur sits tenth at approximately 2,880 points, and Novak Djokovic is eleventh at around 2,840.

The difference between fifth place and eleventh place is approximately 820 points. In a week where Vienna and Basel each offer 500 points to the champion, that gap is closeable in theory — but requires reaching a final, which most players do not.

Paris Is the Variable

The Paris Rolex Masters begins October 31 at the Plenaille Arena and runs through November 8 — the last ATP 1000 event of the season. It offers 1,000 Race points to the champion, with substantial points available at every stage. A deep Paris run can move a player from ninth into the qualifying zone; a first-round exit in Paris while a competitor advances to the final can flip positions dramatically.

Zverev is confirmed No. 1 regardless of what happens in Paris. Every other player from second downward has something at stake in the French capital. Medvedev, who has won Paris before, will be a significant factor in how the bubble resolves. Djokovic, who also has a strong Paris history, is further back but not out of range if he strings results together this week and next.

What Vienna and Basel Contribute

Each event this week offers 45 points per win in the early rounds, building to 250 for a finalist and 500 for the champion. Players currently on the bubble — Tiafoe, de Minaur, Djokovic — are still alive in their respective draws and can move the needle. The standings after this week will set the stage for the Paris entry list, which will determine seedings and draw positions for the final major opportunity of the season.

What's Next

Vienna and Basel both reach their second rounds Thursday and Friday, with quarterfinals set for the weekend. Paris entries close shortly after Vienna and Basel conclude, and the draw for the Rolex Masters will be released by the end of next week. For players outside the top eight in the Race, every match between now and the Paris final on November 8 is consequential. The ATP Finals field will be complete on the last day of Paris.
