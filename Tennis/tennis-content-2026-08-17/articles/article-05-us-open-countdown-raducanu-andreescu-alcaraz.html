US Open 2026: Raducanu and Andreescu Out, Alcaraz Eyes Winston-Salem Return

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | August 17, 2026

The US Open main draw is 13 days away. The injury news arriving this week has added two former champions to the withdrawal list and raised new questions about Carlos Alcaraz's preparation for defending his title. It is a tournament that will begin without its defending women's 2021 champion, without the 2019 women's champion, and with its defending men's champion potentially playing his first competitive match of the summer just one week before the main draw begins.

Raducanu OUT With Stress Fracture

Emma Raducanu (GBR) will not compete at the 2026 US Open. The former champion — who produced one of the most improbable Grand Slam runs in history to win the 2021 title as a qualifier — has been ruled out with a stress fracture in her right lower leg, sustained during the grass-court season. The injury ends what had been a difficult 2026 campaign for Raducanu, who had already withdrawn from Cincinnati.

Raducanu's US Open story carries particular weight. She won the title in 2021 without dropping a set across 10 matches, as a wildcard ranked outside the top 150. The arc of her career since — injuries, form inconsistency, high expectations — has been one of the compelling stories of the women's game. Her absence from Flushing Meadows this year removes one of the draw's most commercially significant names.

Andreescu Also Out

Bianca Andreescu (CAN), who won the 2019 US Open title over Serena Williams in the final, has also withdrawn — this time with a stress fracture in her back. The Canadian had already pulled out of Cincinnati. Her absence continues a pattern of injury disruptions that have prevented Andreescu from recapturing the form that produced her breakthrough title.

Alcaraz's Dilemma: Winston-Salem or Go Cold Into the US Open?

The most consequential US Open fitness story remains Carlos Alcaraz (ESP). The defending champion has not played a competitive match since the Barcelona Open in April — a four-month absence caused by wrist tenosynovitis. He withdrew from Cincinnati and has been gradually returning to training, increasing intensity from strength work to full practice sessions in recent weeks.

His team is reportedly eyeing the Winston-Salem Open (ATP 250, beginning August 23) as a vehicle for match practice ahead of the US Open main draw on August 30. If Alcaraz does play Winston-Salem, he would arrive in New York having competed for just one week — a significant gamble for a defending Grand Slam champion. If he does not, he would face the year's final major having not played a match in nearly five months.

Neither option is comfortable. But the signs from his team are described as cautiously positive — training is progressing, and the wrist issue has not worsened. The competitive Alcaraz, not just the training-court version, will be the determining factor. At 23, with the title to defend and a home crowd at Arthur Ashe Stadium that expects greatness, the question of his fitness is the most important single issue ahead of the US Open.

Sinner: On Track

The brighter news comes from Jannik Sinner's camp. The world No. 1 — who withdrew from Cincinnati with a knee issue — completed a precautionary check-up, and sources describe the situation as stable. Sinner is targeting the US Open and there is no indication of a worsening injury. He has not played since Wimbledon, but his preparation appears on track.

What's Next

US Open Fan Week begins August 23. The draw ceremony is August 27, at which point seedings and brackets will be confirmed. The main draw begins August 30. The women's final is September 12; the men's final, September 13. With Raducanu and Andreescu out, Iga Swiatek — on a seven-match win streak coming off her Toronto title — leads the women's field into New York. On the men's side, Zverev, currently the only top contender with substantial match practice on hard courts, will enter as a frontrunner. The US Open's final shape depends heavily on what Alcaraz and Sinner decide in the next 72 hours.
