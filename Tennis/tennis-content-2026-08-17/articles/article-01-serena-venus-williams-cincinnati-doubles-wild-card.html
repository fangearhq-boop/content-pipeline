Serena and Venus Williams Accept Cincinnati Doubles Wild Card: First Match Together Since 2022 US Open

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | August 17, 2026

They are back together. Serena Williams and Venus Williams have accepted a wild card into the 2026 Cincinnati Open women's doubles draw, making it their first appearance on the same side of the net since the 2022 US Open. The announcement generated more attention than almost any match result of the tournament so far.

Serena, now 44, is continuing a comeback that has been measured in cautious steps. She had been due to partner Venus in doubles at Wimbledon 2026 before a right knee issue forced her withdrawal. Her decision to accept the Cincinnati wild card is a statement: the comeback is not finished. Venus, who also received a singles wild card for the tournament, had her own difficult singles campaign in 2026 — but the doubles invitation carries a different weight entirely.

What Makes This Moment Different

The Williams sisters built one of the most extraordinary partnerships in the history of professional tennis. Together they won 14 Grand Slam doubles titles and three Olympic gold medals. On the individual side, they combined for 30 Grand Slam singles trophies. There has never been a sibling duo in the sport's history that achieved what they achieved together — and apart.

Since the 2022 US Open, they had not shared a doubles court. Serena's retirement announcement and the gradual winding down of her competitive career meant that chapter felt closed. But tennis has a way of reopening chapters that seemed finished, and Cincinnati 2026 is the latest example.

The context makes this moment even more layered. Venus has continued competing through 2026 at 46 years of age, accepting wild cards and facing younger opponents with something still to prove. Her singles results this year have been difficult. But Thursday night in Cincinnati, none of that matters. What matters is the two of them standing on a court again, together.

Serena's Comeback Trajectory

Serena Williams announced in 2022 that she was stepping away from tennis after the US Open, though she was careful with her language — describing it as an "evolution" rather than a retirement. In the years since, her appearances have been limited and each one has been freighted with meaning. The Wimbledon doubles withdrawal with Venus was a setback. Cincinnati is the response.

Competing at 44 on an outdoor hard court in Cincinnati in August heat represents a genuine physical undertaking. The fact that she chose to come back here, with her sister, in a doubles draw that allows her to test herself without the full weight of a singles match, suggests a player managing her body carefully while refusing to let the competitive part of herself switch off entirely.

What's Next

The Williams sisters' doubles match at the Cincinnati Open is scheduled for Monday evening. Whatever the result, the crowd reception will be something that the Lindner Family Tennis Center has not generated this year. Venus remains in the singles draw as well. For Serena, the doubles appearance is both a competitive moment and a signal — the story is not finished yet. Tennis is better when it is telling both sides of it.
