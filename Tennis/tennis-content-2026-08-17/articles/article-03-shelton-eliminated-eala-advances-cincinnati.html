Shelton Eliminated in R2 by Faria; Eala Marches Into Cincinnati R3

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | August 17, 2026

Ben Shelton was the reigning Canadian Masters champion. He had just won back-to-back Montreal titles — joining an exclusive club of players to defend that title in consecutive years — and arrived in Cincinnati as the No. 5 seed with significant momentum. On Saturday, Jaime Faria (POR) ended all of that. The Portuguese player beat Shelton 6-4, 6-4 in the second round — a clean, composed victory that sends a message about just how open this Cincinnati draw has become.

On the women's side, Alexandra Eala (PHI) is writing a very different kind of story. The DC Open champion — who won her title in Washington just two weeks ago — beat Elena-Gabriela Ruse (ROU) 6-1, 3-0 (retired) on Sunday to reach the third round. Eala now holds a 22-9 record on hard courts in 2026. She faces Amanda Anisimova (USA, approximately world No. 10) in their first career meeting on Monday night.

Shelton's Cincinnati Exit

There was no injury excuse available for Shelton's second-round loss. Faria was better: aggressive from the back of the court, holding his serve cleanly and breaking when it mattered in both sets. The final score, 6-4, 6-4, reflects a match where the upset was earned rather than gifted.

Shelton entered the week carrying the weight of exceptional recent results. His Montreal title — won without dropping a set across five matches, culminating in a straight-sets final win over Nakashima — had positioned him as one of the sport's most compelling figures heading into the hard-court swing. Cincinnati was supposed to extend the story. Instead, it ended it early.

This is not unusual. The transition from one Masters event to the next requires a reset that not every champion manages immediately. Shelton now turns his attention to the US Open, which begins August 30. He has three weeks to refocus, and the form he showed in Montreal makes him a genuine contender in New York regardless of Saturday's result.

Eala's Hard-Court Run

Alexandra Eala's 2026 season has been a sustained, impressive argument for her place among the best hard-court players in women's tennis. The Philippine star's DC Open win — defeating Pegula in the final — was the headline moment. But it was built on consistent results: 22 hard-court wins this year, nine losses, a .710 winning percentage on the surface.

Sunday's R2 win over Ruse, completed 6-1 before Ruse retired with injury at 3-0 in the second set, was efficient rather than spectacular. The third round presents a much stiffer challenge. Anisimova is a former junior Grand Slam champion who hits the ball as hard as anyone on tour. This is the kind of match that will reveal whether Eala's Cincinnati run has serious depth or whether the draw's top players will eventually stop her progress.

The first meeting between these two players carries its own uncertainty — neither has data on how the other responds under pressure in a direct encounter. Monday night will be compelling.

What's Next

Eala vs Anisimova is scheduled for Monday evening at the Cincinnati Open. For Shelton, the US Open looms as the primary focus — a chance to arrive at Arthur Ashe Stadium as a Grand Slam contender for the first time with legitimate title form behind him. Saturday's loss in Cincinnati does not change that picture fundamentally, but it does remind everyone that hard courts in August produce upsets in both directions — and that nothing in tennis is guaranteed.
