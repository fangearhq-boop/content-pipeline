### STORY 2: Swiatek Crushes Arango 6-3, 6-0

**Long-Form Post**
This is what in-form Iga Swiatek looks like.

On Sunday in Cincinnati, the defending champion opened her title defence by beating Emiliana Arango — the same Colombian who had just eliminated Venus Williams — 6-3, 6-0 in 72 minutes. Seven straight match wins. Five of seven return games won. Seven of eight service games held.

Swiatek won Toronto last week, beating Rybakina 6-2, 6-3 in the final to snap a 10-month title drought. But Sunday was a different kind of statement. She didn't just win; she made it look routine.

The four-time Roland Garros champion and two-time US Open champion now faces Maria Sakkari (GRE) in R3, leading the H2H 5-4. Sakkari is a dangerous player on any given day. It's exactly the kind of test that separates players in form from players who just got a good draw.

Swiatek is the defending Cincinnati champion. She is seven wins into a streak. She has the best form of any player in the world right now, men's or women's draw.

Is she the US Open favourite?

### STORY 3: Shelton Out; Eala Into R3

**Long-Form Post**
Ben Shelton walked into Cincinnati as the reigning Canadian Masters champion — having won back-to-back titles in Montreal last week. He was the No. 5 seed. He was supposed to be the American story of Cincinnati.

Instead, Jaime Faria (POR) sent him home. 6-4, 6-4. No drama, no injury excuse, just a clean loss in the second round.

It's a reminder that tennis doesn't hand out continuation stories. You have to earn every match on every surface, and Saturday, Faria was better.

On the other side of the coin: Alexandra Eala is very much still earning her moments. The Philippine star — who won the DC Open title just two weeks ago — beat Elena-Gabriela Ruse 6-1, 3-0 (retired) in R2 to move into the third round. She now has a 22-9 record on hard courts in 2026. Tonight she faces Amanda Anisimova (USA, approximately No. 10 in the world) in their first career meeting. This is the kind of stage that defines players.

Eala. Cincinnati. Under the lights. Can she keep this run going?

### STORY 5: US Open Countdown — Withdrawals Mount

**Long-Form Post**
The US Open withdrawal list is growing — and two former champions are already out.

Emma Raducanu, who won the 2021 US Open as a qualifier in one of the most stunning runs in the tournament's history, will not be in New York. A stress fracture in her right lower leg — sustained during the grass-court season — ends her 2026 campaign before the year's final Grand Slam. Bianca Andreescu, who lifted the 2019 trophy, is also out with a stress fracture, this time in her back.

For Carlos Alcaraz, the picture is more hopeful but still uncertain. The defending US Open champion hasn't played a competitive match since April — a four-month absence due to wrist tenosynovitis. His team is reportedly eyeing the Winston-Salem Open (ATP 250, starting August 23) as a way to get competitive match practice before the main draw on August 30. That would mean one week of tennis before defending the biggest title of his career.

Jannik Sinner remains on track. The world No. 1 completed a precautionary knee check-up and sources say the situation has not worsened. He's targeting New York.

US Open Fan Week begins August 23. Draw ceremony August 27. Main draw August 30.
