### STORY 1: Eala Dethrones Fernandez

#### Long-Form Post — 8:00 AM ET

Alex Eala is the story of the summer.

She arrived at Wimbledon in July ranked outside the top 30, defeated Elena Rybakina and Iga Swiatek, and reached the fourth round to become the first Filipino player in Wimbledon history to get that far. The tennis world sat up and took notice. She won the WTA Star of the Swing award for her grass-court season.

Then she brought that same energy to Washington.

In Round 1 on Tuesday, Eala was a set down against former Olympic champion Qinwen Zheng. She won 4-6, 6-4, 6-1. On Wednesday, she faced Leylah Fernandez — the defending DC Open champion, seeded No. 7, a player who had opened her title defense with a 6-1, 6-4 dismantling just two days earlier. Eala won the first set 6-2. She fell behind 5-1 in the second set. Then she came back, forced a tiebreak, and won it 7-1. Match to Eala: 6-2, 7-6.

She is now in the Washington quarterfinals, scheduled for Friday, as an unseeded player. She's beaten two high-profile opponents in back-to-back days. And she's ranked No. 28 in the world, a career high she set in mid-July.

What are you expecting from Eala in the quarterfinals?

### STORY 2: DC Open ATP Results

#### Long-Form Post — 10:30 AM ET

The DC Open men's draw is coming into focus, and the storylines are stacking up.

On Wednesday, defending champion Alex de Minaur finally stepped onto the court and took care of business. He beat Stefanos Tsitsipas 6-4, 3-6, 6-3 — notable not just for the win but for what it erased: de Minaur had been 1-12 against Tsitsipas entering the match. The defending champion needed to flip that trend to open his title run, and he did.

Thursday produced more results. Ben Shelton, the second seed, defeated Ugo Humbert 6-4, 4-6, 6-4 in an all-lefty clash that went the distance. Seventh seed Jakub Mensik won a tight three-setter over Brandon Nakashima 6-2, 6-7(5), 6-3. Taylor Fritz, who has now reached his third straight Washington quarterfinal, beat Kamil Majchrzak 6-3, 6-4 and will face Alex Michelsen next.

The other side of the ledger is harder for American tennis. Learner Tien, seeded fifth and entering with a career-best ranking of No. 16, lost to 38-year-old Adrian Mannarino in the first round. Arthur Fils, the eighth seed, was beaten by 19-year-old Spaniard Rafael Jodar — and Jodar then came back on Wednesday and beat Kei Nishikori too, making a compelling case for being the tournament's dark horse.

Quarterfinals are Friday. The top half of the draw is holding up. The bottom half is more complicated.

Which QF matchup are you most interested in seeing?

### STORY 3: DC Open WTA Upsets

#### Long-Form Post — 12:30 PM ET

The DC Open WTA draw is no longer recognizable.

In the round of 16, two of the four top seeds were eliminated. Polina Kudermetova dismantled second seed Elina Svitolina in a scoreline that tells the whole story: 5-7, 7-6(4), 6-0. After splitting the first two sets, Kudermetova won the third without losing a game. Anna Kalinskaya was even more emphatic, taking out fourth seed Diana Shnaider 6-4, 6-1 — a result that looked more like a warmup than a competitive match.

Top seed Jessica Pegula and third seed Naomi Osaka both survived. Pegula beat Magdalena Frech 6-4, 6-3. Osaka needed three sets against Ashlyn Krueger — 3-6, 6-2, 6-2 — before coming through. Emma Navarro was dominant, beating Cocciaretto 6-0, 7-5.

And in Round 1, a result that deserved more attention: Venus Williams was beaten by Anastasia Potapova 6-3, 6-3. It was Potapova's first-ever win in Washington. For Williams, it extended a losing streak to 12 matches — she has yet to win a match anywhere in 2026. That's a number that sits uncomfortably next to everything she's meant to the sport.

The quarterfinals are Friday. Pegula, Osaka, Eala, Kalinskaya, Kudermetova, Navarro — it's a wide-open draw.

Which player do you see taking it from here?

### STORY 4: Los Cabos — Coleman Wong Upsets Lehecka

#### Long-Form Post — 3:00 PM ET

While Washington is getting most of the headlines this week, Los Cabos has its own story worth telling.

Coleman Wong of Hong Kong walked onto the court Wednesday as a significant underdog against Jiri Lehecka — the tournament's top seed, ranked in the ATP's top 15, a hard-court specialist who has had a strong 2026. After losing the first set 1-6, Wong leveled at a set apiece by winning the second 6-3, then closed it out 6-4 in the third. The ATP Tour described it as the biggest win of Wong's career by ranking. He's now closing in on his top-100 debut.

It's the kind of result that's easy to scroll past in a week loaded with marquee events, but it matters. Wong coming from behind to beat the best player in the draw is the tournament's definitive moment so far.

Cameron Norrie, seeded fifth, also advanced — defeating James Duckworth 4-6, 6-3, 6-4 in a match that required him to find something in the second and third sets after dropping the opener. Norrie is finding form at the right time of the season. Denis Shapovalov, the eighth seed and defending champion, opened with a win as well, going 5-0 at this tournament for his career.

The Los Cabos quarterfinal picture takes shape through today. Tournament runs through August 1.

Did you know Hong Kong's Coleman Wong was in the Los Cabos draw?

### STORY 5: Canadian Open Preview

#### Long-Form Post — 6:00 PM ET

The Canadian Open draws land on Friday, and the field has some notable gaps.

Jannik Sinner — the world No. 1, the reigning Wimbledon champion, the player who won six titles in 2026 — has withdrawn from ATP Montreal. He cited rest ahead of Cincinnati. The tournament director did not take it quietly, publicly criticizing the ATP over the timing and the impact on the event. The reaction across tennis circles was predictably sharp. Sinner joins Novak Djokovic, Carlos Alcaraz, and Jack Draper as significant names not in the draw — the top three in the world are all absent before a ball is hit.

On the women's side in Toronto, defending champion Victoria Mboko is out with injury, along with Karolina Muchova and Emma Raducanu. The list of missing names has reportedly reached double figures.

What's left is still very much worth watching. On the ATP side, Alexander Zverev is the top seed and arrives fresh from winning Roland-Garros in June and reaching the Wimbledon final. Felix Auger-Aliassime is seeded No. 2 and gets to compete at home in Montreal — FAA's first Canadian Open appearance as a top seed by career ranking. On the WTA side, Aryna Sabalenka leads the field in Toronto, with Elena Rybakina, Jessica Pegula, Coco Gauff, and Wimbledon champion Linda Noskova all in the draw.

Draws are Friday. WTA main draw starts August 2. ATP starts August 4.

Who are you picking for the title in Toronto or Montreal?
