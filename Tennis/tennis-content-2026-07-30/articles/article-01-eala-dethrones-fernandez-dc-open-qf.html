Eala Dethrones Defending Champion Fernandez to Reach DC Open Quarterfinals

By Elena Voss | July 30, 2026

Washington, D.C.

Alexandra Eala has spent the summer redefining what is expected of her. She came into the Wimbledon fortnight in early July ranked outside the top 30 and left it as the first Filipino player to reach the fourth round at the All England Club, having taken down Elena Rybakina and Iga Swiatek along the way. The WTA gave her the Star of the Swing award. The tennis world circled her name. And then she flew to Washington and kept going.

On Wednesday evening at FitzGerald Tennis Center, Eala defeated Leylah Fernandez — the seventh seed and the defending DC Open champion — 6-2, 7-6(1) to advance to the quarterfinals of the Mubadala DC Open. The win required her best qualities: the ability to start fast, the nerve to weather adversity, and the firepower to close under pressure.

The Match

Eala seized control from the opening game. Fernandez, who had dispatched Magda Linette 6-1, 6-4 in her first-round match just two days earlier and looked every bit the defending champion, never found her footing in the first set. Eala moved efficiently, exploited angles on both wings, and closed out the opening set 6-2 in under 40 minutes.

The second set became something else entirely. Fernandez raised her level — she had to — and the crowd-pleasing baseline exchanges swung in her direction. At 5-1 in the second set, with Eala serving and the match seemingly tilting back toward the champion, the logical outcome appeared clear. Fernandez serving for a set to level at one apiece looked to be a formality.

It was not a formality. Eala held serve, broke back, and continued peeling away at Fernandez's lead until the two were level at 6-6. The tiebreak was over quickly — 7-1, Eala — with a cross-court forehand winner sealing it. The match to Eala: 6-2, 7-6(1).

Eala told the WTA Tour: "I've already had that in me." She was referring to something larger than the second set deficit — the resilience that was already visible at Wimbledon when she recovered from tight situations to beat two of the world's top players.

A Two-Day Sweep

Wednesday's win was Eala's second in as many days in Washington. On Tuesday she had faced Qinwen Zheng, the Chinese player who won the Olympic singles gold medal in Paris in 2024, in a match she also won from a set down: 4-6, 6-4, 6-1. Beating Zheng in three sets was already a result. Beating Fernandez the next day to reach the quarterfinals elevated the performance into something that demands a different conversation about where Eala belongs.

She is currently ranked No. 28 in the world — a career high she set in July after Wimbledon. The DC Open draw she entered was built around Jessica Pegula as the top seed, with Svitolina and Osaka further up the bracket. Eala came in unseeded. She now holds a quarterfinal berth.

What's Next

Eala's quarterfinal is scheduled for Friday, July 31. The rest of the draw has been upended this week — No. 2 seed Elina Svitolina and No. 4 seed Diana Shnaider were both eliminated in the round of 16 — which means the path to a final is more open than the original bracket suggested. Pegula and Osaka are still in the draw. But so is Eala, who has given no indication that she considers any opponent a ceiling.

The DC Open continues through August 2. The Canadian Open in Toronto follows, beginning August 2 with the WTA main draw. If Eala's form holds, it will be a summer that gets talked about for a long time.
