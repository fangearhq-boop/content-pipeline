Svitolina, Shnaider Upset in DC Open Round of 16; Venus Williams Loses as Losing Streak Hits 12

By Ryan Calloway | July 30, 2026

Washington, D.C.

The DC Open women's draw has been reshaped. Two of the tournament's four top seeds are out after the round of 16, and the quarterfinal bracket heading into Friday has a different look than anyone projected at the start of the week. The upsets came from two players who had been building momentum quietly — Anna Kalinskaya and Polina Kudermetova — and the results were decisive enough to make clear these were not lucky escapes.

Kalinskaya and Kudermetova Shake the Draw

Anna Kalinskaya had come into her match with fourth seed Diana Shnaider with momentum. She had beaten Daria Kasatkina 6-4, 6-1 in Round 1, playing with the kind of clean, aggressive tennis that can derail anyone's expectations. Against Shnaider she produced the same result: 6-4, 6-1, a scoreline that suggests less a tough match and more a systematic dismantling of a player ranked inside the top 10.

Polina Kudermetova's win over second seed Elina Svitolina was more theatrical. The opening two sets were tense — Svitolina won the first 7-5, Kudermetova leveled by winning the second in a tiebreak, 7-6(4). Then the third set happened: 6-0. In seven games, Svitolina won zero. For the second seed to be comprehensively ousted in the final frame after splitting the opening two, the completeness of the final-set loss is the story.

Pegula and Osaka Hold Serve

Not every high seed collapsed. Jessica Pegula, the top seed, moved through without drama. She beat Magdalena Frech 6-4, 6-3 — straight sets, efficient, no service breaks gifted back. Naomi Osaka, seeded third, was tested more thoroughly. She lost the opening set to Ashlyn Krueger 3-6 before reversing the match 6-2, 6-2 in the remaining two sets to advance to the quarterfinals.

Emma Navarro had the cleanest result of the round, beating Elisabetta Cocciaretto 6-0, 7-5. The opening set required no explanation. The second was tighter, but Navarro won it without difficulty once Cocciaretto made a brief run.

Venus Williams and a Losing Streak That Keeps Growing

In Round 1, before any of the seed drama, Anastasia Potapova beat Venus Williams 6-3, 6-3. Potapova had never won a match at the DC Open before Wednesday — the win broke that duck. For Williams, the result extended a losing streak to 12 consecutive matches. She has not won a match in 2026. Her last win came at this very tournament in 2025.

These numbers exist in a particular kind of tension when measured against everything Williams has done in the sport over the previous three decades. She competed. She held her service games. The opponent simply played better in every phase. But 12 consecutive losses and a winless 2026 season, for a player who has reached more Grand Slam finals than anyone in the Open Era, is a number that carries weight regardless of framing.

What's Next

The DC Open women's quarterfinals are Friday, July 31. From the unseeded players, Alexandra Eala — who defeated Fernandez in Round 2 — is in the draw and represents the tournament's most compelling story. Pegula, Osaka, Kalinskaya, Kudermetova, and Navarro round out the field. The semifinals follow Saturday, August 1.
