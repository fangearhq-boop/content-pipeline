Canadian Open Draws Friday With Three Top ATP Stars Absent; Sinner Withdrawal Draws Criticism

By Marcus Cole | July 30, 2026

Montreal / Toronto

The Canadian Open draw ceremonies are Friday, and the announcement boards in Montreal and Toronto will carry notable absences. Jannik Sinner — the world No. 1, the reigning Wimbledon champion, the player who has won six titles in 2026 — will not be competing at IGA Stadium this year. Neither will Novak Djokovic. Neither will Carlos Alcaraz. The top three in men's tennis are all missing from a Masters 1000 event that was supposed to be one of the highlights of the North American hard court swing.

Sinner's withdrawal drew the most immediate reaction. He cited the need for rest before Cincinnati, and the Canadian Open tournament director made his displeasure public, criticizing the ATP's handling of the withdrawal and its timing. The reaction across tennis circles was predictably sharper than the usual diplomatic framing these situations receive.

A Field Reshaped

The men's ATP draw at IGA Stadium in Montreal will be led by Alexander Zverev as the No. 1 seed. Zverev arrived at this point in the season having won the 2026 Roland-Garros title and reached the Wimbledon final — a summer that has confirmed what many suspected about his consistency on every surface. He is a legitimate No. 1-seed replacement and a credible favorite for the title even with a full field.

More compelling from a storyline perspective is the No. 2 seed: Felix Auger-Aliassime, who enters the tournament at a career-high ranking of No. 4, playing in the city where his Canadian following is strongest. FAA reached the quarterfinals at both Roland-Garros and Wimbledon this summer — results that confirmed the ascent that had been anticipated since his early career. Being the top Canadian player at the Canadian Open, seeded second in the absence of the world's top three, is the kind of moment that either launches a tournament run or adds to the pressure narrative. The draw on Friday will determine which bracket challenges he faces.

The Montreal field's other notable additions include Jack Draper, who is attempting to re-enter the tour after a year lost to injury, and a strong European contingent filling the seeds through eight. Three French players are among the seeds, giving the tournament a different flavor than when Sinner or Alcaraz dominate the entry list.

WTA Toronto

The women's field in Toronto has its own set of missing names. Victoria Mboko, who won last year's title and had been positioned as a returning champion storyline, withdrew with injury sustained during the grass-court season. Karolina Muchova, who reached the Wimbledon final in 2026, is also absent. Emma Raducanu will not be in the draw. The full withdrawal count has reportedly reached double figures.

What remains is still a strong field. Aryna Sabalenka is the No. 1 seed — the world's top-ranked player entering on the form that carried her to multiple titles this year. Elena Rybakina is No. 2. Jessica Pegula, currently in the DC Open quarterfinals in Washington, is the No. 3 seed. Coco Gauff is No. 4. And Linda Noskova — the Wimbledon champion who claimed the title at the All England Club as the youngest champion in years — enters as the No. 6 seed, carrying the momentum of a Grand Slam title into her first hard court Masters of the season.

What's Next

The draw ceremonies in Toronto and Montreal are Friday, July 31. The WTA main draw at Sobeys Stadium in Toronto begins August 2. The ATP main draw at IGA Stadium in Montreal begins August 4, with finals in both cities on August 13. The full draw pictures will shift the Canadian Open conversation from who's missing to who the favorites are — and on both sides of the bracket, there are genuine contenders to watch.
