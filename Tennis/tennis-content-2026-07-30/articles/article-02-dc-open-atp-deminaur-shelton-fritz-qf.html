De Minaur Snaps Tsitsipas Trend; Shelton, Mensik, Fritz Reach DC Open Quarterfinals

By Marcus Cole | July 30, 2026

Washington, D.C.

Over three days of action at FitzGerald Tennis Center, the DC Open men's draw has largely sorted itself into a familiar shape at the top, with one important addition: the defending champion is through. Alex de Minaur opened his title defense Wednesday by defeating Stefanos Tsitsipas 6-4, 3-6, 6-3 in a match that required him to flip a career head-to-head record that had long worked against him. De Minaur had been 1-12 against Tsitsipas entering the match. He is now 2-12 — but more importantly, he is in the second round with a clear path ahead.

De Minaur vs. Tsitsipas

The first set was controlled and clinical from de Minaur, who won it 6-4 without allowing Tsitsipas to find the baseline rhythm that typically carries the Greek player into trouble for opponents. Tsitsipas adjusted in the second, leveling at a set apiece by winning 6-3. In the third, de Minaur's footwork and counterpunching wore down Tsitsipas' offense and he closed it 6-3.

The ATP noted the historical significance: for a player who has built his career on hard courts and speed, defeating Tsitsipas — one of the most difficult baseline problems in men's tennis — clears an obstacle that had accumulated over years of attempts. The defending champion did it in the first round, before the tournament's pressure fully builds.

Shelton, Mensik, Fritz All Advance

Thursday's action filled in the rest of the quarterfinal picture. Ben Shelton, the second seed, handled Ugo Humbert 6-4, 4-6, 6-4 in an all-lefty, three-set match that went to the wire before the American closed it out. Jakub Mensik, seeded seventh, fought through a difficult three-setter against Brandon Nakashima — 6-2, 6-7(5), 6-3 — rallying after losing the second-set tiebreak to close with authority.

Taylor Fritz reached his third consecutive Washington quarterfinal, defeating Kamil Majchrzak 6-3, 6-4. He will face Alex Michelsen, who beat Adrian Mannarino 6-4, 7-6(3) in Round 2, in the quarterfinals on Friday.

American Men Struggling

Not all American men had the same experience. Learner Tien, the fifth seed who entered Washington at a career-best ranking of No. 16, was beaten in Round 1 by Mannarino — a 38-year-old French left-hander ranked 47th. Mannarino won 73 percent of Tien's second-serve return points, exploiting the American's serve mechanics from the opening game. It was a tactically thorough defeat of a player considered one of the stronger American prospects on the tour.

Arthur Fils, the eighth seed, fell to 19-year-old Spanish qualifier Rafael Jodar — 7-6(5), 6-3 — a result complicated by the recurring back and hip injury that has followed Fils through the season. Jodar then came back Wednesday and beat Kei Nishikori 6-3, 6-2, establishing himself as one of the tournament's emerging stories. A teenager from Spain has beaten two seeded or notable opponents in two days in Washington, and his name is now worth knowing.

What's Next

The men's quarterfinals at the DC Open are scheduled for Friday, July 31. De Minaur has positioned himself well on the top half of the draw. The lower half — with Shelton, Mensik, Fritz, and Michelsen all still in — should produce a compelling QF day. The DC Open runs through August 2. Canadian Open draws are also on Friday; Montreal main draw play begins August 4.
