Paris Masters Day 3: Paul Topples Rune, Berrettini Completes Masters 1000 Set, Fritz Advances

By Marcus Cole

November 4, 2026

Wednesday morning at La Défense Arena delivered a string of meaningful results that reshuffled the Paris Masters draw and complicated the ATP Race to Turin with just days remaining in the season. Tommy Paul, Matteo Berrettini, and Taylor Fritz all advanced, each with a different kind of story attached to their win.

Paul Ends Rune's Paris Dreams

No. 12 seed Tommy Paul beat 2022 Paris champion Holger Rune 7-5, 6-4 in the kind of controlled, clinical performance Paul has been building toward all indoor season. Rune, who lifted the trophy in this building four years ago, never found his best level against an opponent who neutralized his game with heavy groundstrokes and consistent serve.

The result carries significant weight beyond the scorecard. Paul is in a battle for one of the final spots in the ATP Finals in Turin (November 15–22), and beating a former champion in the second round is exactly the kind of result that moves the needle in that Race. Paul will need to keep winning to have a realistic shot, but Wednesday showed he's capable of competing at the level required.

Berrettini Completes His Masters 1000 Career Set

Matteo Berrettini's 6-4, 3-6, 7-6(8) win over No. 14 seed Brandon Nakashima was the kind of match that tests character. After taking the first set comfortably, Berrettini lost his grip in the second, then regrouped in a tiebreak that swung deep into overtime before the Italian closed it out 8 points to 8 on his second chance.

The significance of the result goes beyond Paris: with this win, Berrettini has now recorded at least one victory at every ATP Masters 1000 event in his career, completing the full set. It's a milestone that speaks to his longevity and quality across different surfaces and conditions — no small thing for a player who has dealt with significant injury interruptions over the last few seasons.

Fritz Battles Past Bonzi in Three Sets

Taylor Fritz was pushed harder than expected by French qualifier Benjamin Bonzi, dropping the first set 3-6 before finding his range to win 6-3, 6-4 in the second and third. Fritz is seeded 11th in Paris and also has a stake in the Race to Turin, making every win essential.

Bonzi, playing in front of a supportive home crowd, made Fritz work for every point in the opening set, but the American's superior ball-striking took over once he settled in. Fritz's draw ahead features stiffer competition, but the win keeps him in the hunt for a quarterfinal run that could change his Turin outlook significantly.

The Day Is Far From Over

Wednesday's program at La Défense Arena still has the two most anticipated matches of the tournament on the schedule. World No. 1 Alexander Zverev faces Lorenzo Musetti on Court Central, with Musetti holding a 3-2 head-to-head advantage and coming off a stunning four-match-point save in his first-round win over Hubert Hurkacz. Following that, No. 2 seed Carlos Alcaraz steps in against Alex Michelsen, carrying zero Paris Masters semifinal appearances in eight previous trips to the event.

What's Next

Paul, Berrettini, and Fritz will all return to La Défense Arena for the third round, where the draw begins to thin and the matchups get significantly tougher. For Paul and Fritz, the scorecard in the Race to Turin gets updated after every result — a quarterfinal appearance for either player would substantially boost their chances of reaching Turin. All eyes now turn to Court Central for the Zverev-Musetti and Alcaraz-Michelsen matches later Wednesday.
