Bergs vs. Paul: Can Belgium's Giant-Killer Reach His First Masters Quarterfinal?

By Ryan Calloway

October 14, 2026 — Shanghai, China

On Monday, Zizou Bergs walked off the Shanghai Masters center court having just beaten Carlos Alcaraz — the No. 2 seed, the defending Japan Open champion, and one of the sport's most exciting players. The crowd was stunned. Bergs was not.

"I believed in myself the whole match," the 26-year-old Belgian said afterward. "It's a big win, but there's more tennis to play."

There certainly is. On Wednesday, Bergs faces Tommy Paul in the Round of 16, with a Masters 1000 quarterfinal — something he has never reached — waiting for the winner.

The Alcaraz Win That Rewrote the Draw

Bergs' 7-5, 7-6(4) defeat of Alcaraz in the third round was the defining moment of Shanghai's opening week. Alcaraz had arrived fresh from defending his Japan Open title, full of confidence and ranked among the favorites. Bergs, ranked around No. 39 in the world, was not supposed to cause problems.

He caused every problem. Bergs played an almost flawless match from the back of the court — moving Alcaraz wide, keeping the ball deep, and refusing to give the Spaniard the short ball he craves. In the tiebreak, Bergs was the calmer player. He converted his chance. Alcaraz could only applaud.

Bergs said afterward that Alcaraz complained about the balls during and after the match — a detail that made the internet rounds quickly. Whatever the conditions, Bergs adapted and Alcaraz did not.

Tommy Paul Stands in the Way

Paul is a different challenge. The American is ranked inside the top 15, has been to Masters 1000 semifinals before, and does not go quietly. He beat defending Shanghai champion Valentin Vacherot 6-4, 6-2 in the third round — a comfortable, professional win that signaled Paul is also playing well this week.

Bergs and Paul have limited history on tour. The Belgian's ability to dictate with his forehand and construct points patiently will be tested against an American who is equally comfortable doing the same. These are two similar players in style — both play from the baseline, both rely on consistency, both have strong serves.

The difference, if there is one, will likely be found in the big moments. Bergs showed against Alcaraz that he can play his best when it matters most.

A Quarterfinal Against Medvedev Awaits the Winner

The stakes could not be clearer: win this match, and you face Daniil Medvedev in the quarterfinals. Medvedev advanced Wednesday morning with a 6-4, 7-5 win over Stefanos Tsitsipas — a performance that confirmed the Russian is back to his dangerous best after the Beijing controversy.

For Paul, a Medvedev quarterfinal is the kind of match he has played before. For Bergs, it would be new territory entirely — his first Masters 1000 QF, against a proven major champion. The scale of that opportunity should not be underestimated.

In Bergs' corner, he carries the momentum of the biggest win of his career. Momentum is a real thing in tennis. Ask Alcaraz.

What's Next

Zizou Bergs meets Tommy Paul in the Shanghai Masters Round of 16 on Wednesday, October 14. The winner faces Daniil Medvedev in the quarterfinals on Thursday, October 15. Shanghai's most unlikely hero is still standing — the question is how far this story goes.
