Sabalenka's Year-End No. 2 Ranking Is Under Threat After Andreeva's WTA Finals Stunner

By Elena Voss | November 12, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

Aryna Sabalenka came to Indian Wells ranked second in the world, ahead of Mirra Andreeva by a margin that has been narrowing all season. After Wednesday's 6-4, 6-0 loss to the No. 3 seed in the WTA Finals round robin, that margin is now under real threat.

Andreeva, 2-0 and confirmed for the semifinals, has had the better tournament. Sabalenka is 1-1 and must beat Elina Svitolina on Thursday to avoid elimination. The gap in both their results at this event — and in the form they have shown this week — is now the central subtext of the year-end rankings battle.

What Wednesday Showed

The scoreline was stark, but the nature of the defeat told a fuller story. Sabalenka had the match under control at 4-1 in the first set. She was executing the game plan she has used to beat the best players in the world for two seasons running. Then Andreeva found another gear.

What followed — 11 consecutive games lost — was not simply a momentum shift. It was Andreeva demonstrating that she can physically and tactically outperform the world No. 2 for the better part of an hour on a stage as pressurized as the WTA Finals. That does not happen unless the player doing it is operating at a very high level. The 6-0 bagel in the second set, in particular, is not the kind of result you associate with a close contest.

For Sabalenka, the question going into Thursday is whether that form was situational or representative of a gap that has opened between the top two and three in the WTA rankings this autumn.

The Rankings Picture

Andreeva has been climbing the WTA rankings steadily throughout 2026. She began the year outside the top 10 and her run to this point — including two round-robin wins at the Finals, one over Sabalenka by a 6-0 margin — has put her within striking range of the No. 2 position.

At the WTA Finals, the prize money is structured to reward deep runs significantly. The further Andreeva advances, and the earlier Sabalenka exits, the closer the final year-end rankings gap between them will be. If Sabalenka loses Thursday and Andreeva wins the title, the gap at No. 2 could be decisively closed.

These are the scenarios that the WTA rankings race makes relevant, and they are worth tracking for the remainder of the week in Indian Wells.

Sabalenka Still Has a Path

None of this is settled. Sabalenka is a two-time Grand Slam champion who has consistently shown she can bounce back from poor performances within the same tournament. Her 2024 and 2025 seasons were defined, in part, by her ability to reset between rounds and play her best tennis under knockout pressure.

Thursday's match against Svitolina is a must-win, but it is a winnable match. Svitolina beat Muchova in three sets on Wednesday and will carry her own momentum into the day. Sabalenka will need to find the level she showed in her opening win over Muchova — the 7-6(2), 7-5 victory that opened Group 2 play — to stay in the tournament and protect her season-ending ranking.

What's Next

Sabalenka vs. Svitolina is Thursday's Group 2 decider at the WTA Finals in Indian Wells. The winner joins Mirra Andreeva in the semifinals. The loser ends their tournament. Year-end rankings implications will follow from whatever happens on Thursday.
