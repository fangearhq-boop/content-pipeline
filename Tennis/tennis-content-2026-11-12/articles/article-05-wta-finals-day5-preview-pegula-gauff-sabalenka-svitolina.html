WTA Finals Day 5: Pegula vs. Gauff and Sabalenka vs. Svitolina Decide the Last Two Semifinal Spots

By Ryan Calloway | November 12, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

Thursday at the WTA Finals in Indian Wells features the most consequential day of the round-robin stage. Both groups play their final matches on the same day, and both have one spot still up for grabs. Linda Noskova and Mirra Andreeva have already clinched their semifinal places. The remaining two will be settled on Thursday.

Group 1 sends Coco Gauff against Jessica Pegula — both at 1-1, both needing a win to advance. Group 2 pits Aryna Sabalenka against Elina Svitolina in the same situation. Win or go home. The round robin ends on Thursday, and four players are still alive for two spots.

Group 1 Decider: Gauff vs. Pegula

Coco Gauff opened the WTA Finals by beating Pegula on Day 1 in a three-set match. She then lost to Linda Noskova on Day 3 in straight sets, leaving her record at 1-1 going into the final group match. For Gauff, Thursday represents a chance to replicate her opening win and reach the semifinals — but also the risk that a second loss in four days ends her tournament before the knockouts.

Jessica Pegula lost to Gauff on Day 1 and beat Marta Kostyuk on Day 3 to even her record. She has been building form through the week. These two players know each other's games deeply — they are among the most-matched rivalries on the WTA Tour, and their meetings are rarely one-sided over a full match.

The player who wins advances to join Noskova in the Group 1 semifinal place. The player who loses ends their WTA Finals week in the round robin.

Group 2 Decider: Sabalenka vs. Svitolina

Wednesday night's results set up a Group 2 decider that neither player would have predicted before the week began. Aryna Sabalenka entered the tournament as the Group 2 favourite and lost 6-4, 6-0 to Mirra Andreeva in 62 minutes. Elina Svitolina, who beat Muchova in three sets on Wednesday, arrives for the decider in better form than the standings suggested she would be at this stage.

Sabalenka must reset quickly. She showed in her opening win over Karolina Muchova — a 7-6(2), 7-5 result — that she can produce her best tennis at this venue. If she reaches that level on Thursday, Svitolina will need to match it.

Svitolina's strength is her consistency and her ability to build long baseline rallies that force errors. Against Sabalenka, who attacks the ball aggressively, that style creates a natural tactical contest. It is a matchup that could easily go three sets.

Beyond the semifinal place, Sabalenka's year-end No. 2 ranking is also on the line. A loss Thursday — and an Andreeva deep run to the final — would bring the top two in the WTA rankings closer together than they have been all season.

What's Next

After Thursday's round-robin finishes, the WTA Finals semifinals are scheduled for Friday and Saturday, November 13–14, in Indian Wells. The final takes place on Sunday, November 15. The four confirmed semifinalists — including whoever advances from Thursday's deciders — will be known by Thursday evening.
