ATP Finals Group Preview: The Lendl Group Is Loaded, the McEnroe Group Is Zverev's to Lose

By Marcus Cole | November 12, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

The 2026 Nitto ATP Finals groups were drawn Thursday, and there is a clear contrast between the two round-robin draws. The Ivan Lendl Group has the most compelling top-to-bottom matchups of the week. The John McEnroe Group looks, at first glance, like it is Alexander Zverev's to navigate — provided his 2026-best form holds through the round robin.

But the history of the ATP Finals is littered with favorites who underestimated group play. With the tournament beginning November 15 in Turin, here is what each group has in store.

Ivan Lendl Group: Alcaraz vs. Medvedev Is the Headline

Carlos Alcaraz arrives in Turin as the hottest player on the men's tour. Five titles in 2026, including a 10-match winning streak through Vienna and Paris. Nine Masters 1000 crowns in his career. The ATP Finals is the one major title missing from his current run, and his motivation for this week is obvious.

The player most likely to test him in group play is Daniil Medvedev. The Russian former world No. 1 has played Alcaraz in Grand Slam finals before and understands the Spanish player's game as well as anyone on tour. Medvedev's surface preference — consistent on hard courts indoors — fits the Inalpi Arena. When these two meet in the round robin, it will carry semifinal-level intensity.

Flavio Cobolli, in his first ATP Finals, has been one of the revelations of the European indoor swing. Frances Tiafoe, also at his first Finals, plays with the kind of aggressive, crowd-pleasing game that can disrupt a opponent's rhythm on a given night. Neither is a favourite to advance, but both are capable of beating Medvedev or Alcaraz when playing at their ceiling.

John McEnroe Group: Zverev Faces Real Opponents, Not Easy Wins

Alexander Zverev's group looks more manageable on paper, but the individuals in it deserve more respect than a simple read of the seedings suggests.

Ben Shelton reached the Paris Masters final two weeks ago, losing to Alcaraz 7-5, 7-5 in a competitive match. His serve is among the most effective in the game on fast indoor surfaces, and his aggressive baseline game can produce short, sharp wins. Zverev has beaten Shelton before, but it is not a free pass.

Arthur Fils has been a top-10 player for most of the second half of the season and plays with the energy and tenacity of someone who wants to prove he belongs at this level. Rafael Jodar, the rookie who made ATP Finals history by qualifying in his debut year on tour, is the most unpredictable player in the draw — there is no established scouting report for how he handles this stage at this pressure level.

Zverev is the year-end No. 1, the Roland Garros and US Open champion, and a two-time ATP Finals winner. His route to the semifinals runs through Shelton, Fils, and Jodar in group play. The McEnroe Group is his to control — not his to sleepwalk through.

What to Watch Starting November 15

The round-robin schedule for the first day of group play will be published on the official ATP Tour site. The tournament runs through November 22, with semifinals on November 20 and the final on November 22.

Four first-time participants — Cobolli, Tiafoe, Fils, and Jodar — make this one of the most generationally mixed ATP Finals fields in recent memory. And the absence of Jannik Sinner, who is out for the season with a knee injury, ensures that two players — Zverev and Alcaraz — carry the weight of being the clear title favorites into the week.

What's Next

The 2026 Nitto ATP Finals begins November 15 at the Inalpi Arena in Turin. The full group-stage schedule and match order of play will be confirmed closer to the tournament's opening day.
