Andreeva Stuns Sabalenka 6-4, 6-0 at WTA Finals; Day 5 Has Two All-or-Nothing Deciders

By Marcus Cole | November 12, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

When Mirra Andreeva fell behind 1-4 in the opening set against Aryna Sabalenka on Wednesday night in Indian Wells, nothing about what followed seemed likely. Andreeva won the next five games to take the set 6-4. Then she won all six games of the second set. Eleven consecutive games. Sixty-two minutes of tennis. The world No. 2 did not win another game after that fourth game of the first set.

Andreeva is in the WTA Finals semifinals. Sabalenka is not — not yet. The 2-0 Russian, in her first WTA Finals, has been the form player of the round robin's second half. The No. 3 seed has not looked like a No. 3 all week.

The Comeback From 1-4

The opening stretch of the match looked like the draw had suggested it would. Sabalenka, the two-time Grand Slam champion, was dictating with her serve and forehand, taking early control of the match and pushing Andreeva wide with the kind of heavy ball that has beaten the world's best players across the last three seasons.

Then Andreeva steadied. She began finding more depth on her groundstrokes, absorbing Sabalenka's pace and redirecting with angles that the No. 2 seed was not prepared for. The five-game run from 1-4 to 6-4 was not a scramble — it was a systematic dismantling of an opponent who had been in control and suddenly found herself outmanoeuvred.

The second set offered no recovery. Sabalenka could not find a foothold from the first game and Andreeva, now playing with full confidence, closed the set to love in 23 minutes. The 6-0 bagel against a player of Sabalenka's calibre — at this stage of a season-ending championship — was one of the more striking scorelines the WTA Finals has produced in recent years.

Svitolina Keeps Her Season Alive

Earlier in the day's Group 2 schedule, Elina Svitolina beat Karolina Muchova in three sets to stay in contention. The result eliminates Muchova at 0-2 and sets up a straight shoot-out between Svitolina and Sabalenka on Thursday.

Group 2 standings after Day 4: Andreeva is at 2-0 and confirmed for the semifinals. Sabalenka and Svitolina are both 1-1 and will play each other on Thursday, with the winner advancing to join Andreeva in the last four. Muchova ends her tournament at 0-2.

Group 1: Pegula vs. Gauff on Thursday

The Group 1 picture is equally high-stakes. Linda Noskova has already secured the first semifinal place — the Czech, in her tournament debut, went 2-0 in the round robin without dropping a set. The second Group 1 spot goes to whoever wins Thursday's match between Jessica Pegula and Coco Gauff, both sitting at 1-1.

Gauff lost to Noskova on Day 3; Pegula beat Marta Kostyuk the same day. For Gauff, a loss Thursday would end her WTA Finals week before the knockouts. For Pegula, a win would be her deepest run at the season-ending event.

What's Next

Thursday's Day 5 schedule in Indian Wells is the most consequential of the round-robin stage. Both groups complete their final matches, with two players advancing to the semifinals alongside Noskova and Andreeva.

The WTA Finals runs through November 15, with the semifinals and final concluding the week. With two spots still open and four players still alive, the second half of this tournament begins Thursday.
