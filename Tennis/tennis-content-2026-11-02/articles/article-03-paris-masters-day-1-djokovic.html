Paris Masters Day 1: Djokovic Faces Basel Champion Vacherot as Last ATP Masters 1000 of 2026 Opens

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap

November 2, 2026

The Rolex Paris Masters opened Monday at La Défense Arena in Nanterre, and the first day of the last ATP Masters 1000 event of the 2026 season came with exactly the kind of stakes the calendar demands. Defending champion Jannik Sinner withdrew before the tournament began with a knee injury. Novak Djokovic, the ten-seed, opened against Valentin Vacherot — who won the Basel title on Sunday and landed in Paris sometime overnight.

The tournament runs through November 8. The champion earns 1,000 Race to Turin points. Everything about the season's endpoint flows through this building this week.

Djokovic vs. Vacherot: The Match That Opens Everything

In pure tennis terms, Novak Djokovic should beat Valentin Vacherot. Djokovic has seven titles in Paris, 50 wins at the event, and the kind of institutional knowledge of this court that turns advantages into certainties for lesser players. He is the most accomplished player at this venue in the history of the event.

The complicating factor is the context. Vacherot played a five-set week in Basel — through the draw, through the quarterfinals, through a three-set comeback final on Sunday. He arrives carrying a second career title and zero days off. Djokovic arrives carrying the knowledge that a first-round loss ends his Turin campaign entirely. He needs a minimum semifinal finish to qualify for the ATP Finals. A loss in round one, and the seven titles, the 50 wins, and the record all become irrelevant to this season's endpoint.

Their head-to-head stands at 1-0 in Vacherot's favor. That is a small sample, but it is the entire sample, and it leans the wrong way from Djokovic's perspective.

Buse Makes Peruvian History

Before the headlining match, Peru had a moment in Paris. Ignacio Buse, ranked around No. 33, beat Zizou Bergs of Belgium 6-2, 6-4 in a first-round match on Monday, becoming the first Peruvian player to win a match at the Paris Masters 1000 event. It is the kind of result that will not make the headline on a week full of Race to Turin narratives, but it belongs in the record of the tournament.

Buse's reward is a second-round match against Daniil Medvedev, the No. 4 seed and confirmed ATP Finals qualifier. That is a steep step up. The history, at least, is already written.

The Draw and What It Means

The seedings have Alexander Zverev as No. 1, Carlos Alcaraz as No. 2, Ben Shelton as No. 3, and Medvedev as No. 4. Alcaraz and Zverev, already qualified for Turin, enter in the second round. Their paths through the draw are the most likely meeting in the final.

For the Race narrative, the players to watch are those on the edge of the cut: Flavio Cobolli, Arthur Fils, Frances Tiafoe, and Rafael Jodar hold the four remaining spots. Alex de Minaur sits 24 points behind Jodar in ninth. Djokovic is further back. Paris, and its 1,000-point top prize, is the last lever anyone can pull.

What's Next

Day 1 results will continue through Monday evening. Djokovic-Vacherot is the session's defining match. Results from the early round will shift Race standings in real time, and the week's shape will become clearer by Tuesday when more of the top seeds enter the draw.

The Paris Masters final is scheduled for Sunday, November 8. Between now and then, the last major prize of the ATP 2026 season is available to any player who wants it badly enough.
