WTA Hamburg Final: Sherif Retires in SF; Korpatsch Gets Hometown Shot at the Title

By Ryan Calloway July 26, 2026

The Semifinal That Changed the Draw

Mayar Sherif had been one of the outstanding stories of the European clay swing's final week. The Egyptian had beaten Paula Badosa twice in eight days — most recently 6-2, 6-3 in Friday's Hamburg quarterfinal — and had established herself as the tournament's most consistent performer heading into the semifinals.

In the Hamburg semifinal on Saturday, Sherif retired against Tamara Korpatsch at 6-1, 2-0. The injury, coming after a long and physically demanding stretch of matches on clay, ended what had been an exceptional week. Korpatsch advances to the final through retirement, but she had earned her place in that semifinal, and a final is what it is regardless of how the bracket resolved.

Korpatsch at Home

Tamara Korpatsch is German, and Hamburg is her home city. She entered the MSC Hamburg Ladies Open as a wildcard — an invitation from tournament organizers that typically goes to local players with limited direct entry ranking. Playing a WTA 500 final in your home city as a wildcard is a script that tournament directors would not dare write, because no one would believe it.

Korpatsch has had a professional career marked by the grind of the lower-level WTA events and ITF circuit. A WTA 500 final in Hamburg changes the context around her name and ranking entirely. Whether she wins or loses on Sunday, she has already produced the result of her career.

Bondar's Three-Hour Battle

Anna Bondar of Hungary reached the final through a different kind of difficulty. Her semifinal against Elina Avanesyan lasted three hours and 29 minutes — a three-set match that neither player was able to control for any extended run. Bondar took the first set 7-6(3), lost the second 4-6, and won the deciding set 7-6(2). The win rewarded her physically and mentally, but it also left her more fatigued coming into Sunday than Korpatsch, who advanced on Sherif's retirement without completing her match.

Bondar has been one of the WTA circuit's solid mid-ranking performers. Her ranking will improve significantly with a WTA 500 final appearance, and a title would represent the most significant result of her career.

What's Next

The WTA Hamburg final is scheduled for Sunday July 26 on the clay at Rothenbaum. After today, the European clay season ends and both players move toward the hard-court swing — the Rogers Cup in Toronto (Aug. 1–11) for the women's draw. Whether it is Bondar or Korpatsch who lifts the Hamburg trophy, the result today closes a week that produced several outcomes no one anticipated when the draw was made at the start of the week.
