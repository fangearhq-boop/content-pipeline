Halys Makes History in Kitzbühel: First ATP Title After 14-Year Journey

By Elena Voss July 26, 2026

A Dream Realized in Austria

Quentin Halys claimed his first ATP Tour title on Saturday, defeating defending champion Alexander Bublik of Kazakhstan 6-4, 7-6(6) in the Generali Open Kitzbühel final. The match lasted one hour and 33 minutes. Halys fired 17 aces. He never once faced a break point.

The result is remarkable on its own terms. The context makes it something else entirely. Halys is 29 years old. He earned his first ATP ranking point in 2012, when he was 15. That is 14 years of professional tennis — of draws and losses and near-misses — before reaching the other side of a title match and winning.

France's First at Kitzbühel

There is a specific piece of history attached to Saturday's win. French players had appeared in Kitzbühel finals before Saturday and had lost every time — going 0-6 in final appearances at this Austrian clay tournament across the Open Era. Halys ended that run. He is now the first Frenchman to win the Generali Open Kitzbühel title in the Open Era.

The opponent was not a soft one. Bublik, the No. 1 seed, had won this title in 2025 and was trying to become the first Kitzbühel champion to successfully defend the title since 2013. He had reached the final with a clinical 6-3, 6-4 win over Tomas Martin Etcheverry in the semifinal. His serve and the variety in his game are well-suited to clay. He led Halys 2-0 in their head-to-head record coming into the final.

None of it mattered on Saturday. Halys moved through the match with control and purpose, converting the first set 6-4 before closing out a tight tiebreak to win the second 7-6(6). His serve carried him whenever the match tightened.

The Ranking Consequence

Halys climbs 32 places in the ATP Rankings following the title, moving to No. 51 — within five spots of his career high of No. 46. The points from a first ATP 250 title will sit in his ranking for 52 weeks, giving him a foundation heading into the North American hard-court swing that begins this coming week.

For a player who has spent most of his career in the 60–100 range of the rankings, this result opens doors. Kitzbühel had a field that included credible players throughout, and the level of the opposition underlines that Halys did not stumble into a title on a weak week. He beat the No. 1 seed in a final, without dropping serve once in the championship match.

What's Next

The European clay season officially closes today, with Estoril, Hamburg, and Prague all running their finals on Sunday. For Halys, the attention now turns to whether he can carry this form into the hard-court season. The National Bank Open in Montreal (Aug. 4–13) and the Cincinnati Open (Aug. 13–23) are the next ATP 1000 events before the US Open begins August 24. A first ATP title at 29 does not guarantee more results to follow — but it removes a ceiling that players spend years trying to break through. Halys broke it in Kitzbühel, and in doing so gave French tennis its first victory at this Austrian tournament in the Open Era.
