Clay Season Ends Today — Four Finals Close the European Swing

By Marcus Cole July 26, 2026

The Final Day of Red Dirt

Sunday July 26 is the last day of clay tennis in professional tennis's 2026 calendar. Four tournaments are settling their champions today, capping a two-week European swing that produced results consistent with what clay tends to do: reward perseverance, invert expectations, and create stories that the seedings never anticipated.

Kitzbühel is already done — Quentin Halys defeated defending champion Alexander Bublik on Saturday to claim his first ATP title and become the first Frenchman to win in Austria's mountain clay event in the Open Era. The three Sunday finals remain: Estoril (ATP 250, Van Assche vs. Blockx), Hamburg (WTA 500, Bondar vs. Korpatsch), and Prague (WTA 250, Snigur vs. Tagger). All three feature players in their first finals at the respective level, and all three produced upsets in the semifinals to get here.

What This Clay Swing Produced

In two weeks since Wimbledon ended, the European clay swing delivered a consistent theme: the expected names did not always survive. Rublev — who had just won the Bastad title, his first in 17 months — was eliminated in the Estoril quarterfinals. Krejcikova, riding an eight-match winning streak from Athens, lost to an 18-year-old in Prague's semifinals. Sherif, who had dominated Hamburg all week, retired injured before the final. Darderi, who led the Tour in clay wins, came within two points of beating Blockx and ended up on the wrong end of one of the comeback results of the week.

The clay swing's final week also saw Halys — who had never won a tour-level title in 14 years of professional tennis — win one on Saturday afternoon in Kitzbühel. Whatever else this clay season delivered, it delivered that.

What Comes Next

The North American hard-court swing begins immediately. The Canadian Open — the National Bank Open — runs the women's draw in Toronto from August 1–11 and the men's draw in Montreal from August 4–13. The draws for both events are expected on July 29–30.

The men's field in Montreal is missing its top players. Jannik Sinner withdrew with an elbow injury. Novak Djokovic is out. Carlos Alcaraz missed the entire clay season with a wrist injury and will not be in Montreal. Jack Draper withdrew with a recurring arm problem. That removes the world's No. 1, No. 3 (Alcaraz), and several of the field's most prominent names. Alexander Zverev, who won the Roland Garros title in May, enters as the likely top seed. Ben Shelton, who won the 2025 edition of the Montreal event, is the defending champion. Daniil Medvedev and Felix Auger-Aliassime round out the field's higher-profile names.

The women's Toronto draw is also missing Jasmine Paolini due to a foot injury. The field remains competitive throughout, however, with Sabalenka, Gauff, and others available.

What's Next

After the Canadian Open (Aug. 1–13), the Cincinnati Masters (Aug. 13–23) follows before the US Open begins August 24. The hard-court swing is the longest and most important stretch of the second half of the season. For the players who spent the last two weeks on clay — winning and losing on slow red dirt — the transition to fast hard courts begins now.
