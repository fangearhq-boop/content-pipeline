Tagger, 18, Stuns Krejcikova in Prague — Into First Career WTA Final

By Elena Voss July 26, 2026

The Upset That Opened Sunday's Final

Barbora Krejcikova arrived in Prague as the No. 2 seed, on an eight-match winning streak that had begun with her title at the Athens Open earlier this month, and carrying the full weight of her credentials as a two-time Grand Slam champion. On Friday, an 18-year-old Austrian named Lilli Tagger took two hours and 50 minutes to eliminate her from the tournament.

Tagger won 6-7(5), 6-3, 7-6(4) — dropping the first set in a tiebreak, regrouping to take the second convincingly, then closing out a deciding tiebreak to complete one of the more significant results of her young career. She is ranked No. 90 in the world. She is competing in her first WTA 250 semifinal at this level. She won.

From Lienz to Prague

Tagger is from Lienz, a city of roughly 12,000 people in the Austrian Alps near the Italian border. She moved to Varese, in northern Italy, to train at the academy run by Francesca Schiavone — the 2010 French Open champion and the first Italian woman to win a Grand Slam singles title. The academy background shows in how Tagger plays: technical clay-court fundamentals, an ability to construct points, and the composure to recover from a set down against an opponent of Krejcikova's quality.

Her junior career gave the first indication of what her senior results might eventually look like. Tagger won the 2025 French Open girls' title at Roland Garros, which pointed toward a clay-court toolkit capable of competing at the highest levels. Prague in 2026 is the first time her senior record has matched what her junior title suggested was possible.

Snigur in the Final

Tagger's opponent in Sunday's Livesport Prague Open final is Daria Snigur of Ukraine, the No. 8 seed, who defeated Czech fifth seed Tereza Valentova 7-6(4), 6-2 in the other semifinal. Snigur has had a strong 2026 season on clay and arrives in the final as the more experienced of the two finalists.

Valentova's semifinal loss ends a memorable Prague week for the Czech player, who had upset defending champion Marie Bouzkova in the quarterfinals. Snigur closed it out with clear authority in the second set after a tight first.

What's Next

The WTA Prague final is scheduled for Sunday July 26 at the Livesport Arena. Tagger playing in her first WTA final, at 18, against a more experienced Snigur is the kind of matchup the Prague Open has historically produced — the tournament has a record of hosting first-time WTA title winners. Tagger's win over Krejcikova on Friday is already the biggest result of her professional career. What happens on Sunday determines whether it leads to something more.
