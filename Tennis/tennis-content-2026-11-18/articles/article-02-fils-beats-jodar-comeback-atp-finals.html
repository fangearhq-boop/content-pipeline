First French ATP Finals Win Since Tsonga 2011: Fils Comeback Keeps Dream Alive

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | November 18, 2026

Arthur Fils had every reason to be heading home on Tuesday. Down a set, in an elimination match, facing an opponent who had beaten him twice already this year — the script seemed to be writing itself. Instead, the Frenchman produced one of the more compelling reversals of this ATP Finals with a 4-6, 6-2, 6-2 comeback win over Rafael Jodar that keeps his tournament alive and places his name in a small but meaningful piece of French tennis history.

With the win, Fils becomes the first Frenchman to win a match at the ATP Finals since Jo-Wilfried Tsonga did it in 2011. That's a 15-year gap, and it carries real significance given how France has produced so many fine clay-court players over that span without any of them making the same stamp on the year-end indoor event.

How the Match Turned

Jodar had the better of the first set, taking it 6-4 and looking like the more composed player in the early exchanges. The Spaniard had already beaten Fils twice in 2026 — both on hard courts — and his head-to-head advantage appeared to be weighing on the Frenchman's game.

Then came the second set. Fils found something — in his footwork, in his timing, in his willingness to absorb Jodar's pace and redirect it rather than try to overpower it. He took the second set 6-2 with a kind of controlled authority that suggested a mental shift as much as a tactical one. The third set followed the exact same pattern. When Fils is dictating with his legs and his hands rather than trying to blast winners, he is a very different player.

The 4-6, 6-2, 6-2 scoreline tells the story of a match that went one way and then another. Jodar now sits at 0-2 and needs both to beat Shelton on Thursday and for Fils to lose to Zverev — a scenario that his coach will describe as theoretically possible and practically very difficult.

What the Win Means for Fils

Fils, 24, is the first Frenchman at the ATP Finals since Gaël Monfils qualified in 2016. His presence in Turin represents a new generation of French men's tennis making a mark at the year-end event, and his comeback win gave him 1-1 in the McEnroe Group.

He now faces world No. 1 Zverev on Thursday — a match that looks, on paper, like an overwhelming challenge. Zverev is already through to the semifinals and was dominant in beating Shelton the same evening that Fils was beating Jodar. But Fils's match on Tuesday showed he has the mental architecture to compete under pressure, and tennis players have a way of producing their best when facing their most difficult opponent.

What's Next

The McEnroe Group finale on Thursday sets up Shelton vs Fils for the second semifinal spot. The player who wins that match joins Zverev in the last four. Fils's route to the semis runs through defeating a player ranked 70 spots above him in the world — Zverev — which would need to happen on the same day that Shelton loses to the out-of-contention Jodar. It's a complicated path, but Fils's win on Tuesday proved he is capable of more than the head-to-head data suggested.
