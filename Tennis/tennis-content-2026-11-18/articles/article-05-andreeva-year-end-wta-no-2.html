Andreeva Ends 2026 as WTA World No. 2 — Year-End Rankings Confirm Historic Season

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | November 18, 2026

Mirra Andreeva's 2026 season has a final number attached to it now that the WTA year-end rankings have been confirmed: No. 2 in the world. The 19-year-old closes the year ahead of Aryna Sabalenka, who drops to No. 3, in a ranking that reflects the trajectory of a season that ended with the WTA Finals championship in Riyadh last Saturday.

The Season in Brief

Andreeva went through the WTA Finals without dropping a set — a 5-0 record across the round-robin and knockout stages that placed her among the most dominant performers in the tournament's recent history. Her final against Elina Svitolina, which she won 7-5, 6-3, was the capstone of a week that saw her beat ranked players on the biggest stage of the year-end circuit.

The WTA Finals result was not an isolated event. Andreeva had been building toward it across a season in which she accumulated points steadily and beat opponents ranked well above her. By the time she arrived in Riyadh, she had already established herself as a genuine title contender rather than a surface specialist or a one-tournament story.

What No. 2 Means at 19

Year-end No. 2 at age 19 is a rare achievement. The WTA record books show that a small number of teenage players have finished this high — the names attached to comparable teenage rankings include some of the sport's all-time great players. Andreeva is now in that conversation, though she would likely prefer to let her results speak rather than be framed in historical terms she hasn't asked for.

She is the third teenager to win the WTA Finals, joining Chris Evert and Maria Sharapova. She is also the first player to win the event on her debut at the tournament since Ash Barty did it in 2019. Those milestones accumulate into something substantial, and the year-end No. 2 is both the punctuation mark on 2026 and the starting point for whatever comes next.

Sabalenka and the Rankings Picture

Aryna Sabalenka finishes the year at No. 3. She entered the WTA Finals holding the No. 1 ranking, but Andreeva's title win in Riyadh and consistent point accumulation across the year were enough to move her to second. The gap between No. 1, No. 2, and No. 3 heading into 2027 will shape the early-season narrative as players look to establish themselves in the rankings before the clay-court season arrives.

What's Next

The 2027 WTA season begins in January. Andreeva starts the year as the second-ranked player in the world and as the defending WTA Finals champion. Her off-season preparation and early 2027 results will establish whether the form of late 2026 represents a sustainable baseline or a peak she will need to rebuild toward. Based on what she showed in Riyadh, the former seems more likely than the latter.
