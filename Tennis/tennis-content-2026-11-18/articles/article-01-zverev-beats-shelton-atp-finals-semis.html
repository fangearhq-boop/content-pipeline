Zverev Books ATP Finals Semifinal Spot With 6-2, 7-6 Win Over Shelton

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | November 18, 2026

Alexander Zverev is through to the ATP Finals semifinals, and he got there in a manner that left very little room for doubt. His 6-2, 7-6(4) win over Ben Shelton on Tuesday at the Inalpi Arena in Turin was a performance defined by serving excellence — 14 aces, zero double faults — and the kind of match management that separates genuine contenders from everyone else.

Zverev is the first player to book a last-four spot at this year's tournament. After winning the opening set comfortably and absorbing Shelton's power game well enough to take the second set in a tiebreak, he extended his all-time record against the American to 7-0. That number has now come to look less like a streak and more like a structural reality: Shelton and Zverev play the same type of aggressive game, but Zverev has consistently found ways to do it slightly better.

How the Match Unfolded

The first set was the more convincing of the two. Zverev moved through Shelton's service games with controlled returns, creating opportunities and converting them cleanly. The 6-2 score was a fair reflection of his dominance in that set rather than a fluke of timing or luck.

The second set was more competitive. Shelton — who struck 15 aces of his own — found his range and made Zverev work harder for every point. Neither player was broken, which pushed the set to a tiebreak. Zverev settled it at 7-4, keeping his composure in the moments that mattered most.

Zero double faults from a player who relies on his serve as heavily as Zverev does is a significant detail. It speaks to the technical discipline he brought into the match, and to the difficulty Shelton would have faced regardless of his own serving. Zverev's first serve was landing well and his second serve held up under pressure — the kind of combination that makes him very hard to break.

The Zverev-Shelton Rivalry

The two players had already met earlier in 2026 in the US Open final, a match Zverev won. Shelton has been one of the most exciting players to watch this season and his ATP Finals debut — having reached 1-1 after Tuesday — showed he belongs in this company. But Zverev's 7-0 record against him is not a statistical quirk. It's a reflection of how well the German reads Shelton's game, neutralises his power, and then creates opportunities of his own.

Shelton now faces Fils on Thursday in the McEnroe Group finale, a match he must win to advance to the last four. His 1-1 record keeps that possibility very much open.

What's Next

Zverev is 2-0 in the McEnroe Group and will play his final group match against Arthur Fils on Thursday. The result of that match is academically significant for group seedings — Zverev might look to conserve energy with the semifinal already secured, or he might seek a perfect group record. He has won the ATP Finals twice before, in 2018 and 2021, and the form he's shown in Turin suggests a third title is well within his range.
