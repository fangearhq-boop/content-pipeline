World No. 1 Sinner Targets China Open Return After Two-Month Absence

By Marcus Cole | September 22, 2026

ATP | Jannik Sinner

The longest Jannik Sinner absence from professional tennis since his arrival among the elite may be coming to an end.

The world No. 1 has been sidelined since July — when he lifted the Wimbledon trophy — with extra-articular tendinous inflammation in his right knee. He missed the Canadian Open, Cincinnati, and the US Open entirely. Now, after a period of treatment and recovery in Turin, Sinner is targeting the China Open in Beijing as the point of his return.

The China Open begins September 30. Eight days from now.

The Injury and Recovery

The condition — inflammation in the tissues surrounding the right knee joint rather than the joint itself — was treated at J Medical in Turin over a three-week period. It is not a structural injury and does not involve the meniscus, cartilage, or major ligaments. Sinner subsequently returned to high-intensity training at the Circolo della Stampa Sporting facility in Turin.

Reports from those around him have been consistently positive. The treatment appears to have been effective, and the decision to target Beijing rather than rushing back for the earlier summer hardcourt events reflects the careful approach his team has taken throughout the recovery process.

Defending Champion in Beijing

The China Open is not simply a convenient return point for Sinner — it is a tournament he owns. In 2025, he defeated Learner Tien 6-2, 6-2 in the final, barely breaking sweat in a dominant title-winning display. As defending champion, he enters the 2026 draw seeded No. 1, which offers a measure of protection in the bracket.

There is, of course, a question of match sharpness. No one plays their best tennis after a two-month break, and Beijing's hard courts offer little margin for error against quality opposition. Sinner's team will be managing his schedule carefully, and he may not play the ATP 500 in Tokyo the following week if the knee requires careful handling.

The Bigger Stakes

Sinner holds the world No. 1 ATP ranking, but Alexander Zverev — who won both the French Open and US Open in 2026 — has been closing the gap in the Race to the ATP Finals. A strong autumn run from Sinner is not just about rankings maintenance; it is about reestablishing his presence at the top of the game after an enforced absence.

If healthy, a Beijing-Shanghai-Paris sequence for Sinner would represent one of the most compelling autumn tennis narratives in recent memory. The whole sport is waiting to see if he can deliver it.

What's Next

The China Open draw will be released on September 28. If Sinner enters as expected, his first-round match will be one of the most-watched events of the week. After Beijing, the Shanghai ATP 1000 begins October 7 — another event where Sinner will be expected to challenge for the title. The ATP Finals in Turin are already within reach; the question is whether Sinner can add to his points total before November arrives.
