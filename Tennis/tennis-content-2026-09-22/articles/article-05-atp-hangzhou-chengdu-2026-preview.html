ATP Asian Swing Begins: Medvedev and Rublev Lead Hangzhou, Vacherot Headlines Chengdu

By Ryan Calloway | September 22, 2026

ATP | Asian Swing

While the tennis world focuses on Laver Cup in London, a different kind of opportunity is opening up in China.

The ATP 250 doubleheader — the Hangzhou Open and the Chengdu Open — begins Wednesday, September 23, running through September 29. With nearly all of the top 10 committed to Laver Cup duty at The O2, the draws in Hangzhou and Chengdu are as open as they have been at any point this season. For the players on the ATP ranking bubble, the timing could not be better.

Hangzhou Open

Daniil Medvedev enters the Hangzhou Open as the top seed. The former world No. 1 has had a difficult 2026 by his own standards, and a strong run in Hangzhou would provide a meaningful boost to both his ranking and his confidence heading into the rest of the Asian swing. Andrey Rublev is seeded second.

The doubles draw in Hangzhou features Quentin Halys and Lucas Miedler as the top seeds, entering as a pair for the first time in Hangzhou. With quality throughout, the singles and doubles competitions should both produce competitive draws.

Chengdu Open

In Chengdu, Valentin Vacherot leads a field that includes Alejandro Davidovich Fokina, Denis Shapovalov, Zizou Bergs, and Sebastian Baez. The tournament also features 17-year-old French talent Moise Kouame, who earned his entry through the PIF ATP Next Gen Accelerator program — one of a number of young players receiving direct access to ATP events as the tour invests in developing the next generation.

Shapovalov, a former top-10 player who has been rebuilding his ranking over the past year, has a real opportunity to collect significant points in a field without the usual top-tier obstacles.

The Bigger Picture

The absence of the Laver Cup contingent — which includes Alcaraz, Zverev, Ruud, Menšík, Cobolli, Fritz, de Minaur, Bublik, and Tien — removes a significant portion of the ranking's upper tier from these events. It is precisely this dynamic that makes ATP 250s during Laver Cup week some of the most tactically interesting events on the calendar. Players who are neither good enough nor high-profile enough to earn Laver Cup selection suddenly find themselves among the favorites.

The Tokyo ATP 500 begins September 30, when the Laver Cup players return to competitive action. But before then, Hangzhou and Chengdu will play out their own story — one that matters considerably to the players competing for ranking position heading into the final stretch of 2026.

What's Next

First-round action begins Wednesday in both cities. Results from Hangzhou and Chengdu will be covered as the week progresses. The Asian swing continues into October with the China Open in Beijing (Sep 30), Shanghai Masters (Oct 7), and ultimately the ATP Finals in Turin in November.
