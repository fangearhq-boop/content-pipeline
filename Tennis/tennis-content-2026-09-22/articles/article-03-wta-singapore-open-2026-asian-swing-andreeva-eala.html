WTA Asian Swing Begins: Singapore Open Upgraded to WTA 500, Andreeva Leads Draw

By Ryan Calloway | September 22, 2026

WTA | Singapore

The WTA fall Asian swing has arrived — and Singapore is playing at a higher level in 2026.

The Singapore Open presented by BNP Paribas has been elevated to WTA 500 status for this edition, making it one of the more valuable events of the autumn stretch. Play is underway at the OCBC Arena, with first-round action running through Tuesday before the tournament moves to the quarterfinals and beyond.

The Draw

Roland Garros champion Mirra Andreeva tops the draw as the No. 1 seed, moving up from No. 2 after Jessica Pegula withdrew from the field. Andreeva — ranked world No. 5 — enters in strong form and will be difficult to beat on any surface. She received an opening-round bye and will enter play in the second round.

Amanda Anisimova is the No. 2 seed. The American has been a consistent presence on the WTA Tour this year and could make a deep run in Singapore. Elise Mertens, a seasoned performer on hard courts, is the No. 4 seed.

Perhaps the most watched player in the draw is No. 3 seed Alexandra Eala of the Philippines. The 21-year-old, currently ranked No. 17 in the world, has established herself as one of the tour's genuine rising talents in 2026. A breakthrough tournament performance in Singapore — in a region that has embraced her tennis career from the beginning — would represent one of the feel-good stories of the swing.

The Bigger Picture

The Singapore Open runs concurrently with the Korea Open in Seoul, a WTA 250 event that also began Monday. Together, the two tournaments represent the opening salvo of a key stretch of the WTA calendar. The China Open in Beijing follows at the start of October, with the Wuhan Open and the WTA Finals completing the autumn run for the top players.

Ranking points are precious at this stage of the season. Players outside the top 10 have real opportunities to climb. Players inside it are trying to consolidate. With the WTA Finals cutoff approaching, every match result counts in ways they simply do not earlier in the year.

What's Next

Second-round and quarterfinal action is expected later this week, with the Singapore Open final scheduled for Saturday, September 27. Updates on Andreeva, Eala, and the rest of the Singapore field will follow as the draw plays out. The Korea Open, also running through Saturday, features Jelena Ostapenko as the top seed.
