Davis Cup 2026 Final 8 Set: The Complete Bologna Field

By Elena Voss | September 22, 2026

Davis Cup | Tennis

The Davis Cup Finals field is complete. All eight nations that will compete in Bologna from November 24-29 are now confirmed, following the conclusion of the second-round qualifying ties over the weekend.

Italy return as hosts and defending champions — the Azzurri have won three consecutive Davis Cup titles, and they will once again enjoy home support at the Unipol Arena in Bologna. The seven remaining places were claimed through hard-fought qualifying matches played across Europe and the Americas.

The Bologna Eight

Germany claimed their spot with Alexander Zverev leading the charge — the US Open champion's presence transforms Germany into genuine title contenders. Spain swept Chile 3-0 to book their place without dropping a rubber. Great Britain edged through against Ecuador 4-0, showing impressive depth even without injured Cameron Norrie. Canada secured their position with Félix Auger-Aliassime delivering a 17-ace performance against France. Czechia completed a stunning comeback from 2-1 down to eliminate the United States 3-2 in Prague. Austria and South Korea rounded out the field, both earning their places through tight qualifying ties.

The full Bologna lineup: Italy, Germany, Spain, Great Britain, Canada, Czechia, Austria, South Korea.

The Storylines to Watch

Germany have not won the Davis Cup since 1993, but with Zverev at the peak of his powers — two Grand Slams won in 2026 — there is a credible argument that this is the most dangerous German Davis Cup team in a generation. Should Zverev maintain the form he showed through the US Open, opposing teams will need something very special to beat them.

Italy's streak is remarkable. Three consecutive titles. The pressure of defending on home soil is real, but the Italians have shown remarkable consistency — and the weight of three consecutive titles suggests this is not a team that simply rides favorable draws. They earn their wins.

Canada, fresh from FAA's heroics against France, will arrive with momentum. Czechia's confidence is sky-high after eliminating the Americans. And Spain — always formidable in Davis Cup — cannot be written off.

What's Next

The draw ceremony will determine the group-stage matchups for the eight nations. With this level of depth — multiple Grand Slam champions in multiple squads — the group stage in Bologna should produce some of the year's best team tennis. Coverage resumes when the draw is made and, ultimately, when the Finals begin on November 24.
