Laver Cup 2026: Team Europe vs Team World — Preview, Rosters and What to Watch

By Marcus Cole | September 22, 2026

ATP | Laver Cup

Three days from now, The O2 Arena in London plays host to one of tennis's most theatrical events — and this year, the stakes feel higher than they have in years.

The 2026 Laver Cup runs September 25-27, pitting Team Europe against Team World in 12 matches across three days. Team Europe enters as the side with the deeper star power. Team World enters as the defending champions.

That combination makes for a compelling setup.

The Teams

Team Europe, captained by Yannick Noah, lines up with Carlos Alcaraz, Alexander Zverev, Casper Ruud, Jakub Menšík, Flavio Cobolli, and Rafael Jódar. Between them, Alcaraz and Zverev have won four of the past six Grand Slams. Zverev — the 2026 French Open and US Open champion — arrives in London having just completed one of the finest hardcourt seasons in recent memory.

Team World, captained by Andre Agassi, fields Taylor Fritz, Alex de Minaur, Alexander Bublik, Learner Tien, Francisco Cerúndolo, and Brandon Nakashima. The squad has been shaped by late changes: Tommy Paul withdrew with an elbow injury and was replaced by Cerúndolo, while Ben Shelton pulled out for Davis Cup duty and Nakashima stepped in.

Fritz and de Minaur anchor Team World's singles effort. De Minaur, in particular, has been one of the most consistent players on tour in 2026 and will be a difficult assignment for anyone in the European lineup.

The Context

Team World's 15-9 victory in San Francisco in 2025 ended an eight-year European winning streak and signaled a genuine shift in the event's competitive balance. For much of Laver Cup's history, Europe's depth of talent made the outcome feel like a formality. Not anymore.

This year, however, the European roster is arguably its strongest in some time. Zverev is the only player in the world who has won two Grand Slams in 2026. Alcaraz, despite a quieter Grand Slam year by his own standards, remains one of the most electrifying players on tour. Menšík and Cobolli have both broken into the world's top 15. Europe's bench is genuine.

The question is whether Team World's dynamic, team-oriented style — which proved so effective in San Francisco — can overcome the individual talent gap. Laver Cup's cumulative points format means momentum matters enormously: a slow start on Day 1 can be overcome, but a team that concedes the opening sessions often finds itself chasing.

The Format

Each of the three days features four matches — three singles and one doubles. Day 1 wins count for one point, Day 2 wins for two points, and Day 3 wins for three. The first team to 13 points wins the Laver Cup. With 24 points available in total, a team that wins the first two days comfortably can put the result beyond doubt before Sunday, though the weighted Day 3 points mean comebacks are always possible.

Day 1 begins with the afternoon session at 1 PM local time, followed by the night session at 7 PM.

What's Next

Coverage of the Laver Cup begins Thursday when the first matches are played. The singles matchups will be revealed ahead of each day. With Zverev, Alcaraz, Fritz, and de Minaur all confirmed, the individual duels over the three days will be among the most watched matches of the tennis calendar. After London, the Asian swing resumes in Tokyo, Beijing, and Shanghai through October.
