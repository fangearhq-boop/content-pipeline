Eala Routs Oliynykova to Reach US Open Third Round — A First for Philippine Tennis

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | September 4, 2026

No. 17 seed Alex Eala defeated Oleksandra Oliynykova 6-1, 6-4 at Louis Armstrong Stadium to reach the US Open third round for the first time in her career, making history for Philippine tennis.

There was no drama this time — just clinical, dominant tennis from a player who is starting to look like she belongs in the second week of a Grand Slam.

Alex Eala, the No. 17 seed from the Philippines, defeated Ukraine's Oleksandra Oliynykova 6-1, 6-4 at Louis Armstrong Stadium on Thursday, advancing to the US Open third round for the first time in her career. In doing so, she became the first Filipino player to reach the third round of the US Open, extending the records she has been setting all summer.

Avenging Strasbourg

There was some history to settle. Earlier in 2026, at the Strasbourg tournament on clay in May, Oliynykova had beaten Eala 3-6, 7-5, 6-3 in the first round. That result stood as the only blemish on their head-to-head record. Hard court, however, is a different surface — and a different Eala.

From the moment the match began, it was clear who was in control. Eala took the opening set 6-1 with minimal resistance. Oliynykova, who had dispatched her in three sets in Strasbourg, had no answer for Eala's pace and precision from the baseline.

Sustained Dominance

The second set required slightly more effort, but Eala did not let her foot off the accelerator. She took it 6-4, wrapping up the match in efficient fashion at Louis Armstrong Stadium — the same court where she beat qualifier Raluca Stoiana 6-1, 6-2 in the first round just two days earlier.

Combined across R1 and R2, Eala has now dropped just 11 games in two matches. That is a dominant statement from the highest-seeded Filipino player in Grand Slam history.

A Season Built for This

Eala arrived at Flushing Meadows as the No. 17 seed — the highest Grand Slam seeding ever for a Filipino. Her 2026 season has been a steady ascent: she reached the DC Open final (defeating Svitolina and Osaka before losing to Pegula 6-4, 6-3), went three rounds at Cincinnati, and carried hard-court momentum into the US Open.

Her previous best at Flushing Meadows was a second-round exit in 2025. That barrier is now behind her.

What's Next

Eala will face an opponent in the US Open third round still to be confirmed. The draw has been relatively kind — she is in position to make a run toward the second week. After reaching the first third round of her Grand Slam career, nothing about her trajectory suggests she plans to stop there.
