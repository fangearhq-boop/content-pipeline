Zverev Survives Second Straight Five-Setter at US Open in Open Era Historic Fashion

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | September 4, 2026

World No. 1 Alexander Zverev defeated Quentin Halys 6-4, 4-6, 7-6(3), 6-7(3), 6-3 in more than four and a half hours, becoming the first top-seeded man in Open Era history to play back-to-back five-set matches at the start of a major.

Alexander Zverev is playing the most exhausting first two rounds in the recorded history of men's Grand Slam tennis — and he is still winning.

The world No. 1 defeated France's Quentin Halys 6-4, 4-6, 7-6(3), 6-7(3), 6-3 on Thursday evening, completing a more than four-and-a-half-hour battle to advance to the US Open third round. Combined with his five-set, four-hour-53-minute victory over Lorenzo Sonego in the first round, Zverev has now spent close to 10 hours on court through just two matches — the equivalent of playing two full five-set matches.

An Open Era First

According to ATP records, no top-seeded man in the Open Era — which dates to 1968 — has ever played back-to-back five-set matches to open a Grand Slam. Zverev is now in territory where no No. 1 seed has been before.

What makes this remarkable is not only the duration but the quality of the opposition. Sonego pushed Zverev to 4h53m in the first round. Halys, a clay-court specialist, proved equally difficult on the hard courts of Flushing Meadows, finding angles and mixing pace in a way that repeatedly troubled the German.

The Pivotal Third Set

The match had a moment that could have ended very differently. Halys led 4-1 in the third set and had a break point that would have given him a 5-1 advantage. Had he converted, the match trajectory changes entirely.

Zverev saved it. He then won the set 7-6 in the tiebreak, taking it 7-3. Halys responded by winning the fourth set tiebreak as well, 7-3 to Zverev's 7-6(3). But Zverev steadied in the fifth and closed it out 6-3 — breaking Halys twice in the deciding set to seal the match.

Questions About the Body

Whether Zverev can sustain this level of physical output through the second week is the central narrative now following the world No. 1. Ten hours of five-set tennis in two matches is an extraordinary physical cost — one that no previous No. 1 seed has chosen, or been forced, to pay this early in a major.

The German has historically shown the ability to recover and perform across long matches. But with a potential quarter-final, semi-final and final still ahead, the legs will be watched closely in every session.

What's Next

Zverev advances to the US Open third round, where his draw and opponent will be determined by ongoing R2 results. One thing is certain: the world No. 1 will arrive with more miles on the body than any top seed in recent memory. How he responds will define his US Open.
