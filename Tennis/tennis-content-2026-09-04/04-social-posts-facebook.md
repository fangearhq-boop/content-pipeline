### STORY 1: Eala def. Oliynykova — Long-Form
**Posting Window:** 8:00 AM ET

Alex Eala just made history at the US Open.

The No. 17 seed from the Philippines defeated Ukraine's Oleksandra Oliynykova 6-1, 6-4 at Louis Armstrong Stadium — and with that win, she became the first Filipino player ever to reach the third round of the US Open.

Earlier this year on clay in Strasbourg, Oliynykova had beaten Eala 3-6, 7-5, 6-3. That was the only blemish on their head-to-head. On hard court in New York, Eala made it look effortless. She dropped just five games. First set — clinical. Second set — kept her foot on the gas.

Add this to R1 where she beat Stoiana 6-1, 6-2, and Eala has dropped just 11 games in two matches at these championships. If you're looking for a player who might do something unexpected in the second week, she's worth your attention.

Who does she face next? The R3 draw has been kind. She's in good position.

This is what it looks like when a young champion finds her best tennis at the right moment.

### STORY 2: Zverev def. Halys — Long-Form
**Posting Window:** 10:30 AM ET

Alexander Zverev is making the US Open look impossibly hard for himself — and somehow still winning.

The world No. 1 defeated France's Quentin Halys 6-4, 4-6, 7-6(3), 6-7(3), 6-3 in more than four and a half hours Thursday night, advancing to the third round of the 2026 US Open. It was his second consecutive five-set match in this tournament. In the first round, he defeated Sonego in 4 hours and 53 minutes. Combined, Zverev has now played nearly 10 hours of tennis across just two matches.

No top seed has ever done this before in the Open Era — played back-to-back five-setters to open a Grand Slam. Zverev is charting new territory.

The match had a pivotal moment in the third set. Halys led 4-1 and had a break point to go up 5-1. Zverev saved it. Won the set 7-6 in the tiebreak. Then Halys took the fourth in another tiebreak. Then Zverev closed it out 6-3.

That's what it looks like when someone refuses to lose. Whether this level of physical output is sustainable — that is the question. There are going to be a lot of eyes on Zverev's legs in R3.

### STORY 3: Gauff def. Badosa — Long-Form
**Posting Window:** 12:30 PM ET

The defending champion is rolling.

Coco Gauff defeated Paula Badosa 6-4, 7-6(7/5) at Arthur Ashe Stadium on Thursday night, advancing to the US Open third round in just under two hours. Gauff looked in control through most of the match; Badosa gave her problems in the second set, taking it to a tiebreak, but the American closed it out 7-5 in the breaker.

Gauff now faces Cristina Bucsa in R3. She has dropped just one set through two rounds at these championships. The defending champion is looking every bit like a genuine contender for another title.

### STORY 4: Rybakina def. Bouzas Maneiro — Long-Form
**Posting Window:** 12:30 PM ET

Less than two weeks ago, Elena Rybakina couldn't walk without pain.

The No. 2 seed retired from her Cincinnati quarterfinal on August 21 with a left ankle injury and arrived at the US Open amid questions about whether she could compete at full capacity.

On Thursday she dispatched Spain's Jessica Bouzas Maneiro 6-2, 6-4 in just over an hour — heavy groundstrokes, big serves, minimal errors. The Kazakh looked anything but injured. Her serve was dominant, her baseline game precise, and Bouzas Maneiro had no answers.

Rybakina is now into the US Open third round, and suddenly her draw looks interesting. If she continues playing at this level, she's a genuine threat to Sabalenka in the top half.
