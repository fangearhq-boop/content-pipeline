Paris Masters Day 2: Musetti Survives Four Match Points to Set Up Zverev Clash

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap

November 3, 2026

The second day of the Rolex Paris Masters produced a match that deserved a better name than "first round." Lorenzo Musetti beat Hubert Hurkacz 7-6(3), 7-6(10), and the score, clean and tidy in a bracket, does not capture what happened in the second-set tiebreak, where Hurkacz saved four match points before Musetti finally converted the tenth point to win 10-8.

Musetti next faces Alexander Zverev, the world No. 1 and top seed, in a second-round match that shapes up as one of the better contests of the week. Hurkacz departs having played a match he could have won several times over. That is the particular cruelty of tiebreaks: all the competitive quality in the world can be undone by four points in the wrong order.

The Tiebreak

The match itself was close throughout. Both sets went to tiebreaks, and neither player was significantly outplaying the other at the baseline. Musetti won the first tiebreak comfortably, 7-3. The second was different.

Hurkacz reached the tiebreak, fell behind, and produced four separate match points — the first at 6-5, then again at 7-6, 8-7, and 9-8. Musetti saved all four. At 9-8, in the fifth attempt to close the match, he converted. The crowd in La Défense Arena had been carried through a sequence that ended exactly the way a neutral would have wanted it to: in the most difficult possible manner for both players.

Musetti had been a finalist at other indoor hardcourt events this season. He carries into the Zverev match the form of a player who wins the close ones. Whether that extends to beating the world No. 1 in a second-round match is a different question.

Other Day 2 Results

Ugo Humbert beat Botic van de Zandschulp 7-6(5), 6-7(9), 6-3 in a three-set match that followed the same pattern as the headline contest: tight, competitive, decided by tiebreaks before Humbert found his level in the third. Humbert is a former Paris Masters finalist who plays his best indoors. He next faces either Brandon Nakashima or Matteo Berrettini in the second round.

Rafael Jodar, the ninth seed with significant Race to Turin implications behind every result, beat Alejandro Tabilo 6-2, 6-3 — the most efficient win of the day. Jodar entered Paris needing points to hold his qualifying position and left Monday with 45 of them. His second-round opponent will follow from Tuesday's matches.

Elsewhere, Alex Molcan defeated French wild card Titouan Droguet, and Ignacio Buse — who became the first Peruvian to win a match at this tournament on Day 1 — will face Andrey Medvedev in the next round.

What's Next

The tournament reaches its second round Wednesday when top seeds Carlos Alcaraz and Alexander Zverev enter the draw. Alcaraz, the No. 2 seed and Vienna champion, plays his opening match against Alex Michelsen or Jiří Lehečká. Zverev faces Musetti in the match that Day 2's headline contest set up.

Paris begins properly Wednesday. The first two days have thinned the field and produced the narratives — Djokovic's survival, Musetti's tiebreak drama — that the bigger names will now be expected to match.
