Djokovic Survives Shoulder Scare to Beat Vacherot in Paris — Turin Hopes Still Alive

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap

November 3, 2026

Novak Djokovic defeated Valentin Vacherot 6-7(5), 7-5, 6-1 in the first round of the Rolex Paris Masters on Monday, but the result needed extracting. For 90 minutes, the match looked like it might deliver the kind of early exit that would end the seven-time champion's season before it properly began. Then Djokovic found something, and the rest of the match was not close.

Vacherot arrived in Paris having won the Swiss Indoors Basel title on Sunday — beating Jiri Lehečká in three sets — and boarded a flight to France the same evening. He played his second match in as many days with the confidence of a man who had beaten Djokovic the one time they had met. He took the first set in a tiebreak and had a break opportunity in the second at 4-4 that would have put him a point from the match.

Djokovic saved three break points. Then he broke Vacherot to lead 5-4, served out the second set, and won the decider 6-1 in a passage of tennis that bore no resemblance to the first two sets. The turnaround from "medical timeout at 2-1 down" to "won the third set in 30 minutes" is the kind of shift that separates Djokovic from nearly everyone who has ever played the game.

The Shoulder and the Comeback

The medical timeout was the clearest sign of what this match cost him. Djokovic called the trainer at 2-1 down in the second set and received treatment on his right shoulder before returning to the court. Whether the treatment resolved anything or simply gave him a reset is not knowable from the outside. What followed suggests the latter mattered as much as the former.

He came back and broke immediately for 2-2. From that point the second set was close — Vacherot had break points at 4-4 that would have changed everything — but Djokovic navigated each of them. The third set carried none of that tension. He broke in the second game, consolidated, and did not look back. The final score of the decider was not a reflection of a competitive set.

The match lasted 2 hours and 35 minutes. It was Djokovic's first win at the 2026 Paris Masters and his latest step in a campaign with clear stakes attached to every point.

The Turin Calculation

Djokovic enters this tournament as the 11th player in the Race to Turin, sitting at approximately 2,940 points — 319 behind ninth-placed Rafael Jodar, who holds the final qualifying spot following Jannik Sinner's withdrawal from the remainder of the 2026 season. Paris is the last event of the Race. When the tournament concludes Sunday, the standings are final.

The minimum requirement analysts have set for Djokovic to qualify is a semifinal. Jodar won his own first-round match on Monday, so a single Djokovic win does not close the gap in isolation. The path requires Djokovic to keep winning and for Jodar's tournament to end before the final stages.

He next faces Rinky Hijikata, the Australian qualifier who beat Luciano Darderi in the first round. It is, on paper, a match Djokovic should win. But Djokovic's paper record in Paris — seven titles, the most of any player in the modern era — did not prevent Monday night from becoming a test he nearly failed.

What's Next

Djokovic plays Hijikata in the second round, with the match expected Tuesday or Wednesday. A win would set up a likely quarterfinal clash with Alexander Zverev, the world No. 1 and top seed, who enters the draw Wednesday with a first-round bye.

The shape of the week is this: every Djokovic win extends his season, and every Jodar loss creates an opening. Neither condition is guaranteed. But after Monday, he is still here. The Turin question remains unanswered, which is itself a form of progress for a player whose chances of qualifying have been in doubt for weeks.
