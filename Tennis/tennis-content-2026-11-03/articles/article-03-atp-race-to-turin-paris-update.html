ATP Race to Turin: Djokovic Wins, Jodar Wins, the Math Is Still Hard

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap

November 3, 2026

Monday at the Rolex Paris Masters was a day of controlled outcomes for the Race to Turin. The players who needed to win won. The gap, as a result, barely moved. Novak Djokovic defeated Valentin Vacherot in the first round. Rafael Jodar beat Alejandro Tabilo 6-2, 6-3. Both did what they had to do. The standings entering Tuesday are close to what they were entering Monday.

Paris is the last event of the ATP Race to Turin. When the tournament concludes Sunday, the standings are final. The ATP Finals begin in Turin on November 15. For Djokovic, and for several players within range of qualifying, this week is the season.

The Current Picture

Alexander Zverev, Carlos Alcaraz, Ben Shelton, and Daniil Medvedev have already qualified for the ATP Finals. Their places are secure regardless of what happens in Paris. That leaves four spots for the remaining field, with Djokovic among those still outside looking in.

Djokovic enters the week approximately 319 points behind ninth-placed Jodar, who holds the last qualifying spot following Jannik Sinner's withdrawal for the remainder of 2026. Paris is worth up to 1,000 points to the champion and 600 to a finalist. Analysts have set a semifinal as the minimum Djokovic requires to have a realistic chance of overtaking Jodar, and that assumes Jodar does not reach the later stages himself.

Jodar's 6-2, 6-3 win over Tabilo on Monday was efficient and points-positive. He is through to the second round with his lead intact. The path for Djokovic to close 319 points requires Jodar's tournament to end while Djokovic's continues — a specific combination that is possible but not within Djokovic's control.

The Draw Complicates Everything

Three of the four confirmed qualifiers — Zverev, Alcaraz, and Medvedev — are in the Paris draw. They will absorb points from the contenders around them. Every loss by a Race hopeful against a confirmed qualifier returns nothing; every win extends their campaign and adds to the standing.

Djokovic's potential path runs through Rinky Hijikata in the second round, then likely into a quarterfinal against Zverev. That is a large ask — but Djokovic has won seven Paris titles, which suggests the draw does not automatically end his campaign. He reached the third set on Monday in good enough form to dominate the decider. Whether he can maintain that through the week, with a shoulder that required treatment, is a question that only the matches will answer.

What's Next

The second round begins Tuesday and runs through Wednesday when Alcaraz and Zverev enter. The Race standings will update meaningfully through each day as contenders win or lose. By Thursday, the picture should be substantially clearer — either Djokovic is still alive with quarterfinal tennis ahead, or Paris has produced its usual early ending for a player who has never solved this tournament for Turin purposes.

The week is short. Six days remain. Every match now has a points tag attached to it, and the players who are still alive in the Race feel that differently from the ones who are already through.
