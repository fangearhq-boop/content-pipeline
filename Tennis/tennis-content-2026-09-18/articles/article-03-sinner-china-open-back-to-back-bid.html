China Open Field Set: Sinner Bids for First Back-to-Back Title Since Djokovic in 2015

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | September 18, 2026

The 2026 China Open entry list is officially confirmed, and it may be the deepest field in the tournament's history. Seven of the world's top 10 players will gather at the National Tennis Center in Beijing for a main draw running September 30 through October 6, with the draw ceremony scheduled for September 28.

At the center of the storyline is a historical challenge: Jannik Sinner, the defending champion, is attempting to become the first man since Novak Djokovic — in 2015 — to successfully defend the China Open title. Djokovic himself is also in the draw, returning after an 11-year absence. Both men arrive in Beijing with something to prove.

Sinner's Return

Sinner enters Beijing carrying the weight of a three-month absence from competitive tennis. The world No. 1 has been sidelined since early summer with an extra-articular tendinous inflammation in his right knee, missing the Canadian Open, Cincinnati, and the US Open entirely — an event he had won in 2024. His last match was months ago. China Open will be his first competitive action of the fall.

In 2025, Sinner's China Open campaign was clinical: he demolished Learner Tien 6-2, 6-2 in the final to claim the title in dominant fashion. A successful title defense would put his name alongside Djokovic in the tournament's record books as back-to-back champions — a feat that has not been achieved since Djokovic himself defended the crown in 2014 and 2015.

The physical test of returning after three months away at a high-stakes ATP 500 event will be real. Whether Sinner's knee holds and whether his game is immediately tournament-sharp remain the open questions.

Djokovic's 29-0 Record

The most extraordinary statistic attached to this draw belongs to Djokovic. His all-time record at the China Open stands at an unblemished 29-0 — six titles, six finals, and not a single loss at this venue across his career. His most recent title came in 2015, when he defeated Rafael Nadal in the final to complete a consecutive defense. No player has won more China Open titles.

Djokovic last competed in Beijing in 2015. After 11 years away, he returns in starkly different circumstances: ranked No. 12 following a first-round US Open exit, outside the top 10 for the first time since 2002, and widely described as being at the defining crossroads of his late career. But 29-0 is 29-0. Whatever his current ranking, the man has never lost a match in Beijing.

Alexander Zverev (No. 2) arrives in Beijing fresh off his US Open title, adding a third major storyline to an already compelling field. Felix Auger-Aliassime (No. 4), Daniil Medvedev, Flavio Cobolli, Alex de Minaur, and Jakub Mensik complete a draw that could have its top section torn apart by the first week.

What's Next

The China Open draw ceremony takes place September 28, revealing first-round matchups and the bracket path each contender will face. Main draw action begins September 30 at the National Tennis Center in Beijing, with the ATP 500 event offering 500 ranking points to the winner. For Sinner, the challenge is defending both the title and his No. 1 ranking. For Djokovic, it's about proving that the US Open was a blip, and that Beijing's magic hasn't faded. And for Zverev, it's about extending the most dominant season any ATP player has produced in 2026.
