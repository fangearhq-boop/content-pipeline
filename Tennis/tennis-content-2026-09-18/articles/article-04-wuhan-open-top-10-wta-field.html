Wuhan Open Completes All-Top-10 WTA Field: Swiatek Joins, Sabalenka Defends 20-1 Record

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | September 18, 2026

The 2026 Wuhan Open (October 12–18) has officially confirmed all 10 of the WTA's top-ranked players, making it one of the most stacked non-Slam events in the history of women's tennis. The latest entrant to confirm is Iga Swiatek, who joins an already remarkable field and completes what is now a clean sweep of the current top 10.

The complete field: Elena Rybakina (No. 1), Aryna Sabalenka (No. 2), Jessica Pegula (No. 3), Coco Gauff (No. 4), Mirra Andreeva (No. 5), Linda Noskova (No. 6), Elina Svitolina (No. 7), Karolina Muchovà (No. 8), Iga Swiatek (No. 9), and Marta Kostyuk (No. 10).

Sabalenka's Unmatched Wuhan Record

No player in modern women's tennis has dominated a single venue the way Aryna Sabalenka has dominated Wuhan. Her all-time record at the Optics Valley International Tennis Centre stands at 20-1, built across three title runs — 2018, 2019, and 2024. She is the defending champion. Her only loss at this event came in last year's semifinals, to Pegula. Outside of that single defeat, she has been untouchable in this city.

Sabalenka arrives in Wuhan as the world No. 2, displaced from her No. 1 ranking by Rybakina's US Open triumph in September. If her 2026 can be redeemed by reclaiming dominance on the Asian Swing, Wuhan is where that story gets written first.

Rybakina's First Test as No. 1

For Elena Rybakina, Wuhan is a different kind of milestone. The 2026 US Open champion — who also won the Australian Open earlier this year — arrives for the first time as the world's top-ranked women's tennis player. This will be her first WTA 1000 event wearing the No. 1 ranking, and it comes against a field that contains the one player who has beaten her more than almost anyone else.

Rybakina and Sabalenka's head-to-head stands at 11-8 in Sabalenka's favor across their careers. But Rybakina has won their last three meetings. In Wuhan, Sabalenka's familiarity with the venue is the counterweight to Rybakina's recent form and momentum.

The rest of the field is no easier. Gauff is the defending Wuhan champion. Andreeva won Roland Garros this year. Noskova won Wimbledon. Swiatek, despite a relatively quiet 2026 by her extraordinary standards, remains one of the most dangerous competitors when motivated. Pegula is the one player to have beaten Sabalenka at this event in recent years.

What's Next

The Wuhan Open first round begins October 12, preceded by the China Open in Beijing — where most of these players will also compete in the WTA Diamond event running September 30 through October 11. The Asian Swing's two-event stretch, with 1,000 ranking points and $4,088,211 in prize money at Wuhan alone, could dramatically reshape the WTA rankings heading into the year-end finals. The player who wins both Beijing and Wuhan would put themselves in an untouchable position atop the standings going into the WTA Finals in November.
