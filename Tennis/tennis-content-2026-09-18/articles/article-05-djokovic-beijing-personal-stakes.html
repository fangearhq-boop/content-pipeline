Djokovic Sends Message to Beijing: What His China Open Return Means at 39

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | September 18, 2026

Novak Djokovic didn't just confirm his entry into the 2026 China Open. He wrote a message to Chinese fans in both English and Chinese — "So excited to be back" — and the response was immediate. Fans who remember his six titles in Beijing over a decade of dominance responded with genuine emotion. Djokovic in Beijing is a story that generates its own gravity.

But beneath the warm reception is something more urgent for the 39-year-old Serb. This China Open return is not merely sentimental. It is, at this particular moment in his career, a mission.

The Lowest Point in Two Decades

The 2026 US Open delivered Djokovic one of the most painful results of his professional life. Seeded No. 5 and widely expected to push deep into the draw, he was upset in the first round by Argentine Mariano Navone in five sets — 7-6(5), 5-7, 4-6, 6-2, 6-1 — in a match that lasted four hours and 36 minutes. It was his first Grand Slam first-round loss since the 2006 Australian Open.

The aftermath in the rankings was immediate. Djokovic dropped from No. 5 to No. 12. For the first time since 2002 — when Roger Federer, Rafael Nadal, and Djokovic himself were still building their legacies — he is outside the ATP top 10. The era of the Big Three has now fully closed, and Djokovic finds himself navigating a circuit that belongs to a new generation.

His path back into the top 10 requires points, and the China Open — an ATP 500 event offering up to 500 ranking points — is the first meaningful opportunity to reclaim them.

A Venue That Has Never Beaten Him

Djokovic's all-time record at the China Open is 29-0. Six titles. Not a single defeat. His titles came in 2009, 2010, 2012, 2013, 2014, and 2015 — and the last of those, in 2015, came when he successfully defended the title he had won the year before. He is the only man in the Open Era to defend the China Open crown.

He has not played in Beijing since that 2015 triumph. Eleven years later, he arrives to a city that has never seen him lose. Whether that remarkable record speaks to genuine affinity with the conditions, the hard-court surface, the altitude of Beijing, or simply the statistical reality that elite players dominate their best venues is a matter of debate. What's not debatable is the record itself.

At 39, with 24 Grand Slam titles, Djokovic doesn't need more trophies to validate his legacy. But professional athletes don't enter tournaments to run out the clock on their careers. He enters Beijing to compete — and to win.

What's Next

Djokovic's Beijing campaign begins with the draw ceremony on September 28, which will reveal his first-round opponent and potential path through the bracket. Main draw action starts September 30 at the National Tennis Center. A deep run in Beijing — quarterfinal or better — would push him meaningfully back toward the top 10. After Beijing, the Paris Masters in November (November 2–8) represents the final significant ranking event before the 2026 regular season closes. If Djokovic's China Open return delivers the results he expects, the narrative of his season could shift dramatically before year's end.
