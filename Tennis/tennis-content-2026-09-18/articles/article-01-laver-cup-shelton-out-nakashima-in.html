Laver Cup Team World Reshuffled: Shelton Out With Davis Cup Conflict, Nakashima Steps In

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | September 18, 2026

With the 2026 Laver Cup just seven days away, Team World has undergone a significant reshuffling — losing two players in the space of a week and forcing captain Andre Agassi to rebuild his lineup on the eve of the event.

Ben Shelton, the US Open runner-up who came within two sets of defeating Alexander Zverev in Saturday's final, has withdrawn from Laver Cup due to a scheduling conflict with Davis Cup. Shelton is committed to play for Team USA against the Czech Republic beginning Friday — a tie that runs directly over the Laver Cup preparation window. His replacement is Brandon Nakashima, currently ranked No. 16 on the ATP Tour, who will make his Laver Cup debut at The O2 Arena in London.

The news comes on top of an earlier withdrawal: Tommy Paul, who had been named to the original squad, pulled out on September 12 after suffering an elbow injury at the US Open. His replacement, Francisco Cerundolo of Argentina, will be making his fourth consecutive Laver Cup appearance.

The Updated Team World

The revised Team World lineup under captain Andre Agassi now reads: Taylor Fritz, Alex de Minaur, Alexander Bublik, Learner Tien, Brandon Nakashima, and Francisco Cerundolo. Two Americans gone, replaced by players from the United States and Argentina respectively.

Team Europe, captained by Yannick Noah, remains unchanged: Carlos Alcaraz, Alexander Zverev, Casper Ruud, Flavio Cobolli, Jakub Mensik, and Rafael Jodar. World No. 1 Jannik Sinner had already been absent from the European squad, sitting out with the knee injury that kept him away from the US Open as well.

Team World still carries genuine firepower. De Minaur and Fritz are proven performers on the Laver Cup stage, and Bublik's unpredictable game can unsettle anyone. Nakashima is in strong form — he reached the Cincinnati Masters semifinal earlier this season and is a solid hard-court player. Cerundolo adds South American clay-court craft, though Laver Cup uses hard courts.

The Rematch That Won't Happen

One of the most anticipated matchups of the Laver Cup had been a potential Zverev-Shelton rematch — just nine days after the pair played one of the more watchable US Open finals in recent years. Zverev won that Sunday final 6-3, 7-6(2), 5-7, 6-2 to claim his second Grand Slam title of 2026, with Shelton serving for an upset in the third set before Zverev closed it out in four.

That rematch is now off the table. Instead, Shelton heads into Davis Cup duty with Team USA, fulfilling his obligation to his country — a commitment that tennis's team-competition calendar made inevitable once the dates collided.

Nakashima, who is two years younger than Shelton at 21, will carry the burden of representing American tennis at The O2. He will be eager to prove himself on the Laver Cup stage for the first time.

What's Next

The 2026 Laver Cup runs September 25–27 at The O2 Arena in London, with Team Europe entering as heavy favorites despite their 2025 defeat — the first time Team World had ever won the event. Agassi's side will need to overcome the squad changes and find immediate chemistry. Full schedule and session details will be released closer to the opening day. For Nakashima and Cerundolo, Laver Cup offers a high-profile platform; for the competition itself, the changes ensure this edition will be remembered for its drama even before a ball is struck.
