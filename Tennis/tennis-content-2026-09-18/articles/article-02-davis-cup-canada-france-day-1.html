Davis Cup QR2 Opens Tonight: Auger-Aliassime Leads Canada vs. France in Quebec City

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | September 18, 2026

The 2026 Davis Cup Qualifiers 2nd Round is underway tonight, and the marquee tie has Canada hosting France at Centre Vidéotron in Quebec City. First serve is scheduled for 5:00 PM ET, with world No. 4 Félix Auger-Aliassime leading Team Canada into one of the biggest matches of the calendar year.

Seven nations are competing across multiple ties this week for seven spots at the Davis Cup Final 8 in Bologna, Italy — the climactic team event scheduled for November 24–29. The winner of Canada vs. France earns a direct berth.

Canada's Lineup

FAA headlines a Canadian squad that mixes experience with emerging talent. He has been confirmed to play both singles and doubles — carrying the lion's share of Canada's competitive burden as he has done throughout his Davis Cup career.

Canada's No. 2 singles player is Liam Draxl, who opens Day 1 play in the first match against France's No. 2 singles. Duncan Chan will make his Davis Cup debut alongside FAA in the doubles rubber scheduled for Day 2.

Playing at home in Quebec City is no small thing. Centre Vidéotron is a loud, modern arena, and the Canadian crowd will create a charged atmosphere from the first ball. FAA, who grew up in Montreal and holds dual French-Canadian citizenship, carries the home city's pride on his shoulders.

France's Challenge

France brings a legitimate squad to Quebec City. Arthur Fils, ranked No. 7 in the world, is the standout name — and a player who has made himself one of the most dangerous on the ATP Tour in 2026. Arthur Rinderknech, Quentin Halys, and Pierre-Hugues Herbert complete a team that has the depth to challenge even a home-court Canada.

France has a strong Davis Cup pedigree. This tie is not a foregone conclusion. The best-of-five format — two singles matches per day, doubles in the middle, followed by two reverse singles — means momentum can shift dramatically between Day 1 and Day 2.

The Stakes

Davis Cup qualifying rounds are a one-tie knockout — lose this, and your nation is out until next year. For Canada, the opportunity to represent their country at the Final 8 in Bologna is worth every effort. FAA, who has won Davis Cup titles before and understands what the team competition means, will be at his most motivated.

Other ties running simultaneously include Great Britain vs. Ecuador at Copper Box Arena in London, Austria vs. Belgium in Vienna, and Chile vs. Germany in Santiago. The global nature of Davis Cup Qualifier week makes this one of the most exciting days on the tennis calendar.

What's Next

Day 1 coverage begins at 5:00 PM ET from Centre Vidéotron, Quebec City. Match 1 sees Liam Draxl square off against France's first singles player, followed by FAA vs. Quentin Halys. Day 2 on September 19 will feature doubles, then the two reverse singles to decide the tie. Results will determine which nation — Canada or France — joins the gathering of the world's best teams in Bologna, Italy in November.
