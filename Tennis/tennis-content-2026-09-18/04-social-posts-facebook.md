### STORY 1: Laver Cup Team World Reshuffled

**FB Long-Form** | 10:30 AM ET
Just seven days before Laver Cup, Team World has lost two players — and the event just got a lot more interesting.

Ben Shelton, the US Open runner-up who came within two sets of beating Alexander Zverev in the final, has withdrawn from Laver Cup. The reason? Davis Cup. Shelton is committed to play for Team USA against the Czech Republic starting Friday — scheduling that puts the Laver Cup clash directly in conflict. He'll be replaced by Brandon Nakashima, who is ranked No. 16 and will make his Laver Cup debut.

The other change happened earlier this month: Tommy Paul withdrew on September 12 after suffering an elbow injury at the US Open. His replacement is Francisco Cerundolo, who is making his fourth consecutive Laver Cup appearance.

So the new Team World, under captain Andre Agassi, looks like this: Taylor Fritz, Alex de Minaur, Alexander Bublik, Learner Tien, Brandon Nakashima, and Francisco Cerundolo. Team Europe — Alcaraz, Zverev, Ruud, Cobolli, Mensik, Jodar under Yannick Noah — remains unchanged.

The biggest casualty of this reshuffle? The Zverev-Shelton rematch. Nine days after their intense US Open final, tennis fans were hoping to see them go at it again on the Laver Cup stage. That won't be happening now.

### STORY 2: Davis Cup QR2 Day 1 — Canada vs. France

**FB Long-Form** | 12:30 PM ET
It starts tonight — and Canada is ready.

The Davis Cup Qualifiers 2nd Round gets underway at Centre Vidéotron in Quebec City, with Canada hosting France in what could be the most-watched tie of the round. First serve is scheduled for 5:00 PM ET.

Leading the charge for Canada is world No. 4 Félix Auger-Aliassime, who will pull double duty — playing both singles and doubles for his home country. FAA is fresh off a deep US Open run, is in top form, and gets to play on home soil in front of a packed Quebec City crowd. That home-court energy could be decisive.

France isn't here to watch, though. Their lineup includes Arthur Fils (No. 7 in the world), Arthur Rinderknech, Quentin Halys, and Pierre-Hugues Herbert. These are legitimate Davis Cup veterans. This is a real contest.

The format is best-of-five: two singles matches today, doubles and two reverse singles tomorrow. The winner earns a spot at the Davis Cup Final 8 in Bologna, Italy (November 24–29), joining host nation Italy and other already-qualified teams.

For FAA and Team Canada, this is a chance to do something special in front of their home fans. For France, it's a reminder that Davis Cup is never easy on the road.

### STORY 3: China Open — Sinner's Back-to-Back Bid

**FB Long-Form** | 3:00 PM ET
The 2026 China Open is shaping up to be one of the most compelling ATP 500 events in recent memory — and it hasn't even started yet.

The entry list is officially confirmed, and seven of the world's top 10 players will gather at the National Tennis Center in Beijing. The main draw runs September 30 to October 6, with the draw ceremony on September 28.

The headline narrative is this: defending champion Jannik Sinner is attempting to become the first man to successfully defend the China Open title since Novak Djokovic did it in 2015. Sinner beat Learner Tien 6-2, 6-2 in last year's final — but he arrives in Beijing after three months away from the tour due to a knee injury. This will be his first competitive match since early June.

And waiting for him? Djokovic himself. The 39-year-old Serb returns to Beijing after 11 years away, carrying an incomprehensible 29-0 all-time record at this event and six titles. He's coming off the lowest point of his recent career — a first-round US Open exit — and has one clear goal: return to the top 10.

Add in Alexander Zverev (fresh off the US Open title), Felix Auger-Aliassime (No. 4), Daniil Medvedev, and more. This field is historically deep.

### STORY 5: Djokovic's Beijing Mission

**FB Long-Form** | 9:00 PM ET
Novak Djokovic wrote a message to Chinese tennis fans this week — and the response has been emotional.

"So excited to be back," he posted in both English and Chinese, confirming his entry into the 2026 China Open in Beijing. For millions of fans who have watched him win six titles at this event, the news of his return after 11 years away carries real meaning.

But for Djokovic — who will turn 40 next year — this is more than a sentimental homecoming. It's a mission.

Following his shocking first-round exit at the 2026 US Open (his first Grand Slam first-round loss since 2006), Djokovic dropped to No. 12 in the ATP rankings. He is, for the first time since 2002, outside the top 10. The goal is simple: get back in before the year is over. A strong run in Beijing — which counts as an ATP 500 event worth up to 500 ranking points — would go a long way toward that.

And Beijing, historically, belongs to Djokovic. His 29-0 all-time record. His six titles. His last successful title defense in 2015. No other city in the world has treated him the way Beijing has.

At 39, with 24 Grand Slam titles and an unmatched legacy, Novak Djokovic doesn't need to prove anything. But he's going to Beijing anyway — because that's who he is.
