WTA Asian Swing Preview: Rybakina Leads All-Top-10 Field to China and Wuhan

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | September 17, 2026

The WTA calendar's Asian Swing is shaping up to be the deepest pair of back-to-back tournaments outside of a Grand Slam — and newly-crowned world No. 1 Elena Rybakina arrives as the clear favorite to dominate both stops.

The swing features two major events: the China Open in Beijing (September 30) and the Wuhan Open (October 12), offering a combined 2,000 WTA ranking points and more than $8 million in prize money. The Wuhan Open alone has confirmed every single player from the WTA top 10, marking one of the most complete fields assembled for a non-Grand Slam event in recent memory.

Rybakina Enters as Runaway Favorite

Elena Rybakina's 2026 season has been nothing short of historic. The Kazakhstani star won the Australian Open in January and the US Open in September, capturing two of the four Grand Slams in a single calendar year and becoming the 30th woman to reach world No. 1. Rybakina now carries 9,901 WTA ranking points into the Asian Swing, holding a lead of more than 1,000 points over No. 2 Aryna Sabalenka.

She arrives in Asia with wind at her back and the confidence of a player who has mastered the biggest stages. Hard courts suit her game particularly well, and both Beijing and Wuhan are played on hard surfaces.

Sabalenka Looks to Reclaim Ground

Aryna Sabalenka, who held the world No. 1 ranking for 99 consecutive weeks before Rybakina overtook her, remains the most dangerous rival. In Wuhan, where Sabalenka has won the title three times, she plays on arguably her best non-Grand-Slam surface. Reclaiming the No. 1 spot requires consistently outperforming Rybakina across the fall calendar — and Wuhan is the ideal starting point.

The two players have met in the 2026 US Open final, where Rybakina won 6-4, 5-7, 6-2 to claim the title. Their head-to-head rivalry will likely define the WTA's top narrative through the end of 2026 and into 2027.

A Loaded Supporting Cast

The Wuhan field extends well beyond the top two. Jessica Pegula (No. 3), one of the most consistent performers on tour, has been a fixture in WTA semifinals all season. Coco Gauff, the defending Wuhan champion, will attempt to retain her title in front of a partisan Chinese crowd. Mirra Andreeva — the Roland Garros champion and perhaps the most remarkable rising story in women's tennis this year — brings clay-court confidence to a hard-court event and has shown the ability to win on any surface. Wimbledon champion Linda Noskova rounds out the top tier of contenders. Marta Kostyuk, who broke into the top 10 for the first time in September, brings career-best form and nothing to lose.

What's Next

The China Open in Beijing opens September 30 with qualifying rounds and first-round play. The Wuhan Open begins October 12 at the Optics Valley International Tennis Centre. Both events offer 1,000 WTA ranking points to the winner. Given the field assembled, both events are likely to produce deep runs by the world's best players and have direct implications on the Race to the WTA Finals in Riyadh later in the season.
