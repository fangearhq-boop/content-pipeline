Davis Cup Qualifiers 2nd Round Preview: Seven Spots, Starting Tomorrow

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | September 17, 2026

The road to Bologna gets serious starting tomorrow.

The Davis Cup Qualifiers 2nd Round gets underway September 18 across multiple venues worldwide, with seven nations fighting for spots in the Davis Cup Final 8 — the year-end team competition taking place November 24–29 in Bologna, Italy. The ties run through the weekend, concluding September 20.

Canada vs. France: The Tie to Watch

The marquee second-round matchup puts Canada against France at Centre Vidéotron in Quebec City, a tie that begins Thursday, September 18 and runs through Friday, September 19. Canada enters as the home nation with the crowd advantage and one of the biggest names in the draw: Felix Auger-Aliassime.

Currently ranked No. 4 in the world, FAA is fresh off a deep US Open campaign and brings both the clay and hard court versatility Davis Cup demands. Home soil in Quebec City — where the Canadian crowd is among the most passionate in North American tennis — gives Canada a genuine advantage that can't be quantified in rankings alone.

France arrives as one of the tournament favorites. The French team carries a storied Davis Cup tradition and strong depth across the ATP rankings. The tie is projected to be a two-day battle requiring all available match slots.

Other Key Ties This Weekend

Beyond Canada versus France, several other nations compete across September 19–20. Chile hosts Germany in Santiago in a tie that pits South American clay-court specialists against Germany's depth. The winner in each tie earns a direct berth to Bologna, where the Final 8 format has produced some of the most electric team tennis in the sport's history.

Italy, as the host nation of the Final 8, receives an automatic spot. Australia and other nations have already secured their places through earlier qualifying rounds. The second-round ties decide the remaining seven berths.

The Stakes: Bologna in November

The Davis Cup Final 8 in Bologna (November 24–29) is the culmination of the season-long team competition that replaced the traditional Davis Cup format. Eight nations play a knockout tournament across six days in a single city, with the atmosphere routinely described as one of the most electric in all of tennis.

For the players involved in this weekend's qualifying ties, the motivation is clear: represent your country on tennis's biggest team stage. For a player like Auger-Aliassime, Davis Cup victories have historically provided a different emotional resonance than individual ATP results.

What's Next

All second-round qualifying ties conclude by Sunday, September 20. Results will determine the complete eight-nation field for Bologna. The Davis Cup Final 8 draw is expected shortly after qualifying concludes, setting the bracket for November's team championship.
