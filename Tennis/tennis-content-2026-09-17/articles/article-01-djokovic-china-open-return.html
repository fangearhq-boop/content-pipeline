Djokovic Returns to China Open After 11-Year Absence, Seeking Top-10 Comeback

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | September 17, 2026

Novak Djokovic is coming back to Beijing — and he has unfinished business.

The 24-time Grand Slam champion confirmed Wednesday he will compete at the China Open, the ATP 500 event in Beijing running September 30 through October 6, ending an 11-year absence from a tournament where he owns the most titles of any player in the event's history. In a bilingual post shared across his social media channels — written in both English and Chinese — the 39-year-old Serb said he was "so excited to be back."

It's a return that carries more weight than a simple schedule confirmation. Djokovic arrives in Beijing at a crossroads in his late career, looking to rebuild after the most shocking result of his professional life.

A Stunning Fall at Flushing Meadows

Earlier this month, Djokovic suffered a first-round exit at the 2026 US Open — his first loss in the opening round of a Grand Slam since the 2006 Australian Open, ending a streak of 20 years without such a defeat. Argentine Mariano Navone stunned the No. 5 seed in five sets, 7-6(5), 5-7, 4-6, 6-2, 6-1, in a grueling four-hour, 36-minute battle at Arthur Ashe Stadium.

The loss dropped Djokovic from No. 5 to No. 12 in the ATP rankings — his lowest position since 2002 — and left him outside the top 10 for the first time in over two decades. With the Race to Turin already dominated by Jannik Sinner (No. 2, qualified) and Alexander Zverev (No. 1, qualified), Djokovic's path to the Nitto ATP Finals has effectively closed for 2026.

But China Open is still very much in his sights.

A Tournament Built for Djokovic

No player has won the China Open more times than Novak Djokovic. His six titles in Beijing — 2012, 2013, 2014, 2015, and beyond — make it one of the most reliable stops on his calendar. His most recent triumph in the Chinese capital came in 2015, after which he did not return to the tournament.

The reunion is not purely sentimental. Djokovic has a clear strategic goal: returning to the ATP top 10 before the year ends. A run in Beijing — even a semifinal — could make a meaningful difference in his rankings before the Paris Masters in November closes the regular season.

A Stacked Field Awaits

Djokovic's return comes in the context of a China Open field that may be the deepest in the tournament's history. Defending champion Jannik Sinner — who beat Learner Tien in the 2025 final — is returning from a knee injury that kept him out of the US Open, targeting Beijing as his comeback event. Alexander Zverev, fresh off winning the US Open title, is also in the entry list. Felix Auger-Aliassime (No. 4), Daniil Medvedev, and Alex de Minaur round out a field featuring seven of the ATP's top-10 players.

For Djokovic, it's the kind of high-stakes environment he has thrived in throughout his career. Six titles in Beijing suggest the conditions — hard court, fast-paced ATP 500 format — suit his game well.

What's Next

Djokovic's China Open campaign begins September 30 in Beijing. Tournament draw details and his first-round schedule will be announced closer to the event. His results in Beijing could determine whether he can return to the top 10 before the Paris Masters (November 2–8) and whether he can mount a case for inclusion in any end-of-year exhibition invitations. All eyes are on the six-time champion's first steps back in Beijing — and whether 11 years away from this court has dimmed any of his brilliance there.
