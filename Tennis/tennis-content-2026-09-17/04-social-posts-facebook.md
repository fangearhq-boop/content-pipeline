### STORY 1: Djokovic China Open Return

**FB Long-Form** | 10:30 AM ET
He's been gone for 11 years. Now he's coming back.

Novak Djokovic has confirmed he'll play the China Open in Beijing starting September 30 — marking his first appearance at the ATP 500 event since he won his sixth and most recent title there in 2015. The announcement came via a bilingual social media post from the 24-time Grand Slam champion, who wrote "So excited to be back" in both English and Chinese.

The return comes at a significant moment in Djokovic's career. Just weeks ago, he suffered the most shocking loss of his career at the 2026 US Open, falling to Argentine Mariano Navone in the first round in five sets over four hours and 36 minutes — his first Grand Slam first-round exit since the 2006 Australian Open. The defeat dropped him from No. 5 to No. 12 in the ATP rankings.

But Beijing has always been a happy hunting ground for the Serb. Six titles. Six finals. A perfect record at the China Open. Now at 39, he returns with a clear mission: prove that the US Open exit was a blip, not a sign of things to come — and climb back into the top 10.

He'll face the defending champion Jannik Sinner (also returning from a knee injury), world No. 2 Alexander Zverev, and No. 4 Felix Auger-Aliassime in what promises to be one of the deepest China Open fields in years.

### STORY 2: Laver Cup Full Squads

**FB Long-Form** | 12:30 PM ET
The matchups are set. The stakes are real. Laver Cup 2026 is here in 8 days.

Both full six-player squads have been confirmed for the ninth edition of the Laver Cup, taking place September 25–27 at The O2 Arena in London.

Team Europe, captained by French legend Yannick Noah, will be led by Carlos Alcaraz and US Open champion Alexander Zverev. They're joined by Casper Ruud, Flavio Cobolli, Jakub Mensik, and Rafael Jodar. One glaring absence: world No. 1 Jannik Sinner, who is sitting out with a knee injury and is instead targeting a return at the China Open.

Team World, captained by Andre Agassi, counters with a formidable lineup: Ben Shelton (fresh from his US Open final run), Taylor Fritz, Alex de Minaur, Alexander Bublik, Learner Tien, and Tommy Paul.

Team Europe has won every single Laver Cup since the tournament began in 2017 — until last year, when Team World broke through for their first-ever title. Now the Americans and Australians on Team World are looking to make it back-to-back.

With Sinner missing and Agassi's squad full of firepower, this is the most competitive Laver Cup in years. Who are you rooting for?

### STORY 3: Davis Cup Qualifiers

**FB Long-Form** | 10:30 AM ET
Davis Cup qualifying begins tomorrow — and it's one of the most competitive draws in years.

The second round of 2026 Davis Cup qualifying gets underway Thursday, September 18, with seven spots up for grabs at the Davis Cup Final 8 in Bologna, Italy (November 24–29). The marquee matchup sees Canada take on France at Centre Vidéotron in Quebec City over two days, September 18–19.

Leading Canada is Felix Auger-Aliassime, currently ranked No. 4 in the world and fresh off a deep run at the US Open. If anyone gives Canada a chance against the French, it's FAA — and playing on home soil in Quebec City gives Canada a clear advantage. France, one of the tournament favorites, is expected to be led by Ugo Humbert.

Other second-round ties run through the weekend, including Chile hosting Germany in Santiago. The winners from each tie will join Italy (host nation and automatic qualifier), Australia, and other already-qualified nations in Bologna for the year-end team competition.

Davis Cup remains one of tennis's most electric team events — the roar of a home crowd can change everything. Which match are you watching this weekend?

### STORY 5: WTA Asian Swing

**FB Long-Form** | 6:00 PM ET
The WTA Asian Swing is coming — and the fields look absolutely loaded.

Starting with the China Open in Beijing (September 30) and continuing to the Wuhan Open (October 12), the Asian Swing will feature the deepest women's tennis fields outside of a Grand Slam. The Wuhan Open, in particular, has confirmed all top-10 WTA players, led by newly-crowned world No. 1 Elena Rybakina.

Rybakina — who won both the Australian Open and US Open in 2026 — enters the Asian Swing as the clear favorite. But she'll face stiff competition from No. 2 Aryna Sabalenka (a three-time Wuhan champion trying to reclaim the title and her No. 1 spot), No. 3 Jessica Pegula, defending Wuhan champion Coco Gauff, Roland Garros winner Mirra Andreeva, and Wimbledon champion Linda Noskova.

With 1,000 ranking points and $4 million in prize money at each stop, the stakes are enormous. The Asian Swing could reshape the WTA standings heading into the year-end tournaments.
