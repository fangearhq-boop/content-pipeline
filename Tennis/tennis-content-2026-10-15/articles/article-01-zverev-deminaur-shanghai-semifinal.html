Zverev Crushes de Minaur in Under 80 Minutes to Reach Shanghai Semifinals

By Marcus Cole

October 15, 2026 — Shanghai, China

Alexander Zverev sent a message to the rest of the Shanghai Masters field on Thursday: the ankle may be a concern, but the tennis is not.

The world No. 1 and top seed dismantled Alex de Minaur 6-4, 6-4 in the quarterfinals, completing his victory in under 80 minutes in a performance that was clinical, efficient, and entirely one-directional. Zverev broke de Minaur once in each set and never once faced a break point. He is now in the semifinals of the Shanghai Masters for the second consecutive year.

A Performance That Answered All Questions

The conversation around Zverev entering Thursday's match had partly been about his ankle. He had mentioned the issue after his grueling three-set win over Alexander Bublik in the Round of 16, noting that it was "acting up again." Some expected de Minaur — an elite mover who wins by making opponents hit one more ball — to exploit any discomfort.

He never got the chance. Zverev was sharp from the first game, controlling rallies with his heavy forehand and serving with the authority that has defined his 2026 season. De Minaur, who had not dropped a set or a service game entering the quarterfinal, could not find a way through.

The Australian is one of the fittest players on tour and a genuine threat to any opponent on the day. But Zverev was simply better — more direct, more precise, and unwilling to engage in the long exchanges that de Minaur thrives on. The 6-4, 6-4 scoreline was a fair reflection of how little resistance the top seed encountered.

Year-End No. 1 Playing Like It

Zverev's year-end World No. 1 status was mathematically confirmed earlier this week when Carlos Alcaraz was knocked out of the draw by Zizou Bergs. But the number had already been sealed on the court: Zverev has been the most consistently dominant player on the ATP Tour in 2026, and he is playing like it here in Shanghai.

He has yet to drop a set in this tournament. He won tight first sets against opponents who tested him, then tightened his game in the second. Against de Minaur, there was no tight first set at all — just 80 minutes of high-quality, relentless tennis.

His semifinal opponent will be Alejandro Davidovich Fokina, who advanced when young Frenchman Arthur Gea — the tournament's breakout story — retired with a right leg injury after just 22 minutes of their quarterfinal. Fokina is reaching his fourth career Masters 1000 semifinal. He has shown this week that he is capable of beating elite opponents on his best day.

The Other Side of the Draw

While Zverev and Fokina are confirmed for Saturday's first semifinal, the second is still to be decided. Daniil Medvedev and Tommy Paul will meet in one quarterfinal on Friday, with Casper Ruud and Rafael Jodar in the other. The two winners will play each other in a semifinal on Saturday.

Paul has had a remarkable 2026 season, reaching the quarterfinals at every ATP Masters 1000 hard-court event of the year. Medvedev, who has been in excellent form since his disqualification in Beijing, is the likely favorite. Jodar, who upset Hubert Hurkacz earlier in the draw, faces Ruud in a match that is harder to call.

A potential Zverev–Medvedev final remains in view. But first, Zverev needs to see off Davidovich Fokina — a player who has surprised before.

What's Next

Alexander Zverev faces Alejandro Davidovich Fokina in the Shanghai Masters semifinals on Saturday, October 17. The second semifinal will be between the winners of Friday's quarterfinals: Medvedev vs. Paul and Jodar vs. Ruud. The final is scheduled for Sunday, October 18.

Zverev is the heavy favorite. He is playing some of the best tennis of his career, and he has made it look easy at a tournament where nothing is supposed to be easy.
