Wuhan Open QF Field: Sabalenka Out, Ann Li In, and a Tournament Wide Open

By Elena Voss

October 15, 2026 — Wuhan, China

The Wuhan Open quarterfinals are arriving — and the draw looks nothing like anyone expected at the start of the week.

The three-time champion Aryna Sabalenka is gone, beaten by Ann Li in one of the year's great upsets. No. 2 seed Jessica Pegula is gone, eliminated by Jelena Ostapenko. In their place, a quarterfinal field that is as open and unpredictable as the Wuhan draw has been in years.

Ann Li: The Story of the Tournament

Ann Li, ranked outside the world's top 50, is in the quarterfinals of the Wuhan Open after defeating top seed Aryna Sabalenka 3-6, 6-4, 7-6(5) in one of the most extraordinary matches of the WTA season. The American saved nine match points before converting on the tenth to end Sabalenka's unbeaten Wuhan record — eight years, three titles, not a single loss.

Li now faces Janice Tjen for a spot in the quarterfinals. She is the tournament's biggest story and, after that win, one of the most watched players in women's tennis right now.

Ostapenko on a Run

Jelena Ostapenko is also making waves. The Latvian first knocked out No. 2 seed Jessica Pegula — saving three match points in the process — and then dispatched Leylah Fernandez 6-1, 1-6, 6-4 to reach her first Wuhan quarterfinal in nine years.

Ostapenko's game — aggressive, flat, and high-risk/high-reward — is well-suited to conditions that reward clean hitting. She is a genuine threat to whoever she faces in the quarterfinals.

Chwalinska's Breakthrough

Maja Chwalinska, the 16th seed, made her first-ever WTA 1000 quarterfinal on Wednesday, beating world No. 11 Iva Jovic 6-4, 6-1 in a performance that was far more dominant than her seeding would suggest. She faces Maria Sakkari in the quarterfinals.

Sakkari and Elina Svitolina both advanced in straight sets and are among the more experienced remaining players in a draw that has shed nearly all its top seeds.

Swiatek: Still Standing

Iga Swiatek, seeded seventh and with WTA Finals qualification still very much in play, is advancing through the Wuhan draw with the kind of purpose her ranking demands. She reached the Round of 16 after a dominant 6-2, 6-1 win over compatriot Magdalena Frech and faces her next opponent ahead of a projected quarterfinal appearance.

With Sabalenka out, Swiatek is now the highest remaining seed in the draw. A Wuhan title would be a significant statement — both for her WTA Finals position and for her place in the year-end conversation.

What's Next

The Wuhan Open quarterfinals are scheduled for Thursday and Friday, October 15–16, with semifinals on Saturday, October 17, and the final on Sunday, October 18. Ann Li plays Janice Tjen, Jelena Ostapenko faces her quarterfinal opponent, Maja Chwalinska meets Maria Sakkari, and Iga Swiatek remains in contention for her first Wuhan Open title.
