Ann Li Stuns Sabalenka on 10th Match Point, Ending the Legend of Wuhan's Unbeatable Champion

By Elena Voss

October 15, 2026 — Wuhan, China

For eight years, Aryna Sabalenka had never lost a tennis match in Wuhan. Three titles, an unbroken winning run stretching back to 2018 — a record so remarkable that her arrival at the venue each season came with the near-certain expectation of more of the same.

On Tuesday, Ann Li ended it.

The American ranked outside the world's top 50 defeated Sabalenka 3-6, 6-4, 7-6(5) in the Wuhan Open Round of 16 in one of the most dramatic and significant upsets of the 2026 WTA season. The official WTA account captured the moment precisely: "THE BIGGEST WIN OF HER CAREER."

Ten Match Points, One Stunning Finish

The match lasted two hours and 27 minutes, and the decisive third-set tiebreak alone felt like a whole tournament compressed into a few games.

Li reached match point nine times. Nine times, Sabalenka — three-time Wuhan champion, No. 1 seed, one of the most clutch competitors in women's tennis — found a way through. A winner here, an error forced there, each reprieve extending what seemed like an impossible situation for the defending champion.

On the tenth match point, Li converted. The streak was over.

Sabalenka had received a right knee medical timeout during the match and committed 44 unforced errors — nearly double Li's 28. The performance was below her usual standard, and Li found the openings that were created, exploiting Sabalenka's groundstroke lapses with clean, flat ball-striking that grew sharper as the match went on.

What the Streak Meant

Sabalenka's Wuhan record was one of the genuinely remarkable venue-specific achievements in professional tennis. She won her first title there in 2018. She won again in 2019. The pandemic interrupted the calendar, but when Wuhan returned in 2024, Sabalenka was there — and she won again.

Through those years, through title wins and title losses at other events, through a career that has included major after major, the Wuhan record stayed intact. It was not a coincidence: Sabalenka's power game suits the conditions, and she brings an almost supernatural confidence to courts where she has won before.

That confidence, on Wednesday, was not enough.

Li's win was her first over Sabalenka in four career meetings. The American has shown flashes of her potential throughout 2026, but nothing of this magnitude. The biggest win of her career is now also one of the biggest upsets of the WTA season.

A Tournament Wide Open

The loss eliminates Sabalenka from the Wuhan Open — the last WTA 1000 event of the 2026 season — and reshapes the quarterfinal picture significantly. The champion who had seemed most likely to carry her record to a fourth title is gone in the second round.

In Sabalenka's place, Li advances to face Janice Tjen for a quarterfinal spot. The draw, already thinned of No. 2 seed Jessica Pegula by Jelena Ostapenko, is now also without its top seed.

Iga Swiatek, still in the tournament and with WTA Finals qualification at stake, faces a very different path than she might have imagined at the start of the week.

What's Next

Ann Li faces Janice Tjen in the Wuhan Open Round of 16 for a quarterfinal berth. Aryna Sabalenka's 2026 Wuhan campaign is over — the streak, which stood since 2018, is no more. The Wuhan Open quarterfinals are scheduled for October 16, semifinals for October 17, and the final for October 18.
