### STORY 1: Laver Cup 2026 Final

**Long-Form Post — 10:30 AM ET**
Team Europe reclaimed the Laver Cup on Sunday at The O2 in London with a comprehensive 13-5 victory over Team World — erasing Team World's 2025 title defense in convincing fashion.

Day 3 went Europe's way from the very start. Flavio Cobolli and Jakub Menšík opened the session by defeating Taylor Fritz and Alex de Minaur 7-5, 6-3 in doubles — and then Alexander Zverev closed the door for good, beating Learner Tien 7-6(3), 6-3 to clinch the trophy. The final score tells the story of the week: Europe won 13-5. Team World had a brief run of success after ending Europe's 8-Cup winning streak in San Francisco last year, but Zverev, Alcaraz, Ruud, Cobolli, Menšík, and Jódar looked like they had a point to prove in London.

Europe's all-time Laver Cup record now stands at 9-1. One of the great team events in tennis — and this year it belonged to the blue side.

### STORY 2: Singapore Open Final

**Long-Form Post — 8:00 AM ET**
Leylah Fernandez closed out one of the most chaotic WTA weeks of the year with a dominant final performance, defeating Talia Gibson 7-5, 6-0 to win the WTA 500 Singapore Open on Sunday.

Fernandez won the first set in a competitive 7-5 battle, and then completely took over in the second — winning it without dropping a game. It was her sixth career WTA title and her second at the 500 level, a result that underlines her place among the WTA's top performers.

The week had a lot of noise around it. Gibson — an Australian qualifier who had beaten Sakkari in the quarterfinals — reached the final after opponent Tatiana Prozorova was forced to withdraw via the WTA's concussion protocol in the semifinals, a move Prozorova very publicly disputed. But on Sunday it was simply a tennis match, and Fernandez was the better player.

Gibson, playing in her first ever WTA final, has a lot to build on from Singapore.

Fernandez now heads into the China Open. What do you think of her chances in Beijing? 🎾

### STORY 3: China Open Draw Released

**Long-Form Post — 12:30 PM ET**
The China Open draws dropped today, and there's a lot to unpack.

On the men's side: Alexander Zverev enters as the No. 1 seed and opens against Cameron Norrie. But the most interesting bracket placement is No. 6 seed Novak Djokovic — returning to Beijing for the first time in 11 years — who opens against Nuno Borges. Zverev and Djokovic are on the same side of the draw, meaning if both progress, they'd meet in the quarterfinals. Djokovic's all-time Beijing record is 29-0 with six titles. He hasn't played here since 2015. This could be one of the storylines of the week.

Daniil Medvedev is the No. 3 seed, Felix Auger-Aliassime is No. 2. Jannik Sinner — the defending champion — is absent with his knee injury.

On the women's side: Elena Rybakina enters as the No. 1 seed for the first time in her career, having taken the top ranking from Aryna Sabalenka last month. Sabalenka is the No. 2 seed. The top half of the draw sets up a potential Rybakina vs. Sabalenka semifinal — a rematch of the US Open final that fans have been waiting for.

Beijing starts Wednesday. Who are you watching? 🎾

### STORY 4: Hangzhou Open Final

**Long-Form Post — 3:00 PM ET**
The Hangzhou Open final is set for Monday, and it features one of the best underdog stories of the Asian swing.

Daniil Medvedev, the No. 1 seed, will face Kyrian Jacquet of France in the championship match. Jacquet came into the week ranked outside the top 100. He beat No. 3 seed Tomás Martín Etcheverry in straight sets in the Round of 16 — the best win of his career — then kept going. And now he's in the final.

Medvedev reached his 68th career semifinal in Hangzhou and dispatched Coleman Wong to book his place in the final. He's the heavy favourite going in, but the week has shown anything is possible.

With most of the top-10 committed to Laver Cup duties in London, the Hangzhou title was always going to go to someone unexpected. Jacquet vs. Medvedev in the final still feels like a story worth watching.

Will Jacquet pull it off, or does Medvedev add to his tournament wins list tomorrow? 🎾

### STORY 5: Chengdu Open Final

**Long-Form Post — 6:00 PM ET**
After a week of upsets, the Chengdu Open final is set — and it's Hubert Hurkacz vs. Denis Shapovalov on Monday.

The path to the final wasn't straightforward for either player. Hurkacz defeated Lloyd Harris in a tense semifinal that went to a 7-6(10) first-set tiebreak before the Pole took it 7-6(10), 6-4. Shapovalov's road was more dramatic: he fell behind in a super-tiebreak shootout with the 38-year-old Frenchman Adrian Mannarino, lost the first set 6-7(6), then came back to win the next two 6-3, 6-2 and close it out.

It's a quality final between two players who didn't arrive in Chengdu as the headliners. The No. 1 seed (Vacherot) was out in the quarterfinals. The defending champion (Tabilo) was out in the quarterfinals. Mannarino, who'd been the run-of-the-week story all week, fell at the last hurdle to Shapovalov.

Hurkacz vs. Shapovalov. Chengdu final. Monday.
