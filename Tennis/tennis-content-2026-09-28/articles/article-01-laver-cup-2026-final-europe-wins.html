Team Europe Reclaims Laver Cup with 13-5 Win, Zverev Clinches for Fourth Time

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | September 28, 2026

Team Europe reclaimed the Laver Cup on Sunday at The O2 in London, defeating Team World 13-5 in a commanding performance that erased the sting of last year's defeat in San Francisco. Alexander Zverev, who has made a habit of being Europe's closer, delivered the decisive point for the fourth time in his career — and he did it with characteristic composure, defeating Learner Tien 7-6(3), 6-3 to seal the trophy on Day 3.

The final scoreline was emphatic. Europe won 8 of the 12 matches played across three days, a dominant result that reflected their depth throughout the event. Flavio Cobolli and Jakub Menšík had earlier set the stage by winning the Day 3 doubles, defeating Taylor Fritz and Alex de Minaur 7-5, 6-3, before Zverev walked out for the singles clincher.

How Europe Built the Lead

Europe had control from Day 1. Casper Ruud opened the event with a hard-fought super-tiebreak win over Francisco Cerúndolo, Rafael Jódar delivered in his Laver Cup debut by dispatching Alexander Bublik 6-2, 6-3, and the doubles pairing of Carlos Alcaraz and Menšík took three points from four on Friday. Team World's lone Day 1 point came from Brandon Nakashima, who beat Menšík 6-1, 3-6, [10-7] to at least keep the deficit manageable.

Day 2 turned out to be the match's pivotal session, with wins worth two points each. Europe extended their lead through the double-points format, with Zverev's victory over de Minaur among the Day 2 highlights. By the time Day 3 arrived Sunday, Europe held a lead that made the outcome a formality — but they played through it with the same focus that had defined the week.

Zverev's Laver Cup Legacy

Zverev has now personally clinched the Laver Cup on four occasions: Chicago 2018, Geneva 2019, Boston 2021, and now London 2026. No player on either team holds a comparable record as the clutch performer when the trophy is on the line. In London, he had already contributed heavily throughout the event before stepping up in the final singles match when it mattered most.

His 2026 season has been historic. He won the French Open in June, defeating Carlos Alcaraz in a five-set final, and followed it up with the US Open title over Ben Shelton in New York. Adding a Laver Cup clinch caps what may be remembered as the best year of his career. Zverev will turn his attention next to the China Open in Beijing, which begins Wednesday.

World's Difficult Week

Team World had won the event for the first time in 2025, ending Europe's run of eight consecutive titles. But defending in London proved beyond them. Fritz, de Minaur, Bublik, Nakashima, Tien, and Cerúndolo never found the collective momentum they had in San Francisco. Nakashima's Day 1 win over Menšík was the competitive high point for World's side.

The composition of the World team was disrupted heading into the event. Ben Shelton had withdrawn due to a Davis Cup commitment, replaced by Nakashima. Tommy Paul had stepped back with an elbow injury, replaced by Francisco Cerúndolo. With a reshuffled roster, Team World faced a Europe side that had been building chemistry through the week.

What's Next

The Laver Cup's conclusion marks the end of the team tennis portion of the autumn swing. Players from both squads now head to the Asian swing, with the China Open beginning in Beijing on Wednesday, September 30. Zverev enters as the ATP No. 1 seed in Beijing; Alcaraz is expected in Tokyo for the ATP 500 event running the same week. Europe's all-time Laver Cup record now stands at 9-1 — with Team World's only win remaining the 2025 edition in San Francisco.
