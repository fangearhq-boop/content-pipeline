China Open Draw Released: Zverev vs. Djokovic Potential QF, Rybakina Enters as WTA No. 1 Seed

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | September 28, 2026

The China Open draws are set. The ATP 500 and WTA 1000 events begin Wednesday in Beijing, and Monday's draw release delivered the bracket storylines the tennis world will be watching for the rest of the week.

On the men's side, the most compelling placement is in the bottom half of the bracket: No. 1 seed Alexander Zverev opens against Cameron Norrie, while No. 6 seed Novak Djokovic — returning to Beijing for the first time in 11 years — faces Nuno Borges in the first round. If both win through to the quarterfinals, they will meet on the same side of the draw. It would be one of the event's marquee matchups.

Djokovic's Return to Beijing

Djokovic has not competed at the China Open since 2015. Before stepping away, his Beijing record was 29-0 — a remarkable all-time dominance that included six titles. He is now 39 years old and enters this week ranked No. 12 in the world after a difficult US Open exit in the first round against Navone. His stated goal for the autumn swing is returning to the top 10 before the Paris Masters in November.

Opening against Borges is a manageable draw for a player with Djokovic's Beijing history and surface credentials, but he will need to play his way back to form after a period away from competition. His potential quarterfinal against Zverev — the reigning US Open champion — would be the most dramatic matchup of the week if it materializes.

ATP Seedings and Sinner's Absence

The ATP draw features Zverev as the No. 1 seed, Felix Auger-Aliassime as No. 2, and Daniil Medvedev as No. 3. Alex de Minaur is seeded No. 5. The event's defending champion, Jannik Sinner — who beat Learner Tien 6-2, 6-2 in the 2025 final — officially withdrew this week citing his ongoing right knee injury. Sinner has not competed since Wimbledon in July and the China Open was his first targeted return. His absence creates a genuinely open field.

WTA Draw: Rybakina Enters as the New No. 1

On the women's side, Elena Rybakina enters the China Open as the No. 1 seed — the first time in her career she has held that distinction at a tournament draw. Rybakina took the WTA World No. 1 ranking from Aryna Sabalenka in September after winning the 2026 US Open. Sabalenka is the No. 2 seed in Beijing.

Both players are in the same half of the draw, setting up a potential semifinal clash — a rematch of the US Open final, which Rybakina won 6-4, 5-7, 6-2. If the draw plays out as expected, a Rybakina-Sabalenka Beijing semifinal would be the defining match of the WTA week.

The rest of the top seeds include Jessica Pegula (No. 3), Coco Gauff (No. 4), Mirra Andreeva (No. 5), and Iga Swiatek (No. 9). The field is among the deepest of the autumn calendar.

What's Next

The China Open begins Wednesday, September 30, with first-round action across both draws. Matches will continue through early October for the ATP side (final October 6) and mid-October for the WTA draw (final approximately October 11). Race to the ATP Finals and Race to the WTA Finals points are on the line in Beijing, with significant implications for Turin and Riyadh as the season reaches its final months.
