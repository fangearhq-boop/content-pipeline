Fernandez Wins Singapore Open, Dismisses Gibson 7-5, 6-0 for Sixth Career Title

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | September 28, 2026

Leylah Fernandez claimed the WTA 500 Singapore Open title on Sunday, defeating Talia Gibson 7-5, 6-0 at OCBC Arena to bring a chaotic and drama-filled week to a clean, definitive conclusion. It was Fernandez's sixth career WTA title and her second at the 500 level — a result that confirms her place among the WTA's consistent top performers heading into the Chinese swing.

Fernandez won the first set in a competitive opener, pushing through a close 7-5 scoreline that reflected Gibson's ability to stay in points. But from the second set onward, there was only one player who looked capable of winning. Fernandez dominated from the baseline, dropped no games, and wrapped up the match with the kind of authority that makes it easy to forget how turbulent the week around her had been.

A Week Full of Drama

The Singapore Open will be remembered as much for the controversy surrounding Tatiana Prozorova's semifinal withdrawal as for the tennis itself. Prozorova was forced to withdraw from her semifinal against Talia Gibson after the WTA applied its concussion protocol, ruling that Prozorova — who had been struck in the face during a doubles quarterfinal the previous day — could not safely compete. Prozorova publicly disputed the decision, stating the ball had grazed her cheek and shoulder and that her poor balance-test results were the product of three consecutive three-plus-hour matches, not a head injury. The WTA defended the protocol.

Gibson, already having upset No. 7 seed Maria Sakkari in the quarterfinals, was placed into the final via the walkover. Playing in her first career WTA final, the Australian qualifier represented one of the week's underdog stories — but on Sunday she found Fernandez a step above at every point in the match.

Fernandez's Career Trajectory

Fernandez's Singapore title is her sixth WTA singles championship. Her career has been marked by brilliant performances on the biggest stages — most notably her run to the US Open final in 2021, where she upset multiple top-three players including Naomi Osaka, Simona Halep, and Elina Svitolina before losing to Emma Raducanu in a landmark match.

Her second WTA 500 title underlines consistent performance at the Tour's upper tier. Her results over the past two years have shown a player maturing into a regular contender rather than an occasional brilliance. She is seeded for the China Open and brings momentum into Beijing after one of the cleaner final performances of the Singapore tournament's history.

What's Next

The WTA Asian swing now continues into Beijing, where the China Open — a WTA 1000 event — begins Wednesday, September 30. Fernandez will be among the field as the Tour's attention shifts from Singapore to the Chinese capital. The China Open draw was released Monday, with newly crowned WTA World No. 1 Elena Rybakina as the top seed. The potential Rybakina vs. Aryna Sabalenka (No. 2 seed) semifinal is the WTA's marquee match of the week.
