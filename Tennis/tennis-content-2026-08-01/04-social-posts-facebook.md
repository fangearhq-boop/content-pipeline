### STORY 1: Eala Beats Svitolina — SF vs. Osaka

#### Long-Form Post — 8:00 AM ET

Alexandra Eala is going to the semifinals in Washington.

Her quarterfinal on Friday: she defeated Elina Svitolina — the world No. 10, a Grand Slam finalist, a former top-5 player — 6-3, 6-4. It was exactly the score she posted against Svitolina when they met in Berlin earlier this season. There is something to be said for knowing your opponent. Eala clearly does.

Now step back and look at her full DC Open run. In the first round, she faced Qinwen Zheng — the reigning Olympic champion — from a set down and won 4-6, 6-4, 6-1. In the second round, she faced Leylah Fernandez — the defending DC Open champion — rallied from 5-1 down in the second set and won 6-2, 7-6. In the quarterfinals, she dispatched Svitolina in straights.

She is unseeded. She is ranked No. 28. She arrived at this tournament as a wildcard story, fresh off a Wimbledon run where she became the first Filipino player to reach the fourth round. Now she's in a WTA 500 semifinal against Naomi Osaka.

If the summer had a player, it's Eala. And Saturday's semifinal is her biggest test yet.

Who do you think wins between Eala and Osaka?

### STORY 2: DC Open ATP QF Upsets

#### Long-Form Post — 10:30 AM ET

The DC Open men's draw was supposed to be orderly. It wasn't.

On Friday, the top two seeds were eliminated in the same quarterfinal session. Brandon Nakashima — an American ranked outside the top 20 — knocked out defending champion Alex de Minaur, the No. 1 seed, 7-6(5), 6-4. De Minaur had opened the week by snapping a 12-1 career deficit against Tsitsipas. He leaves without the title he came to defend.

In the other upset, Alejandro Tabilo of Chile eliminated Ben Shelton, the No. 2 seed and one of the most powerful servers in the game. The Chilean has quietly put together a strong summer and made the most of his opportunity.

Two top seeds gone, yes. But the story with the most forward momentum may belong to Rafael Jodar. The 19-year-old Spaniard beat Arthur Fils in the first round, beat Kei Nishikori in the second round, and on Friday beat No. 6 seed Lorenzo Musetti 1-6, 6-1, 6-4 in a match that showcased exactly the kind of high-wire resilience you want from a young player. He was bageled in the first set. He won the second and third combined 7-1. He is now in the semifinals, and regardless of what happens next, he will enter the ATP Top 20 for the first time.

Taylor Fritz, the No. 3 seed, is the last top-four player standing. He survived against Alex Michelsen after being down a match point. Semicentennials on Saturday: Nakashima vs. Fritz, Jodar vs. Tabilo.

Which match are you most looking forward to?

### STORY 3: Los Cabos Final

#### Long-Form Post — 12:30 PM ET

The Mifel Tennis Open final in Los Cabos is a story of two very different careers arriving at the same place on a Saturday afternoon.

Denis Shapovalov has been here before — he is a past Los Cabos champion and knows what it takes to win on these courts. On Friday he defeated Cameron Norrie 6-3, 5-7, 6-4 in a match that required him to weather the second set before coming through in the third. He is healthy and competing here despite the injury challenges earlier in his season.

Across the net will be Arthur Gea. The 21-year-old Frenchman, ranked No. 127 coming into the week, will enter the Top 100 for the first time when the tournament concludes — regardless of today's result. He spent the week eliminating higher-ranked opponents at every turn, including No. 3 seed Cerundolo in the quarterfinals and then defeating Coleman Wong 7-6(7), 6-3 in Friday's semifinal. The tie-break in that match went to the wire before Gea closed it out.

This is Gea's first ATP Tour final. He arrived as an afterthought; he leaves as a name worth knowing.

Final is today. Shapovalov the seasoned champion against Gea the breakthrough story.

Who do you think lifts the trophy?

### STORY 4: Canadian Open Draws

#### Long-Form Post — 3:00 PM ET

The Canadian Open draws are out, and there's plenty to work through.

On the WTA side, the National Bank Open in Toronto (Sobeys Stadium) gets underway with main draw action on August 2. Jessica Pegula is the No. 1 seed in a 96-player field — which makes this a particularly interesting week, given that Pegula is also in the DC Open semifinals this very Saturday. If she wins in Washington, she'll head north with enormous momentum and the top billing in Toronto to match.

On the ATP side, Alexander Zverev heads to Montreal as the No. 1 seed. Zverev won Roland Garros in 2026 and reached the Wimbledon final; he is in the form that warrants top billing at any tournament. Felix Auger-Aliassime is seeded No. 2 at home in Montreal — a first for him at a Masters 1000 level event, and the kind of seeding the home crowd will be excited about from the first day.

The wildcard news from Montreal has a subplot worth following: Jack Draper received a wildcard to the ATP draw. Draper, the British No. 1 who held a career-high ranking of No. 4 before injuries derailed his 2026 season, has not played a tour-level match this year. The Montreal wildcard is his first competitive opportunity to rebuild.

Main draw play begins August 2 for the WTA and August 4 for the ATP.

Who are you rooting for in Toronto or Montreal?

### STORY 5: DC Open WTA QF Full Wrap

#### Long-Form Post — 6:00 PM ET

The DC Open WTA quarterfinals produced four results on Friday — clean, decisive, and setting up two compelling semifinal matchups for Saturday.

Jessica Pegula, the No. 1 seed, was the picture of efficiency. She defeated No. 5 seed Anna Kalinskaya 6-3, 7-5 without surrendering a set. Pegula has played consistent tennis throughout the tournament and is the heavy favorite to reach Sunday's final.

Diana Shnaider, the No. 4 seed, had a tougher draw but delivered. She beat Liudmila Samsonova 6-3, 6-4 in straight sets to book her semifinal spot. Shnaider has been one of the steadier stories of the WTA 2026 summer season.

Naomi Osaka, seeded No. 3, needed three sets to dispatch Elisabetta Cocciaretto 4-6, 6-4, 6-3. Osaka dropped the first set but found her level over the next hour and a half. Her semifinal reward: Alexandra Eala.

And Eala — who beat No. 10 Svitolina 6-3, 6-4 in the day's other match — is the story everyone will be following in Washington for the next 24 hours.

Semifinals Saturday: Pegula vs. Shnaider; Eala vs. Osaka. The DC Open WTA final is Sunday August 2.

Which semifinal are you more excited about?
