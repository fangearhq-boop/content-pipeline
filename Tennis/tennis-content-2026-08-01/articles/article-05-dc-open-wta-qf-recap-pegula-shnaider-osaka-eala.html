DC Open WTA Quarterfinal Recap: Pegula, Shnaider, Osaka, Eala All Advance to Semifinals

By Elena Voss | August 1, 2026

Washington, D.C.

All four DC Open WTA quarterfinals were played on Friday, and all four produced decisive results. No. 1 seed Jessica Pegula, No. 4 seed Diana Shnaider, and No. 3 seed Naomi Osaka all advanced in straight sets. Alexandra Eala, unseeded, continued her remarkable run by defeating Elina Svitolina. The semifinals are set for Saturday: Pegula vs. Shnaider and Eala vs. Osaka.

Pegula Handles Kalinskaya

Jessica Pegula defeated No. 5 seed Anna Kalinskaya 6-3, 7-5 in the day's opening quarterfinal. Pegula has been the model of consistency throughout the week in Washington — she has not dropped a set through four matches and has moved through the bracket with a controlled, almost mechanical efficiency. The score does not fully capture how in command she was for most of the match; Kalinskaya extended the second set but Pegula held her nerve and closed it out without incident.

Shnaider Beats Samsonova

Diana Shnaider, seeded No. 4, defeated Liudmila Samsonova 6-3, 6-4 in a match between two Russian players. Shnaider has been among the more impressive performers in the draw — her baseline game on hard courts suits the surface, and she has handled her opponents with a directness that reflects her confidence in her own game. The win over Samsonova was her most complete performance of the week.

Osaka Needs Three to Beat Cocciaretto

Naomi Osaka, the third seed, was the only quarterfinalist who required three sets on Friday. She lost the opening set to Elisabetta Cocciaretto 4-6, then found her range and won the second 6-4 and the third 6-3 to complete the win. Osaka has had moments of vulnerability throughout the tournament, but she has consistently found her level when she needed to. Her reward is a semifinal against Eala — the player who has beaten Zheng, Fernandez, and Svitolina in sequence to arrive at the same stage.

Eala Continues Her Run

The Eala story has been covered extensively this week for good reason. Her 6-3, 6-4 win over Svitolina was efficient and confident — a repeat of their Berlin result from earlier in the season. She has not dropped a set in her last two matches in Washington and has made three consecutive elite scalps look routine. Her semifinal against Osaka will be the most anticipated match of the DC Open this weekend.

What's Next

The DC Open WTA semifinals are Saturday, August 1. Pegula faces Shnaider; Eala faces Osaka. The final is Sunday, August 2, at FitzGerald Tennis Center in Washington. The Canadian Open in Toronto begins WTA main draw action on the same day as the DC Open final, meaning the summer hard-court calendar offers no pause for the game's top players. Whoever lifts the trophy in Washington will have earned it through a demanding six-day schedule in the July heat.
