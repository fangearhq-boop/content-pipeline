Eala Defeats Svitolina to Reach DC Open WTA Semifinals, Faces Osaka on Saturday

By Ryan Calloway | August 1, 2026

Washington, D.C.

The run continues. Alexandra Eala defeated Elina Svitolina 6-3, 6-4 in the DC Open WTA quarterfinals on Friday, advancing to the semifinals in Washington and setting up a Saturday showdown against No. 3 seed Naomi Osaka. The Philippines star, unseeded and ranked No. 28, has now eliminated three consecutive elite opponents at FitzGerald Tennis Center without dropping a set in her last two matches.

It was a Berlin repeat — Eala had beaten Svitolina at that event earlier in the 2026 season with the identical score of 6-3, 6-4. Whatever adjustments Svitolina may have intended to make, Eala had an answer for them.

A Systematic Dismantling

Svitolina arrived in Washington as a legitimate semifinal contender. The Ukrainian veteran has won more than 20 WTA titles across her career, reached a career-high ranking of No. 3, and has consistently found her best tennis on hard courts. She was the kind of player who was supposed to represent a ceiling for Eala's run.

Eala treated the quarterfinal as though no such ceiling existed. She moved fluently from the baseline, kept Svitolina in uncomfortable positions throughout the first set, and closed out 6-3 with the quiet efficiency of someone who already knew how the afternoon was going to unfold. The second set was more contested, but Eala was the more composed player in the critical moments, sealing it 6-4 to complete the win.

The victory adds another name to Eala's growing list of high-profile conquests in Washington: Qinwen Zheng (Olympic champion) fell in the first round, Leylah Fernandez (defending champion) fell in the second, and now Svitolina (world No. 10) falls in the quarterfinals. Each match has required Eala to beat someone ranked or rated above her heading into the contest. She has done it three times in as many days.

The Summer Arc

Eala's 2026 has been building toward something. In early July she arrived at Wimbledon ranked outside the top 30, and she departed from the All England Club as a fourth-round finalist who had beaten Elena Rybakina and Iga Swiatek — the first Filipino player in Wimbledon history to advance that far. The WTA gave her the Star of the Swing award for the grass-court season. Her ranking rose to No. 28, a career high.

She brought that confidence to Washington. And the results have not just matched the expectation — they have exceeded it.

What's Next

Eala faces No. 3 seed Naomi Osaka in the DC Open WTA semifinals on Saturday, August 1. Osaka advanced by defeating Elisabetta Cocciaretto 4-6, 6-4, 6-3 in her quarterfinal. The two have not met many times on the tour, and Osaka — playing her best tennis of the 2026 season — will be the most difficult test Eala has faced this week.

The other DC Open WTA semifinal matches No. 1 seed Jessica Pegula against No. 4 seed Diana Shnaider. The final is scheduled for Sunday, August 2. The Canadian Open in Toronto follows, with WTA main draw play beginning the same day. Eala, regardless of her result on Saturday, has cemented her status as one of the most compelling stories in women's tennis this summer.
