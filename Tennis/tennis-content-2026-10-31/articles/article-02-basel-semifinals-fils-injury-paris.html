Basel Semifinals Set — But Fils Back Injury Three Days Before Paris Masters Raises Alarm

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap

October 31, 2026

The Swiss Indoors Basel quarterfinals on Friday produced the results and set the Saturday semifinal lineup, but the most significant news did not come from any winner. Arthur Fils, the second seed and a key player in the Race to Turin, retired from his match against Taylor Fritz trailing 6-2, 2-0 with lower back discomfort. The Paris Masters starts in three days.

Fils is currently ranked seventh in the ATP Race to Turin. He needs a significant performance in Paris to secure his place at the ATP Finals in November. A withdrawal from Paris — or an early-round exit while managing pain — would be a significant setback for his qualification chances.

Friday's other results: Valentin Vacherot beat Holger Rune 6-3, 3-6, 6-3 to extend one of the tournament's surprise runs. Jiří Lehečká dispatched Swiss wild card Jérôme Kym 6-4, 6-2. Jan-Lennard Struff beat Nuno Borges 6-2, 6-3, continuing his remarkable week after his historic upset of Novak Djokovic in the round of 16.

The Vacherot Story

Valentin Vacherot's run to the Basel semifinals deserves its own accounting. He entered the week outside the top 60 in the world rankings — unseeded in a draw that included the top players on the ATP Tour. He beat Rafael Jódar, the third seed, to reach the quarterfinals. On Friday, he followed that by beating Holger Rune, a former top-four player and a Basel title contender in his own right, in three sets.

Vacherot's game is patient and relentless — he retrieves, redirects, and waits for opponents to make errors under pressure. Against Rune, who was leading 3-1 in the second set before Vacherot pulled it back to take the tiebreak, that approach wore down the Dane's ability to sustain offensive pressure. It is a style that can work for stretches against elite players, and this week in Basel, it has worked for an entire tournament run.

His opponent Saturday is Taylor Fritz. Fritz, who reached the semis without Fils completing their quarterfinal, arrives better rested. He's a different kind of challenge from Rune — bigger serve, flatter groundstrokes, more comfortable at indoor pace. Vacherot will need to absorb even more before he can create openings.

Lehečká vs. Struff

The other Basel semifinal features two players riding significant momentum. Jiří Lehečká has been one of the week's steadiest performers. He won his round of 16 and quarterfinal without drama, and enters Saturday's match against Struff having had more recovery time than anyone else in the draw.

Struff's week has been the tournament's defining narrative — beating Djokovic in the round of 16 for the first time in his career, then eliminating Borges efficiently in the quarterfinals. The German is playing with a confidence that comes from doing something no one on tour had done before. He's 36 years old, ranked No. 47 in the world, and in a Basel semifinal having beaten the greatest player of his era to get there.

Lehečká's ranking (No. 21) and consistency give him the edge on paper. But Struff's form this week argues against dismissing him.

What's Next

The Basel semifinals are Saturday, October 31. The Basel final is Sunday. Whatever happens in the semis, the tournament has already produced more drama than most ATP 500 events manage in a full week — Kym over Shelton, Struff over Djokovic, Vacherot's consecutive upsets, the Wawrinka career farewell.

For Arthur Fils, the immediate question is whether he can compete in Paris. His camp has not made a statement on his status. The Paris first-round draw is Monday, with matches beginning Tuesday. The Race to Turin depends on him being on a court in Nanterre.
