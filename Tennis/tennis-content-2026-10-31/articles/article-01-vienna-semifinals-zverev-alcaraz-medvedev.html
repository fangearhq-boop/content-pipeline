Vienna Semifinals: Zverev vs. de Minaur and Alcaraz vs. Medvedev Set Up Stunning Saturday

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap

October 31, 2026

The 2026 Erste Bank Open semifinals in Vienna feature four of the best players on the ATP Tour on the same Saturday afternoon. Alexander Zverev, the world No. 1, faces Alex de Minaur. Carlos Alcaraz meets Daniil Medvedev, who confirmed his ATP Finals spot on Friday. The Vienna final is Sunday, November 1.

Zverev reached the semis in the most convincing fashion of the week. On Friday, he dismantled French qualifier Arthur Fery 6-0, 6-2 in just 58 minutes. It was a performance consistent with the No. 1 ranking he will officially hold when the new ATP rankings are released Monday. Fery, who had surprised the draw by reaching the Vienna quarterfinals, had no answer for Zverev's depth from the baseline.

De Minaur's path to the semis required more work. He edged Matteo Berrettini 7-6(4), 6-2, saving the tiebreak from a difficult position before pulling clear in the second set. It continues a strong autumn swing for the Australian, who has been one of the more consistent performers in the Race to Turin conversation.

Zverev vs. de Minaur: A Familiar Mismatch?

The head-to-head tells one story. Zverev leads de Minaur 9-4 overall, including an 8-0 record in traditional ATP Tour matches. That dominance is hard to overlook, particularly on a hard-court indoor surface where Zverev's power game is at its most effective.

De Minaur has made Vienna his event in recent years, reaching the semifinals in each of the past two editions. He is a different player than the one who dropped eight straight to Zverev in tour-level play — quicker around the court, more aggressive returning, more willing to take the ball early. But Zverev's serve and forehand remain the biggest weapons in Vienna's draw this week, and he has shown no vulnerability so far.

The question for de Minaur is whether his defensive speed can disrupt Zverev's rhythm long enough to take a set. If he gets one, the match becomes unpredictable. If Zverev locks in his first-serve percentage, it could be another short afternoon.

Alcaraz vs. Medvedev: The Tenth Time

Carlos Alcaraz and Daniil Medvedev will meet for the 10th time in their careers on Saturday, the first time in a non-Grand Slam setting in quite a while. Alcaraz holds a 6-3 edge in their head-to-head. He reached the semis with a 6-4, 7-6(2) win over Tommy Paul on Friday.

Medvedev arrives at this semifinal with a specific kind of freedom. He locked in his ATP Finals berth by defeating Adrian Mannarino in his quarterfinal, which means Turin is confirmed regardless of what happens in Vienna. He can play without consequences — and Medvedev at his best, with nothing to lose in the immediate bracket sense, is a difficult player to prepare for.

The Russian's recent form has been strong enough to make this a genuine contest. He won the Almaty title last week. He has been moving well and returning with the flat, penetrating ball-striking that makes his defensive counterpunching so effective. Alcaraz's variety and athleticism are the natural counters, but Medvedev at an indoor hard court is a very different challenge than Medvedev on clay.

What's Next

The winners of both Vienna semifinals meet Sunday in the Erste Bank Open final, with 500 ATP ranking points and Race-to-Turin credit on the line. For Zverev and Alcaraz, a title deepens their lead in the Race. For de Minaur, a run to or past the final could be the difference between qualifying for the ATP Finals and missing Turin. Medvedev's spot is already secured, but a Vienna title would provide a substantial ranking boost heading into Paris.

The Rolex Paris Masters — the season's last Masters 1000 event — begins Monday, November 2. Several players in Vienna's draw will transition directly from Saturday afternoon into a Paris first-round match by Tuesday. The calendar offers no margin.
