Paris Masters Draw 2026: Zverev No. 1, Djokovic Seeded 10th, Main Draw Starts Monday

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap

October 31, 2026

The 2026 Rolex Paris Masters draw was unveiled Friday evening at the Plenitude Arena in Nanterre. Alexander Zverev is the No. 1 seed. Carlos Alcaraz is No. 2. Ben Shelton No. 3, Daniil Medvedev No. 4. The main draw begins Monday, November 2, and runs through the final on Sunday, November 8. Qualifying rounds continue Saturday and Sunday.

The Paris Masters is the last ATP Masters 1000 event of 2026, carrying 1,000 Race to Turin points for the champion and 600 for the finalist. It is also the last meaningful event before the ATP Finals in Turin (November 15–22), making the draw's structure — who faces whom in the early rounds — more consequential than at almost any other tournament of the year.

The top eight seeds receive first-round byes, entering the bracket at the round of 32 on Tuesday. Seeds nine and below play first-round matches on Monday. Novak Djokovic, seeded 10th, begins his Paris Masters with a Monday first-round match.

Djokovic's Situation

Djokovic enters Paris outside the top eight in the ATP Race to Turin standings, which means he has not clinched an ATP Finals berth. Four spots are gone — Zverev, Alcaraz, Shelton, and Medvedev are all confirmed. Djokovic is fighting for one of the four remaining places alongside Fabio Cobolli, Frances Tiafoe, Rafael Jódar, Alex de Minaur, Taylor Fritz, Jan-Lennard Struff, and others.

His path in Paris is the critical variable. If he draws a difficult opening match, every point he drops in the early rounds brings his qualification chances into question. With 1,000 points at stake and the Finals as the explicit target, Djokovic's Paris run is the last significant data point in his 2026 season.

He arrives from Basel, where he lost to Jan-Lennard Struff in three sets in the round of 16 — the first time in their career head-to-head that Struff had beaten him. His form is inconsistent by his own standards, but his ability to raise his level in big moments remains intact. Paris has historically been a strong event for him.

The Top Seeds' Picture

Zverev arrives as the clear favorite. He has already secured the year-end No. 1 ranking and will officially hold it from Monday. His form this week in Vienna — where he beat Arthur Fery 6-0, 6-2 in 58 minutes on Friday — is the kind of commanding performance that defines a top seed. He has time to rest before his Paris first-round bye on Tuesday.

Alcaraz, the No. 2 seed, also has a bye. He is in the middle of a strong indoor autumn having just beaten Tommy Paul in the Vienna quarterfinals. His Paris track record includes a run to the 2023 final and quarterfinals appearances in subsequent years.

Shelton, No. 3, exits Basel early after his stunning round-of-16 loss to Swiss wild card Jérôme Kym. He has already clinched the ATP Finals, which reduces the pressure of Paris without removing the competitive stakes of a title run.

Arthur Fils, who was entered in the Paris draw, left the Basel quarterfinals with lower back discomfort on Friday. His status for Monday's first round is unclear at the time of writing. He was listed among the seeds and is counted among the Race contenders; a withdrawal would be felt in both his bracket slot and the Race standings.

What's Next

Qualifying rounds continue Saturday and Sunday. The main draw first round is Monday, November 2, with seeds 9 through the unseeded field in action. The top eight seeds enter Tuesday. The tournament concludes November 8.

The ATP Finals field will be finalized during Paris Masters week, with each round potentially eliminating or confirming contenders in the Race. By the time the Paris final is played November 8, the eight players heading to Turin will be known.
