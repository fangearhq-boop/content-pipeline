"This Match Describes My Whole Career": Kukushkin Wins Farewell Match at Almaty Before Facing Tsitsipas

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | October 21, 2026

Mikhail Kukushkin walked onto the court in Almaty on Monday with something most players his age don't have left: one more match to win. The 38-year-old Kazakhstani received a wildcard into the 2026 Almaty Open — the final tournament of his professional career, he had announced — and promptly delivered the kind of win his two decades on tour have been built on. Two tiebreaks, 7-6(4), 7-6(3), a first ATP Tour victory since 2024, and a sendoff that felt like it was scripted.

"This match describes my whole career," Kukushkin told Tennis Majors after the win. It's hard to argue. Tight margins, unrelenting pressure, and a result that never felt comfortable until the final point. That's been the Kukushkin calling card since he turned professional in 2006.

Twenty Years of Professional Tennis

Kukushkin's career is the kind that doesn't produce the trophies and rankings that define legacies in the history books, but it produces the grinding victories that fill out the depth of the ATP Tour. He climbed to a career-high of No. 39 in February 2019, making him, at his peak, a player capable of making any draw uncomfortable for any opponent.

He won exactly one ATP singles title in his career: the St. Petersburg Open in 2010. He reached the third round at Grand Slams. He made deep runs in challengers. He beat players ranked much higher than him in matches that decided nothing except that day's result — and that was enough to keep him competing for 20 years. Players like Kukushkin don't make the front pages of tennis sites very often, which is precisely why his farewell is worth marking when it happens.

Playing in his home country of Kazakhstan adds a layer to the retirement that a clay court in Europe couldn't provide. The Almaty crowd, familiar with what Kukushkin represents for Kazakhstani tennis, was there to send him off in front of his own people.

Tsitsipas Awaits in Round 2

Kukushkin's second-round opponent is Stefanos Tsitsipas, who defeated Aleksandar Kovacevic 7-6(9), 3-6, 6-3 on Tuesday to advance. The matchup is a vivid illustration of the career arc: Tsitsipas, a former world No. 3 and multiple Grand Slam finalist, representing the generation that came after Kukushkin's prime; Kukushkin, a veteran playing his penultimate match, representing everything the ATP Tour looks like below the headline names.

In a normal week, Tsitsipas versus Kukushkin is a first-round result you scroll past without much thought. This week, in Almaty, in front of a home crowd, with retirement announced, it becomes something else. Whether Kukushkin wins or loses is almost beside the point — though you can be sure he won't make it easy.

What's Next

Kukushkin will play Tsitsipas in the second round of the 2026 Almaty Open, the final ATP Tour match of his professional career regardless of the result. The Almaty Open (ATP 250) continues through October 26. Top seeds include Daniil Medvedev and Andrey Rublev, with the main draw offering a farewell stage that very few players get to choose on their own terms.

For the rest of the 2026 indoor season, the ATP calendar moves toward Vienna and Basel — both ATP 500 events opening October 26 — and then the Paris Rolex Masters. Kukushkin's story ends here in Almaty, but the season he's departing still has a great deal left to decide.
