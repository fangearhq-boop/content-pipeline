### Long-Form Post Alexandra Eala is one win away from making history. The 22-year-old from the Philippines defeated No. 3 seed Naomi Osaka 6-4, 6-2 Saturday in the DC Open WTA semifinals, becoming the first Filipino player to reach a WTA 500 singles final. She was composed, aggressive, and never let Osaka settle into the match. Her path to the final tells the whole story: she defeated Qinwen Zheng in the first round, then knocked out defending champion Leylah Fernandez in the second round, then eliminated world No. 10 Elina Svitolina in the quarterfinals, and now Osaka. Four matches, four wins, four opponents who had every reason to expect the upset to go the other way. In the other semifinal, top seed Jessica Pegula proved why she is one of the most mentally tough players on tour. She trailed 2-5 in the first set against No. 4 Diana Shnaider before rallying to win 7-5, 6-4 in 1 hour 27 minutes. It is Pegula's fourth WTA final of 2026. Today's DC Open WTA final — Pegula vs. Eala — is set for 12 PM local time. Who are you rooting for in today's final? --- ### Image Post Caption Eala through Osaka. Eala through Svitolina. ### Long-Form Post Arthur Gea has his first ATP title — and he earned every bit of it. The 21-year-old Frenchman defeated defending champion Denis Shapovalov 6-3, 6-4 in 88 minutes Sunday at the Mifel Tennis Open by Telcel in Los Cabos, Mexico. Gea controlled both sets, saved all but one break point, and closed out the match without ever allowing Shapovalov to find his rhythm. For Gea, who entered the tournament ranked No. 127, this title is transformative. He will break into the ATP top 100 for the first time — a milestone he had been approaching all season. The win caps a week in which he also defeated third-seeded Alejandro Cerundolo and Coleman Wong, the young qualifier from Hong Kong who had created the tournament's biggest story earlier in the week with his first-round upset of top seed Jiri Lehecka. Shapovalov, the defending champion, was hunting back-to-back titles at a tournament where he has historically played well. Instead, he met a player with nothing to lose and everything to prove.
