Eala Stuns Osaka, Becomes First Filipino in WTA 500 Final — Faces Pegula in DC Open

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | August 2, 2026

Alexandra Eala made tennis history Saturday afternoon at the William H.G. FitzGerald Tennis Center in Washington, D.C., defeating No. 3 seed Naomi Osaka 6-4, 6-2 to become the first Filipino player to reach a WTA 500 singles final. She will face No. 1 seed Jessica Pegula in the championship match Sunday.

The scoreline does not fully convey the quality of the performance. Eala, ranked No. 28 and unseeded in the draw, was composed throughout against a former world No. 1 in her first career meeting against the Japanese star. She broke early in each set, held comfortably behind her serve, and closed without a trace of nerves in what was the biggest match of her young career.

A Week That Will Define a Career

To understand what Eala has accomplished this week, consider her path. She defeated Qinwen Zheng — a former Olympic champion — in the first round, coming from a set down to win 4-6, 6-4, 6-1. She then eliminated defending champion Leylah Fernandez 6-2, 7-6(1) in the second round, rallying from 5-1 down in the second set. In the quarterfinals, she knocked out world No. 10 Elina Svitolina 6-3, 6-4 — matching the exact scoreline from their earlier meeting in Berlin this season. And on Saturday, she beat Osaka.

Four matches. Four victories. All against opponents seeded, ranked, or pedigreed higher than herself.

"I'm just trying to play each match the same way," Eala said after the win. She is the first Filipino to reach a WTA 500 final — a milestone that extends a remarkable summer that already included a run to the fourth round at Wimbledon earlier this month.

Pegula Rallies From 2-5 to Reach Her Fourth Final of 2026

In the other semifinal, top seed Jessica Pegula demonstrated the mental strength that has made her one of the most consistent players on tour this season. Trailing 2-5 in the first set against No. 4 seed Diana Shnaider, Pegula did not panic. She won seven of the next eight games to take the set 7-5, then closed out 6-4 in the second in a match that lasted 1 hour and 27 minutes.

It is Pegula's fourth WTA final of 2026 — a number that speaks to her consistency at the sharp end of tournaments all season. The American has been one of the tour's most reliable closers, and she arrives in Sunday's final with the ranking, the experience, and the momentum to be the heavy favorite.

The Final: No. 1 vs. the Unseeded Underdog

Sunday's DC Open WTA final sets up one of the tournament's most compelling championship matches in recent memory. Pegula is the world's top seed, a seasoned competitor with four finals appearances this season alone. Eala is 22 years old, unseeded, and ranked No. 28 — and she has beaten four consecutive opponents who had every reason to be favored.

The match is scheduled to begin at 12 PM local time at Rock Creek Park Tennis Center. The WTA 500 level carries significant ranking points and prize money, with the winner also receiving a substantial boost to their season-end standing.

For the Philippines, regardless of Sunday's result, this week will be celebrated. Eala has already accomplished something no Filipino player had done before at this level of women's tennis.

What's Next

Sunday's DC Open WTA final — Pegula vs. Eala — starts at 12 PM local time (Washington, D.C.). Both players will then head to the WTA Toronto National Bank Open, which opens its main draw on Monday, August 3. Sabalenka (1), Rybakina (2), Pegula (3), and Gauff (4) are the top seeds. Eala's DC Open result may earn her a seeding in Toronto — a tournament that runs through August 13.
