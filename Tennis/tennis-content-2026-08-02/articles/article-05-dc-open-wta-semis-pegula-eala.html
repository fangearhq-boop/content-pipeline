DC Open WTA Semifinals: Pegula Rallies From 2-5 Down, Eala Dismisses Osaka — Final Today

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | August 2, 2026

Saturday's DC Open WTA semifinals produced two sharply contrasting stories: one match defined by a comeback from the brink, the other by a performance that never allowed the favorite to find her footing. Together, they set the stage for Sunday's final between No. 1 seed Jessica Pegula and unseeded Alexandra Eala.

Pegula Fights Back From 2-5

Top seed Jessica Pegula found herself in genuine difficulty early in her semifinal against No. 4 Diana Shnaider. The Russian teenager, in her first DC Open semifinal, broke early and built a 5-2 lead in the opening set that had every appearance of a potential upset taking shape.

Pegula won seven of the next eight games.

Starting at 2-5 down, Pegula increased her first-serve percentage, began attacking Shnaider's second serve with greater aggression, and simply outplayed her opponent in the most important stretch of the match. She took the set 7-5, and the momentum shift that came with it carried into the second. Pegula closed out 6-4 in 1 hour and 27 minutes total.

It is the fourth WTA final of 2026 for Pegula, who has been one of the most reliable front-runners on the tour this season. "I just tried to stay in it," she said afterward. Her capacity to maintain mental focus under pressure — the kind that a 2-5 scoreline in a semifinal creates — is a large part of why she continues to reach finals consistently.

Eala Is Dominant Against Osaka

In the second semifinal, Alexandra Eala did not offer Naomi Osaka the chance to build any momentum. Playing their first career meeting, Eala took the first set 6-4 with controlled aggression from the baseline, and the second 6-2 in a performance that was as commanding as the scoreline suggests.

Osaka, the former world No. 1 and three-time Grand Slam champion, was unable to find her best tennis against a player who moved well, hit deep, and showed no visible nerves in the biggest semifinal of her life. The 6-4, 6-2 scoreline against the No. 3 seed is Eala's best result of the week and perhaps the best individual performance of her career to this point.

With the win, Eala becomes the first Filipino player to reach a WTA 500 singles final — a milestone that carries the weight of a tennis-mad nation behind her in Sunday's championship match.

Final: No. 1 Seed vs. Unseeded No. 28

Sunday's DC Open WTA final matches the tournament's most experienced and decorated player in Pegula against a 22-year-old who has beaten four opponents this week who were all higher-ranked or higher-seeded. Eala has defeated Zheng, Fernandez, Svitolina, and Osaka without dropping a set in the quarterfinals or later.

The final begins at 12 PM local time at Rock Creek Park Tennis Center in Washington, D.C.

What's Next

Sunday's WTA final — Pegula vs. Eala — begins at noon in Washington. Following the DC Open, the tour moves to the Canadian Open in Toronto, where the WTA main draw opens Monday, August 3. Sabalenka (1), Rybakina (2), Pegula (3), and Gauff (4) are the top seeds. The ATP Montreal main draw begins Tuesday, August 4.
