Cincinnati Preview: Sinner Chases History, Alcaraz Returns, Zverev Eyes No. 1

By Marcus Cole | August 5, 2026 | Cincinnati Open Preview

The Cincinnati Open begins August 13, and the storylines heading into the Western & Southern Open are as compelling as any Masters 1000 of the year. Jannik Sinner is hunting history. Carlos Alcaraz is making his long-awaited return. Alexander Zverev is carrying the best form of his career. And the US Open is just 10 days after Cincinnati ends.

Everything about this event matters more than usual.

Sinner: Six Masters 1000 in a Row?

Jannik Sinner has won every Masters 1000 he has entered in 2026. That is five titles — a number that would define most players' entire careers. He then defended his Wimbledon crown, making it six elite titles across the year so far. The world No. 1 arrives in Cincinnati as the undisputed best player on the planet.

A sixth consecutive Masters 1000 title would be an Open Era record. No player has achieved it. The closest comparisons are Novak Djokovic's dominant stretches in 2015 and 2016, but even those did not produce this kind of consecutive-title run within a single season.

The caveat worth noting: Sinner is not the defending champion at Cincinnati. Alcaraz won the title in 2025 when Sinner retired from the final due to illness. That provides an interesting wrinkle — the record is available, but there is no points-defense pressure, which could cut both ways mentally. Sinner has shown throughout 2026 that records and milestones do not distract him.

Alcaraz: The Return Everyone Is Waiting For

Carlos Alcaraz shut his season down in April after suffering a right wrist injury. He missed the French Open. He missed Wimbledon. A player who was expected to be one of Sinner's two or three main rivals at every major for the next decade has been absent from the biggest stages of 2026.

He returns at Cincinnati as the defending champion, which carries its own symbolic weight. Alcaraz has won at the highest level — two Grand Slams, multiple Masters titles — and he is motivated in the way that only extended absence produces. Five months off, training hard, hungry to play. If his wrist is fully healthy, he is immediately one of the most dangerous players in the draw.

The question marks are real, though. Five months away from match play is a long time. Even the most gifted players need competitive matches to sharpen timing and intensity. Alcaraz knows this better than anyone. Cincinnati offers five to seven possible matches before the US Open — exactly the kind of ramp-up he needs.

Zverev: New Grand Slam, Same Chase

Alexander Zverev won his first Grand Slam title at Roland Garros in 2026, converting after years of near-misses. He reached the Wimbledon final before losing to Sinner. He is currently ranked No. 2 in the world but is among the leaders in the Race to Turin, sitting within meaningful range of Sinner atop the year-end standings.

Zverev enters Cincinnati as the world No. 2 and arguably in the best form of his career. His serve is elite. His forehand is a weapon. And the mental hurdle of winning a first Grand Slam — often the hardest barrier for great players — has been cleared. He will not be a pushover for anyone in the draw, Sinner included.

The Women's Field

The WTA Cincinnati draw features Aryna Sabalenka at the top, which is where she sits at every hard-court event this season. Defending the form she showed in her Toronto opener, Sabalenka will arrive in Ohio as the player to beat.

Elena Rybakina, Coco Gauff, Iga Swiatek, and Jessica Pegula round out the top tier. Swiatek is using the Canadian and Cincinnati swing to build hard-court form ahead of the US Open. Gauff, the 2023 US Open champion, remains a serious contender on her home surface. And Rybakina's flat, penetrating ball-striking is always dangerous on faster hard courts.

What's Next

Cincinnati begins August 13 and ends August 23. The US Open starts one day later, on August 24. There is no rest period between the two — players who make deep runs in Cincinnati will arrive in New York on short recovery time.

That scheduling tension, combined with the historical stakes around Sinner, the emotional resonance of Alcaraz's return, and Zverev's form coming off a Grand Slam breakthrough, makes Cincinnati the most anticipated Masters 1000 of the second half of 2026.

It is the final chapter before the season's last Grand Slam. Every match will matter.
