Sabalenka Opens Toronto With 24th Hard-Court Win in 25 Matches

By Ryan Calloway | August 5, 2026 | Toronto, Canada

Aryna Sabalenka made it 24 wins in her last 25 hard-court matches on Wednesday, defeating Japanese qualifier Moyuka Uchijima 6-3, 6-3 in her opening match at the WTA 1000 National Bank Open in Toronto. It was an efficient, no-nonsense display from a world No. 1 who arrived in Canada with a clear purpose.

Sabalenka dropped seven games across the two sets and showed no particular signs of rustiness after a grass-court season that ended earlier than expected. She moved well, hit cleanly through both wings, and never allowed Uchijima to build momentum from the baseline.

For the Japanese qualifier, the result was not unexpected — Sabalenka is simply a different level of player on hard courts. What the match provided was a clean hit-out for the top seed ahead of more demanding opponents further in the draw.

A Motivated World No. 1

Context matters when reading Sabalenka's results right now. Her 2026 has been exceptional in aggregate — she is the world's best player and has the statistics to back it up. But Wimbledon ended in the Round of 16 on grass, and a quarterfinal exit at Roland Garros on clay suggested that, on surfaces other than hard, even Sabalenka is vulnerable to the right opponent on the right day.

Hard courts are where she is most dangerous. The 24-for-25 record this season on the surface is not a fluke — it reflects a player whose power and movement are ideally suited to fast conditions. Toronto is the first major hard-court event of the US Open series for the women, and Sabalenka's performance on Wednesday confirmed she is already in the right place mentally and physically.

The Road Ahead in Toronto

Sabalenka's second-round opponent is Zhang Shuai, the Chinese veteran who advanced when Jelena Ostapenko retired from their match trailing 6-0, 4-0. Zhang, a resilient and experienced player, will require more from Sabalenka than Uchijima did — but the Belarusian will be a significant favorite.

The real tests in the Toronto draw will come if Sabalenka meets seeded players deep in the tournament. The women's draw features Iga Swiatek, Elena Rybakina, Coco Gauff, and Jessica Pegula, all of whom are capable of defeating anyone on their best day. If Sabalenka is the player she has been on hard courts in 2026, she will be difficult to stop.

What's Next

Toronto runs through August 13, overlapping with the final preparations before the US Open, which begins August 24. For Sabalenka, a deep run here — followed by Cincinnati — would produce exactly the kind of confidence and match sharpness she needs heading into the year's final Grand Slam.

Wednesday was a first step. The steps that follow will be more revealing. But with Sabalenka operating at 24-for-25 on the surface that matters most right now, the starting position is a strong one.
