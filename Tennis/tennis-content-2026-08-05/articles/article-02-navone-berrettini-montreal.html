Navone Saves 11 of 12 Break Points to Stun Berrettini in Montreal

By Marcus Cole | August 5, 2026 | Montreal, Canada

Mariano Navone produced one of the most remarkable break-point saving performances of the 2026 ATP season on Tuesday, clinging on to defeat Matteo Berrettini 6-3, 6-7(5), 6-3 in the first round of the National Bank Open in Montreal. The Argentine saved 11 of the 12 break points he faced across 156 minutes of hard-court combat — a number that tells its own story.

Berrettini, the powerful Italian, created danger on almost every Navone service game. He had the weapons to break: a vicious return, ferocious groundstrokes when he connected, and the experience of winning at Masters 1000 level. What he could not do was convert.

Navone answered every threat. He came up with clutch first serves when he needed them, redirected Berrettini's attacks into the corners when he needed to extend rallies, and simply refused to let the Italian hold any momentum for longer than a game or two. It was a masterclass in pressure management.

A Match Stretched Across Two Days

The drama was amplified by the tournament's weather conditions. Rain in Montreal halted play during the early rounds, and the Navone-Berrettini match was split across two days. Navone had to return to the court after an overnight delay, with the match in progress and the tension intact. He showed no signs of a reset in either direction — just the same focused, methodical performance that had carried him to a first-set lead.

Berrettini won the second set in a tiebreak, creating the possibility that the momentum had shifted. Navone responded by controlling the third set from the baseline and closing it out 6-3 to complete the win.

Navone's Career Best

The victory is Navone's 19th of the 2026 season — a career high, surpassing his previous bests of 18 wins in each of the prior two years. That number gains added significance because it was achieved on hard courts, away from the clay surface where Navone built his reputation.

The Argentine, ranked approximately No. 44 in the world, entered 2026 as a legitimate clay-court threat but a question mark on faster surfaces. Montreal is providing an answer. His serve-plus-groundstroke combination has proven adaptable, and his mental resilience — the thing that saved him 11 break points on Tuesday — travels across every surface.

Berrettini's Frustrating Pattern

For Berrettini, the defeat continues a frustrating trend. The Italian, ranked around No. 40, has made multiple returns from injury over the past three seasons, each one offering fresh hope that he can re-establish himself as a major force on the ATP Tour. Each time, the wins come in patches but the sustained results at Masters 1000 level remain elusive.

He is not a faded player — his serve remains elite and his forehand, when he is locked in, can beat anyone. But to win at the level he is capable of, he needs to convert those break point opportunities. Against Navone, he did not. Against the top players, that tends to be even harder.

What's Next

Navone advances to the second round in Montreal, where the draw could produce a significant test. In a tournament that has lost some top-end depth with the absences of Jannik Sinner, Carlos Alcaraz, and Novak Djokovic, there is genuine opportunity for a player like Navone to go deep if his level holds.

The Canadian Open continues through August 13. Whether Navone can maintain Tuesday's break-point immunity against stiffer opposition will be the story to watch. But for now: 19 wins, a career best, and a performance for the highlight reel. The Argentine is not just a clay player anymore.
