Draper Breaks Down in Tears as Atmane Ends Latest Montreal Comeback

By Elena Voss | August 5, 2026 | Montreal, Canada

Jack Draper was in tears during the third set of his first-round loss to Terence Atmane on Tuesday at the National Bank Open in Montreal. The scoreline read 6-3, 2-6, 6-2 when it was over. The emotional toll was harder to quantify.

Draper, the former world No. 4 and one of Britain's brightest tennis prospects, had accepted a wildcard into the Montreal Masters just days after withdrawing from the Citi Open in Washington with a recurring bone bruise in his left arm. His coaching team had weighed the risk. His body had given uncertain signals. He played anyway.

The decision made emotional sense. Draper has spent most of 2026 watching from the sidelines. He missed the entire clay-court swing. He withdrew from Wimbledon. Each time a window seemed to open, the arm shut it again. The wildcard was his best chance to log match play before the US Open, which begins August 24.

For a set, it looked like the gamble was paying off. Draper lost the opener but won the second set decisively, showing glimpses of the big-serving, heavy-hitting tennis that pushed him into the world's top four. The crowd in Montreal responded. The match was alive.

Then, in the third set, something shifted. Draper began showing visible discomfort. By the middle stages of the third set he was in tears — a public moment of grief and frustration from a player who has not had many moments of joy this season. Atmane, composed throughout, closed out the match 6-2 to advance to the second round.

Atmane's Performance

Terence Atmane, a 24-year-old Frenchman who has been quietly improving on the ATP Tour, deserves credit for his part in the result. He attacked Draper's serve with precision, kept his own first-serve percentage high, and refused to let the match become sloppy or emotional. Against an opponent fighting through pain and uncertainty, Atmane remained clean and clinical.

It is the kind of win that builds confidence. Atmane moves into the second round in Montreal, where he will likely face a stiff test — but he will do so having beaten a quality player on a significant court.

The Injury Picture

The bone bruise in Draper's left arm — his playing arm — has been the defining story of his 2026 season. The injury first surfaced during the spring and has refused to fully resolve. Each recovery timeline has pushed back the target date for a return to sustained competition.

This was his first Masters 1000 appearance since the Queen's Club event at Eastbourne in June. The rust was visible, the arm clearly not right, and the progression through the third set told the story of a player whose body is simply not ready for best-of-three intensity at Masters level.

What's Next

The US Open question now dominates the conversation around Draper. The tournament in New York begins August 24, giving him roughly three weeks to recover and make a decision. There are two paths: push through and compete in the final Grand Slam of the season, or shut down the 2026 campaign entirely and prioritize a clean build into 2027.

No announcement has been made. But the sight of Draper in tears at a Montreal Masters first-round exit tells you everything about how much this season — and how much getting back to the form that made him a top-four player — means to him. He is not a player who gives up easily. That quality has served him well throughout his career. Right now, it may also be the thing that needs to be carefully managed by the people around him.

The next few weeks will determine whether Britain's most talented men's tennis player in a generation gets a final crack at the year's last Grand Slam — or whether 2026 ends here, quietly, in Montreal.
