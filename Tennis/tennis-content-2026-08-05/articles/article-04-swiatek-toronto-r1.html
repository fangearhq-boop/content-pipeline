Swiatek Opens US Open Build-Up With Dominant 6-0, 6-3 Win in Toronto

By Elena Voss | August 5, 2026 | Toronto, Canada

Iga Swiatek launched the hard-court chapter of her 2026 season emphatically on Wednesday, defeating Sara Bejlek 6-0, 6-3 in the first round of the WTA 1000 National Bank Open in Toronto. The former world No. 1 dropped just three games and looked sharp and settled throughout — encouraging signs for a player who is determined to make an impact on the surface where she has historically had to work hardest.

Bejlek, a Czech player who qualified through to the main draw, offered limited resistance in the first set. Swiatek won six straight games without dropping a point on serve in the opener, a clinical display that signaled exactly the level of intensity she plans to bring to the US Open swing. The second set was more competitive, with Bejlek finding her range more regularly, but Swiatek responded to every push and closed out 6-3.

The Hard-Court Question

Swiatek's record on hard courts has always generated discussion. She has won a US Open — the 2022 title in New York — and she has demonstrated the ability to compete at the highest level on the surface. But the raw power statistics, the movement patterns, and the ball-bounding qualities of hard courts are different from clay, and so is the tactical picture that suits Swiatek best.

In 2026, that conversation has deepened. Aryna Sabalenka has been dominant on hard courts, claiming the world No. 1 ranking and recording a 24-for-25 hard-court win rate this season. Swiatek's clay season was outstanding, as it typically is, but the hard-court stretch from now until the end of August is where her ranking position and Grand Slam ambitions for 2026 will be most seriously tested.

Wednesday's bagel-set opening is the right kind of start — efficient, low-cost in energy, and confidence-building. The opponents will get harder from here.

Tournament Position

Swiatek advances to the second round in Toronto, where she will face a stiffer test than Bejlek presented. The WTA Toronto draw includes Sabalenka (No. 1 seed), Elena Rybakina, Coco Gauff, and Jessica Pegula, among others. If Swiatek is going to make a deep run, she will almost certainly need to go through at least one of those players before the final.

The women's draw in Toronto is one of the strongest assembled outside the Grand Slams this season. For a player trying to build momentum ahead of the US Open, there is no better proving ground — every win here carries genuine weight.

What's Next

Toronto runs through August 13. Swiatek will likely play again on Thursday or Friday in the second round. After that, Cincinnati (August 13-23) provides a second chance to rack up match wins before the US Open begins August 24.

The next three weeks will tell a great deal about what version of Swiatek shows up in New York. Wednesday's 6-0, 6-3 opening was not a shock — she is a top-tier player and she was supposed to win that match. The real measurement comes against opponents who can push back. If Swiatek wins those matches as convincingly as she won this one, the conversation about whether she can challenge Sabalenka at the US Open gets very interesting, very quickly.
