The Handshake That Wasn't: Svitolina's WTA Finals Runner-Up Story

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | November 16, 2026

Elina Svitolina's return to the WTA Finals final was everything her journey deserved. At 32, eight years after winning the title in Singapore in 2018, she navigated the draw at Indian Wells with poise, determination, and the sharp tennis that has defined the best periods of her career. What followed at the trophy ceremony was something harder to describe cleanly — and it dominated the conversation after the match.

How Svitolina Got There

Svitolina's path to the final began with a strong round-robin stage, punctuated by a 6-2, 6-1 semifinal win over Linda Noskova — a performance that left no doubt about her readiness. Through the week, she combined defensive tenacity with well-timed aggression, the same combination that made her a champion in 2018.

The final against Andreeva, however, was a different challenge. The 19-year-old came into Sunday's match having already beaten Svitolina 6-4, 6-0 in the round robin, and she carried that momentum forward. Svitolina found moments — winning a marathon game in the second set, saving a match point — but Andreeva held firm and closed it out 7-5, 6-3.

The Ceremony

The post-match handshake did not happen. It was not a surprise. Since Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, the Ukrainian Tennis Federation has maintained a policy under which Ukrainian players decline to shake hands with Russian and Belarusian players. Svitolina has followed this policy consistently throughout her career in this period — she also declined the handshake with Aryna Sabalenka earlier in the same tournament.

Svitolina's runner-up speech thanked the Indian Wells crowd, her team, and Ukraine. The name of her opponent was not mentioned. Andreeva, speaking after her, publicly praised what she called Svitolina's "amazing" season. Svitolina responded with a silent nod from her chair.

The reaction on social media was swift and divided. Some observers criticized Svitolina for failing to acknowledge the champion, calling it disrespectful. Others argued that the Ukrainian player has consistently and transparently explained her position, that it has nothing to do with Andreeva as an individual, and that expecting her to depart from it at the trophy ceremony misunderstands what the policy represents.

The Bigger Picture

The sport has navigated these tensions since 2022 without a uniform answer. The WTA allowed Russian and Belarusian players to continue competing under a neutral flag, leaving each individual player to determine how they handle on-court protocols. Svitolina has been among the most visible in explaining her choices, and she has been consistent.

What is not in dispute is the quality of her 2026 WTA Finals run. As a 32-year-old runner-up reaching the final of a season-ending championship against a 19-year-old on the ascent, the result itself carries meaning — a competitor still operating at the sport's highest level, long after many might have stepped back.

What's Next

Svitolina enters the off-season as one of the strongest performers at the WTA Finals this year, whatever the final result column reads. She will return to the tour in 2027 with her standing among the top players confirmed. How she and Andreeva's rivalry develops — and how the sport continues to manage its political context — remains one of the defining ongoing stories in women's tennis.
