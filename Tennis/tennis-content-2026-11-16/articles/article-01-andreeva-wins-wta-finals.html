Andreeva Wins WTA Finals Without Dropping a Set — First Teen Champion Since Sharapova

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | November 16, 2026

Mirra Andreeva walked off the court at Indian Wells on Sunday as a champion, a record-breaker, and the undisputed finishing statement of the 2026 WTA season. The 19-year-old defeated Elina Svitolina 7-5, 6-3 in the WTA Finals final to complete a flawless tournament run — five matches, five wins, zero sets dropped.

The achievement places Andreeva in select company. She became only the third teenager in history to win the WTA Finals, joining Chris Evert and Maria Sharapova. Sharapova won the event in 2004, making Andreeva the first teenage champion in 22 years. She also became the first player to win the WTA Finals in her debut appearance since Ash Barty accomplished the same feat in 2019.

How the Final Unfolded

The match took 1 hour and 48 minutes, with Andreeva controlling the rallies throughout while Svitolina searched for openings. The first set required resolve from the young Russian. Svitolina broke back and won a marathon game to level at 5-5 before Andreeva steadied and took the set 7-5.

The second set offered a glimpse of what might have been. Svitolina saved a match point but sent an overhead wide on the next attempt, handing Andreeva the title 6-3. Andreeva threw her racket into the air, then ran to embrace her coach Conchita Martinez and her team.

Earlier in the round robin stage, Andreeva had beaten Svitolina even more emphatically, 6-4, 6-0. She also beat Jessica Pegula 6-3, 6-4 in the semifinals, navigating the entire draw without conceding a set.

The Ceremony

The trophy presentation added another dimension to the story. Svitolina did not shake Andreeva's hand after the match, consistent with the Ukrainian Tennis Federation's standing policy that Ukrainian players will not shake hands with Russian or Belarusian players — a stance the organization has maintained since Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022. Svitolina also declined the post-match handshake with Aryna Sabalenka earlier in the event.

During her runner-up speech, Svitolina thanked the crowd, her team, and Ukraine. She did not name Andreeva or offer congratulations. Andreeva, speaking after her, publicly praised Svitolina's season as "amazing." Svitolina acknowledged the remark with a nod from her seat. The contrasting approaches immediately dominated coverage of the ceremony on social media.

The Season in Context

Andreeva ends 2026 at a career-high WTA No. 2 — a rise that seems improbable in its pace but logical in retrospect for anyone who watched her play this year. She has combined a baseline game built on relentless consistency with the capacity to lift her level in critical moments, a combination that became increasingly evident in the second half of the season.

At the WTA Finals specifically, she looked like the strongest player in the field from the first match, and the final was no exception. The event's format — round-robin qualifying followed by a single semifinal and final — rewarded sustained excellence, and Andreeva produced it without interruption.

What's Next

Andreeva now enters the off-season as the sport's freshest champion. The WTA calendar resumes in January 2027 with the Australian Open hard-court swing, where she will arrive as one of the year's most closely watched players. After this week, that attention is entirely earned.
