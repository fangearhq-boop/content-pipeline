No Sets Dropped, No Doubts: Andreeva's WTA Finals Run Tells the Full Story of Her Season

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | November 16, 2026

By the time Mirra Andreeva walked off the Indian Wells court on Sunday as the 2026 WTA Finals champion, the arc of her season had been written in full. Five wins. Five sets given up: none. A career-high year-end ranking of WTA No. 2. And a place alongside Chris Evert and Maria Sharapova in a category of one of the rarest achievements in tennis: a teenager winning the season-ending championship.

The WTA Finals Itself

Andreeva's performance at the WTA Finals was the most concentrated expression of everything she built in 2026. She went 3-0 in round-robin play, beat Jessica Pegula 6-3, 6-4 in the semifinals, and defeated Elina Svitolina 7-5, 6-3 in the final. She dropped zero sets across the five matches. Opponents found no entry point — not in the round robin against Svitolina (who she beat 6-4, 6-0), not in a semifinal against one of the tour's most experienced hardcourt players, and not in the final itself.

What that run showed was not just talent but temperament. The WTA Finals format — round-robin before knockout — rewards players who can sustain their level day after day without a single bad match costing them. Andreeva managed that without apparent difficulty.

What She Accomplished in 2026

The numbers alone do not fully capture a season that established Andreeva as more than a prospect. She ended the year at WTA No. 2 — higher than she had ever been — and achieved it by being one of the most reliable performers on the tour throughout the season, not just in a single tournament run.

At 19, she joins Chris Evert and Maria Sharapova as the only teenagers to have won the WTA Finals. Sharapova's title came in 2004, making Andreeva the first teenage champion in 22 years. She also became the first player to win the WTA Finals in a debut appearance since Ash Barty did it in 2019 — a comparison that speaks to the difficulty of winning this event under any circumstances, let alone as a first-time participant.

The Competitor Behind the Numbers

After winning the title, Andreeva said that she had to become "more brave and aggressive in important moments" to achieve what she did this year. The phrasing reflected a player who has not simply been carried by talent but has thought deliberately about her development. The WTA Finals, with its compressed schedule and deep field, required exactly the kind of mental adjustment she described.

She coached by former WTA No. 1 Conchita Martinez — a relationship that has clearly influenced her approach to both tactics and high-pressure situations. How much of this year's growth is sustainable, and how much was the product of an exceptional run of form, is a question only 2027 will answer.

What's Next

Andreeva enters the off-season at No. 2 in the world with a WTA Finals title to her name and months before the Australian Open hard-court swing in January. She will begin 2027 as one of the two players every other competitor on tour is measuring themselves against. For a player who turned 19 this year, that is an unusual position — and by everything she showed in 2026, she has earned it.
