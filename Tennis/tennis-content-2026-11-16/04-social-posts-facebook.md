### STORY 1: Mirra Andreeva Wins WTA Finals

**Long-Form Post (8:00 AM ET)**

History was made in Indian Wells on Sunday.

Mirra Andreeva, just 19 years old, won the 2026 WTA Finals by defeating Elina Svitolina 7-5, 6-3 in the final — completing the tournament without dropping a single set across five matches. It was a performance that placed her name alongside Chris Evert and Maria Sharapova as the only teenagers to ever win the WTA season-ending championship.

She is also the first player to win the WTA Finals on debut since Ash Barty did it in 2019, and she ends the year at a career-high WTA No. 2.

Sunday's ceremony added an emotional footnote. Svitolina — who has not shaken hands with Russian or Belarusian players since Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine — declined the post-match handshake. In her runner-up speech, she thanked the crowd, her team, and Ukraine, without mentioning the champion. Andreeva, for her part, publicly praised Svitolina's "amazing" season.

### STORY 3: Svitolina Runner-Up — Handshake Controversy

**Long-Form Post (12:30 PM ET)**

Elina Svitolina's run to the 2026 WTA Finals final deserves to be remembered on its own terms.

Eight years after winning the title in 2018, the 32-year-old Ukrainian made it back to the final, defeating Linda Noskova 6-2, 6-1 in the semifinals. It was a remarkable return for a player who has dealt with serious physical setbacks and carried the weight of representing Ukraine throughout.

In the final, she lost to Andreeva 7-5, 6-3. What followed at the trophy ceremony drew more attention than the match itself. Svitolina declined the post-match handshake — consistent with the Ukrainian Tennis Federation's standing policy regarding Russian and Belarusian players. Her runner-up speech thanked the crowd, her team, and Ukraine, but did not mention the champion. Fans have been divided over whether the omission was understandable or inappropriate.

Andreeva, to her credit, called Svitolina's season "amazing." Svitolina acknowledged the remark with a nod.

There is no clean way to cover this story. How do you feel about how the ceremony played out?
