Laver Cup 2026: 48 Hours to The O2 — Final Preview of Teams, Matchups and Format

By Elena Voss | September 23, 2026

ATP | Laver Cup

Two days from now, The O2 Arena in London becomes the center of the tennis world. The 10th edition of the Laver Cup — the sport's most theatrical team event — begins Friday, September 25, with Team Europe facing defending champions Team World across three days of play.

Europe has dominated this event since its 2017 inception, holding an all-time 8-1 advantage. But Team World's emphatic 15-9 victory in San Francisco last year proved the competition has genuine balance now. Europe want The O2 back. Team World intends to keep what it earned.

The Teams

Team Europe, captained by Yannick Noah, lines up with six players who, collectively, have won four of the last six Grand Slams. Carlos Alcaraz brings his big-match brilliance. Alexander Zverev arrives in London as the reigning French Open and US Open champion — arguably the form player in men's tennis right now. Casper Ruud, Jakub Menšík, Flavio Cobolli, and Rafael Jódar round out the squad, with Arthur Fery (Britain's top player) named as Europe's alternate.

Team World, captained by Andre Agassi, is shaped around Taylor Fritz and Alex de Minaur in the singles anchor roles. De Minaur has been one of the most consistent players on tour throughout 2026 and is a difficult assignment for any opponent. Alexander Bublik brings unpredictability, Learner Tien the youth and upside, Francisco Cerúndolo and Brandon Nakashima the depth. Both Nakashima and Cerúndolo joined the squad as late replacements — Ben Shelton withdrew for Davis Cup duty, Tommy Paul pulled out with an elbow injury — but Team World has historically thrived with a united bench regardless of late changes.

The Format

Three sessions are played each day — three singles and one doubles. Day 1 matches are worth one point per win, Day 2 worth two points, and Day 3 worth three. The first team to reach 13 points wins the Laver Cup. With 24 points available in total and the back-end matches worth most, no lead is safe until the final calculation is done.

Day 1 begins with the afternoon session at 1 PM local time, followed by the evening session at 7 PM. The morning session on Days 2 and 3 replaces the second evening slot.

The Story Lines

The biggest individual narrative going into Friday is Zverev's form. He won two Grand Slams in 2026 (Roland Garros and the US Open) and arrives in London as arguably the most dangerous player in the world on any surface. Playing for Team Europe in the city where he has had some of his best results adds another layer to an already compelling run.

De Minaur is Team World's trump card. The Australian has been relentlessly consistent all year, converting on big moments and rarely giving opponents anything for free. In a format where singles margins are narrow, his ability to win tight matches in the third set is exactly what Team World needs.

The Fery angle is also worth noting. Arthur Fery, named as Europe's alternate, is one of the UK's brightest tennis talents. Should he see court time at The O2, the atmosphere from a London crowd watching one of their own play for Team Europe will be something.

What's Next

The Laver Cup begins Friday, September 25 with the afternoon session at 1 PM local (London). Coverage runs through Sunday, September 27. Following the event, the focus of world tennis shifts to Asia — Tokyo, Beijing, Shanghai, and Wuhan — where October's slate of ATP 500s, ATP 1000s, and WTA events awaits. Watch for match-by-match updates from The O2 starting Friday morning.
