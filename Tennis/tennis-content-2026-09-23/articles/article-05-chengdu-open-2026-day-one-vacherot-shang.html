Chengdu Open 2026 Day 1: Vacherot Leads, Shang Roars, Kouame Makes His Debut

By Marcus Cole | September 23, 2026

ATP | Chengdu Open | Day 1

While the tennis world's attention is largely fixed on London for the Laver Cup, the city of Chengdu is doing its best to compete. The ATP 250 Chengdu Open began play Wednesday, and for the sport's emerging names and ambitious veterans, it is the week that matters.

Valentin Vacherot headlines the draw as the No. 1 seed, with defending champion Alejandro Tabilo returning to defend the title he won here in 2025. Shang Juncheng — China's most prominent active player — brings the crowd noise that makes this tournament one of the most atmospheric 250-level events on the calendar.

The Defending Champion

Tabilo's title defense is one of the tournament's defining narratives. The Chilean claimed his Chengdu crown in 2025 and will be targeting a repeat in front of a partisan crowd that, while primarily behind Shang, respects a champion defending his territory.

Tabilo is a consistent performer on hard courts and enters the week with solid motivation: a second Chengdu title would be a significant statement about his standing in the mid-tier of the ATP Tour, where competition for ranking points in the 250s is fierce.

The Home Hero

Shang Juncheng may not be the top seed, but he is the player every fan in Chengdu came to see. The young Chinese player has been developing steadily on the ATP Tour and plays a brand of tennis that is exciting to watch — flat, aggressive, and built around big first strikes.

The home crowd advantage in Chengdu is real. Shang has spoken about how much it means to compete in front of Chinese fans, and that energy has translated to strong performances in the past. Whether it carries him deep into this year's draw remains to be seen, but the atmosphere when he is on court will be unlike anything else this week in tennis.

Kouame and the Next Generation

Perhaps the most intriguing name in the Chengdu draw is Ulrich Kouame — 17 years old, American, and here on a wild card through the ATP's Next Gen Accelerator program. Kouame is one of the most touted junior talents in the world right now, and the Chengdu wild card is his first taste of ATP main draw tennis.

The NextGen Accelerator program exists specifically to give top junior talents early main draw exposure, and Kouame is exactly the profile it was designed for. Don't expect a deep run from a 17-year-old facing seasoned tour professionals, but watch for moments that suggest what the future holds.

Depth in the Field

Denis Shapovalov and Alejandro Davidovich Fokina add genuine depth to a draw that doesn't need it only on paper. Shapovalov, the Canadian veteran, has had a frustrating relationship with consistency in recent years but remains a genuine threat on hard courts when his first serve and forehand are firing. Davidovich Fokina is similarly dangerous on a good day.

What's Next

The Chengdu Open runs through September 29. First-round matches continue Wednesday and Thursday. Shang and Tabilo will be the first results to watch, and Kouame's debut match will attract attention beyond just the NextGen community.
