Sinner May Skip Asian Swing Entirely — Vienna Return Emerges as Option

By Marcus Cole | September 23, 2026

ATP | Jannik Sinner | Injury

The Jannik Sinner injury story has taken its most significant turn yet. World No. 1 Jannik Sinner, who had been targeting the China Open in Beijing for his return from a right-knee tendon injury, could now bypass the entire Asian swing and make his comeback in Vienna in late October instead.

That assessment came from Stefano Pescosolido, a former Italian ATP professional, speaking to Italian outlet Tuttosport. "I think he's a little more careful from a physical standpoint," Pescosolido said. "He wants to avoid relapses."

The Injury Timeline

Sinner has not played a professional match since defending his Wimbledon title on July 12, defeating Alexander Zverev in four sets. Shortly after, he was diagnosed with extra-articular tendon inflammation in his right knee and began a three-week treatment program at JMedical in Turin.

He resumed high-intensity training at the Circolo della Stampa Sporting club in Turin, and earlier reports indicated he was targeting a return at the China Open (September 30). That target now appears less certain. No official withdrawal from Beijing has been announced as of Wednesday, but the language from those around Sinner has shifted noticeably toward caution.

The Reasoning

Sinner's ATP Finals spot in Turin (November) is already secure — he is No. 2 in the Race to Turin with 7,950 points, behind only Alexander Zverev. That cushion removes much of the urgency to rush back.

Additionally, the conditions in China in late September and early October — particularly the humidity in Beijing — are not ideal for a player returning from a knee injury. Pescosolido's suggestion of Vienna (October 26 – November 1) as a return date would give Sinner a tune-up event on indoor hard courts just before the Paris Masters (November 2-8), providing a more controlled re-entry to the tour.

The tradeoff is points. Sinner is the defending champion at the China Open, where he beat Learner Tien 6-2, 6-2 in last year's final. Skipping means dropping those ranking points when the anniversary window expires.

What It Means for the Race and the Draw

If Sinner skips Beijing, Alexander Zverev — who enters the China Open at No. 2 in the world — becomes the clear favourite and the natural beneficiary of an open draw. Novak Djokovic, returning to Beijing for the first time since 2015, also stands to gain from an absence by the defending champion.

For the Race to Turin, Zverev could close the gap significantly. He trails Sinner by 1,700 points entering the Asian swing, and a strong run through Beijing and Shanghai would put him within striking distance of the top spot by November.

What's Next

Sinner is expected to make a final decision on the China Open within the next few days — the draw in Beijing is scheduled for September 28. If he withdraws before the draw, a lucky loser or alternate will take his slot. His team has not publicly commented on the reports. Watch for any official communication from Sinner's camp ahead of the draw date, and check the ATP Tour entry lists for changes.
