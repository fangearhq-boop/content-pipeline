Hangzhou Open 2026 Day 1: Medvedev Headlines Wide-Open ATP 250 Draw

By Elena Voss | September 23, 2026

ATP | Hangzhou Open | Day 1

The ATP 250 Hangzhou Open opened Wednesday, and for the players who chose to stay in China rather than make the trip to London for Laver Cup, the timing could not be better. With nearly every player ranked in the top 10 either at The O2 Arena or watching from home, the $1,039,090 prize pot in Hangzhou is up for grabs.

Daniil Medvedev is the No. 1 seed, and on paper the Russian should be among the clear favourites in a depleted field. He received a first-round bye and will face the winner of the match between Valentin Royer and Adam Walton in the second round.

The Field

The top four seeds at Hangzhou are Medvedev (1), Andrey Rublev (2), Tomás Martín Etcheverry (3), and Quentin Halys (4). It is a field built of genuine quality — Medvedev and Rublev are both former top-5 players trying to re-establish themselves in the rankings after uneven seasons — but a field shaped significantly by who is absent.

Alcaraz, Zverev, Sinner, Fritz, de Minaur, and the rest of the Laver Cup contingent are all at The O2. That absence creates space for the next tier of players to stake their fall claim, and Hangzhou is one of the more realistic routes onto that stage.

Medvedev's Motivation

For Medvedev specifically, this week carries real urgency. He was ranked in the world's top 5 as recently as 2025, but a difficult season — including early exits at three of the four Grand Slams — has dropped him down the rankings. He is currently outside the Race to Turin's top 8, and a title run in Hangzhou would inject points and momentum before Beijing and Shanghai begin.

Indoor hard courts remain one of Medvedev's best surfaces. His ability to construct points from the back of the court and return serve at an elite level makes him a formidable opponent when the game slows down indoors.

Rublev's Opportunity

Andrey Rublev at No. 2 is another significant storyline. The Russian has been a consistent ATP circuit presence for years but has yet to break through to a Grand Slam final. A title run at Hangzhou would not solve that, but it would represent a positive data point in what has been an inconsistent 2026 for Rublev.

A Medvedev–Rublev final at the Hangzhou Open would be an intriguing matchup — both players share a Russian background, have similar baseline styles, and have split their historical head-to-heads fairly evenly.

What's Next

First-round play continues in Hangzhou through Thursday, with the tournament scheduled through September 29. Medvedev's second-round match will be one of the early featured matches once his bye sees him into round two.
