Djokovic Stunned by Navone in Historic First-Round Exit at 2026 US Open

By Elena Voss

August 31, 2026

US Open 2026

It had never happened before — not once in Novak Djokovic's 22 years of Grand Slam competition at Flushing Meadows. But on Sunday night, under the lights at Arthur Ashe Stadium, history was made in the most unlikely and painful way imaginable.

Djokovic, the No. 4 seed and four-time US Open champion, fell to Argentine qualifier Mariano Navone 7-6(5), 5-7, 4-6, 6-2, 6-1 in four hours and 36 minutes. It is the first time in his career that Djokovic has lost in the opening round of the US Open, and only the second time since the 2006 Australian Open that he has lost in the first round of any Grand Slam.

It was not a match free of context. Djokovic was visibly ill throughout, vomiting courtside on multiple occasions, struggling to maintain his footing and intensity in the later stages. He held the first set in a tiebreak and battled back to level in the second, but by the fourth set, the illness had taken its toll. Navone, 25, played with remarkable composure and capitalised on every opportunity.

The Records That Fell

The loss carries extraordinary statistical weight. Djokovic had won 78 consecutive Grand Slam first-round matches before Sunday's defeat — a streak that stretched back decades. His last R1 Grand Slam defeat, at the 2006 Australian Open, came when he was 18 years old and still a developing player on the tour. He had never lost a first-round match at the US Open in his entire career.

The 39-year-old has now gone 12 consecutive Grand Slam events without adding to his record 24 major titles. His pursuit of a 25th Grand Slam — a milestone that has defined his final years on tour — will now extend at minimum into 2027.

"I don't want to make excuses," Djokovic said after the match. "Mariano played well. I wasn't feeling my best, but he deserved to win tonight."

Navone's Breakthrough Moment

For Mariano Navone, the victory is the defining result of his career. The 25-year-old Argentine, ranked outside the top 50 entering the tournament, had drawn significant attention by qualifying through three rounds to reach the main draw. Defeating the most decorated player in Grand Slam history in five sets, in front of a packed Arthur Ashe crowd, is the kind of win that can reshape a career.

Navone won 65 percent of his first-serve points in the match and broke Djokovic seven times. He was relentless in the final two sets, winning 12 of the last 16 games played.

Draw Implications

Djokovic's exit has significant implications for the remaining men's draw. He was seeded fourth and had been considered a genuine title contender despite his age. With Jannik Sinner having withdrawn before the tournament due to a knee injury, and No. 1 seed Alexander Zverev on the opposite side of the draw, Djokovic's section now opens considerably for the surviving seeds.

Defending champion Carlos Alcaraz (No. 2) is on the same side of the draw as Zverev and faces his own first-round test on Day 2 against Roman Safiullin.

What's Next

Mariano Navone advances to the second round of the US Open for the first time in his career and will face a seeded opponent next. For Djokovic, the defeat raises genuine questions about his path forward. The 2027 Australian Open — where he has won 10 titles — looms as his next Grand Slam opportunity.

The 2026 US Open continues through Sunday, September 13. Day 2 on Monday features a marquee slate headlined by Alcaraz's return and No. 1 seed Aryna Sabalenka's opening match.
