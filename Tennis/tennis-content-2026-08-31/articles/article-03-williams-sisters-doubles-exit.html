Serena and Venus Williams Fall in First-Round US Open Doubles — A Grand Slam Farewell

By Marcus Cole

August 31, 2026

US Open 2026

It ended the way so many chapters do in long careers — quickly, quietly, and in the first round. But the significance of what Serena and Venus Williams did just by stepping on the doubles court together at the 2026 US Open should not be measured in set scores.

The Williams sisters fell in the first round of the women's doubles draw at Flushing Meadows, closing the book on their Grand Slam doubles reunion. For fans who had waited years to see them play together again at a major, the result stung. But the moment itself — the presence, the legacy, the sheer audacity of two tennis legends returning to the court where they built so much of their shared history — was worth every second.

A Reunion With Deep Roots

Serena and Venus won the US Open doubles title in 1999 and again in 2009. They hold 14 Grand Slam doubles titles together — a partnership record in the Open Era that few players will ever approach. Their last Grand Slam doubles appearance before 2026 came at the 2022 US Open, where they also fell in the first round, to Lucie Hradecka and Linda Noskova.

The 2026 reunion carried extra emotional weight. Serena, who retired from singles competition in 2022 before returning to competitive tennis in 2026, received a wild card for the women's doubles alongside Venus. Venus, 46 years old and playing as a singles wild card, had already exited the tournament after losing in R1 to Sofia Kenin on Day 1.

Together, they were given one last chance at Flushing Meadows — and they took it without hesitation.

What the Moment Meant

Their partnership spans more than two decades. Serena and Venus first paired together in Grand Slam doubles in the late 1990s, and their combination of athleticism, court sense, and sheer competitive will made them the most successful doubles partnership of their generation. Even in decline, they drew full crowds and standing ovations simply by walking on court.

The 2026 US Open doubles appearance was as much a celebration as a competition. The fans at Arthur Ashe understood that — and gave them the reception they deserved.

What's Next

With the doubles exit, Serena and Venus Williams have completed their 2026 US Open campaigns. Whether either player returns to competitive tennis after this event remains to be seen — both have remained coy about their long-term futures. Serena has indicated she would "listen to her body" as the year progresses.

The 2026 US Open continues through Sunday, September 13. Day 2 on Monday features Alcaraz's highly anticipated return, Sabalenka's opening match, and the Fils-Tsitsipas first-round clash.
