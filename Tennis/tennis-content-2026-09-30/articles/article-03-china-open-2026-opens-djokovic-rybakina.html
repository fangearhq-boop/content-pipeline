China Open 2026 Opens: Djokovic Returns to Beijing, Rybakina Enters as World No. 1

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | September 30, 2026

The China Open is back. The ATP 500 and WTA 1000 double-header at the National Tennis Center in Beijing began its main draw Tuesday with one of the most star-studded combined events on the calendar — and the storylines are rich from the first day.

Novak Djokovic is making his first appearance at the China Open since 2015. The 39-year-old Serbian legend is 29-0 all-time in Beijing with six titles, a record so dominant it's become part of tennis mythology. After a first-round exit at the US Open, Djokovic returns to a city where he has never lost — opening against Nuno Borges on Tuesday. Even Borges acknowledged the psychological weight of the moment. "More pressure for him," the Portuguese player said. It was meant lightly, but it wasn't entirely wrong.

ATP Draw: Zverev and Djokovic on Collision Course

The big news in the ATP 500 draw is that No. 1 seed Alexander Zverev and Djokovic (No. 6 seed) have landed in the same quarter of the draw, setting up a potential quarterfinal meeting. Zverev — the Race to Turin leader after his US Open title — opens against Cameron Norrie.

The draw also features Andrey Rublev, Hubert Hurkacz, and Jakub Menšík, fresh off the Laver Cup. Notably absent: Jannik Sinner, who withdrew Monday due to his ongoing knee injury. Sinner was the defending champion in Beijing but confirmed Tuesday he would not play. His next target is the Shanghai Masters, beginning October 7.

WTA Draw: Rybakina's First No. 1 WTA 1000

On the women's side, Elena Rybakina steps onto the Beijing courts with a number she's never worn before. The Kazakh star — who became the first Kazakh player ever, male or female, to hold the World No. 1 ranking on September 14 — is entering her first WTA 1000 event as the top seed.

Rybakina won her third Grand Slam at the 2026 US Open, defeating Aryna Sabalenka 6-4, 5-7, 6-2 to end Sabalenka's 99-week reign at the top. Sabalenka enters Beijing as No. 2 seed, with Coco Gauff (No. 3) and Mirra Andreeva (No. 4) also in the draw.

Perhaps the most eagerly anticipated draw detail: Gauff and Iga Swiatek — who has fallen to No. 8 after a difficult 2026 — are in the same quarter, setting up a potential quarterfinal that would be their 17th career meeting. Swiatek leads the head-to-head 11-5, but Gauff won their only 2026 meeting at the United Cup.

What's Next

The first-round action continues Tuesday and Wednesday, with quarterfinals expected toward the end of the week. For the ATP, the event runs through October 6; the WTA 1000 extends through October 11. With multiple top seeds in action and the Djokovic return story as its centerpiece, the China Open looks set to be the defining event of the fall Asian swing.
