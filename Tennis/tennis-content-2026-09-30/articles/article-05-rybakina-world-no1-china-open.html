Rybakina Enters China Open as World No. 1 — First Kazakh Player Ever to Reach Top Ranking

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | September 30, 2026

Elena Rybakina stepped onto the Beijing practice courts this week with a new designation: World No. 1. The China Open is her first WTA 1000 event since claiming the ranking, and the significance of the moment is not lost on anyone who has followed women's tennis in 2026.

Rybakina reached the top of the WTA rankings on September 14, the day after her 2026 US Open title translated into the official rankings update. She had defeated Aryna Sabalenka 6-4, 5-7, 6-2 in the final to claim her third Grand Slam — joining Wimbledon (2022) and the Australian Open (2026). In doing so, she ended Sabalenka's remarkable 99-week reign at No. 1.

History Made in Kazakhstan

The milestone extends beyond tennis. Rybakina is the first Kazakh player — male or female — ever to hold the World No. 1 ranking in professional tennis. Kazakhstan has produced Davis Cup competitors and WTA Tour regulars over the years, but the No. 1 ranking had remained out of reach until now.

Born in Moscow and representing Kazakhstan since 2018, Rybakina has always carried the flag with quiet determination. Her game — a massive serve, clean flat groundstrokes, and a defensive baseline skill set that few can match — has steadily evolved into one of the most complete packages in women's tennis. Three Grand Slams in four years is the result.

The WTA Landscape

Rybakina's ascent has coincided with a dramatic reshuffling of the WTA rankings. Iga Swiatek, who held No. 1 for 99 consecutive weeks and was widely regarded as the dominant force in women's tennis, has fallen to No. 8 following a difficult 2026. She won just one title this year and was beaten by Rybakina in the US Open fourth round.

The field Rybakina faces in Beijing is formidable. Sabalenka (No. 2), Coco Gauff (No. 3), and Mirra Andreeva (No. 4) are all seeded and capable of winning the tournament outright. Swiatek (No. 8) and Gauff are in the same quarter, setting up a potential quarterfinal for the 17th time in their rivalry — a match that now carries extra significance as part of the battle to chase Rybakina at the top.

What's Next

Rybakina opens her China Open campaign this week, with the WTA 1000 event running through October 11. A strong run would cement her position as the unquestioned world leader heading into the indoor season. Having already qualified for the WTA Finals in Shenzhen in November, she will be the player everyone at the top of the draw is trying to beat.
