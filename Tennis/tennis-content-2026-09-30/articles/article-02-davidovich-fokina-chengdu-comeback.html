Davidovich Fokina Wins Chengdu Open in Comeback from Injury — First Hard Court Title

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | September 30, 2026

Alejandro Davidovich Fokina hadn't played a tour match since late July. His lower back had forced him off the court after losing to Alex Molcan at Umag, and three months of recovery followed. When he finally returned to competition in Chengdu this week, nobody knew what to expect.

He won the whole tournament.

Davidovich Fokina defeated Hubert Hurkacz 6-4, 7-6(9-7) in 1 hour 46 minutes Monday to claim the Chengdu Open title — the first hard court title of his career after four previous hard court final losses, and his second ATP title overall.

Never Broke, Never Broke Down

The match statistics told the story of a player who never doubted himself. Davidovich Fokina did not face a single break point in the entire final. He held every service game cleanly against Hurkacz, one of the best servers on the ATP Tour, and combined that consistency with the kind of relentless groundstroke pressure that makes the Spaniard so difficult to play against when he's healthy.

The second set came down to a tiebreak — and that's where Davidovich Fokina truly announced himself. He fell into a 9-5 deficit against four set points. He saved every one of them. He closed out the tiebreak 9-7.

"In that tiebreak, I told myself to just play each point," Davidovich Fokina said. "I wasn't thinking about how many set points they had. I was just playing tennis."

A Career-Defining Week

Before this week, Davidovich Fokina's only ATP title had come at Mallorca earlier in 2026 on clay and grass. He had come close on hard courts — four finals, four losses. There was a question mark over whether he could translate his clay-court game to the faster surface.

The Chengdu week answered it definitively. He dropped no sets in seven matches, never faced a break point in the final, and won on return. It was the complete package. At 27, he now has two titles on two different surfaces and a legitimate case as one of the most improved players on tour in 2026.

"I want to dedicate this victory to my whole team," he said, emotional on the podium. "They were with me through the injury. They believed in me when I wasn't sure I believed in myself."

What's Next

Davidovich Fokina will move on to the China Open in Beijing and the Shanghai Masters as the Asian swing continues. Fresh off three months of recovery and now with a title in hand, he's one of the most dangerous floaters in any draw this fall. For Hurkacz, the runner-up finish still adds Race to Turin points as the Polish No. 1 continues his push for the ATP Finals.
