Sinner Withdraws from China Open, Targets Shanghai Return

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | September 30, 2026

Jannik Sinner will not play the China Open. The World No. 1 officially withdrew from the Beijing event Monday, releasing a statement that ended the speculation about his return and pointed toward a new date on the calendar.

"I'm very sorry to announce that I won't be able to play in Beijing this year," Sinner said. "My knee is not where I would like it to be and I'm not ready to compete yet. I will do everything possible to be there on court as soon as I can."

His next target is the Shanghai Masters, which begins October 7.

The Injury Toll

Sinner's last competitive match was the Wimbledon final in July, which he won. The right knee injury that followed has now cost him four consecutive top-level events: the Canadian Open, the Cincinnati Open, the US Open, and the China Open. He is the defending champion in Beijing, where he beat Learner Tien 6-2, 6-2 in the 2025 final.

The absence has had real consequences. Sinner remains No. 1 in the official PIF ATP Rankings — where he leads Alexander Zverev by 1,810 points — but Zverev now leads the Race to Turin by approximately 700 points after his US Open title. The Race to Turin determines seedings for the ATP Finals in November, making Shanghai suddenly one of the most important weeks of Sinner's season.

What Changed

Earlier reports had Sinner traveling to Beijing and targeting a return as early as September 30. That optimism did not hold. His team's cautious approach — which his agent Claudio Pescosolido had described as prioritizing long-term health over racing back — ultimately prevailed.

Shanghai at an ATP 1000 level offers 1,000 ranking points and a significant Race to Turin payday. If Sinner returns there and performs well, he can reclaim ground on Zverev. If he misses Shanghai too, the Race to Turin picture becomes much more complex heading into the final indoor swing.

What's Next

The Shanghai Masters runs October 7-18. If Sinner is healthy enough to play, it will be his first competitive match in more than two months. The indoor season that follows — Vienna, Basel, Paris — would then complete his year. For now, the tennis world waits. Sinner has given no specific timeline beyond Shanghai as the next target.
