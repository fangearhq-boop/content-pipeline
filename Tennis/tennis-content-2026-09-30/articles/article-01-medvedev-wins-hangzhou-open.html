Medvedev Wins Hangzhou Open, Defeats Rublev 7-5, 6-4 for 24th Career Title

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | September 30, 2026

Daniil Medvedev dispatched Andrey Rublev 7-5, 6-4 in 93 minutes at the Hangzhou Open final on Monday, claiming his 24th career ATP title with a performance built around 23 aces and a serve that Rublev could not crack from first point to last.

Medvedev never dropped his serve in the match. That was the story. In an all-Russian final with Race to Turin points on the line, Medvedev reduced the contest to a simple equation: hold, pressure on return, collect breaks when they came. He broke Rublev once in each set, made it stand, and walked off the court as champion.

Third Title of the Year

Hangzhou is Medvedev's third title of 2026, joining Brisbane (January) and Dubai (February). After a quiet middle stretch of the season, his form on the Asian hard courts has been sharp. He beat Roman Safiullin in the semifinal before handling Rublev in the final.

The win extends Medvedev's all-time head-to-head lead over Rublev to 8-2. The two are longtime rivals and former training partners, and Rublev had entered the final in strong form — he won his 400th career ATP match in the semifinal against Frenchman Kyrian Jacquet. It wasn't enough against Medvedev's serving machine on Monday.

"Andrey played well this week," Medvedev said after the match. "But today my serve was working, and when that happens it becomes very difficult for anyone."

Race to Turin Picture

With Jannik Sinner absent from the Asian swing due to injury, every ATP 250 and 500 title this fall carries outsized weight in the Race to Turin. Medvedev sits at No. 6 in the ATP rankings and needed points to keep his ATP Finals hopes alive. Hangzhou delivers exactly that — 250 ranking points for the champion.

Rublev adds 165 points as finalist. Both players now head to Beijing for the China Open, which opened its main draw Tuesday and runs through October 6.

What's Next

Medvedev is expected to compete at the China Open (ATP 500) in Beijing this week and then the Shanghai Masters (ATP 1000) beginning October 7. With the ATP Finals in Turin scheduled for November, the Asian swing is his final window to lock up points before the season's showcase event. After Hangzhou, he's given himself momentum.
