Rybakina Into US Open R4 After French Open Revenge Win — WTA No. 1 Is Two Wins Away

By Marcus Cole | September 6, 2026

Elena Rybakina defeated Yuliia Starodubtseva 7-6(6), 6-3 to reach the US Open Round of 16, avenging her French Open second-round loss to the Ukrainian. With the victory, Rybakina now needs just two more wins to mathematically claim the WTA world No. 1 ranking from Aryna Sabalenka.

Elena Rybakina came to Flushing Meadows with three goals: win matches, stay healthy, and chase the world No. 1 ranking. After Saturday's 7-6(6), 6-3 victory over Yuliia Starodubtseva, all three remain on track.

The win was a tightly-contested affair in the first set — the tiebreak went to 8-6 in Rybakina's favor before she closed it out — before the Kazakh pulled away more decisively in the second. The final scoreline understates the first-set tension. It does not understate the result's significance: Rybakina is through to the Round of 16 and inching closer to a ranking milestone she has been targeting all season.

The French Open Debt Paid

When Starodubtseva defeated Rybakina in the second round at Roland Garros earlier this year, it was one of the more notable early-round upsets of the 2026 clay season. Rybakina, then battling to stay fit and maintain her ranking, exited a major earlier than expected against the Ukrainian.

Saturday's result erases that account. Hard courts suit Rybakina's game — heavy first serve, aggressive groundstrokes, minimal over-hitting — better than clay, and she came out of Starodubtseva's serve-focused baseline game in control.

The ankle injury that forced her retirement in Cincinnati at the end of August, which had been the primary fitness concern heading into the US Open, showed no visible effect on Saturday. Rybakina moved freely and served with her characteristic power throughout the match.

The Math: WTA No. 1 Is Within Reach

The ranking calculation is straightforward: Rybakina needs to reach the US Open semifinals — two more wins beyond Saturday's result — to mathematically displace Aryna Sabalenka at the top of the WTA world rankings.

Sabalenka has held the No. 1 position for an extended stretch in 2026, and defending her US Open points from last year means her baseline is high. But Rybakina winning through to the semifinals — or further — while Sabalenka exits early would shift the math decisively.

The added subplot: if both players advance deep into the draw, they could meet in the quarterfinals. Sabalenka and Rybakina are in the same quarter of the draw, and their H2H sits at 6-6 career. A quarterfinal match between them, with the world No. 1 ranking as a side prize, would be one of the best matches of the 2026 season regardless of what comes before or after it.

Health and Confidence

Perhaps the most significant development for Rybakina is simply that she is here and healthy. The Cincinnati ankle retirement — she had been unable to walk without pain before the US Open — raised genuine questions about whether she would be competitive at Flushing Meadows at all. She has answered those questions with performances, which is the only answer that counts.

Two wins to go. One ranking position to gain. The motivation could not be clearer.

What's Next

Elena Rybakina faces her Round of 16 opponent in the US Open fourth round later this week at Flushing Meadows. A win puts her in the quarterfinals and one round closer to the WTA No. 1 ranking. The mathematical threshold is a semifinal appearance; a run to the final or title would make it mathematically certain regardless of Sabalenka's results. For the Kazakh, who has carried the weight of the No. 1 pursuit across the entire back half of the 2026 season, the US Open represents the clearest path to the milestone she has been chasing.
