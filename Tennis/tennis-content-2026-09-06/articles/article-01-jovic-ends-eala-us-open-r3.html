Jovic Ends Eala's Historic US Open Run in Three-Hour Arthur Ashe Night Session

By Elena Voss | September 6, 2026

Iva Jovic defeated Alex Eala 7-5, 3-6, 7-5 in 3 hours and 3 minutes at Arthur Ashe Stadium Saturday night, sending the Philippines' first-ever seeded US Open player home in the third round and propelling Jovic into the first Round of 16 appearance of her career.

When Alex Eala led 4-2 in the third set, it looked like Philippine tennis history was about to be made.

It wasn't. Iva Jovic rallied to win five of the last six games, closing out a 7-5, 3-6, 7-5 victory in 3 hours and 3 minutes at Arthur Ashe Stadium in front of a crowd that had come expecting exactly this kind of fight — and got it.

The result extends Jovic's career head-to-head record against Eala to 3-0. It sends Jovic to the US Open Round of 16 for the first time in her career, where she will face No. 4 seed Coco Gauff on Monday in an all-American fourth-round collision.

A Match of Momentum Swings

The first set went to Jovic 7-5 after a tight, physical opening that set the tone. Eala, the No. 17 seed and the first Philippine player ever to earn a seeding at this tournament, steadied herself and took the second set 6-3 to level the match and send the contest into a deciding third.

When Eala broke early in the third and built to 4-2, a historic result felt within reach. But Jovic — who had been in this position before in their three meetings — found something on her serve and pushed Eala back. Five of the last six games went to the American, and with them, the match.

The scoreline reads 7-5, 3-6, 7-5. The effort on both sides of the net was considerably closer than that.

What Eala Accomplished

The loss should not diminish what Eala built at this US Open. She arrived at Flushing Meadows as the No. 17 seed — the highest seeding ever held by a Philippine player at a Grand Slam. She defeated two opponents before falling in the night session of the third round, on Arthur Ashe, in front of a crowd that had turned out specifically to watch her.

The run produced Philippine tennis's biggest-ever US Open moment. For a tennis program that has historically struggled to make an impression at Grand Slam level, having a player seeded in the top 20 and reaching the third round represents a genuine milestone — not a stepping stone.

At 22, Eala has years to build on this. The 2026 US Open is a high-water mark, not a ceiling.

Jovic's Breakthrough

For Iva Jovic, Saturday was a confirmation. The 21-year-old American has spent much of 2026 showing she belongs in conversations about the depth of the US women's game. A first US Open Round of 16 appearance — achieved by winning a three-hour night-session match on Arthur Ashe Stadium — makes the case more powerfully than any ranking number.

Her reward is a match against Gauff, who defeated Cristina Bucsa 6-3, 6-4 on Saturday afternoon and is riding a nine-match win streak. Gauff leads their head-to-head 1-0, from a meeting on clay in Rome earlier this year. This will be their first encounter on hard courts.

With both players through, the US Open is guaranteed to have an American woman in the quarterfinals regardless of Monday's result. That's a statement about the depth of a generation.

What's Next

Gauff is the clear favorite based on ranking and their meeting history, but Jovic arrives to the match with more momentum than any player in the women's draw. For Alex Eala, attention turns to the Asian swing of the tour and a chance to build on the ranking and confidence earned at Flushing Meadows.
