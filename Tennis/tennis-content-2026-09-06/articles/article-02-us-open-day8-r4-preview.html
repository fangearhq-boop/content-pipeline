US Open Day 8: Alcaraz vs. Paul Leads a Round of 16 Loaded With Americans and Stakes

By Marcus Cole | September 6, 2026

Sunday at Flushing Meadows brings the US Open Round of 16 — and the draw has delivered. Defending champion Carlos Alcaraz faces Tommy Paul, Frances Tiafoe meets Daniil Medvedev in the night session, and Ben Shelton battles Stefanos Tsitsipas. Four American men. One defending champion. One Sunday.

Somewhere between the draw ceremony and Saturday night, the 2026 US Open became something special for American tennis fans.

Four American men — Carlos Alcaraz excluded, as the Spaniard is a different kind of Flushing institution — have reached the Round of 16 simultaneously. Tommy Paul (No. 20), Frances Tiafoe, Ben Shelton (No. 8), and on the other side of the net from the Americans, the defending champion himself. Sunday is the day where that number either holds or shrinks.

Alcaraz vs. Paul: The Day Match That Defines Day 8

The afternoon match at Arthur Ashe Stadium between No. 2 seed Carlos Alcaraz and No. 20 seed Tommy Paul is the defining contest of Day 8. Alcaraz is the defending champion and has not dropped a set at this tournament, arriving to the Round of 16 having extended his Flushing Meadows career record to 27-3 — the best winning percentage at this venue in the Open Era.

Paul earned his place the hard way. His third-round win over Alexander Bublik went five sets, 6-4, 3-6, 6-7(4), 6-1, 6-3, before he sealed the deal. Paul is a popular figure in New York, capable of generating enormous crowd energy at Arthur Ashe, and a legitimate hardcourt threat on his best days.

The head-to-head, however, is not kind to Paul: Alcaraz leads 6-2 overall and has won their last five meetings. That kind of run represents a pattern, not a trend.

Tiafoe vs. Medvedev: The Night Session Fight

Frances Tiafoe's return from right-hand cyst surgery (approximately August 8) has been one of the quiet stories of this tournament. His third-round win over No. 22 seed Vacherot came in straight sets, and now he faces former US Open champion Daniil Medvedev in the night session at Arthur Ashe.

Medvedev, the 2021 US Open champion, is a different proposition than Vacherot: precise, relentless from the baseline, and experienced at managing the unique atmosphere of night matches at Flushing. Tiafoe's crowd support will be enormous. Whether it's enough is the question that makes this match worth watching.

Shelton vs. Tsitsipas: A Career First for the Greek

No. 8 seed Ben Shelton faces Stefanos Tsitsipas in what is technically Tsitsipas's first US Open Round of 16 appearance. Tsitsipas, a two-time Grand Slam finalist, made his way through the draw with a notable upset of No. 10 seed Fils in the first round and has been building momentum since.

Shelton is one of the tournament's most powerful servers — his 147 mph ace against Shapovalov in the third round has been replaying across social media since Saturday. Against Tsitsipas, that serve will be the first weapon to deploy.

Women's R4: Sabalenka Opens Against Townsend

On the women's side, Aryna Sabalenka opens Day 8 play against Taylor Townsend, a World No. 96 who walked into the pre-match press conference and told reporters she was "pumped" for the occasion. Sabalenka has won 16 consecutive matches at Flushing Meadows and has not dropped a set at this year's US Open. She is bidding to become the first woman to win three consecutive US Open titles since Serena Williams in 2012, 2013, and 2014.

Townsend has nothing to lose and everything to gain. That's what makes the early afternoon match worth tracking before the men's marquee begins.

What's Next

ESPN and ABC carry Day 8 coverage from 11 AM ET, with primetime beginning at 7 PM ET. The order of play: Sabalenka vs. Townsend, followed by Paul vs. Alcaraz at Arthur Ashe. The night session features Medvedev vs. Tiafoe and, at Louis Armstrong, Shelton vs. Tsitsipas. By Sunday night, the US Open quarterfinal picture will be significantly clearer — and so will the story of this tournament.
