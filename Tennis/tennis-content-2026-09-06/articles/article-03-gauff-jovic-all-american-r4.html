Gauff-Jovic All-American R4 Is Set — One American Woman Is Guaranteed a US Open Quarterfinal

By Ryan Calloway | September 6, 2026

Coco Gauff's clinical 6-3, 6-4 win over Cristina Bucsa and Iva Jovic's three-hour night-session victory over Alex Eala have set up an all-American women's Round of 16 on Monday — guaranteeing the US a woman in the quarterfinals for the first time in recent memory.

By the time the Saturday night session at Arthur Ashe Stadium ended, a unique situation had arrived at the 2026 US Open: two American women are meeting in the Round of 16, which means an American woman is going to the quarterfinals no matter what.

Coco Gauff dispatched Cristina Bucsa 6-3, 6-4 in the afternoon to extend her win streak to nine matches and reach the fourth round for the fifth consecutive year. Hours later, Iva Jovic defeated Alex Eala 7-5, 3-6, 7-5 in a three-hour night-session battle to earn her first US Open Round of 16 spot.

They will meet Monday in the night session at Arthur Ashe.

Gauff's Methodical Build

Coco Gauff has not looked brilliant at this US Open — she has looked controlled, which may be the more dangerous version of the defending champion. Against Bucsa, she won 6-3, 6-4 in a match that rarely produced extended rallies. Gauff served well, moved Bucsa around the baseline, and closed efficiently.

Her nine-match win streak coming into the Round of 16 is not a coincidence. The 22-year-old has spent 2026 becoming a more complete player, particularly at net — a facet of her game that was the subject of criticism in previous years. Whatever she worked on during the offseason has been visible in New York.

Jovic's Arrival

If Gauff has been the expected American story, Jovic has been the unexpected one. The 21-year-old walked into the 2026 US Open as the No. 14 seed, but seeds are numbers — what Jovic demonstrated in the night session against Eala was character.

Trailing 4-2 in the third set, Jovic found something and won five of the last six games to close out the match 7-5, 3-6, 7-5. It is her third career win over Eala, moving the head-to-head to 3-0, and it sends her into the largest match of her young career.

Against Gauff, Jovic will be the underdog on paper. In practice, she arrives at Monday's match off a genuine performance — the kind that builds confidence faster than anything else in sport.

The Head-to-Head and What It Means

Gauff and Jovic have met once before: at Roland Garros warm-up event in Rome earlier this year, on clay, where Gauff won 5-7, 7-5, 6-2. The clay surface, the way both players recover and slide — none of that translates directly to the hard courts of Flushing Meadows.

This will be the first time they've played on hard court. Jovic's serve, which was a weapon against Eala on Saturday night, will be more potent here than it was in Rome. Gauff's groundstrokes will be more consistent. The match is genuinely open, and with a home crowd ready to cheer for whoever is playing better, Monday at Arthur Ashe has all the ingredients of a memorable night.

What's Next

Coco Gauff meets Iva Jovic in the US Open Round of 16 on Monday, September 7, at Arthur Ashe Stadium in the night session. The match is the headline women's contest of Day 9. Gauff is the No. 4 seed, the defending champion, and leads H2H 1-0. Jovic is the No. 14 seed, in her first career US Open R4 appearance, and carrying the momentum of a three-hour victory. The winner reaches the US Open quarterfinals. At least one American will be there regardless. The better question is: which one?
