Swiatek Wins Career-First All-Tiebreak Match Over Bouzkova, Sets Zheng Qinwen Showdown in R4

By Elena Voss | September 6, 2026

Iga Swiatek defeated No. 25 seed Marie Bouzkova 7-6(3), 7-6(3) on Saturday — the first time in her career she won a match by winning both tiebreaks — to advance to the US Open Round of 16 for the sixth consecutive year. She will face Zheng Qinwen, who produced a stunning comeback against Madison Keys.

Iga Swiatek had never, in her entire professional career, won a tennis match by winning both tiebreaks.

Saturday at the US Open, she did. Against No. 25 seed Marie Bouzkova, Swiatek grinded through a 7-6(3), 7-6(3) result in 2 hours and 27 minutes — a tighter contest than the world No. 3 likely expected — to reach the US Open fourth round for the sixth consecutive year.

The result is a win. The performance was not vintage Swiatek, which matters heading into a fourth-round matchup that is less straightforward than a ranking comparison might suggest.

Swiatek's Tightest Match of the Tournament

Bouzkova is ranked No. 25 in the world, but she plays considerably bigger than that number implies on a hardcourt surface. She pushed Swiatek into two tiebreaks and was competitive in both, dropping the first 7-3 and the second 7-3 but making Swiatek work for every point.

The fact that Swiatek found herself in two tiebreaks without her dominant ball-striking was arguably more telling than the final score. When Swiatek is operating at peak level, tiebreaks are rare. At Flushing Meadows in 2026, something has been slightly off — manageable, but not settled.

The US Open R4 does not offer easier opponents. It offers better ones.

Zheng Qinwen: The Wild Card Arrives

Zheng Qinwen, who had defeated Madison Keys on Saturday in conditions that should have ended her tournament, will be waiting in the fourth round. Against the No. 22 seed Keys, Zheng trailed and faced a match point, then rallied from 5-0 down in the third set to win. It is exactly the kind of performance that tells you a player's belief is at its highest.

Against Swiatek, Zheng carries significant historical context: she is 1-7 in their head-to-head overall, but her lone win came at the 2024 Paris Olympics — a match that Swiatek has not forgotten. When Zheng is playing at her best and Swiatek is playing at less than hers, the previous history is not irrelevant.

The match is not the following day. It is Tuesday, giving Swiatek time to reset. The smart bet remains the Pole, but smart bets and Grand Slam fourth rounds are not always compatible.

The Streak Context

Swiatek reaching the US Open R4 for the sixth straight year is a consistency metric that doesn't get enough attention in the annual conversation about the WTA's best players. Six consecutive appearances at this stage of a single major requires avoiding injury, maintaining form through a full season, and navigating increasingly dangerous draws. That she has done it without winning the US Open yet — she has been the best player in the world for several years — is the one unfinished piece of her record.

This could be the year it changes. Or Zheng Qinwen, coming off a match point comeback, could have something to say about that.

What's Next

Iga Swiatek faces Zheng Qinwen in the US Open Round of 16 on Tuesday, September 8, at Flushing Meadows. Swiatek leads the head-to-head 7-1, with Zheng's only win coming at the 2024 Paris Olympics. A win for Swiatek would put her into the quarterfinals and within three matches of the US Open title she has yet to win. A loss would be the upset of the tournament — and given what Zheng did to Keys on Saturday, it is not impossible.
