Rybakina Crushes Fernandez to Win Ningbo Title — First as World No. 1

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | October 25, 2026

Elena Rybakina arrived at the 2026 Ningbo Open as the world No. 1 and the defending champion. She left it the same way, having issued a 76-minute reminder that she is the best player in women's tennis right now.

The Kazakhstani superstar defeated Leylah Fernandez 6-2, 6-2 on Sunday to claim her 15th career title and her first since ascending to the top of the rankings. It was also Rybakina's second consecutive Ningbo crown, bringing her all-time record at the tournament to a perfect 8-0.

Dominant From the First Game

There was never a moment in the final that suggested anything other than a Rybakina victory. She broke Fernandez early in the first set and never looked back, winning games in bunches with the kind of clean, powerful tennis that has made her all but unplayable on hardcourts this season.

Fernandez, to her credit, had no reason to hang her head. The Canadian had enjoyed a remarkable run through the Ningbo draw — an unexpected journey that few saw coming when the week began. But in the final, she ran into a different caliber of opponent entirely.

Fernandez Rockets Up the Rankings

Even in defeat, Fernandez's week was transformative. Her run to the final lifts her from No. 30 to No. 23 in the world rankings — a significant jump that opens doors for the 2027 season. She had not made a WTA 500 final in some time, and this result re-establishes her as a genuine threat on this surface.

Fernandez has shown in the past that she performs best on big occasions — her 2021 US Open run remains one of the most memorable in recent Grand Slam history. Getting back to a final at this level is exactly the kind of momentum she needs heading into the offseason.

Rybakina's Season in Context

For Rybakina, this was the sixth final of her 2026 season and her fourth title. The fact that she has now claimed her first title as world No. 1 — the first time she has held that ranking — adds another chapter to a remarkable few years for the Wimbledon 2022 champion.

She enters the WTA Finals at Indian Wells (November 8–15) as the top seed, the defending Ningbo champion, and carrying the form of a player in complete control of her game. Aryna Sabalenka, who lost to Rybakina in last year's WTA Finals final, will almost certainly be her biggest challenge.

What's Next

Rybakina's focus now turns fully to the WTA Finals at the Indian Wells Tennis Garden — an event making its debut in California after years in Riyadh. The round-robin format means no match can be taken lightly, and the full field of eight — including Sabalenka, Coco Gauff, Mirra Andreeva, and Karolina Muchova — ensures that the best players in the world will be at their best.

For Fernandez, the Ningbo result is a springboard. She finishes the season ranked No. 23 and will look to build on this momentum when the 2027 swing begins in January.
