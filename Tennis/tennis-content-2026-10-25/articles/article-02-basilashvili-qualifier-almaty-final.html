Qualifier Basilashvili Stuns Tsitsipas, Earns Almaty Final vs. Defending Champion Medvedev

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | October 25, 2026

The 2026 Almaty Open has produced the kind of story that makes tennis fans remember why they love the sport. Nikoloz Basilashvili — a qualifier ranked No. 128 in the world — has reached the final of the ATP 250 event after defeating No. 7 seed Stefanos Tsitsipas 6-4, 6-7(5), 6-3 in a two-hour, 15-minute semifinal battle.

In Sunday's final, Basilashvili will face top seed and defending champion Daniil Medvedev, who reached the final after Alexander Bublik retired early in their semifinal.

Coming Through the Qualifying Draw

Before any main-draw match was played, Basilashvili had already been working. He came through qualifying to earn his spot in the Almaty field — a path that requires winning multiple matches before the official draw even begins. That he went on to beat a seeded player, then another, and finally the seventh seed in the tournament makes the achievement all the more extraordinary.

It is Basilashvili's first ATP Tour final in more than four years. The Georgian was once ranked inside the top 10 in the world, and at his peak he was one of the most dangerous ball-strikers on the ATP Tour. Life at No. 128 is a long way from those heights — but this week in Almaty has been a reminder of what he is still capable of.

Tsitsipas Stunned in Three Sets

Tsitsipas entered Saturday's semifinal as the heavy favorite. The Greek entered the Almaty draw as the seventh seed and had been in solid form through the week. But Basilashvili took the first set convincingly, fell behind in the tiebreak of the second, then came back to win the third set and the match 6-4, 6-7(5), 6-3.

It was an upset that confirmed Basilashvili's form this week was no fluke. And it sets up the kind of final that the ATP's smaller events thrive on — a living legend of the game coming through qualifying to challenge the defending champion.

Medvedev Arrives as Defending Champion

Medvedev's path to the final was less dramatic but no less comfortable. Bublik's retirement in the semifinal handed the top seed an easier route to Sunday, and the Russian arrives as the defending champion having won the Almaty title in 2025 — defeating Corentin Moutet in that final.

The head-to-head between the two finalists stands at 1-0 in favor of Medvedev, and on paper the Russian is the clear favorite. But Basilashvili has already proven this week that he is not here just to participate.

What's Next

The Almaty final takes place Sunday at 5:30 PM local time. For Basilashvili, a title would be one of the most remarkable comeback stories in recent ATP history. For Medvedev, it would be back-to-back Almaty titles and another step toward securing his spot among the contenders in the final Race to Turin standings.

Either way, Almaty 2026 has already given tennis fans something worth remembering. The qualifier from Georgia against the champion from Russia — that story writes itself.
