Medvedev Faces Bublik in Almaty SF as Qualifier Basilashvili Stuns Mannarino

By Elena Voss | October 24, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

The quarterfinal round at the Almaty Open produced exactly the kind of drama an ATP 250 event needs to stand out, and now the draw has delivered two highly watchable semifinals for Saturday's action in Kazakhstan.

Daniil Medvedev, the defending champion and top seed, remained on cruise control, defeating Mikhail Atmane 6-4, 6-4 to set up a semifinal showdown against Alexander Bublik. The Russian's path to the final looks straightforward on paper — he leads Bublik in their head-to-head record 7-1, with Bublik's lone win coming on the grass in Halle — but Bublik himself arrived in the semis by surviving one of the tougher quarterfinal battles of the week. The third-seeded Kazakhstani held off fifth seed Karen Khachanov 4-6, 7-6(5), 7-6(5), saving the match in back-to-back tiebreaks to reach his country's home-tournament semifinal.

The week's biggest story may be in the other half of the draw. Nikoloz Basilashvili, who entered Almaty as a qualifier, turned in a statement performance Thursday, dismantling seventh seed Adrian Mannarino 6-3, 6-1. Basilashvili will now face Stefanos Tsitsipas, who had no trouble with James Duckworth in their quarterfinal, winning 6-3, 6-4. The pair are well-acquainted — Tsitsipas leads their head-to-head 3-1 — but a Georgian qualifier in the Almaty semifinals is not a result anyone predicted at the start of the week.

Medvedev's Title Defense and the ATP Race Context

For Medvedev, the Almaty campaign carries stakes that extend well beyond a title defense. The winner of the tournament earns 250 ATP Race to Turin points — modest in isolation, but consequential before a week in which both Vienna and Basel begin, offering 500 points each to their respective champions. Medvedev currently sits among the group of players still chasing one of the five remaining ATP Finals spots. A semifinal win and final title in Kazakhstan would provide meaningful cushion entering the indoor European double-header.

Bublik, meanwhile, has his own Race motivations. The crowd in Almaty has cheered the home Kazakhstan favorite enthusiastically all week, and a finals appearance would be a significant career result on home soil.

What's Next

Both semifinals take place Saturday, October 24. The Almaty Open final is Sunday, October 25. Medvedev and Bublik face off with the defending champion carrying the better recent form and a dominant head-to-head record. In the other half, Tsitsipas will need to handle the qualifier heat from Basilashvili. The week closes with Vienna and Basel both opening their first rounds Sunday — meaning the race for Turin points never actually stops.
