Kartal Shocks World No. 6 Svitolina to Reach First WTA 1000 Semifinal

By Marcus Cole

October 16, 2026 — Wuhan, China

Sonay Kartal walked into the Wuhan Open as a qualifier ranked No. 170 in the world. On Friday, she walked out of the quarterfinals as one of the best stories in women's tennis this fall.

The 24-year-old Briton defeated World No. 6 Elina Svitolina 6-2, 2-6, 6-4 to reach the Wuhan Open semifinals — the first WTA 1000 semifinal of her career. She had never defeated a Top 10 player before this match. She chose a big one to break the streak.

A Qualifier Who Came to Play

Kartal's path through the Wuhan draw has been a study in precision and fearlessness. She entered the tournament without a seed, without the fanfare reserved for established names, and without the luxury of a favorable draw position. What she had was a game that could neutralize hard-hitters and a mindset that did not blink at big occasions.

Against Svitolina, she showed both qualities. The opening set was dominant — 6-2, a statement that the Ukrainian had not brought her best. Svitolina steadied in the second, winning 6-2 in the mirror image of the opening, and setting up a decisive third.

In the third, Kartal was the better player when it counted. She held serve with authority and found her openings against a Svitolina who, while composed, could not match the Briton's level at critical moments. The 6-4 third set was a commanding close to a match that Kartal thoroughly deserved to win.

What It Means for Svitolina

For Svitolina, the loss carries significant consequences beyond the scoreboard. The Wuhan Open is the final WTA 1000 event of the 2026 season, and her elimination in the quarterfinals is a serious blow to her hopes of qualifying for the WTA Finals in Riyadh. A deeper run was essential; a quarterfinal exit does not provide the points she needed.

Svitolina has had a complicated 2026. She returned from injury earlier this year with genuine momentum and climbed back to a ranking that reflected her quality. But converting that form into deep runs at the biggest events has proven difficult, and this week in Wuhan was meant to be a chance to change that narrative.

A qualifier who had never beaten a Top 10 player was not the opponent she expected to end her season on a WTA 1000 stage.

The Road to the Semifinal

Kartal's run in Wuhan deserves full recognition. She has navigated a draw that included established players and done so with a game that is built for hard courts. Her serve has been reliable, her baseline play disciplined, and her ability to close out sets from the front a consistent theme.

In the semifinals, she faces Jelena Ostapenko — the hard-hitting Latvian who defeated Diana Shnaider in Friday's other quarterfinal. Ostapenko is explosive and dangerous, capable of dismantling any opponent on her best day. But Kartal has answered every challenge so far, and Svitolina was the biggest one she has faced.

For British tennis, this is a significant moment. Kartal's run at Wuhan is evidence that the talent exists to compete at the highest level of the game. Whether she can take one further step on Saturday is the next question.

What's Next

Sonay Kartal faces Jelena Ostapenko in the Wuhan Open semifinals. The Wuhan final is scheduled for Sunday, October 18. Win or lose on Saturday, Kartal's week has already been a career-defining achievement — her first WTA 1000 semifinal, sealed against one of the top players in the world.
