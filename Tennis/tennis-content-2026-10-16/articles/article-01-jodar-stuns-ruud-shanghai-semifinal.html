Jodar Stuns Ruud to Reach First ATP Masters 1000 Semifinal at Shanghai

By Ryan Calloway

October 16, 2026 — Shanghai, China

Rafael Jodar has spent the better part of this week losing the first set and winning the match. On Friday, he did it again — this time against Casper Ruud — and with it, secured the biggest result of his career.

The 12th-seeded Spaniard defeated Ruud 4-6, 6-4, 6-3 in the Shanghai Masters quarterfinals to reach his first ATP Masters 1000 semifinal. It was a characteristic performance from Jodar: composed in the face of adversity, relentless in the clutch, and somehow always there when it mattered most.

The Pattern That Defines a Run

Jodar's tournament in Shanghai has followed a remarkably consistent template. Against Botic van de Zandschulp, he lost the first set. Against Jiri Lehecka, he dropped the opener again. Against Ruud, it happened a third time. And in all three cases, Jodar found a way through.

There is something about the pattern that speaks to his mental makeup. Lesser players panic when things go wrong in the first set. Jodar appears to recalibrate, identify what is not working, and adjust. The second and third sets against Ruud were notably sharper than his opening — more aggressive on his forehand, more decisive at the net.

Ruud, ranked around the 24th spot in the world, arrived in the quarterfinals off a semifinal run in recent weeks and is a reliable performer at this level. Jodar's victory over him was not a fluke. It was earned through superior tennis over the final two sets of a competitive quarterfinal.

ATP Finals Hopes Come Alive

The ATP Finals in Turin is the season-ending goal for any player on tour, and Jodar's Shanghai run has moved his prospects significantly. A semifinal at a Masters 1000 carries substantial ranking points, and the combination of this result and his earlier performances in the fall swing means he now sits firmly in contention for one of the eight available spots.

Before this week, Jodar's qualification bid was largely a long shot. The Nitto ATP Finals rewards consistency over an entire year, and Jodar's results earlier in the season did not put him in the conversation. Shanghai has changed that. A final, should he reach it, would make his qualification near-certain. Even a semifinal exit gives him a strong case.

The draw has been kind in the bracket, but Jodar has also needed to be good — and he has been. He has beaten a qualifier, a top-15 seed, and now Ruud in four matches. There are no soft victories on the Shanghai draw.

The Semifinal Awaits

Jodar's semifinal opponent will be the winner of the Daniil Medvedev vs. Tommy Paul quarterfinal, which was also played Friday. Medvedev is the fifth seed, a former Shanghai champion, and one of the most dangerous players on hard courts in the world. Paul has been exceptional in 2026, reaching the quarterfinals at every ATP Masters 1000 hard-court event of the year.

Either opponent presents a genuine test. But Jodar has shown throughout this week that he does not wilt under pressure — he responds to it.

The other semifinal, confirmed earlier this week, is Alexander Zverev against Alejandro Davidovich Fokina. The world No. 1 is the tournament favorite and has not dropped a set in Shanghai. The final is scheduled for Sunday, October 18.

What's Next

Rafael Jodar faces the winner of Medvedev vs. Paul in the Shanghai Masters semifinals on Saturday, October 17. If he advances to the final, he will meet either Alexander Zverev or Alejandro Davidovich Fokina. The Shanghai Masters final is Sunday, October 18.

Win or lose on Saturday, Jodar's week in Shanghai is one he will not forget. He came in as a 12th seed with modest expectations, dropped first sets, and kept winning. That is the mark of a player who belongs at this level — and who may yet belong in Turin.
