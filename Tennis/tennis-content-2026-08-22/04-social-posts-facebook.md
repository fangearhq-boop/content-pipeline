### STORY 1: Sinner Withdraws From 2026 US Open

#### Long-Form Post

The news tennis fans dreaded has arrived. Jannik Sinner has officially withdrawn from the 2026 US Open with a right knee injury — and for the first time in his career, he won't play in a Grand Slam he entered.

It's a brutal ending to what had been a historic season. Sinner went 44-3 this year, won six titles, and claimed the first five Masters 1000 events of the year at Indian Wells, Miami, Monte-Carlo, Madrid, and Rome. He was, statistically, the most dominant player in the world.

Then the knee started talking. He withdrew from Montreal. He pulled out of Cincinnati. And now, the US Open is off the table too. He says he's "very sad and disappointed." The target is the Asian hard-court swing in October — Beijing and Shanghai.

What does this mean for Flushing Meadows? The men's draw just lost its No. 1 seed. Carlos Alcaraz — who is returning after his own four-month wrist injury layoff — is now the defending champion and the highest-ranked player in the field. He's also extremely rusty, with no matches since April 14.

The US Open has never needed its draw ceremony more than it does right now.

### STORY 2: Nakashima Stuns Fritz; All-American Cincinnati SF

#### Long-Form Post

Brandon Nakashima just did something nobody saw coming. 🎾

The American 27th seed walked onto the Cincinnati hard courts on Friday and beat No. 6 seed Taylor Fritz — the man who had beaten him four of the five times they'd played — 7-6(9), 4-6, 6-3.

The match turned in a single tiebreak. Fritz was playing some of his best tennis in the first set — 14 aces, just three points lost behind his first serve. And yet Nakashima saved it, fought through an extraordinary 32-shot rally at 4-4, and edged the tiebreak 11-9.

That sequence changed everything. Fritz couldn't recover, and Nakashima saw it out in three sets.

Now Nakashima has the Tour-leading 22 hard-court wins this season. Back-to-back Masters 1000 semifinals — both at Montreal and Cincinnati — the first time in his career he's strung those results together.

And on Saturday? He plays Frances Tiafoe. The first all-American men's Cincinnati semifinal since Andy Roddick and Mardy Fish in 2010.

American tennis is having a summer.

### STORY 3: Tiafoe Beats Musetti

#### Long-Form Post

Cincinnati has always been Frances Tiafoe's stage. On Friday, he reminded everyone why.

Tiafoe beat Lorenzo Musetti 7-6(2), 7-5 to advance to the Cincinnati semifinals — his first Masters 1000 SF in two years. The last one? Also Cincinnati, in 2024.

He dominated the first-set tiebreak 7-2 after Musetti had led 3-2 in the set. When Musetti broke back at 5-5 in the second, the crowd held its breath — Tiafoe broke straight back, served it out, and triggered what was described as the loudest roar of the week in Mason, Ohio.

This is a remarkable turnaround for a player who retired in Montreal just weeks ago with a hand injury. Tiafoe came back to Cincinnati and reached the final four of a Masters 1000 event.

On Saturday, he meets Brandon Nakashima. An all-American semifinal. One of them goes to a Masters 1000 final.

The US Open is 8 days away. The form couldn't be better timed.

### STORY 4: WTA Cincinnati Semifinals

#### Long-Form Post

Saturday's WTA Cincinnati semifinals have everything. 🎾

Match one: Iga Swiatek — on a 10-match winning streak — takes on Jessica Pegula, who fought back from a set down to beat Anisimova in the quarters. Swiatek leads their head-to-head 7-5. The last time they played, at the Italian Open in May, Swiatek won 6-1, 6-2. Pegula is going to need to find something different.

Match two is where the drama runs even deeper. Coco Gauff — the defending US Open champion — faces Sara Bejlek, the 20-year-old Czech who stunned Aryna Sabalenka two days ago in one of the week's biggest upsets. Bejlek's first WTA 1000 semifinal. Gauff's chance to remind the field who the reigning champion is.

Sitting just offstage: Elena Rybakina, who retired against Swiatek on Thursday with a left ankle injury while trailing 4-1. Her US Open status is now uncertain. If she can't go, the women's draw in New York loses one of its strongest contenders.

Cincinnati is delivering. The US Open is one week away.

### STORY 5: US Open 2026 Preview

#### Long-Form Post

The 2026 US Open starts August 30 — and it's already the most wide-open Grand Slam in recent memory.

With Jannik Sinner now withdrawn with a knee injury, the No. 1 seed is gone. Carlos Alcaraz — returning from a four-month wrist injury layoff with zero warm-up matches — arrives as both the defending champion and the highest-ranked player who will actually be in Flushing Meadows. He hasn't played a competitive match since April 14.

The prize money is historic: $108 million total, up 21% from last year, the largest in tennis history. Players have more to play for than ever — at a tournament where the field is more unpredictable than it's been in years.

Fan Week begins this Sunday, August 23. The draw ceremony is Thursday, August 27. Seedings will be based on the Monday, August 24 rankings. The main draw starts Sunday, August 30. Women's final: Saturday, September 12. Men's final: Sunday, September 13.

Fourteen players have already withdrawn from the field. Rybakina's ankle is the latest fitness concern.

The only certainty? This tournament will surprise us.
