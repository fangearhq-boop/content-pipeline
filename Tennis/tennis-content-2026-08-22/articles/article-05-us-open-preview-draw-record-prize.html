US Open 2026 Preview: No Sinner, Record Prize Money, and the Most Open Draw in Years

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | August 22, 2026

The 2026 US Open begins August 30, and almost everything about this year's edition feels different. The world's No. 1 player has withdrawn. The defending champion is returning from a four-month injury layoff. The prize money is the highest in tennis history. And the draw — not yet released — could shake out in almost any configuration imaginable.

US Open Fan Week begins Saturday, August 23, when qualifying rounds start and fans can watch practice sessions at the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center in Flushing, Queens. The main draw ceremony takes place Thursday, August 27. The first matches are Sunday, August 30.

Record Prize Money

The 2026 US Open will distribute $108 million in total prize money — a 21% increase from 2025 and the largest prize fund in the history of any tennis tournament. Players across the men's and women's draws will compete for a share that rewards performance at every level of the draw, from qualifiers to champions.

The increase reflects a tournament that has continued to grow its commercial footprint and is willing to invest back into the players. For those who make deep runs at Flushing Meadows, the financial reward is greater than it has ever been.

Alcaraz: Defending Champion, Rusty Return

Carlos Alcaraz won the 2025 US Open and is back in 2026 as the defending champion. He is also the only top-two player who will actually compete, following Jannik Sinner's withdrawal on Friday due to a right knee injury.

That is where the straightforward part of the Alcaraz narrative ends. He has not played a competitive match since April 14 at the Barcelona Open, when a right wrist injury — tenosynovitis — forced him to stop. He missed Roland Garros. He missed Wimbledon. He is arriving at a Grand Slam without any warm-up events, betting that four months of preparation work will compensate for the absence of match sharpness.

His team has made clear they took a cautious approach to the recovery, prioritizing long-term fitness over a rushed return. That patience may pay dividends — or it may show in the early rounds against opponents who have been competing all summer. The US Open will answer that question quickly.

A Wide-Open Men's Draw

Without Sinner, the men's competition opens up substantially. Alexander Zverev, who has been one of the most consistent players on the hard courts this year, is a realistic title contender. Frances Tiafoe and Brandon Nakashima, both coming out of Cincinnati with strong runs, have momentum heading into New York. Flavio Cobolli and Arthur Fils, who are contesting their own Cincinnati semifinal on Saturday, will arrive with form behind them regardless of who wins.

Seedings will be finalized after the August 24 rankings are published and officially used as the seeding basis on draw day. Fourteen players have already withdrawn from the field, reshaping the bracket before it has even been constructed.

Women's Field: Health Questions and High Stakes

The WTA field is led by Aryna Sabalenka, the defending women's champion, who was upset by Sara Bejlek in Cincinnati's quarterfinals this week. Elena Rybakina is a concern after retiring with a left ankle injury during her Cincinnati semifinal against Swiatek. Her US Open status remains unclear, and with eight days until the main draw begins, there is limited time to assess and resolve the issue.

Iga Swiatek, fresh off a 10-match winning streak and still alive in Cincinnati, is in the best form she's shown on the North American hard court swing. Coco Gauff, the defending US Open women's champion, is also deep in Cincinnati. The draw ceremony will tell us whether their paths to a New York final are clear or converge early.

What's Next

US Open Fan Week starts Saturday, August 23. The draw ceremony is Thursday, August 27, with seedings based on the August 24 rankings. The main draw begins Sunday, August 30. Women's final: Saturday, September 12. Men's final: Sunday, September 13. All matches take place at the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center in Flushing, Queens.
