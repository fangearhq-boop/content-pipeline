Jannik Sinner Withdraws From the 2026 US Open With Knee Injury

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | August 22, 2026

Jannik Sinner will not play the 2026 US Open. The world's best tennis player has officially withdrawn with a right knee injury — his first-ever Grand Slam withdrawal — leaving Carlos Alcaraz as the only top-two player who will step onto the courts at Flushing Meadows next week.

Sinner said he was "very sad and disappointed." Given the season he'd had before the knee gave out, those words land harder than most injury statements do.

A Historic Season Cut Short

Through the end of his competitive matches in 2026, Sinner held a 44-3 record and had won six titles. Most strikingly, he claimed the first five Masters 1000 events of the year in succession — Indian Wells, Miami, Monte-Carlo, Madrid, and Rome. No player in the Open Era had started a season with five consecutive Masters 1000 titles.

The knee started causing problems during the grass court season. He withdrew from Montreal and from Cincinnati — both times citing the same right knee tendon inflammation — before the US Open withdrawal made the situation official. No surgery is required, but the timeline for recovery was long enough that competing at a Grand Slam was not an option.

His doctors and team have targeted the Asian hard-court swing in October — the Beijing and Shanghai tournaments — as the next competitive window. For Sinner fans, that means a two-month wait.

The Third Grand Slam in a Row

There is a larger narrative building around the top of men's tennis. This is the third consecutive Grand Slam where either Sinner or Carlos Alcaraz — the two players who have dominated the sport — has been absent due to injury.

Alcaraz missed Roland Garros and Wimbledon with a right wrist injury. Now Sinner misses the US Open. The two best players in the world keep rotating through the injury list, and the sport's biggest events keep taking place without one of them present.

The difference this time is that Alcaraz has confirmed his return. He will defend the US Open title he won in 2025 — coming off a four-month layoff and with no warm-up matches. Sinner's withdrawal makes Alcaraz the clear headline name in the men's draw by default. Whether he has the match sharpness to back it up is another question entirely.

What the Draw Looks Like Now

With Sinner's 13,450 ranking points sitting idle, the No. 1 seed spot is formally his but practically meaningless — he will not take the court. The seedings will be released after the draw ceremony on August 27, based on the August 24 rankings.

The men's draw is legitimately wide open. Alcaraz, returning after a four-month absence, inherits the role of favourite by default. Players like Alexander Zverev, Frances Tiafoe, Brandon Nakashima, Flavio Cobolli, and Arthur Fils — all of whom have had strong North American summer runs — now see paths that would have been harder to imagine with a fit Sinner present.

Fourteen players have already withdrawn from the 2026 US Open field. The draw ceremony on August 27 will be the most anticipated in years.

What's Next

Sinner begins rehabilitation in Europe with an eye toward Beijing and Shanghai in October. For the US Open, the draw ceremony takes place Thursday, August 27, with seedings based on the Monday, August 24 rankings. The main draw begins Sunday, August 30 at the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center in Flushing, Queens. US Open Fan Week starts Saturday, August 23.
