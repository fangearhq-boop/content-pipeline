WTA Cincinnati Semifinals: Swiatek Faces Pegula; Gauff Meets Cinderella Bejlek

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | August 22, 2026

Saturday's WTA Cincinnati semifinals are built around two of the best storylines the week has produced: a Iga Swiatek winning streak that has reached 10 matches, and a 20-year-old Czech player whose run to the final four has already included the biggest upset of the tournament.

Neither semifinal has a clear favourite written in stone. That is exactly why the WTA half of Cincinnati deserves as much attention as the men's bracket this weekend.

Swiatek vs. Pegula: Streak vs. Determination

Iga Swiatek enters Saturday's first match on a 10-match winning streak, having advanced to this point when Elena Rybakina retired in their quarterfinal with a left ankle injury while trailing 4-1 in the opening set. The retirement was frustrating for Rybakina fans but did nothing to diminish what Swiatek has built this week.

She faces Jessica Pegula, who fought through a two-set deficit and a medical timeout to beat Amanda Anisimova 6-4, 2-6, 7-6(4) in Thursday's quarterfinal. The final set alone lasted over an hour. Pegula has shown the kind of grinding resilience that can upset a streaking player if the conditions are right.

History, though, favors Swiatek. She leads their head-to-head 7-5, and the last time these two played — at the Italian Open in May — she won 6-1, 6-2. Pegula will need to find a different solution than she did in Rome if she wants to reach the final. On a hard court, with her own strong baseline game and the home American crowd behind her, she has enough to make this competitive.

Gauff vs. Bejlek: The Defending Champion vs. the Week's Biggest Story

Coco Gauff enters as the defending US Open champion, and her performance in Cincinnati this week has reminded the field that she is very much in form heading to Flushing Meadows. She has moved through the draw efficiently and arrives in the semifinal looking like the player many expect to defend her Grand Slam title.

Standing opposite her is Sara Bejlek. The 20-year-old Czech, ranked No. 35 in the world, already had the story of the tournament in hand before Saturday: she stunned Aryna Sabalenka 7-6(7), 6-4 in Thursday's quarterfinal, coming back from 5-1 down in the first-set tiebreak to win six consecutive points and complete the upset. It was Bejlek's first career victory over a top-10 opponent, and her first WTA 1000 quarterfinal.

Now she is in the semifinals. Gauff is a higher-ranked opponent than Sabalenka on form this week, but upsets on this scale rarely arrive in isolation. Bejlek will not be overawed. The question is whether she can repeat the energy and precision that dismantled the world's best player two days ago.

Rybakina's Ankle — The US Open Subplot

The week's injury story that extends beyond Cincinnati: Elena Rybakina's left ankle retirement raises genuine questions about her readiness for the US Open. She had been a strong title contender in Cincinnati before the ankle forced her off the court while trailing 4-1 against Swiatek. No official update has been provided about the severity, but with the main draw beginning in New York on August 30, her team will be working hard to assess and treat the problem before it becomes a Grand Slam absence.

What's Next

Both WTA Cincinnati semifinals take place on Saturday, August 22. The final is scheduled for Sunday, August 23. The US Open draw ceremony is Thursday, August 27, with seedings based on Monday's August 24 rankings. The main draw begins Sunday, August 30 at the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center.
