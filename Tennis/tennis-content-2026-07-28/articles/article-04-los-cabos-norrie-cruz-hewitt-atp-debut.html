Dad Watching Courtside: Cruz Hewitt Makes ATP Debut in Los Cabos as Norrie Breaks Drought

By Elena Voss | July 28, 2026

Los Cabos, Mexico

The Mifel Tennis Open by Telcel Oppo opened its 10th anniversary edition in Los Cabos, Mexico on Monday, running concurrently with the DC Open and delivering a pair of stories that would have been at home in either tournament. One was a veteran breaking a losing streak. The other was a teenager stepping into a moment most players never reach at 17.

Cruz Hewitt's ATP Debut

Cruz Hewitt, 17 years old and the son of two-time Grand Slam champion Lleyton Hewitt, reached the Los Cabos main draw through qualifying. It was not a straightforward path — he benefited from two retirements in qualifying rounds, including one against American Zachary Svajda — but he got there, and he earned his spot legitimately under the rules of the tour.

Lleyton Hewitt was in the stands watching. The image of a father — one of the great fighters Australian tennis has produced — watching his son step onto an ATP court for the first time carries a weight that results cannot measure.

The results themselves were admirable. Cruz faced Marcos Giron, a seasoned tour professional, and lost 6-3, 7-6(2). The second set lasted into a tiebreak. At 17, making a main draw debut against a ranked opponent, winning a set in a tiebreak is not a small thing. It is the kind of result that says the next few years will be worth watching.

Norrie Ends the Drought

The cleaner result of the day came from Cameron Norrie. The British fifth seed had not won a match since April — a six-match losing streak across multiple events — and walked into Los Cabos with something to prove. He proved it emphatically, defeating Aleksandar Kovacevic 6-4, 6-1 in 1 hour and 22 minutes. The Kovacevic match was the kind of performance that makes Norrie difficult to evaluate: when his game clicks, it genuinely clicks, and Monday it clicked from the first game.

Top seed Jiri Lehecka leads a field that also includes Luciano Darderi (2), Francisco Cerundolo (3), Karen Khachanov (4), Corentin Moutet (6), and Denis Shapovalov (8), who is here defending the title he won in 2025. Shapovalov has confirmed his participation despite a rib fracture suffered at Queen's Club and a shoulder injury that saw him retire from his Wimbledon first-round match against Pablo Carreno Busta. His presence is a testament to persistence, even if the results in his first match are uncertain at time of writing.

The Tournament

Los Cabos is an ATP 250 event on hard courts, giving players outside the top tier a chance to accumulate ranking points and form ahead of the Canadian Open and Cincinnati. The 10th anniversary edition carries particular significance for a tournament that has grown in stature since its debut. Top-4 seeds receive byes into the second round; the rest play through from Monday.

What's Next

Cameron Norrie advances to the second round and faces a seeded player next. Cruz Hewitt's tournament is over, but his career has taken an important step forward. The Los Cabos draw continues through August 1. The Canadian Open draw is July 31, with men's Montreal play beginning August 4.
