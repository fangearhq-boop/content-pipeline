Revenge on Home Soil: Atmane Upsets Tiafoe 4-6, 6-3, 6-4 to Open DC Open

By Elena Voss | July 28, 2026

Washington, D.C.

Frances Tiafoe walked onto the court at William H.G. FitzGerald Tennis Center on Monday as the tournament's most anticipated American presence — the sixth seed, the hometown favorite, the crowd's rallying point. By the time Terence Atmane shook his hand at the net, Tiafoe was done for the week, beaten 4-6, 6-3, 6-4 in a result that sent ripples through the Mubadala Citi DC Open bracket from the opening day.

Atmane, 24, had entered the match 0-3 against Tiafoe in 2026, including a loss in the Wimbledon first round just weeks earlier. That record told one story. His game on Monday told another. The Frenchman played with controlled aggression, pressing Tiafoe's backhand relentlessly until the errors accumulated — 25 unforced errors in total, 15 from the backhand wing. Tiafoe, for his part, offered a blunt self-assessment: "I just felt off."

The match unfolded in the familiar three-act pattern of an upset. Tiafoe won the first set comfortably, looking settled and assertive. Atmane recalibrated in the second, found his level, and began exploiting the backhand side. By the third set, the momentum had fully shifted. Tiafoe's error count climbed, Atmane's confidence grew, and the result — first DC Open opening-round loss for Tiafoe since 2021 — was sealed.

The Rest of Monday's Results

The upset was the story of Day 1, but the rest of the top of the draw held. Taylor Fritz, the third seed, handled Zizou Bergs efficiently — 6-3, 6-4 — without the Belgian ever threatening. Lorenzo Musetti was even more dominant, winning the first set 6-0 and leading 3-1 in the second when Matteo Arnaldi retired. Kei Nishikori, competing in his 40s and still capable on hard courts, outlasted Marton Fucsovics 6-7(3), 6-3, 6-4 in a three-set contest.

The draw continues to work itself out, and the top half still has plenty of firepower. Alejandro Tabilo advanced in three sets on Monday as well. But the absence of Tiafoe — the would-be crowd magnet — changes the energy of the week.

Tuesday's Marquee Matchup

Monday's shocking result immediately shifts attention to Tuesday, where the defending champion steps onto the court for the first time. Alex de Minaur, seeded No. 1 and the 2025 DC Open title-holder, opens against Stefanos Tsitsipas, who received a wildcard into the draw. The ATP has billed it as the must-watch clash of Round 1 — a top seed versus a former world No. 3 with nothing to lose on a wildcard entry.

Also on the Tuesday schedule: Ben Shelton (2), Arthur Fils (8), Jakub Mensik (7), and Learner Tien (5) all have first-round matches. The bracket is quickly sorting itself, and Day 2 promises to reveal whether the top seeds can hold the line or whether Monday's upset was a sign of things to come.

What's Next

Atmane advances into the second round of the DC Open and will face a seeded player. For Tiafoe, the tournament is over. He remains a crowd favorite here and carries local support into future editions, but Monday's result is a setback coming into the North American hard court swing. The DC Open continues through August 2. The Canadian Open in Montreal begins August 4, with draws on July 31.
