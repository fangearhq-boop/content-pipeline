Canadian Open Seedings Set: Sabalenka Leads Loaded Women's Draw; Zverev Tops Depleted Men's Field

By Marcus Cole | July 28, 2026

Montreal / Toronto

The draw ceremony for the National Bank Open is three days away, scheduled for July 31, and the seedings are in place on both sides. The contrast between the two draws is striking: one of the strongest women's fields in Canadian Open history, and a men's bracket that Forbes has already described as "an absolute disaster" following a triple headline withdrawal.

WTA Toronto — A Deep and Complete Field

The Women's National Bank Open in Toronto (Aug 1–11) has confirmed 72 of the WTA's top 75 players. Aryna Sabalenka is the top seed, followed by Elena Rybakina, Jessica Pegula, Coco Gauff, and Mirra Andreeva. Iga Swiatek is seventh.

The most notable name in the seedings is No. 6: Linda Noskova. The 21-year-old Czech won Wimbledon three weeks ago — defeating Karolina Muchova 6-2, 5-7, 6-3 in the final — to claim her first Grand Slam title and become the youngest Wimbledon women's champion in 15 years. Noskova arrives in Toronto as a new Slam champion, seeded sixth in one of the strongest Masters 1000 fields of the year. The path she takes through the bracket will be one of the week's primary narratives.

Amanda Anisimova (8) and Elina Svitolina (9) round out the early seedings. The depth below the top 10 is equally impressive, with 72 of the world's best confirmed in the draw. This is the kind of field the Canadian Open has built toward for years.

ATP Montreal — Wide Open After Triple Withdrawal

The men's draw in Montreal (Aug 4–13) tells a different story. Jannik Sinner, who defended his Wimbledon title three weeks ago with a 6-7(9), 7-6(2), 6-3, 6-4 win over Alexander Zverev in the final, withdrew citing health reasons. Novak Djokovic, who reached the Wimbledon semifinal before falling to Sinner, also pulled out. Carlos Alcaraz, who has not played since suffering a wrist injury at the Barcelona Open on April 14, confirmed his absence as well — he is targeting Cincinnati and the US Open.

The three players who won the last seven Grand Slam singles titles will not be in Montreal.

Into the gap step Alexander Zverev, the Wimbledon runner-up who has risen to world No. 2, as the top seed. Felix Auger-Aliassime is seeded second — a homecoming appearance for the Montrealer who will have the capacity crowd behind him from the first point. Other seeds include Ben Shelton (defending), Daniil Medvedev, Casper Ruud, Jiri Lehecka, Flavio Cobolli, and Alexander Bublik.

The tournament has maintained that 71 of the ATP's top 72 players (Alcaraz the lone exception) will compete. In terms of sheer quantity, the field is intact. In terms of star power at the very top, the absences are significant. That also means the draw is genuinely open in a way it would not have been with Sinner, Djokovic, and Alcaraz present.

What's Next

The draw ceremony takes place July 31. First-round play for both events begins August 2. Women's Toronto runs August 1–11; men's Montreal runs August 4–13. Both are at the Masters 1000 level, making them among the most significant tournaments on the calendar outside the four Slams. The DC Open and Los Cabos (both underway this week) serve as warm-up events for players entering the Canadian Open.
