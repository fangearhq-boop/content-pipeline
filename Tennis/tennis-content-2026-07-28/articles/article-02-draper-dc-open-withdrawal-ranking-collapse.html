No. 4 to No. 147: Jack Draper Withdraws from DC Open as Injury Wipes Out 2026

By Marcus Cole | July 28, 2026

Washington, D.C.

Jack Draper came to Washington with a wildcard and what must have felt like a narrowing window. The left arm bone bruising that has haunted him since before the Australian Open was not done with him. Before he had played a single point in the main draw of the Mubadala Citi DC Open, Draper withdrew, citing increased soreness. Mackenzie McDonald stepped in as lucky loser.

The withdrawal is the latest in a 2026 season that has been written almost entirely in absences. Australian Open: missed. Roland-Garros: missed. Wimbledon: missed. DC Open: withdrew. The British No. 1 who reached a career-high ranking of No. 4 in 2025 now sits at No. 147, a number that would have seemed unthinkable when he arrived on Tour as one of the sport's most anticipated young players.

The Anatomy of a Collapse

Bone bruising is a deceptively persistent injury. Unlike a muscle strain, which responds reliably to rest and rehabilitation timelines, bone bruising can linger, worsen under match intensity, and recur after apparent recovery. Draper's left arm has been through all of that.

He had not played a Grand Slam match in 2026 before Washington week. The wildcard here — a gesture of goodwill from a tournament that wanted its draw strengthened by his name — expired without use. The ranking consequences compound with each missed event: points earned at these tournaments in 2025 expire, the ranking drops further, and the case for automatic entry into future draws weakens.

The toll is significant. From 147, Draper would need wildcards for both the Canadian Open in Montreal and the Cincinnati Masters. His US Open entry is not guaranteed on ranking alone. Each tournament he misses is another opportunity to fall further behind and another delay in the rehabilitation of his position in the game.

The Road Back

Draper, who is 23, has time on his side in ways that rankings do not capture. At No. 4, he was considered a legitimate future Grand Slam contender — powerful, aggressive, with one of the best first serves on Tour and a game that translates across surfaces. None of that has changed in theory. In practice, he needs to get back on court.

He has named Montreal and Cincinnati as targets, contingent on wildcard acceptance, and has confirmed his commitment to the US Open. The ATP Tour has typically been willing to extend wildcards to players returning from verified injury absences. Whether his arm holds up for three weeks of hard court tennis to close the North American swing remains the central question of his season.

His statement that he is "grateful for the struggle" reads as defiance more than acceptance — the kind of framing a player uses when he is not ready to concede that the struggle is not yet over.

What's Next

Draper is targeting Montreal (August 4–13, wildcard needed), Cincinnati (August 10–23, wildcard needed), and the US Open (August 24). If he can play all three and accumulate points, his ranking would recover meaningfully toward automatic acceptance in future events. The DC Open continues through August 2 without him. The Canadian Open draw is July 31.
