Fernandez Opens Title Defense in Dominant Fashion; Eala-Zheng Headlines Tuesday

By Ryan Calloway | July 28, 2026

Washington, D.C.

If Leylah Fernandez had any questions about how her season has positioned her heading into the DC Open, she answered them on Monday. The seventh seed and defending champion defeated Magda Linette 6-1, 6-4 in 1 hour and 19 minutes — clean, purposeful, and entirely in control. The title defense has begun the way every title defense hopes to begin.

Fernandez, 23, has described entering this tournament with "the right mindset and attitude," and the performance matched the words. Linette, a capable veteran who has reached Grand Slam quarterfinals, could not get a foothold in the first set and only marginally more purchase in the second. Fernandez moved well, converted her opportunities, and gave the impression of someone who is comfortable with her game on this surface.

The top four seeds in the women's draw — Jessica Pegula (1, No. 3 world), Elina Svitolina (2), Naomi Osaka (3), and Diana Shnaider (4) — all had first-round byes, meaning they enter competition in the second round. That setup protects the marquee names while allowing the lower seeds and qualifiers to sort themselves out in Round 1. Fernandez was the highest-seeded player in action on Monday and made the most of it.

Tuesday: Eala vs. Zheng Takes Center Stage

If Monday belonged to Fernandez, Tuesday's spotlight moves to Alex Eala. The Philippines' 22-year-old, currently at a career-high ranking of No. 29, faces Qinwen Zheng on Stadium Court in what the WTA has positioned as one of the most anticipated first-round clashes of the week. Eala's rise through the rankings over the past 12 months — driven by consistent results on both clay and grass — has made her one of the names non-tennis fans have come to recognize, and a matchup against Zheng offers a genuine quality test.

The pair had been entered together in the doubles draw, but Eala withdrew from doubles to devote full attention to singles. She offered a graceful explanation: "Venus is someone who deserves a partner who can give their all." It speaks to Eala's respect for Williams while also signaling the seriousness with which she is approaching her singles campaign.

Venus Williams Returns

On Tuesday, Venus Williams also steps onto the DC Open courts for the first time this year, facing Anastasia Potapova. Williams enters with an 11-match losing streak and, at 45, is in a phase of her career where every appearance carries the weight of legacy rather than the expectation of results. Washington crowds have long had a soft spot for Williams, and a first-round win against a quality opponent would be one of the week's better stories.

What's Next

Fernandez advances to the second round and awaits her next opponent. Tuesday's Eala-Zheng match on Stadium Court is the day's main event; Venus-Potapova adds veteran intrigue.
