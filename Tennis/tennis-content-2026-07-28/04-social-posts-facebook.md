### STORY 1: Tiafoe Upset at DC Open

#### Long-Form Post — 8:00 AM ET

Frances Tiafoe walked onto the court at FitzGerald Tennis Center on Monday as the biggest name on the home side — the local favorite, the crowd's reason to show up. By the time it was over, Terence Atmane had beaten him 4-6, 6-3, 6-4, and Tiafoe was left with 25 unforced errors and not much else to say beyond "I just felt off."

Atmane, the 24-year-old Frenchman, had lost to Tiafoe three times already in 2026, including in Wimbledon Round 1. That losing record didn't seem to faze him. He pressed, Tiafoe's backhand cracked — 15 of those 25 errors came off that wing — and the upset was complete. Tiafoe's first opening-round exit in Washington since 2021.

The rest of Monday's results largely held to form: Taylor Fritz dispatched Zizou Bergs 6-3, 6-4; Musetti was even more clinical, winning 6-0 and leading 3-1 when Arnaldi retired; Kei Nishikori outlasted Marton Fucsovics in three sets. But the Tiafoe result is what everyone is talking about.

Tuesday shifts the attention to the top of the draw. Alex de Minaur, seeded No. 1 and the defending champion, opens against Stefanos Tsitsipas in what the ATP has called the marquee Round 1 clash of the week. Ben Shelton, Arthur Fils, Jakub Mensik, and Learner Tien are also on the Day 2 schedule.

Which match are you most looking forward to on Day 2?

### STORY 2: Jack Draper Withdrawal

#### Long-Form Post — 10:30 AM ET

Jack Draper arrived in Washington this week with a wildcard and, presumably, hope that his 2026 season might finally get started. Instead, he's heading home before playing a single match. Left arm bone bruising — the same injury that ended his Australian Open run, cost him Roland-Garros, and kept him out of Wimbledon — flared up again, and he withdrew before his first round.

It's hard to overstate what this means for Draper's ranking. He reached a career-high of No. 4 in 2025. He is now No. 147 in the world. The path back to relevance runs through wildcard requests — he'll need them for Montreal and Cincinnati — and then US Open qualifying or a direct acceptance if his ranking recovers in time. He said he's "grateful for the struggle," which is the kind of thing you say when the alternative is despair.

At 23, there's still a long road ahead. But 2026 has been a year written entirely in absences for a player who many considered the future of British tennis and a genuine Grand Slam contender.

What would a healthy Draper at a major look like right now?

### STORY 3: DC Open WTA

#### Long-Form Post — 12:30 PM ET

The women's draw got underway at the DC Open on Monday, and the defending champion wasted no time making her intentions clear. Leylah Fernandez came out against Magda Linette and didn't give her a foothold in the match — 6-1, 6-4 in 1 hour and 19 minutes. Clean, purposeful tennis from a player who has carried this title since 2025 and seems determined to do so again.

The top four seeds — Jessica Pegula, Elina Svitolina, Naomi Osaka, and Diana Shnaider — all had first-round byes. They'll enter the tournament in Round 2 as the lower seeds fight it out for the right to face them.

Tuesday offers two must-watch matches on Stadium Court. Alex Eala, the Philippines standout who entered the week at a career-high No. 29, faces Qinwen Zheng in what the WTA has highlighted as one of the most anticipated first-round clashes of the week. And Venus Williams steps onto the court against Anastasia Potapova, looking to reverse an 11-match losing streak. Eala pulled out of doubles alongside Williams to focus fully on singles — a decision she explained gracefully, noting that Venus "deserves a partner who can give their all."

Who are you rooting for in the Eala-Zheng match?

### STORY 4: Los Cabos ATP 250

#### Long-Form Post — 3:00 PM ET

While attention in the tennis world is largely on Washington this week, the ATP 250 in Los Cabos, Mexico opened Monday with its own share of compelling storylines — including one that had nothing to do with rankings or seedings.

Cruz Hewitt, 17 years old and son of two-time Grand Slam champion Lleyton Hewitt, made his ATP main draw debut. He got there the hard way: qualifying via two matches, both opponents retiring before the match finished. His father was watching from the stands. In the main draw he faced Marcos Giron and lost 6-3, 7-6(2) — but the debut was made, and the Hewitt name returns to the ATP circuit in a new chapter.

The actual tournament result of note: Cameron Norrie, seeded fifth, defeated Aleksandar Kovacevic 6-4, 6-1 in just 1 hour and 22 minutes, ending a six-match losing streak that stretched back to April. That kind of clinical performance from Norrie — when it goes clean, it really goes clean — suggests he may be finding form again at the right moment of the season.

Denis Shapovalov is also in the field defending the title he won here last year, competing despite a rib injury from Queen's Club and a shoulder issue that saw him retire from his Wimbledon first-round match.

A 17-year-old making his ATP debut in front of his famous father — does that story resonate with you?

### STORY 5: Canadian Open Seedings

#### Long-Form Post — 6:00 PM ET

The draw ceremony for the National Bank Open is set for Friday, July 31, and the seedings are now locked in on both sides of the border. This year's edition arrives with some notably familiar names missing from the men's field — and a women's draw that may be the strongest in Canadian Open history.

In Toronto (WTA 1000, Aug 1–11): Aryna Sabalenka is the top seed, followed by Elena Rybakina. Coco Gauff is fourth. And perhaps most intriguingly, Wimbledon champion Linda Noskova — the 21-year-old Czech who just became the youngest Wimbledon women's champion in 15 years — enters seeded sixth. Seventy-two of the WTA's top 75 players have confirmed entry.

In Montreal (ATP Masters 1000, Aug 4–13): Alexander Zverev, Wimbledon runner-up and now world No. 2, heads the men's draw. Felix Auger-Aliassime is seeded second in his home country. The big story — and Forbes called it "an absolute disaster" — is the combined absence of Jannik Sinner (health, post-Wimbledon defense), Novak Djokovic (injury), and Carlos Alcaraz (wrist since April). The three players who won the last seven Grand Slams will not be in Montreal.

That means Zverev, FAA, and the rest face a genuinely open draw. The Canadian Open has always delivered drama. This year, the wide-open men's bracket may deliver more of it than usual.

Which player are you most excited to watch at the Canadian Open?
