China Open Draw Day: Zverev's No. 1 Opportunity, Rybakina's First Crown as World No. 1 in Beijing

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | September 27, 2026

The draw for the 2026 China Open — the ATP 500 / WTA 1000 event at the National Tennis Center in Beijing — will be released Monday, September 28, ahead of competition beginning September 30. The tournament runs through October 6 for the men and October 11 for the women, and arrives at one of the most interesting moments of the season for both tours.

Zverev's Shot at No. 1

On the men's side, the week's defining narrative belongs to Alexander Zverev. Jannik Sinner — who has dominated the ATP Tour in 2026, amassing five Masters 1000 titles and completing the Career Golden Masters — officially withdrew from Beijing on September 25 with a knee injury, citing the same issue that has kept him out since Wimbledon. With Sinner unable to accumulate points in Beijing, Zverev's lead in the ATP Race to the Finals transforms into something larger: a genuine opportunity to close the gap in the overall ATP rankings and potentially claim the No. 1 ranking for the first time in his career.

Zverev has never held the World No. 1 ranking. He arrives in Beijing as the holder of the Roland Garros and US Open titles from 2026 — the most decorated season of his career — and carrying the Race lead. A strong Beijing performance, combined with Sinner's absence, creates a scenario where the top spot in the rankings becomes theoretically reachable heading into the autumn indoor swing.

The ATP Field

Confirmed entries in the ATP 500 draw include Zverev, Novak Djokovic, Alex de Minaur, Daniil Medvedev, Felix Auger-Aliassime, and Flavio Cobolli. Djokovic's return to Beijing is notable on its own terms: the Serbian great has a 29-0 all-time record at the China Open across six titles and is making his first appearance in the event after an 11-year absence, following his first-round exit at the 2026 US Open to Navone. He arrives ranked No. 12, with a stated goal of returning to the top 10 before Paris in November.

The ATP 500 prize stands at $4,089,385. The draw will be seeded Monday once the full entry list is confirmed after the Laver Cup and Asian swing's Saturday-Sunday results.

Rybakina as WTA World No. 1

The WTA 1000 draw carries its own headliner. Elena Rybakina enters Beijing as the WTA World No. 1, having secured the ranking on September 14 by defeating Zheng Qinwen in the quarterfinals of the US Open. This will be Rybakina's first time taking the court as the No. 1 seed at a WTA 1000 event, and Beijing has historically been a major stage for marquee women's tennis.

Defending WTA champion Amanda Anisimova is in the draw, along with Aryna Sabalenka, Coco Gauff, Iga Świątek, and Mirra Andreeva. Three seeds have reportedly withdrawn from the WTA draw ahead of the release, creating potential bracket openings. The WTA 1000 prize pool stands at $9,415,725 — one of the year's most valuable tournaments for Race points.

What's Next

The China Open draw releases Monday, September 28. Competition begins September 30, with the ATP 500 final scheduled for October 6 and the WTA 1000 final for October 11. The event marks the last major hard-court event before the start of the indoor season, with players then turning to the 1000-level events in Shanghai and Wuhan before the year-end Finals in Turin and Riyadh.
