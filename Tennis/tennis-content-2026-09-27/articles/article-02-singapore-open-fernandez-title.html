Leylah Fernandez Wins Singapore Open, Claims 6th WTA Title With 7-5, 6-0 Defeat of Gibson

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | September 27, 2026

Leylah Fernandez defeated Talia Gibson 7-5, 6-0 in the final of the WTA 500 Singapore Open on Sunday to win the sixth title of her career, completing a week in which she took down the tournament's No. 1 seed en route to the trophy.

Gibson, the Australian qualifier who had reached her first WTA final after Tatiana Prozorova's controversial concussion protocol withdrawal in Saturday's semifinals, made the opening set competitive and converted a set point that briefly threatened to take the match in a different direction. Fernandez held her nerve, won the first set, and then closed out the match by winning nine consecutive games.

A Week of Dominant Tennis

The title was earned across a run of performances that highlighted what makes Fernandez dangerous when she's fully committed to a week. In the quarterfinals, she defeated No. 1 seed Mirra Andreeva — the Roland Garros champion and WTA No. 5 — in what was the result of the tournament. The semifinal against Maja Chwalinska required a comeback from a set down before Fernandez found her best form. She carried that form into Sunday's final without losing a set across the full back half of the draw.

The win is Fernandez's first title since the Japan Open in October of the prior year, and it arrives at a moment when the WTA's Asian swing is building toward its major events in Beijing and Wuhan. She now moves higher in the WTA Race to the Finals standings heading into those tournaments.

Gibson's Run Through a Disrupted Draw

For Talia Gibson, the Singapore Open will be remembered as the best result of her WTA career. The Australian qualifier's path to the final ran through Maria Sakkari in the quarterfinals — a win that would have qualified as the match of the week under any circumstances. Her advancement to the final came via walkover after Saturday's semifinals were disrupted by Prozorova's withdrawal under the WTA's concussion protocol, following an incident during doubles play on Friday.

Prozorova publicly disputed the decision, saying she did not believe she had suffered a concussion and that her balance test results reflected fatigue rather than a head injury. The WTA defended the protocol's application. The controversy remained a backdrop to Sunday's final — but Gibson earned her place through match wins and deserved her spot regardless of how the bracket developed.

What's Next

The WTA Asian swing continues in Beijing, where the WTA 1000 China Open begins September 30 at the National Tennis Center. Elena Rybakina enters as the new World No. 1 — having secured the ranking at the US Open — and will be the No. 1 seed in the WTA draw for the first time. The draw is set for release Monday, September 28, with competition beginning the following Tuesday. Fernandez's Singapore title puts her in strong form for the upcoming stretch of high-points events.
