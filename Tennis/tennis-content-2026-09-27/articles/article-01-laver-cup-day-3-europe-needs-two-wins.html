Laver Cup Day 3: Europe Leads 7-5, World Needs Perfect Sunday to Retain Title

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | September 27, 2026

Team Europe enters Sunday's decisive Day 3 session at The O2 in London holding a 7-5 lead over Team World, having survived the most dramatic afternoon of the 2026 Laver Cup to hold their advantage after Saturday's four matches were split evenly.

With every Day 3 match worth three points, the arithmetic is straightforward but the pressure is immense: Team Europe needs any two of three wins to reach 13 points and claim the Cup. Team World, defending the title they won 15-9 in San Francisco last year, must win all three matches — a perfect Day 3 — to overtake Europe and retain the trophy.

What Happened on Day 2

Saturday produced four matches and four momentum swings. Learner Tien opened the day session for Team World, defeating Flavio Cobolli 6-3, 2-6, [10-7] in the super-tiebreak to pull World level at 3-3 and inject energy into a previously subdued World camp. Alex de Minaur then delivered the day's most stunning moment — clawing back from two match points down against Alexander Zverev in the super-tiebreak to win [11-9], a sequence that had the entire O2 on its feet. World held a 5-3 lead after two matches.

Europe refused to fold. Carlos Alcaraz took the court against Taylor Fritz and found himself in a near-identical position — two match points down in the super-tiebreak — before winning [13-11] to give Europe 5-5. The evening doubles pairing of Casper Ruud and Zverev then closed out the session 6-4, 7-6(7) over Bublik and Nakashima to restore Europe's lead heading into Sunday.

Day 3 Lineup

The opening match of Sunday's session pits Flavio Cobolli and Jakub Menšík (Europe) against Alex de Minaur and Taylor Fritz (World) in doubles. The opening frame carries significant strategic weight: Europe winning the doubles makes the Cup almost unlosable from a math standpoint, while a World win in doubles gives them a foothold with two singles matches still to play.

The singles slate brings the marquee matchups of the day. Alexander Zverev (Europe) takes on Learner Tien (World) in what will be a rematch of sorts — Tien won Saturday's singles to give World their first point of the day. Then, in the final match that could decide the entire competition, Carlos Alcaraz (Europe) faces Alex de Minaur (World). Alcaraz is 6-0 all-time against the Australian — an all-time record that de Minaur will be desperate to rewrite on one of tennis's biggest stages.

Europe's Historical Edge

Europe has won eight of the nine Laver Cups held since the event's inaugural 2017 edition. The one loss came last year in San Francisco, where a Team World led by Ben Shelton — who has since withdrawn from this year's edition — won 15-9. That result made this year's event particularly meaningful for a European squad that includes Alcaraz and Zverev, the two players who share a World No. 1 and No. 2 ranking in the ATP Race to the Finals.

Team World, captained by Andre Agassi, has spoken publicly about wanting to prove the 2025 victory was not a one-off. De Minaur's comeback win Saturday was exactly the kind of moment that would back up that claim. Whether World can find three more of them on the same day remains the question.

What's Next

Day 3 is underway Sunday at The O2 in London. Following the conclusion of the Laver Cup, the tennis calendar turns immediately to Beijing, where the China Open (ATP 500 / WTA 1000) begins September 30. The draw will be released Monday. Jannik Sinner's absence from the ATP draw opens the door for Zverev to extend his lead in the Race — a compelling subplot that will carry from this weekend's action directly into next week.
