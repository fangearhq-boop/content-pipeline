Davis Cup Finals Field Set: Eight Nations Confirmed for Bologna

By Marcus Cole | September 21, 2026

Davis Cup | Team Tennis

The Davis Cup Finals field is complete. Following the conclusion of the second-round qualifying ties last weekend, all eight nations have been confirmed for the Final 8 in Bologna, Italy, scheduled for November 24-29, 2026.

Italy earns automatic entry as host nation and will be joined by seven qualifiers: Spain, Germany, Great Britain, Canada, South Korea, Austria and Czechia. The USA and France, both considered genuine contenders entering the weekend, were among the nations who failed to advance.

How the Qualifiers Unfolded

The most dramatic result came in Prague, where Czechia defeated the United States 3-2 in a tie that went the full distance. With the series level at 2-2, Jakub Mensik proved the hero for the Czech Republic, defeating Ben Shelton 5-7, 6-4, 6-3 in the deciding rubber. Jiri Lehecka also played a pivotal role, beating Learner Tien 6-3, 7-5 in Saturday's reverse singles to put Czechia in front. The result leaves Shelton with a 0-3 career record in Davis Cup singles.

Canada made it look straightforward in Quebec City. Felix Auger-Aliassime sealed a 3-1 win over France with a gutsy 4-6, 7-6(5), 6-4 victory over Arthur Rinderknech, serving up 17 aces in the process. Canada had won the opening two rubbers via Gabriel Diallo and the now-dominant Auger-Aliassime, and the result was never really in doubt after the first day.

Great Britain booked their Finals spot at the Copper Box Arena in London, beating Ecuador 4-0 without the injured Cameron Norrie. Arthur Fery — fresh from reaching the Winston-Salem final — and Toby Samuel contributed significant wins, with Henry Patten and Neal Skupski handling the doubles.

Spain, Germany, South Korea and Austria completed the qualifying eight without the same level of drama. Spain beat Chile 4-0, Germany defeated Croatia 3-1, South Korea edged Argentina 3-2, and Austria overcame Japan 3-2.

Notable Absences

The USA's elimination was the headline result of the weekend. A team that included world No. 4 Ben Shelton and No. 16 Learner Tien lost to a Czech side led by world No. 17 Mensik and Lehecka — a result that underlines the depth now present in European tennis. Tommy Paul's pre-tournament withdrawal with an elbow injury, which also saw him miss Laver Cup, did not help the American cause.

France's exit was less surprising given their opponents. Canada, led by the in-form Auger-Aliassime, proved too strong in Quebec City. Arthur Fils and Ugo Humbert were unable to mount a meaningful challenge.

The Final 8 Takes Shape

With the eight nations confirmed, the draw for the Final 8 round-robin and knockout rounds will be released ahead of the November event. The format features two groups of four, with the top two from each group advancing to the semifinals.

The presence of Spain — led by a formidable singles and doubles roster — makes them an immediate favorite. Germany, boosted by their Croatian win and the continued development of a strong young team, will also be among the contenders. Canada, riding the form of Auger-Aliassime, and Czechia, who just upset the United States, will both feel they can compete for the title.

Italy, as host nation, will carry the enormous weight of home expectation at the SuperTennis Arena Bologna Fiera.

What's Next

The Davis Cup Finals draw will be released in the coming weeks. The eight nations will then compete in Bologna from November 24-29, with the Final played on the closing day. For Czechia and Canada specifically, momentum is now everything — both nations arrive at the Finals in strong form, having just defeated quality opposition.

Before Bologna, attention in the tennis world turns quickly to this week's Laver Cup in London (September 25-27) and then the China Open in Beijing beginning September 30.
