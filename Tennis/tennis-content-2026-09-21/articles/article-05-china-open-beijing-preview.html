China Open Preview: Sinner Returns, Zverev, Djokovic All Converge in Beijing

By Elena Voss | September 21, 2026

ATP | WTA | China Open

The fall swing begins in earnest when the China Open opens in Beijing on September 30, and the field set for the National Tennis Center already guarantees it will be the most talked-about event of the season's final stretch.

Seven of the ATP top 10 are confirmed for the men's draw. The WTA side features Elena Rybakina in her first major event as world No. 1. And the subplots — Jannik Sinner's return from injury, Novak Djokovic's record-laden comeback, Alexander Zverev's title push — give the tournament more narrative weight than almost any event outside of a Grand Slam.

Sinner: The Defending Champion Returns

The main event on the men's side is Jannik Sinner's return to the tour after months away with a right knee injury. Sinner missed the Canadian Open, Cincinnati and the US Open entirely, yet his points bank has kept him at No. 1 in the world throughout. He arrives in Beijing to defend the 2025 title he won there — the most recent player to win back-to-back China Open titles was Novak Djokovic in 2014-2015.

The question surrounding Sinner is not one of talent but of match readiness. Coming back from an extended injury absence, even with strong training form behind you, the sharpness of competitive matches is difficult to simulate in practice. His first few rounds in Beijing will be watched closely for signs of how quickly he has regained his edge.

Alexander Zverev, entering as the US Open champion and world No. 2, will be the biggest threat to any Sinner run. Zverev's 51-13 season record speaks to his consistency; he is perhaps the most in-form player on the planet right now and will not give the defending champion an easy path.

Djokovic's Beijing Reckoning

There are few better stories in tennis than Novak Djokovic's return to Beijing, a venue where he has an astonishing 29-0 all-time record and six titles to his name. His last appearance there was 11 years ago.

The 39-year-old enters the China Open ranked No. 12 after his US Open first-round exit to Mariano Navone — the first time he has lost a Grand Slam first-round match since the 2006 Australian Open. With the Paris Masters beginning November 2, Djokovic needs points and, perhaps more importantly, match wins to rebuild confidence ahead of the final events of the year. A return to the Beijing court where he is historically unbeatable may be exactly what he needs.

Whether his all-time record in the city actually translates in 2026 is another matter — the field has evolved considerably since his last visit — but the motivation is real and the venue is right.

The Women's Draw

Elena Rybakina arrives in Beijing for her first major event since becoming WTA world No. 1 following her US Open title. The 26-year-old Kazakhstani claimed the ranking by ending Aryna Sabalenka's 99-week run at the top — and now faces the pressure of defending her new status on the sport's biggest stages.

Sabalenka, who fell to No. 2 after the US Open final loss, will be in the same draw and is a clear contender for the title. Their head-to-head has tilted toward Rybakina recently — she has won the last three meetings — but Sabalenka on hard courts in Asia is always a dangerous proposition.

The full WTA draw will run from September 30 through October 11, providing nearly two weeks of premium women's tennis before the tournament transitions to the next phase of the Asian swing.

What's Next

The China Open draw is released on September 28. First-round matches begin September 30. For those following the ATP race, the points available in Beijing are significant — the men's champion earns 500 ranking points, making it a pivotal event for anyone in pursuit of Sinner or battling for a spot at the ATP Finals in Turin. For the women, the China Open is a 1,000-point event that could reshape the WTA Race to the Finals. Watch this space.
