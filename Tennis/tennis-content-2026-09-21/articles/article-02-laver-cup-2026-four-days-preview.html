Laver Cup 2026: Four Days to The O2 — Key Matchups and What to Watch

By Elena Voss | September 21, 2026

Laver Cup | Team Tennis

The Davis Cup qualifying dust has barely settled, and already the tennis world is turning its attention to the next big event: the 2026 Laver Cup, which begins in just four days at The O2 Arena in London.

Running from September 25-27, the ninth edition of the tournament pits Team Europe against Team World in a format that has consistently produced some of the most electric moments in recent men's tennis. This year promises to be no different.

The Teams

Team Europe, captained by Yannick Noah, enters with a roster that reads like a who's who of the current game. Carlos Alcaraz (world No. 3) and Alexander Zverev (No. 2, two-time 2026 Grand Slam champion) lead the charge, supported by Casper Ruud (No. 13), Jakub Mensik (No. 17), Flavio Cobolli (No. 9) and Rafael Jodar (No. 24). Europe has won eight of the nine all-time Laver Cup editions.

Team World, captained by Andre Agassi, is somewhat reshaped from its original announcement. Ben Shelton withdrew to focus on Davis Cup; he was replaced by Brandon Nakashima. Tommy Paul had already stepped aside with an elbow injury in mid-September, replaced by Francisco Cerundolo. The team that remains — Taylor Fritz (No. 10), Alex de Minaur (No. 6), Alexander Bublik (No. 11), Learner Tien (No. 16), Nakashima and Cerundolo — is still formidable, and Team World are the defending champions after their 2025 victory.

Matchups to Watch

The Alcaraz vs Fritz duel is the one every neutral wants to see. Alcaraz brings his relentless athleticism and shot-making; Fritz is perhaps the steadiest of the Team World players and has beaten Alcaraz in recent events. Their head-to-head will be a genuine toss-up.

Zverev versus de Minaur has the hallmarks of a classic. Zverev, energized by his US Open title, faces de Minaur — last year's Laver Cup MVP and one of the most combative competitors in the game. De Minaur will be desperate to prove Team World can defend.

Mensik versus Tien is a subplot the tennis world will be watching with particular interest. The two played each other in the Davis Cup Qualifiers just 48 hours ago — Tien had beaten Mensik in that tie's opening match, before Mensik turned the tables by defeating Shelton in the decisive rubber. Now they come together under Laver Cup conditions, wearing their continental colors. The score is 1-1 in their very recent head-to-head.

Format Reminder

The Laver Cup's scoring system remains one of the most distinctive in tennis. Matches are worth one point on the Friday, two points on the Saturday, and three points on the Sunday. The first team to reach 13 points wins the trophy. If the score reaches 12-12, a match tiebreak determines the outcome.

This progressive format means that Sunday's matches carry enormous weight. A team that falls behind early can still win if it performs on the final day — and conversely, a team that builds a big early lead can see it evaporate quickly. It makes for compelling viewing throughout all three days.

What's Next

Laver Cup 2026 runs September 25-27 at The O2 Arena in London. Sessions begin each afternoon, with broadcast coverage available across major tennis networks. For those following along: the first points go on the board this Friday. Team Europe will be heavily favored based on rankings and history, but Team World have proved they know how to win this event.
