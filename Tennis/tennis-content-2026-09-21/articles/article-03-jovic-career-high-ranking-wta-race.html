Jovic's Career-High No. 13: What the Guadalajara Defense Means for the WTA Race

By Ryan Calloway | September 21, 2026

WTA | Rankings

The Monday rankings release confirmed what Guadalajara had already announced: Iva Jovic is now officially the world No. 13, a career high, after successfully defending her WTA 500 title over the weekend.

Jovic earned 451 ranking points by beating compatriot Peyton Stearns 6-4, 6-2 in an all-American final on Saturday — making her a back-to-back champion at the same tournament, a feat that even the most decorated professionals rarely achieve. But it is the downstream effects of that title, the ranking implications and the WTA Race position, that make the story even more compelling.

The New World No. 13

Reaching No. 13 represents more than a number for Jovic. It places her on the doorstep of the world's top 10, the cohort of players who get the best seeds, the late-night showcourt slots and the first calls from sponsors. At 20 years old, with two WTA titles at the same venue on her resume, she is no longer a rising prospect — she is a genuine top-10 contender.

The 451 ranking points from the Guadalajara title came on top of an already-strong 2026 season. The victories accumulated throughout the year had been building this ranking steadily, but defending a WTA 500 title — and thus collecting the full 451-point haul rather than losing defended points — was a crucial factor in clearing this milestone.

WTA Race to the Finals

Perhaps even more significant for Jovic's immediate future is her position in the WTA Race to the Finals: No. 11. The WTA Finals in Riyadh is contested by the top eight players in the Race at the end of the season, meaning Jovic is currently three spots outside the cutoff.

That said, No. 11 in the Race is a highly competitive position that can shift dramatically with results in October and November. High-value events remain — the China Open in Beijing (Sep 30), the Wuhan Open (Oct 12-18, $4,088,211, 1,000 points) and the BNP Paribas WTA Finals qualifier events all offer the kind of points that can move the Race standings quickly.

For Jovic to qualify, she likely needs a minimum of one more semifinal or better at a WTA 1000 event. That is not a given — the competition at the top of the WTA is as fierce as it has ever been — but it is entirely within her capacity given the form she showed this week.

The Bigger Picture for American Tennis

The all-American final in Guadalajara — Jovic defeating Stearns — was a significant moment for U.S. women's tennis. Two Americans in a WTA 500 final signals genuine depth, not just individual brilliance. Stearns, appearing in her first WTA 500 final, showed she belongs in that tier of competition even if she could not close the deal against Jovic.

Jovic now has two WTA titles, both at the same tournament. That kind of consistency at a specific venue is unusual and suggests a player who has genuinely mastered those conditions. Whether she can translate that mastery to new surfaces and stages will define how far she goes in the next phase of her career.

What's Next

Jovic will turn her attention to the Asian swing, beginning with the China Open in Beijing on September 30. She will be seeded based on her new No. 13 ranking, which should ease her path through the early rounds against lower-ranked opponents. Beyond Beijing, the Wuhan Open offers 1,000 points and could be decisive in her WTA Finals push.

The WTA Finals qualification cutoff date comes at the end of October. Jovic has roughly five weeks to make her case. Based on what she showed in Guadalajara, her candidacy should not be dismissed.
