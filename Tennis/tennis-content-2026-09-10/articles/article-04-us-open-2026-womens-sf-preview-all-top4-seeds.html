All Four Top Seeds Reach US Open Women's Semifinals — First Time Since 1975

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | September 10, 2026

US Open 2026 | Women's Semifinals | Sabalenka | Pegula | Rybakina | Gauff | WTA

The 2026 US Open women's draw has delivered something the sport has not seen at Flushing Meadows in more than 50 years. For the first time since 1975, all four top-seeded players have advanced to the women's singles semifinals. The bracket is set: No. 1 Aryna Sabalenka vs. No. 3 Jessica Pegula, and No. 2 Elena Rybakina vs. No. 4 Coco Gauff. Both matches take place Thursday at Arthur Ashe Stadium.

It is a rare alignment in women's tennis, where upsets and injury withdrawals regularly disrupt draws before the final stretch. This year, the best players made it through. And they arrive at the semifinals with compelling storylines in every direction.

Sabalenka vs. Pegula: A 2024 Final Rematch

Aryna Sabalenka is the two-time defending US Open champion. She won the title in 2024 and 2025, becoming one of the few players in the Open Era to win the tournament in consecutive years. A third straight title would put her alongside a very short list of champions — the last player to win three consecutive US Opens was Serena Williams, who did it from 2012 to 2014.

Her opponent Thursday is Jessica Pegula, the No. 3 seed from the United States, who has reached the last four at Flushing Meadows three years in a row. Pegula beat Emma Navarro on Wednesday to advance, coming back from a first-set deficit in the process. She faced Sabalenka in the 2024 final and lost in straight sets. Sabalenka leads their career head-to-head 9-4.

What Pegula has on her side this year is experience. Three straight semifinals, a 2024 final, and the confidence that comes from having been in these high-pressure situations before. Whether that is enough against Sabalenka — who has now gone 11 consecutive matches at Flushing Meadows without dropping a set before this tournament — remains the central question.

Rybakina vs. Gauff: The New No. 1 vs. the 2023 Champion

Elena Rybakina's quarterfinal win over Zheng Qinwen on Wednesday did more than book her place in the semifinals. It clinched the world No. 1 WTA ranking for the first time in her career, ending Sabalenka's 99-week run at the top. When the new rankings come out Monday, the women's tennis order will look different than it has in nearly two years.

Rybakina faces Coco Gauff, who came through her own dramatic quarterfinal on Wednesday — saving match points in the second set before winning 2-6, 7-6, 6-2 over French Open champion Mirra Andreeva. Gauff won the US Open in 2023 and knows exactly what this stage of the tournament demands.

The two players have met before at this level this year. Rybakina beat Gauff in the 2026 Australian Open final. This is a rematch that the whole tennis world has been circling since the draw came out. The incoming No. 1 against the defending New York champion. Neither player will approach Thursday without full confidence.

What Thursday Looks Like

Arthur Ashe Stadium will host both women's semifinals Thursday, with coverage on CBS and ESPN beginning in the afternoon. The women's final is scheduled for Saturday. Whoever emerges from Thursday's two matches will carry both the weight of the occasion and the momentum of a remarkable two weeks of tennis.

This final four is the best possible outcome for the tournament — four elite players, four distinct storylines, and a bracket that could have produced any of four worthy champions. The 2026 US Open has given women's tennis a semifinal round worth the occasion.

What's Next

Both women's semifinals take place Thursday at Arthur Ashe Stadium. The women's final is Saturday, September 12. On the men's side, the semifinals are set for Friday: Frances Tiafoe vs. Ben Shelton in an all-American matchup, and Alexander Zverev vs. Karen Khachanov. The men's final is Sunday, September 13.
