Rybakina Beats Zheng 3-6, 6-1, 6-4 and Clinches World No. 1 Ranking for First Time

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | September 10, 2026

US Open 2026 | Elena Rybakina | World No. 1 | WTA Rankings | Quarterfinals

Elena Rybakina walked off Arthur Ashe Stadium on Wednesday night not just as a US Open semifinalist, but as the incoming world No. 1. Her 3-6, 6-1, 6-4 victory over Zheng Qinwen secured enough points to push her past Aryna Sabalenka in the WTA rankings. When the new rankings are released Monday, Sabalenka's 99-week reign at the top will be over — and Rybakina will take her place.

It is the first time in her career that Rybakina has reached No. 1. It is also a first for Kazakhstan: no Kazakh player, male or female, has ever topped a Grand Slam tour's world rankings. Rybakina arrived in New York managing questions about her fitness after an ankle injury earlier in the season. She leaves with one of the most significant milestones in women's tennis this year.

A Match That Told the Whole Story

The quarterfinal against Zheng followed a familiar pattern for Rybakina this tournament — a slow start, then a takeover. Zheng, the Olympic champion from China, took the first set 6-3 and looked sharp in the early exchanges. Rybakina's game can sometimes take a set to fully engage, and Wednesday was no different.

The second set was a different story entirely. Rybakina won 6-1, barely a contest. The third set was tighter but the outcome was not seriously in doubt. Rybakina served powerfully, moved efficiently, and closed it out 6-4. She is through to the semifinals and the historical record books.

The End of Sabalenka's 99-Week Run

Sabalenka has been the dominant force in women's tennis for the better part of two years. Her 99 weeks at No. 1 represent one of the longest consecutive stretches at the top of the WTA Tour in recent memory. She has won three Grand Slam titles in that span, including two US Open championships. Losing the No. 1 spot at a tournament where she is still competing for the title is a significant development.

Rybakina's ascent to No. 1 has been gradual but unmistakable. She won the 2026 Australian Open title earlier this year and has been among the most consistent players on the tour throughout the season. Her arrival at the top is not a surprise — but the timing, in the middle of a Grand Slam, underlines just how strong her form has been.

A Historic Milestone for Kazakhstan

The significance of Rybakina's No. 1 ranking extends beyond her own career. Kazakhstan has produced solid tennis players over the years, but none have reached the summit of either the ATP or WTA rankings. Rybakina, who was born in Moscow but has represented Kazakhstan since 2018, has carried the country's flag in tennis to a place it has never been before.

The reaction in Kazakhstan is expected to be significant. The country's tennis federation has steadily invested in the sport, and Rybakina's success — three Grand Slam finals, two titles, and now a No. 1 ranking — represents a return on that investment that few could have anticipated even three years ago.

What's Next

Rybakina will take the court Thursday as the incoming world No. 1 to face Coco Gauff in the women's semifinals at Arthur Ashe Stadium. Gauff defeated Mirra Andreeva on Wednesday after saving match points in a second-set tiebreak. The two players met in the 2026 Australian Open final, where Rybakina won the title.

This is one of the most compelling matchups of the 2026 Grand Slam season. The new No. 1 against the 2023 US Open champion. Whoever wins plays in Saturday's women's final.
