Gauff Saves Match Points, Rallies Past Andreeva 2-6, 7-6, 6-2 to Reach US Open Semifinals

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | September 10, 2026

US Open 2026 | Coco Gauff | Mirra Andreeva | Quarterfinals | WTA

Coco Gauff did not look like a player headed to the US Open semifinals when the match began. Mirra Andreeva won seven of the first nine points on Wednesday at Arthur Ashe Stadium, dominated the first set in just 26 minutes, and had the crowd wondering whether the defending 2023 champion's tournament was about to end. Then Gauff did what she has done all fortnight: she found her level and finished the job.

Gauff defeated Andreeva 2-6, 7-6(7), 6-2 in a quarterfinal that swung completely in the second set. She saved match points in the tiebreak, won a 39-shot rally to take a 5-3 lead, and then held on to close out the breaker 9-7. The third set was never in doubt. Gauff is back in the US Open semifinals.

A First Set to Forget

Andreeva entered the quarterfinal as one of the most dangerous opponents left in the draw. The 19-year-old Russian, who claimed the 2026 French Open title on clay, has spent the last year developing a hard-court game to match her baseline power. She showed all of it in the first set.

Andreeva's serving and groundstroke placement were exceptional from the opening game. She controlled the rallies and Gauff had no answer — losing the set 2-6 in a manner that felt comfortable rather than contested. At that point, Andreeva looked like the player who was going to advance.

The Tiebreak That Changed Everything

The second set was different. Gauff pushed back, the crowd engaged, and neither player was able to hold a service advantage for long. They reached the tiebreak, and that is where the match turned on a single point.

Gauff won a 39-shot exchange to move ahead 5-3. The rally lasted long enough that the crowd had risen before it ended. Andreeva clawed back to 6-6, and then Gauff produced the most important tennis of her day — saving match points and closing the tiebreak 9-7. Whatever Andreeva had brought to the match was now gone. The third set went to Gauff, 6-2.

Gauff's Resilience Defines Her US Open

This victory follows a pattern Gauff has built over her career at Flushing Meadows. She is not always the most dominant player on the scoreboard, but she finds a way. The 2023 title was not built on flawless tennis — it was built on resilience, on the ability to reset when things go wrong, and on a belief that the match is never settled until the last point.

Against Andreeva, those qualities were on full display. Gauff absorbed a first set as poor as any she has played this fortnight, and then turned around and produced her best tennis at the exact moment she needed it.

What's Next

Gauff advances to the US Open women's semifinals and will face Elena Rybakina on Thursday at Arthur Ashe Stadium. Rybakina clinched the world No. 1 WTA ranking with her own quarterfinal victory on Wednesday over Zheng Qinwen. The two players met in this year's Australian Open final, with Rybakina winning that title.

It is a rematch with everything at stake. A US Open final spot, a shot at the title Gauff won three years ago, and for Rybakina, the chance to win a Grand Slam as the world's top-ranked player. The semifinal is one of the most anticipated matches of the 2026 season.
