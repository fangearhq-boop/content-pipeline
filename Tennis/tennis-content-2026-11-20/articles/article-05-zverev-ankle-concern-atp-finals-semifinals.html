Zverev Hurts His Ankle Beating Fils — Can He Go Full Intensity in the Semifinals?

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | November 20, 2026

Alexander Zverev completed Thursday's win over Arthur Fils. He finished the McEnroe Group 3-0, dropped no sets across six matches, and recorded his 21st career ATP Finals victory. The result was everything it needed to be. What happened in the closing stages raised a different question entirely.

Zverev appeared to hurt his right ankle late in the second-set tiebreak against Fils. After closing out the match 8-6 in the tiebreak, he was visibly checking the joint before leaving the court. He did not retire, he did not call for a medical timeout, and he won the match. But the footage was noticed, and the ankle is now part of the story of this tournament.

What Is at Stake

Zverev is not simply a participant at the ATP Finals — he is the year-end No. 1 and, on most assessments, the tournament favourite. He won this event in 2018 and 2021. His group-stage performance this week, three wins and zero sets dropped, has only reinforced that view. His semifinal opponent will be determined on Friday evening when Medvedev and Tiafoe play the Lendl Group decider.

An ankle injury, even a mild one, changes the dynamic. The ATP Finals is played on a fast indoor surface at the Inalpi Arena. Lateral movement, quick direction changes, and explosive footwork are central to competing at this level. If Zverev is managing the ankle rather than playing freely, his level could drop enough to change the balance of a semifinal against an opponent who will be healthy and motivated.

What to Watch Before Saturday

The timeline is short — Thursday evening to Saturday semifinal. Any statement from Zverev's team, any change to his training schedule on Friday, or any medical update from the ATP Finals communications will carry weight. Players routinely manage minor ankle issues across a tournament week, and completing the Fils match was a reasonable sign that the injury is not severe. But there is a difference between finishing a match under adrenaline and backing up 48 hours later in a knockout round.

Zverev has shown before that he can compete through physical difficulty. His resilience is part of what makes him a champion at this event. The question is whether this ankle concern becomes a footnote in a semifinal win or the central chapter of a shock result. The answer arrives on Saturday.

What's Next

Zverev faces the winner of Medvedev-Tiafoe in the ATP Finals semifinals on Saturday at the Inalpi Arena in Turin. His health update before the match will be one of the most closely watched pieces of pre-match information of the week. In the other semifinal, Carlos Alcaraz plays Ben Shelton — a matchup that also takes place Saturday, with Alcaraz carrying an 11-match winning streak and Shelton playing in his first-ever ATP Finals last four.
