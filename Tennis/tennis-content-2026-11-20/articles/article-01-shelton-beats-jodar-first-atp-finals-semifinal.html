Ben Shelton Reaches ATP Finals Semifinals for the First Time — Beats Jodar 7-6(8), 7-5

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | November 20, 2026

Ben Shelton is in the ATP Finals semifinals for the first time, and he earned it on Thursday in a match that required him to grind. He beat Rafael Jodar 7-6(8), 7-5 at the Inalpi Arena in Turin — 14 aces, two hours and seven minutes, and a first-set tiebreak that stretched to 10-8. It was the kind of win that tests character as much as it tests skill, and Shelton passed.

The result puts him in the last four alongside Carlos Alcaraz and Alexander Zverev, with the fourth semifinalist — either Daniil Medvedev or Frances Tiafoe — to be decided on Friday. Shelton finished the McEnroe Group at 2-1. For Jodar, who was playing in his first year at the ATP Finals, the tournament ends without a win at 0-3.

How the Match Unfolded

The first set was decided by a tiebreak that neither player found easy to close. The score reached 10-8 — an extended exchange of points that tested nerve as much as ability. Shelton eventually converted, taking the set and immediately gaining the momentum advantage.

The second set followed a steadier pattern. Shelton's serve — 14 aces for the match — was a consistent weapon that gave him free points and limited Jodar's ability to build pressure on return. Shelton closed it 7-5 and walked off the court having secured his place in the knockout round for the first time.

Jodar, 23, is a player who reached Turin by qualifying in his debut year on the ATP Tour — an achievement that brought its own significance. He pushed Shelton hard in this match and in earlier rounds showed competitive quality. But three group-stage losses is a harsh landing for a first-year participant, and there was no route back for him after the tiebreak.

What It Means for Shelton

Shelton has been to the ATP Finals before, but reaching the last four changes his status at the event. He has been one of the tour's most exciting players since his rise — a big serve, genuine athleticism, and the ability to close out matches with pace. Getting to a semifinal at the season-ending championship is a marker that matters.

His semifinal opponent is Alcaraz — the No. 2 seed, the group winner, and the player with 11 consecutive wins entering this week. Alcaraz beat Tiafoe and Medvedev in the group stage without facing a break point in either match. That is the challenge in front of Shelton on Saturday. He has beaten Alcaraz before in their career meetings, which means the matchup is not simply one-sided — but Alcaraz's current form is as good as it has been all season.

What's Next

Shelton faces Alcaraz in the ATP Finals semifinals on Saturday at the Inalpi Arena in Turin. It will be his first ATP Finals semifinal, and one of the most anticipated matches of the week. Meanwhile, Friday brings the final Lendl Group match — Medvedev versus Tiafoe — which decides the fourth semifinalist. The winner of that match will face Alexander Zverev on Saturday, who completed the McEnroe Group 3-0 on Thursday evening but left the court with concern over his right ankle.
