Swiatek vs. Chwalinska: The First All-Polish Semifinal in WTA 1000 History

By Elena Voss

October 17, 2026 — Wuhan, China

When Iga Swiatek and Maja Chwalinska walked onto the court for their Wuhan Open semifinal on Saturday, they made history before the first ball was struck. No two Polish players had ever met in the semifinal of a WTA 1000 tournament. Until now.

What made the moment even more unusual is that these are not strangers. Swiatek and Chwalinska have known each other for years, trained together coming up through the ranks, and have spent their careers following parallel paths through the women's game. On Saturday, those paths crossed at the highest level either has reached at this tournament.

How Each Got Here

Swiatek's route to the Wuhan semifinal was efficient and authoritative. The world No. 1 defeated Ann Li 6-4, 6-1 in the quarterfinals to reach her 25th WTA 1000 semifinal — a record that no other active player can match. It was also her first-ever Wuhan semifinal, completing what the WTA described as a sweep of reaching at least the semifinals at all 16 WTA 1000 events.

Chwalinska's path was harder but equally impressive. The 2026 French Open runner-up — one of the most significant results of her career — beat Maria Sakkari in a three-set quarterfinal that went to 7-5 in the decider. It was her first-ever WTA 1000 semifinal, and the win over Sakkari underscored that her French Open run was not a one-off.

Both players arrived with reasons to believe they could win. Swiatek's experience and consistency at this level made her the clear favorite. Chwalinska's French Open final form suggested she could compete with anyone on her day.

A Friendship Tested

Their only previous meeting on the WTA Tour came at an ITF event in Stockholm in November 2016, when both were teenagers and their professional careers were barely beginning. Swiatek won that match 6-2, 6-3. Chwalinska has developed considerably since then, and the 2026 French Open final is evidence enough that the gap between them has narrowed.

Both players spoke about the unique nature of their match in pre-match comments. Swiatek acknowledged it would be strange to compete against someone she had known for so long. Chwalinska said she had known Swiatek "forever" — and that the history between them made the match something different from a normal competitive meeting.

That kind of pre-existing relationship can complicate things or clarify them. For players who have competed against each other in practice for years, there are no tactical surprises. Each knows how the other plays. On Saturday, the question was who would execute better when the points mattered most.

The Stakes

The winner of this semifinal faces Sonay Kartal in Sunday's Wuhan Open final — the British qualifier who had already produced one of the biggest shocks of the tournament by beating Jelena Ostapenko in tiebreaks. For Swiatek, a final against Kartal would represent a title opportunity at a tournament she had never previously won. For Chwalinska, it would be a chance to become the first Polish player other than Swiatek to win a WTA 1000 event in recent history.

Either way, Polish tennis has had an extraordinary weekend in Wuhan.

What's Next

The winner of Swiatek vs. Chwalinska plays Sonay Kartal in the Wuhan Open final on Sunday, October 18. The result of Saturday's semifinal will be confirmed in the next update. For either finalist, Sunday represents a chance to close out one of the more historically significant weeks at the Wuhan Open.
