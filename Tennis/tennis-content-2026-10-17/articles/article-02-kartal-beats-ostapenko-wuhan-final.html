Kartal Beats Ostapenko in Consecutive Tiebreaks to Reach Wuhan Open Final

By Marcus Cole

October 17, 2026 — Wuhan, China

Sonay Kartal has done something that seemed impossible when she walked onto the Wuhan Open court as a qualifier ranked No. 170 in the world. She is now in the final.

The British player defeated Jelena Ostapenko 7-6(11), 7-6(5) in the Wuhan Open semifinals on Saturday — a match decided by a single point across two tiebreak sets. When it was over, Kartal had won 88 total points to Ostapenko's 87. Tennis has rarely been thinner.

Two Hours and Twelve Minutes of Everything

Ostapenko, the 2017 French Open champion, is one of the most dangerous players in the draw on any given day. She hits as hard as anyone on the women's tour, takes risks that others avoid, and produces matches that are difficult to predict from one game to the next. On Saturday she had nine aces to Kartal's one.

Kartal survived by competing on every single point. She served 7 double faults — equal to Ostapenko's total — but kept finding ways to stay in tiebreaks that could have gone either way. The first tiebreak required 22 total points before Kartal won it 13-11. She closed the second more comfortably, 7-5, but the margin throughout was always slender.

What this match demonstrated about Kartal is that she belongs in these moments. Six months ago she was recovering from a back injury and uncertain when she would return. On Saturday she outlasted a Grand Slam champion in one of the year's tightest contests.

The Story Behind the Story

Kartal's run in Wuhan began as a footnote and has become one of the season's defining narratives. She returned to competition after a lengthy absence, entered the main draw as a qualifier, and has since beaten No. 6 Elina Svitolina and No. 34 Jelena Ostapenko in succession.

Before this tournament, she had never beaten a Top 10 player at WTA level. She has now beaten two Top 40 players in a row, both in three sets or the equivalent. The mental fortitude required to produce that kind of consecutive result is not something that can be manufactured. It is either present or it is not. In Kartal, it clearly is.

Her ranking — No. 170 entering the tournament — will rise dramatically regardless of the final result. A title would represent the biggest win by a qualifier at WTA 1000 level in recent memory.

The Final Awaits

Kartal's opponent in Sunday's Wuhan final will be either Iga Swiatek or Maja Chwalinska, who contested the second semifinal on Saturday. Swiatek is the world No. 1, and Chwalinska is the 2026 French Open runner-up — both are formidable opponents. But Kartal has shown this week that seedings and rankings are irrelevant to her game plan.

She has already beaten more than anyone expected. Sunday is simply the next match.

What's Next

Sonay Kartal plays the Wuhan Open final on Sunday, October 18, against either Iga Swiatek or Maja Chwalinska. A title would make her the lowest-ranked finalist to win a WTA 1000 event in modern history and complete one of the most unlikely comeback runs of the season.
