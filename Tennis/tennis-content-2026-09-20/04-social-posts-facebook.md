### STORY 1: Jovic Defends Guadalajara Title

**Long-Form [10:30 AM ET]**

Iva Jovic is back-to-back champion in Guadalajara — and she made it look easy.

The 20-year-old American defeated compatriot Peyton Stearns 6-4, 6-2 in the WTA 500 final on Sunday, successfully defending the title she won at this same tournament last year.

The win lifts Jovic to a career-high ranking of world No. 13 and moves her to No. 11 in the WTA Race to the Finals. With the WTA Finals in late October and a run of events still to play, qualification remains very much within reach.

It was a dominant week from Jovic, who dropped just one set throughout the entire tournament. Stearns, unseeded and in her first WTA 500 final, pushed in the first set but Jovic raised her level when it mattered most.

All-American finals have become a theme in women's tennis lately — and Jovic is proving she's the one who comes through when it counts.

### STORY 2: Czechia Stuns USA 3-2

**Long-Form [8:00 AM ET]**

A week after reaching the US Open final, Ben Shelton's form hit a wall in Prague — and the United States has been eliminated from the Davis Cup Final 8 as a result.

Czechia staged an incredible comeback, winning the final two rubbers of the tie to beat the USA 3-2. Jakub Menšík defeated Shelton 5-7, 6-4, 6-3 to level the tie at 2-2, before Jiří Lehečka completed the job with a 6-3, 7-5 victory over Learner Tien in the decisive fifth rubber.

For Shelton, it was a sobering trip to Prague. He lost to Lehečka in the first meeting and then to Menšík in the third — leaving him 0-3 in his Davis Cup career. The American played beautifully at the US Open but has clearly not found his footing in the longer best-of-five team format.

Tien, to his credit, had a brilliant Davis Cup debut — defeating Menšík in the opening rubber — but could not hold off Lehečka when it mattered.

Czechia heads to the Davis Cup Finals in Bologna (November 24-29) with real confidence. The USA, meanwhile, will have to wait another year.

### STORY 3: Canada Clinches Davis Cup Final 8

**Long-Form [12:30 PM ET]**

Félix Auger-Aliassime delivered one of his best performances of the year when it mattered most — and Canada is going to Bologna.

FAA defeated Arthur Rinderknech 4-6, 7-6(5), 6-4 on Sunday in Quebec City to seal Canada's 3-1 victory over France, booking their place at the Davis Cup Finals in Bologna in late November.

The numbers tell the story: 17 aces, just one double fault across the match. FAA served brilliantly under pressure in a two-hour, 21-minute battle, saving the tie when France had a chance to level at 2-2.

Canada entered the weekend as favorites but had to work for every point. FAA had also won on Friday against Quentin Halys to give Canada a 2-0 lead, and Liam Draxl's contribution was equally vital.

It continues a golden era for Canadian tennis. FAA, Draxl, and a deep doubles pair have given Canada consistent Davis Cup presence — and their form heading into Bologna will make them a real contender.

### STORY 4: Great Britain Qualifies for Davis Cup Final 8

**Long-Form [3:00 PM ET]**

Great Britain have punched their ticket to the Davis Cup Finals — and they did it without their No. 1.

With Cameron Norrie sidelined through injury, GB needed their supporting cast to step up against Ecuador at the Copper Box Arena in London. Arthur Fery and Toby Samuel delivered on Day 1 — and the squad closed out the tie on Day 2 to qualify for the Finals in Bologna.

Samuel had beaten Andres Andrade 4-6, 6-4, 6-1 and Fery followed with a clinical 7-5, 6-0 win over Alvaro Guillen Meza to put GB in the driving seat. Needing just one more win from three remaining rubbers, the team finished the job.

Norrie's injury absence was a concern coming into the tie, but this GB group proved they have enough depth to get the job done. Fery, in particular, is emerging as one of British tennis's most reliable performers.

Bologna awaits. The Final 8 in late November will see GB competing against the likes of Canada, Czechia, South Korea, and Austria.

### STORY 5: Laver Cup 2026 — 5 Days Out

**Long-Form [6:00 PM ET]**

Davis Cup weekend is done — and now the countdown to Laver Cup is well and truly on.

Just five days until Team Europe and Team World take the court at The O2 in London for one of tennis's most entertaining spectacles. September 25-27 brings Laver Cup 2026, and the rosters are loaded.

Team Europe has Carlos Alcaraz and Alexander Zverev leading the charge, backed up by Casper Ruud, Jakub Menšík, Flavio Cobolli, and Rafael Jódar. Team World counters with Taylor Fritz and Alex de Minaur as their standard-bearers, plus Alexander Bublik, Learner Tien, Francisco Cerúndolo, and Brandon Nakashima.

Notably, Ben Shelton is OUT after his Davis Cup commitment, with Nakashima stepping in. Tommy Paul also missed out due to elbow injury, replaced by Cerúndolo.

The format: three days, three singles and one doubles per day, with points escalating through the weekend. First to 13 wins.

Captains Yannick Noah (Europe) and Andre Agassi (World) will be trying to inspire their squads. Agassi's team is 0-7 all-time in the Laver Cup — they've never won. That story line alone makes it worth watching.
