Czechia Stuns USA 3-2: Shelton's Davis Cup Woes Deepen

By Ryan Calloway | September 20, 2026

Davis Cup | USA | Czechia

One week after standing on the grandest stage in tennis, Ben Shelton found himself on the wrong side of a Davis Cup upset — and the United States has been eliminated from the Final 8.

Czechia completed a stunning comeback in Prague on Sunday, winning the final two rubbers of their second-round qualifier to defeat the USA 3-2 and book a spot at the Davis Cup Finals in Bologna. Jakub Menšík defeated Shelton 5-7, 6-4, 6-3 in the fourth rubber, before Jiří Lehečka sealed it 6-3, 7-5 against Learner Tien in the decisive fifth match.

It was a painful ending for the Americans, who had led 2-1 in the tie after Tien's brilliant debut win and Shelton's earlier singles appearance — only to watch Czechia roar back with back-to-back victories when it mattered most.

Shelton Struggles Again

The numbers make uncomfortable reading for Shelton. He is now 0-3 in his Davis Cup career, having lost twice in this single tie alone. On Friday, Lehečka had beaten Shelton 6-4, 6-4 in straight sets. Two days later, Menšík did the same — this time in three sets — leaving Shelton winless in team tennis after reaching world No. 4 and a US Open final.

The contrast is stark. In Flushing Meadows, Shelton served bombs and played fearless tennis. In Prague, the big serve was not enough against opponents who had done their homework, and Shelton's movement and return were tested in ways Zverev had not quite managed in the final.

Menšík, only 19, played with tremendous composure. After Shelton won the first set, the Czech teenager did not panic — he raised his level in the second and ran away with the third to level the tie. It was a performance well beyond his years.

Tien's Debut, Then Heartbreak

The story of the tie was not just Shelton. Learner Tien, making his Davis Cup debut on Saturday, was exceptional — defeating Menšík 6-2, 6-4 to give the USA a 2-1 lead. For a brief moment, American hopes were real.

But Lehečka's experience proved the difference in the deciding rubber. The Czech No. 2 defeated Tien 6-3, 7-5 in a controlled performance, ending the tie and ending the USA's Davis Cup campaign for 2026.

Tien, despite the loss in the fifth rubber, leaves Prague with his reputation enhanced. He has a significant Laver Cup commitment ahead — and the tie showed he can compete at the highest level of team tennis.

Czechia Heads to Bologna

For Czechia, qualification for the Finals is hugely significant. A nation with deep Davis Cup history — multiple titles in the team's Czech Republic and Czechoslovakia era — will arrive in Bologna as dark horses with a dangerous all-court game.

The Davis Cup Finals run November 24-29 in Bologna. Czechia joins Canada, South Korea, Austria, and Great Britain among the confirmed qualifiers heading into the Final 8.

What's Next

For Shelton, the Davis Cup chapter closes with more questions than answers about his form away from Grand Slams. His next meaningful competition will be the Asian swing — the China Open in Beijing on September 30 and the Shanghai Masters in October — where he will look to reassert himself on the ATP Tour after a bruising week in Prague.

For the USA, the Davis Cup elimination is a disappointment but not a disaster. Tien showed he belongs at this level, and the team's future, alongside players like Tommy Paul and Sebastian Korda, remains bright.
