Laver Cup 2026: Five Days Out — Full Preview of Europe vs The World at The O2

By Marcus Cole | September 20, 2026

Laver Cup | Preview

Davis Cup weekend is over. The rosters are set. The countdown is real.

In just five days, Team Europe and Team World will take the court at The O2 in London for the 2026 Laver Cup — three days of team tennis, rising stakes, and the kind of electric atmosphere that only this event produces. September 25-27 cannot come quickly enough.

The Teams

Team Europe, captained by Yannick Noah, is headlined by two of the tour's absolute best: Carlos Alcaraz and Alexander Zverev. The two have dominated men's tennis across 2026 — Zverev winning the French Open and US Open — and arrive as heavy favorites.

The full Team Europe roster: Carlos Alcaraz, Alexander Zverev, Casper Ruud, Jakub Menšík, Flavio Cobolli, Rafael Jódar.

Team World, captained by Andre Agassi, is led by Taylor Fritz and Alex de Minaur — two of the most consistent players outside the European core. Fritz has had a strong 2026 season and de Minaur's speed and fighting spirit make him dangerous on any surface.

The full Team World roster: Taylor Fritz, Alex de Minaur, Alexander Bublik, Learner Tien, Francisco Cerúndolo, Brandon Nakashima.

Notable Absences and Replacements

Ben Shelton was originally named for Team World but withdrew due to scheduling conflicts with the Davis Cup tie — leaving the squad before Czechia eliminated the USA. Brandon Nakashima was named as his replacement.

Tommy Paul also withdrew, this time through an elbow injury, with Francisco Cerúndolo stepping in from Argentina.

For Team Europe, Jannik Sinner is absent — the Italian has been managing a knee injury and is targeting the China Open on September 30 for his return to competition. His absence gives Menšík, Cobolli, and Jódar more court time than they might otherwise have expected.

The Intrigue: Tien vs Mensik

One of the more unusual subplots of this Laver Cup involves Learner Tien and Jakub Menšík. Just this weekend, Tien defeated Menšík in the Davis Cup opener in Prague — an excellent straight-sets win for Team USA that briefly gave them a 1-0 lead in the tie. Now, 48 hours later, the two will be teammates at Laver Cup.

Tennis is full of these contradictions, and Laver Cup thrives on them. Opponents on Friday, teammates on Thursday.

Format and Stakes

The Laver Cup format escalates through the weekend: each match is worth one point on Friday, two on Saturday, and three on Sunday. The first team to 13 points wins. If the score reaches 12-12 on Sunday, a doubles tiebreak decides the event.

Team World has never won the Laver Cup in its history. Agassi's side has come close in recent editions, but Europe has always found a way. The pressure of that record — and the quality of this year's Team World roster — makes 2026 feel different. Alcaraz and Zverev give Europe enormous firepower, but Fritz and de Minaur are not going to lie down.

What's Next

Laver Cup runs September 25-27 at The O2 Arena in London. Tickets are sold out, but the event will be broadcast on Sky Sports in the UK and Tennis Channel in the United States. Coverage begins Friday with the afternoon session at 1:00 PM BST.

After Laver Cup, the attention of the tennis world shifts east — the China Open in Beijing opens September 30, followed by the Wuhan Open and Shanghai Masters in October. The final push of the 2026 season is underway.
