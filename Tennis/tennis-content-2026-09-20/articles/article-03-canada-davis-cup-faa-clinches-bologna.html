FAA Aces His Way to Bologna: Canada Beats France 3-1 for Davis Cup Finals Spot

By Marcus Cole | September 20, 2026

Davis Cup | Canada | France

When Félix Auger-Aliassime needed to be the difference-maker, he delivered exactly that — 17 aces, a clutch three-set performance, and a Davis Cup Finals ticket for Canada.

Auger-Aliassime defeated Arthur Rinderknech 4-6, 7-6(5), 6-4 on Sunday at the Videotron Centre in Quebec City to seal Canada's 3-1 victory over France in their second-round Davis Cup qualifier. Canada is heading to the Finals in Bologna, Italy, November 24-29.

It was a match that tested FAA early — Rinderknech took the first set and pushed to a tiebreak in the second — but the Canadian No. 1 served his way out of trouble and carried his team home in two hours and 21 minutes.

17 Aces and One Double Fault

The serving numbers were extraordinary. Auger-Aliassime recorded 17 aces across the match with just one double fault, a combination that left France with almost no way back whenever the match turned to his serve.

In the second-set tiebreak, FAA was clinical under pressure, converting at 7-5 to level the match. From there, he controlled the third set with trademark big serving and consistent groundstrokes, closing out 6-4.

It was a performance that spoke to FAA's evolution as a pressure player. He had entered the tie as Canada's top singles hope and delivered on both days — Friday's win over Quentin Halys (7-6(5), 4-6, 6-3) gave Canada a 2-0 lead, and Sunday's final clinched the series.

Draxl's Contribution

Liam Draxl played an equally important supporting role. The young Canadian had contributed to Canada's early lead on Day 1, helping establish the cushion that FAA eventually converted into qualification. Canada's depth in this tie was a genuine strength — not a one-man show.

Canada's Davis Cup Journey

Canada has become one of Davis Cup's most consistent participants at the top level. FAA, now a senior leader of the team at 26, has grown into the role his talent always promised. The team's composure in a hostile tie — even on home soil, the pressure of representing a nation in a best-of-five format demands a particular mental makeup — was evident throughout the weekend.

The Finals in Bologna will feature the best eight nations remaining in the competition. Canada will arrive in Italy with confidence and a genuine belief they can compete for the title.

What's Next

For FAA and Canada, attention now turns to the ATP Tour's fall swing. The China Open and Shanghai Masters will be key opportunities for rankings points before the Davis Cup Finals demand attention again in November. Canada's draw in Bologna — which will be determined by the Finals format — will shape their path to a potential title run.

For France, a Davis Cup exit is a setback. The Coupe Davis is a source of national pride, and a 3-1 loss on the road in Canada will sting. The French squad will have a year to regroup before the next qualifying cycle.
