Jovic Defends Guadalajara Title, Reaches Career-High No. 13

By Elena Voss | September 20, 2026

WTA | Guadalajara

Iva Jovic is a back-to-back Guadalajara champion — and at 20 years old, she is making one of tennis's most compelling cases for a place among the WTA elite.

The American defeated compatriot Peyton Stearns 6-4, 6-2 on Sunday to successfully defend her WTA 500 title at the Guadalajara Open presentado por Santander. It was a dominant display in the all-American final, with Jovic dropping just one set across the entire week.

The victory lifted Jovic to a career-high world ranking of No. 13 and moved her to No. 11 in the WTA Race to the Finals — keeping her qualification hopes very much alive heading into the final stretch of the season.

A Statement Win

Stearns, appearing in her first WTA 500 final, made the opening set competitive but could not match Jovic's consistency from the baseline. The 22-year-old from Texas took the match to Jovic early, but Jovic raised her level at critical moments and closed out the first set 6-4.

The second set was one-way traffic. Jovic controlled the tempo, moved Stearns around the court, and wrapped up the match 6-2 to complete a thoroughly convincing defense of her title.

It was the first career meeting between the two Americans, and Jovic gave a clear verdict on who holds the upper hand right now.

Ranking Milestone

For Jovic, the victory represents far more than another title. Reaching a career-high of No. 13 in the world is the culmination of a breakthrough year that has included multiple deep runs at the game's biggest events.

No. 11 in the WTA Race also means the WTA Finals — limited to the top eight in the Race — remains a realistic target. With several high-value tournaments remaining in October and November, Jovic will need a strong final stretch, but her form suggests she is capable of making the push.

Serena Williams' former coach Rick Macci, commenting on the win, predicted Jovic could challenge the top 10 before year's end — a bold call, but one not without basis given her recent results.

All-American Storyline

The all-American WTA 500 final was a notable moment for U.S. tennis. Both Jovic and Stearns represent a new generation of American women pushing into the top tier of the tour, and their meeting in Guadalajara was a sign of growing domestic depth.

Jovic has now won both of her WTA titles at the same venue. It is a remarkable coincidence — or perhaps evidence of a genuine affinity for the clay-hard courts of Guadalajara. Either way, she owns this tournament in a way that few players own any particular event.

What's Next

Jovic will have little time to celebrate. The Asian swing looms — the China Open in Beijing begins September 30, followed by the Wuhan Open — with the WTA Finals in late October providing the ultimate carrot at the end of the season. If she can maintain this form through October, a finals debut in her age-20 season would be one of the stories of the tennis year.

For Stearns, a first WTA 500 final is a significant step. She will return to the draw stronger for the experience.
