Bergs Stuns Alcaraz 7-5, 7-6(4) in Shanghai — Biggest Win of Belgian's Career

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | October 13, 2026

Zizou Bergs had never beaten a player ranked this high. On Monday at the Shanghai Masters, the 27-year-old Belgian changed that in stunning fashion, defeating second-seeded Carlos Alcaraz 7-5, 7-6(4) to record the biggest victory of his professional career.

The result sent shockwaves through the draw. Alcaraz, the defending champion who arrived in Shanghai fresh from winning the Japan Open title, was eliminated in the third round. More significantly, his exit mathematically confirmed Alexander Zverev as the 2026 ATP Year-End No. 1.

How Bergs Did It

Bergs, ranked outside the ATP's top 30 entering the tournament, was the decided underdog. But his game plan was clear from the opening game — attack the baseline, come forward when the opportunity arose, and hold his nerve in pressure moments.

The first set was tightly contested. Bergs broke Alcaraz at a critical moment to take it 7-5. The second set went deeper, with both players trading holds until a tiebreak. Bergs converted at 7-4, closing out the match and sending the crowd into a buzz.

Bergs had arrived at Shanghai with momentum: a 6-0, 6-3 first-round win over Ignacio Buse and a 6-3, 6-1 victory over Vit Kopriva. His game has been clicking. Against Alcaraz, it reached a new level.

Bergs' Shanghai Run in Context

This is not the first time Bergs has made waves in Shanghai. In 2025 he reached the quarterfinals, where Novak Djokovic stopped him. A repeat run in 2026 would already represent an impressive achievement. Beating Alcaraz along the way makes it remarkable.

The head-to-head between Bergs and Alcaraz is now 1-1. Their only previous meeting ended in a Alcaraz victory in Tokyo. Monday's match flipped that record entirely.

What's Next

Bergs faces American Tommy Paul in the Round of 16. A win there would send him to the quarterfinals, where the level only gets harder — potentially facing the winner of Medvedev and Tsitsipas. For Alcaraz, the early exit means a long journey home and a recalibration before Vienna and Paris-Bercy close out the season. He has not lost his No. 2 ranking, but the Race to Turin picture has shifted significantly with his departure from Shanghai.
