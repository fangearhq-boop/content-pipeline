WTA Wuhan Open Round 1: Eala Upset, Sabalenka-Swiatek QF Path Set

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | October 13, 2026

The final WTA 1000 event of the 2026 season opened in Wuhan, China this week, and the first round has already delivered a surprise. Thirteenth seed Alexandra Eala lost to Chinese qualifier Han Shi 4-6, 6-3, 6-3 on Monday, becoming one of the first notable first-round exits of the tournament.

Eala won the opening set convincingly but faded over the next two hours. Han Shi, playing in front of an energized home crowd, stayed consistent and took the match in three. It was a difficult result for Eala, who had been one of the most talked-about players of the Asian swing.

Noskova Withdraws

Linda Noskova, one of the Czech Republic's brightest young prospects, withdrew from the tournament before her first match with a left calf injury. The withdrawal opens up her section of the draw, giving a potential path to the later rounds for whichever players are in her bracket.

Other first-round results saw Maria Sakkari advance comfortably, defeating Donna Vekic 6-4, 6-2. Ludmilla Samsonova won a three-set rollercoaster against Karolina Siniakova, and Dayana Yastremska defeated Bucsa in three sets.

The Draw's Biggest Storyline: Sabalenka vs. Swiatek

The tournament's most anticipated potential matchup is already generating significant buzz before a ball has been struck. Aryna Sabalenka and Iga Swiatek have been drawn into the same quarter of the Wuhan Open, creating a route to a quarterfinal meeting that would be their first of the 2026 season.

That storyline alone — two of the tour's biggest names meeting for the first time in 2026, with WTA Finals Race implications — has made Wuhan one of the must-watch events of the final weeks of the season. Quarterfinals are scheduled for October 16. Two more rounds must be won before that potential collision becomes reality.

What's Next

The Wuhan Open runs through October 18. Second-round play continues on October 13-14, with the quarterfinals set for October 16 and the final on October 18. Also absent from the draw: Zheng Qinwen, who won the China Open in Beijing last weekend, and Mirra Andreeva — both skipping Wuhan after deep runs in Beijing. The field remains strong with Sabalenka, Swiatek, and Sakkari all in action.
