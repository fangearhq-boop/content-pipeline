Zverev Makes History as 2026 ATP Year-End No. 1 — First German Ever

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | October 13, 2026

Alexander Zverev has made history. After Carlos Alcaraz fell to Zizou Bergs in the third round of the Shanghai Masters on Monday, Zverev mathematically clinched the 2026 ATP Year-End No. 1 — becoming the first German player ever to finish a season at the top of the ATP rankings.

The milestone caps what has been a dominant and historic season for the 29-year-old. Zverev won both the French Open, defeating Flavio Cobolli in Paris, and the US Open, beating Ben Shelton in New York. A two-Grand-Slam season. Now a Year-End No. 1. The Germans have found their greatest tennis champion.

Why This Is Historic

Germany has produced world-class tennis talent for decades — Boris Becker and Michael Stich both reached the pinnacle of the sport in the Open Era. But neither finished an entire season at No. 1. Zverev is the first German to do so.

He is also the first German to hold the No. 1 ranking since Boris Becker's era. It is a distinction that has waited over three decades for a German player to claim, and Zverev has finally delivered it.

The confirmation arrived through Alcaraz's third-round exit against Belgian Zizou Bergs. With Jannik Sinner out for the rest of 2026 season due to injury, and Alcaraz now eliminated, no player can catch Zverev in the Race to Turin standings. The official year-end rankings will be published on November 23.

A Season Built on Grand Slams

Zverev's 2026 season will be remembered as the year everything came together. After years of being considered a perennial contender who could not close at the majors, Zverev erased all doubt with back-to-back Grand Slam titles.

At Roland Garros, Zverev powered through the French Open draw to defeat Flavio Cobolli in the final. At Flushing Meadows, he dispatched Ben Shelton in the US Open final. Two majors in a single season is a mark of greatness. Combined with the Race points accumulated throughout the year, it has delivered him the year's highest honor.

What's Next

Zverev remains in contention at the Shanghai Masters, where he faces Alexander Bublik in the quarterfinals. If he advances to the semifinals, he will likely face Alex de Minaur. The German has every incentive to finish Shanghai deep and carry momentum into the ATP Finals in Turin, where he will arrive as the world's best player.

The ATP Finals run from November 9-16 in Turin, Italy. Zverev will enter that tournament as No. 1 seed — and as a man who has already answered every question about his ability to win when it matters most.
