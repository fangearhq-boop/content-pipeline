Shelton After US Open Final Loss: "This One Stings" — But America's Next Champion Has Arrived

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | September 14, 2026

Ben Shelton did not try to hide how much Sunday hurt.

"I'm motivated. Disappointed with how today went. Happy with how I fought throughout the week, two weeks. Excited with the progress that I feel that I've made, but yeah, this one stings 100 per cent," the 23-year-old American said after falling to Alexander Zverev 6-3, 7-6(2), 5-7, 6-2 in the 2026 US Open Men's Final.

The loss was real. The storybook finish did not come. But what Shelton accomplished in reaching the final — and what that moment represented — will not be forgotten regardless of the scoreline.

A Historic Final Appearance

Shelton became the first Black American man to play in a Grand Slam final since MaliVai Washington appeared at Wimbledon in 1996. He was the first Black American man in a US Open final since Arthur Ashe won the title in 1972 — the last time an American of any background claimed this trophy on the men's side was Andy Roddick in 2003.

In two weeks at Flushing Meadows, Shelton defeated Francis Tiafoe in the semifinals in four sets before making his run through the draw to reach the final. The crowd at Arthur Ashe Stadium was electric for him throughout, the hope of ending a 23-year American men's drought pulling at every point.

Zverev, his more experienced opponent, proved too steady on the day. But Shelton's performance over two weeks was a statement about where American men's tennis is heading.

A Late Bloomer's Perspective

Shelton has always taken the long view of his development.

"I'm a late bloomer. That's just the way that it's always been," he said. "I haven't always been successful the very first time that I've been in finals, whether it was from when I was really young all the way up to the Challenger level and now the ATP, Grand Slam level."

He knows the blueprint: show up, compete, learn from the loss, come back better. He has done it at every level of the sport. There is no reason to think Sunday will be any different.

What's Next

Shelton's message for what comes next was clear: "I'm going to try to keep getting better and show up in Australia even stronger than I showed up here."

The 2027 Australian Open is four months away. Shelton will enter that tournament with the experience of a Grand Slam final on his resume and the hunger of someone who knows exactly what he is missing. For American tennis fans waiting 23 years, the next best shot may well be in Melbourne — with this same 23-year-old leading the charge.
