Rybakina Officially WTA No. 1: New Rankings Released Monday After US Open Win

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | September 14, 2026

It is now official.

With Monday's WTA rankings release, Elena Rybakina of Kazakhstan sits at No. 1 in the world — the culmination of a remarkable 2026 season that saw her overcome a significant ankle injury and defeat two-time defending US Open champion Aryna Sabalenka in the final.

Sabalenka's 99-week run at the top of the rankings is over. Rybakina steps in as the new world No. 1.

A Historic Ranking

Rybakina becomes the 30th player in WTA history to hold the No. 1 ranking. More significantly, she is the first Kazakhstani player — in either the ATP or WTA — to reach world No. 1 in any discipline. Tennis history in Kazakhstan begins with this ranking.

Her path to No. 1 was not without adversity. A left ankle injury at the Cincinnati Open in August threatened her US Open participation. She was reportedly unable to walk without pain before the tournament began. She arrived in New York in doubt, competed through the discomfort, and claimed the title.

A Historic Season

Rybakina had already won the 2026 Australian Open, making her the winner of both hardcourt Grand Slams in 2026. She is just the sixth woman since 1988 to win both the Australian Open and the US Open in the same calendar year. Rybakina and Sabalenka also became the first pair of women's players to contest both hardcourt major finals in the same year since Steffi Graf and Arantxa Sánchez Vicario in 1994.

Her 14th career WTA title came at Flushing Meadows on Saturday, with the 6-4, 5-7, 6-2 victory completing an extraordinary season.

The Race to the WTA Finals

Rybakina leads the Race to the WTA Finals by a 1,007-point margin heading into the Asian swing. The WTA Finals in November will be her opportunity to bookend a remarkable season with one more title.

Sabalenka, despite the heartbreak of losing two US Open finals and her No. 1 ranking, remains the world No. 2 and will be chasing to reclaim the top spot as the year continues.

What's Next

The China Open in Beijing begins September 30 — Rybakina's first tournament as WTA No. 1. She enters the Asian swing as the player everyone is targeting. Whether the new top ranking brings added pressure or added motivation remains to be seen, but her resilience throughout 2026 suggests she will embrace the challenge.
