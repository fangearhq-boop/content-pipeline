From Flushing to the Far East: What to Expect From Tennis' 2026 Asian Swing

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | September 14, 2026

The US Open is over. The trophies have been handed out. The confetti has settled at Arthur Ashe Stadium.

Now the tennis world turns east.

The Asian swing — one of the most compelling stretches of the tennis calendar — begins in just two weeks, bringing a full schedule of high-stakes events across Japan and China before the European indoor season and year-end finals bring the 2026 campaign to a close.

The Schedule

The Asian Games in Nagoya, Japan kick off the swing from September 27 through October 3, with tennis competition at the Higashiyama Park Tennis Center. Players representing their nations will compete for gold medals, adding a unique flavor to the standard tour calendar.

The China Open in Beijing is the marquee tournament, running from September 30 through October 6 on the ATP 500 side, and September 30 through October 11 for the WTA event. Beijing has historically produced top-level tennis and is expected to again with both draws stacked with Major champions.

The Shanghai Masters — the only ATP Masters 1000 tournament on the Asian swing and one of the most coveted events outside the four Majors — follows in October. The exact dates have not yet been confirmed, but players build their schedules around it every year.

The Six Kings Slam exhibition, an unofficial but high-profile event, is scheduled for October 21-24 in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, offering another opportunity to see top players compete before the final push of the year.

Players to Watch

Jannik Sinner headlines the list of returning players. The Italian world No. 1 was forced to skip the US Open with a right knee tendon inflammation — his first-ever Grand Slam withdrawal. Sinner, who compiled a 44-3 record and six titles through the first half of 2026, was targeting the Asian swing as his return date. A strong showing in China and Shanghai would remind everyone why he was considered the favorite at Flushing Meadows.

Alexander Zverev enters the swing having just won his second Grand Slam of the year. His goal will be consolidating the No. 1 ranking and preparing for a potential run at the ATP Finals in Turin. Expect him to be a heavy favorite in Shanghai.

Elena Rybakina makes her debut as WTA No. 1 in Beijing. Playing with the No. 1 ranking for the first time is a different experience — every opponent is locked in from the first point. Rybakina's grit throughout 2026 suggests she will handle it well.

Ben Shelton, fresh from his historic US Open final run, will be looking to build on the momentum of his breakout fortnight in New York. He has spoken about returning stronger in Australia; the Asian swing is where that process begins.

What's Next

After Asia, the indoor hard courts of Europe follow in October and November, with Paris, Vienna, and Basel on the men's side and tournaments building toward the WTA Finals in Riyadh. The year-end championships — ATP Finals in Turin (ATP) and WTA Finals (WTA) — will determine the final rankings and hand out the last titles of a remarkable 2026 tennis season.
