Two Grand Slams in One Year: How Zverev Turned 2026 Into a Historic Season

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | September 14, 2026

When Alexander Zverev stepped onto the court at Arthur Ashe Stadium on Sunday, he already had Roland Garros 2026 in his pocket. When he walked off with the US Open trophy a few hours later, he had completed something rare: two Grand Slam titles in a single calendar year.

It had not been done since Novak Djokovic's run in 2021. It was the culmination of a season that saw Zverev, 29, finally become the dominant force the tennis world always believed he could be.

The Journey to Roland Garros

Zverev had entered 2026 carrying the weight of multiple near-misses at the majors. The breakthrough came on the clay of Paris. In a dramatic five-set final against Flavio Cobolli of Italy — 6-1, 4-6, 6-4, 6-7(5), 6-1 — Zverev claimed his first Grand Slam title, finally converting after years of coming close.

It was, as ATP Tour described it, "turning heartbreak into glory." Zverev had reached Grand Slam finals before and come up short. Roland Garros 2026 ended that narrative.

Building to the US Open

Zverev arrived in New York in September as the No. 1 seed and the man to beat. His 2026 match record heading into the final two weeks stood at an exceptional 51-13 — a .797 win rate across 64 matches. He had dropped service games throughout the tournament but largely controlled his own destiny.

The final against Ben Shelton followed a similar pattern: early dominance, a third-set wobble when Shelton broke him for the first time in 66 service games, then an authoritative response in the fourth. Zverev's 6-3, 7-6(2), 5-7, 6-2 win was the culmination of two weeks of consistent excellence.

Historical Context

Zverev becomes the first German man to win multiple Grand Slam titles in a single calendar year. Boris Becker, Michael Stich, and Zverev himself are the only Germans to win men's Grand Slam singles titles in the Open Era — and Zverev is the first to claim two in one year.

The ATP Tour noted after his Roland Garros win that he joins Boris Becker and Michael Stich in carving Grand Slam history for Germany. Sunday's result adds another chapter to that legacy.

At 29, with his dominant form and two majors secured in 2026, Zverev heads into the final stretch of the season as arguably the best player in the world — and the clear favorite to win the year-end No. 1 ranking.

What's Next

The Asian swing follows the US Open, with the China Open and Shanghai Masters the marquee events before year-end. Zverev will be looking to cement his position and, potentially, make a run at the year-end ATP Finals in Turin. The question being asked in tennis circles now is not whether Zverev can win again — it is how many more he can add.
