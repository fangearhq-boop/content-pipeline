Zverev's 60th Win in Shanghai Keeps Year-End No. 1 Dream Alive

By Elena Voss | October 12, 2026

Alexander Zverev recorded his 60th victory of 2026 at Shanghai Masters on Saturday, navigating a three-set battle with Quentin Halys. With Sinner absent and Djokovic falling early, the German's first Year-End No. 1 has never been more attainable.

Sixty Wins and Counting

Alexander Zverev walked off the Shanghai court on Saturday with more than just a Round of 16 place. His 6-4, 6-7(8), 6-3 win over Quentin Halys was his 60th victory of the 2026 season — a number that tells a larger story about just how dominant the German has been this year.

The win did not come easily. Halys, the French qualifier, pushed Zverev to the edge in a second-set tiebreak that went to 8-all before the top seed finally fell behind. But the third set was decisive: Zverev converted his early break and served out the match confidently. The ankle concern from his second-round win over Wu Yibing — when he took a nasty fall late in the match — appeared to be a non-issue.

The Path to History Is Clear

For most of 2026, Zverev has operated in the shadow of Jannik Sinner's imperious form — until Sinner's injury forced him off the tour after Wimbledon. With Sinner absent and confirmed out for the rest of the season, Zverev leads the ATP Race to Turin with approximately 8,650 points.

Novak Djokovic fell in the second round of Shanghai, ending whatever faint hopes he might have harbored for a Year-End No. 1 surge. That leaves Zverev without a credible challenger for the top spot. Barring an unexpected withdrawal or catastrophic collapse, the Year-End No. 1 ranking appears to be his to lose.

Should he claim it, the achievement would be unprecedented in German tennis history. No German player has ever ended a season ranked No. 1 in the world.

What the 60-Win Season Means

Sixty wins by early October is an elite-level total. The ATP record for most wins in a calendar year is 82, set by Guillermo Vilas in 1977 when the tour calendar was larger. In the modern era, Djokovic reached 70 wins in 2012. Hitting 60 by October 12 means Zverev is on pace for one of the strongest seasons in recent memory.

His titles in 2026 include major events, and he has consistently reached the later rounds of every tournament he has entered. Even when results have not gone perfectly — including his quarterfinal loss to Djokovic in Beijing — Zverev has remained healthy and competitive across all surfaces.

What's Next

Zverev faces Alexander Bublik in the Shanghai Round of 16. The Kazakh's big serve makes him a dangerous opponent, but Zverev's all-court game should give him the edge. Beyond Shanghai, Vienna and Paris-Bercy remain on the ATP calendar before the Year-End Championships in Turin. If Zverev can get deep in Shanghai — ideally to the final or beyond — the Year-End No. 1 could be mathematically sealed before the final swing of the season even begins.
