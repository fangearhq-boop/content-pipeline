Zheng Qinwen Makes History as First Chinese Champion at WTA Beijing

By Marcus Cole | October 12, 2026

The wildcard from China stunned No. 4 seed Mirra Andreeva 7-5, 6-4 in the 2026 China Open final to become the first Chinese player to win the historic event.

A Wildcard's Path to Glory

Zheng Qinwen arrived in Beijing as a wildcard ranked No. 54 in the world. Seven matches later, she lifted the China Open trophy — becoming the first Chinese player in history to win the event, and cementing her return from a debilitating right elbow injury that had kept her away since Tokyo 2024.

In the final, played on October 11, Zheng defeated World No. 4 Mirra Andreeva 7-5, 6-4 in 1 hour and 43 minutes. The win was accomplished despite Zheng dropping 78 games across her seven matches — the most conceded by any WTA 1000 champion since the format began in 1990.

"I never gave up," Zheng said after the match. "This week, every match was a battle."

The First Set: Zheng Saves Three Set Points

The final was far from straightforward. Andreeva, who had already claimed three WTA titles in 2026 and was playing her fifth final of the year, pushed the defending champion hopeful to the brink. In the 10th game of the first set, with Andreeva serving, Zheng faced three set points.

She saved all three. What followed was remarkable: Zheng won the final 11 points of the first set in a row, closing it out 7-5. The momentum shift was decisive.

In the second set, Zheng broke in the 9th game and served out the match to close 6-4, completing a stunning upset that sent the Chinese crowd into rapture.

The Road to the Final

Zheng's run through the draw was nothing short of extraordinary. As a wildcard with low expectations, she outplayed higher-seeded opponents at every turn. In the quarterfinal, she defeated No. 6 seed Elina Svitolina 6-3, 7-6(6). In the semifinal, she dispatched No. 13 seed Elise Mertens 7-5, 6-2 — Mertens, who had already beaten No. 3 Coco Gauff and No. 1 Iga Swiatek earlier in the week.

The victories over Svitolina and Mertens made the final against Andreeva the final obstacle to a historic achievement.

Andreeva Denied Her No. 3 Moment

For Andreeva, the defeat was a significant blow. A win would have elevated the Russian teenager to World No. 3 in the WTA rankings — her highest-ever ranking. It was her fifth final of 2026, a season in which she had already established herself as one of the most exciting young players in the world.

She had also defeated Barbora Bartunkova 6-2, 6-2 in the semifinal to advance, and entered the final as the clear favorite against the unseeded Zheng. The defeat will sting, but it does little to diminish an outstanding 2026 season that has already included three WTA titles.

Historic Context

Zheng's victory has multiple layers of significance. It is her sixth WTA title overall — and her first since the Japan Open in late 2024, before her right elbow injury forced her off the tour. Her return had been gradual, and entering Beijing as a wildcard ranked No. 54 suggested she was still rebuilding.

Instead, she produced a performance that no previous Chinese player had managed on home soil at one of the sport's elite events. The China Open, a WTA 1000 event played annually in Beijing, had never been won by a Chinese player in its modern era — until now.

Zheng is also widely regarded as one of tennis's brightest young talents, having reached the Olympic singles gold medal match in Paris 2024. This title, won in the most improbable fashion, may signal the full arrival of her comeback.

What's Next

Zheng will see her ranking rise significantly from No. 54 following this victory, potentially cracking the Top 20. For Andreeva, the focus shifts to maintaining her place in the WTA Finals race. The WTA season continues with events in Europe before the WTA Finals in Riyadh later this fall.

Meanwhile, the historic nature of Zheng's win is sure to resonate across Chinese tennis and inspire the next generation of players. On the sport's biggest home stage, she delivered when it mattered most.
