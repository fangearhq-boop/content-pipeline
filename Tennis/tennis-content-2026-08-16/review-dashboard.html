### Long-Form Post Thiago Agustín Tirante. Remember that name. The 23-year-old Argentine, ranked No. 50 in the world, walked onto center court in Cincinnati on Saturday and did something almost nobody has done in the last two decades: he beat Novak Djokovic at a Masters 1000 tournament after losing the first set. It has happened just 14 times since 2005. Tirante is now on that very short list. The match was strange and dramatic in equal measure. Djokovic took the opener convincingly — looking every bit the 3-time Cincinnati champion returning after a 2-year absence. Then, at 2-2 in the second set, he appeared to vomit at a changeover and took a medical timeout. Whatever happened in that moment, the match changed completely. Tirante broke twice in the second set, then held on through a gripping decider to win 2-6, 6-4, 6-4. The Cincinnati comeback Djokovic fans had been anticipating is over. Now the question is whether the 38-year-old will be healthy and ready for the US Open, which begins in 8 days. Tirante called it the biggest win of his career. It might be the biggest result of the tournament. What does this mean for Djokovic's US Open? Drop your thoughts below. ---

### Long-Form Post You really can't script this stuff. Rafael Jodar — 19 years old, coming off his first Masters 1000 semifinal in Montreal last week — was staring down a 1-5 deficit in the third set against Denis Shapovalov on Saturday. Two match points were on the table. The match seemed over. Then his shoelaces broke. Not once. Twice. Right there in the middle of the comeback, with Shapovalov pacing the baseline trying to maintain focus while the teenager from Spain changed his shoes — not once, but twice in the final set. The Canadian was livid. "I can't do anything," Shapovalov reportedly said afterward. But Jodar? He just played tennis. He saved the match points. He won six straight games from 1-5. He closed it out 7-5, 4-6, 7-5. He is now 14-3 in three-set matches in 2026. The shoelace chaos, the age-19 calm, the back-to-back massive weeks — this kid is becoming the most entertaining player on tour. What do you make of Rafael Jodar's summer? ---

### Long-Form Post There are moments in sport that feel like quiet endings, even if nobody is ready to say so out loud. Venus Williams walked onto the Cincinnati courts on Saturday with a wildcard, ranked 615th in the world at 46 years old, and played Emiliana Arango — a 25-year-old Colombian qualifier ranked No. 95. Serena Williams was in the stands, watching. Arango won 6-2, 6-2 in 65 minutes. Venus is now 0-14 in singles matches in 2026. Her last singles win came in July 2025. At 46, she keeps showing up, keeps accepting wildcards, keeps walking out in front of crowds who come to see history rather than tennis results. That takes something. Whatever we think about the scoreline, the courage to keep competing at this level, in this sport, at this age, is genuinely remarkable. There was something poignant about Serena being there in Cincinnati. Two sisters, one of them still fighting on the court, the other watching from the stands. Whatever Venus decides next about her career, she has given this sport more than most players ever will. What do you want to say to Venus Williams today? ---
