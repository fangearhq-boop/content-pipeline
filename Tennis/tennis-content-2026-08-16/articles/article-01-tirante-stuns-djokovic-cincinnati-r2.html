Tirante Stuns Djokovic in Cincinnati R2: The Biggest Upset of the Hard-Court Swing

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | August 16, 2026

Thiago Agustín Tirante has now defeated Novak Djokovic at a Masters 1000 tournament. Read that sentence again. The 23-year-old Argentine, ranked No. 50 in the world, arrived in Cincinnati as a player capable of causing an upset on a good day. On Saturday, he did something that requires historical context to appreciate fully: he beat Djokovic after losing the first set at Masters 1000 level — something that had happened just 14 times since 2005.

The final score was 2-6, 6-4, 6-4. The story behind it was considerably stranger.

A Match That Turned at a Changeover

Djokovic took the first set convincingly, looking every bit the 3-time Cincinnati champion who had returned after a 2-year absence from the tournament with unfinished business in mind. His groundstrokes were sharp, his movement assured. At 2-2 in the second set, something changed. Djokovic appeared to vomit at a changeover and called for a medical timeout before play resumed.

Whatever happened in that moment, the match shifted entirely. Tirante began pressing forward, and Djokovic — one of the most resilient players in the history of the sport — could not find the level that had dominated the opening set. Tirante broke twice in the second set to level at one apiece, then played a composed deciding set to take the match 6-4.

The Historical Weight of This Win

To put Tirante's achievement in context: only 14 players have beaten Djokovic at Masters 1000 level after dropping the first set since 2005. That is across two decades and hundreds of matches. Djokovic is almost uniquely difficult to eliminate once he takes the opener, combining elite fitness with one of the most psychologically formidable presences the sport has ever produced.

Tirante was not supposed to be on that list. But he is now — and he did it in a match where Djokovic was clearly compromised. That the Serbian still pushed Tirante to a full decider, even while dealing with a physical issue significant enough to require medical attention, speaks to the champion's stubbornness. That Tirante stayed calm and clinical throughout speaks to his composure.

The Argentine called it the biggest win of his career. The numbers back that up.

Djokovic's Cincinnati Dream Is Over — and US Open Questions Loom

This was Djokovic's first competitive match since losing to Jannik Sinner in the Wimbledon semi-finals — a gap of more than a month. He had chosen Cincinnati specifically as his hard-court warm-up before the US Open, arriving as a three-time champion with a 10-match win streak at the Lindner Family Tennis Center. The plan was to shake off the rust and build momentum before Flushing Meadows.

That plan is now in ruins. Djokovic exits in the second round, with eight days until the US Open main draw begins on August 30. He will arrive at Arthur Ashe Stadium without competitive match practice on hard courts, and with questions about his physical health that Saturday's match only deepened.

He is not alone in that predicament. Jannik Sinner (knee) and Carlos Alcaraz (wrist) both withdrew from Cincinnati before the tournament began. Of the game's three biggest names, not one will have completed a hard-court event before the year's final Grand Slam.

What's Next

Tirante advances to the third round in Cincinnati and will be one of the most closely watched players in the draw. With Djokovic eliminated, the men's bracket opens further — Zverev remains the top seed and has survived his own scare against Norrie, while young players including Rafael Jodar (who completed his own stunning win on Saturday) have established themselves as genuine threats.

For Djokovic, the attention turns immediately to the US Open. The draw is released August 27. Whether the 24-time Grand Slam champion arrives healthy and competitive at Flushing Meadows will be the central question of the fortnight. For now, Cincinnati belongs to Tirante.
