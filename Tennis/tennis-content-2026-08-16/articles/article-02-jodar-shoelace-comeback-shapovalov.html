Jodar Saves Match Points, Breaks Shoelaces, Breaks Shapovalov: Cincinnati R2 Instant Classic

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | August 16, 2026

Rafael Jodar was down 1-5 in the third set. He had already dropped his serve multiple times. Denis Shapovalov, the experienced Canadian, was serving for what looked like a clean-cut victory. Then Jodar's shoelaces broke — and everything got stranger from there.

What followed was one of the most talked-about matches of the 2026 hard-court swing. Jodar defeated Shapovalov 7-5, 4-6, 7-5 on Saturday in Cincinnati to advance to the third round of the Western and Southern Open, completing a six-game winning streak from 1-5 in the deciding set while managing not one but two mid-match shoe changes and a fuming opponent who believed the delays were affecting his rhythm.

The Shoelace Controversy

At 5-3 in the third set, with Jodar serving and his comeback building real momentum, his left shoelace snapped. He needed to stop and change his shoes. Shapovalov, whose concentration had been tested throughout the match, was visibly agitated. The delay, coming in the middle of Jodar's extraordinary run of six consecutive games, struck the Canadian as more than coincidence.

"I can't do anything," Shapovalov said afterward — a line that captured his frustration at a situation that fell entirely outside his control. Whether the delays genuinely disrupted his rhythm or whether Jodar would have completed the comeback regardless is a question that will be debated on tennis social media for days.

What is not in question is the scoreline. Jodar saved multiple match points when it mattered, won the games when they counted, and advanced. "I cannot control that," Jodar said of the broken laces — the kind of calm response from a teenager that is either enormously impressive or enormously infuriating, depending on which side of the net you were on.

The Numbers Behind the Comeback

Jodar is now 14-3 in deciding sets in 2026. That is an elite closing record for a 19-year-old playing in only his second full season on tour. In three-set matches, he consistently finds a way — whether through shot-making, physical fitness, tactical adjustment, or, apparently, footwear logistics.

The Cincinnati win follows a career-defining week in Montreal, where Jodar reached his first Masters 1000 semifinal. Two weeks ago, he was a rising prospect with potential. Now, after back-to-back massive results against established players, he is a legitimate threat at every event he enters.

A New Spanish Star on the Hard Courts

Spain has long been the country that produces clay-court specialists — players who dominate on the slow, heavy red dirt of Roland Garros and the European spring swing before struggling to translate that game to the faster hard-court surfaces. Jodar is different. His 2026 hard-court season has been his best: aggressive from the baseline, physically durable in long matches, and with a mental edge that has shown up repeatedly when lesser players would fold.

At 19, with a Montreal SF and a Cincinnati R3 appearance in consecutive weeks, Jodar is no longer someone the top players can afford to overlook in draw previews. He is a threat.

What's Next

Jodar advances to the third round of Cincinnati, where he will face a draw that has already been dramatically altered by Saturday's results — including Novak Djokovic's stunning defeat to Tirante. With the bracket opening ahead of him, Jodar's quarterfinal credentials are genuine.

Shapovalov, meanwhile, will now turn his attention to the US Open, which begins its qualifying rounds on August 24 and the main draw on August 30. The Cincinnati loss stings, but Shapovalov is capable of deep Grand Slam runs when his serve and forehand are working. After the shoelace drama, he may be motivated to channel his frustration productively in New York.

As for Jodar — the kid who should not have been able to win from 1-5 down against a player of Shapovalov's caliber, the one who broke his laces twice and kept playing, who saved match points and ran off six straight games — he keeps finding a way. Cincinnati is just the latest example.
