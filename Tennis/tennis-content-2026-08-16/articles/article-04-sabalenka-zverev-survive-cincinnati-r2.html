Sabalenka and Zverev Survive Set-Down Scares in Cincinnati R2

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | August 16, 2026

On the same day Novak Djokovic was stunned by Tirante and Rafael Jodar was completing an improbable comeback against Shapovalov, the two No. 1 seeds at the 2026 Cincinnati Open were also given uncomfortable reminders that nothing comes easily at this tournament.

Aryna Sabalenka (BLR), the WTA world No. 1 and top seed, dropped the first set to Talia Gibson (AUS) before rallying to win 4-6, 6-1, 7-5 in the second round on Sunday. Alexander Zverev (GER), the ATP's top seed in a draw that lost both Jannik Sinner and Carlos Alcaraz to injury before play began, was also pushed to three sets, beating Cameron Norrie (GBR) 3-6, 6-3, 6-3 in a late-night finish on Saturday.

Both advance. But the scorelines tell a story about a tournament that is refusing to be predictable.

Sabalenka's Three-Set Battle

Talia Gibson, the 22-year-old Australian, is the kind of player who can be dangerous on a given day — steady from the baseline, composed under pressure, willing to exchange balls from the back of the court with anyone on tour. Gibson had those qualities on display in Cincinnati's first set, holding Sabalenka at bay long enough to take it 6-4.

Sabalenka's response in the second was the version of her that wins titles: relentlessly aggressive, using her serve as a weapon, generating pace that Gibson couldn't absorb. A 6-1 second set leveled the match. The third was tighter — 7-5, with both players holding serve through the middle of the set before Sabalenka found her decisive break. The world No. 1 is through, but not before being made to work for it.

The WTA No. 1 ranking stakes add weight to every match Sabalenka plays in Cincinnati. For Rybakina to overtake her, the Kazakh would need both to win the title and see Sabalenka fall before the quarterfinals. The world No. 1 has just ensured that particular scenario becomes less likely with her second-round win.

Zverev's Late-Night Survival

The ATP side of the Cincinnati draw has already been dramatically reshaped. Sinner and Alcaraz are absent entirely. Djokovic has been eliminated by Tirante. Shapovalov has been defeated by Jodar. The field that remains has an unexpectedly wide-open quality, and Zverev — the Roland Garros champion and No. 1 seed — is aware that his path to the title is both clearer and more treacherous than it might have appeared on paper before the tournament began.

His match against Norrie ran late into Saturday evening, and Zverev was not at his best in the opening set, which the Briton took 6-3. The second and third were more familiar territory — aggressive groundstrokes, serve working, dictating rather than reacting — and Zverev closed it out 6-3, 6-3 to advance.

The German has the experience and the game to go deep in Cincinnati, but the pattern of losing the first set and then recovering is one he will want to eliminate as the rounds get harder.

What's Next

Sabalenka moves into the third round of a WTA draw that features a genuine No. 1 ranking battle and a defending champion — Iga Swiatek, who won her first title of 2026 in Toronto last week — with every incentive to defend in Cincinnati. Rybakina's second-round match was still ahead at the time of writing, maintaining the intrigue around the ranking picture.

Zverev enters the third round as the clear favorite in the ATP bracket, but the lessons of Saturday's results are plain: in the 2026 Cincinnati Open, no lead and no reputation is safe. Tirante and Jodar have already made that point forcefully. The top seeds are on notice.
