US Open 2026: Eight Days Away and the Fitness Crisis Deepens

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | August 16, 2026

The US Open main draw begins on August 30. The draw ceremony is August 27. Qualifying starts August 24. Eight days from now, the final Grand Slam of the year begins — and the state of the men's draw is unlike anything the sport has seen in the pre-tournament buildup of a major in years.

Three of the four biggest names in men's tennis will arrive at Flushing Meadows without having played a competitive hard-court match in the lead-up: Jannik Sinner (knee), Carlos Alcaraz (wrist), and now Novak Djokovic, who was eliminated from the Cincinnati warm-up tournament in the second round on Saturday while visibly unwell. Only Alexander Zverev, the reigning Roland Garros champion, has been competing and winning.

The Fitness Picture, Player by Player

Jannik Sinner (ITA, World No. 1): The reigning Wimbledon champion withdrew from Cincinnati with a knee injury. He has confirmed his intention to compete at the US Open, and he is the projected No. 1 seed based on current rankings. But he will arrive at Arthur Ashe Stadium without competitive hard-court preparation after Wimbledon — a significant gap heading into a tournament played on one of the fastest hard-court surfaces in the game.

Carlos Alcaraz (ESP, World No. 2): The defending US Open champion has not competed in a match since the beginning of the year due to persistent wrist issues. He has missed the clay season, Wimbledon, and now Cincinnati. His participation at Flushing Meadows remains genuinely uncertain. If he plays, he does so as a wildcard question — defending champion on paper, physical unknown in practice.

Novak Djokovic (SRB): The 24-time Grand Slam champion chose Cincinnati specifically as his hard-court warm-up. He has now been eliminated in the second round, visibly ill during the match against Tirante. His Wimbledon semi-final loss to Sinner was his last win in a major. The questions around his fitness heading to New York are real and unanswered.

Alexander Zverev (GER, Roland Garros 2026 champion): The one elite men's player who has been consistently competing and advancing through the hard-court swing. He survived a first-set loss to Norrie in Cincinnati R2 and remains the tournament's top seed. Entering the US Open with match practice, a Grand Slam title this year, and no reported injuries, Zverev is arguably the clearest favorite among the game's upper tier.

The Women's Picture Is More Settled

On the WTA side, the approach to the US Open has been considerably healthier. Iga Swiatek snapped her title drought by winning the Toronto title last week, defeating Rybakina in the final. She enters Cincinnati as the 7th seed and defending champion, with momentum and match practice.

Aryna Sabalenka, the world No. 1 and projected top seed at Flushing Meadows, survived a three-set battle with Talia Gibson in Cincinnati on Sunday and continues competing. Elena Rybakina (No. 2) and Coco Gauff (No. 4) are both in the Cincinnati draw.

Ben Shelton, who won back-to-back Canadian Open titles in Montreal, has established himself as the most in-form American man ahead of the US Open. Shelton's Cincinnati appearance will be watched closely as the home crowd anticipates a deep run at the year's final slam.

What's Next

The US Open draw ceremony takes place on Thursday, August 27. The main draw begins Sunday, August 30. Qualifying begins Monday, August 24, with 32 players advancing to the main draw by Thursday, August 27.

The women's final is scheduled for Saturday, September 12. The men's final is Sunday, September 13.

Before then, Cincinnati continues through August 23, and the results there — whoever advances and how they play — will shape the US Open narrative that begins to build in the days ahead. What is already clear is that the 2026 US Open arrives with a level of uncertainty around its top names that makes any firm prediction essentially worthless. The draw on August 27 will be the next major piece of the puzzle.
