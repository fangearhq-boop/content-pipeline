Two ATP 500s, One Week: Vienna and Basel Open October 26

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | October 20, 2026

The ATP indoor season opens with a double-header. Both the Vienna Open (Erste Bank Open) and the Swiss Indoors Basel launch on October 26, giving the tour's top players — and its legends — a week to shine before Paris. Two different draws. Two different stories. One week.

Vienna: Zverev vs. Alcaraz on Indoor Hard

Alexander Zverev heads to Vienna as the top seed and the confirmed 2026 Year-End No. 1. He won the French Open and US Open this season and has little left to prove — except, perhaps, the Erste Bank Open title, which has eluded him. Vienna is one of the few elite indoor events still missing from his trophy cabinet.

The counterweight is Carlos Alcaraz, who accepted a wild card for the Vienna draw. After a disappointing Shanghai exit at the hands of Zizou Bergs, Alcaraz took the invitation to an ATP 500 — unusual for a player of his ranking — to re-find his footing on indoor hard courts before Paris. If the draw cooperates, a Zverev–Alcaraz final is entirely plausible.

The full Vienna seedings: Zverev, Alcaraz (wild card/No. 2 seed), Daniil Medvedev, Flavio Cobolli, Alex de Minaur, Frances Tiafoe, Tommy Paul, and Jakub Mensik.

Basel: Shelton, Djokovic, and a Night for Wawrinka

Ben Shelton arrives in Basel as the top seed following a strong second half of the season. His indoor numbers are improving, and he is one of the sport's most compelling hard-court attackers. But the headlines at Basel belong to two men with Swiss connections.

Novak Djokovic has accepted a wild card and enters the St. Jakobshalle having struggled in Asia — his earliest-ever Shanghai exit came in the second round. Indoor hard courts have historically been more kind to Djokovic than outdoor slow surfaces, and Basel, where he has lifted the trophy multiple times, remains one of his favourite events.

Stan Wawrinka, at 41, was also given a wild card. The three-time Grand Slam champion is a Basel icon, and the tournament is honouring him on opening night, October 26, with a dedicated tribute event. Whether Wawrinka advances deep into the draw is secondary to the moment: the Swiss legend, in the Swiss arena, one more time.

Basel seeds: Shelton, Félix Auger-Aliassime, Arthur Fils, Djokovic (wild card), Rafael Jódar, Taylor Fritz, Alejandro Davidovich Fokina, and Jiří Lehečka.

What's Next

Both tournaments run through November 1 and feed directly into the Paris Rolex Masters, which opens October 31 — meaning some players will transition immediately from Vienna or Basel to Bercy. The scheduling squeeze is intentional: the ATP indoor swing builds in intensity across three consecutive events. Vienna and Basel are the opening acts. Paris is the headline.

For Fokina, seeded No. 7 at Basel, it is a chance to prove his Shanghai title was not a one-week anomaly. For Djokovic, it is a redemption arc after the Asian swing disappointment. For Wawrinka, it is something else entirely — a goodbye that deserves to be witnessed.
