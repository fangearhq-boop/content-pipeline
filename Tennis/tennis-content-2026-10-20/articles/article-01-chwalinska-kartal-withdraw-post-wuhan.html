The Price of Wuhan: Chwalinska and Kartal Both Withdraw With Injuries

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | October 20, 2026

The 2026 Wuhan Open final may have produced two historic performances, but both players are now paying the physical cost. Maja Chwalinska has withdrawn from the Ningbo Open with a left thigh injury, and Sonay Kartal has pulled out of the Japan Women's Open citing a right knee problem — the first post-Wuhan news for two players who, 48 hours ago, were fighting for a title.

Neither injury was caused in the final itself. Both players cited the accumulated strain of a deep tournament run: Chwalinska played seven matches in eight days to win her first WTA title, while Kartal's path through Wuhan included three victories over top-30 opponents and a nearly two-and-a-half-hour final.

Historic Ranking Gains Confirmed

The withdrawals come as Monday's rankings update locked in the career-changing numbers. Chwalinska officially moves to world No. 11 — her first appearance in the world's top 20. She entered Wuhan ranked No. 22 and spent the week defeating Iga Swiatek and Sonay Kartal to take the title.

Kartal's rise is even more striking in raw numbers: she entered Wuhan at No. 170 and exits with a ranking of No. 49, a single-week gain of 121 places. The 24-year-old Briton had returned from six months off the tour after a back injury, making the jump all the more remarkable. She is now the highest-ranked British woman in WTA history at this stage of the year.

A Pattern for Both Careers

The withdrawals are not unusual after a grueling tournament run. What is unusual is the scale of what both players accomplished in the week preceding them. Kartal became the first British woman to reach a WTA 1000 final in the Open Era. Chwalinska joined Naomi Osaka, Bianca Andreescu, and Victoria Mboko as the only players to win their first career WTA title at the 1000 level.

Both players will return. The WTA Finals in Indian Wells are scheduled for November 8–15, and Chwalinska's new No. 11 ranking places her firmly in the qualifying race. Kartal, at No. 49, would need a further strong result to qualify this year — but her trajectory suggests the WTA Finals are coming for her eventually.

What's Next

With both players sidelined for the immediate next events, attention shifts to the broader WTA calendar. The tour's indoor season continues through October and November, culminating in the WTA Finals. For Chwalinska, the question is whether her body recovers in time to compete for a finals berth. For Kartal, the focus shifts to 2027 and building on the top-50 ranking she just achieved.

One thing is certain: both players left Wuhan with more than trophies and prize money. They left with proof that they belong at the top of the game.
