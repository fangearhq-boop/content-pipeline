Rankings Monday: Fokina, Jódar, Chwalinska, and Kartal Rewrite the Leaderboard

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | October 20, 2026

The ATP and WTA rankings updates published Monday reflect the full impact of the 2026 Asian swing — and the numbers are dramatic. The weeks of Shanghai and Wuhan produced career-high rankings for four players and reshuffled the standings across both tours.

ATP Rankings: Fokina and Jódar Rewarded

Alejandro Davidovich Fokina rose from No. 26 to No. 13 — the highest ranking of his career, confirmed by Monday's update. The Spaniard's maiden ATP Masters 1000 title at the Shanghai Rolex Masters brought a surge of points that vaulted him past a dozen players in a single week. At 26 years old, Fokina has now won three titles in 2026 and is the first Spaniard to win the Shanghai Masters in the event's history.

Rafael Jódar, 20, also climbed to a career-high ranking following his extraordinary Shanghai run — defeating Daniil Medvedev in the semifinals before losing to Fokina in the final. The exact figure was not published in time for this report, but multiple sources confirmed a career-best position in the ATP rankings. Jódar's ATP Finals chances remain alive.

Alexander Zverev holds firm at No. 1, the confirmed 2026 Year-End No. 1. His position at the top is unassailable.

WTA Rankings: Historic Moves for Chwalinska and Kartal

Maja Chwalinska enters the world's top 20 for the first time, landing at No. 11 after winning the 2026 Wuhan Open. She began the tournament seeded No. 22 and ends the week having beaten the world No. 1 and the world No. 170 en route to her first career title at the 1000 level. Her ranking trajectory this year — from a qualifier at Roland-Garros to a top-10 contender — is among the most remarkable arcs in women's tennis in 2026.

Sonay Kartal's gain of 121 places in a single week — from No. 170 to No. 49 — is one of the largest single-event ranking surges in recent WTA history. The 24-year-old from Brighton had entered Wuhan having played very little tennis in the preceding six months following a back injury. She now holds a career-high ranking and is confirmed as the first British woman to reach a WTA 1000 final in the Open Era.

What's Next

With the Asian swing complete, the focus turns to the European indoor season. Vienna and Basel both open October 26 and run through November 1. The Paris Rolex Masters follows on October 31. For players who gained points in Asia, the question now is whether they can hold those gains — or build on them — as the year-end reckoning approaches. The Race to Turin on the ATP side remains unsettled, and the WTA Finals race at Indian Wells has one spot still open.
