### STORY 1: Alcaraz def. Faria 4-6, 6-0, 6-3, 6-2 — Into US Open R3

**Long-Form Post**
Window: 8:00 AM ET

Carlos Alcaraz dropped the first set. Then he dropped five games in the second set. And then it was over.

The defending US Open champion recovered from a shaky opening to defeat Portugal's Jaime Faria 4-6, 6-0, 6-3, 6-2 in 2 hours and 40 minutes at Arthur Ashe Stadium on Thursday, advancing to the third round of the 2026 US Open.

Faria — ranked No. 72 — started well, serving solidly and breaking Alcaraz in the seventh game to build a 5-3 lead before closing out the first set with an ace. For a moment, it looked as if the defending champion might be in real trouble.

But the second set told a very different story. Alcaraz upped his pace, Faria's serve fell apart, and the Spaniard broke three times to take the set without losing a game. He then held his level through the third and fourth sets to move through comfortably.

"Tough match for me," Alcaraz said afterward. "Obviously the first set, I was feeling great, I had break point, I missed it." He acknowledged the difficulty of the opening frame but stayed composed when it mattered.

Alcaraz will face China's Wu Yibing in the third round. He is now two wins away from the quarterfinals. The title defence is very much alive.

### STORY 2: Tien def. Monfils — Farewell at Louis Armstrong

**Long-Form Post**
Window: 10:30 AM ET

Gael Monfils is done at the US Open. After 18 appearances, 35 match wins, and a career unlike any other, the 40-year-old Frenchman's Grand Slam journey ended Thursday at Louis Armstrong Stadium.

No. 14 seed Learner Tien defeated Monfils 6-3, 0-6, 6-3 in 121 minutes. Tien was composed, crafty, and clinical — everything the moment required against a player who had spent 20 years thrilling crowds at Flushing Meadows.

Monfils had arrived in New York for one final farewell. He had celebrated his 40th birthday just two days earlier with a vintage four-set win over Adolfo Daniel Vallejo, briefly becoming the oldest man to win a US Open match since Jimmy Connors in 1992. The crowd at Arthur Ashe had roared for him. The story was perfect.

Then Tien came along.

The 20-year-old American seized control in the first set, slipped through a bad patch in the second when Monfils levelled brilliantly (6-0), then steadied to take the third and the match.

What happened next may be the lasting image of this match. After the final point, Tien walked to the net — and asked Monfils to sign a ball for him. That moment, a 20-year-old honouring the legend he had just beaten, said everything about what Monfils meant to the sport.

Monfils will play Paris Masters later this season and then retire. He has given tennis more than most. Today was goodbye.

### STORY 3: Eala vs. Oliynykova — US Open R2 Rematch

**Long-Form Post**
Window: 12:30 PM ET

Alex Eala came to New York with unfinished business. On Thursday, she gets a chance to settle it.

The No. 17 seed and Philippine tennis trailblazer faces Ukraine's Oleksandra Oliynykova in the second round of the 2026 US Open at Louis Armstrong Stadium — a rematch of a match Eala lost in Strasbourg just months ago.

That Strasbourg clash, played in May 2026 on clay, ended 3-6, 7-5, 6-3 in Oliynykova's favour. Eala had the first set but couldn't close it out. It was the kind of loss that stays with a player.

Now they meet again — on hard court at Flushing Meadows, a surface Eala has made her own. She arrived at this US Open ranked No. 17, the highest Grand Slam seeding in Philippine tennis history, and backed it up immediately with a demolition of qualifier Mary Stoiana in the first round: 6-1, 6-2 in 78 minutes.

Oliynykova is no pushover. She defeated American wild card Reese Brantmeier 6-4, 6-4 in round one and leads the head-to-head 1-0. But this is a different surface and a different Eala from the one Oliynykova faced in Strasbourg. The Philippines is watching.
