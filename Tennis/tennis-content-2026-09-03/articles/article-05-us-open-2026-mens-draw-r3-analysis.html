Men's Draw at US Open Is Wide Open — and Alcaraz Is the Man to Beat

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | September 3, 2026

With Sinner withdrawn, Djokovic and Fils eliminated in the first two rounds, and Monfils gone after his emotional farewell, the 2026 US Open men's draw has lost all of its established structure — and Carlos Alcaraz stands ready to fill the void.

It has been the strangest opening week in recent US Open memory. By the time Thursday's results are final, the 2026 men's singles draw will have already lost its world No. 1 (Jannik Sinner, knee withdrawal before the tournament), its three-time champion (Novak Djokovic, first-round exit on Day 1), the reigning Cincinnati champion (Arthur Fils, second-round exit to Stefanos Tsitsipas), and a 40-year-old legend in his farewell appearance (Gael Monfils, second-round exit to Learner Tien).

What remains is a draw without a dominant favourite — and one defending champion doing exactly what defending champions are supposed to do.

Alcaraz Is Through

Carlos Alcaraz defeated Jaime Faria 4-6, 6-0, 6-3, 6-2 Thursday to advance to the US Open third round. He was tested in the first set, then asserted himself comprehensively across the next three. He will face China's Wu Yibing in round three.

The Spaniard arrived at this tournament after 139 days off the tour with a wrist injury. He has now won two matches back — both in a controlled manner after early wobbles — and looks increasingly like a player who has found his level at the right time. In the absence of Sinner, Djokovic, and Fils, Alcaraz is the clear No. 1 target in the men's draw.

Who Else Is Through

Learner Tien, No. 14, defeated Monfils Thursday and enters the third round as a dark horse. The 20-year-old American has the game to go deep — and the composure he showed in handling the emotional weight of the Monfils farewell suggests a mental strength beyond his years.

Stefanos Tsitsipas advanced to the third round after his second-round win over Fils — the match that prompted the biggest upset discussion of the tournament's opening days. Tsitsipas, who had arrived at this US Open with a 0-5 record against Fils, produced 50 winners to eliminate the Cincinnati champion. He is playing with confidence.

Top seed Alexander Zverev has yet to play his second-round match — he takes on Quentin Halys in Thursday's evening session. No. 3 seed Felix Auger-Aliassime faces Karen Khachanov on Thursday. No. 9 seed Taylor Fritz plays Mattia Bellucci. Alex de Minaur, the No. 6 seed, has advanced past his second-round match and is already in the third round.

A Draw That Invites New Champions

Here is the reality of the 2026 US Open men's draw heading into round three: the player who wins this tournament may never have won a Grand Slam before. Tsitsipas has reached Grand Slam finals but carries the burden of being the best player never to have won one. Tien is 20 years old and playing the tennis of his life. De Minaur has been close before. Alcaraz is the defending champion.

When the top five men's singles players in the world are either absent, eliminated, or yet to get out of round two, the bracket becomes genuinely unpredictable. That unpredictability is what makes this US Open one of the most compelling in years.

What's Next

The third round of the 2026 US Open men's singles begins Friday and Saturday. Alcaraz against Wu Yibing will be a key early test for the defending champion. Zverev, FAA, and Fritz all need to complete their second-round business Thursday before the picture sharpens further.

By the end of the week, the quarter of the draw is going to clarify rapidly. And with no clear pre-tournament favourite standing, every remaining player has reason to believe this could be their US Open.
