Tien Ends Monfils' Final US Open — and Then Asked Him for a Signed Ball

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | September 3, 2026

No. 14 seed Learner Tien defeated Gael Monfils 6-3, 0-6, 6-3 in 121 minutes Thursday at Louis Armstrong Stadium, ending the 40-year-old Frenchman's farewell run at his 18th and final US Open.

The final point fell at Louis Armstrong Stadium on Thursday. Learner Tien pocketed the last winner, and Gael Monfils' Grand Slam career was over.

Tien, the No. 14 seed and 20-year-old American, defeated the Frenchman 6-3, 0-6, 6-3 in 121 minutes to advance to the third round of the 2026 US Open. It was clinical, crafted, and competitive tennis. But the moment that will outlast the score came after the last point.

Tien walked to the net. He asked Monfils to sign a ball for him.

That was the goodbye.

The Match

Monfils had arrived at this US Open buoyed by one of the great moments of his career. Two days earlier, on his 40th birthday, the Frenchman had defeated Adolfo Daniel Vallejo 2-6, 6-1, 6-2, 6-0, becoming the oldest man to win a US Open match since Jimmy Connors in 1992. The crowd had roared. The story felt almost too perfect.

Tien, however, came with a clear game plan and the composure to execute it. The 20-year-old American — left-handed, compact at 5-foot-11, and built more for precision than spectacle — is a near-opposite of the 6-foot-4 showman across the net. Tien broke through in the first set and won it 6-3, controlling rallies from the baseline and limiting the moments of Monfils magic that might have shifted the crowd.

The second set was Monfils at his brilliant, unpredictable best. He levelled 6-0, stripping Tien of an entire set without allowing a game. Louis Armstrong came alive. For a few minutes, the old Monfils — the leaping, lunging, winner-smashing entertainer — was fully present.

Then Tien regrouped. He took the third set 6-3 to close the match, denying Monfils the comeback narrative his send-off deserved.

A Career Unlike Any Other

Gael Monfils has played 18 US Opens. He has won 35 Grand Slam singles matches at Flushing Meadows over his career. He reached the US Open quarterfinals in 2016. He played his first US Open in 2004 as a teenager. He is, by any measure, one of the most beloved players the tournament has ever seen.

On Tuesday night, he was the oldest active player in the Grand Slam draw. He turned 40. He won a match. He thrilled a crowd. He reminded everyone why they fell in love with him in the first place.

On Thursday, Tien reminded him that professional tennis is also unforgiving. Monfils gave everything he had. It simply wasn't enough this time.

What's Next

Gael Monfils will continue his farewell season and compete at the 2026 Paris Masters later this year before retiring from professional tennis. His Grand Slam career is finished — but his career is not. He leaves with a legacy defined not by titles but by every crowd he ever brought to its feet at Flushing Meadows, Roland Garros, and beyond.

Learner Tien, meanwhile, advances to the third round at the 2026 US Open. At 20 years old, he carries the potential of a player who could be at Flushing Meadows for many more decades. In honouring Monfils with a signed ball, he showed he already understands what this sport is really about.
