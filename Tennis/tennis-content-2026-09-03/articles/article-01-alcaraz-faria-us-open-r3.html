Alcaraz Survives Faria Scare to Reach US Open Third Round

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | September 3, 2026

Defending champion Carlos Alcaraz dropped the first set but dominated the next three, defeating Jaime Faria 4-6, 6-0, 6-3, 6-2 in 2 hours and 40 minutes at Arthur Ashe Stadium to advance to the US Open third round.

Carlos Alcaraz has been here before. First set down, under scrutiny, the crowd wondering whether the defending champion has truly found his best form after five months away from the tour. And then, in a flash of relentless tennis, the doubts dissolve.

The Spaniard defeated Portugal's Jaime Faria 4-6, 6-0, 6-3, 6-2 Thursday in 2 hours and 40 minutes at Arthur Ashe Stadium, moving into the third round of the 2026 US Open with the kind of performance that begins with a stumble and ends with a statement.

A Shaky Opening

Faria, ranked No. 72 in the world, came out with nothing to lose and quickly made that apparent. Serving solidly, the Portuguese player broke Alcaraz in the seventh game to build a 5-3 lead, then held to love — capping the set with a booming ace — to take the first 6-4.

Alcaraz had his chances early. He reached break point at a critical moment in the first set but could not convert. "Tough match for me," he said afterward. "Obviously the first set, I was feeling great, I had break point, I missed it."

The defending champion was finding his rhythm but not quite fast enough to prevent Faria from taking the opening frame.

Then the Shift

The second set was a different story entirely. Faria's serve, sharp in the first set, began to leak. Alcaraz raised his tempo, moved through his groundstrokes with more zip, and broke the Portuguese player three times to claim the set 6-0 without the loss of a game.

From there, the match never came back into question. Alcaraz controlled the third set 6-3, then closed out the fourth 6-2 to complete the victory in just under two and a half hours. It was the kind of recovery that shows a player's competitive instincts working exactly as they should.

A Champion's Return

Context matters for this result. Alcaraz arrived at the 2026 US Open having not played competitive tennis since April 14 in Barcelona, where a wrist tendon injury cut short his spring season and ruled him out of Roland Garros and Wimbledon. He had been away for 139 days before winning his first-round match against Roman Safiullin 6-4, 6-4, 6-4 on Monday.

Back-to-back victories suggests he is rounding into form. A first-set loss against Faria could have unsettled a player still shaking off match rust. Alcaraz went 18-0 in the second through fourth sets combined. That is not rust. That is a defending champion finding his feet.

What's Next

Alcaraz advances to the third round of the 2026 US Open, where he will face China's Wu Yibing. That match will be his clearest indication yet of where his form truly sits heading into the business end of these championships.

In a draw already missing Jannik Sinner (knee withdrawal), Novak Djokovic (first-round exit), and Arthur Fils (second-round exit), Alcaraz enters the third round as the clear favourite. He will not be taking any more first sets for granted.
