Djokovic Chases 31-0 in Beijing as China Open Day 3 Unfolds

By Marcus Cole | October 2, 2026

BEIJING —

Novak Djokovic has never lost a match in Beijing. In 30 trips to the National Tennis Center, across six championship runs dating back to 2009, he has left each time with his record intact. On Friday, Day 3 of the 2026 China Open, he faces the next challenge to that remarkable streak: Chinese wild card Yunchaokete Bu, ranked No. 104 in the world.

A win over Bu would push Djokovic's all-time Beijing record to 31-0, a number that has no parallel in the modern game at any single ATP tournament. But Djokovic is not approaching this week with easy confidence. In his first-round victory over Nuno Borges on Wednesday — a 6-3, 7-6(2) result that was cleaner than the scoreline suggests — the 39-year-old required a medical timeout at 2-3 in the second set before composing himself to close the tiebreak 7-2.

The Wild Card in the Way

Bu is a 22-year-old Chinese player who has carved out a niche as one of his country's most promising hard-court competitors. Entering the China Open on a wild card, he brings crowd support that could add an unusual dimension to what is otherwise a straightforward matchup on paper. Djokovic, who is seeded sixth in Beijing after a season interrupted by injury, will need to manage whatever rust remains from a layoff that kept him out of competition between Wimbledon in July and his return last week.

The match is being watched closely not just for the record, but because of what lies beyond it. Djokovic and top seed Alexander Zverev are in the same quarter of the ATP draw — a potential quarterfinal clash that would be the marquee match of the entire China-swing week. For that meeting to happen, both men need to keep advancing.

WTA Action Running in Parallel

On the women's side of the China Open, Day 3 also features second-round action with Elena Rybakina — the new WTA World No. 1 — entering the court for her first competitive match as the top-ranked player in the world. That milestone, combined with the Djokovic storyline on the men's side, makes the National Tennis Center a particularly compelling venue on Friday.

Zverev, the No. 1 ATP seed and Race to Turin leader, is also expected to be in action this week. His path in Beijing could include a matchup against Djokovic in the quarterfinals — a reunion of two players who have spent decades producing memorable clashes at the highest levels of the game.

What's Next

If Djokovic defeats Bu, he advances to the Round of 16 at the China Open and moves one step closer to a potential Zverev quarterfinal. His Beijing record and overall form will be scrutinized heading into the Shanghai Masters (October 7-18), the penultimate Masters 1000 of the season, where a deep run could significantly bolster his Race to Turin standing and strengthen his case for a spot at the ATP Finals in Turin in November.
