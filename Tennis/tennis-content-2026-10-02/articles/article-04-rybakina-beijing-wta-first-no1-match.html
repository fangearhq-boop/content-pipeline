Rybakina Steps Onto Court as World No. 1 at Beijing WTA 1000

By Elena Voss | October 2, 2026

BEIJING —

Elena Rybakina made history this September when she won the US Open, defeating defending champion Aryna Sabalenka 6-4, 5-7, 6-2 to claim her third Grand Slam title and become the first Kazakh player — man or woman — ever to hold the world's top tennis ranking. On Friday, she steps into that history at a WTA 1000 event for the first time.

Rybakina enters her second-round match at the China Open in Beijing facing Alina Charaeva. It is a routine fixture on paper, but it carries a quiet significance: the world's best player, playing her first WTA 1000 match with No. 1 next to her name. Everything from this point forward is new territory for both Rybakina and Kazakhstan's sporting legacy.

A Loaded Draw Around Her

The Beijing draw is far from simple beyond Friday's matchup. Aryna Sabalenka is seeded second, and a Rybakina-Sabalenka rematch in the semifinals is a real possibility — a reprisal of their US Open final on a similar hard-court surface, with different stakes. Coco Gauff sits third, Mirra Andreeva fourth, and Iga Swiatek is seeded eighth after a difficult second half of the season.

Swiatek's presence in the draw carries its own narrative weight. Once the dominant force in women's tennis, she has fallen to No. 8 in the world and trails the Race to Singapore qualifying cutoff by 650 points. Beijing is one of her last meaningful chances to force her way back into the picture.

What the No. 1 Ranking Means for Rybakina

Reaching world No. 1 has not happened quickly or easily for Rybakina. The 27-year-old Kazakh spent years in the top five, repeatedly reaching the late stages of major tournaments, before her 2026 US Open win over Sabalenka finally elevated her to the summit. Before that, she had won Wimbledon in 2022 and the Australian Open in 2026 — three Slams total.

The transition from contender to top seed carries different pressures. Opponents target you specifically. Draws are built around you. Every result gets measured against the expectation of No. 1 rather than the aspiration of it. How Rybakina handles Beijing — particularly if she faces Sabalenka again — will offer early evidence of how she manages those pressures.

What's Next

Rybakina plays her second-round match on Friday in Beijing, with the tournament running through October 11. After Beijing, the Shanghai Masters begins on October 7, but that is an ATP event — Rybakina's WTA schedule after Beijing would likely turn to the Wuhan Open (October 12-18) and the year-end WTA Finals in Singapore. She leads the Race to Singapore standings at 7,492 points, well clear of Sabalenka in second place, and is already well-positioned to arrive at the Finals as the top seed.
