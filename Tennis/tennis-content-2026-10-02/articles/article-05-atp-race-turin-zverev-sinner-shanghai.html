If Sinner Misses Shanghai, Zverev's Race to Turin Lead Could Become Insurmountable

By Marcus Cole | October 2, 2026

BEIJING —

The ATP Race to Turin has, for months, been framed as a two-man race between Alexander Zverev and Jannik Sinner. But with Sinner's decision on the Shanghai Masters expected Thursday and the overwhelming likelihood being that he withdraws, the Race may soon stop being competitive at the top — and begin being a frenzied scramble for the six qualifying spots beneath them.

Zverev currently leads Sinner 8,650 to 7,950 in Race standings — a 700-point margin that looks manageable until you apply Shanghai's point math. Sinner earned 1,000 Race points in Shanghai last year. If he withdraws, those points expire from his total. The net swing, including Zverev's likely modest haul from Shanghai regardless of his result, could shift the gap from 700 to somewhere north of 4,000 points.

The Math of a Missed Tournament

To understand the magnitude, consider the projection: Sinner is expected to lose approximately 3,550 Race points by sitting out Shanghai, while Zverev — even if he exits in the second round — would shed only around 1,080. That is a potential swing of 2,470 points in Zverev's favor from a single tournament.

After Shanghai, only Vienna (October 26, ATP 500) and Paris-Bercy (October 28-November 1, Masters 1000) remain before the ATP Finals cutoff. Sinner would need to win both — or win one and go deep in the other — just to bring the gap back to something meaningful. With a right knee that has sidelined him since Wimbledon and a conservative treatment plan that now includes surgery as a last resort, that scenario borders on unrealistic.

Zverev, by contrast, enters Shanghai as the No. 1 seed in Beijing, in form, and with two Grand Slam titles already confirmed this season. The German has navigated 2026 with remarkable consistency and appears to be in the form of his career.

The Real Race: Six Spots Behind Two

Where the Race becomes genuinely compelling is below the two men who have already qualified. Six additional spots in Turin are up for grabs, and the standings reveal a tight cluster of contenders.

Ben Shelton sits third at 4,430 points, with Carlos Alcaraz close behind at approximately 4,050. But the group below them is tightly packed and the gaps are uncomfortably small. Daniil Medvedev sits at around 2,420 points, while Arthur Fils, Alex de Minaur, and Jiří Menšík are within 35 points of each other near 1,860. A good week at Shanghai could propel any of them into the qualification zone; a first-round exit could drop them back out of contention.

Players like Tommy Paul, Holger Rune, and Sebastian Korda are also in the mix further back, keeping the bubble discussion active through the final weeks of the season.

What's Next

The Race to Turin will crystallize significantly over the next two weeks as Shanghai unfolds. Zverev's strong form makes him the clear favorite to extend his lead; the question is how much, and whether Sinner's eventual return — whenever it comes — will allow him to mount any late-season challenge. For every other player in the field, Shanghai is an opportunity that the calendar will not provide again. The six spots available are finite. The pressure to perform is now.
