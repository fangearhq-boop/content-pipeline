Sinner and Team Face Shanghai Decision as Surgery Risk Surfaces

By Ryan Calloway | October 2, 2026

MILAN —

Thursday, October 2 is the day Jannik Sinner and his team have circled to make their definitive call on whether he will travel to Shanghai for the Rolex Masters (October 7-18). According to reports from puntodebreak.com, that date was explicitly designated as the decision point — and with the tournament less than a week away, no announcement has yet been made.

Italian and international media reports have both painted a cautious picture. Tennisnow.com, citing Italian sources, reported that Shanghai is "unlikely" for Sinner. More recently, new details have emerged that go beyond the immediate tournament question. If Sinner's current conservative treatment fails to resolve his iliotibial band syndrome, surgery becomes a genuine — if extreme — possibility.

The Injury in Detail

Iliotibial band (ITB) syndrome is a condition that affects the band of connective tissue running along the outside of the thigh, connecting the hip to the knee. When inflamed or irritated, it can cause significant pain and restriction, particularly during the explosive lateral movement and repeated impact of professional tennis.

Sinner's treatment approach has prioritized conservative management: rest from competition, physical therapy, injections, and intensive rehabilitation targeting the hip and glute muscles, which play a critical stabilizing role in reducing stress on the ITB. His team has described surgery as a last resort — but the fact that it now warrants explicit mention from medical staff signals that the situation is being managed carefully.

Sinner has not competed since winning Wimbledon in July, a four-month absence that has already cost him appearances at the Canadian Open, Cincinnati, US Open, and China Open. Shanghai would be the fifth consecutive missed event.

The Race Stakes

Beyond the immediate injury picture, Sinner's decision carries significant implications for the ATP Race to Turin. He currently sits second at 7,950 Race points, trailing Alexander Zverev's 8,650. The gap stands at 700 points — manageable in isolation. But Sinner is projected to lose approximately 3,550 Race points if he withdraws from Shanghai, where he was the defending champion. Zverev, even playing and exiting early, would shed only around 1,080 of his points.

The math means Sinner could fall behind Zverev by more than 4,000 Race points by the time Shanghai concludes, with only Vienna and Paris-Bercy left before the ATP Finals cutoff. While Sinner has already mathematically qualified for Turin, the race for the year-end No. 1 ranking would become dramatically more one-sided.

What's Next

The tennis world is waiting for official confirmation on Sinner's Shanghai plans. If he withdraws, attention will shift to his timeline for return and whether Vienna (October 26) or Paris-Bercy (October 28-November 1) are realistic targets before the ATP Finals begin in Turin on November 9. The longer the layoff extends, the more consequential each remaining tournament becomes — not just for the Race standings, but for Sinner's match sharpness heading into the year's final major event.
