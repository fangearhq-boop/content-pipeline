Sinner's Lyon Consultation Raises Doubts About Vienna and Paris — 2026 Season May Be Over

By Elena Voss October 5, 2026

The Lyon Visit

Days before formally withdrawing from the Rolex Shanghai Masters, Jannik Sinner traveled to Lyon to consult with a knee specialist. The visit, which took place on Friday October 3, preceded his official withdrawal announcement by hours. On Saturday, Sinner posted a brief statement to Instagram Stories: "Unfortunately I won't be able to play at the Rolex Shanghai Masters this year as I had hoped."

The Lyon consultation signals that the conservative treatment approach Sinner's team has been following has not progressed as hoped. Surgery has been repeatedly described as a last resort, but the fact that he sought a specialist opinion before missing his sixth consecutive tournament suggests the recovery is more complex than initially communicated.

Vienna and Paris Now in Question

Multiple Italian tennis outlets, citing sources close to the Sinner camp, are now reporting that the Vienna Open (October 26) and the Paris Masters (November) are increasingly unlikely as well. If those reports prove accurate, Sinner will have missed every ATP event from the Canadian Open in August through the end of the regular season — a stretch of more than four months without competitive tennis.

His last match remains the Wimbledon final, which he won in July for his second Grand Slam title of 2026 and fifth overall. At the time, the narrative was that he would return for the US Open. He did not. The China Open was the next target. He withdrew before it began. Shanghai was framed as the realistic return point. He has now pulled out of that too.

What Is Secure

Whatever happens from this point forward, two things are certain for Sinner: he is already qualified for the ATP Finals in Turin in November — he has enough points to secure his place — and he remains the official ATP Rankings World No. 1. Race to Turin math may shift dramatically in Zverev's favor over the coming weeks, but the year-end rankings position requires a more complete accounting.

Sinner's team has not provided a timeline for his return. The 2027 season, which begins at the Australian Open in January, appears to be the most realistic target for his next competitive appearance.

What's Next

The next formal update will likely come when Sinner's name either appears or does not appear on the Vienna Open entry list, with the event's entry deadline approaching later this month. Until then, the tennis world continues to monitor what is becoming one of the more extended absences for a world No. 1 in recent memory. Vienna, Paris, and the ATP Finals in Turin are all still technically on the calendar. Whether Sinner appears at any of them will define how the 2026 season is ultimately remembered for its best player.
