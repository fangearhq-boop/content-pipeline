Djokovic Defeats Zverev to Go 32-0 in Beijing; Faces Medvedev in Today's Semifinal

By Marcus Cole October 5, 2026

A Record That Defies Explanation

Novak Djokovic came from a set down to defeat top seed Alexander Zverev 4-6, 6-4, 6-4 in the China Open quarterfinals on Sunday evening, and the number that matters most has nothing to do with that specific scoreline. It is 32-0. That is Djokovic's all-time match record at the China Open in Beijing — six titles, not one loss, in matches spanning more than a decade of his career.

Beating the ATP Race to Turin leader in a three-set match, in a city he has made his personal fortress, is exactly the kind of result that reminds a tennis audience why Djokovic occupies a unique space in the sport. He dropped the first set and absorbed early pressure from a player who arrived as the heavy favorite. He then systematically dismantled what Zverev was doing and closed out the match on his own terms.

Zverev's Challenge Falls Short

Alexander Zverev, who has won two Grand Slams in 2026 — Roland Garros and the US Open — entered this quarterfinal as the most formidable opponent Djokovic had faced in Beijing in some time. Zverev is the ATP Race to Turin leader with 8,650 points. He is playing some of the best tennis of his career. And he took the first set convincingly, making it appear for roughly 90 minutes that this might finally be the match to break the streak.

It was not. Djokovic's second-set response was precisely what has characterized his entire Beijing run since 2007 — measured, efficient, and devastating when it needed to be. Zverev will leave Beijing with a quarterfinal finish and additional Race to Turin points, but the loss ends what would have been one of the week's defining storylines had he prevailed.

Semifinals: A Familiar Opponent

Djokovic's semifinal opponent is Daniil Medvedev, who defeated Francisco Cerundolo 7-6(4), 6-3 in his quarterfinal match. It is their third meeting in 2026 — Djokovic has won both previous encounters this year. On the opposite side of the draw, Alex de Minaur overcame Andrey Rublev in a dramatic three-setter (1-6, 6-2, 6-3) and will face Hubert Hurkacz, who edged Karen Khachanov 7-6(2), 6-2.

The four semifinalists represent four countries and four very different roads to Beijing's last four: Djokovic's historic consistency, Medvedev's calculated power, de Minaur's relentless court coverage, and Hurkacz's booming serve. The China Open final is Sunday, October 6.

What's Next

Djokovic versus Medvedev takes place today, Monday October 5, at the National Tennis Center in Beijing. A Djokovic win would put him into his seventh China Open final with a chance to extend his all-time record to 33-0 and capture a seventh title in the Chinese capital. Medvedev, who won three ATP titles in the 2026 season including Hangzhou last week, enters the semi knowing the numbers are against him — but that the opportunity to land the biggest win of his fall campaign is right in front of him.
