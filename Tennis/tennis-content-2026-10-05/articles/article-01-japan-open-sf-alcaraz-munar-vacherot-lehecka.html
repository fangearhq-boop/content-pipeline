Japan Open Semifinals: Alcaraz vs. Munar, Vacherot vs. Lehecka — Tokyo's Final Four

By Elena Voss October 5, 2026

The Draw Is Complete

All four semifinalists are confirmed at the Kinoshita Group Japan Open Tennis Championships, and the matchups promise the kind of contrast that makes late-week tournament tennis worth watching. Carlos Alcaraz, the defending champion, faces compatriot Jaume Munar in an all-Spanish semifinal on one side. On the other, Monégasque qualifier Valentin Vacherot takes on Czech veteran Jiri Lehecka, with a place in Tuesday's final on the line for both.

The quarterfinal stage produced sharp results. Lehecka disposed of Argentine qualifier Adolfo Daniel Vallejo in straight sets — 6-1, 6-1 — joining Alcaraz (who survived Denis Shapovalov's retirement), Munar (who handled Kyrian Jacquet 6-4, 6-2), and Vacherot (who upset No. 2 seed Arthur Fils 7-5, 4-6, 6-4) in the final four. It is a semifinal field with a clear favorite and three players who have earned their way there in very different ways.

Alcaraz: Seeking Back-to-Back

Carlos Alcaraz has never lost at the Japan Open. His 7-0 all-time record in Tokyo stands as one of the more quietly impressive streaks in his brief but already decorated career, and nothing about this week suggests it is in serious danger. He has moved through the draw with the kind of controlled authority that comes from genuine confidence on a surface and in a setting that clearly suits him.

Facing Jaume Munar changes the dynamic somewhat. Munar is a Spaniard himself — from Mallorca, as Alcaraz is from Murcia — and the two know each other well from years on the Spanish tennis circuit. The head-to-head historically favors Alcaraz, but Munar's form this week has been exceptional. He came through qualifying and has won four consecutive matches without dropping a set en route to the semifinal. That kind of momentum is difficult to prepare for.

No. 1 seed, defending champion, 7-0 in Tokyo. The numbers are firmly on Alcaraz's side.

Vacherot: The Qualifier Who Won't Stop

If there has been one story at this year's Japan Open, it is Valentin Vacherot. The 24-year-old from Monaco came through qualifying — meaning he won three matches before the main draw even started — and has since beaten the No. 2 seed in the quarterfinals. His 7-5, 4-6, 6-4 victory over Arthur Fils on Sunday was a gutsy, complete performance from a player with nothing to lose and everything to gain.

Jiri Lehecka, his semifinal opponent, represents a significant step up. The Czech, ranked inside the top 30, is an experienced and dangerous player on hard courts who dispatched Vallejo with clinical precision in the quarterfinals. But Vacherot's week has been built on belief, not logic — and at this stage of the tournament, that counts for something.

A Vacherot final appearance would be one of the more surprising results of the ATP season.

What's Next

The Japan Open semifinals take place today, Monday October 5. Alcaraz versus Munar is the headline match and will likely carry the majority of the afternoon's attention. Vacherot and Lehecka follow. The tournament concludes with the final on Tuesday, October 6. Alcaraz enters as the odds-on favorite to claim back-to-back Japan Open titles — a feat that would make him just the second player to do so in the modern era of the tournament.
