WTA Beijing QFs: Gauff, Swiatek, Sabalenka, and Andreeva All Alive After Rybakina's Exit

By Ryan Calloway October 5, 2026

A Wide-Open Field

The WTA China Open quarterfinals arrived with more intrigue than expected because the world's top-ranked player is already watching from home. Elena Rybakina, who entered Beijing as the No. 1 seed playing her first WTA 1000 event since claiming that ranking in September, fell in the second round to Alina Charaeva, ranked No. 118 in the world. The upset — 3-6, 6-4, 6-3 in two hours and 11 minutes — was only the second time in WTA ranking history that a world No. 1 lost her very first match in that ranking.

With Rybakina gone, the draw opened up for the field that remained, and Monday's quarterfinals bring together exactly the kind of competition Beijing deserves at this stage: No. 2 seed Aryna Sabalenka, No. 3 Coco Gauff, No. 4 Mirra Andreeva, and No. 8 Iga Swiatek — a two-time Beijing champion — are all still competing for the title.

Gauff-Swiatek on a Collision Course

The most anticipated potential matchup in the quarterfinals involves Gauff and Swiatek, who were drawn into the same quarter of the WTA China Open draw. If both win their respective QF matches, they would meet in the semifinals — a matchup that carries both historical weight and current-season narrative weight.

Swiatek's 2026 season has been one of gradual decline from the heights she occupied for the previous three years. The Polish star fell to No. 8 in the rankings following a difficult second half of the season, but arriving in Beijing with two titles at this venue in her history, she is not simply here to make up the numbers. Her game looks sharper than it has in several months, and a deep Beijing run would be the boost her season needs before the year closes.

Gauff, ranked No. 3, has been one of the most consistent performers on tour this year. She leads Swiatek in their 2026 head-to-head meetings, though Swiatek holds the edge across their full career record.

WTA Finals Implications

Beijing is one of the last major points opportunities before the WTA Finals in Singapore. The Race to WTA Finals standings matter enormously for the players just outside the qualification bubble. A title is worth 1,000 points; a semifinal run earns 395. Every match from this point carries significant weight for the players fighting to secure their place in Singapore.

Rybakina leads the WTA Race (7,492 points) despite her Beijing exit. Sabalenka (6,485), Pegula (5,825), Andreeva (5,722), and Gauff (5,654) follow. Swiatek, in 10th place with 3,584 points, needs a strong week to re-enter the qualification picture. Every win in Beijing matters.

What's Next

WTA quarterfinals at the China Open are scheduled for Monday and Tuesday, October 5-6, at the National Tennis Center in Beijing. The tournament runs through October 11. With Rybakina out and the four remaining contenders all capable of winning the title on their best day, Beijing is set to deliver one of the week's compelling finishes on either tour.
