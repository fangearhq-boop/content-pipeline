### STORY 1: WTA Finals Final Day

**Long Form — 8:00 AM ET**

The 2026 WTA Finals final is today in Indian Wells, and it's a rematch with a very different set of stakes.

Mirra Andreeva and Elina Svitolina first met six days ago in the group stage. Andreeva won 6-4, 6-0 — a result that set the tone for her entire week at the event. She has since gone 4-0 without dropping a single set, adding wins over Sabalenka, Shnaider, and Pegula in that order. Zero sets dropped across four matches at a WTA Finals. That's remarkable at any age; at 19, in a first Finals appearance, it's something else entirely.

Svitolina arrives on a different kind of momentum. She stunned Sabalenka in the group stage, then dismantled 2026 Wimbledon champion Linda Noskova 6-2, 6-1 in the semifinals. She is the 2018 WTA Finals champion and the oldest finalist at this event since Venus Williams in 2017. A second title would be one of the great tennis comebacks of the decade.

The H2H says 0-2 Andreeva in this tournament. The pedigree and experience says Svitolina is capable of flipping it. That tension is what makes today worth watching from the opening game.

Year-end No. 1 Alexander Zverev opens against Rafael Jodar not before 2:00 PM CET. Jodar is a 20-year-old who became the first player in ATP history to qualify for the Finals in the same year he made his tour-level debut — a statistical milestone that tells you everything about what kind of season he has had. Zverev leads their head-to-head 1-0 from the Roland Garros quarterfinals and enters as the heavy favorite, but Jodar has earned the right to be on that court.

He has had one of the best seasons of his career, and Turin represents the capstone of that run. Shelton, meanwhile, went all the way to the Paris Masters final just days ago and arrives on form of his own. Shelton leads the H2H 2-1.

Carlos Alcaraz — the tournament favorite — does not play until Monday. But today is a proper opening day with two matches that matter for the McEnroe Group standings.

### STORY 3: Svitolina's Road to the Final

**Long Form — 12:30 PM ET**

Elina Svitolina is in the WTA Finals final, and the path she took to get there says a great deal about where she is right now.

She arrived at Indian Wells with a 1-2 career record against Sabalenka at this tournament format. On Friday, she beat her 6-3, 3-6, 6-1 — converting her fourth match point in a third set that exposed every frustration Sabalenka had been carrying all week. Then, in the semifinals, she faced Linda Noskova: the 2026 Wimbledon champion, a player who had gone 2-1 through the group stage without looking particularly troubled. Svitolina beat her 6-2, 6-1. It wasn't close.

That kind of week — beating the No. 2 seed and the Wimbledon champion back-to-back — has earned her a place in the final. At her age, she is the oldest finalist at the WTA Finals since Venus Williams in 2017. She is also the 2018 champion of this event, which means she knows what it takes to win it.

The challenge in front of her is a 19-year-old who hasn't dropped a set at this tournament. Andreeva is 0-2 in their H2H this week, including a 6-4, 6-0 group-stage loss. Svitolina knows what that felt like. The question is whether she can answer it differently when it matters most.

### STORY 5: Andreeva's Title Bid

**Long Form — 6:00 PM ET**

Before today's WTA Finals final even starts, it's worth pausing to register what Mirra Andreeva has done at this event.

She beat Svitolina 6-4, 6-0 on Day 1. She beat Sabalenka 6-4, 6-0 on Day 4 in a match that wasn't close from the third game onwards. She beat Shnaider 6-2, 6-1. She beat Pegula 6-3, 6-4 in the semifinals. Four matches. Zero sets lost. No opponent has taken a set off her across any of those matches.

At 19 years old, she is the first teenager to reach the WTA Finals final since 2004. She is ranked No. 3 in the world and, depending on how the final goes, could finish the year within striking distance of Sabalenka's No. 2 spot. Sabalenka exited this tournament 1-2 in the group stage.

The player standing between Andreeva and the title is Elina Svitolina — the 2018 champion, the oldest finalist here since Venus Williams in 2017, and a player who has shown the resilience and game to win big matches. It is not a foregone conclusion. But the case for Andreeva has been built in real time, one dominant performance at a time, all week long.
