Alcaraz vs. Tiafoe, Medvedev vs. Cobolli: ATP Finals Day 2 Preview

By Ryan Calloway | November 15, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

Monday's Day 2 at the Inalpi Arena in Turin brings the Lendl Group into action for the first time, and it opens with the match most people came to the 2026 ATP Finals to see. Carlos Alcaraz, the second seed and tournament favorite, faces Frances Tiafoe not before 2:00 PM CET. Daniil Medvedev then takes on Italian debutant Flavio Cobolli in the second Lendl Group match of the day. Both results will immediately shape the group standings with five more days of play to go.

Alcaraz vs. Tiafoe: Form Meets Disruption

Carlos Alcaraz arrives in Turin on a nine-match winning streak through the Vienna Open and the Paris Masters. He beat Ben Shelton 7-6(5), 7-5 in the Paris final to claim his fifth title of 2026 — adding to the Australian Open, Doha, Tokyo, and Vienna — and called his indoor form this season the best of his career. He is seeking his first ATP Finals title after reaching the 2025 final.

Frances Tiafoe brings a very different kind of challenge. He is making his ATP Finals debut in Turin, arriving as a player who has beaten top-10 opponents before in high-pressure situations and whose athleticism and ball-striking can generate explosive tennis on any surface. Tiafoe's game is built to disrupt favorites: he moves fast, takes the ball early, and plays with the confidence of someone who does not feel overmatched by a big name on the other side of the net. First ATP Finals match, full house in Turin, against the hottest player in tennis. That is a good context for Tiafoe.

Alcaraz is the clear favorite. His form, his depth of experience in big matches, and his ability to impose his game on hardcourt make him difficult to stop when he is running well. But the gap between them is not as wide as the results this autumn might suggest.

Medvedev vs. Cobolli: The Regular Faces the Debutant

Daniil Medvedev is a fixture at the ATP Finals and one of the best hardcourt players of his generation. He has reached the final of this tournament before but has not won it. Turin, with its fast indoor conditions, suits his game — he controls rallies from the baseline with one of the highest first-serve percentages in the draw and has the tactical patience to grind down opponents who come in hot.

Flavio Cobolli is in his first ATP Finals, and he is doing so as an Italian in Italy — which matters. The Inalpi Arena crowd will give him energy that no away opponent can replicate, and in a first round-robin match, that environment could either settle him or overwhelm him. Cobolli's season has been one of steady growth, and qualifying for Turin is the proof of it. He will need everything he has found this year just to stay competitive with Medvedev over three sets.

Group Standings and the Day 2 Stakes

The Lendl Group does not play again on Day 1 — both Lendl Group matches are on Monday, which means the entire group table opens at once. After Day 2, every player in the group will have played one match, and the standings will be clear. With only three round-robin matches per player before the semifinals, every win carries full weight from the start.

An Alcaraz win and a Medvedev win on Monday would set up an Alcaraz vs. Medvedev match later in the week as a potential group-stage decider — a rematch of their multiple Grand Slam final meetings, in a format neither has used to settle things before.

What's Next

ATP Finals Day 2 is Monday, November 16 at the Inalpi Arena in Turin. Alcaraz vs. Tiafoe not before 2:00 PM CET; Medvedev vs. Cobolli follows. Day 1 results from the McEnroe Group — Zverev vs. Jodar and Shelton vs. Fils — will also be known by Monday morning, giving a fuller picture of where the week is heading.
