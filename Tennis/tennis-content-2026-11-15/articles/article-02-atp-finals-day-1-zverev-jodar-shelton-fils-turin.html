ATP Finals Opens in Turin: Zverev Faces Jodar, Shelton Takes On Fils in Day 1

By Ryan Calloway | November 15, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

The 2026 Nitto ATP Finals opens today in Turin, and Day 1 at the Inalpi Arena offers two McEnroe Group matches with more backstory than a typical round-robin opener. Year-end No. 1 Alexander Zverev takes on Rafael Jodar not before 2:00 PM CET. Ben Shelton and Arthur Fils close the day at 8:30 PM CET. Neither match is straightforward, and both carry storylines that go beyond the Group standings.

Zverev vs. Jodar: A Historic Qualifier Opens Against the Top Seed

Alexander Zverev arrives in Turin having already accomplished what he came here to do — the year-end No. 1 ranking is clinched, with two Grand Slam titles (Roland Garros and the US Open) anchoring one of the most complete seasons of his career. He is a two-time ATP Finals champion, winning in 2018 and 2021, and enters this tournament as the top seed in the McEnroe Group alongside Shelton, Fils, and Jodar.

Rafael Jodar is the reason Day 1 carries particular historical weight. The 20-year-old became the first player in ATP history to qualify for the season-ending Finals in the same year he made his tour-level debut — a milestone that required not just wins but wins against increasingly strong opposition as his ranking climbed from nowhere into the top eight. Zverev leads their head-to-head 1-0, having beaten Jodar in the Roland Garros quarterfinals earlier this year. Jodar knows he can compete with the best; he just hasn't beaten Zverev yet.

Shelton vs. Fils: A Frenchman's Moment, an American's Form

Ben Shelton arrives in Turin having just played the Paris Masters final — a straight-sets loss to Carlos Alcaraz, but a final nonetheless. His form over the past month has been among the best of his career, and his serve gives him a weapon capable of disrupting any opponent regardless of surface or conditions. Shelton leads his head-to-head with Fils 2-1.

Arthur Fils carries a different kind of significance into this match. He is the first Frenchman to qualify for the ATP Finals since Gaël Monfils in 2016 — a full decade removed from the last time France had a representative in this field. For French tennis, which has been building toward this kind of moment for years, the fact of Fils qualifying is already a result worth celebrating. Whether he can convert that into a win against Shelton tonight is a separate question, but the storyline is real.

Group Dynamics and What Day 1 Means

The McEnroe Group has Zverev as the clear favorite, but the other three spots are genuinely competitive. A win today means two points in the group-stage standings and significant breathing room heading into the remaining two matches each player must play. A loss does not eliminate anyone from semifinal contention — four players, two advance, two round-robin wins is the standard benchmark — but it increases the pressure on subsequent matches.

Alcaraz, the most anticipated player in the draw, does not play until Monday's Day 2 when he opens the Lendl Group against Frances Tiafoe. Today belongs to the McEnroe Group.

What's Next

Results from Day 1 will shape the McEnroe Group narrative heading into the rest of the week. If Zverev wins as expected, he moves to the top of the group early and plays with less pressure in subsequent matches. A Jodar upset would be one of the story moments of the week. The Shelton-Fils match is legitimately difficult to call — Shelton's serve and form, Fils's motivation and the Frenchman-at-the-Finals angle. Watch the first set closely. Neither player can afford a slow start given the tight group-stage format. ATP Finals Day 2 is Monday, November 16, with Alcaraz vs. Tiafoe and Medvedev vs. Cobolli in the Lendl Group.
