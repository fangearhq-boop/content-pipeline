Andreeva Enters the Final 4-0: The Run That Has Redefined the WTA's Season Finale

By Elena Voss | November 15, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

Before Sunday's WTA Finals final tips off, it is worth cataloging what Mirra Andreeva has actually done at this event. Not the narrative — the matches. Four matches. Four wins. Zero sets lost. Against the No. 2 seed, the No. 3 player, a Wimbledon finalist, and the player who saved a match point to get here. That is the foundation she brings into the title match.

Match by Match

Day 1 of the group stage, November 9: Andreeva beat Svitolina 6-4, 6-0. It was a commanding performance in which she broke the veteran's rhythm early and then did not give it back. Svitolina held momentum only briefly in the first set before Andreeva steadied and won the second without contest.

Day 4, November 11: Andreeva beat Aryna Sabalenka 6-4, 6-0. Sabalenka came in ranked No. 2 in the world and was considered one of the two or three favorites to win this event. The match followed an almost identical shape to the Svitolina win — competitive first set, one-sided second. Sabalenka won four games across both sets. That was not a close match.

Day 6, November 13: Andreeva beat Diana Shnaider 6-2, 6-1. A complete performance in 62 minutes, the fastest match of the group stage, completing the 3-0 group-stage sweep without having dropped a single set across nine individual games.

Semifinals, November 14: Andreeva beat Jessica Pegula 6-3, 6-4 — their first career meeting on tour. Pegula had saved a match point against Noskova to reach the semifinals. She played a full three sets worth of tennis in that quarterfinal. Against Andreeva, she won a total of seven games.

The Historical Context

At 19 years old, Andreeva is the first teenager to reach the WTA Finals final since 2004. That alone would be a significant footnote in any season. The circumstances that surround it make it more. She is not here as a precocious talent who got hot for a week and drew a good bracket. She went through Sabalenka and four other opponents without losing a set. She had already won 50 matches earlier in the season than any player since Caroline Wozniacki in 2009. The WTA Finals run is the punctuation at the end of a year of sustained excellence.

The Ranking Stakes

Aryna Sabalenka entered this tournament ranked world No. 2. Andreeva is currently ranked No. 3. Sabalenka lost two of three group matches and exited at the round-robin stage. Depending on how Sunday's final goes, the year-end No. 2 ranking could change hands. A WTA Finals title for Andreeva would not just be a career landmark — it would be a signal about where the rankings sit heading into 2027.

What's Next

The WTA Finals final is today in Indian Wells. Andreeva plays Svitolina — the player she beat 6-4, 6-0 in the group stage, the 2018 champion, the most experienced performer in the draw. If Andreeva wins, she becomes a WTA Finals champion at 19. If Svitolina wins, she claims her second title and delivers the greatest comeback result of the week. Either outcome completes a 2026 WTA Finals that has been, start to finish, an event worth watching.
