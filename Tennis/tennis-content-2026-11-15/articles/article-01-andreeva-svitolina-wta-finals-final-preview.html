Andreeva vs. Svitolina: Everything to Know About the 2026 WTA Finals Final

By Marcus Cole | November 15, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

The 2026 WTA Finals final is a rematch — and that context changes everything about how to read Sunday's title match in Indian Wells. Mirra Andreeva and Elina Svitolina first met six days ago in the group stage. Andreeva won 6-4, 6-0 and went on to sweep through the rest of the week without losing a set. Svitolina found another gear entirely in the knockout rounds to get back to this moment. What happens between them this afternoon will close a remarkable season-ending event.

Andreeva's Week in Numbers

Andreeva has played four matches at the 2026 WTA Finals and lost zero sets across all four. She beat Svitolina 6-4, 6-0 on Day 1 of the group stage. She beat Aryna Sabalenka 6-4, 6-0 on Day 4. She beat Diana Shnaider 6-2, 6-1 to complete a perfect 3-0 group stage. She then beat Jessica Pegula 6-3, 6-4 in their first career meeting in the semifinals.

At 19 years old, she is the first teenager to reach the WTA Finals final since 2004. That statistic sits alongside others that make the same point: she is playing the best tennis of a career that is only beginning, at a moment when it matters most.

Svitolina's Route Back

Svitolina's path to the final looks very different on paper. She went 1-1 in the group stage — winning the Group 2 decider against Sabalenka 6-3, 3-6, 6-1 on Friday — before dismantling 2026 Wimbledon champion Linda Noskova 6-2, 6-1 in the semifinals. The Noskova result in particular was a statement: Noskova had gone 2-1 through the group stage and arrived as one of the most dangerous players remaining in the draw.

Svitolina is the 2018 WTA Finals champion. She is the oldest finalist at this event since Venus Williams in 2017. Those facts are part of the argument she makes in every match: she has done this before, she knows how pressure plays, and she does not flinch in the moments that break other players.

The Head-to-Head and What It Means

Andreeva leads their head-to-head 2-0 at this tournament, with the group-stage 6-4, 6-0 as the most recent data point. That match was not competitive in any traditional sense — Svitolina held only limited moments of continuity before Andreeva's movement and ball-striking took control.

But a semifinal against Pegula and a week of knockout tennis later, Svitolina is not the same player who walked off that group-stage court. The final is a different match, with different stakes, and both players know exactly what the other brings. Svitolina's ability to extend points, vary pace, and construct defensive rallies is precisely the type of game that can disrupt Andreeva's preferred rhythm. Whether she can do it for two sets — or more — is the central question of Sunday's final.

What's Next

The 2026 WTA Finals final is Sunday, November 15 in Indian Wells. The winner takes home a share of the $10 million prize purse and the year-end title. For Andreeva, a win would also put Sabalenka's year-end No. 2 ranking under meaningful pressure — Sabalenka exited this tournament 1-2 in the group stage and has watched the gap between them close all week. For Svitolina, a second WTA Finals title would be one of the defining results of her career. Watch for how the first few games set the tone: if Andreeva gets off to a fast start as she has in every match this week, Svitolina will need to find answers quickly.
