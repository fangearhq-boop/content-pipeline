Cruz Hewitt Wins First ATP Match in Washington — 22 Years After Lleyton's DC Title

By Marcus Cole | July 29, 2026

Washington, D.C.

Cruz Hewitt is 17 years old, ranked No. 612 in the world, and on Tuesday he won his first ATP Tour match at the Mubadala Citi DC Open — the same tournament his father, Lleyton Hewitt, won in 2004. Lleyton was courtside watching when his son defeated Marcos Giron 6-3, 6-4 in 74 minutes.

Twenty-two years separate the father's Washington title from the son's breakthrough win. It is the kind of number that turns a solid first-round result into something that will be replayed for years. Cruz entered the event as a qualifier, ranked 612th in the world, seeded against nobody and expected by no one outside his own camp to do much. He handled Giron, a veteran American tour professional, without dropping his serve and without needing a third set to sort out.

In doing so, Cruz Hewitt became the youngest player to win a match in Washington since Kei Nishikori accomplished the same feat in 2007. He is four years younger than his father was when Lleyton Hewitt first broke through on the ATP Tour.

A Win Built on Composure

The match itself was not a drama. Cruz Hewitt played with a steadiness that belied his age and ranking, converting on his opportunities and refusing to hand Giron the soft errors that might have opened the second set. He was, by every account, flawless on serve — he did not face a break point in the match.

Giron is not a pushover. He is a ranked tour professional who has beaten seeded players before. The 6-3, 6-4 scoreline does not read as a fluke. It reads as a statement by a teenager who arrived in Washington as a qualifier and left it as an ATP winner.

Cruz's path to DC Open qualification had itself been a story. He recently reached the Wimbledon junior singles final, losing to American Jordan Lee in the championship match. The junior final at Wimbledon is one of the most scrutinized results in the sport for those watching next-generation talent. Cruz Hewitt had already introduced himself before Tuesday's win.

The Legacy Dimension

What makes Tuesday's result different from a routine first-round qualifier win is the setting. Lleyton Hewitt won the Washington tournament in 2004 — the same courts, the same tournament, the same late July heat. He is now his son's traveling support system, the figure in the stands watching a 17-year-old carry a family legacy into the ATP Tour.

There is a line of succession in professional tennis, and very few players actually come from tennis families in the way Cruz does. Lleyton Hewitt reached No. 1 in the world. He won this title here in 2004. And on Tuesday, he watched from courtside as his son added his name to the same list.

What's Next

Cruz Hewitt advances to the second round of the DC Open, where he will face a higher-ranked opponent from the main draw. A second-round win at a WTA/ATP 500 event would be significant regardless of age. For a 17-year-old qualifier ranked 612th in the world, it would be extraordinary.

The DC Open continues through August 2. The North American hard court swing — Washington, Montreal, Cincinnati, New York — is now fully underway. Cruz Hewitt arrived in Washington as a name to watch. He leaves Round 1 as a story everyone is following.
