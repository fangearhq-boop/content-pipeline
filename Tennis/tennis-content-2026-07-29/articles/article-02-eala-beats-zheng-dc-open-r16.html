Eala Comes Back From a Set and a Break to Defeat Olympic Champion Zheng at DC Open

By Ryan Calloway | July 29, 2026

Washington, D.C.

Alexandra Eala was down a set and a break against the Olympic champion, and then she was not. The 23-year-old from the Philippines recovered from a 4-6, 3-4 deficit to defeat Qinwen Zheng 4-6, 6-4, 6-1 on Tuesday at the Mubadala Citi DC Open, advancing to the Round of 16 in a match that shifted decisively and suddenly in her direction.

Zheng, the 2024 Paris Olympic gold medalist, left the handshake looking unmoved. Eala left the court in celebration. The contrast was notable. It was Eala's biggest win at this level of the WTA Tour and set up a Wednesday showdown against No. 7 seed Leylah Fernandez, the defending DC Open champion.

The Comeback

The first set went to Zheng without much resistance — 6-4, with Zheng controlling points from the baseline and Eala making errors she could not afford. Into the second, Zheng broke again to go up 4-3. The match appeared to be heading in one direction.

Then Eala won 12 of the next 13 games. That run does not happen by accident, and it does not happen without a technical and mental reset. Eala held serve, broke back, held again, and eventually dictated the terms in a third set that Zheng never recovered from. The final 6-1 third set was as clear a statement as a scoreline can make.

Eala, who has risen to No. 29 in the world — a career high — noted after the match that she had already had moments like this in training. "I've already had that in me," she said, per WTA sources. The composure in the second and third sets suggested as much.

The Handshake

These things get noticed. Zheng's post-match handshake was cold, efficient, and brief. SI.com reported on the frostiness. In a sport where handshakes carry meaning, a tight-lipped opponent walking away quickly signals something beyond routine disappointment. It added a competitive edge to what was already a headline result.

What's Next

Eala faces Fernandez in the Round of 16 on Wednesday at 11:00 AM ET on Stadium Court. Fernandez won her first-round match Monday — 6-1, 6-4 over Magda Linette — looking settled and dominant. The defending champion is on form. Eala comes in with momentum from a comeback win over the Olympic champion.

It is a match neither player could have scripted better, and the DC Open bracket is better for it. The Washington WTA draw has found its Wednesday centerpiece.
