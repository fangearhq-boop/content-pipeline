Historic Exits: Zverev Loses No. 1 Debut, Djokovic's Turin Dream Ends in Paris

By Ryan Calloway | November 7, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

Wednesday at the Rolex Paris Masters produced two of the most consequential and surprising results of the 2026 season. Alexander Zverev lost his first match as world No. 1. And Novak Djokovic, whose entire autumn had been aimed at qualifying for the ATP Finals, fell to a qualifier and watched his Turin dream end.

Both exits happened on Day 3 of the draw. Both were seismic.

Zverev's Historic Stumble

Alexander Zverev walked onto court on Wednesday, November 4, as the world's No. 1 player for the first time in his career. He had won the French Open and the US Open in 2026, accumulated enough points to surpass everyone above him, and officially ascended to the top ranking. His first match at No. 1 was at a tournament he had won before, in a city he knew well. It was supposed to be a celebration.

Lorenzo Musetti did not cooperate. The Italian beat Zverev 7-6(5), 6-2 — straight sets, clinical, convincing. Musetti handled the moment and the occasion. Zverev called it a "bad" loss in his post-match comments.

What made the defeat historic: Zverev became the fifth player since ATP Rankings began to lose his opening match as world No. 1. He was the first to do so in straight sets. The list of players who entered a tournament ranked No. 1 and lost their opening match is short and exclusive — and Zverev joined it on the day he was celebrating a career milestone.

Djokovic: Turin Over

For Novak Djokovic, the Paris Masters was not a celebration — it was a necessity. The 10-time champion of this event needed to reach at least the semifinal to qualify for the Nitto ATP Finals in Turin. He arrived as the 10th seed, beat Vacherot in the first round in a grueling three-set match, and then ran into qualifier Rinky Hijikata in the second round.

Hijikata beat him. The Australian qualifier — who had entered through qualifying with nothing to lose — took down one of the greatest players in the sport's history and ended his ATP Finals campaign in the process. Djokovic, who had spent months working toward Turin qualification, fell short. This will not be his year in Turin.

At 39, every Paris Masters campaign carries additional weight. Whether Djokovic returns to Accor Arena again remains to be seen. This particular chapter closed earlier than anyone anticipated.

What's Next

Both Zverev and Djokovic are out of the draw, leaving the last four to Shelton, Van Assche, Alcaraz, and Ruud. For Zverev, the ATP Finals in Turin (November 15-22) remain on the horizon — he is already qualified as world No. 1 — and the Paris result is a painful but isolated stumble. For Djokovic, the 2026 season appears to be over in terms of championship competition. The focus shifts to whether he will announce plans for 2027.
