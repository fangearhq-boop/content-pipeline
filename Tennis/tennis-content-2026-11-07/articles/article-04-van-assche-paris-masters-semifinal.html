The Kid From Paris: Van Assche's Wildcard Run Reaches the Semifinals

By Elena Voss | November 7, 2026 | Tennis Fanrecap

When Luca Van Assche was handed his wildcard entry for the 2026 Rolex Paris Masters, it was understood as an opportunity — a chance for the 21-year-old Parisian to play in front of his home crowd at Accor Arena in Nanterre, to gain experience, and perhaps to win a match or two. It was not supposed to end in the semifinals.

And yet here he is.

The Path No One Predicted

Van Assche opened against Mariano Navone and barely broke a sweat. He held every service game, broke when it counted, and won 6-3, 7-5. It was tidy and efficient. First round done.

Then came 15th seed Learner Tien. This was different. Tien played aggressively and pushed Van Assche to the edge — but Van Assche saved nine of the ten break points he faced and won 7-6(4), 6-4. Saving nine of ten break points in a match is the kind of defensive resilience that separates players who hang around from players who go deep.

The round of 16 brought Lorenzo Musetti — fresh off a stunning 7-6(5), 6-2 defeat of world No. 1 Alexander Zverev. Musetti was the story of the tournament up to that point, the man who had knocked out the top seed in his very first match as No. 1. Van Assche beat him. The arena shook.

In the quarterfinals, qualifier Rinky Hijikata extended him to three sets: 6-4, 6-7(4), 7-5. Van Assche held his nerve and held his serve when it mattered most. He is in the semifinals.

What It Means for French Tennis

French tennis has been searching for a home hero at the Paris Masters for years. The country that gave the sport the clay-court capital of the world has not always produced players capable of going deep at its own indoor Masters event. Van Assche, with his fighting game and his ability to dial up the defense when the crowd needs it most, has given French fans something to believe in this week.

He described Paris as "the Masters 1000 with the best atmosphere," and he would know — he has grown up watching the tournament and dreaming of this moment. Now he is living it.

The Semifinal Challenge

Ben Shelton stands between Van Assche and a Paris Masters final. Shelton hit 19 aces in his quarterfinal win over Tommy Paul and serves at speeds that neutralize the defensive game that has carried Van Assche this far. The American is the third seed and the heavy favorite.

But Van Assche has not been expected to win a single match this week. That track record counts for something — and the crowd at Accor Arena will make sure he knows it.

What's Next

Van Assche faces Shelton today in the first semifinal at Accor Arena, Nanterre, not before 2:30 PM ET. If he wins, he would face the winner of Alcaraz vs. Ruud in Sunday's final. Win or lose, this week has already written a chapter in French tennis history.
