The 23-Year American Men's Grand Slam Drought Is About to End

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | September 11, 2026

Andy Roddick won the 2003 US Open on a September afternoon at Arthur Ashe Stadium, defeating Juan Carlos Ferrero in straight sets. That was 23 years ago. Since that day, no American man has won a Grand Slam tennis title — not at the US Open, not at Wimbledon, not at Roland Garros, not at the Australian Open. Twenty-three years of watching other nations' players lift the trophies.

That drought ends Sunday. It is mathematically guaranteed.

How We Got Here

For much of the 2010s and early 2020s, the dominance of Novak Djokovic, Rafael Nadal, and Roger Federer made the drought feel almost abstract — no nationality was going to break through against those three in their prime. But as that era faded, American men kept coming close without closing. Taylor Fritz reached the 2024 US Open final. John Isner and Andy Roddick himself kept the dream alive in their eras. None of them could take that final step.

This year, the draw opened. Jannik Sinner, the world No. 1, was absent from the tournament entirely. Carlos Alcaraz, the defending champion, reached the quarterfinals — and then met a 22-year-old from Atlanta who had never beaten a top-3 opponent in his career.

Ben Shelton beat Alcaraz 6-7(5), 6-1, 6-3, 1-6, 7-6(10-7) in a match that ended at 3:33 AM, the latest finish in US Open history. He was 0-17 against top-3 opponents before that night. He served aces at 146 mph to close it out while the Flushing Meadows night crew and a stadium full of exhausted fans roared.

Tiafoe's Path and Place

Frances Tiafoe has been to the US Open semifinals before. He reached the last four in 2022, becoming the first American man to do so at Flushing Meadows in 16 years. He came back. He is in the final four again in 2026, and this time, both he and his fellow American are in the semifinal together.

Tiafoe beat Daniil Medvedev in the round of 16 — his first career win over Medvedev — and then dispatched Alex Michelsen in five sets in the quarterfinals. He is 28 years old and playing some of the best tennis of his career. His 25th hard-court win of the 2026 season was Tour-leading entering this week. He is 10-0 at Louis Armstrong Stadium across his entire career.

Tiafoe and Shelton represent different generations. Tiafoe was the trailblazer, the one who first started making American men's tennis feel possible again. Shelton arrived like a force of nature. Tonight they play each other, and the result is almost secondary to what has already been achieved.

The Last All-American Men's Grand Slam Final

The last time two American men faced each other in a Grand Slam final was at the 2002 US Open, when Pete Sampras defeated Andre Agassi in what turned out to be Sampras's final Grand Slam title. That was 24 years ago. Sunday's final will be the first all-American men's Grand Slam final since that day.

What's Next

Tonight's semifinal between Shelton and Tiafoe at Arthur Ashe Stadium will determine which American advances to Sunday's final. Whoever wins will face either Alexander Zverev or Karen Khachanov, who play the first men's semifinal Friday afternoon. The final is Sunday at Arthur Ashe. Twenty-three years after Roddick, an American man will lift the US Open trophy. The only question is which one.
