Shelton vs. Tiafoe: The All-American US Open Semifinal That Changes Everything

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | September 11, 2026

Tonight at Arthur Ashe Stadium, Ben Shelton and Frances Tiafoe will play each other in the US Open men's semifinals. One of them will walk off the court having guaranteed himself a Grand Slam final. The other will watch from the locker room — but will still know that an American man is playing for the title on Sunday. Either way, something ends tonight: a 23-year wait.

How They Got Here

Ben Shelton's path to Friday's semifinal requires its own paragraph. In the quarterfinals, he faced No. 2 seed Carlos Alcaraz — the defending champion who had won 31 of his last 32 Grand Slam matches. Shelton had never beaten a top-3 opponent in his career. He was 0 for 17 heading into that match.

He won anyway, 6-7(5), 6-1, 6-3, 1-6, 7-6(10-7), in a match that ended at 3:33 AM — the latest finish in US Open history. When it was over, Shelton, 22, was through to the semifinals, and Alcaraz's reign as defending champion was finished in the most dramatic fashion imaginable.

Frances Tiafoe's route was more consistent but no less impressive. He defeated Daniil Medvedev in the round of 16 in straight sets — 7-6(1), 6-4, 7-6(6) — ending a head-to-head drought that had stretched to seven matches. It was his 25th hard-court win of the year, a Tour-leading mark. He beat Alex Michelsen in five sets in the quarterfinals to reach his third US Open semifinal in five years. Tiafoe is 10-0 at Louis Armstrong Stadium for his career.

What Tonight Means

The last American man to win a Grand Slam title was Andy Roddick at the 2003 US Open. Since that September afternoon in Flushing Meadows, American men have been spectators in the sport's biggest moments — reaching finals occasionally, winning titles never. Taylor Fritz reached the 2024 US Open final and lost. The drought continued.

Tonight ends that possibility. With Shelton and Tiafoe meeting in the semis, there is no path through this draw that does not involve an American man hoisting the trophy on Sunday. The 23-year drought will end on this court, in this tournament. The only question left is which American does it.

Both are playing for something beyond themselves. Both know what this moment represents for American tennis, for their country, and for the generations of young players who will be watching.

What's Next

Tonight's all-American semifinal at Arthur Ashe Stadium begins in the evening session. The winner faces either Alexander Zverev or Karen Khachanov in Sunday's men's final. Watch Shelton's serve and Tiafoe's energy on Arthur Ashe — where he has never lost — as the deciding factors. American tennis has waited 23 years for this weekend. It has arrived.
