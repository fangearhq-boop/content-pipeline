Sabalenka vs. Rybakina: What's at Stake in Saturday's US Open Women's Final

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | September 11, 2026

When Aryna Sabalenka and Elena Rybakina walk onto Arthur Ashe Stadium on Saturday for the 2026 US Open women's final, they will bring with them one of the richest rivalries in the women's game and enough individual stakes to fill a week of tennis coverage. This is their third Grand Slam final meeting — and the biggest one yet.

Sabalenka's Place in History

Aryna Sabalenka has won the last two US Open titles, claiming the 2023 and 2024 crowns at Arthur Ashe. A victory on Saturday would make her the first woman to win three consecutive US Open titles in the Open Era, placing her in the rarest of company.

She has been exceptional throughout this fortnight. In the semifinals, she defeated No. 3 Jessica Pegula 7-5, 6-2 with the clinical efficiency she has shown all tournament. Her serve, her forehand, and her refusal to back down in pressure moments are all operating at peak levels. Sabalenka on hard courts in late summer is a formidable machine.

Rybakina's Moment

Elena Rybakina arrives at Saturday's final as the world's new No. 1-ranked player — she secured the ranking when she won her quarterfinal earlier this week, ending Aryna Sabalenka's 99-week reign at the top. She is the first Kazakh player, male or female, to reach world No. 1 in the history of professional tennis.

More urgently for Saturday: Rybakina beat Sabalenka in January's Australian Open final, which makes her the 2026 hardcourt challenger. A victory at the US Open would mean she wins both hardcourt majors of the calendar year — the Australian Open and the US Open — an achievement only the sport's true greats have managed.

She defeated Coco Gauff 3-6, 6-4, 6-4 in Thursday's semifinal after dropping the first set. Rybakina has a habit of finding her best when she needs it most. That habit will be tested again on Saturday.

The Numbers That Matter

Sabalenka leads the overall head-to-head 10-7, but the grand stage has been kinder to Rybakina in their final meetings. They split their two previous Grand Slam final encounters, with Rybakina's January 2026 Australian Open win being the most recent.

One statistical pattern stands out across their 17-match rivalry: the player who wins the first set has gone on to win the match in 14 of those 17 meetings. That number makes Saturday's opening games feel even more consequential than usual. Whoever seizes control early will carry momentum that is historically very difficult to overcome.

What's Next

Saturday's final at Arthur Ashe Stadium will determine whether Sabalenka joins the Open Era's most exclusive club or Rybakina completes one of the most impressive seasons any player has had in recent years. Both outcomes would be historic. Both players have earned their place in the final. The match begins Saturday afternoon in Flushing Meadows, New York.
