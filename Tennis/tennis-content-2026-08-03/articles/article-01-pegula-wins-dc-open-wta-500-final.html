Pegula Wins DC Open WTA 500 Title After Rain Suspends Final Overnight

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | August 3, 2026

Jessica Pegula is the 2026 DC Open champion. It took an overnight rain delay, a Monday morning resumption, and one more set of tennis to settle it, but the result was the same one most expected before Washington's summer weather intervened: the world's No. 1 seed closed out Alexandra Eala 6-4, 6-3 to claim her 12th career WTA singles title and her third of 2026.

The match had begun Sunday afternoon at Rock Creek Park Tennis Center following a three-hour rain delay that pushed the opening toss to well past the original scheduled start. Pegula won the first set 6-4 in commanding fashion. Then, with Eala leading the second set 2-1, thunderstorms returned. Officials suspended play at approximately 3:57 PM ET. Both players left the court. The final would have to wait until Monday.

When they returned at noon Monday, Pegula resumed control. She broke Eala's serve to take a 3-2 lead and never relinquished it, closing out the set 6-3 in what was a clean and efficient performance from the American. She showed the kind of mental clarity that has defined her 2026 season: she won the Dubai title in February, Charleston in April, and now Washington in August.

The Week That Will Define Eala's Career

Whatever the final scoreline says, the week belongs to Alexandra Eala as much as anyone. The 22-year-old from the Philippines reached the DC Open final having never done anything like it before — and having beaten four consecutive opponents who had every reason to expect a different result.

She opened her DC Open with a comeback win over Qinwen Zheng, a former Olympic champion, rallying from a set down to win 4-6, 6-4, 6-1. She then eliminated defending champion Leylah Fernandez 6-2, 7-6(1) in the second round, coming back from 5-1 down in the second set. In the quarterfinals, she knocked out world No. 10 Elina Svitolina 6-3, 6-4 — the exact scoreline from their earlier meeting in Berlin this season. And in the semifinals, she handled Naomi Osaka, the No. 3 seed, 6-4, 6-2 in the biggest match of her young career.

By reaching the final, Eala became the first Filipino player to advance to a WTA 500 singles final — a milestone that the tennis world in the Philippines celebrated regardless of Monday's outcome. She leaves Washington with a career-high live ranking of approximately No. 24, up from No. 28 heading into the tournament.

Pegula's Title in Context

Three titles in 2026 — Dubai, Charleston, and now Washington — puts Pegula among the most consistent closers on the WTA tour this season. She has now won 12 career WTA singles titles and has shown repeatedly this year that she performs best in the biggest moments. In Sunday's semifinal, she trailed Diana Shnaider 2-5 in the first set before winning 7-5, 6-4. Against Eala on Sunday night, she conceded the lead in the second set before the rain arrived. On Monday morning, she came back and finished the job.

The DC Open title is Pegula's second at this event, her first since 2019. It arrives at an important point on the calendar — she now heads to Toronto, where she is seeded third at the WTA National Bank Open behind Sabalenka and Rybakina.

What's Next

Both players now travel to Toronto for the WTA National Bank Open, which began its main draw Sunday. Aryna Sabalenka is seeded first, Elena Rybakina second, and Pegula third. Eala's DC Open run may earn her a seeding in Toronto as well. The WTA 1000 event runs through August 13 at Sobeys Stadium, followed by the Cincinnati Open and then the US Open, which begins August 24 in New York.
