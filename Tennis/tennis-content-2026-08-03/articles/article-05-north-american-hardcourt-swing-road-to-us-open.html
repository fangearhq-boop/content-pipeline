Sinner, Alcaraz, and the US Open: What the North American Hard Court Swing Tells Us

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | August 3, 2026

Six weeks remain before the US Open begins in New York. The North American hard court swing is officially underway — and the 2026 season has arrived at one of its defining junctures with three of its most compelling players watching from the sideline.

Jannik Sinner won Wimbledon in July, defending his 2025 title and adding a second Grand Slam in London to his growing résumé. The victory came at a cost: an elbow injury sustained during the tournament forced him out of the Canadian Open. He is targeting Cincinnati, which begins August 13, as his return. Whether he arrives there healthy and sharp will tell us a great deal about the state of the world's No. 1 player heading into the US Open.

Carlos Alcaraz has been absent even longer. The Spaniard, No. 3 in the world, suffered a right wrist injury at the Barcelona Open in April and has not competed at tour level since. He has posted encouraging updates from his academy in Spain — a photograph hitting on court, a brief video message suggesting recovery is on track — but has been absent from Roland-Garros, Wimbledon, and the Canadian swing. He is targeting Cincinnati and the US Open. For a player who won the US Open in 2025, the six-week window before New York is all he has to rebuild his match sharpness.

The Open Road

Their absence creates genuine opportunity. Alexander Zverev is the ATP's No. 2-ranked player and enters Montreal as the clear favorite after his 2026 Roland-Garros title and Wimbledon final appearance. He has the form, the big-match experience, and now a Grand Slam title to his name. A Montreal title would further consolidate his position as the player who defines this swing.

Felix Auger-Aliassime has a home draw in Montreal at his highest-ever Masters 1000 seeding. Ben Shelton is the defending champion. Daniil Medvedev won the Canadian Open in 2021 and remains a hard-court threat. Alex de Minaur has been one of the tour's most consistent performers all season.

Meanwhile in Washington, the DC Open final between Taylor Fritz and 19-year-old Rafael Jódar takes place this afternoon. Jódar — who entered the tournament unseeded and is already guaranteed an ATP Top 20 debut — represents the other storyline of the moment: the rising generation making its presence felt before Sinner and Alcaraz return.

The Six-Week Picture

After the Canadian events (Toronto and Montreal, both through August 13), the Western and Southern Open in Cincinnati runs August 9-17. That is the last major warm-up before the US Open. Cincinnati has historically been the tournament where the returning stars re-enter the conversation — and 2026 may follow that pattern exactly if Sinner and Alcaraz both make it back by then.

By the time the US Open begins August 24 in Flushing Meadows, the North American swing will have answered several important questions: Who is the best hard-court player in the game right now? How sharp are Sinner and Alcaraz after their respective injuries? And has the next generation — Jódar, Shelton, the players making noise this summer — earned a legitimate claim on the biggest stage of the year?

What's Next

The ATP National Bank Open in Montreal runs through August 13. The WTA National Bank Open in Toronto also runs through August 13. The Western and Southern Open in Cincinnati begins August 9 for the ATP and August 10 for the WTA. The US Open main draw begins August 24 in New York, with both men's and women's draws running through September 6.
