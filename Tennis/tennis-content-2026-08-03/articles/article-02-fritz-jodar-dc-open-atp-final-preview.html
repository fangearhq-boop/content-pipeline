Fritz vs. Jódar: DC Open ATP Final Set for Monday After Rain Forces Postponement

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | August 3, 2026

The DC Open ATP 500 final was supposed to happen Sunday. Instead, rain won the first set and postponed the match to Monday afternoon at the William H.G. FitzGerald Tennis Center. Taylor Fritz and Rafael Jódar return to the court today, with the match scheduled no earlier than 1:00 PM ET — after the WTA final completes its own Monday morning resumption.

The delay has not diminished the storylines. If anything, it has given them more time to breathe. Fritz is the No. 3 seed in the draw — not the No. 1 seed, that distinction belonged to Alex de Minaur, who fell in the quarterfinals — and a proven competitor at the biggest moments of the American hard court swing. Jódar is 19 years old, unseeded, and already guaranteed something remarkable: an ATP Top 20 debut this week, regardless of today's result.

How They Got Here

Fritz's path to the final was not easy. He came from a set down against Brandon Nakashima in the semifinals, losing the first set 3-6 before winning 6-3, 6-3 in sets two and three to close it out. Earlier in the week, he saved a match point against Alex Michelsen in the quarterfinals. Fritz has been in tight situations this week and found his way out of each one.

Jódar's route was defined by the same quality: the ability to lose the first set and come back to win. Against Alejandro Tabilo in the semifinals, he dropped the opening set in a tiebreak 6-7(5), then took the next two 6-4, 6-4. That was the second consecutive match this week that Jódar came back from a set down to win. He also defeated Lorenzo Musetti, the No. 4 seed, in the quarterfinals to put himself in this position.

By reaching the final, Jódar locked in an ATP Top 20 debut that he will carry into Montreal and beyond. He won his first ATP title on clay in Marrakech in April 2026. He reached the quarterfinals of Roland-Garros on his Grand Slam debut earlier this year. He is 19. All of this has happened in one calendar year.

The Matchup

Fritz brings experience and consistency on hard courts. He has been to 23 career ATP finals and knows how to manage the pressure of a championship match. He is a former US Open finalist, and hard courts are where he has done his best work at the top of the game.

Jódar brings something different: the momentum of a player who is performing beyond expectations and has nothing to lose. His game is built for the moment — he is not managing expectations or protecting a ranking. He is building one. That is a different kind of pressure, and it often runs in favor of the younger player.

The head-to-head is clean: this is their first career meeting at any level.

What's Next

The 2026 DC Open ATP 500 final takes place Monday afternoon at the William H.G. FitzGerald Tennis Center in Washington, D.C., scheduled to begin no earlier than 1:00 PM ET. Both Fritz and Jódar are expected in the ATP Montreal draw, which opens its main draw Tuesday, August 4, at IGA Stadium. The ATP Masters 1000 in Montreal — where Zverev is the top seed and FAA is seeded second — runs through August 13.
