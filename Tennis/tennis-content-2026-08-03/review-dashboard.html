Pegula Wins DC Open WTA 500 Final — Eala Makes History
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Jessica Pegula is the 2026 DC Open champion — and it took a rainstorm and a Monday morning resumption to get there. Pegula and Alexandra Eala began their WTA 500 final Sunday afternoon in Washington, D.C. after a three-hour rain delay had already pushed the start back. Pegula won the first set 6-4 in dominant fashion. Then, with Eala leading the second set 2-1, thunderstorms returned and officials suspended play at approximately 3:57 PM ET. The match would have to wait until Monday. When they returned to Rock Creek Park Tennis Center at noon ET, Pegula picked up where she left off. She won the second set 6-3 to claim her 12th career WTA singles title — and her third of 2026, having also won in Dubai and Charleston. This is her second DC Open title, first since 2019. For Eala, the week is still historic regardless of Sunday's scoreline. The 22-year-old from the Philippines became the first Filipino player to reach a WTA 500 singles final, defeating Zheng, Fernandez, Svitolina, and Osaka in the process. She leaves Washington with a career-high live ranking of approximately No. 24. The rain took the drama out of the finish — but it could not take away what this week meant. Did you watch the DC Open WTA final? What did you make of Pegula's mental strength throughout the tournament? --- ### Image Post Caption Pegula wins. Eala makes history. Monday morning in Washington delivered the final chapter of one of the best WTA weeks of the summer.

DC Open ATP Final — Fritz vs. Jódar Today
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post The DC Open ATP 500 final is finally happening — and it will be worth the wait. Rain washed out Sunday's final between Taylor Fritz and Rafael Jódar before it could begin, pushing the showdown to Monday afternoon at the William H.G. FitzGerald Tennis Center. The match is scheduled for approximately 1:00 PM ET, after the WTA final wrapped up this morning. Fritz is the No. 3 seed at this tournament — not the No. 1 seed; that was Alex de Minaur, who fell in the quarterfinals. Fritz reached the final by defeating Brandon Nakashima 3-6, 6-3, 6-3 in the semis. He has been here before: this is his 23rd career ATP final, with experience at the biggest moments on the American hard court swing. Jódar, on the other side of the net, is 19 years old and entering the top tier of men's tennis in real time. The Spaniard beat Lorenzo Musetti (4 seed) in the quarterfinals, then came back from a first-set tiebreak loss against Tabilo in the semis to win 6-7(5), 6-4, 6-4. That was his second consecutive comeback win from a set down this week. By reaching this final, Jódar has already guaranteed himself an ATP Top 20 debut, regardless of what happens today. He won his first ATP title on clay in Marrakech in April. This would be his first on hard courts, and his first ATP 500 title. Fritz vs. Jódar. Experience against momentum. One of them becomes the 2026 DC Open champion today. Who wins: Fritz the veteran, or Jódar the teenager on the rise? --- ### Image Post Caption Fritz vs. Jódar. Rain moved it to Monday. WTA Toronto Opens — Venus Williams Falls, Sebov Wins for Canada
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post The National Bank Open is underway in Toronto, and Day 1 had everything: a veteran icon losing, a Canadian advancing, and rain causing havoc with the schedule. The headline result came from Kamilla Rakhimova of Uzbekistan, who defeated Venus Williams 6-4, 6-1 in a rain-delayed first-round match. Rakhimova won nine of the last 11 games once she found her rhythm, and Williams — 46 years old and a seven-time Grand Slam champion — was eliminated in the opening round. It is the kind of result that draws attention even on a packed tournament schedule. Canadian fans got a moment to cheer as well. Katherine Sebov, 27, defeated Aoi Ito of Japan 6-3, 6-4 to become the first Canadian player to advance in the 2026 NBO main draw. It was her first WTA 1000 win since Miami 2023. Another Canadian, wild card Ariana Arseneault, fell 6-2, 6-4 to Jessica Bouzas Maneiro of Spain. Sloane Stephens also earned her spot in the main draw via qualifying. The day's rain disruptions left some results uncertain. Bianca Andreescu's first-round match against Nikola Bartunkova was scheduled for Sunday evening but results were not confirmed at the time of writing. Top seeds Aryna Sabalenka (1) and Elena Rybakina (2) have first-round byes and open their tournaments beginning Tuesday. The 2026 National Bank Open in Toronto runs through August 13. Are you following the WTA Toronto draw? Who is your pick to win the title this week? --- ### Image Post Caption Venus falls on Day 1 in Toronto. Sebov advances for Canada. ### Long-Form Post The ATP Masters 1000 in Montreal opens its main draw tomorrow, Tuesday August 4 — and before a ball is struck, it is already a tournament defined by who is not there. Jannik Sinner, the world No. 1 who just successfully defended his Wimbledon title, is out with an elbow injury sustained at the All England Club. He is targeting Cincinnati. Carlos Alcaraz, No. 3 in the world, has been out since a wrist injury at the Barcelona Open in April. Novak Djokovic has also withdrawn. That is three of the most compelling players in men's tennis absent from what should be one of the summer's most important events. Into that void steps Alexander Zverev as the No. 1 seed, carrying the momentum of his 2026 Roland-Garros title and a Wimbledon final appearance. Felix Auger-Aliassime is seeded No. 2 — the first time in his career he has been seeded that high at a Masters 1000 — and he plays in front of a home Montreal crowd that will be fully behind him. Ben Shelton, the defending champion from the 2025 edition, is seeded No. 3. Daniil Medvedev, a 2021 Montreal champion, is seeded fifth. Zverev and Taylor Fritz are in the same quarter of the draw, setting up a potential quarterfinal meeting. Zverev beat Fritz in straight sets at Wimbledon. The tournament director was blunt in his frustration with the withdrawal wave, calling it an "absolute disaster" in public comments. The draw opens Tuesday. The tournament runs through August 13. Which player do you think emerges from Montreal? Zverev, FAA, Shelton — or someone outside the top seeds? --- ### Image Post Caption Montreal opens tomorrow. Zverev (1), FAA (2) in his home city. Hard Court Swing Begins — Sinner, Alcaraz, and the Road to the US Open
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Six weeks to the US Open. The hardcourt swing is underway. And tennis is playing out a fascinating transitional moment. Three of the world's top five men are sidelined. Sinner won Wimbledon in July but sustained an elbow injury in the process and is targeting Cincinnati as his return. Alcaraz has been out since April with a wrist injury and is also targeting Cincinnati, with the US Open as his primary goal. Djokovic has opted out of both Toronto and Montreal, going straight to Flushing Meadows. For those three players, the next six weeks are about recovery, preparation, and arriving healthy in New York. That leaves an unusual amount of open road for the next tier. Zverev — already a 2026 Grand Slam champion after Roland-Garros — enters Montreal as the clear favorite. FAA, Shelton, de Minaur, and Medvedev all have legitimate paths to a Masters 1000 title that they would rarely have when all the top names are in the draw. Then there is the DC Open ATP final happening right now in Washington, where one of Fritz or 19-year-old Rafael Jódar adds a 500-level title to their summer resume. The US Open begins August 24. By then, the field may look different — Sinner and Alcaraz are targeting returns — but the shape of who has played best in the hardcourt swing will be clearer. Montreal, Cincinnati, and whatever happens in Washington this afternoon will set the table. Who is your pick to win the US Open men's title? --- ### Image Post Caption Six weeks to the US Open.
