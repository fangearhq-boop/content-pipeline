Serena and Venus Williams Fall in Emotional Cincinnati Doubles Debut

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | August 18, 2026

The legendary sisters led 9-8 in the match tiebreak before a double fault ended their Cincinnati run — their first doubles match together since the 2022 US Open.

They came within a double fault of something magical.

Serena Williams (44) and Venus Williams (46) made their emotional return to doubles tennis together Monday night at the Cincinnati Open, playing their first match as a partnership since the 2022 US Open. They pushed Marta Kostyuk and Peyton Stearns to the limit before falling 2-6, 6-1, 10-8 in a match tiebreak that had the crowd at Lindner Family Tennis Centre holding its collective breath.

The sisters entered the tournament on a doubles wild card, and the anticipation surrounding their reunion — nearly four years in the making — was unlike anything Cincinnati had seen in years. From the moment they walked onto the court together, the night felt significant.

A Night of Swings and Heartbreak

The first set went to Kostyuk and Stearns in a hurry, 6-2, as the formidable pair — Kostyuk ranked around No. 10 in singles and Stearns a capable hard-court operator — controlled the tempo and kept errors to a minimum.

But the Williams sisters responded in the second set with something closer to the tennis that made them legends. They moved through it 6-1, and suddenly the capacity crowd began to dare themselves to dream. A match tiebreak would decide everything.

What followed was a rollercoaster. The sisters found themselves in position — leading 9-8 with a chance to win the match. But a Venus double fault on match point gave Kostyuk and Stearns the critical point they needed, and they closed it out 10-8 to complete the victory.

The Bigger Picture

The loss stings, but the moment matters. Serena last competed at Cincinnati in 2022. The Williams sisters last played doubles together at the 2022 US Open, a sentimental occasion that many assumed would be their final shared match on tour.

The fact that they came back — accepting a Cincinnati wild card, walking out under the lights, and taking a sharp Kostyuk/Stearns pairing to a match tiebreak — is the story in itself. These are athletes in their 40s who could have retired years ago and lived comfortably on their respective legacies. Instead, they keep showing up.

Venus carries a 0-14 singles record in 2026, and Serena has stepped back from the full WTA circuit. But in doubles, together, something changes. The energy was different. The crowd was different. The night was different.

What's Next

The Williams sisters' Cincinnati run is over, but their presence elevated the tournament and created a highlight that will be replayed for years. Both sisters are expected to continue monitoring the US Open schedule — Serena and Venus have both confirmed interest in the final Grand Slam of the year, which begins August 30 at the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center in New York.

For Kostyuk and Stearns, the Cincinnati doubles draw awaits as they advance to the next round.
