Medvedev's Asian Swing Ends in Shanghai Controversy — Jodar Knocks Him Out

By Marcus Cole

October 18, 2026 — Shanghai, China

Daniil Medvedev will not be in the Shanghai Masters final this year. The Russian was eliminated by Rafael Jodar in the semifinals after a match that went sideways well before the final point — and in a way that became more of the story than the tennis itself.

It is a difficult end to an Asian swing that had already cost him in Beijing. And it raises questions about how Medvedev handles pressure moments at a stage of his career when his results need to be speaking for themselves.

The Umpire Confrontation

The moment that defined the match came when Medvedev disputed a line call. He argued that a shot from Jodar had landed out. The umpire disagreed. The replay showed the ball had landed in — Jodar won the point.

Medvedev's response escalated beyond the normal frustration of a losing call. He clashed verbally with the umpire, questioned the decision at length, and at one point threatened to retire from the match. He did not retire. He played on — and lost the semifinal to the 20-year-old Spaniard.

Jodar's poise through the entire episode was notable. He allowed Medvedev's confrontation to play out, held his focus, and went on to win. That composure under unusual pressure is a quality that bodes well for what comes next in his career.

Beijing First, Then Shanghai

The Shanghai controversy did not arrive in isolation. Earlier in the Asian swing, Medvedev had been disqualified from his Beijing semifinal after hitting a ball that struck a spectator. He lost his prize money for the semifinal result and issued a public apology, calling the incident an accident.

Two incidents at back-to-back events, both drawing attention for the wrong reasons. Medvedev has built a career that includes genuine Grand Slam success and multiple deep runs at Masters events. The 2026 Asian swing will not be part of that legacy.

The Shanghai Streak Ends

For several consecutive years, Medvedev had reached the Shanghai Masters final. That run — cited in coverage as six consecutive appearances — is now over. He exits in the semifinals this time, beaten by Jodar, who went on to reach his first Masters 1000 final before falling to Fokina.

The outcome is a reminder that even the most consistent records in tennis have expiration dates. Medvedev's dominance at this particular event gave way this year to a younger player who had nothing to lose and executed under pressure.

What's Next

Daniil Medvedev's next opportunity is the Paris Rolex Masters, beginning October 26. It is the final ATP Masters 1000 event of the 2026 season and one of the last chances to influence year-end rankings. Medvedev will need to produce results on the court and, given the last two weeks, avoid the kind of incidents that draw the focus away from his tennis.
