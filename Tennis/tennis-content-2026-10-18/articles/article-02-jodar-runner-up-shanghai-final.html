At 20, Jodar Reaches Shanghai Final and Reshapes His Season's Meaning

By Elena Voss

October 18, 2026 — Shanghai, China

Rafael Jodar did not win the 2026 Shanghai Masters. He lost in the final to Alejandro Davidovich Fokina, 1-6, 7-6(5), 6-4, in a match where he led by a set and a break before the older Spaniard found his footing and turned it around.

And yet this week cannot be described as anything other than a breakthrough. At 20 years old — the No. 12 seed, ranked in the mid-twenties — Jodar defeated Casper Ruud in the quarterfinals and then upset Daniil Medvedev in a semifinal the world is still talking about. He reached his first Masters 1000 final. He leaves Shanghai a different player than he arrived.

The Semifinal Nobody Expected

Before the final, there was the semifinal against Medvedev. The match was competitive from the start, but it became memorable in a moment when Medvedev disputed a line call, clashed with the umpire, and threatened to retire from the match. The replay showed the ball had landed in — Jodar's point, the umpire's decision correct. Medvedev played on, but the dynamic had shifted.

Jodar, 20 years old and playing in his first Masters 1000 semifinal, kept his composure through the entire episode. He won the match. He won it against a player who had reached six consecutive Shanghai finals, a player ranked among the world's best for most of the past five years. That win alone would have been the story of his week under normal circumstances.

Then he made the final and took the first set 6-1.

A Final That Slipped Away

The first set was Jodar's in every respect. He broke early, served well, and won it in less than half an hour. He went up a break in the second set and had a genuine opportunity to close the match. Fokina, to his credit, fought back. The second-set tiebreak went to Fokina 7-5, the momentum swung, and the third set became a contest between the older Spaniard's growing confidence and the younger one's fading grip on the match.

Fokina broke for the title in the third. Jodar could not break back. Final score: 1-6, 7-6(5), 6-4 to Fokina.

Losing from a first-set lead in a Masters 1000 final at 20 is not a cautionary tale. It is a data point about a player who will be back here, or somewhere like here, many times. The question is not whether Jodar can reach these stages. He just proved he can. The question is what happens when he does it the next time.

The ATP Finals Picture

Jodar's Shanghai run has real implications for the season-ending ATP Finals in Turin. His ranking points from this week — a finalist result — place him in contention for the qualification spots still available. The Paris Rolex Masters, beginning October 26, represents his next major opportunity, and he arrives there with momentum that did not exist seven days ago.

For a 20-year-old with no prior Masters 1000 final on his record, that is a significant place to be heading into the season's final stretch.

What's Next

Rafael Jodar heads to the Paris Rolex Masters, the last ATP Masters 1000 event of the year, with his ATP Finals qualification still alive. His week in Shanghai — a quarterfinal win over Ruud, a semifinal win over Medvedev, and a runner-up finish — will be remembered as the moment when his career trajectory became undeniable. He is 20. This is early work.
