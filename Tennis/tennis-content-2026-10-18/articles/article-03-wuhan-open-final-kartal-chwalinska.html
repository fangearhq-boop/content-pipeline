Kartal vs. Chwalinska: Two First-Time Finalists Decide the Wuhan Open

By Ryan Calloway

October 18, 2026 — Wuhan, China

The 2026 Wuhan Open final pits Sonay Kartal against Maja Chwalinska in a matchup that nobody had on their bingo card entering the week. Kartal, ranked No. 170 in the world and returning from six months out with a back injury, beat Jelena Ostapenko in a grinding two-tiebreak semifinal. Chwalinska, ranked No. 22, defeated Iga Swiatek 7-5, 6-1 in a result that raised eyebrows across the tennis world.

It is the first WTA 1000 final for both players. Kartal is the lowest-ranked finalist in the WTA 1000 era, which began in 2009. The Wuhan Open, the final WTA 1000 event of 2026, will produce a first-time champion regardless of who wins.

How Kartal Got Here

Sonay Kartal's route to the final has been built on resilience that mirrors her broader season. Six months off with a back injury. A return to competition. Then, in Wuhan, a series of results that no one outside her team would have forecast.

Her semifinal against Ostapenko lasted two hours and 12 minutes. She won 7-6(11), 7-6(5) — two tiebreaks, a total of 88-75 points across the match. The scoreline is a testament to how close the match was throughout and how well Kartal held herself together in the decisive moments. Earlier in the week she had beaten Elina Svitolina, the No. 6 seed, for her first career win over a top-10 player.

Kartal, British and 24 years old, is guaranteed to jump into the world's top 50 in next week's rankings regardless of the final result. That alone marks the week as transformational for her career.

How Chwalinska Got Here

Maja Chwalinska's path ran through Iga Swiatek, the reigning world No. 1 and a player who had never lost a WTA 1000 semifinal on the hard courts of Wuhan before this week. Swiatek came into the match dealing with what appeared to be a leg issue. Chwalinska took advantage decisively: 7-5, 6-1, with the second set turning into a one-sided display of power hitting from the Polish 22-year-old.

It was Chwalinska's first WTA 1000 semifinal, and she won it with the authority of someone who belongs. The 2026 Roland Garros runner-up has been building through the year. This is the moment her clay-court form translates to the hard-court conversation at a major level.

A Final Without a Favorite

Kartal and Chwalinska have never played each other before. Neither player enters the final with experience at this stage of a WTA 1000 event. On paper, Chwalinska's ranking — No. 22 — and her recent run of results might make her a slight favorite. In practice, a player ranked No. 170 who has just beaten a former Roland Garros champion and a former world No. 6 in succession is not going to be undone by rankings alone.

The Wuhan Open is the final WTA 1000 of 2026. It closes the Asian swing and sets up the run to the WTA Finals in Guadalajara. The winner today adds a Masters title to their record and strengthens whatever case they are building for the end of the season.

What's Next

The Wuhan Open final is scheduled for Sunday, October 18. The result will determine which of Kartal or Chwalinska becomes the first player to claim this title as their debut WTA 1000 champion. Both players' rankings will move significantly following the result, with implications for WTA Finals qualification and the end-of-year rankings picture. The WTA Finals take place in Guadalajara later this year.
