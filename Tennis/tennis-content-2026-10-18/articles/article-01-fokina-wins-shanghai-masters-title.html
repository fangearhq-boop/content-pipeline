Fokina Wins Shanghai Masters in Comeback Final Over Jodar

By Marcus Cole

October 18, 2026 — Shanghai, China

Alejandro Davidovich Fokina is the 2026 Shanghai Masters champion. The Spaniard came back from a set and a break down to defeat fellow Spaniard Rafael Jodar 1-6, 7-6(5), 6-4 on Sunday, earning his first ATP Masters 1000 title in a match that lasted approximately two hours and 27 minutes.

It is the biggest title of Fokina's career — his third of the year and the one that confirms he belongs at the highest level of the game. He leaves Shanghai ranked a career-high No. 13 in the world and becomes the first Spanish player to ever lift the Shanghai Masters trophy.

Down, Then Back Up

The first set was not what Fokina needed. Jodar, the 20-year-old who had made this run entirely on his own terms, came out with relentless power and won the opener 6-1 in just 27 minutes. He went up a break in the second, and for a spell, the match looked like it might be heading toward an improbable title for the teenager.

Fokina refused to let it happen. He broke back, held his serve, and forced a tiebreak. In the tiebreak, the margins were minimal — he won it 7-5, and the complexion of the match shifted. Where the first set had belonged entirely to Jodar, the second-set recovery gave Fokina something to build on.

The third set confirmed it. Fokina played with control and aggression, found the break at the right moment, and closed out 6-4 to complete a comeback that had looked uncertain through the first hour of play.

A Milestone With Historical Weight

Winning in Shanghai carries specific meaning in the context of Spanish tennis. Spain has produced 16 ATP Masters 1000 champions; Fokina is now the 16th Spaniard to achieve the milestone and the first to do it at this event. The Shanghai Masters has existed in its current form since 2009, and in that time, Rafael Nadal claimed it once — in 2019 — but no Spaniard had won it since. Fokina's name is the second on that list.

More broadly, it is a statement about where his career is heading. At 26, Fokina has spent stretches of the past few years in the range of No. 20 to No. 30, talented enough to threaten the top players on any given day but without the consistency to break through at the highest level. A Masters 1000 title changes that conversation. His ranking will reflect it. His confidence going into the Paris Masters and the end of the season should reflect it, too.

A Narrow Margin Over a Worthy Opponent

Jodar, 20 years old and ranked No. 12 seed in Shanghai, made this final happen through his own merit. He had beaten Casper Ruud in the quarterfinals and then defeated Daniil Medvedev in a semifinal that came with significant controversy — Medvedev clashed with the umpire over a line call and threatened to retire from the match. Jodar navigated all of it and reached his first Masters 1000 final.

Losing in the final to a player who came back from 1-6, 0-2 down is not a failure. Jodar played the more aggressive first set and had every opportunity to close the match out earlier. Fokina simply produced more under pressure, and that is the difference between winning and losing at this level.

What's Next

Alejandro Davidovich Fokina begins the Paris Rolex Masters — the last ATP Masters 1000 event of 2026 — on October 26 as a champion. His No. 13 ranking puts him firmly in contention for the ATP Finals in Turin, though the field is tight. Rafael Jodar, despite falling in the final, leaves Shanghai with an enhanced ATP Finals case and a ranking boost that reflects a week of genuine results.
