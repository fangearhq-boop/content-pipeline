Auger-Aliassime Withdraws From Home Masters Event With Back Injury He Can't Serve Through

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | August 9, 2026

Felix Auger-Aliassime was the No. 2 seed at his home Masters 1000 tournament for the first time in his career. He will not be playing a single match.

Auger-Aliassime withdrew from the ATP National Bank Open in Montreal on Sunday with a back injury that prevented him from serving. The injury began during practice and did not improve over time. Tournament organizers gave him an additional 24 hours to recover. After that window passed, FAA's answer was the same: he could not compete.

"Essentially, I couldn't serve, so it's impossible for me to play at the level that the competition requires," Auger-Aliassime said in his withdrawal statement.

A Career Milestone Missed

The No. 2 seeding at a Masters 1000 event was a first for Auger-Aliassime. In a draw that was already missing Jannik Sinner, Carlos Alcaraz, and Novak Djokovic — all absent from the Canadian swing — FAA had reached a seeding that reflected both his ranking and the opened-up nature of the field.

Montreal is also his home tournament. Auger-Aliassime is from Montreal. He has competed here repeatedly through his career with the kind of crowd support that few players receive at any tournament in the world. The combination of home advantage, career-best seeding, and a wide-open draw made this one of the most significant Montreal opportunities of his career.

He will not get to play it. The back injury did not give him a choice.

Another Name on the Injury List

FAA joins a growing list of players who have exited the Canadian swing early this week. Alcaraz withdrew from Cincinnati due to wrist tenosynovitis that has kept him out since April. Jasmine Paolini withdrew from both Washington and Toronto with a foot injury. Emma Raducanu did not appear in Toronto. Ajla Tomljanovic announced on Sunday that she is ending her 2026 season entirely.

The summer hard-court swing — from the Canadian Open through Cincinnati to the US Open — is the most physically demanding stretch of the calendar. The compressed schedule, the surface transition from clay to hard courts, and the heat of North American summers produce exactly the kind of sudden injuries that have taken multiple players out of Montreal and Toronto this week.

For FAA, the timing is particularly difficult. Back injuries are notoriously unpredictable in terms of recovery timeline. A player who cannot serve on one day can sometimes return to full function within a week; other injuries in the same category require months. Nothing has been confirmed about the severity of his condition or a return timeline.

What's Next

Auger-Aliassime's next scheduled event after Montreal would be the Cincinnati Open, which begins on August 13. Whether he can compete there depends on how his back responds over the coming days. Beyond Cincinnati, the US Open starts on August 24, and FAA's direct entry into the main draw gives him time to assess his condition without the pressure of qualifying. Montreal will be covered by other players this week. The question for FAA and his team is how quickly they can get him back on court in preparation for Flushing Meadows.
