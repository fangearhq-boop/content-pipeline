Alexandrova Cracks the Sabalenka Code Again to End Toronto Title Defense

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | August 9, 2026

Ekaterina Alexandrova has figured out Aryna Sabalenka twice now. On Saturday at the WTA National Bank Open in Toronto, she did it on the most unforgiving stage of the summer.

Alexandrova defeated No. 1 seed Sabalenka 7-6(3), 4-6, 6-4 in the Round of 16, eliminating the defending champion and world No. 1 from the tournament in two hours and 29 minutes. It is the biggest upset of the Canadian swing and one of the more significant results of the WTA season.

The scoreline tells the story with reasonable accuracy: a tense first-set tiebreak that Alexandrova controlled, a second set where Sabalenka regrouped and found her rhythm, and a deciding third where the pressure landed on the world No. 1 and did not move until it was too late.

The Second Time Is the Statement

The first time Alexandrova beat Sabalenka was in Doha in 2025. That result was notable but could be filed as an outlier — a bad day for a dominant player, the kind of loss that gets explained away and rarely repeated. What happened Saturday cannot be explained in those terms.

Sabalenka had won this tournament in 2025. She arrived in Toronto as the world No. 1, the top seed, and the most dominant hard-court player in the women's game over the past two seasons. Alexandrova had one win over her in a dozen attempts. The expectation, reasonable and widely shared, was that this would look like most of the other meetings.

It did not. Alexandrova controlled the first-set tiebreak 7-3, which required execution under pressure against a player who rarely loses that kind of moment. Sabalenka found answers in the second set and levelled the match. In the third, Alexandrova held through the pressure and closed 6-4. The head-to-head, which once seemed so lopsided, is now level: 5-5.

What This Means for Sabalenka

The Toronto title defence is over. That is the immediate headline, but it carries weight for reasons beyond this week. Sabalenka has been the most reliable performer on hard courts all season, and results like this one are rare.

The deeper question is whether Saturday represents a genuine vulnerability or an exceptional performance from Alexandrova. The answer is almost certainly both. There are specific patterns in how Alexandrova constructs her game against aggressive baseliners — she redirects pace rather than generating it, she uses the angles, she does not flinch in tiebreaks. Against Sabalenka's power, that approach can work if the execution holds. On Saturday, it held.

The WTA rankings will update on Monday. Sabalenka losing here does not threaten her position at No. 1, but the points from defending a WTA 1000 title will not arrive this week.

Alexandrova into the Quarterfinals

Alexandrova advances to the Toronto quarterfinals, which begin on Monday. She has now beaten the world No. 1 twice in career meetings and is playing the kind of tennis that earns deep runs at this level. Her quarter of the draw had been considered Sabalenka's to navigate without serious resistance. It no longer looks that way.

The rest of the Toronto field, including Swiatek, Gauff, and Rybakina, now has one fewer obstacle to work around. For Alexandrova, the challenge is what to do with momentum this significant. She has proved she can solve the code. The question now is whether she can keep solving problems as the week gets harder.

What's Next

Alexandrova moves into the Toronto quarterfinals on Monday or Tuesday. Sabalenka's season continues, though the Canadian Open title she won in 2025 will not be successfully defended. The US Open begins on August 24, and the women's draw just became more open than it was 24 hours ago. After Saturday in Toronto, nobody should be surprised if Alexandrova is still competing deep into next week.
