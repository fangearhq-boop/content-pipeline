Swiatek Exorcises Roland Garros Ghost With Statement Win Over Kostyuk in Toronto

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | August 9, 2026

The first set went exactly the wrong way. Marta Kostyuk, the player who had knocked Iga Swiatek out of Roland Garros in May, was sharp from the start, comfortable, and won the opener 6-3. Familiar patterns. Familiar discomfort.

Then Swiatek won 12 of the next 15 games.

The final score in the WTA National Bank Open Toronto Round of 16 was 3-6, 6-1, 6-2, and the message it sent about Swiatek's hard-court form was unambiguous. "I'm in a different place for sure," she said after the match. It was a short sentence carrying significant weight.

The Roland Garros Context

When Kostyuk beat Swiatek at Roland Garros in May, it was one of the bigger results of the clay season. Kostyuk, a player who has never been afraid of the moment, controlled that match with an efficiency that surprised even her own supporters. Swiatek, the four-time Roland Garros champion and a player who defines herself by her performances on clay, left Paris earlier than expected.

The first set in Toronto on Saturday might have looked like a continuation of that story. Kostyuk was accurate, physical, and precise. She won it comfortably. The match seemed to be unfolding in a recognizable direction.

What Swiatek did next was not a comeback in the dramatic sense — it was more like a recalibration. She stopped the errors. She found her depth. She made Kostyuk work for every point in a way the Ukrainian had not been required to work in the first set. By the time the second set ended 6-1, it was already clear that the match had fundamentally changed.

Dropping Three Games From a Set Down

The numbers from the second and third sets are stark. After losing the opener, Swiatek dropped just three games across the final two sets combined. A 6-1, 6-2 finish, starting from behind, requires a particular kind of mental composure — the ability to treat a set deficit as information rather than as damage.

Kostyuk is not a player who collapses easily. She has beaten Swiatek before. She has experience in these situations and the game to compete at this level on hard courts. What happened in sets two and three was not Kostyuk losing her level — it was Swiatek raising hers, sharply and without apology.

The quote after the match — "I'm in a different place for sure" — suggests that Swiatek is not just playing better but thinking differently about these encounters. Whatever adjustment she made between the first and second sets, it worked with enough authority that the match was effectively decided by the midpoint of the second.

Toronto Quarterfinals Await

Swiatek advances to the Toronto quarterfinals, joining Alexandrova and a draw that has already produced its biggest story of the week. Sabalenka, the No. 1 seed and defending champion, was eliminated earlier on Saturday. The women's side of the Canadian Open is fully open now, and Swiatek's form suggests she is one of the genuine contenders.

The US Open begins on August 24. A deep run in Toronto would give Swiatek match volume, form, and a narrative of momentum coming out of a Roland Garros exit that her season needed to answer. Saturday was a good start to that answer.

What's Next

Swiatek plays in the Toronto quarterfinals on Monday or Tuesday. On the other side of the draw, Alexandrova's upset of Sabalenka changes the landscape for the rest of the field. For Swiatek, the challenge is maintaining the quality of sets two and three across harder opponents than Kostyuk. If she can, a return to the WTA 1000 final — and the kind of form she needs heading into Flushing Meadows — is entirely achievable.
