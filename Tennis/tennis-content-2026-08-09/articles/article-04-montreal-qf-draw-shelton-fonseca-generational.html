Montreal QF Draw Is Wide Open: Shelton, Fonseca, Tien, Mensik, Van de Zandschulp Set to Decide a Masters Title

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | August 9, 2026

When Ben Shelton defended his Canadian Open title in Montreal last year, he was the youngest champion at a Masters 1000 in a generation. This week, the draw forming around him looks like the one he created — a field full of young, fearless players who have decided the established order does not apply to them.

Saturday's Round of 16 results completed most of the Montreal bracket. Botic van de Zandschulp defeated Hubert Hurkacz 3-6, 7-6(4), 7-5. Learner Tien dismantled Tommy Paul 6-3, 6-2. Jakub Mensik edged Terence Atmane 7-5, 6-4. Combined with earlier eliminations of Zverev, Medvedev, Fritz, and Ruud, the tournament has arrived at the most wide-open Masters 1000 quarterfinal field in recent memory.

Quarterfinals begin Monday and continue Tuesday.

The Saturday Results in Full

Van de Zandschulp's win over Hurkacz was the last significant upset in a week defined by them. Hurkacz is a former Masters 1000 champion and a player ranked inside the top 10. Van de Zandschulp is unseeded in Montreal and has spent most of his career below the upper tier of the tour. To win a third-set decider against Hurkacz after dropping the first set requires something more than good fortune. The Dutchman earned that result.

Tien's 6-3, 6-2 win over Paul was straightforward in the scoreline and emphatically earned. Tommy Paul is a top-20 player and a proven Masters 1000 competitor. Tien — the 12th seed — won the match with a precision and efficiency that suggested someone playing well above his ranking. He has been one of the tournament's more consistent performers.

Mensik's win over Atmane kept the 13th seed in the bracket. Mensik, one of the more technically accomplished young players on the ATP Tour, has quietly reached the quarterfinals without significant fuss. The Czech has work to do in terms of name recognition, but the results have been accumulating.

Shelton and the Question of Experience

Ben Shelton is the only player left in the Montreal draw ranked inside the top 10. He is also the defending champion — a detail that carries meaning in a tournament where the draw has been stripped of its traditional heavyweights.

Shelton has been the tournament's most dominant performer. He has not dropped a set. He dispatched Bergs 6-3, 6-2 in the third round with a ruthlessness that looked entirely intentional. The serve, which is among the best on the tour, has functioned the way it needs to — as a platform, not just a weapon.

The question about Shelton in these situations has always been whether the mental experience of having already won a Masters 1000 title changes how he processes pressure. The evidence from this week suggests the answer is yes. He is playing with a confidence that goes beyond natural ability. He has been here before, and he is comfortable with what being here requires.

The Generational Opportunity

The players who will contest the remaining spots in Montreal's quarterfinals are, broadly speaking, not players who have held Masters 1000 trophies. Van de Zandschulp, Tien, and Mensik are names still accumulating major-tournament credibility. They are here because they played well enough to beat players who had established themselves above their level.

That is what makes this Montreal draw genuinely interesting rather than just statistically unusual. The top seeds fell, but the players who replaced them have not been pushed along by circumstance — they earned their results. Fonseca beat Tsitsipas and Ruud back-to-back. Tien rolled Paul. VdZ beat Hurkacz in a three-setter that required a comeback from a set down.

The quarterfinals will determine which of these players can sustain this level for one more round — and which ones were here because the draw gave them an opportunity rather than because they are ready to win the whole thing.

What's Next

Quarterfinals begin Monday in Montreal. Shelton, Fonseca, Tien, Mensik, and Van de Zandschulp headline the remaining field. The semifinals follow on Wednesday, with the final on Sunday, August 16. The winner will have earned a Masters 1000 title by beating a field that eliminated No. 1 Zverev, No. 5 Medvedev, Fritz, Hurkacz, Ruud, and Paul along the way. That credit should not be diminished by the upsets — if anything, the bracket proves the opportunity was there for players willing to take it.
