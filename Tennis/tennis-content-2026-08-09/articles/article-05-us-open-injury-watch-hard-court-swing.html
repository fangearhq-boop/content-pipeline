US Open Injury Watch: Alcaraz, Tomljanovic, Paolini, Raducanu All Out — Six Weeks to Flushing Meadows

By Marcus Cole | Tennis Fanrecap | August 9, 2026

The US Open begins on August 24. As of this weekend in Montreal and Toronto, the injury list heading into the hard-court swing has never been longer at this point in the calendar year.

Four significant names have either confirmed absences or ended their seasons entirely. The players who remain healthy have a draw that will look different than anyone anticipated in early summer. The questions that are already forming around Flushing Meadows are less about who will win and more about who will even make it there.

Carlos Alcaraz: Cincinnati Withdrawal, US Open in Doubt

The defending US Open champion has not played a competitive match since April. Alcaraz withdrew from the Cincinnati Open this week due to continuing right wrist tenosynovitis — the same injury that kept him out of Roland Garros and Wimbledon. He had been added to the Cincinnati entry list, which briefly raised hopes of an early return. Those hopes were extinguished when the withdrawal was confirmed.

Alcaraz will almost certainly arrive at the US Open without competitive match preparation since early spring. Whether that is survivable depends entirely on how his wrist has healed and how quickly his sharpness returns on hard courts against opponents playing tournament tennis every week. The best players in the world can find their level without a full competitive runway, but the defending champion skipping his only meaningful warm-up before a Slam is genuinely unprecedented in recent years.

Ajla Tomljanovic: Season Over

On Sunday, Ajla Tomljanovic announced she is ending her 2026 season. Her statement was direct: "It has been a tough year battling minor injuries, so I've decided to end my season earlier than planned to be healthy and ready for 2027." Her last match was at Wimbledon in the first round, where she lost to Ekaterine Bolkvadze.

Tomljanovic has fought injury throughout her career, including a significant knee issue that affected multiple seasons. This is not the same situation, but the pattern of accumulated strain — "minor injuries" across a full season — has produced the same outcome: a decision to rest rather than risk compounding damage before next year's calendar begins.

Jasmine Paolini: Foot Injury, Toronto Withdrawal

Jasmine Paolini withdrew from both WTA Washington and the Toronto National Bank Open with a foot injury she has been managing since Wimbledon. "Unfortunately, I have decided to withdraw from Washington and Toronto so that I can give my foot a little more time to rest," she said.

Paolini reached the Wimbledon quarterfinals this year and had been one of the women's game's most consistent performers entering the summer swing. A foot injury that pulls her out of two consecutive hard-court events is concerning context for US Open preparation. She is expected to target a return at Cincinnati, which begins on August 13 — giving her roughly ten days before the US Open to prove she is ready.

Emma Raducanu: Toronto Withdrawal

Emma Raducanu's name appeared in the WTA Toronto withdrawal list this weekend. The British No. 1 has been managing a grade-4 stress fracture in her right lower leg since being diagnosed earlier this summer. At the time of diagnosis, her team identified the US Open start date of August 30 as the earliest realistic return timeline.

She remains on that recovery path. The Toronto withdrawal is consistent with a controlled rehabilitation approach, and her situation is different from the others on this list in that the injury has been known and managed with a specific target in mind. Whether she makes it to Flushing Meadows on schedule is still an open question.

What's Next

Cincinnati begins on August 13, giving players two weeks of hard-court competition before the US Open draw is made. Paolini and potentially Raducanu are the names to watch for return confirmation. Alcaraz's presence at Cincinnati, now confirmed as absent, would have been the best indicator of his US Open readiness — that window is closed. The US Open's defending champion will arrive in New York without a competitive match since April. The draw, the health, and the results from Cincinnati over the next two weeks will clarify what the Flushing Meadows field actually looks like heading into August 24.
