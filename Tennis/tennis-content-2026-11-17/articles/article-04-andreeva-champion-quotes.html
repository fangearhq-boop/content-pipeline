Champion Quotes: Andreeva's Post-WTA Finals Reflections Reveal a Fierce Competitor

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | November 17, 2026

Mirra Andreeva lifted the WTA Finals trophy in Indian Wells on Sunday evening and said all the right things — except that none of them were the kind of polished non-answers that athletes reach for on a big stage. She was direct, precise, and occasionally funny. The quotes that emerged from her champion's press conference and on-court interview are, in several ways, a better portrait of her than the tennis itself.

"She Is Not Getting the Win"

The defining line came when she was asked about the hardest moment in the final against Elina Svitolina — the point in the first set when Svitolina broke back and the match seemed to tighten. Andreeva's answer: "It was the second time in my life where I just thought I am going to die on the court but she is not getting the win."

That sentence requires very little context to land. It is not the language of someone recounting a moment of doubt. It is the language of someone who, in the middle of the most stressful situation she had encountered that day, turned inward and made a decision. The outcome bore her out. She won the first set 7-5 and the second 6-3, completing a tournament in which she won five matches without dropping a single set.

"I Want to Thank Myself"

Her first words on the court, before the microphone had properly been raised to her, were: "I want to thank myself." Social media had something to say about that immediately. Some found it arrogant; most found it refreshing — a 19-year-old who had just produced one of the best tournament runs in WTA Finals history, saying plainly that she deserved credit for it.

She did go on to thank her coach Conchita Martinez, her team, and her family. But the opening statement stayed in the conversation, partly because it fit so neatly with how she plays — a player who trusts her own decision-making under pressure and rarely looks to external anchors to explain what she does.

"Consistent, Happy and Work"

When asked to summarize her 2026 season in one phrase, she offered three words: "consistent, happy and work." It is the kind of answer that becomes more interesting the more you think about it. Consistency is a craft quality — it describes what her game produced across a full season. Happiness is a mental state — it reflects the environment she and her team built around the results. Work is the method. The three are listed in the order that makes the most sense if you are being honest about what it actually takes to get from ranked outside the top 50 to WTA No. 2 in the span of one season.

"I Am Going to Celebrate, Get a Car"

Perhaps the most relatable moment came when she was asked about her post-tournament plans. "I am going to celebrate, get a car," she said. The WTA Finals prize package includes a Mercedes-AMG SL 55, and Andreeva, apparently, was already thinking about that. She is 19. It is a reasonable thing to look forward to.

What's Next

Andreeva now enters the off-season as the WTA's freshest champion and its No. 2-ranked player. The Australian Open hard-court swing begins in January, and she will arrive as one of the most closely watched players in the draw. After this week, the attention is entirely earned — and if her post-match remarks are any guide, she is ready for it.
