ATP Finals Day 3: Fils vs Jodar Must-Win, Then Zverev vs Shelton in McEnroe Group

By Elena Voss | Tennis Fanrecap | November 17, 2026

The ATP Finals in Turin enters its third day with the John McEnroe Group providing the drama. Two matches are on the schedule, and they carry very different stakes. In the afternoon, Arthur Fils and Rafael Jodar meet in a match that neither player can afford to lose. In the evening, Alexander Zverev and Ben Shelton — both 1-0 after Day 1 wins — face off in what shapes up as a potential group-stage preview of the knockout rounds.

Fils vs Jodar: The Elimination Match

Arthur Fils lost his opening match to Ben Shelton 6-2, 3-6, 6-3 on Day 1. Rafael Jodar, the first player to qualify for the ATP Finals in his debut year on tour, fell to Alexander Zverev 6-4, 6-4. For both men, another loss ends any realistic path to the semifinals.

Their head-to-head record adds an interesting layer. Jodar leads 2-1, with both of his wins this year coming in straight sets on hard courts — the same surface they are playing on in Turin. That said, Fils, the first Frenchman at the ATP Finals since Gaël Monfils in 2016, has shown the capacity to turn a rivalry in a single performance, and he will be motivated coming off a first-match defeat.

Jodar, meanwhile, is operating in territory no player has entered before. Making the ATP Finals in his debut tour year is itself a remarkable achievement, but the draw has not been kind. He beat a World No. 1 during his qualifying run; now he needs to beat a World No. 6 in Turin to stay in contention.

Zverev vs Shelton: The H2H Question

The evening session belongs to two players who entered Day 3 at 1-0. Alexander Zverev, the World No. 1 and two-time ATP Finals champion, dispatched Jodar 6-4, 6-4 in his opener. Ben Shelton, ranked No. 3, outlasted Fils 6-2, 3-6, 6-3 in a tense three-setter.

The head-to-head is emphatically in Zverev's favor: 6-0 in career matches, including a five-set victory over Shelton in the 2026 US Open final. Numbers that one-sided rarely come without explanation. Zverev's left-handed serve sits up awkwardly for Shelton's return patterns, and Zverev's baseline consistency tends to neutralize the American's power game when he is at his best indoors.

Shelton, however, is not a player who is easily deterred by head-to-head records. His serve is one of the fastest on the ATP Tour, his athleticism is elite, and he has beaten world-class opponents in high-pressure moments before. The 6-0 mark will require an answer eventually; Shelton may decide Tuesday evening is the moment to provide one.

What's Next

Wednesday's matches will determine how the McEnroe Group resolves. The Day 3 results set up the final round of group play, in which every match carries semifinal implications. If Zverev beats Shelton tonight and Fils beats Jodar, the final round will feature four players with something at stake. If the favorites hold, the McEnroe Group picture could be nearly settled by the end of this week.
