Alcaraz Dismantles Tiafoe 6-2, 6-2 to Open ATP Finals — Opponent Calls Him "Superman"

By Ryan Calloway | Tennis Fanrecap | November 17, 2026

Carlos Alcaraz arrived in Turin with a 10-match winning streak and a reputation for raising his level at big events. On Monday evening at the Inalpi Arena, he did exactly that — opening his ATP Finals campaign with a 6-2, 6-2 dismantling of Frances Tiafoe in just 69 minutes. It was the kind of performance that leaves little room for analysis and plenty for appreciation.

Tiafoe, one of the more candid voices on the ATP Tour, delivered the most fitting summary in his post-match press conference: "It's unfair — he's like Superman." The comparison was not intended as a complaint. It was a recognition of what Alcaraz is producing right now, and what his opponents are facing when they walk onto the same court.

How the Match Unfolded

Alcaraz converted 4 of 5 break point opportunities on Tiafoe's serve, dictating nearly every exchange from the baseline while maintaining the kind of footwork that makes his shot selection look effortless. Tiafoe made it competitive in short bursts — his power game has the capacity to unsettle most players — but Alcaraz neutralized the best of it and punished anything short.

The first set was settled quickly. Alcaraz raced ahead, broke early and often, and held firm when Tiafoe pushed back. The second set followed the same pattern. At no point did the match feel like it was in doubt. The 69-minute clock was not a reflection of a close contest; it was simply the time it took for someone playing this well to close out an opponent.

For Tiafoe, making his ATP Finals debut, the result stings, but the stage is not unfamiliar territory. He has beaten top-five players before and reached the US Open semifinals in 2022. He faces Flavio Cobolli next, in a match that both players will need to win to keep their semifinal hopes alive.

Alcaraz's October-November Run

The 10-match winning streak now on the line in Turin did not arrive by accident. Alcaraz won the Vienna and Paris Masters events back-to-back in October and early November, and his results in those two events suggested he had found something in his indoor game — a surface that has historically suited some of his rivals more than him.

In Paris, he beat Ben Shelton in the final 7-6(5), 7-5. In Vienna, he was similarly dominant. The pattern across both events was relentless first-strike tennis: aggressive serving, early baseline pressure, and a willingness to attack short balls with the kind of conviction that leaves opponents guessing rather than preparing.

None of that has changed in Turin. If anything, Monday's performance suggested the form has been maintained, not simply carried over. Alcaraz looked sharp from the first point and never let up.

What's Next

Alcaraz is 1-0 in the Ivan Lendl Group and faces Daniil Medvedev on Wednesday in what could serve as a de facto group-stage decider. Medvedev also opened with a clinical win over Cobolli, leaving both players undefeated after the first round of group matches.

An Alcaraz-Medvedev clash at the ATP Finals carries obvious weight — the two have met in Grand Slam finals before, and their styles produce compelling tennis. If the form Alcaraz showed on Monday carries into Wednesday, it will take something very specific to slow him down.
