2026 Singapore Grand Prix: Race Day Preview — Verstappen on Pole, Russell From the Back, Storms Incoming

By Ryan Calloway | October 11, 2026 | F1 Fanrecap

The lights go out at Marina Bay Street Circuit at 1pm BST (8am ET) today for the 2026 Singapore Grand Prix, and the race could not have a more charged starting point. Max Verstappen leads from pole position after qualifying. George Russell starts from the very back of the 20-car grid. Thunderstorms are forecast. And Kimi Antonelli, the 19-year-old who leads the Drivers' Championship by 89 points, will be tracking every position from inside the cockpit of his Mercedes.

Sixty-two laps around one of Formula 1's most demanding circuits. Everything is on the line.

Verstappen Hunts His Second Singapore Victory in 48 Hours

It has been a remarkable Singapore weekend for Max Verstappen and Red Bull. He won Saturday's rain-hit sprint race from pole, and he lines up again at the front of the grid for today's Grand Prix. If Verstappen converts this into victory, it would be a double win in one of the most prestigious race weekends on the calendar.

For Verstappen, the championship picture is complex but interesting. He sits fifth in the standings, but a strong Sunday result — especially if it combines with frontrunners stumbling — could move him back into mathematical contention heading into the final rounds of the season.

Russell's Near-Impossible Task

George Russell's Singapore weekend has been a study in compounding misfortune. He qualified second for the sprint on Friday, appeared well-placed to recover championship points from Antonelli. Then the sprint started in the rain, and Russell crashed out on lap 1. Then came the penalty announcement: Mercedes has exceeded its permitted engine component allocation, and Russell will take a grid drop to start the Grand Prix from 20th.

Marina Bay Street Circuit is one of the hardest tracks in Formula 1 to overtake. The concrete barriers allow no room for error, the corners are slow and technical, and the racing line through most sections is narrow and defined. Starting 20th here is not the same as starting 20th at Monza or Spa. Every pass will require patience, perfect execution, and significant assistance from the safety car or from other drivers' misfortune.

Russell has performed miracles before in his career. Today, he needs another one.

The Weather Factor

Thunderstorms are forecast for race day at Marina Bay — and that is not a small detail in Singapore. Saturday's sprint was held in wet conditions and produced chaos almost immediately, with Russell's crash and the McLaren incident between Norris and Piastri reshaping the race within the first few laps.

If the Grand Prix starts or transitions to wet conditions, overtaking becomes slightly more achievable and strategy becomes far more complex. Rain in Singapore has historically produced some of the most dramatic moments the sport has seen. A safety car in the right window could theoretically help Russell recover multiple positions in a single lap.

Whether the storms arrive during the race or hold off entirely, teams will have contingency plans prepared. The tyre call — and when to make it — could decide the result as much as any pure pace advantage.

What's Next

After Singapore, the F1 calendar continues with five more races through the end of the season. The United States Grand Prix in Austin (approximately November 1) is the next major milestone in the championship picture — and potentially the venue where Antonelli could clinch the 2026 Drivers' title if today goes his way.

For now, the focus is entirely on Marina Bay. Sixty-two laps. Storms incoming. The championship on the edge. This is what Formula 1 is for.
