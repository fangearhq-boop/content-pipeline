Norris Warns of 2026 Tire Challenge at Las Vegas: 'Extremely Narrow Operating Window'

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | November 14, 2026

Fresh off his Brazilian Grand Prix victory, Lando Norris is already focused on Las Vegas — and his warning about the 2026 cars' narrow tire operating window suggests the Strip circuit could produce significant unpredictability next weekend.

Lando Norris does not wait long to analyze the next challenge, even after a race victory. Following his win at Interlagos — a wet, technically demanding race that saw him start from pole and lead 14.6 seconds over Max Verstappen at the flag — the McLaren driver has turned his attention to Las Vegas with a specific caution for all teams: the 2026 cars are going to find the Strip circuit particularly demanding.

At the heart of the concern is the tire operating window. The 2026 F1 technical regulations brought sweeping changes to how the cars generate downforce and how they interact with the tire compounds. Norris's experience suggests that window — the temperature range within which the tires perform optimally — is notably tight compared with what drivers were accustomed to in previous generations of machinery.

Why Las Vegas Amplifies the Problem

Las Vegas presents a unique set of challenges that make tire temperature management particularly complex. The circuit runs at night, when ambient temperatures drop and tarmac conditions can shift significantly across a race distance. Street circuits also tend to offer less consistent grip than permanent racing facilities, which creates additional uncertainty in predicting how tires will behave from lap to lap.

When you combine a narrow operating window with a cooling night circuit, an evolving tarmac surface, and the extreme speeds of the Strip's long straights followed by heavy braking zones, you have a recipe for teams getting it wrong in ways they did not expect. Norris's warning is a signal that McLaren has already identified this as a risk and is beginning its Las Vegas weekend strategy from this week.

McLaren's Approach

McLaren arrives in Las Vegas with a heavily-upgraded car that clearly worked well in the Brazilian conditions. Whether the same package can be tuned for Las Vegas is the question their engineers will be working to answer this week. The team will spend significant time in the simulator preparing setup configurations that account for the tire challenge Norris described.

Norris himself is in strong form — his Brazil victory was his third of the season and ended a seven-race winless drought that had been building pressure on the team. That win demonstrated his ability to perform in challenging conditions, which may actually make him a stronger candidate to manage Las Vegas despite the tire complexity he is flagging.

What's Next

Las Vegas practice sessions in a week's time will offer the first real data on how the 2026 cars interact with the Strip circuit under race conditions. How teams respond to that data — and how drivers adapt to whatever the tire compounds deliver on the night — will be a primary storyline of the race weekend.

Norris has warned the paddock. Now comes the race to find the solution first.
