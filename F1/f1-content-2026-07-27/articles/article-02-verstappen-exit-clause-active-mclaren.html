Verstappen's Red Bull Exit Clause Is Now Active — McLaren Move Enters Closing Stages

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | July 27, 2026

Max Verstappen can now leave Red Bull Racing. The exit clause written into his contract — which allows him to trigger a move in 2027 if he is outside the top two positions in the Drivers' Championship at the summer break — has officially become active following the conclusion of the 2026 Hungarian Grand Prix.

Verstappen finished sixth in the championship standings at the break. The condition is met. The door is open.

Reports from SportBible described Verstappen's discussions with McLaren as being in the "closing stages" of what has been a lengthy and carefully managed negotiation. And in a development that feels increasingly like a precursor to a broader team move, Red Bull has already confirmed that Gianpiero Lambiase — Verstappen's long-time race engineer and one of the most respected technical voices in the paddock — will leave to join McLaren as Chief Racing Officer ahead of the 2028 season.

How the Exit Clause Works

Verstappen's Red Bull contract runs through 2028. However, a clause embedded in that deal allows him to trigger an exit to 2027 under the condition that he is not within the top two positions in the Drivers' Championship at the start of the summer break. The clause reflects a reality of high-performance F1 contracts: top drivers often insist on performance-based opt-outs to ensure they are not locked into a team that has fallen behind competitively.

Red Bull tried to neutralize the clause in June. The team held contract talks with Verstappen in which they reportedly offered to buy out the escape clause. Verstappen refused. More recently, reports indicated Red Bull was exploring restructuring the contract to remove the clause entirely — but that attempt also appears to have failed.

Verstappen's Public Position

Verstappen has been characteristically guarded when questioned about his future. When asked at a media session whether he could commit to Red Bull beyond 2026, he replied: "There's nothing to say." When pressed further, he gave the same response. It was not a denial. It was not a confirmation. It was the answer of a man who has made a decision and is not yet ready to announce it.

His refusal to commit to Red Bull publicly, combined with the confirmed exit clause activation and the Lambiase departure, paints a clear picture — even if the official announcement has not yet come.

The Lambiase Factor

Gianpiero Lambiase joining McLaren is significant beyond its immediate personnel implications. He worked alongside Verstappen through four consecutive Drivers' Championship seasons and is considered one of the best driver-engineer partnerships in the sport's modern era. The fact that he is heading to McLaren in 2028 — and that Verstappen's own future is now an open question pointing in the same direction — suggests the two men may have coordinated their decisions.

Red Bull is therefore facing the prospect of losing both the driver who delivered its recent championship era and the engineer who guided him through it. That is a generational reset for a team that has defined the sport for the better part of a decade.

What's Next

The summer break is traditionally the moment when F1's biggest off-track decisions become public. An official announcement from Verstappen about his future is expected before the season resumes in Belgium. If confirmed, McLaren would enter the second half of 2026 with one of the sport's most anticipated future lineups already secured for 2027 onward. Red Bull would enter the same period needing to plan a future without their most decorated driver.
