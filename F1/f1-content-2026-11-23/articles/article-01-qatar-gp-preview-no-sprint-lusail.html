Qatar Grand Prix 2026: Full Weekend Schedule, No Sprint, Everything You Need to Know

By Ryan Calloway | November 23, 2026 | F1 Fanrecap

Formula 1 returns to Lusail International Circuit next weekend for the penultimate round of the 2026 season, and there is an important scheduling note for fans: the Qatar Grand Prix will not feature a sprint race. This year's visit follows the traditional three-practice, one-qualifying, one-race format that many fans prefer, giving teams and drivers more preparation time before Sunday's race.

The race itself is set for Sunday, November 29, with Practice 1 and Practice 2 taking place on Friday, November 27. Practice 3 and qualifying run Saturday, November 28, with the floodlit circuit expected to deliver the kind of dramatic atmosphere Lusail has become known for across recent Formula 1 seasons.

The Circuit

Lusail International Circuit is a clockwise layout of approximately 5.4 kilometers with 16 turns, run entirely under permanent floodlighting. The race distance for Sunday is 57 laps. The lap record stands at 1:23.196, set during a previous Qatar Grand Prix.

The circuit is demanding on tires — heat buildup in the desert environment compounds degradation, particularly on rear compounds, and teams will need to manage their tire allocations carefully across the sprint-free weekend. With three practice sessions available, there will be more data for engineers to work with than in sprint weekends, where Friday's second session is replaced by a sprint shootout.

What to Watch

The title may belong to Kimi Antonelli, but the battles behind him are genuinely compelling. Lewis Hamilton enters Qatar three points ahead of George Russell and Lando Norris in the drivers' championship, making every qualifying position and race finish critical for all three. Meanwhile, McLaren and Red Bull are separated by nine points in the constructors' standings — another fight that could go either way across the final two rounds.

Qatar has historically been a circuit where tire strategy creates meaningful variation in race results. Drivers starting from different positions can find themselves with strategic options that reward careful planning in the opening laps. With the championship battles so tight, teams will be balancing individual driver needs against constructors' points implications.

What's Next

Practice begins Friday, November 27 at Lusail. Qualifying follows Saturday evening. The race fires off under the lights on Sunday, November 29. After Qatar, only Abu Dhabi remains — the season finale will determine final championship positions for everyone still in a points fight, making these last two rounds among the most significant of the entire 2026 season.
