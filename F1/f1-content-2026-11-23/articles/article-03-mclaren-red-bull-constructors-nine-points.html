McLaren vs. Red Bull: Nine Points for Third in the Constructors With Two Races Remaining

By Elena Voss | November 23, 2026 | F1 Fanrecap

Mercedes and Ferrari have secured their positions at the top of the 2026 constructors' championship, but the battle behind them is unresolved. Red Bull holds third place with 440 points. McLaren sits fourth with 431. Nine points separate them with two races — Qatar and Abu Dhabi — remaining on the calendar, and the difference between third and fourth carries meaningful financial and reputational implications.

The constructors' championship prize money is distributed based on final standings, and the gap between successive positions grows at the top of the table. For teams operating at the highest level of the sport, the difference between finishing third and fourth represents tens of millions of dollars — funding that filters directly into car development for the following season.

How the Battle Has Developed

Red Bull's position in the standings has been built primarily on Max Verstappen's consistent results throughout 2026, including his win in Las Vegas. Verstappen's season has not reached the heights of his championship-winning campaigns, but his ability to convert grid positions into points has kept Red Bull in contention throughout the year.

McLaren's resurgence under Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri has been one of the season's notable storylines. Their Las Vegas one-two — Norris in second, Piastri in third — was a reminder that McLaren's car remains competitive at the right circuits. With both drivers inside the top five of the drivers' standings and two strong performances potentially available across the final two rounds, McLaren's path to overtaking Red Bull is real.

Qatar's Role

Lusail's characteristics favor cars that are strong on tire management and generate effective downforce without excessive drag on its extended straight sections. Both McLaren and Red Bull have shown strong pace at the circuit in previous years, suggesting the battle could be very close through the weekend.

The key variable is reliability. A retirement for either team would swing the nine-point margin dramatically. With nine points essentially representing one position difference across a full race, the constructors' fight will add additional strategic weight to every decision McLaren and Red Bull make across both Qatar and Abu Dhabi weekends.

What's Next

The Qatar Grand Prix qualifying on November 28 and race on November 29 will be the first major checkpoint in this battle. Both teams will need clean, incident-free weekends to maximize their constructors' haul. The final standings will be determined by what happens in Abu Dhabi — but every point scored in Qatar sets the stage for how that finale unfolds.
