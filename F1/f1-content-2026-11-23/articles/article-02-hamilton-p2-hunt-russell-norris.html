Hamilton's Hunt for P2: Three Points Separate Three Drivers With 58 Left to Score

By Marcus Cole | November 23, 2026 | F1 Fanrecap

Lewis Hamilton enters the final two rounds of the 2026 Formula 1 season holding third place in the drivers' championship, but his grip on that position — and his chance at finishing as runner-up to world champion Kimi Antonelli — is tenuous. Hamilton has 267 points. George Russell and Lando Norris each have 264. Three points separate three drivers with 58 still available across Qatar and Abu Dhabi.

Hamilton moved past Russell for third in the standings after the Las Vegas Grand Prix, where he finished seventh. It was not a spectacular result, but it was enough to leapfrog Russell. Norris, who finished on the podium in Las Vegas, sits level with Russell. All three drivers could realistically end the season in second place.

What 58 Points Means

Fifty-eight points across two traditional-format races translates to two race wins plus bonus points, or two podiums with consistent scoring. No driver in this battle can afford a retirement or a poor qualifying performance — not with rivals capable of picking up large points hauls in the same events.

The math favors consistent, clean racing over aggressive gambles. A driver who qualifies in the top three and finishes on the podium in both Qatar and Abu Dhabi is likely to end up as runner-up. But Formula 1 rarely delivers a simple script, and the same unpredictability that has made the 2026 season so compelling will follow the championship battle into its final chapter.

Three Different Approaches

Hamilton, Russell and Norris bring different strengths to these final two circuits. Hamilton's experience across multiple championship battles gives him a mental framework for managing pressure, though Ferrari's performance at specific circuits will determine whether that experience can be converted into points.

Russell has shown consistent pace throughout 2026 in a Mercedes that has been the year's dominant package. Norris and McLaren have been strong at high-speed circuits, and Lusail's characteristics tend to favor cars with strong downforce levels and effective tire management — both of which McLaren has demonstrated this season.

What's Next

Qualifying for the Qatar Grand Prix on Saturday, November 28 will be the first meaningful test of the three-way battle. Grid positions at Lusail carry particular weight, as overtaking, while possible, requires a significant pace advantage or a strategic opportunity. The driver who qualifies highest among the three has a meaningful advantage heading into Sunday's race. After Qatar, Abu Dhabi provides a final opportunity to settle the question of who finishes behind Antonelli in the history books.
