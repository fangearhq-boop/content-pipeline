Norris Wins Brazil, Championship Battle Rages On With Three Races Left

By Ryan Calloway | November 11, 2026

Lando Norris delivered a commanding victory at the São Paulo Grand Prix last weekend, winning by over 10 seconds in a 71-lap race that ran without a safety car. The result extended the 2026 Formula 1 Drivers' Championship battle into the final stretch, with three grands prix remaining and 129 points still available.

Norris Delivers at Interlagos

Interlagos has a tradition of producing defining championship moments, and the 2026 São Paulo Grand Prix continued that legacy in dramatic fashion. Lando Norris drove a flawless race to take victory with a margin of more than 10 seconds — a result that confirmed the McLaren driver's exceptional form at the business end of the season.

The race ran 71 laps without a safety car, which rewarded the team that best managed its strategy across a long and demanding afternoon. Norris and McLaren executed to near perfection, leaving rivals unable to close the gap despite their best efforts in the closing stages.

What Three Races Means for the Championship

With Las Vegas (November 22), and two further rounds to close the season, there are 129 points still available for every driver. In a sport where momentum shifts can be dramatic, that is an enormous number — large enough to swing the title in almost any direction depending on how the final races unfold.

The key variables are what they always are at this stage: reliability, strategy calls under pressure, and the ability of championship contenders to stay composed when everything is on the line. Norris has shown in Brazil that he is capable of exactly that level of execution. The question is whether he can sustain it across three more demanding race weekends.

Las Vegas Looms Large

The Las Vegas Grand Prix on November 22 is the next critical milestone in the championship fight. The Strip Circuit offers a unique combination of high-speed straight-line sections and technical corners that challenge teams across all aspects of car setup. It is a venue that tends to produce clear separation between teams with well-balanced cars and those that struggle to find the right compromise.

Qualifying on the Las Vegas circuit has historically been important — track position matters, and the aerodynamic demands of the circuit can expose setup weaknesses quickly. Teams will spend the next 11 days refining their data to arrive as well-prepared as possible.

What's Next

The 2026 F1 season still has a story to tell. Norris's win in Brazil proved that McLaren has the pace to compete for race victories at any circuit on the calendar. The championship is not settled, the final three races promise drama, and Las Vegas offers the perfect stage for the title fight to reach its climax.

For fans hoping for a season finale to remember, every result from here on carries maximum significance. The 2026 Formula 1 championship is going down to the wire.
