Mercedes Are Constructors' Champions Again — and They Did It Without Lewis Hamilton

By Elena Voss | November 11, 2026

Mercedes clinched the 2026 Formula 1 Constructors' Championship at the Brazilian Grand Prix, reaching 652 points with three races still remaining. For the first time in over a decade, the Silver Arrows did it without Lewis Hamilton — with Kimi Antonelli and George Russell delivering a campaign that validated the team's bold generational transition.

A Title Built on a New Foundation

When Mercedes announced that Lewis Hamilton would leave for Ferrari at the start of the 2026 season, the question was not whether the team could survive the transition — it was whether they could thrive through it. The answer arrived in Brazil: a Constructors' Championship sealed with three races to spare.

Kimi Antonelli, the Italian teenager who stepped into one of the most storied seats in motorsport history, delivered a season that defied every reasonable expectation. His pace, his composure, and his consistent accumulation of points across diverse circuits around the world served notice that Mercedes had not merely replaced Hamilton — they had found a new standard-bearer for the Silver Arrows.

Russell: The Steady Anchor

George Russell's contribution to the title cannot be understated. While Antonelli commanded much of the attention as the season's breakthrough performer, Russell provided the kind of experienced, dependable scoring that championship-winning teams require from both their drivers.

Russell's race management, his ability to maximize points on circuits where outright pace was not available, and his role in helping the team develop their car through the season all contributed to the 652-point total. Championships are not built on headline performances alone — they are built on accumulated points across 21-plus races, and Russell delivered precisely that.

What This Title Means for F1

Mercedes' 2026 Constructors' Championship signals something important about the structure of Formula 1 as a competition: great teams find ways to remain great even as personnel changes. The Silver Arrows have operated with a level of institutional quality that transcends any individual driver, and 2026 proved it conclusively.

For the sport more broadly, the emergence of Antonelli as a genuine championship-caliber driver — and the possibility that the 2026 season is the first of many title campaigns for the young Italian — adds a compelling new character to F1's long-term narrative. The passing of the torch from Hamilton to Antonelli may be remembered as one of the pivotal moments in the sport's modern era.

What's Next

With the Constructors' title secured, Mercedes can approach the final three races with freedom. The team will continue supporting both drivers in their personal championship battles, while also using the remaining rounds to refine development directions for 2027.

Whatever the final standings look like, 2026 will be remembered as the year Mercedes began their next chapter — and their next era of dominance. The Silver Arrows are champions again, and they look built to remain so.
