Here is how it works: F1 teams are allowed to use a limited number of certain power unit elements across a full season. When a team exceeds those allocations — for reliability, performance, or development reasons — the driver takes a grid penalty, usually meaning they start several places further back than their qualifying position.

For any driver, a grid penalty is a points risk. For the championship leader, it is potentially a crisis moment.

Ferrari has closed ground on Mercedes in the second half of the season and has been described as "a thorn" in the title hunt. Hamilton (P2, 169 pts) has been consistent all season. The championship is not over. With 291 points still available across 12 races, nothing is settled. But the grid penalty warning is the most significant complication Antonelli has faced in an otherwise dominant first half.
