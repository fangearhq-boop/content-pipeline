Cadillac F1 at Midseason: How the 11th Team's Debut Year Is Unfolding

By Marcus Cole | August 13, 2026 | F1 Fanrecap

Formula 1 has 11 teams in 2026, and the newest of them — Cadillac F1 — arrives at the Dutch Grand Prix having navigated half a debut season in the most technically demanding year the sport has produced in recent memory. The 2026 regulations overhauled everything: cars, power units, aerodynamics. Every team started from a new baseline. For a team that had never raced in F1 before, the leveling was an opportunity. The summer break is now over. The second half starts in eight days.

The Team Cadillac Built

Cadillac's approach to its debut season was to surround the car with experienced people. Team Principal Marcin Budkowski brings significant paddock knowledge to the operation. The driver lineup pairs two veterans who have been F1 constants: Valtteri Bottas, the six-time Constructors' champion across his Mercedes years, who contributes technical feedback and development expertise; and Sergio Perez, who spent years as Max Verstappen's Red Bull teammate before this chapter with the American newcomers.

The grid that surrounds Cadillac in 2026 is a mix of youth and experience. The season's only full rookie is Arvid Lindblad at Racing Bulls. At Red Bull itself, Isack Hadjar races alongside Verstappen. Yuki Tsunoda, after five seasons as a full-time driver, stepped back to a reserve role. The paddock has thinned its purely developmental seats — Cadillac, paradoxically, chose veterans.

The Perez Subplot

Heading into the Dutch GP, Cadillac has a subplot that the team would likely prefer to manage quietly: Sergio Perez is reportedly drawing interest from rival teams. In F1, the public surfacing of driver interest mid-season can mean several things — a driver is performing well and being courted, a driver's contract situation has become visible through paddock conversations, or a combination of both.

For Cadillac in a debut season, the scenario of managing transfer speculation around one of its two drivers adds a layer of internal complexity that established teams navigate more easily. The team needs Perez focused on the second half of the season. They need his technical feedback on a car that is still being developed and understood. An unsettled contract situation is a distraction neither side can fully afford.

What the Second Half Holds

The 2026 technical regulations reset gave every team a year-one slate. For Cadillac, the back half of the season is where the genuine data-gathering begins — how their development path compares to rivals who started with more institutional knowledge, where their car finds speed relative to competitors who have been refining their packages since winter testing.

The championship is out of reach for a debut team. The goals are more fundamental: points when circumstances allow, learning the rhythm of the F1 calendar, establishing a culture and an engineering baseline that can be built on in 2027. For a team that will eventually want to be competitive in this sport, 2026 is where the foundation gets laid. The Dutch GP is the first race of what they actually learn about themselves in the second half. Zandvoort in eight days.
