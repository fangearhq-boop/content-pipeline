Dutch Grand Prix Preview: Eight Days to Zandvoort's Final F1 Race, in Sprint Format

By Ryan Calloway | August 13, 2026 | F1 Fanrecap

Eight days from Thursday, Formula 1 will end its summer break at one of the most emotionally loaded venues on the calendar. Circuit Zandvoort in the Netherlands hosts the Dutch Grand Prix on August 21-23 — and when the cars cross the finish line on Sunday afternoon, it will be for the last time in Formula 1 history. The circuit exits the calendar after 2026, closing a six-year chapter that was built around one driver and leaves as something larger than that.

The weekend carries an additional twist: it is Zandvoort's first-ever Sprint event. Six years of Dutch Grands Prix at this circuit and none of them had a Sprint format. The last race at the track will be defined by one.

The Weekend Schedule

Teams return from the mandatory F1 summer shutdown on August 17, giving engineers and mechanics just four days to prepare before Sprint Qualifying begins. The compressed timeline adds another variable to an already heightened weekend.

The Sprint schedule at Zandvoort runs as follows: Sprint Qualifying on Friday at 3:30 p.m. local time; the Sprint itself on Saturday at 11:00 a.m. local, followed by Grand Prix Qualifying at 3:00 p.m.; and the full Grand Prix on Sunday at 3:00 p.m. local — 9:00 a.m. ET. The circuit measures 4.259 kilometers and the race distance runs 72 laps.

The Last Chapter of Zandvoort's Return

Zandvoort returned to Formula 1 in 2021 as a commercial and cultural bet on Max Verstappen's rise. The Netherlands had not hosted a Grand Prix since 1985, and the circuit's reinvention was designed around Dutch passion for their star driver. The banking at Hugenholtz corner, the passionate orange-clad crowds, the festival atmosphere — Zandvoort became one of the most atmospheric venues on the calendar almost immediately.

Six races later, the circuit's time on the calendar is up. Liberty Media and Formula 1 rotate venues for commercial and geographic reasons, and Zandvoort's run has reached its natural end. That the final race falls in a season where Verstappen's future with Red Bull is genuinely uncertain — his exit clause is active and an announcement is expected this weekend or before it — adds a layer of historical weight that the circuit's designers could not have scripted.

The Championship Context

Kimi Antonelli arrives at Zandvoort leading the drivers' championship with 219 points — 50 ahead of Lewis Hamilton in second. The 21-year-old Mercedes driver has been dominant in the first 11 rounds: six wins, six pole positions, nine podiums. But the second half begins with a known complication: Mercedes has warned that both Antonelli and George Russell are expected to take grid penalties for exceeding power unit component allocations. When those penalties come, and at which races, may determine whether Antonelli's lead is enough.

Zandvoort is also the first test of how the field shapes up post-summer break. Both Mercedes and Ferrari will have had weeks to develop their packages. The Sprint format adds early championship points to the equation on Saturday. Eight days. The season is about to get very loud again.
