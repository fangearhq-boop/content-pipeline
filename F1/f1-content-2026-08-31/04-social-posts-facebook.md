### STORY 2: Ferrari ADUO-2 Engine Debuts at Monza — No Grid Penalty

**Long-Form Post (10:00 AM ET)**
Ferrari just handed Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc a weapon — and they're both allowed to use it without consequences.

The Scuderia's ADUO-2 power unit upgrade has been cleared for its race debut at the 2026 Italian Grand Prix at Monza. Both Hamilton and Leclerc will receive the updated engine, which delivers approximately 15 additional horsepower through revisions to both the combustion chamber and turbocharger, plus a new Shell fuel blend. Better still for Ferrari — neither driver incurs a grid penalty. The upgrade stays within allocation.

At Monza — the most power-sensitive circuit on the F1 calendar, with its long, flat straights and minimal downforce — 15 horsepower translates to a genuine competitive advantage. Ferrari estimates the gain at around 0.2 seconds per lap. Over 53 laps, that adds up.

The timing is almost poetic. Ferrari's home race. A new engine breathing fire. And the championship leader starting from the back of the grid. Hamilton, who sits 53 points behind Antonelli, has never had a better window to make a title statement than this Sunday.

### STORY 3: Antonelli Confirmed Starting from Back at Monza — His Home Race

**Long-Form Post (12:00 PM ET)**
Kimi Antonelli's 2026 season has been a masterclass in consistency and composure. But this Sunday at Monza — his home race, in front of his home crowd — the Italian prodigy will start from the very back of the grid.

Mercedes boss Toto Wolff confirmed the decision: Antonelli will take a full power unit change at the Italian Grand Prix, having exceeded his allocation on the V6 engine, exhaust system, and MGU-H. The resulting grid penalty will likely drop him to the back of a 20-car grid.

But this is a strategic call by Mercedes, not a crisis. With Antonelli holding a 59-point championship lead, the team has calculated that Monza is the best place to absorb the hit. After this race, he'll have fresh components for the final seven grands prix — and every one of those races matters when you're defending a title.

The math is still workable. Antonelli can finish fifth or sixth from the back, collect five or fewer points, and still retain a substantial lead. But if rivals like Hamilton or Norris seize the moment, the picture gets more interesting.

### STORY 4: Monaco GP ICA Appeal — Verdict Could Come in Mid-September

**Long-Form Post (2:00 PM ET)**
The Monaco Grand Prix might not be fully settled until mid-September.

The FIA's International Court of Appeal heard arguments from McLaren and Red Bull on August 25 in Paris — but the judges have indicated the final verdict will take two to three more weeks. That puts the ruling in the week of September 8-15.

Here's the background: Pierre Gasly received two 5-second penalties for pitlane speeding in Monaco, measured at 60.1 km/h and 60.4 km/h. That dropped him from third to seventh. Alpine appealed, and stewards overturned the penalties after FOM admitted the pitlane speed measurement reference point at Monaco's unique pit entry was inaccurate — meaning Gasly (and other drivers) were effectively penalised for speeds measured incorrectly.

The reinstatement put Gasly back to P3. McLaren's Oscar Piastri dropped to P5. Red Bull's Isack Hadjar dropped to P4. Both teams are now arguing that the stewards exceeded their authority when they accepted Alpine's right-to-review case in the first place.

Gasly currently holds his P3 result. Whether he keeps it depends on the ICA.
