Italian Grand Prix Preview: Antonelli Starts Last, Ferrari Debuts New Engine, Norris Hunts History

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | August 31, 2026

Italian Grand Prix · Formula 1 · Race Preview

Five days. The 2026 Formula 1 Italian Grand Prix at Monza runs September 4-6, and it arrives as one of the most strategically loaded race weekends of the entire season. Championship leader Kimi Antonelli will start from the back of the grid. Ferrari debuts a significant new engine upgrade. And Lando Norris — fresh off back-to-back victories — arrives as the betting favorite at 6/4 to claim a historic hat-trick. If you have ever needed a reason to tune into Monza, the 2026 Italian GP provides several.

The Big Picture: Antonelli Takes the Hit

The defining story entering Monza is Kimi Antonelli's grid penalty. Mercedes boss Toto Wolff confirmed this week that Antonelli will take a full power unit change — exceeding his allocation on the V6 engine, exhaust system, and MGU-H — and start the Italian Grand Prix from the back of the 20-car grid. In front of his home crowd. At a circuit named after a man who drove for his home nation.

The penalty is a strategic call. Antonelli holds a 59-point championship lead and Mercedes has chosen to absorb the punishment at the venue where their driver can lose the most points safely, leaving him with fresh components for the final seven races of the season. The logic is sound — but the optics are uncomfortable. Antonelli, the Italian prodigy who came up through the Mercedes junior academy, will spend much of Sunday afternoon navigating through traffic rather than leading the race.

This creates an unprecedented window for Antonelli's rivals. Qualifying Saturday at 15:00 BST will set the front of the grid — and Antonelli will not be on it.

Ferrari's ADUO-2 Changes the Equation

Into this gap steps Ferrari with its biggest technical statement of 2026. The Scuderia's ADUO-2 power unit upgrade — featuring revised combustion chamber geometry, a redesigned turbocharger, and a new Shell fuel blend — is cleared for its race debut at Monza. Both Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc will use the upgraded unit. Neither incurs a grid penalty.

The estimated gain is approximately 15 additional horsepower and 0.2 seconds per lap at Monza specifically. The Temple of Speed, with its flat-out chicanes and long main straight, is the most power-sensitive circuit on the calendar — making Monza the ideal venue for Ferrari to debut their engine step. Hamilton, who sits 53 points behind the championship lead, has never been closer to a realistic title challenge in his Ferrari tenure.

Norris the Favorite, McLaren in Form

Lando Norris arrives at Monza having won two consecutive grands prix. The Dutchman's victory at Zandvoort was a statement — McLaren's race pace was dominant, and Norris was clinical. Monza's track characteristics — minimal downforce, high average speed, premium on efficient aerodynamics — historically suit McLaren's package.

At 6/4 with bookmakers, Norris is the market's pick. A win here, combined with a poor result from Antonelli, could reduce the championship gap from 83 points to 60 or fewer — transforming a comfortable lead into a genuine title fight heading into September.

Schedule at a Glance

Free practice runs Friday September 4. Qualifying Saturday September 5 at 15:00 BST (10:00 AM ET). The race goes green Sunday September 6 at 14:00 BST (9:00 AM ET) over 53 laps.

What's Next

The Italian Grand Prix has the potential to be the defining moment of the 2026 Formula 1 season. A Norris victory, a Hamilton podium, and an Antonelli recovery drive all happening simultaneously would create a championship narrative that lasts through the final seven rounds. Monza never disappoints — and this year, it promises something extraordinary.
