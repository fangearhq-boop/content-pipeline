Ferrari's ADUO-2 Engine Cleared for Monza — Hamilton and Leclerc Get 15hp Boost with No Grid Penalty

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | August 31, 2026

Ferrari · Formula 1 · Technical · Italian Grand Prix

Ferrari has received final clearance to debut its ADUO-2 power unit upgrade at the 2026 Italian Grand Prix, and the timing could not be more significant. Both Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc will race with the revised engine at Monza — gaining approximately 15 additional horsepower through internal combustion chamber revisions, a redesigned turbocharger, and a new Shell fuel formulation — without incurring any grid penalties. The upgrade stays within Ferrari's seasonal allocation for both drivers.

What the ADUO-2 Delivers

Ferrari's power unit development programme — officially the Alternative Development Unit Optimization plan, now in its second phase — has targeted two separate performance steps across the 2026 season. The ADUO-2 represents that second step, completing the initial planned uplift with an estimated 15 horsepower added through hardware changes to the internal combustion engine (primarily the combustion chamber geometry and turbocharger impeller redesign) as well as a new Shell fuel blend developed specifically for this configuration.

The result, according to Ferrari, is approximately 0.2 seconds per lap faster at Monza — a figure that becomes significant across 53 race laps. Ferrari also reports improvements to torque delivery and the distribution of electrical energy deployment around the lap, which is particularly relevant for Monza's acceleration zones out of the chicanes.

The Strategic Significance

Debuting the ADUO-2 at Monza is no accident. The Italian circuit is the most power-sensitive venue on the F1 calendar — the one track where raw horsepower advantage translates most directly to lap time. Ferrari's engine department, led by Enrico Gualtieri, has prioritized this debut for months. The reliability programme on the dyno is complete. The approval is in. Monza gets the upgrade.

Critically, Ferrari avoids a grid penalty. Unlike the Mercedes situation — where Antonelli is taking a penalty precisely to get fresh components — Ferrari is still within its engine allocation and receives the upgrade free of charge in terms of grid position. That means Hamilton and Leclerc qualify from wherever their pace puts them, with full access to the updated power unit from the very first corner.

What It Means for Hamilton's Title Hopes

Lewis Hamilton enters Monza 53 points behind championship leader Kimi Antonelli. That is a significant gap — but Antonelli will start from the back of the grid due to his own engine penalty. Hamilton, meanwhile, has never had a better technical platform or a more favorable circumstance to close that deficit in a single race.

The ADUO-2 gives Ferrari a realistic shot at their first Italian GP win in years. Hamilton has already claimed one victory and five podiums this season. A Monza win — in front of the Tifosi, with the extra engine power, and with the championship leader circulating at the back — would be one of the defining moments of his Ferrari career.

What's Next

The 2026 Italian Grand Prix begins with Free Practice on Friday September 4. Qualifying runs Saturday September 5 at 15:00 BST. The race is Sunday September 6 at 14:00 BST over 53 laps at Autodromo Nazionale Monza. Ferrari's ADUO-2 will make its race debut when those lights go out — and the championship picture could look very different by Sunday evening.
