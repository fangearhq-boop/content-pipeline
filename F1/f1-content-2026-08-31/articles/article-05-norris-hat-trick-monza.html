Can Norris Make It Three in a Row at Monza? The Title Race Just Got Interesting

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | August 31, 2026

Lando Norris · McLaren · Italian Grand Prix · F1 Championship

Two weeks ago, the 2026 Formula 1 championship looked settled. Kimi Antonelli led by a comfortable margin, McLaren was competitive but not dominant, and the title conversation had begun to pivot toward confirmation rather than competition. Then Lando Norris won two consecutive races. Then Antonelli's team announced he would start the Italian Grand Prix from the back of the grid. The Formula 1 narrative — never quite willing to stay still — has pivoted again. The title race is back on.

Norris in Career-Best Form

Lando Norris's recent trajectory is remarkable. After a first half of the season punctuated by inconsistency — partly linked to a technical floor issue that affected Oscar Piastri's car more than his own — Norris has found his rhythm at exactly the right moment. His victory at Zandvoort in the Dutch Grand Prix was his second consecutive win, a performance that reminded the paddock that McLaren's 2026 package, when operating at its ceiling, can match or beat anyone in a race.

At Monza, the track characteristics lean into McLaren's strengths. The Temple of Speed rewards aerodynamic efficiency and straight-line acceleration more than any other circuit on the calendar. Norris arrives as the 6/4 betting favourite — the market's way of acknowledging that Antonelli's starting position makes a McLaren victory the most likely outcome.

The Mathematics: What Monza Could Do to the Championship

Norris enters the Italian Grand Prix 83 points behind Antonelli with eleven races remaining after Monza. In isolation, that gap is challenging but not impossible to close. In the context of Monza — where Antonelli starts from the back and Norris starts near the front — a maximum points swing is achievable in a single afternoon.

Scenario: Norris wins (25 points), Antonelli finishes sixth (8 points). Net swing: 17 points. Gap closes to 66. Over five more races of sustained pressure and occasional Antonelli difficulty, the title becomes genuinely contested. That's the calculus driving the renewed excitement heading into Monza.

The Variables Norris Cannot Control

Antonelli is, among other things, an exceptional racing driver. From the back of the grid at a circuit with genuine overtaking opportunities, a points finish is probable. The question is how deep into the top ten he can recover — and whether Russell, Hamilton, or a Ferrari locks out the podium positions ahead of him while Norris leads.

George Russell — second in the championship and a former race winner in 2026 — will not be handing Norris the victory. Ferrari's Hamilton, with the new ADUO-2 engine and a 53-point deficit to close, will be equally motivated. The Italian GP shapes up as a three-way fight at the front, with Antonelli navigating from the back, and the championship standings shifting meaningfully by Sunday evening regardless of the outcome.

What's Next

Qualifying at Monza on Saturday September 5 (15:00 BST) will reveal how the front rows stack up — and whether McLaren's pure speed can translate to pole position as it did at Zandvoort. Then the race on Sunday September 6 (14:00 BST, 53 laps) will tell the story. If Norris stands on the top step, the final seven races of 2026 become unmissable. The Italian Grand Prix never needed more of a reason to be compelling. This year, it has many.
