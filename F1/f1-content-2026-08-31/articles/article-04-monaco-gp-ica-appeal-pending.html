Monaco GP ICA Appeal Heard in Paris — Verdict Expected Mid-September as Gasly Holds P3

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | August 31, 2026

Formula 1 · Monaco Grand Prix · FIA Appeal · Pierre Gasly

The 2026 Monaco Grand Prix may not have a settled result until mid-September. The FIA International Court of Appeal convened in Paris on August 25 to hear arguments from McLaren and Red Bull — both of whom are fighting to overturn the reinstatement of Alpine's Pierre Gasly to third place in Monaco — but the court has indicated it will need two to three more weeks to deliver its verdict. For now, Gasly holds P3 and the championship standings reflect the current result.

The Background: A Measurement Error and Its Consequences

The Monaco Grand Prix controversy traces back to the race itself, when Gasly received two separate five-second time penalties for pitlane speeding. Officials recorded speeds of 60.1 km/h and 60.4 km/h — fractionally above the 60 km/h limit — measured during two separate pit stop cycles. The penalties dropped Gasly from his original third-place finish to seventh.

Alpine successfully sought a review of the decision, arguing that the speed measurements were taken from an incorrect reference point at Monaco's uniquely configured pitlane entry. The FOM — Formula One Management, which owns and operates the timing and tracking infrastructure — acknowledged the discrepancy, confirming that the distance measurement at that point in Monaco's pitlane did not align with the standard reference used at other circuits. Gasly and four other drivers had been penalised based on readings that were, in effect, inaccurate relative to the actual start of the speed-limited zone.

Stewards accepted Alpine's right-to-review application and rescinded the penalties, restoring Gasly to third. McLaren's Oscar Piastri dropped to fifth. Red Bull's Isack Hadjar dropped to fourth.

The Appeal Arguments

McLaren and Red Bull have each formally appealed, presenting arguments at the Paris ICA hearing on August 25. The core of their case is procedural: both teams contend that the stewards acted outside their remit when they accepted Alpine's right-to-review application in the first place. Under F1 regulations, a right to review must be based on a "new significant element" — and both teams argue that the pitlane measurement issue did not meet that threshold.

FOM's acknowledgment of the measurement discrepancy was cited by Alpine as the new element. Whether the ICA agrees with the stewards' decision to accept that argument is now the central question before the court.

What a Ruling Could Mean

If the ICA upholds McLaren and Red Bull's appeal, Gasly would lose his P3 and revert to seventh — restoring points to Piastri and Hadjar, and affecting Alpine's standing in the Constructors' Championship. If the ICA dismisses the appeal, Gasly's reinstatement stands and the Monaco result is confirmed as-is. A verdict either way will carry precedent for future right-to-review applications.

What's Next

The ICA verdict is expected in the two-to-three week window following the August 25 hearing — meaning a ruling could arrive as early as the week of September 8, or as late as the week of September 15. The Italian Grand Prix at Monza runs September 4-6, meaning the Monaco verdict could land shortly after — a layered situation for teams monitoring both their Monza results and their pending Monaco points. The F1 calendar does not wait for courts.
