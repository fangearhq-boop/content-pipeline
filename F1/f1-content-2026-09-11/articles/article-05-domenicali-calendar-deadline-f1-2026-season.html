Domenicali's Calendar Deadline Arrives This Week — What Replaces Bahrain and Saudi Arabia?

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | September 11, 2026

FOM CEO Stefano Domenicali committed to publishing a revised 2026 F1 season calendar by mid-September, with Malaysia already confirmed as one replacement event and Imola and Portimão as the leading contingency options for remaining Middle East gaps.

The 2026 Formula 1 season has been operating with a calendar that has a visible gap in it — and Stefano Domenicali made a specific promise about when that gap would be filled.

Mid-September, he said. The deadline is this week.

The context: earlier in the season, regional security concerns related to US military activity near the Strait of Hormuz prompted FOM to cancel the Bahrain and Saudi Arabian Grand Prix rounds. Formula 1's relationship with Middle Eastern rounds has been a significant financial and strategic part of the calendar in recent seasons, and losing two events in that region mid-season required replacement planning on a compressed timeline. Malaysia's Sepang circuit — which hosted F1 until 2017 and maintained enough of its infrastructure for a return — was announced as a confirmed replacement for one of the cancelled rounds, scheduled for October 2-4.

The question that remained open was what happens to the rest of the calendar, particularly the rounds at Qatar (November 27-29) and Abu Dhabi (December 4-6) that remain officially listed but carry uncertainty given the broader regional environment.

The Contingency Options

Imola and Portimão have emerged as the leading candidates for any additional calendar changes. Both circuits hosted Formula 1 in recent seasons — Imola during the COVID-era Emilia Romagna rounds, Portimão during the Portuguese Grand Prix period — and both retain the infrastructure and FIA homologation status required for an F1 event. Neither would need to be built from scratch, which is the primary constraint on short-notice calendar additions.

From a commercial perspective, both venues are in Europe, which means easier logistics for teams based in the United Kingdom and Italy. European rounds also tend to draw larger television audiences from markets that are deeply embedded in the championship's fan base. If Qatar or Abu Dhabi were to be replaced by Imola, for instance, the final championship rounds would move from the Gulf to the Apennines — a shift with genuine atmospheric implications for how the title is contested and witnessed.

What Domenicali's Announcement Will Mean

When the revised calendar lands — expected no later than approximately September 15 — it will set the logistical parameters for the final stretch of the championship. Teams plan their personnel rotations, shipping containers, and travel arrangements weeks in advance. Every circuit change creates cascading planning challenges. The longer FOM waits to publish the final schedule, the more compressed those preparations become for all parties.

For the championship narrative, the calendar question also has an aesthetic dimension: whether the 2026 title fight between Antonelli and Russell concludes in the Gulf under desert lights, or at an Italian or Portuguese circuit in front of a passionate European crowd, is not irrelevant to the story the season tells as it closes. The venue shapes the memory.

What's Next

Domenicali's announcement is expected in the next several days, with the mid-September commitment making any delay beyond this week notable. Follow F1 Fanrecap for calendar news as the updated schedule is published and the season's final shape becomes clear.
