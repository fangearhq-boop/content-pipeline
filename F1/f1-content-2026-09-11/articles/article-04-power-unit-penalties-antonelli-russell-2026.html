The Hidden Wildcard: Both Antonelli and Russell Will Take Grid Penalties Before the Season Ends

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | September 11, 2026

Both championship contenders Kimi Antonelli and George Russell have exceeded their permitted power unit element allocations and will take mandatory grid penalties in the final 9 rounds — the timing of those penalties could significantly shape the 2026 title fight.

The 2026 Formula 1 championship has a detail that has not received the attention it deserves: both of the drivers fighting for the title will, at some point in the remaining 9 rounds, be penalized before they have even started a race.

Both Kimi Antonelli and George Russell have exceeded their permitted allocations of power unit components under Formula 1's technical regulations. The penalty structure for this is non-negotiable — drivers who exceed the set number of elements receive mandatory grid drops, or in cases of additional new elements, a pit lane start. The only variable in the equation is when.

How the Penalty System Works

Formula 1 limits each driver to a set number of power unit elements per season: internal combustion engines, turbochargers, motor generator units (MGU-H and MGU-K), energy stores, and control electronics. The standard allocation varies by component. Once a driver exceeds the permitted number of a given component, a grid penalty is applied at the next race — or the team may choose to take a new component at a later race if the penalty would apply immediately.

In practice, teams perform careful calculations to determine which race is the most strategic moment to absorb a mandatory penalty. The ideal choice is a circuit where grid position is not the primary determinant of race position — venues with long straights, multiple DRS zones, and historically high rates of overtaking. Taking a back-of-grid start at such a circuit, while damaging, does not necessarily produce a catastrophic points result. A driver who starts 20th at a circuit like Monza, Baku, or Spa can regularly recover into the points.

Taking a penalty at Monaco, Singapore, or — potentially — Madring, where track position is more difficult to recover, is the scenario teams are working hard to avoid.

What This Means for the Championship

The penalty question adds a layer of calculation to the final stretch of the championship that could genuinely matter. If Antonelli takes his penalty at a circuit where he can recover to third or fourth place, and Russell does not — or takes his at a similarly recovery-friendly venue — the impact on the standings may be minimal. But if one driver strategically coordinates a penalty round with a circuit where their car performs below par anyway, the calculus changes.

The more explosive scenario is if both drivers take their penalties in the same race. The grid for that event would feature both title contenders starting from deep in the field, creating a race from the back that could be one of the more extraordinary points scrambles of the season. The probability of that happening is low — teams typically avoid the theatrical by timing their penalty races carefully — but it is not zero.

What is certain: at some point in the remaining 9 rounds, both Antonelli and Russell will start a race from further back than their pace deserves. How those races unfold will be one of the defining chapters of the 2026 championship.

What's Next

Team principals and sporting directors at Mercedes will be managing the penalty timing question carefully across the upcoming rounds, balancing calendar circuit characteristics against championship position and race-specific competitive expectations. Any official announcement of a penalty element being introduced will signal which race is the chosen drop round.
