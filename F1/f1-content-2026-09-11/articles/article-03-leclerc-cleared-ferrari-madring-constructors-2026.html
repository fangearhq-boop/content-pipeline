Leclerc Cleared After 60G Monza Crash — Ferrari Arrives at Madring Needing a Turnaround

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | September 11, 2026

Charles Leclerc has passed all medical checks following his heavy Monza crash and the temporary right-eye vision issue that followed, and will race at the Spanish Grand Prix in Madrid — where Ferrari needs both cars to score heavily to stay alive in the Constructors' Championship.

The impact at Parabolica registered at 60G. Charles Leclerc lost the rear of his Ferrari on Lap 2 of the Italian Grand Prix at Monza, spun through the gravel, and hit the tyre barrier hard enough to require a red flag for circuit safety repairs. He walked to the medical car under his own power — but later revealed that in the immediate aftermath of the impact, his right eye was not showing him clear vision.

The vision issue, brief as it was, triggered a full neurological and ophthalmological evaluation as a condition of Leclerc's race entry at the Spanish Grand Prix. Dr. Isabel Ornaque, the Madring circuit's medical director, required the assessment before granting clearance. The results came back clean. No abnormalities. Leclerc is racing in Madrid.

The medical clearance is, in one sense, the easiest part of Ferrari's problem this weekend. The harder part is 87 points.

The Constructors' Reality

Ferrari trails Mercedes by 87 points in the Constructors' Championship with 9 rounds remaining. For context: 87 points is approximately the value of two wins plus three second-place finishes. Ferrari needs to outperform Mercedes by that margin across the remaining calendar to win the Constructors' title.

Lewis Hamilton's P6 result at Monza was not the kind of performance that closes gaps of that size. Leclerc's DNF was a direct points loss at a circuit that historically suits Ferrari — Monza, despite its power-track reputation, has been a circuit where the Scuderia finds competitiveness in recent seasons. To not score points there was a failure in exactly the wrong direction.

Madring offers a different kind of opportunity. No team has data on this circuit. The equal ignorance creates a baseline where Ferrari's simulator engineers start the same distance from the answer as Mercedes, Red Bull, or McLaren. If Ferrari's car concept is genuinely competitive and their engineering team can build a strong setup faster than rivals, a new circuit is where that can show up most clearly.

Leclerc's Personal Stakes

In the Drivers' Championship, Leclerc sits fifth, trailing Antonelli, Russell, Hamilton, and Norris in the standings. The title is not a realistic target at this stage of the season. But Leclerc's performance matters to Ferrari's constructors' push and to his own standing — a strong second half of the season at a track like Madring, where no driver has reference advantages, is exactly the kind of run that reshapes narratives.

The brief vision issue after Monza has been fully explained and fully cleared. Leclerc himself described the problem as lasting only a "very short" period and affecting his right eye before resolving completely. The medical process was thorough; the outcome was unambiguous. He is healthy, he is clear to race, and he arrives at Madring with the competitive motivation that typically follows a DNF at his home-circuit-equivalent event.

What's Next

Qualifying Saturday afternoon will give the first real read on where Ferrari stands relative to Mercedes on a neutral surface. A Leclerc pole position or front-row start would be the first evidence that the Madring reset has worked in Ferrari's favor. A Sunday double-points finish from both Leclerc and Hamilton would begin the process of making the 87-point gap feel less like an inevitability.
