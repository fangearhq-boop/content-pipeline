Russell Fastest in Singapore FP1 — But Sunday's Race Starts from the Back

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | October 9, 2026

George Russell set the fastest time in Singapore Grand Prix practice on Friday, clocking 1:32.274 seconds at Marina Bay Street Circuit. Charles Leclerc was 0.198 seconds back in second for Ferrari, with Lando Norris completing the top three for McLaren.

For Russell and Mercedes, it was a session that demonstrated genuine pace. And it was, in the larger picture of this Singapore weekend, almost entirely beside the point.

One Session, One Hour

The 2026 Singapore Grand Prix is the first-ever Formula 1 Sprint weekend held at Marina Bay — which means teams and drivers get exactly one hour of free practice before Sprint Qualifying begins on Friday evening. One session to read the circuit, understand the tyre behavior on the night circuit, calibrate the setup for a street track that punishes any mistake instantly and irreversibly.

Russell used the session well. The 1:32.274 represented genuine pace, and Mercedes looked composed and competitive in the only preparation time they'll get before the competitive action begins. Leclerc's Ferrari was close but not close enough, and Norris's presence in the top three confirmed McLaren remains a factor throughout the championship field.

The Penalty That Defines the Weekend

Russell's back-of-grid start for Sunday's Grand Prix was confirmed by Mercedes earlier this week, following his engine failure in Malaysia that required the installation of his fifth power unit of the season — exceeding the 2026 allocation of four. The penalty will see him dropped to the back of the Grand Prix grid regardless of where he qualifies on Saturday night.

There is, however, one meaningful escape hatch. The grid penalty applies only to the Grand Prix, not to the Sprint. Russell can qualify for Sprint Qualifying tonight and start Saturday's 21-lap sprint race from wherever that session places him. Points are on offer in the Sprint — up to 8 for a win — and Russell cannot afford to leave any of them behind.

What FP1 Tells Us

The fastest time in a single practice session at a street circuit is a limited data point. Teams run different fuel loads, tyre compounds and setup philosophies in practice, and the full picture only emerges in qualifying. But Russell being fastest — with Leclerc and Norris in his wake — signals that Mercedes has a car that suits the characteristics of Marina Bay.

The circuit's narrow corridors, 19 corners and barriers placed at the very edge of the tarmac reward mechanical grip and low-speed traction over outright aerodynamic efficiency. Historically, Mercedes has been competitive here. The question is whether that competitiveness can manifest in a weekend that requires so much from such little preparation time.

What's Next

Sprint Qualifying runs Friday evening at 8:30 PM Singapore time. Russell's performance there will determine the Sprint grid and, with it, his best opportunity to score championship points before Sunday's damage-limitation exercise begins. With Kimi Antonelli 84 points ahead, every point in every session now matters in a way it didn't at the start of this season.

FP1 showed Mercedes is quick. Now comes the part where quick has to convert into points — starting tonight.
