First F1 Sprint at Marina Bay: Your Complete Guide to the Singapore GP Weekend

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | October 9, 2026

The 2026 Singapore Grand Prix is making history before anyone turns a wheel in anger. For the first time in Formula 1's long history at Marina Bay Street Circuit, this weekend will feature the Sprint format — meaning an entirely different race weekend structure from every Singapore GP that came before it.

If you're used to the traditional three-practice format and aren't sure what to expect, here is everything you need to know.

The Full Weekend Schedule

All times in Singapore Time (SGT / UTC+8), with Eastern Time equivalents:

  • Friday, Oct. 9 — Free Practice 1: 4:30 PM SGT (4:30 AM ET)
  • Friday, Oct. 9 — Sprint Qualifying: 8:30 PM SGT (8:30 AM ET)
  • Saturday, Oct. 10 — Sprint Race: 5:00 PM SGT (5:00 AM ET)
  • Saturday, Oct. 10 — Grand Prix Qualifying: 9:00 PM SGT (9:00 AM ET)
  • Sunday, Oct. 11 — Singapore Grand Prix: 8:00 PM SGT (8:00 AM ET)

Note that because this is a Sprint weekend, there are NO second or third practice sessions. Drivers get one hour on Friday afternoon, then Sprint Qualifying begins. That is all the preparation time available before competitive running starts.

How the Sprint Format Works

Sprint Qualifying (tonight) determines the grid for Saturday's Sprint Race — a 21-lap race around Marina Bay. Points are awarded to the top eight finishers: 8-7-6-5-4-3-2-1. Sprint results do NOT determine the Grand Prix grid.

Grand Prix Qualifying on Saturday night determines the GP grid independently. So drivers who perform poorly in Sprint Qualifying can still start Sunday's race from a strong position — and vice versa.

This is the sixth and final Sprint weekend of the 2026 season, making Singapore the last chance to earn sprint points before the regular race results take over for the remainder of the calendar.

Why Singapore Is a Special Challenge

Marina Bay Street Circuit is 4.927 kilometers long with 19 corners. It is a night race, running under floodlights that turn the city-state's harbor skyline into one of the most visually spectacular backdrops in motorsport. The circuit is fast in places but unforgiving everywhere — barriers line the edges of the track, and any contact typically ends a driver's race immediately.

The sprint format intensifies these already demanding conditions. Teams have limited data from a single practice session to optimize setup, tyre strategy and race pace. Drivers who qualify well tend to stay where they are; those who don't face an extremely difficult task in recovery. The combination of sprint points, overnight setup changes and championship pressure makes this one of the most strategically complex weekends of the year.

What's Next

Sprint Qualifying begins tonight at 8:30 PM SGT. The results will tell us a great deal about the pace order heading into a critical championship weekend — both for the Sprint points available Saturday and the GP qualifying that follows. For Kimi Antonelli, George Russell and their title battle, the weekend effectively begins tonight.

Marina Bay has never hosted a sprint before. In every way that matters, this is new territory.
