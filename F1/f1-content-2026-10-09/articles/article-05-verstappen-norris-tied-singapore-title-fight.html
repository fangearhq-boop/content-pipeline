Verstappen and Norris Arrive in Singapore Tied at 188 — The Other Championship Battle

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | October 9, 2026

While the spotlight of the 2026 Formula 1 season has rightly fixed on Kimi Antonelli's march toward history, a separate championship battle is developing in the midfield standings — and it deserves more attention than it's getting.

Max Verstappen and Lando Norris arrive at the Singapore Grand Prix locked together at exactly 188 points each in the Drivers' Championship. Lewis Hamilton leads them at 214. Charles Leclerc sits between them at 191. The gap separating third through sixth in the championship is 26 points — less than a single Grand Prix win.

This is not a fight for the title. But it is a fight for legacy, positioning and the unofficial crown of "best of the rest" in a season dominated by one driver and one team.

Verstappen's Malaysian Resurgence

Max Verstappen's victory in Malaysia last weekend was his first win of the 2026 season — a milestone both for himself and for Red Bull Ford Powertrains, which claimed its first victory under the new 2026 technical regulations. It was also Ford's first Formula 1 victory since 2004, a meaningful moment for a manufacturer that returned to the sport with championship ambitions but found the early part of this season dominated by Mercedes.

Verstappen finished 2.307 seconds ahead of Antonelli at Sepang, with Hamilton third, Leclerc fourth and rookie Isack Hadjar fifth. The margin of victory was comfortable but the story was the competitiveness of the Red Bull machine in a circuit that has historically suited it. Verstappen has now won back-to-back Malaysian Grand Prix events.

He arrives in Singapore with momentum and a car that showed genuine race pace in Malaysia. The question is whether that carries to a street circuit, where Red Bull's form historically varies more than their pace on high-speed permanent tracks.

What Norris Needs

Norris has been the most consistent driver in the McLaren camp this season — enough to match Verstappen's points total despite not winning recently. McLaren's car has genuine pace, but converting that pace into victories at specific circuit types has been the gap between them and Mercedes at the top of the standings.

Singapore is a circuit where McLaren has had competitive showings in recent years. The low-speed, high-downforce characteristics of Marina Bay suit cars with strong mechanical grip and aerodynamic efficiency rather than raw power. If McLaren can qualify strongly on Saturday night, Norris has a genuine opportunity to move ahead of Verstappen in the standings by Sunday evening.

What Singapore Could Change

In a single weekend, the P3-P6 standings could shift significantly. A Verstappen win with a Norris retirement, or vice versa, could create a gap of 25+ points overnight. Hamilton and Leclerc are also just a strong weekend away from reclaiming third overall. The four-way battle for third place in the 2026 Drivers' Championship could come down to the final race.

For teams that didn't win the title this season, the standings positions matter for prestige, development funding allocation and the narrative heading into 2027. Third place in the championship is not a consolation prize — it's a statement.

What's Next

Sprint Qualifying tonight will be the first genuine read on which car suits Marina Bay best in 2026 conditions. Saturday's Sprint offers an early opportunity to shift the standings, and Sunday's full race is where the biggest points can be won or lost. For Verstappen, Norris, Hamilton and Leclerc, every qualifying lap and every race lap in Singapore has championship value.

The fight at the top of the 2026 Drivers' Championship may be settled. The fight behind it is very much alive.
