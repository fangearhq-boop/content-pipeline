Colapinto's 5-Place Penalty Follows Him to Bahrain — Norris Keeps Pushing for Race Ban

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | September 29, 2026

Franco Colapinto will start the Bahrain Grand Prix at Sepang with a 5-place grid penalty already on his record. The stewards' decision from the Azerbaijan Grand Prix stands, and it will cost Colapinto five positions on the starting grid at Sepang when the field lines up on October 4.

The penalty stems from a collision in Baku that ended Lando Norris's race. Norris, who was competing from a strong starting position, was taken out in an incident the stewards determined was Colapinto's fault. The Argentine driver described the contact as unintentional. The stewards saw a driving error serious enough to warrant the penalty.

Norris Pushing Further

A 5-place grid penalty has not satisfied Lando Norris. The McLaren driver has continued to push for a more severe punishment, publicly calling the Baku incident "completely unacceptable" and arguing that a race ban is the appropriate response.

Race bans in Formula 1 are issued through the penalty points system — a driver accumulates points per incident, and reaching 12 within a 12-month period triggers an automatic one-race ban. Whether Colapinto's Baku incident resulted in enough penalty points to move him closer to that threshold has not been officially confirmed.

Norris's public pressure is also a message to the stewards: that the standard response to incidents at his level of severity is insufficient. Whether it changes anything for Bahrain is a separate question from whether it builds a longer-term pattern of scrutiny around Colapinto's driving.

What's Next

Colapinto starts from the back section of the Bahrain grid on October 4. Sepang's wide layout and multiple racing lines make overtaking more feasible than some circuits, which may limit the penalty's full impact if he has the pace. The question is whether Norris's continued lobbying produces any further action before the race.
