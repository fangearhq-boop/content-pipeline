Verstappen Confirms Engine Penalty Is Coming — Red Bull Awaits Damage Assessment

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | September 29, 2026

Max Verstappen was not subtle about it. "There definitely has to be a penalty at some point," he said after the Azerbaijan Grand Prix. The question Red Bull is now trying to answer is whether that point is the Bahrain Grand Prix at Sepang on October 4, or further down the calendar.

The situation began during Baku qualifying, when Verstappen suffered an internal combustion engine failure that restricted him to eighth on the grid — a damaging position on a circuit where starting position matters enormously. Red Bull responded by fitting a new ICE, turbocharger, exhaust set and power unit ancillary component before the race.

Why No Penalty in Baku

The immediate question was why Verstappen did not receive a grid penalty for the race itself. The answer is the season's power unit allocation rules. Every component fitted in Azerbaijan remained within Verstappen's permitted annual allowance, meaning the FIA confirmed no penalty was owed. He started P8, recovered to second, and left Baku with his season intact — if significantly more complicated.

Team principal Laurent Mekies was careful with his language after the race: "A future grid drop remains uncertain until the team has inspected the removed engine." The removed engine is currently being analyzed at Red Bull's facility. If the team establishes that it can be repaired and reused, the allocation question does not arise again. If it cannot, using a fresh unit at any future race exceeds the limit and triggers the grid penalty Verstappen says is inevitable.

The Tension in Baku

The technical failure came at a moment that amplified everything. Verstappen arrived in Baku needing to reduce a 66-point deficit to championship leader Kimi Antonelli. Qualifying eighth, when the car had shown frontrunning pace, was exactly the result that cannot happen in a title chase that is already running out of calendar.

Reports from within the Red Bull camp described Verstappen telling the team to "p*** off" after the qualifying session. That reaction does not emerge from nowhere. Verstappen has spent the middle portion of 2026 watching Antonelli build a lead that should not exist in a season where Red Bull was expected to be a championship-winning operation. An engine failure in qualifying, followed by a race in which he finished second to George Russell while the championship leader escaped in fifth, is the kind of weekend that tests a team's internal relationships.

The Penalty Timeline

Red Bull has three race weekends to work with before the Singapore sprint event: Bahrain at Sepang (Oct 4), Singapore (Oct 11), and then a longer break. If a penalty is coming, taking it at Sepang may be preferable to taking it at Singapore, where the sprint format means two results on the same weekend and a grid drop's damage is amplified.

Verstappen is 66 points behind Antonelli. A 10-place grid drop at a conventional race costs him perhaps five to eight points compared to a normal race weekend. It is manageable. A 10-place grid drop at a sprint weekend, where qualifying and sprint results also factor, creates a more complicated hole to dig out of.

What's Next

Red Bull expects to have a decision on the engine analysis before the Bahrain GP grid is set. If the removed unit is salvageable, Verstappen races from a normal grid position. If it is not, he takes a penalty and adds another sentence to a 2026 season that has rarely gone the way Red Bull envisioned.

The Bahrain GP at Sepang begins Friday, October 2. The grid for Sunday, October 4 locks in Saturday evening local time.
