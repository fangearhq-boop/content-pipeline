Ferrari Issues Full Confidence Statement for Vasseur as Horner Rumors Swirl

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | September 29, 2026

Ferrari held an unusual pre-race media briefing on Tuesday morning, and it had nothing to do with car setup. A Ferrari spokeswoman delivered a pointed message: the team has full confidence in team principal Fred Vasseur, and he is going nowhere.

The briefing was called ahead of the Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia, which begins Friday at Sepang International Circuit. The timing was deliberate. Less than 24 hours earlier, a Times interview with Christian Horner had gone public — and the former Red Bull boss named Ferrari as the only Formula 1 team he would consider returning to the pit wall for, without the ownership stake he has been prioritizing in any potential comeback.

The Horner Factor

Horner's comments were precise and calculated. He did not name Vasseur. He did not say Ferrari should make a change. But naming the team at all, with "only" in the sentence, at this point in the season, amounts to a grenade rolled into the paddock.

Ferrari's response was swift enough to confirm that they understood exactly what was happening. The hastily arranged briefing — "hastily" being their own word, not a characterization — signals that the rumors have reached a level where silence would be read as confirmation.

Vasseur himself gave the game away after the Azerbaijan Grand Prix. When asked about his position, he said the team was "not working in perfect serenity," a phrase that tracks considerably more candid than a team principal usually allows. Persistent rumors around his future have been his description, as well as "frustrating."

Ferrari's Season in Context

The backdrop is a 2026 campaign that has not delivered what Ferrari needed. They entered the year with widespread rule changes, and their chassis was widely considered the best in the field at the season opener. Their engine, however, has consistently fallen short of the power output produced by Mercedes and Red Bull.

Ferrari currently sits second in the constructors' championship — a position that sounds respectable until you note the gap: 160 points behind Mercedes leaders with eight rounds remaining. That gap is essentially insurmountable. A second-place finish in the constructors' championship, in a year when the chassis was supposedly the class of the field, is not the outcome that satisfies the Scuderia.

Charles Leclerc has publicly expressed frustration. Vasseur has responded with promises of upgrades. The internal pressure that produces a public "not working in perfect serenity" comment is the kind that tends to outlast the confidence votes meant to contain it.

What's Next

Ferrari will be in the paddock at Sepang this week, running a car that needs to close a 160-point gap it cannot realistically close. Whether Vasseur is still in his seat when the 2026 season ends in Abu Dhabi in late November remains an open question — one that Ferrari's Tuesday briefing tried to close and likely only kept open.

For his part, Horner has not formally applied for any position. He has an interview on record and a name that will now come up every time Ferrari loses a race for the rest of the year.
