Bahrain GP in Malaysia — Antonelli Arrives as Favorite for Sepang's Title Crossroads

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | September 29, 2026

The 2026 Bahrain Grand Prix will not be held in Bahrain. The original race was cancelled in April following the conflict in Iran, which disrupted the region's logistics and infrastructure to a degree that made hosting a Formula 1 event impossible. Malaysia stepped in. The race runs at Sepang International Circuit from October 2 to 4, Round 16 of 23.

It is the kind of scheduling anomaly that sounds confusing until you're inside the paddock, where it has simply become the race that defines the second portion of this season. The championship is real, the stakes are real, and the track that will host them is one of the most complete tests in the sport.

The Circuit

Sepang has not hosted a Formula 1 race since the Malaysian Grand Prix was dropped from the calendar in 2017. The circuit's return, under these unusual circumstances, has generated genuine excitement from drivers who know its characteristics: a wide layout that allows multiple racing lines through high-speed sequences, combined with heavy-braking chicanes and 90-degree corners that punish any mistake in braking reference points.

The weather is the wild card that no simulation adequately prepares a team for. Sepang's tropical climate produces temperatures in the low 30s Celsius throughout the weekend, and the humidity adds to the physical load on drivers during a race that runs 56 laps. Sudden downpours — heavy, brief and capable of appearing with little warning — have historically transformed Sepang races from one kind of event into something entirely different.

Antonelli's Position

Kimi Antonelli enters Sepang with 302 points and a 66-point lead over George Russell. The numbers are comfortable. The recent form is slightly less so.

In Azerbaijan, Antonelli qualified 16th. He recovered to fifth place — a result that, in isolation, sounds fine. In the context of a championship race, starting 16th suggests something went wrong before the race weekend started. Russell, starting from the front row, won the race. Antonelli left Baku with his lead intact, which is a victory in itself, but Baku demonstrated that the momentum is no longer entirely in one direction.

At Sepang, Antonelli is expected to return to a more conventional front-row qualifying position. Mercedes has been the dominant technical package of 2026, and Sepang's characteristics — particularly the demand for sustained power through its long straight sections — play to that strength.

Russell's Response

George Russell arrives at Sepang as the driver who most recently beat Antonelli in a straight fight. The Baku victory was his third win of the season and it moved him to within 66 points of a title that had looked settled. That gap still requires something to change — but it requires less change than it did before Baku.

Russell's task at Sepang is to win while Antonelli finishes third or lower. That combination reduces the gap to roughly 40 points and makes Singapore, which follows the week after, into a genuinely decisive weekend.

What's Next

Free Practice 1 at Sepang begins Friday, October 2 at 12:30 local time (UTC+8). The race runs Sunday, October 4. With Singapore's sprint weekend following immediately after on October 9-11, this two-week stretch could define whether 2026 is a championship procession or a genuine fight.
