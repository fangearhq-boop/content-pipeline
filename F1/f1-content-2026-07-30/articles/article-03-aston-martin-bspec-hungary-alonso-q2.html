Sixteen Parts Changed Overnight — Alonso Reaches Q2 for the First Time in 2026

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | July 30, 2026

Aston Martin arrived at Hungary with a 16-part B-spec overhaul, used their first curfew exemption of the season to install it overnight, and watched Fernando Alonso reach Q2 for the first time all year. Step two arrives at Zandvoort.

Fernando Alonso described reaching Q2 as a feeling he had "missed." That one sentence tells you everything about how bad Aston Martin's 2026 season had been before Hungary — and how meaningful the B-spec upgrade is for what comes next.

The team debuted a 16-part overhaul of the AMR26 at the Hungarian Grand Prix on July 24-26, the most extensive single upgrade package they have introduced in the 2026 campaign. Mechanics worked through the night using Aston Martin's first curfew exemption of the season to install the parts before Friday practice. The list included essentially every aerodynamic surface and multiple suspension elements: front wing, front wing endplate, nose, front corner, floor body, floor corners, floor fences, floor edge, diffuser, sidepod inlet, engine cover, cooling louvres, rear suspension, rear corner, rear wing, beam wing, and rear wing endplate.

Team Principal Mike Krack had identified two primary problems the upgrade was designed to address: poor baseline aerodynamic efficiency and weight compliance issues that had handicapped the car through the first ten races of the season. Both had contributed to a season in which Aston Martin regularly qualified at the back of the grid — a painful situation for a team that finished fourth in the constructors' standings just two years ago.

What Q2 Means

In isolation, qualifying second to last would not seem like a milestone. For Aston Martin in 2026, it is. The team had spent five consecutive qualifying sessions with both cars starting from the back two rows, an extended period of irrelevance that had begun to raise questions about the fundamental competitiveness of the AMR26's concept.

Alonso reaching Q2 in Hungary — eliminated there, not in Q1 — represents the first evidence that the car can compete in the midfield. The gap to the Q2 elimination line has tightened. Alonso's own assessment in qualifying was direct: he could "fight a little bit more." His race result — 14th — was not spectacular. But his pace in qualifying suggested something had changed.

"That adrenaline was missed," Alonso said after qualifying. The quote captures both the quality of the upgrade and the difficulty of the season that preceded it.

What Comes Next

The Hungary package is explicitly labeled by the team as step one of a two-part process. Step two arrives at the Dutch Grand Prix at Zandvoort (Aug 21-23), where Aston Martin will also benefit from Honda's sole 2026 power unit upgrade. F1 analysts have described the full two-phase package as potentially transformative for the second half of the season. Alpine's Franco Colapinto issued an "ominous warning" about Aston Martin's upgrade potential when commenting on what he'd seen in Hungary.

Whether the B-spec genuinely repositions Aston Martin in the midfield or merely makes them a more competitive back-of-midfield car remains to be seen. The Dutch GP will provide clearer data than Hungary, as the second phase compounds the gains from the first.

What's Next

Zandvoort on August 21-23 is the first test of whether Hungary was a genuine step forward or a circuit-specific anomaly. Both Alonso and Stroll will enter the weekend with renewed purpose — and the rest of the grid will be watching to see whether Aston Martin's second-half transformation is real.

For Alonso at 45, the B-spec's arrival may be the final chapter of his Formula 1 story. He has yet to confirm whether he will continue beyond 2026. A competitive car in the second half could change that calculation. A performance like Hungary's, multiplied over the final eleven rounds, would at least give him something worth staying for.
