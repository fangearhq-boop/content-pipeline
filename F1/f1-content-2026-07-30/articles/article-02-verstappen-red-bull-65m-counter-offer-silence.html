Red Bull Offered Verstappen €65 Million a Year. He Hasn't Said Yes. October Is Coming.

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | July 30, 2026

The counter-offer is on the table: €65M per year through 2029, with one condition — remove the exit clause. As of July 28, Verstappen has not responded. McLaren is waiting in the wings. The silence says everything.

Red Bull Racing has made their move in the most consequential driver negotiation in Formula 1.

The team has submitted a new contract proposal to Max Verstappen offering approximately €65 million — roughly $72 million — per year through 2029. The deal would represent a significant pay increase and an extension of his current arrangement, keeping him at the team that delivered four consecutive World Championship titles. The offer is, by any measure, the largest contract Red Bull has ever put on the table.

There is one condition: the exit clause must go.

Verstappen's current contract contains performance-related clauses that allow him to trigger an early exit in October of this year. The threshold is finishing in the top two of the Drivers' Championship at the summer break. Verstappen is currently seventh. The clause is fully available to him. Red Bull's offer requires him to sign away that option in exchange for the money and the multi-year commitment. As of July 28 — per reporting from The Judge 13 — Verstappen has not responded to the proposal.

Why the Silence Is Significant

A driver with €65 million per year on the table who has not responded has reasons for not responding. The most obvious reason is that the exit clause has tangible value — and Verstappen understands that value precisely because McLaren, with the most competitive car in the field for stretches of this season, represents a compelling destination.

Sport Bible reported on July 7 that Verstappen and McLaren are in the "closing stages" of negotiations for a 2027 move. McLaren CEO Zak Brown has denied any such discussions publicly, reiterating the team's commitment to Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri. Paddock sources note that Verstappen's race engineer Gianpiero Lambiase — who has worked with Max since 2016 — is reportedly in early discussions with McLaren about a potential 2028 move, a circumstantial detail that aligns with a Verstappen departure scenario.

Verstappen's public answer when asked about his future: "There's nothing to say." It is the kind of answer a driver gives when there is, in fact, a great deal to say, and none of it is ready to be said publicly.

The October Clock

The exit clause can be triggered in October. Between now and then, Red Bull must evaluate whether their offer is worth maintaining, whether to improve it further, and whether the gap between their car and the McLaren can close enough to change the calculation. Verstappen must decide whether €65 million per year at Red Bull is worth more than a competitive McLaren seat and another realistic shot at a championship.

None of these questions have simple answers. Red Bull is not simply a worse team than McLaren on pure pace — the gap has been significant but not permanent. Verstappen's record at Red Bull is unrivaled in the modern era. The emotional and professional history between driver and team is real.

But the exit clause exists for a reason. Verstappen insisted on it. Red Bull agreed to it. The fact that they now want it removed — and are willing to pay €65 million per year to make that happen — tells you exactly how seriously they are taking the possibility that he uses it.

What's Next

The Dutch Grand Prix at Zandvoort (Aug 21-23) is Verstappen's home race and the first event after the summer break. Whether he is still a Red Bull driver when the contract expires — or on his way to McLaren — the circuit will provide the defining emotional backdrop for whatever comes next.

October is the deadline. The silence continues. Red Bull is waiting for an answer they may not receive.
