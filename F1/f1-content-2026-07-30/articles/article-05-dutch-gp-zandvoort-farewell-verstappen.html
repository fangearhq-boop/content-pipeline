The Last Race at Zandvoort: A Farewell with Verstappen's Future in the Balance

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | July 30, 2026

August 21-23 at Circuit Zandvoort is more than the first race back from summer break. It's the last Formula 1 race ever held at this circuit — and for Max Verstappen, it could be the final home race of his Red Bull career.

Some circuits carry an identity that transcends the racing itself. Monza is Italian passion. Monaco is glamour and history. Silverstone is the soul of the sport. And Zandvoort, since 2021, has become the cathedral of the Orange Army — the sea of Dutch orange that floods the sand dunes every August to watch Max Verstappen race at home.

On August 21-23, the Dutch Grand Prix will take place at Zandvoort for the last time. F1 will not return to this circuit after 2026. The end of an era, in a literal sense.

The timing makes the occasion something beyond a race. Verstappen's performance-based exit clause at Red Bull can be triggered in October. He is currently seventh in the championship standings — well outside the top-two threshold that would ordinarily disqualify the clause's activation. Unless the second half of the season reshapes the standings dramatically, Verstappen enters October with every right to use the clause and explore what a move to McLaren might offer.

That makes August 21-23 potentially his last home race as a Red Bull driver. In a circuit that has defined five years of his dominance.

A Sprint Weekend at Home

The 2026 Dutch Grand Prix is also the first Sprint weekend ever held at Zandvoort. The format adds a Sprint Qualifying session on Friday, a 24-lap Sprint Race on Saturday morning, Grand Prix qualifying on Saturday afternoon, and the main race on Sunday. For the Orange Army, more competitive sessions means more chances to see Verstappen challenge at the front — regardless of what Red Bull's car might be capable of on a pure pace basis.

Aston Martin's second B-spec upgrade step arrives here too, along with Honda's 2026 power unit update — developments that will reshape the midfield picture and potentially alter race strategies across the grid. The circuit itself has unique characteristics: the banked Turn 3, the technical infield, and the coastal North Sea winds that make the surface slippery in unexpected ways. It rewards setups that feel uncomfortable on smoother tracks.

The Farewell Context

Zandvoort returned to the F1 calendar in 2021 after a 36-year absence. From the first race, it was clear the circuit had a different energy from anything else on the calendar — not just loud, but ferocious in its support for a single driver. Dutch fans created an atmosphere that the rest of the paddock found remarkable and sometimes overwhelming.

That the circuit is leaving the calendar is partly commercial: ticket revenue, logistics costs, infrastructure investment, and the economic challenges of a Dutch market that has grown more expensive since 2021. The circuit put on extraordinary events but could not make the economics work beyond 2026.

Whatever the reasons, the last race at Zandvoort will be an emotional moment for Dutch motorsport fans and for Verstappen himself — a driver who has never been shy about how much his home race means to him.

What's Next

The Dutch Grand Prix on August 21-23 is Round 12 of 22. The championship picture will be clearer by Sunday evening — both in terms of what the second-half car performance looks like and in terms of where the title race stands. For Verstappen, it is a chance to perform in front of his people before the October decision that will define the next chapter of his career.

The last race at Zandvoort will have no shortage of storylines. That, if nothing else, is entirely in keeping with the spirit of the place.
