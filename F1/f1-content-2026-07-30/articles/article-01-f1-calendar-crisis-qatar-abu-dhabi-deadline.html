F1 Sets September Deadline for Qatar and Abu Dhabi — European Finale Is the Backup Plan

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | July 30, 2026

Bahrain and Saudi Arabia were cancelled in March. Now F1 CEO Domenicali confirms Qatar and Abu Dhabi face a mid-September decision. If they fall, the season ends in Europe. Imola is waiting.

Formula 1 has already cancelled two races in the Middle East this season. The question is whether that number becomes four.

F1 CEO Stefano Domenicali confirmed Wednesday that the sport has set a mid-September deadline to determine whether the Qatar Grand Prix (Nov 27-29) and the Abu Dhabi Grand Prix (Dec 4-6) can proceed as scheduled. The context is the ongoing geopolitical instability in the region — the same conditions that led to the cancellation of the Bahrain and Saudi Arabian Grands Prix in March, after US and Israeli airstrikes on Iran triggered retaliatory missile and drone attacks that struck Gulf states.

"The 2026 season will definitively end in Europe if the Middle East races cannot go ahead," Domenicali said. The contingency plan is already in place. Imola is the primary candidate to host the Qatar Grand Prix weekend. Turkey, Portugal, and Hockenheim have been identified as options for Abu Dhabi's slot. The World Endurance Championship has already announced it will not race in the Middle East at year-end, lending additional institutional weight to F1's concerns.

The Scale of What This Means

No Formula 1 season has ever lost four race weekends to geopolitical cancellations. F1 cancelled the 2020 Australian Grand Prix at the last minute due to COVID-19 and rearranged that year's calendar significantly — but that was a global event affecting every sporting organization simultaneously. What is happening in the Middle East in 2026 is specific, ongoing, and carrying an entirely different character.

The Gulf region has been central to F1's commercial expansion for more than two decades. The sport has invested heavily in relationships with Bahrain, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and Abu Dhabi — relationships that now face structural uncertainty. Even if Qatar and Abu Dhabi proceed in 2026, the broader trajectory of F1's Middle East presence is being called into question in ways that will outlast any single season.

Domenicali acknowledged the European contingency brings its own logistical complications. A race at Imola in late November or Hockenheim in early December would represent the latest point of the year F1 has raced since the expansion of the calendar into Gulf time zones. Weather uncertainty is real. Attendance would be affected. The television windows are different.

What September Decides

The mid-September deadline gives teams, promoters, and broadcasters approximately six to seven weeks to finalize plans before the Dutch Grand Prix (August 21-23) restarts the season from the summer break. Any decision to cancel Qatar or Abu Dhabi will come with enough notice for the European contingencies to mobilize — circuits that are already on standby, per Domenicali, and have been contacted in advance.

Domains beyond F1 are making the same calculations. The WEC's decision to exit the Middle East calendar provides a useful indicator: organizations with the flexibility to move are moving, without waiting for events to force their hand.

What's Next

The mid-September deadline is the next definitive moment in this story. Before then, the 2026 Dutch Grand Prix at Zandvoort (Aug 21-23) returns F1 to action after the summer break, with the championship battle between Kimi Antonelli (+50 points over Hamilton) taking center stage. The calendar question will be answered while racing resumes.

If Qatar and Abu Dhabi are confirmed, F1 concludes in the Gulf as planned. If they're cancelled, F1 closes in Europe — and the sport will face deeper questions about the future shape of its calendar in a region that has changed fundamentally since the first Middle East F1 race in 2004.
