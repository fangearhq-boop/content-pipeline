Williams' "Almost Entirely New Car" Arrives at Baku — Eight Days Until Vowles' Promise Is Tested

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | September 18, 2026

Williams Racing

James Vowles has not been shy about the scale of what is coming to Baku. The Williams Racing team principal described the upgrade package arriving at the Azerbaijan Grand Prix as "almost an entirely new car" — an ambitious characterization that carries the implicit promise of meaningful performance improvement for a team that has been mired in ninth place in the Constructors' Championship with just 11 points all season.

His drivers are less certain. Carlos Sainz called the upgrade a significant step but issued a "stark warning" to fans expecting a transformation: this is not a rescue package. Alex Albon was similarly grounded, acknowledging the upgrade "won't fix the season." Both drivers know the performance deficit Williams carries relative to the midfield — and a single package, however comprehensive, does not close that gap overnight.

How Williams Got Here

The 2026 season began badly and got worse. Williams missed the January pre-season shakedown at Barcelona, falling behind in the development cycle before the first practice session of the year. The new regulations cycle that began in 2026 — changed floor regulations, updated aerodynamic rules — required teams to have early data to optimize their packages. Williams entered the season without it.

The consequences played out predictably. Williams scored what points they could in the season's early rounds, then went scoreless from Monaco onward. With 11 points in the Constructors' Championship, they are ninth of ten teams. Only one team trails them. The car that Albon and Sainz have been racing is not competitive with the pace the top eight teams are setting.

What the Upgrade Is — and What It Isn't

Vowles' "almost entirely new car" description suggests comprehensive aerodynamic revisions, floor updates, and possibly revised suspension geometry — the categories of changes that affect a car's fundamental behavior through corners and under braking. If the characterization is accurate, this is a car that will feel meaningfully different to Albon and Sainz in the cockpit from the opening practice session.

But Sainz's warning is worth heeding. Williams is not battling for points positions from a baseline deficit of half a second. They are, at some circuits this year, multiple seconds off the leading teams' qualifying pace. An "almost entirely new car" that delivers a half-second or full-second improvement would be a genuine achievement — and still might not put them in Q3 or in fighting positions for the bottom points.

Albon's nuance is also instructive. "Won't fix the season" is an acknowledgment that even a strong Baku result does not undo nine months of development deficit or change the Constructors' Championship standing materially. What Baku can do is validate. It can confirm that Vowles' rebuild project is finding the right direction. It can give Sainz and Albon something to report back with that advances the team's development program. It can, on the right day, score points.

Why Baku Is Both a Harsh and Useful Test

Street circuits are unforgiving of car imbalance. A new aerodynamic package that has not been refined through race-weekend data can behave unpredictably at the walls of Baku, where margin for error is measured in centimeters. If the upgrade introduces characteristics Albon and Sainz have not felt before, the adaptation process begins under maximum pressure.

But Baku's chaos factor also creates opportunity. Safety cars and red flags reset strategy. Grid positions and tire windows matter differently here than at a conventional circuit. On a weekend where the fastest cars can be neutralized by circumstances, a faster Williams — even modestly faster — has more ways to score.

What's Next

Williams' Baku upgrade debut begins in free practice on September 23. The first competitive data arrives in qualifying on September 25. The race runs Saturday, September 26. Vowles, Sainz, and Albon will all face questions about the upgrade throughout the weekend — and the answers come from lap times, not press conferences. If Williams exits Baku with points, the narrative around the team shifts. If not, the 2026 season closes as the rebuild project it has always been. Eight days to find out.
