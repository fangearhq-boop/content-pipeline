F1's End-of-Season Calendar Is Still Unresolved — Qatar and Abu Dhabi Remain in Limbo

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | September 18, 2026

F1 Calendar 2026

Stefano Domenicali promised mid-September. Mid-September came and went. And Formula 1 has said nothing about the Qatar Grand Prix or the Abu Dhabi Grand Prix.

Both races — Qatar scheduled for November 29, Abu Dhabi for December 6 — have been under review for months following ongoing regional conflict that already forced the cancellation of the Bahrain and Saudi Arabian Grands Prix earlier in the 2026 season. Domenicali publicly set a mid-September deadline to resolve the uncertainty. That window is now closed. The silence from F1 headquarters is conspicuous.

What the Cancellations Would Mean

If Qatar and Abu Dhabi are cancelled, the 2026 F1 season would require replacement races — or it would simply end earlier than originally planned. Reports have identified Imola as a leading contingency candidate for at least one of the slots. The Autodromo Enzo e Dino Ferrari in the Emilia-Romagna region of Italy would represent a dramatically different atmosphere for a season finale compared to the Yas Marina Circuit's famous sunset backdrop.

A season that began with Bahrain and Saudi Arabia cancelled and ends without Qatar or Abu Dhabi would represent a significant reshaping of F1's relationship with the Middle East and the financial agreements underpinning those deals. Those races carry substantial hosting fees. Replacement races do not produce equivalent commercial returns.

The Championship Implications

For Kimi Antonelli and the 2026 championship, the calendar uncertainty has limited practical impact. His lead is large enough — 81 points with nine rounds remaining — that whether the season ends at Abu Dhabi or São Paulo changes the calendar math but not the likely outcome. The title will be Antonelli's before those final weekends regardless.

For teams fighting over Constructors' Championship positions — particularly Ferrari and McLaren, separated by 52 points — the number of races matters more. Fewer races mean fewer opportunities for the trailing team to close a gap. If the season ends at São Paulo rather than Abu Dhabi, McLaren loses two rounds in which to catch Ferrari. For them, the calendar resolution is urgent competitive information.

The Precedent

The 2026 season is not the first time F1 has navigated a disrupted calendar. Races have been cancelled, modified, and replaced throughout the sport's history. But removing four Middle Eastern events in a single season — Bahrain, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and Abu Dhabi — would represent the largest single-season reduction from any regional grouping in the modern era. The sport's expansion into the Gulf region was one of its defining commercial narratives of the 2010s and 2020s. A four-race absence tests the durability of those commercial foundations.

What's Next

F1 cannot defer this decision indefinitely. Teams need notice for logistics. Broadcasters need scheduling certainty. The drivers' and teams' calendars run on fixed frameworks. Expect an announcement before the end of September — possibly before the Baku weekend begins September 23. The two most likely outcomes are confirmation that Qatar and Abu Dhabi will proceed as planned, or a replacement calendar announcement that names Imola or another European venue. Either way, the uncertainty ends soon.
