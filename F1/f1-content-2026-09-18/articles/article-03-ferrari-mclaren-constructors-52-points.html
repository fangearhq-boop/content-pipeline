Ferrari Leads McLaren by 52 in Constructors' — 9 Races Left, Gap Is Closing

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | September 18, 2026

Constructors' Championship

Mercedes won the 2026 Constructors' Championship months ago — the Silver Arrows have 503 points and a 145-point lead over the field that renders the top spot essentially locked. But the battle directly below them is very much alive. Ferrari holds second place by 52 points over McLaren with nine races remaining, and McLaren has been on a trajectory that suggests the gap will not hold without a fight.

After the Italian Grand Prix at Monza — one of the season's most power-unit-dependent circuits — Ferrari's lead over McLaren stood at 59 points. After the Spanish Grand Prix in Madrid, it stood at 52. Seven points closed in one race weekend. McLaren is moving in the right direction.

The Teams and Their Strengths

Ferrari's 2026 season has been defined by their power unit advantage and a new technical philosophy that prioritizes race-day stability over qualifying pace. Charles Leclerc and Lewis Hamilton have both been competitive, sitting third and fourth in the Drivers' Championship respectively. On circuits with long straights and high-speed corners — Monza, Baku, Abu Dhabi — Ferrari's engine advantage translates directly into lap time.

McLaren has been building from a different foundation. Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri are among the most technically demanding drivers on the grid in terms of aerodynamic optimization, and McLaren's chassis package has shown consistent strength on flowing, medium-speed circuits. Their aerodynamic updates through the second half of the season have been noticeable. The closing of the constructors' gap is not coincidence — it is methodology.

Why Baku Is a Ferrari Track

The Azerbaijan Grand Prix is one of Formula 1's most extreme power-circuit events. The 2.2-kilometer main straight on the Baku Boulevard section produces the highest top speeds of the season, where raw engine output and low-drag configuration matter more than aerodynamic downforce and cornering efficiency. On paper, this is a Ferrari weekend.

But Baku also produces safety car periods, unexpected failures, and chaos that disrupts any track-characteristics analysis. McLaren has shown they can maximize safety car opportunities and manage tire strategy effectively. A theoretical Ferrari advantage does not automatically translate into a Ferrari result. Norris and Piastri have won races at circuits where McLaren was not expected to.

The Stakes of the Final Nine

With approximately 403 points available across the remaining nine races and their associated sprints, the 52-point gap between Ferrari and McLaren is meaningful but not decisive. A swing weekend — where one team scores maximum points and the other scores nothing — would close or extend the gap by roughly 44 points in a single round. Two such weekends in McLaren's favor would put the second-place fight effectively level heading into the final races.

Ferrari cannot afford complacency. The Italian team has not finished the season with a Constructors' runner-up position since their recent rebuilding years. Holding on to second through the final stretch of the calendar would represent a genuine achievement for the team under their current technical leadership.

What's Next

The Azerbaijan Grand Prix on September 26 is the first of the nine remaining rounds, and the first real test of whether Baku's power-circuit profile adds to Ferrari's lead or helps McLaren chip away further. Both teams will be running their most recent specification packages at Baku — Ferrari's engine power, McLaren's aerodynamics. After Baku: Singapore, Japan, and the rest of a compressed autumn calendar that runs through October and into November. The 52-point conversation continues for at least three more months.
