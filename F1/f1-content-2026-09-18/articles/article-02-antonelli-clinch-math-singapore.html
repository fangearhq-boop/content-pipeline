Antonelli Cannot Clinch at Baku — But Singapore on October 5 Is the Target

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | September 18, 2026

Drivers' Championship

The title cannot be won at Baku. The numbers are unambiguous: even with maximum points on September 26, the gap between Kimi Antonelli and George Russell cannot close far enough in a single race weekend to hand Antonelli the championship. Baku is a step, not a destination.

The destination is Singapore. October 5. That is the earliest possible date on which Kimi Antonelli can become the 2026 Formula 1 World Champion — and one of the most historically significant champions the sport has ever produced.

The Exact Math

After the Spanish Grand Prix — Round 14 of 23 — Antonelli leads the Drivers' Championship with 292 points. George Russell is second with 211, a gap of 81 points. Nine rounds remain, with 233 points still available to be distributed across 9 Grands Prix and one Sprint.

For Antonelli to clinch at Singapore, two things need to happen first. At Baku, he needs to score maximum points — pole position, race victory, fastest lap. Then, in Singapore, he needs to win the Sprint race. That combination would extend his lead enough that a modest result in the Singapore Grand Prix itself — a fourth-place finish, or 12 points by any combination — crosses the title threshold. The Singapore GP runs Sunday, October 5.

This is not a farfetched scenario. Antonelli has won eight Grands Prix in 2026. He has taken six pole positions. He has podiumed 12 times in 14 starts. A maximum-points weekend at Baku is exactly what his season has looked like. A Sprint win at Singapore extends a pattern he has established throughout the year.

The Record He Would Break

Sebastian Vettel became the youngest Formula 1 World Champion in history when he won the 2010 title at the age of 23 years and 134 days. That record has stood for 16 years. No driver has come close.

Kimi Antonelli was born on August 12, 2006. If he clinches the championship at the Singapore Grand Prix on October 5, 2026, he would be approximately 20 years and 54 days old. The margin by which he breaks Vettel's record would be more than three years. In the annals of Formula 1, a gap of that magnitude between records is almost unprecedented.

The word prodigy has been applied to many drivers entering Formula 1. Antonelli has converted that label into something harder: data. Eight wins. Twelve podiums. An 81-point championship lead with nine races remaining. Whatever the superlatives say, the results have been more impressive than the language.

What Russell Needs

George Russell, meanwhile, faces an increasingly mathematical wall. He needs Antonelli to fail — multiple times, in significant fashion — while he himself wins. In 14 races, Antonelli has given Russell one genuine opportunity for that kind of advantage. It did not materialize into the kind of lead reversal that changes a 81-point gap.

Russell is still racing. He will win races. He may win in Singapore. But the trajectory of the 2026 season points in one direction. The question is when, not if.

What's Next

The Azerbaijan Grand Prix runs September 26 at Baku City Circuit. The Singapore Grand Prix follows, with practice beginning October 2 and the race on October 5. If the Singapore clinch scenario unfolds, Antonelli needs maximum points at Baku this Saturday, a Sprint win the following weekend, and 12 points in the Marina Bay race. Three steps. Three weeks. One title. Watch this space.
