Fernando Alonso's Summer of Decision: "This Is Probably My Last Barcelona Race"

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | August 7, 2026

F1 / Fernando Alonso / Aston Martin / Retirement

Fernando Alonso is not the kind of man who says things he does not mean. So when he stood in Barcelona earlier this season and said, "This is probably my last race in Formula 1 here," people listened.

Not because it was a retirement announcement. It wasn't. But because in 23 years of Formula 1 racing — across seven teams, two world championships, and 32 victories — Alonso has always told the truth about where he is. The phrasing was deliberate. "Probably." Not "definitely." But also not nothing.

The summer break is when he promised the decision would come.

The Situation at Aston Martin

Alonso's contract with Aston Martin expires at the end of the 2026 season. He is 44 or 45 years old — depending on the month — and in his 23rd Formula 1 season. The team around him has changed significantly. Adrian Newey, the legendary designer who shaped Red Bull's championship-winning cars, was hired from Red Bull specifically to redesign Aston Martin's future. A Honda power unit partnership is in place. The team's trajectory is upward in a way it has not been for most of Alonso's tenure there.

But "upward" and "competitive" are not the same thing. And the gap to Mercedes and Ferrari has not yet closed enough for Alonso to feel that the project is complete.

His Own Logic

Alonso articulated his decision framework with the clarity that has always defined him: "If we are competitive, there is more chance that I stop. If we are not competitive, it will be very hard to give up without trying again."

It is the logic of a man who will not leave with unfinished business — unless the business is finished. If Aston Martin has given him a car that can fight at the front, he can step away knowing he extracted maximum value from the project. If the car is still short, his perfectionist instinct will push him back.

Sky Sports F1 journalist Ted Kravitz, one of the best-connected reporters in the paddock, has publicly predicted that Alonso retires at the end of 2026. Whether that view reflects conversations in the paddock or simply reading the room, it aligns with what most observers expect.

What This Season Has Looked Like

The 2026 Aston Martin has shown flashes. The car has benefited from Newey's early influence on the development direction, and the Honda power unit is among the more competitive in the field. But Alonso has not yet had the race-winning car he came to Aston Martin hoping to find. The team sits in the midfield of the Constructors' standings — competitive but not yet genuinely threatening the leaders.

For Alonso, that is the specific cruelty of his situation. He is still clearly fast enough. The question is whether the machinery will ever fully match him before he is gone.

What's Next

The Dutch GP on August 21 is the next race, and it arrives as Alonso promised his summer decision would land. Watch for any statement from Alonso or Aston Martin in the days between now and the race weekend. A retirement announcement — if it comes — would likely arrive before Zandvoort rather than at it. Whatever the answer is, it will mark the end of one of the most remarkable chapters in the sport's history.
