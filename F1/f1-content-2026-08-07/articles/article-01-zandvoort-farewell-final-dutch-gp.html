Zandvoort's Final Chapter: The 2026 Dutch GP Is the Last F1 Race at the Circuit

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | August 7, 2026

F1 / Dutch Grand Prix / Circuit Zandvoort / Max Verstappen

In two weeks, Formula 1 returns to Circuit Zandvoort for the last time.

The 2026 Dutch Grand Prix — scheduled for August 21-23 — has been confirmed as the final F1 race at the Dutch circuit. What began as a triumphant homecoming in 2021 will conclude after six editions, six sell-outs, and six years of some of the most electric atmospheres in the modern Formula 1 calendar.

There is no gentle way to say goodbye when the stakes are this high.

Six Years of Orange

When Zandvoort returned to the F1 calendar in 2021 after a 36-year absence, it was simultaneously a nostalgia play and a bet on Max Verstappen's rising star. The Dutch fans delivered on that bet in ways nobody could have fully anticipated.

The circuit sold out every year. The grandstands turned orange. Verstappen won at home — multiple times — and each victory became a national celebration compressed into a narrow strip of dunes and tarmac on the North Sea coast. There was a specific electricity at Zandvoort that few other tracks could replicate: the banking in the Hugenholtz corner, the way the grandstands wrapped around the track, and the sound of 100,000 Dutch fans every time their driver crossed the line.

All of that ends this month.

A Sprint Finale

The 2026 Dutch GP introduces a first for the circuit: a sprint weekend format. The schedule runs across three days — Sprint Qualifying on Friday, the 24-lap Sprint Race and full GP Qualifying on Saturday, and the 72-lap main race on Sunday. It is the first time Zandvoort has hosted the sprint format in its modern F1 incarnation.

Every ticket for the weekend is sold out. The fans, whether or not they know this is the last race, will show up the way they always have.

Verstappen's Home Race Under the Shadow of a Decision

The emotional weight of this farewell is magnified by one fact: Max Verstappen will race at Zandvoort with his Formula 1 future genuinely uncertain.

Verstappen's Red Bull contract contains a performance exit clause that became active after his DNF at the British Grand Prix, which made it mathematically impossible for him to finish in the top two of the championship at the summer break. He sits sixth in the standings, 103 points behind championship leader Kimi Antonelli. From October, he can formally notify Red Bull of his intention to leave.

His talks with McLaren about a potential 2027 drive have been described as being in closing stages by multiple outlets. Whether a deal materializes or not, Verstappen will drive his home race in front of fans who have followed his career since karting, knowing that the chapter he built at Zandvoort is ending — one way or another.

What's Next

The Dutch Grand Prix weekend begins August 21. Sprint Qualifying is Friday at 4:30 PM local time, with the Sprint Race Saturday morning and the main race Sunday at 3:00 PM local time. Every session is expected to be a sellout. Whatever happens on the track, Zandvoort will send Formula 1 off the same way it always has — with orange flags, full grandstands, and an atmosphere that cannot be manufactured anywhere else.
