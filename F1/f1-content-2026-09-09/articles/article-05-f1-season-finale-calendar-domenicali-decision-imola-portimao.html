The F1 Season Finale Decision Is Coming This Week — Domenicali Said Mid-September, and Mid-September Starts Thursday

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | September 9, 2026

FOM CEO Stefano Domenicali promised a decision on the 2026 season finale by mid-September. That window opens Thursday, right as the Madrid Grand Prix weekend begins. Here's where things stand.

The Formula 1 calendar has had an open wound since the cancellation of the Bahrain and Saudi Arabian Grands Prix earlier this year. What the season's final shape looks like — which circuits close the 2026 campaign, how many rounds remain — has been a question without a public answer since those races were removed from the schedule.

That answer is about to arrive. FOM CEO Stefano Domenicali committed publicly to a decision "by mid-September." This Thursday, September 12, is when mid-September begins. The announcement could come during the Madrid race weekend — or it could follow immediately after, early next week. Either way, the window is now.

The Background

The Bahrain and Saudi Arabian Grands Prix were cancelled earlier in 2026 due to geopolitical instability in the region. Their removal created gaps in the calendar that F1 and the FIA have been working to fill through a combination of existing contract extensions and contingency venue negotiations.

Malaysia Sepang was added as a replacement round (October 2-4), filling one of the vacant dates and giving the Asian leg of the schedule additional weight. Qatar (November 27-29) and Abu Dhabi (December 4-6) remain on the calendar and are not affected by the cancellations.

The debate has centered on the end of the season — specifically whether races in the Gulf region that were programmed for November and December remain viable, and if not, what replaces them as the championship's closing rounds.

The Contenders

Two venues have emerged as leading contingency options in reporting throughout the summer: Imola and Portimao.

The Autodromo Internazionale Enzo e Dino Ferrari at Imola last hosted a Formula 1 Grand Prix in 2024 before being removed from the calendar in the subsequent restructure. The circuit retains full homologation infrastructure. Its name recognition and Italian fan base make it a commercially attractive option, and the November weather window would be cooler but manageable.

The Autodromo Internacional do Algarve at Portimao held two pandemic-era Grands Prix in 2020 and 2021. The Portuguese circuit is remembered fondly by drivers for its flowing layout and natural amphitheater spectator sections. It would require reactivating infrastructure that has not seen Formula 1 in five years but has continued operating as a racing venue in the interim.

Neither option is a perfect substitute for the Gulf races, which offered unique atmospheric conditions and strong TV markets. But both represent viable alternatives that could be staged without requiring new circuit construction.

Why the Timing Matters

The Madrid race weekend is about to consume the entire paddock's attention. Domenicali's announcement — whenever it comes — will either arrive before FP1 and immediately be overshadowed by on-track action, or it will land in the week after the race, when the championship implications of Sunday's result have had time to settle.

For the teams, the practical implications of the announcement are immediate. Travel planning, logistics, sponsor commitments, and personnel scheduling all depend on knowing the final calendar. The longer the decision is deferred, the more complex those arrangements become.

For fans, the announcement ends months of uncertainty about where the 2026 Formula 1 championship will be decided. With Antonelli 66 points clear and eight rounds remaining after this weekend, the finale venue could very well be where the championship is confirmed — making the calendar decision's significance extend beyond pure logistics.

What's Next

Watch for Domenicali's announcement during or immediately after the Madrid Grand Prix weekend. The mid-September commitment means a decision is expected within days.
