Leclerc Is Cleared for Madring. Now Ferrari Needs to Make the Most of It.

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | September 9, 2026

Charles Leclerc passed all medical checks after his Monza crash and is cleared to race in Madrid. For Ferrari, that clears the minimum requirement. The real challenge — 87 points down in Constructors' — starts Friday.

Charles Leclerc will race at Madring this weekend. That much was confirmed Monday when Dr. Isabel Ornaque, the medical director for the Spanish Grand Prix, issued full clearance following imaging and neurological evaluation in Monaco. The 23G crash at Monza's Parabolica on Lap 2 of the Italian Grand Prix, which caused right-eye vision issues in the immediate aftermath, left no structural damage.

Leclerc is cleared. He is healthy. He is racing.

Now comes the part that actually matters to Ferrari's season.

The Numbers Ferrari Is Facing

After Round 15, Ferrari's position in the championship is uncomfortable. Charles Leclerc has 155 points — fifth in the Drivers' Championship, 112 points behind Antonelli and already a very long shot for the individual title. Lewis Hamilton, in his first full season with Ferrari, has 191 points — third in the Drivers', 76 behind Antonelli, still in theoretical contention but requiring a significant collapse from the championship leader.

In the Constructors' Championship, Ferrari trails Mercedes by 87 points. That is the number with the clearest viable path. A strong double-points finish from Hamilton and Leclerc at Madring, combined with a difficult weekend for Mercedes, could reduce that gap meaningfully. But it requires exactly the kind of result Ferrari has been unable to sustain consistently against Mercedes' pace advantage this season.

With eight rounds remaining after this weekend, the window for a Constructors' recovery is real but demanding. Ferrari cannot absorb another race like Monza, where Leclerc retired on Lap 2 and Hamilton managed only sixth place.

Why Madring Is Ferrari's Opportunity

Every race on the 2026 calendar so far has carried the weight of Mercedes' accumulated setup knowledge. At circuits like Barcelona, Silverstone, and Monza, teams with decades of data have a structural advantage even before FP1 begins. Ferrari has been competitive throughout the season but has rarely been able to eliminate the marginal advantages that come from institutional experience.

Madring changes that dynamic. For this one weekend, no team has raced here. Nobody has a real-world baseline. The banked corner, the new Tarmac, the specific grip characteristics — these are unknowns for Ferrari and Mercedes equally. A circuit that starts from zero is a circuit where aggressive setup decisions carry less downside risk. Ferrari's engineers have had the same amount of time with Madring data as their rivals: since the circuit's finalized design was released, not a day more.

If Ferrari's simulation work has landed closer to the optimal setup than Mercedes' has, Sunday could produce a very different result than the season's average competitive order. That is not a certainty — it is a genuine possibility that this race offers in ways that established circuits don't.

Leclerc's Personal Motivation

There is a dimension to this weekend beyond points and constructors' standings. Charles Leclerc missed the chance to race at Monza — the circuit most associated with Ferrari's Italian identity — after his Lap 2 retirement. He didn't get to fight there, didn't get to respond to Antonelli's extraordinary recovery drive in front of the same tifosi who had cheered both of them across the season.

Madrid is not Monza. But it is a chance to race rather than watch. Leclerc has had weeks to prepare mentally and physically for a race weekend, to study the Madring layout in the simulator, and to arrive ready rather than recovering. The clearance was the prerequisite. The performance will tell you the rest of the story.

What's Next
