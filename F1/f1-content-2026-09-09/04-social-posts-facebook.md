### STORY 1: Madrid Grand Prix Debut at Madring

**FB Long-Form**
Window: 8:00 AM ET

Formula 1 is going to Madrid this weekend — and nobody has ever been there before.

That might sound like an obvious statement about any new circuit. But at Madring, it means something specific: every team, every driver, every engineer arrives at the 2026 Spanish Grand Prix with simulator data and nothing else. No historical lap times. No setup precedents. No knowledge of how the Tarmac behaves in race conditions at IFEMA Madrid's purpose-built hybrid circuit.

This is F1 in its purest form — everyone on equal footing, for one weekend.

The Madring is a 5.47 km, 22-turn layout that combines public road sections with permanent track, built around the IFEMA exhibition center on Madrid's northeastern edge. Its headline feature is the longest banked corner in modern Formula 1 — a steep, dramatic turn that will load tires differently than anything teams have experienced this season. The main straight sees top speeds approaching 340 kph. Elevation changes run throughout the lap.

The race weekend begins Friday, September 11. FP1 is at 1:30 PM local time (7:30 AM ET), FP2 at 5:00 PM local (11:00 AM ET). Qualifying is Saturday at 4:00 PM local (10:00 AM ET). The race — the first Grand Prix ever held in Madrid, on F1's 81st circuit — starts Sunday, September 13 at 3:00 PM local (9:00 AM ET).

Weather: dry and sunny all weekend, temperatures around 30°C with highs of 33°C on race day.

### STORY 4: Madring Circuit Guide

**FB Long-Form**
Window: 2:00 PM ET

Every circuit on the Formula 1 calendar has decades of data behind it. Monaco. Silverstone. Monza. Spa. Even the newer venues — Baku, Las Vegas, Miami — have been raced at least twice before the 2026 season began.

Madring has been raced zero times. This Friday is the first practice session that has ever been run at the IFEMA Madrid circuit. This weekend, Formula 1 writes the first lines of data for what may become a calendar fixture for decades.

Here is what teams and drivers are working with:

**The Circuit**
5.47 kilometers, 22 corners. The Madring sits at the IFEMA exhibition center on Madrid's northeastern edge — a hybrid circuit that combines sections of public road with purpose-built permanent infrastructure. This combination creates transitions between different surface types, different grip levels, and different load characteristics from corner to corner.

**The Banked Corner**
The circuit's defining feature is a steeply banked turn — the longest and steepest banked corner currently on the Formula 1 calendar. Banked corners create unique tire loading dynamics. The high-speed forces involved push tires differently than a conventional flat-radius turn, and teams have been modeling this feature all season without ever seeing it in real conditions.

**The Straight**
On the main straight, cars reach approximately 340 kph — demanding DRS strategy and strong drag-reduction performance from every car. This puts a premium on low-drag configurations, which must be balanced against downforce requirements for the technical sections.

**The Unknown**
Race conditions in Madrid will be warm and sunny — 30°C throughout the weekend, reaching 33°C on race day. Hot conditions on new Tarmac can produce variable tire degradation, which is another unknown that teams must manage in real time rather than from historical reference.

Every team walks into this weekend equal. The best engineers, the fastest drivers, and the sharpest strategic decisions will decide who is fastest on Sunday.

With the Madrid weekend about to consume all paddock attention, Domenicali's announcement window is now or after the race result Monday morning. F1 fans who have been waiting since the cancellations will know their season calendar before the Constructors' Championship race for this year is resolved.
