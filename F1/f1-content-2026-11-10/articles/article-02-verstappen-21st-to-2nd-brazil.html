Verstappen's Remarkable Charge from 21st to 2nd Is Drive of the Brazilian GP

By Marcus Cole | November 10, 2026

When Max Verstappen lined up 21st at Interlagos on Sunday, it looked like damage limitation at best. What followed was something else entirely.

The three-time world champion drove one of the great performances of his career at the Brazilian Grand Prix, charging from near the back of the grid to second place at the checkered flag. He finished behind only Lando Norris, who had led for most of the race, and ahead of his Red Bull teammate Isack Hadjar, who took third from a far more favorable grid position.

How He Did It

The conditions at Interlagos on Sunday were the kind that separate elite drivers from everyone else — wet through the opening phase and gradually drying as the race progressed. Timing decisions on tire changes, braking points on a slippery surface, and the judgment calls that come with a changing track are exactly the areas where Verstappen has separated himself from the field across his career.

Starting 21st — reportedly due to a qualifying incident — he needed everything to go right: clean early laps, the right tire strategy, and the kind of overtaking execution that is simply not replicable by most drivers. He produced all of it.

By the time the race settled into its second half, Verstappen had carved through the midfield and was hunting down the front-runners. The final classification — 1st Norris, 2nd Verstappen — does not fully capture just how far back he started, or how methodically he worked his way forward.

What It Means

Verstappen cannot win the 2026 Drivers' Championship. He trails Kimi Antonelli by 111 points with three races remaining, and the mathematics are simply unavailable. What Sunday showed, though, is that the competitive spirit that drove him to three consecutive titles has not dimmed.

Some drivers, faced with a points gap they cannot overcome, coast through the final races. Verstappen drove at Interlagos as though the championship was on the line. For fans, for Red Bull, and for the sport, that matters.

Hadjar's Podium

Verstappen's teammate Isack Hadjar delivered his own strong result by finishing third, completing a productive day for Red Bull that could easily have been overshadowed by Verstappen's heroics. Hadjar's podium was a reminder that the team has genuine depth in its lineup and something to build on heading into the final rounds.

What's Next

Las Vegas is next on November 21. Verstappen will be looking to continue building on the momentum of his Brazilian performance at a circuit that rewards the kind of precision and aggression he showed at Interlagos. Even with no championship to chase, the remaining races matter — to the team, to the fans, and clearly to Verstappen himself.
