Leclerc Demoted After Brazil Penalty as Ferrari Launches SF-26 Investigation

By Ryan Calloway | November 10, 2026

It was not the Sunday that Ferrari had hoped for at Interlagos, and the post-race fallout has made it even harder to process.

Charles Leclerc crossed the finish line in seventh place at the Brazilian Grand Prix, only to be demoted to eighth after the FIA handed him a five-second time penalty for pushing Liam Lawson of Racing Bulls wide during a late-race overtake. The penalty cost Leclerc a position and added to what was already a frustrating day for the Scuderia.

The Penalty and Its Context

The incident between Leclerc and Lawson came late in the race when Leclerc was fighting for position. The FIA determined that Leclerc had moved on Lawson and pushed the Racing Bulls driver wide, applying the five-second penalty that dropped him behind in the final classification. Leclerc had been among a group of drivers assessed penalties in Brazil — Kimi Antonelli and Ollie Bearman also received five-second penalties, though neither lost a place as a result.

For Leclerc, the penalty was the punctuation mark on a race that had not gone well from the start.

Hamilton's Public Frustration

Lewis Hamilton, Ferrari's other driver, finished ninth on the road. After the race, Hamilton was vocal in his frustration, saying publicly that he was "devastated" by the SF-26's pace at Interlagos. The quote captured a wider sentiment within the team about the car's performance as the season enters its final stretch.

Ferrari has since announced that it has launched an investigation into the SF-26's performance. For a team that entered 2026 with championship aspirations, the season's final phase has been a source of genuine concern.

What's Next

Ferrari heads to Las Vegas on November 21 needing answers — both on the car's technical performance and on the operational side that has contributed to some of their recent difficulties. The constructors' championship is out of their reach (Mercedes clinched it in Brazil), so the remaining races become about pride, development data, and setting up 2027. That is a significant step down from where the team hoped to be at this point in the season.

Both Leclerc and Hamilton will be looking to put Brazil behind them in Las Vegas and give the fans a reminder of what Ferrari can be when everything clicks.
