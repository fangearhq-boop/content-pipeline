Antonelli Leads F1 Drivers' Championship by 111 Points — Three Races to Make History

By Elena Voss | November 10, 2026

It is no longer a question of whether Kimi Antonelli will be the 2026 Formula 1 World Champion. It is simply a question of when.

After Sunday's Brazilian Grand Prix, the Mercedes driver leads the standings by 111 points from Max Verstappen, with three rounds remaining. The updated standings: Antonelli 390 points, Verstappen 279, Russell 262, Hamilton 261. There is not enough points available in the remaining races for anyone to close that gap.

The Math Is Decisive

Formula 1 awards 25 points for a race win, with additional points available via sprint races in some rounds. Even in the most favorable scenario for his rivals — taking every available point while Antonelli scores nothing — the gap cannot be closed in three rounds. The 2026 Drivers' Championship belongs to Antonelli.

Sunday at Interlagos was not Antonelli's finest day. He was leading the race before spinning at Turn 1 on Lap 20, dropping through the field and eventually finishing fifth. Even so, he left Brazil with his championship lead intact and his title inevitable.

A Historic Rookie Season

What makes Antonelli's pending title so extraordinary is the context. This is his debut full season in Formula 1 — a category he entered this year after graduating from the junior feeder series. For a rookie to not only compete with established champions but to dominate the standings is a feat that happens perhaps once a generation.

His season has included race victories, podiums, and the kind of consistent points haul that championship campaigns are built on. Even in difficult rounds like Brazil, where mistakes happened, the points cushion he has accumulated across the season proved its worth.

The Title Could Come in Las Vegas

The next race is the Las Vegas Grand Prix on November 21. Depending on the results of Antonelli and those chasing him, the title could be mathematically confirmed in Las Vegas — a celebration that would take place on the Las Vegas Strip in front of one of the sport's most global audiences. It would be a memorable scene regardless of whether it unfolds there or in one of the subsequent rounds.

What's Next

Three races remain: Las Vegas, and then two more. Each is an opportunity for Antonelli to put the finishing touches on what will be remembered as one of the most remarkable debut championship seasons in Formula 1 history. The sport has found its next great champion.
