Ferrari's Quiet Constructors Battle — Hamilton and Leclerc Keep Pushing at Singapore

By Elena Voss | October 7, 2026

The Drivers' Championship narrative in 2026 has been dominated by Kimi Antonelli and George Russell. But beneath that headline battle, Ferrari has been quietly building a case in the Constructors' Championship, and Singapore represents another opportunity to close the gap.

Lewis Hamilton's third-place finish in Malaysia keeps Ferrari's tally climbing. Charles Leclerc sits fourth in the Drivers' standings with 191 points, Hamilton third with 214. Combined, Ferrari is scoring points at a rate that suggests the Constructors' fight for second is far from settled.

Hamilton's First Ferrari Season

Hamilton's move to Ferrari was the defining transfer story of the 2025-26 offseason. After seven World Championships at Mercedes, he chose to spend what many assumed would be the final years of his career in Maranello. The 2026 campaign has been a solid if not spectacular first season — consistent podiums, no dramatic failures, and a learning curve that has clearly been navigated successfully.

At 41, Hamilton is still producing results that justify his place in one of the sport's most coveted seats. Malaysia's third place was characteristically professional — not the dominant win his fans wanted, but valuable championship points executed without error.

Leclerc and the Home Front

Charles Leclerc remains Ferrari's designated No. 1 driver in terms of championship performance. His fourth place in the standings at 191 points represents a frustrating season — close enough to the podium of the championship to make you wonder what might have been with a different bounce of racing luck, but not close enough to be a genuine title contender at this stage.

Leclerc and Hamilton have worked effectively together at Ferrari, and their combined Constructors points total shows the result of a team that has two drivers capable of scoring big on any given weekend. At Singapore, if both drivers deliver top-five finishes, Ferrari makes another statement in the Constructors table.

Singapore and the Ferrari Opportunity

Marina Bay has historically been a circuit where Ferrari has competed strongly. The tight, technical nature of the street circuit, heavy on braking stability and mechanical grip, suits certain car characteristics that Ferrari have developed well over the years. With their technical team likely to have analyzed their Malaysia setup carefully, Singapore could be a venue where Ferrari close the gap to the manufacturers' standings leader.

The Constructors' title may be out of reach — Mercedes' combined total is well ahead — but runner-up in the Constructors' Championship would be a significant result for the Maranello team and a foundation to build on for 2027.

What's Next

Ferrari arrives at Singapore with both drivers motivated to score. The Sprint format means additional points are available Saturday — a Sprint win or top-three from Hamilton or Leclerc would be a meaningful statement. Watch how Ferrari adapt their setup with limited practice time under the Singapore night lights. That adaptation will tell a lot about where this car stands on a technical street circuit.
