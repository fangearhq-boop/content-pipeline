Qatar GP Qualifying Tonight: Antonelli Tops FP1 and FP2 at Lusail as Championship Climax Approaches

By Ryan Calloway

November 28, 2026

Kimi Antonelli arrived at Lusail already carrying the 2026 Drivers' Championship, and he spent both practice sessions on Friday making sure everyone knew it. The young Mercedes driver set the fastest time in FP1 and repeated the feat in FP2, posting a 1:23.077 in the afternoon session to stay ahead of his nearest rivals heading into tonight's qualifying (18:00 GMT, 21:00 local).

George Russell backed him up in second during FP2, 0.127 seconds behind. Lando Norris was third, Oscar Piastri fourth, and Max Verstappen fifth. The order matters in the context of a championship fight that is still very much alive between Verstappen and Lewis Hamilton for the P2 position in the final standings.

Antonelli Controls the Weekend

Practice is practice — no one forgets that. Teams use Friday to gather data, test setups, and understand tyre behavior rather than purely chase lap times. But when the same driver tops both sessions at a circuit that tends to produce predictable qualifying orders, it signals genuine pace.

Antonelli has already secured the championship that eluded every other competitor this season. At 20 years old, he became the youngest F1 champion in history with a dominant campaign that produced nine victories and 402 points. What he is chasing in Qatar and Abu Dhabi is the conclusion that completes the story — finishing the season with the same dominance that defined it.

At Lusail specifically, Antonelli will have studied the data carefully. The Qatari track rewards aerodynamic efficiency and tyre management under high-speed cornering loads. Mercedes has shown an ability to perform well across varied circuit types, and Friday's pace suggests the car is well-balanced for the conditions.

Verstappen at P5 — Significance or Setup Run?

Max Verstappen's P5 in FP2 will attract scrutiny, particularly given the championship implications of this weekend. If Verstappen qualifies and races near the front, he comfortably defends the 37-point advantage he holds over Lewis Hamilton. If his car is genuinely off the pace, Saturday's qualifying and Sunday's race become much more complicated.

Red Bull rarely shows all their cards in Friday practice. The P5 position in FP2 is consistent with a team that may be prioritising race setup over qualifying pace early in the weekend. Verstappen has won at Lusail three consecutive times and understands this circuit as well as any driver on the grid. His Friday position should not be overread as a sign of weakness.

The gap to Antonelli in FP2 was not publicly confirmed in the initial results, but Russell's 0.127-second deficit places the top of the timesheet in a relatively close cluster. How that cluster reshapes itself in Q1 through Q3 will determine the conversation heading into Sunday.

What Qualifying Means for the Championship

Tonight's session carries championship weight beyond just pole position. For Lewis Hamilton, strong qualifying is essential to building the race setup needed to outscore Verstappen by approximately 19 points on Sunday. For the Russell-Norris battle for P4 in the standings, qualifying position directly influences their strategic options during the mandatory 2-stop race.

Perez, carrying a five-place grid penalty regardless of where he qualifies, will be watching the session with a different agenda — finding the grid position that allows him to minimise the damage of starting further back.

What's Next

Qualifying runs tonight starting at 18:00 GMT. The race is Sunday, November 29 at 16:00 GMT. The championship fight, the Russell-Norris P4 duel, and the broader season finale storylines all converge this weekend in Qatar before the final round in Abu Dhabi. Antonelli's practice dominance sets the expectation. The qualifying session in a few hours will confirm whether that expectation was warranted.
