Hamilton's Last Stand: 37 Points Down on Verstappen With Just 50 Left Across Qatar and Abu Dhabi

By Marcus Cole

November 28, 2026

Lewis Hamilton needs 37 more points than Max Verstappen across the final two races of the 2026 Formula 1 season. That is the number. With 50 points available across Qatar and Abu Dhabi, the math is narrow but technically possible. It requires near-perfection from Hamilton, meaningful misfortune from Verstappen, or some combination of both. As qualifying night approaches at Lusail, Hamilton's bid for the P2 championship position enters its final chapter.

Verstappen leads the P2 battle at 304 points. Hamilton is at 267. Those are the standings after 21 rounds, following Verstappen's third consecutive Las Vegas victory last Saturday. Each race adds to the gap in Verstappen's favor — or erases a portion of it in Hamilton's. At Qatar this weekend, there is no longer any room for a mid-pack finish, a mechanical retirement, or a points-scoring exit from contention that leaves the gap exactly as it was.

The Specific Math

The maximum available from a single race is 25 points for a win plus 1 bonus point for fastest lap — 26 total if everything goes right. For Hamilton to gain the maximum on Verstappen in one race, he would need to win with fastest lap while Verstappen finishes outside the points entirely. Even then, that is only 26 points — not enough on its own to close a 37-point gap.

What is realistically required: Hamilton needs to outscore Verstappen by roughly 19 points in each of the final two races. That means consistent podium performances from Hamilton, ideally race wins, while Verstappen finishes outside the top 10 or suffers mechanical trouble. These are not impossible scenarios, but they are unlikely combinations that require sustained good fortune to materialise.

The sporting reality is that Verstappen needs only to manage his weekend. A strong qualifying, a points finish in the race, and the gap either closes insufficiently or stays exactly where it is. Verstappen has won three consecutive times at Las Vegas; he knows how to close out a season.

Qatar Is a Different Track

Lusail Circuit does not necessarily favor any single team the way some other circuits do, but the characteristics — high-speed corners, long straights, and significant tyre thermal load — tend to produce close battles and strategic uncertainty. The mandatory 2-stop strategy this year adds layers of complexity that neither driver can control entirely.

Hamilton showed P5 form is within reach for Verstappen in FP2, with the Red Bull driver behind the Mercedes and McLaren cars. If that gap carries into qualifying and the race, Hamilton has genuine opportunities. But Verstappen's pattern in qualifying is to find time that Friday practice sessions do not indicate. He has rarely been caught off-guard by a Saturday session that overturns a Friday form guide.

What Hamilton Said

Hamilton has remained committed publicly to pushing until the mathematics become impossible. At 41 years old, in his first full season with Ferrari, he has spoken about the motivation of competing for every available championship position until the flag drops on Abu Dhabi. The P2 fight with Verstappen has been the defining battle of the second half of the season — a weekly reminder that the championship may belong to Antonelli, but the fight for the final standings spots is fully alive.

What's Next

Qualifying tonight at 18:00 GMT gives Hamilton the first measurement of the weekend. A front-row position changes the conversation for Sunday. A result outside the top five makes the arithmetic even harder. The race is Sunday at 16:00 GMT. If Hamilton cannot substantially close the gap in Qatar, the Abu Dhabi finale becomes a ceremonial conclusion rather than a championship chase — 37 points in one race is achievable only in extreme scenarios.
