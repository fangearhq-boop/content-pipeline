Russell and Norris Enter Qatar Dead Even at 264 Points: The P4 Battle That Won't Be Decided Until Abu Dhabi

By Elena Voss

November 28, 2026

The P4 championship fight between George Russell and Lando Norris is a coin flip with two races left. Both drivers enter the Qatar Grand Prix weekend with 264 points apiece — an exact tie that transforms the final stages of the 2026 season into a straight head-to-head contest. In FP2 on Friday, Russell went P2 and Norris P3. The practice session separated them by a narrow margin. The championship will narrow it further over the next two race weekends.

How They Got Here

The two drivers have tracked each other closely for most of the second half of the season, trading positions in the standings as results fluctuated. Neither managed to build a decisive gap. Russell had the advantage heading into Las Vegas, but the race outcome tightened the battle back to a numerical dead heat. With 50 points remaining across Qatar and Abu Dhabi, every race finish and fastest lap point matters equally.

Russell drives for Mercedes, the team that produced the 2026 champion in Kimi Antonelli. His car is well-suited to Qatar based on Friday's practice data — P2 behind his own teammate in FP2 is a strong signal of performance on a circuit where Mercedes has historically been competitive. The challenge is that McLaren's car is equally fast, and Norris has shown the ability to find qualifying tenths that practice sessions do not indicate.

What P4 Means in the Final Standings

Finishing P4 in the Drivers' Championship for a season in which Kimi Antonelli was dominant at P1 carries real significance. It signals that you were the fourth-best driver across a 23-race season — a result that reflects team performance, driver consistency, and the ability to maximise scoring opportunities regardless of race outcome. For both Russell and Norris, it is also a personal pride point and a marker for contract and performance discussions.

Neither driver is competing for the championship itself — Antonelli clinched that weeks ago. But the internal battle for final standings positions in a year where the top-four separations are tight is exactly the kind of secondary storyline that makes F1's later season races compelling viewing even after the championship is decided.

Qatar Could Break the Tie — Or Deepen It

If both Russell and Norris finish in similar positions on Sunday — say, P3 and P4 — they will score similar points and carry a near-equal gap into Abu Dhabi. The final race then decides everything. If one of them significantly outscores the other in Qatar — a race win versus a midfield finish, for instance — the Abu Dhabi finale becomes a mathematically simpler picture.

The mandatory 2-stop structure at Lusail and the safety car variability that tends to accompany it mean Sunday's race is unlikely to follow a simple script. Both drivers have everything to gain from an aggressive, early strategic call that puts them ahead of the other on the road.

What's Next

Qualifying tonight at 18:00 GMT gives both Russell and Norris their first meaningful measurement of the weekend. How they qualify relative to each other will indicate the trajectory heading into Sunday's race at 16:00 GMT. With 264 points each and 50 available, every decision over the next two race weekends matters. The P4 championship will not be settled tonight, but it may become clearer after qualifying which driver has the momentum advantage entering the season's final stretch.
