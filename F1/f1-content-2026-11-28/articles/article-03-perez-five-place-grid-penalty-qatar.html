Perez Starts Five Places Back: How the Grid Penalty Complicates an Already Difficult Qatar Weekend

By Elena Voss

November 28, 2026

Sergio Perez will carry a five-place grid penalty into Sunday's Qatar Grand Prix, applied to his qualifying position after the FIA held him fully responsible for the collision with Franco Camara during the Las Vegas Grand Prix. Whatever position Perez qualifies tonight at Lusail, he will begin the race five rows lower on the grid. It is a significant disadvantage on a track where clean air and early positioning are valuable — and where the mandatory 2-stop strategy already creates enough chaos without the additional burden of fighting through traffic from deep on the grid.

What the Penalty Means in Practice

Five grid places at a race like Qatar translate directly into lost opportunity. The opening lap at Lusail is a high-speed affair where positions are won and lost in the first corner and through the long first sector. Teams starting from the lower midfield typically spend the opening five to ten laps fighting for track position rather than executing the race strategy they had planned.

For Perez, this means that even if he qualifies in a solid position — say, inside the top eight — he will start the race from outside the top 10. The mandatory 2-stop structure creates strategic levers to recover, but those levers work best from the front of the field, not from behind a pack of cars all making similar stop windows.

The Las Vegas Context

The FIA's finding was clear: Perez was fully responsible for the contact that ended Camara's race in Las Vegas. The five-place penalty reflects the severity of the judgment. Camara, who was making his Formula 1 debut at Haas last weekend, was removed from the race without completing a meaningful stint — a result the stewards determined was caused entirely by Perez's driving.

Red Bull will be working to minimise the damage from both the penalty itself and the broader reputational context of the incident heading into the season's final stages. With the constructors' championship picture still being sorted and Verstappen's P2 fight against Hamilton in progress, Perez needs to maximise every opportunity to contribute points to the team's total.

The 2-Stop Variable

Pirelli's 27-lap maximum tyre stint limit at Qatar forces every car into at least two pit stops across the 57-lap race. For Perez starting from deep on the grid, this creates specific timing challenges. His first stop may come under crowded conditions, with other cars cycling through their own stops at similar windows. A slow stop, a close call in the box, or a tire that does not come up to temperature immediately could compound the qualifying penalty and make a points finish increasingly difficult.

Red Bull's pit crew is among the strongest in the paddock, and Perez is an experienced race manager who understands how to work through a difficult strategic position. The route back to the points exists. It simply requires everything to go right from a compromised starting point.

What's Next

Qualifying tonight at 18:00 GMT determines Perez's starting position before the five-place drop is applied. The key question heading into Sunday: can a Red Bull car still be competitive enough in race trim to recover the positions lost from the grid penalty, or does Lusail's track characteristics and the 2-stop mandatory structure make a points finish impossible from so far back? The race at 16:00 GMT on Sunday provides the answer.
