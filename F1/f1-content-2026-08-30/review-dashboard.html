### Long-Form Post Window: 2:00 PM ET THE MONACO GRAND PRIX PODIUM IS STILL UP IN THE AIR 🇲🇨 Here is where the Pierre Gasly Monaco appeal situation stands as of today: 📋 WHAT HAPPENED In the Monaco Grand Prix, Gasly received a pit lane speeding penalty — along with several other drivers — based on speed measurements at Monaco's unique pitlane entrance. Alpine later filed a successful Right of Review, demonstrating that the pitlane speed measurement system had been operating incorrectly. FOM admitted the error. Gasly was reinstated to P3. However, the complication is that other penalised drivers — like Oscar Piastri — had already served their penalties during the race, losing positions they never got back. ⚖️ THE APPEAL Both McLaren (representing Piastri's interests) and Red Bull formally appealed Gasly's reinstatement to the FIA's International Court of Appeal (ICA). The closed-door hearing took place on August 25 in Paris, in front of a four-judge panel. ⏱️ THE TIMELINE The judges have indicated the verdict will NOT be immediate — the case involves complex arguments about how served penalties should interact with overturned rulings. According to reports, the decision is expected in approximately two to three weeks from the hearing date (August 25), putting the verdict around mid-September. Current status: Gasly holds P3 in the Monaco GP results. Alpine retains those championship points. Everything could change in two weeks. ### Long-Form Post Window: 5:00 PM ET KIMI ANTONELLI'S TITLE LEAD IS INTACT — BUT MONZA COULD TEST IT SEVERELY 🏆 The numbers are straightforward: Kimi Antonelli leads the 2026 Formula 1 World Championship by 59 points. He has been extraordinary all season — an Italian teenager (now 20) leading a world championship in his debut season, often under enormous pressure. But here is the thing about Monza: Antonelli will start from the very back of the grid. Mercedes made the calculated decision to take a complete power unit change at Monza — accepting a grid penalty in exchange for fresh, reliable components for the final seven rounds of the 2026 season. The logic is sound in theory: sacrifice one race to ensure you have the best possible machinery heading into the run-in. The math, however, is unforgiving. Starting from the back at one of F1's fastest circuits, with Lando Norris in red-hot form and Ferrari at their motivational peak on home soil, puts Antonelli in a position he has never been in before this season. What are the scenarios? • Antonelli fights from back to P6, Norris wins: Gap shrinks from 59 to ~34 points • Antonelli fails to score, Norris wins: Gap closes to ~34 points (25-pt swing) • Antonelli fights to P8, Russell or Hamilton wins: Gap shrinks, Mercedes "team" stays ahead • Ferrari wins at home: The championship narrative completely resets For Antonelli, this weekend is about damage limitation.
