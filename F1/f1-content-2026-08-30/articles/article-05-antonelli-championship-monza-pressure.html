Antonelli Leads by 59 Points — But Starts Last at His Home Race. Can He Survive Monza?

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | August 30, 2026

Kimi Antonelli · Mercedes · F1 Championship · Italian Grand Prix

Kimi Antonelli has done everything right this season. The 20-year-old Italian, in his debut Formula 1 campaign with Mercedes, has built a 59-point championship lead that, three weeks ago, looked like the foundation of a comfortable title run. Then came the power unit decision — and now everything Antonelli has built faces its most significant single-race test.

At Monza. His home circuit. Starting from the back of the grid.

Why Mercedes Made This Call

Mercedes' decision to take a complete power unit change at Monza is, in the cold logic of championship mathematics, defensible. The team is effectively sacrificing one weekend to secure fresh, reliable components for the remaining seven rounds: Azerbaijan, Singapore, Austin, Mexico, Brazil, Las Vegas, and Abu Dhabi. Seven races representing 175 maximum championship points. Having mechanical confidence for that entire run-in is worth absorbing a grid penalty now — especially with a 59-point buffer.

The calculation only breaks down if rivals turn Antonelli's compromised Monza weekend into a watershed moment. With Lando Norris carrying back-to-back victories into the Temple of Speed, that risk is very real.

The Damage Limitation Exercise

Starting from the back of the grid at Monza is not automatically catastrophic. The circuit has long, fast straights and relatively few overtaking zones compared to its reputation — but a car with strong power-unit performance, which the Mercedes W17 certainly has, can make progress during the early laps as the field spreads out.

The danger is not in the overtaking itself — it is in the exposure. A driver fighting through traffic at 350 km/h faces risks that a driver running in clear air does not. Wheel-to-wheel incidents, contact with mid-field competitors, unpredictable tire strategies from those fighting for points — all of these become variables that the championship leader must navigate while his rivals run free at the front.

Mercedes confirmed there will be no team orders governing how George Russell and Lewis Hamilton conduct themselves in the race. Both drivers — each capable of winning at Monza — will race for their own results, which means Antonelli will not benefit from support from within his own team.

The Scenarios That Matter

The championship arithmetic depends heavily on how the race unfolds. If Antonelli recovers to a top-six or top-eight finish and Norris wins, the gap shrinks but remains healthy — perhaps 40 to 50 points depending on the specific finishing positions. If Antonelli is involved in an incident and fails to score while Norris takes maximum points, the gap could close to as few as 58 points with six races after Monza still to run.

It is worth noting: 58 points with six races remaining is not comfortable for a championship leader. It is one exceptional weekend from a full-blown title fight.

What's Next

Practice sessions at Monza begin Friday, September 4. Qualifying is Saturday, September 5. The Italian Grand Prix runs Sunday, September 6 at 2:00 PM local (8:00 AM ET). After Monza, the title fight may look very different — or Antonelli may demonstrate that championship leaders get to the back of the grid precisely because they know how to get back to the front. The answer comes Sunday.
