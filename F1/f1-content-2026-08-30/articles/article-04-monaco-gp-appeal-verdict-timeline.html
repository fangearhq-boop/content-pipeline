Monaco GP Appeal Heard — But Gasly's P3 Won't Be Settled Until Mid-September

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | August 30, 2026

Pierre Gasly · Alpine · Monaco Grand Prix · FIA · F1 Appeals

The FIA's International Court of Appeal heard arguments on August 25 in Paris. The four-judge panel listened to McLaren, Red Bull, and Alpine make their respective cases regarding Pierre Gasly's reinstated podium finish at the Monaco Grand Prix. And then it adjourned — without a verdict — because the complexity of what is being decided requires more time than a single hearing day can provide.

According to reports, the decision is not expected for another two to three weeks from the hearing date, which places the ruling around mid-September. Until then, Pierre Gasly officially holds third place in the Monaco Grand Prix, and Alpine retains those championship points.

How We Got Here

The origins of this dispute trace back to race day in Monaco, where several drivers — including Gasly and Oscar Piastri — received pit lane speeding penalties based on measurements taken at Monaco's unique pitlane entrance. The penalties were applied and served. The race ended, results were published, and that seemed to be that.

Then Alpine filed a Right of Review. The argument: the pitlane speed measurement system at Monaco was operating incorrectly. Formula One Management, the commercial rights holder, acknowledged the error. The FIA's stewards accepted Alpine's review and reinstated Gasly to the third-place position he had held before the penalty.

The complication: Piastri had already served his penalty during the race, dropping positions that he never recovered. Reinstating Gasly to P3 did not restore Piastri's lost time. McLaren, arguing on Piastri's behalf, and Red Bull — whose driver also had an interest in the reclassification — formally appealed to the ICA.

The Legal Question

The issue before the four-judge panel is not simple. At its core, the question is: when a penalty is served during a race and later found to have been incorrectly applied, what remedy is appropriate? Simply reinstating the driver who was penalised does not undo the advantage already lost by drivers who served their own (equally incorrect) penalties and did not have the opportunity for redress.

McLaren's argument, in effect, is that the reinstatement of Gasly created an inequitable outcome. Red Bull's position aligns with that concern. The ICA must determine whether the reinstatement was the correct legal remedy under F1's sporting regulations — or whether a different approach should have been taken.

The complexity of these arguments is precisely why the verdict is expected to take weeks rather than days.

What Hangs in the Balance

If the ICA upholds Gasly's P3, Alpine retains the championship points and the Monaco GP results stand as currently classified. If the ICA overturns the reinstatement, Gasly loses his podium finish, Piastri moves back up to his position, and Alpine's points total changes accordingly.

For Alpine and Gasly specifically, losing those Monaco points would hurt — a podium finish represents 15 championship points, and in a tightly contested Constructors' Championship, every point is significant.

What's Next

The verdict is expected around mid-September — potentially arriving in the week following the Italian Grand Prix at Monza (September 4-6). When the ruling comes, it will either close the Monaco chapter for good or open another round of debate about how F1 should handle post-race penalty adjustments in situations where penalties have already been served. The sport's regulators are navigating genuinely unprecedented territory.
