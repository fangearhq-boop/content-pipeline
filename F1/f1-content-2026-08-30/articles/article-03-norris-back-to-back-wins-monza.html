Norris Has Won Back-to-Back — Is Monza the Race That Makes Him a Real Title Threat?

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | August 30, 2026

Lando Norris · McLaren · Italian Grand Prix · F1 Championship

Lando Norris is the best driver in Formula 1 right now. Over the past two race weekends, he has been simply unstoppable — back-to-back victories that have reminded the paddock of what McLaren at their absolute best looks like. And now comes Monza, with Kimi Antonelli starting from last and the championship suddenly feeling fragile in a way it has not for months.

The Form Is Undeniable

Norris's victory at the Dutch Grand Prix was the statement win of the second half of the season. Zandvoort, with its banked corners and uniquely demanding technical requirements, is not a circuit that rewards passivity — and Norris was anything but passive. He drove with the kind of controlled aggression that champions use, managing tire performance while maintaining the pace needed to win comfortably.

That makes consecutive wins. Two races since the mid-season break, two victories. The McLaren MCL60 is operating at its maximum, and Norris is extracting every ounce of performance the car has to offer.

The Arithmetic at Monza

Before this weekend, the championship gap was 83 points — daunting but not impossible with seven races remaining. The standard assumption was that Antonelli would continue to collect points at a consistent rate while Norris chased. At Monza, the dynamic changes.

Antonelli will start from the back of the grid. In a best-case scenario for Mercedes, their driver fights brilliantly through traffic and salvages meaningful points — perhaps a top-ten finish. In a worst case, an incident in the opening laps leaves him out of the points entirely. Ferrari's ADUO-2 power unit debut at a circuit where straight-line speed is everything adds another variable.

If Norris wins and Antonelli scores zero points, the gap drops from 83 to 58 with seven races remaining — 175 maximum points still available. Suddenly the championship is genuinely alive.

Why Monza Suits McLaren

Monza is the fastest circuit in Formula 1 — drivers spend approximately 80% of each lap at full throttle, reaching speeds above 350 kilometers per hour on the famous main straight. It is a circuit that rewards power, efficiency, and low drag. McLaren has shown all season that they can extract strong straight-line performance from the MCL60, and Norris's smooth driving style minimizes the tire wear that can become a factor on Monza's abrasive surface in the later stages.

This is not a guarantee. Ferrari's upgraded power unit could change the power balance. But the conditions are favorable for Norris to extend his winning streak to three — which would put him in championship territory nobody anticipated.

What's Next

The Italian Grand Prix runs September 4-6, with the race on Sunday at 2:00 PM local (8:00 AM ET). For Norris, this weekend is the clearest path yet to turning what has been a strong second half of the season into a genuine championship run. The question is not whether he can win at Monza — it is what happens to the leader when he does. The next chapter of the 2026 Formula 1 season starts Sunday.
