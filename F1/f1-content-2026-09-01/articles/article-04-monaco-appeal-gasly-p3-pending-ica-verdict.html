Monaco Grand Prix Result Still Unresolved — ICA Verdict on Gasly's P3 Expected This Month

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | September 1, 2026

The Monaco Grand Prix was held on June 7. It is now September 1. The official final result is still not confirmed.

What began as a standard race-day penalty dispute has evolved into Formula 1's most legally complicated result of the 2026 season — and the FIA's International Court of Appeal has yet to deliver its verdict, nearly three months after the race itself.

The Full Timeline

The chain of events that created this situation:

  • June 7: Monaco Grand Prix race. Alpine's Pierre Gasly finishes third on track. Post-race, he receives two five-second penalties for speeding in the pit lane — dropping him from P3 to P7.
  • June 12: Alpine successfully exercises its right of review, presenting evidence that FOM's timing data contained a measurement error. Stewards accept the evidence and restore Gasly to P3. Red Bull's Isack Hadjar drops off the podium.
  • Late June: Both McLaren and Red Bull Racing lodge formal appeals with the FIA International Court of Appeal, arguing the stewards exceeded their jurisdiction in reversing the original penalty.
  • August 25: The ICA holds its hearing at FIA headquarters in Paris, behind closed doors. Both sides present their arguments.
  • September 1: Verdict still pending. The FIA has indicated a likely timeline of mid-September.

The Core Legal Question

The appeal does not dispute that FOM's timing data contained an error. The FIA itself acknowledged the measurement mistake. What McLaren and Red Bull are arguing is procedural: did the stewards have the authority, under the FIA Sporting Code, to reverse a penalty on the basis of evidence that was disputed — even if that evidence was subsequently accepted?

It is a narrow legal argument with significant sporting consequences. If the ICA rules that the stewards exceeded their remit, Gasly loses P3. Isack Hadjar (Red Bull) regains the podium. Championship points across the midfield are redistributed. In a season this competitive, that redistribution matters.

The Stakes for the Title

Gasly currently sits fifth in the Drivers' Championship with Alpine. Hadjar is sixth with Red Bull. The point difference between P3 and P7 at Monaco is 10 points. That swing could affect both drivers' final championship positions, and potentially shift Constructor standings in the midfield battle.

The ICA's mid-September verdict is expected to land during or shortly after Italian Grand Prix weekend. Formula 1 could simultaneously be navigating its most consequential championship race weekend and its most contentious legal decision of the year.

What's Next

The ICA verdict is expected in mid-September. When it arrives, it will either confirm Gasly's P3 or restore Hadjar to the podium — and close the book on the longest-running race result dispute in recent F1 memory. Until then, Monaco 2026 remains, officially, unresolved.
