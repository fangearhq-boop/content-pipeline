F1 2026 Championship Standings: Antonelli's 59-Point Lead Is on the Line at Monza

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | September 1, 2026

For most of the 2026 Formula 1 season, Kimi Antonelli has looked untouchable. Six wins. Six pole positions. Ten podiums in fifteen races. A 59-point lead in the Drivers' Championship heading into the Italian Grand Prix.

This weekend, the picture changes.

Antonelli starts Sunday's race from the back of the 20-car grid after Mercedes took a strategic full power-unit change on his car. Lando Norris — who has won the last two races and sits 83 points behind — starts near the front. The math of what could happen in one afternoon at Monza is stark.

The Current Standings

Heading into Round 16 at Monza, the Drivers' Championship reads:

  1. Kimi Antonelli (Mercedes): 242 points
  2. George Russell (Mercedes): 183 points
  3. Lewis Hamilton (Ferrari): 183 points
  4. Lando Norris (McLaren): 159 points

Antonelli's 59-point lead over Russell and Hamilton represents more than two race victories' worth of cushion. With eight races remaining after Monza, the championship is still very much Antonelli's to lose — but this weekend creates the largest single opportunity for that gap to close.

The Norris Factor

Norris is the reigning World Champion and has won the last two races. At the Hungaroring, he converted pace into a dominant victory. At Zandvoort — a circuit where Mercedes typically struggles — he did it again. He arrives at Monza as the 6/4 favourite, with a McLaren that suits the low-downforce characteristics of the Temple of Speed.

If Norris wins and Antonelli finishes last after starting from 20th, the maximum 25-point swing compresses Antonelli's lead to 34 points with seven races remaining. That is still a meaningful advantage — but it is not comfortable. A 34-point lead with seven races left is a championship that can be won or lost in a single bad weekend.

Mercedes' Calculation

Mercedes chose Monza for the penalty deliberately. The long straights at the Autodromo Nazionale Monza create overtaking opportunities that do not exist on tighter circuits. The team's data showed that Antonelli's best chance of recovering from a back-of-grid start exists here, not at upcoming venues on the calendar.

Fresh power-unit components will also give Antonelli a mechanical advantage in the final seven races — potentially delivering stronger reliability and performance when points are at their most critical. It is a calculated risk that accepts short-term pain for long-term gain.

What's Next

The Italian Grand Prix runs Sunday, September 6 at 14:00 BST — 53 laps around Monza. By the time the chequered flag falls, the 2026 championship picture could look very different. Or Antonelli could recover to a points-scoring position and remind everyone why he built that lead in the first place. Either way, this is the race the title fight needed.
