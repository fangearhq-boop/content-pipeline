F1's 2026 Season Finale Is Still in Question — Qatar and Abu Dhabi Under Review, Europe on Standby

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | September 1, 2026

The 2026 Formula 1 season has already been shaped by forces beyond the paddock's control. Bahrain and Saudi Arabia were removed from the original 24-race calendar due to the ongoing conflict in the Middle East, reducing the season to 22 rounds. Now, with the calendar entering its final stretch, the same uncertainty threatens the finale.

Formula 1 is approaching a mid-September deadline to determine whether Qatar and Abu Dhabi — the season's planned final two rounds — can proceed as scheduled. If they cannot, F1 has confirmed a contingency: the season will end in Europe.

The Current Calendar Context

The 2026 season's European leg continues this week with the Italian Grand Prix at Monza (September 4-6), followed by the new Madrid Grand Prix (September 12-14, making the Spanish capital's Formula 1 debut) and the Azerbaijan Grand Prix in Baku (September 24-26).

After Baku, the calendar is expected to move toward the Middle East for its traditional end-of-year swing. Qatar and Abu Dhabi are scheduled as the penultimate and final rounds respectively — completing what was designed to be a clean conclusion to Kimi Antonelli's championship defense.

Whether that conclusion happens in the Gulf now depends on a mid-September decision that F1 must make, factoring in security assessments, FIA recommendations, and the practical logistics of hosting a Formula 1 event in a conflict-adjacent region.

What a European Finale Would Mean

A European ending to the 2026 season would be logistically significant but not unprecedented. F1 has historically held European season finales, and multiple circuits on the continent have the infrastructure to host a last-minute championship round. The specific replacement venues have not been announced.

For drivers, teams, and fans, the differences extend beyond travel. Middle Eastern circuits offer different weather, track characteristics, and crowd atmospheres than their European equivalents. A title decided in, say, Silverstone or Interlagos feels different from one settled in Abu Dhabi under the floodlights.

The championship implications depend on timing. If the decision is made in September and alternate venues are confirmed promptly, teams can adapt. A late or last-minute change creates significant operational challenges and could affect car development priorities.

What's Next

The mid-September deadline — likely to fall during or shortly after Italian GP weekend — is when F1 will announce its decision. If Qatar and Abu Dhabi receive the green light, the calendar proceeds as planned. If not, the season's final chapter gets rewritten. In a championship as tight as 2026's, even the venue of the finale matters. F1 fans will have their answer soon.
