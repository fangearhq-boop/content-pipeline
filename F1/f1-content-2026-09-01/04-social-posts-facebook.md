### STORY 2: Ferrari ADUO-2 Power Unit Debuts at Monza

**Long-Form Post (10:00 AM ET)**
Ferrari's biggest upgrade of the 2026 season is finally here — and the timing couldn't be more dramatic.

The Scuderia has confirmed that its ADUO-2 power unit will make its race debut at the Italian Grand Prix this weekend. Both Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc will receive the evolved six-cylinder engine, which delivers approximately 15 additional horsepower through a new turbocharger, a revised combustion chamber, and an updated Shell fuel blend. Crucially, neither driver will receive a grid penalty — Ferrari managed their power unit allocations to ensure both cars start from their qualified positions.

The ADUO-2 is the first of a two-phase performance push Ferrari outlined earlier this season, targeting a total gain of approximately 30 horsepower. Phase one arrives now. Phase two is expected later in the year.

And the timing? Ferrari is wearing a one-off tribute livery this weekend — inspired by Michael Schumacher's 1996 F310, complete with red and black accents, gold wheels, and special one-off race suits for Hamilton and Leclerc. Lewis Hamilton, who once publicly said he wanted an all-red Ferrari, is getting as close as possible to that dream at the circuit where Schumacher became a legend.

Ferrari is 53 points behind Mercedes in the Constructors' Championship. Monza is where they start closing that gap.

### STORY 4: Monaco Appeal — Gasly's Podium Still in Limbo Heading Into September

**Long-Form Post (12:00 PM ET)**
It has been nearly three months since the Monaco Grand Prix — and we still do not have a confirmed final result.

The saga began on June 7, when Alpine's Pierre Gasly finished third on track in Monaco only to receive two five-second penalties for speeding in the pit lane, dropping him to seventh. Alpine then exercised its right of review on June 12, presenting evidence that FOM's timing data contained an error. Stewards accepted this evidence and restored Gasly to third — while Isack Hadjar (Red Bull) dropped off the podium.

Both Red Bull and McLaren lodged appeals with the FIA International Court of Appeal. The ICA heard the case on August 25 at FIA headquarters in Paris, behind closed doors. The core question: did the stewards exceed their authority by reversing the original penalty using timing data that was disputed but not definitively proven to be wrong?

As of September 1, the verdict is still pending. Motorsport Week reported the FIA indicated a likely timeline of mid-September. That means the Monaco result could be settled during Italian GP weekend — or shortly after.

If Gasly loses his appeal: Hadjar regains the podium, and championship points are redistributed. In a season this tight, every point matters.

### STORY 5: F1 Season Finale — Qatar and Abu Dhabi Rounds Under Review Due to Middle East Conflict

**Long-Form Post (8:00 AM ET)**
The 2026 Formula 1 season was already unconventional — and the finish line might be, too.

Formula 1 is approaching a mid-September deadline to decide whether the season's final two rounds in Qatar and Abu Dhabi can proceed as scheduled. The ongoing conflict in the Middle East has cast uncertainty over the Gulf venues. F1 has confirmed it has a contingency plan: if Qatar and Abu Dhabi cannot safely host their races, the season will conclude in Europe instead.

The 2026 calendar was already trimmed from 24 to 22 races after the Bahrain and Saudi Arabian Grands Prix were removed for the same reasons. Two more changes would be significant — both logistically and symbolically.

For drivers and teams, the venue change could affect preparation, fan travel, and the atmosphere around the championship finale. For the title fight, a European ending would mean cooler weather, different circuit characteristics, and — potentially — a very different outcome.

F1's mid-September deadline falls in the same window as the Italian GP. Expect clarity soon.
