Haas Confirm Camara for Las Vegas After Dropping Ocon, Hirakawa Takes FP1 Seat

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | November 16, 2026

Haas have confirmed that Rafael Câmara will race at the Las Vegas Grand Prix in place of Esteban Ocon, who was removed from the team after the Mexico City Grand Prix. Ryo Hirakawa will take part in Free Practice 1 in Oliver Bearman's car.

The American team went into the Las Vegas weekend without the driver they started the season with. Esteban Ocon was already due to leave Haas at the end of 2026 — now he has left earlier than that, removed from his race seat following the Mexico City Grand Prix. Rafael Câmara, the Ferrari academy talent and current F2 championship leader, will take that seat for Las Vegas. Ryo Hirakawa, the Toyota WEC champion, will drive Bearman's VF-26 in Free Practice 1 on Thursday.

It is a significant reshuffling for a team already navigating an unusual season. Haas have now used four drivers in 2026 — Bearman, Ocon, Hirakawa, and Câmara — a figure that creates questions about the team's options for the final two rounds in Qatar and Abu Dhabi.

Ocon's Exit — Ahead of Schedule

Ocon's departure from Formula 1 for now is confirmed. The Frenchman had been signed through 2026 but Haas exercised whatever contractual leverage they had to end the arrangement early. The team's statement after Mexico City was clear: he will no longer compete with the team during the remainder of the season.

What this means for Ocon's longer-term future in F1 is unresolved. At 29, he has not driven his last race; but finding a grid seat for 2027 will be more complicated with an early-season removal on the record.

Câmara's Opportunity

Rafael Câmara is not an unknown quantity in this context. He is already signed as a Haas driver for 2027. This Las Vegas weekend is, in one reading, simply his 2027 season arriving a few months ahead of schedule. He made his debut in Brazil and now follows that with a race on the Strip — a circuit with its own unique demands and media attention.

The Ferrari academy pipeline has produced a succession of young drivers who have been placed at customer teams to build experience. Câmara leads F2 heading into the final rounds and brings form as well as talent. What he does in Las Vegas and beyond will shape perceptions heading into his official freshman season.

Hirakawa in FP1

Ryo Hirakawa's story is compelling in its own right. The Toyota endurance racing champion made his Formula 1 debut in Mexico City and finished 10th — a result that represented Haas's best performance in several rounds. Now he returns for FP1 in Las Vegas, taking Bearman's car for the Thursday morning session.

Hirakawa's path — WEC title, then Formula 1 practice, then a race debut, then another practice — reflects the kind of lateral route into the top category that has become more common as teams look for talent and financial arrangements that make the mathematics of their driver programs work. His 10th at Mexico has earned him this Las Vegas slot and perhaps a conversation about more.

What's Next

The FIA driver quota issue — Haas having used four of their allowed drivers in 2026 — creates a genuine puzzle for Qatar and Abu Dhabi. One report noted that the American team may be approaching a limit that restricts their options for the final two rounds. Bearman will almost certainly race; the second seat in those rounds remains an open question that Haas have not yet resolved publicly.

For this weekend, though, the focus shifts to Câmara on the Strip and what he can produce against the backdrop of one of Formula 1's most theatrical race venues. His opportunity is real. How he uses it will define the narrative of his Las Vegas chapter.
