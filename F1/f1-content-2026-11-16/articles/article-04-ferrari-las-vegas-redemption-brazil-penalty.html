Ferrari Arrive in Las Vegas With a Point to Prove After Brazil Nightmare

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | November 16, 2026

Charles Leclerc and Lewis Hamilton head to Las Vegas needing a performance reset after Sao Paulo, where Leclerc was demoted by a penalty and Hamilton described himself as "devastated" by the SF-26's race pace.

Ferrari left Interlagos with a file of things to investigate and a mood inside the garage that the team's public statements did not fully capture. Charles Leclerc was demoted from seventh to eighth after a five-second penalty for his contact with Liam Lawson. Lewis Hamilton, entering his second season in Maranello, used the word "devastated" to describe his feelings about the SF-26's performance in Brazil. The team launched an internal investigation into the car's weaknesses before the paddock had even left South America.

Now they are in Las Vegas, one of only three remaining rounds in the 2026 championship. The constructors' title belongs to Mercedes. What Ferrari are racing for in these final three events is pride, points, and the data that will shape the development direction of their 2027 machine.

What Went Wrong in Brazil

The Leclerc penalty came late in the race during a moment of pressure under braking. His contact with Lawson was judged by the stewards to have caused an avoidable collision, and the five-second addition dropped him behind the Racing Bulls driver in the final classification. For a team already struggling with pace, losing a position through on-track discipline compounded what was already a difficult result.

Hamilton's situation was different and arguably more concerning. His pace comments pointed to a fundamental concern about the SF-26's performance characteristics in Sao Paulo's conditions rather than a one-off incident. When a driver of Hamilton's experience uses language that direct about a car's limitations, it represents genuine frustration rather than managed communications.

The investigation Ferrari launched will examine whether what happened in Brazil reflects circuit-specific factors — Interlagos has specific characteristics around high-speed stability and tire degradation — or whether the SF-26 carries a deeper compromise that shows up at certain circuits regardless of setup.

What Las Vegas Could Offer

Las Vegas is a street circuit, and street circuits create different demands than permanent racing venues. Traction out of slow corners, braking stability over bumpy surfaces, and the ability to run a confident setup in cool temperatures are all relevant here. Ferrari's SF-26 has shown different characteristics at different types of circuits over the 2026 season — not linearly competitive but not linearly weak either.

If the Brazilian pace issue was at least partly circuit-specific, Las Vegas provides an immediate opportunity to demonstrate that. If the same problems appear on Saturday night — Hamilton unable to find pace, Leclerc managing car limitations rather than racing freely — the final two races in Qatar and Abu Dhabi become the last chances to avoid a demoralizing end to the season.

Leclerc's Championship Position

Leclerc sits fifth in the drivers' standings at 251 points. Hamilton, in fourth, has 261. Both remain in a position where a strong Las Vegas weekend could consolidate their standings and ensure a respectable final season ranking. The constructors' battle is settled, but the individual championship positions at this stage reflect the year's work and will be what people remember in December.

Leclerc in particular has the kind of temperament that tends to respond to adversity with focused aggression. His Las Vegas weekend historically has been limited — this circuit has a short history — but his ability to extract pace from a street circuit when the car allows it is well-documented. If the team delivers a setup that lets him drive freely, he is capable of a result that changes the narrative of Ferrari's season ending.

What's Next

Free Practice 1 on Thursday gives Ferrari their first read on whether the work done since Sao Paulo has addressed the car's issues or at least identified them specifically enough to target. The team's development pipeline continues through the final flyaway rounds, and any upgrade fitted in Las Vegas would represent a commitment to 2026 performance rather than pure 2027 preparation.

What happens under the lights on Saturday will tell the paddock — and Ferrari — a great deal about where this car stands. For a team with the resources and the ambition that Ferrari brings to every season, finishing 2026 with momentum rather than questions is the minimum they expect from these final three rounds.
