Defending Vegas Winner Verstappen Eyes P2 Title With 17-Point Lead Over Russell

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | November 16, 2026

Max Verstappen arrives in Las Vegas as the defending winner of the 2025 Grand Prix on the Strip, now facing three-way championship pressure from George Russell and Lewis Hamilton with 75 points remaining across the final three races.

The 2026 Las Vegas Grand Prix does not carry a world title fight. Kimi Antonelli ended that conversation weeks ago. What it carries instead is something arguably more intense in its specificity: a three-way battle for second in the drivers' championship, with Verstappen leading Russell by 17 points and Hamilton by 18, and a full 75 points still available across Las Vegas, Qatar, and Abu Dhabi.

Verstappen knows this circuit. He won here in 2025, and the memory of that result — a dominant performance on a street circuit that rewards precision and cold-weather tire management — arrives in Las Vegas alongside everything else he is carrying heading into Saturday night's race.

The P2 Mathematics

Three races means three opportunities for the positions to shift. At 17 points ahead of Russell and 18 ahead of Hamilton, Verstappen has a margin that is real but not comfortable. If he finishes outside the top five in Las Vegas while Russell or Hamilton wins, the gap can swing substantially. A race retirement could flatten the lead entirely.

What Verstappen needs in Las Vegas is not a win, necessarily — it is a points finish that keeps the gap intact or expands it. At the same time, the nature of competitive driving means that managing for position rather than racing for the front does not come naturally to any driver capable of competing for P2 in the world championship. He will race for the win. The question is whether the result matches the approach.

Verstappen's Brazil Drive as Context

The form heading into Las Vegas is not in question. At Sao Paulo, Verstappen started 21st following grid penalties and finished second — a 19-place recovery across 71 laps in wet-then-drying conditions that reminded observers of the qualities that have made him a multiple world champion. He closed within eight seconds of the nearest Red Bull after his second pit stop and tracked Norris's pace through the race's later stages.

Red Bull's car has been stronger than its constructors' position suggests, with Verstappen in particular extracting more from the VF-26 on race days than its single-lap pace might indicate. Las Vegas — a circuit with long straights and traction-sensitive slow corners — plays to some of the car's strengths, and to Verstappen's ability to manage tire degradation over distance.

Russell and Hamilton: The Threat is Real

George Russell arrives at Mercedes' home-team-celebration weekend — his constructor already champions — with a motivation that Verstappen cannot overlook. At 262 points, 17 behind, Russell is within one strong weekend of making the battle genuinely equal with two rounds to spare after Las Vegas. Lewis Hamilton, at 261 points and 18 behind, has the same calculation available to him.

The irony of the situation is that two Mercedes drivers are chasing one Red Bull driver for second place while their own team has already wrapped up the constructors' title. Mercedes have no reason to manage their approach tactically; both Russell and Hamilton will race as aggressively as the moment demands. For Verstappen, that means two cars from the fastest constructor working independently to displace him from P2.

What's Next

Qualifying on Friday will set the grid order that determines how much exposure Verstappen has to the Russell-Hamilton threat in the early laps. A front-row start reduces the risk of early-race contact and gives him clean air. A midfield qualifying position — not impossible if Red Bull's single-lap pace continues to trail Mercedes — creates a trickier situation for the race.

Whatever happens in qualifying, Saturday night under the lights will tell us more about the state of the P2 fight than the standings before the first green flag. Verstappen has been here before: title fights, pressure moments, the Strip circuit that he won on last year. The form is there. Whether the machinery and the race circumstances cooperate is what Las Vegas 2026 will determine.
