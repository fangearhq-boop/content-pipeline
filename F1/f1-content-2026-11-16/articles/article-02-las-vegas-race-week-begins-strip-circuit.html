F1 Race Week Is Here: Las Vegas Strip Closes as Grand Prix Build-Up Begins

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | November 16, 2026

Formula 1's Las Vegas Grand Prix race week has officially begun, with road closures taking effect around the Strip circuit as teams prepare for Saturday night's race on November 21.

Las Vegas looks different this week. Sections of the Strip — from Spring Mountain Road to Harmon Avenue — are closing to regular traffic as Formula 1 completes its transformation of one of the world's most famous streets into a racing circuit. Race week for the 2026 Las Vegas Grand Prix is here, with the lights going out for the actual race on Saturday night, November 21, at 8:00 PM local time.

For those watching from the United Kingdom, that means a 4:00 AM Sunday morning alarm. For those in Las Vegas and across the United States, it means a Saturday night under the neon lights that the city has built its entire identity around. The 2026 Las Vegas Grand Prix is not just a race — it is a spectacle designed around the idea that a racetrack through a glittering city is something the sport has always been capable of.

The Circuit and the Schedule

The Las Vegas Street Circuit runs for 6.201 kilometres and features 17 turns. The long straight down the Strip remains the defining characteristic — a stretch where the fastest Formula 1 cars in the world will reach their highest speeds of the weekend while framed by the lights of the casinos on either side.

The event runs three days: Free Practice 1 and 2 on Thursday evening, Free Practice 3 and Qualifying on Friday evening, and the 50-lap race on Saturday evening. The structure concentrates the competitive action into the late hours, which produces the specific atmospheric quality that has made Las Vegas one of the season's most watched events.

Weather: The Variable Nobody Can Control

Temperature matters in Formula 1, and Las Vegas this week is demonstrating why. The start of race week sees temperatures in the upper 60s Fahrenheit — manageable for the Pirelli tire compounds that will be used this weekend. By race day on Saturday, the forecast points to the upper 50s.

That ten-degree drop is not trivial. Las Vegas in November runs cool enough that tire operating windows can become narrow, requiring teams to work harder to get the rubber up to working temperature before braking zones begin to matter. Teams preparing their setups on Thursday will be balancing two thermal scenarios: practice runs in one temperature range and a race run in conditions that may feel meaningfully different.

Lando Norris flagged the tire window concern in the lead-up to Las Vegas, noting the "extremely narrow operating window" that the 2026 cars present on street circuits in cool conditions. His McLaren carries an upgraded package from Brazil, but the question of whether that upgrade performs as expected in conditions different from the test data will not be answered until Thursday's first running.

The City in Race Mode

Local transit routes are running special Race Week routing through November 25, with the Regional Transportation Commission of Southern Nevada adjusting bus services to accommodate the closures. The city's hospitality infrastructure — already built around high-volume events — shifts into a gear that only the Las Vegas Grand Prix requires.

Zoox, the autonomous vehicle company, has been named the official autonomous ride-hail partner of this year's event. That detail speaks to the specific way Las Vegas positions its F1 race: as a convergence point for technology, entertainment, and sport that no other Grand Prix quite replicates.

What's Next

Free Practice 1 on Thursday is the first competitive running, and teams will use it to gather data on tire behavior, car balance, and the specific character of the Las Vegas surface. The championship picture — Antonelli's title secured, the P2 battle between Verstappen, Russell, and Hamilton genuine and unresolved — adds the competitive dimension to what is already a spectacular backdrop.

By the time Saturday night's lights go out at 8:00 PM local, Las Vegas will have done what it always does: turned something that could be simply a race into something considerably larger. The circuit is ready. The city is ready. What the drivers deliver on Saturday will determine whether 2026 adds a memorable chapter to the Strip's growing Grand Prix history.
