17 Points, 75 Available: The Verstappen-Russell Las Vegas Reckoning

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | November 19, 2026

Max Verstappen leads the Drivers' Championship second-place fight by 17 points. George Russell is 17 behind. Lewis Hamilton is 1 behind Russell. With three races remaining — Las Vegas, and two more after that — 75 points are still available to be distributed. The mathematics of this battle could not be tighter.

The 2026 Heineken Las Vegas Grand Prix is not just the third-to-last race of the season. For Verstappen, Russell, and Hamilton, it is a pressure point from which the standings could swing dramatically in either direction.

Verstappen's Position

Verstappen enters Vegas as the man defending his position. At 279 points, he leads Russell and Hamilton by a margin that feels comfortable only until you remember how volatile street circuits can be. A Safety Car at the wrong moment, a tire puncture, a strategy misfire — any single event could wipe out his lead in one afternoon.

He is also the race favorite. Verstappen won the Las Vegas Grand Prix in 2023 and 2025 and has shown across this season that he can manufacture results from difficult grid positions — his recovery from 21st at the São Paulo Grand Prix is the most recent evidence. A win on Saturday night would go a long way toward securing second place in the standings.

Russell's Opportunity

George Russell won the 2024 Las Vegas Grand Prix. He knows this circuit and has extracted a dominant result here before. His Mercedes W17 is built for low-temperature street circuits, which plays directly into the conditions Las Vegas delivers in November.

Russell needs to outscore Verstappen across the final three races without necessarily relying on the Red Bull driver making an error. The most likely path is straightforward excellence: front-row qualifying, a measured race, big points. A race win on Saturday combined with a Verstappen incident would reshape the entire conversation heading into the final two rounds.

Hamilton: The Dark Horse

Lewis Hamilton's presence just one point behind his own former teammate Russell adds another layer of complexity. Hamilton in his first season with Ferrari has had an up-and-down campaign by his standards, but his 261 points represent a consistent scoring effort that keeps him relevant in every conversation about the top of the standings.

Hamilton has never won in Las Vegas. In a season full of narrative twists, a Hamilton victory on the Strip in Ferrari red would rank among the most remarkable.

What's Next
