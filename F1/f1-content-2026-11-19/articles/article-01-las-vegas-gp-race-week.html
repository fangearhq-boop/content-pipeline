Race Week in Vegas: FP1 and FP2 Launch the 2026 Las Vegas Grand Prix

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | November 19, 2026

Formula 1's most glamorous event of the season is officially underway. The 2026 Heineken Las Vegas Grand Prix Race Week began Thursday with Free Practice 1 and Free Practice 2 on the Las Vegas Strip Circuit — 6.201 kilometers of asphalt through one of the world's most iconic entertainment destinations.

This is Round 21 of 23 on the 2026 F1 calendar. Qualifying follows on Friday night, with the race itself set for Saturday evening under the lights of the Las Vegas Strip. All 20 drivers take to the circuit for the first time this weekend, and Thursday's sessions will tell teams a great deal about how their cars behave in the unique conditions this venue presents.

The Circuit

The Las Vegas Strip Circuit features 17 turns, three long straights, and a lap distance of 6.201 kilometers. The 50-lap race is a true test of setup balance: teams need enough straight-line speed to maximize the three DRS zones while maintaining enough downforce to hold lap time through the technical sections.

What sets this circuit apart from the rest of the calendar is the temperature. Las Vegas nights in November are cold by race-circuit standards. Cold asphalt significantly slows tire warm-up during opening laps and can make the first stint dangerously unpredictable. Teams that solve the warm-up window quickly tend to run the race on their own terms.

Championship Context

Kimi Antonelli clinched the 2026 Drivers' World Championship at the Mexico City Grand Prix earlier this month — the youngest driver in F1 history to do so. He arrives in Las Vegas with 390 points and nothing left to prove on the title front, though winning is still the objective for every driver every weekend.

What makes Las Vegas compelling this year is the ferocious fight behind him. Max Verstappen holds second place with 279 points. George Russell is third on 262 points, just 17 behind. Lewis Hamilton sits fourth with 261 points — a single point behind his former Mercedes rival. That three-way battle for second place through the final three races of the season is as dramatically tight as any title fight in recent memory.

What to Watch in Practice

Thursday's practice sessions are about data collection. Teams will evaluate which tire compounds degrade most favorably on the Strip surface, test their low-drag configurations on the long straights, and begin to understand how much performance their car holds relative to rivals. Any team that makes a significant setup miscalculation in practice can find itself locked into a compromised configuration through qualifying and the race.

The drivers will also be reacquainting themselves with the track's specific demands — particularly the high-speed third sector and the braking zones that catch drivers out when tire temperatures are not in the optimal range.

What's Next

Qualifying on Friday night is where the Las Vegas GP truly begins to take shape. The front rows at this circuit are significant — overtaking opportunities exist on the long straights but are fewer than a traditional power circuit, making grid position consequential.
