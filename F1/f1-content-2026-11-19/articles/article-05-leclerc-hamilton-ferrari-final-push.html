Leclerc's Math Problem: Ferrari Needs a Vegas Push to Close On Hamilton

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | November 19, 2026

Charles Leclerc enters the Las Vegas Grand Prix with 249 points and a gap of 12 to Lewis Hamilton in fourth place. With three races remaining and 75 points available, the mathematics still permit Leclerc to overturn the deficit. What they require, however, is consistent outscoring of a driver who has been excellent in the second half of the 2026 season.

Ferrari versus Mercedes. Leclerc versus Hamilton. The final three rounds of 2026 have one more subplot beyond the Verstappen-Russell headline battle.

Leclerc's Season in Review

Leclerc's 2026 campaign has been marked by exceptional qualifying performances and mixed race results. He has extracted grid positions beyond what the SF-26's raw pace merited on multiple occasions, demonstrating the qualifying ceiling of his ability. In races, however, tire management and strategic calls have occasionally cost him positions he held at the front of a stint.

The consistent pressure of hunting Hamilton in the standings adds a new dimension to the final races. Leclerc cannot afford to give up points through conservative decision-making. He needs to attack each weekend from the start.

Hamilton's Ferrari Journey

Lewis Hamilton's first season with Ferrari has been one of adaptation. Early in the year the transition was visible — small adjustments in driving style, learning a new team's operational rhythms, and finding the SF-26's limits. By mid-season, Hamilton found a groove, and his points haul in the second half of the year reflects a driver increasingly at home in a new environment.

At 261 points and fourth in the standings, Hamilton has contributed materially to Ferrari's No. 2 position in the Constructors' Championship. He will not give up fourth place without a fight.

Las Vegas and Ferrari's Challenges

Historically, the Las Vegas Strip Circuit has not been a natural home for Ferrari. The track's demand for low-drag configurations and cold-temperature tire performance do not align perfectly with the SF-26's characteristics, which favor circuits with more sustained corner sequences and warmer track surfaces.

That said, Leclerc's qualifying pace is capable of masking car deficiencies, and a grid position in the top five would give Ferrari an opportunity to execute a race strategy that maximizes points scoring.

What's Next

Tonight's practice sessions will give Leclerc and Hamilton early data on relative pace. Qualifying on Friday will begin to separate the two in terms of race positioning. For Leclerc, Saturday's race is must-watch: a podium finish, combined with any setback for Hamilton, could make the final two rounds of the season the most dramatic Ferrari has experienced in years.
