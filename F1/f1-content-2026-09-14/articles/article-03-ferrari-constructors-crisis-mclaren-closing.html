Ferrari's Madring Double Disaster: Hamilton DNF, Leclerc VSC Miss — McLaren Now Just 52 Points Back

By Marcus Cole | September 14, 2026

Ferrari's worst weekend of 2026 may have arrived at the worst possible time. A brake failure ended Hamilton's race on Lap 7. A VSC strategy miss cost Leclerc a podium. And McLaren is now 52 points behind Ferrari in the Constructors' Championship.

Ferrari needed a big weekend at the Madring. Instead, they got their worst.

Lewis Hamilton retired on Lap 7 with brake failure — his first DNF of the season, zero points, and with it, a formal concession of his title hopes. Charles Leclerc, running well throughout the race and positioned for a podium finish, was kept out during the virtual safety car that Kimi Antonelli pitted under — the window that decided the race. Leclerc finished fourth. Combined Ferrari haul: 12 points. While Mercedes collected 25 and McLaren took 15.

The Constructors' Championship standings after Madring: Mercedes 503 points. Ferrari 358 points. McLaren 306 points. The gaps that matter: Mercedes leads Ferrari by 145. McLaren trails Ferrari by just 52.

The VSC That Got Away

When the virtual safety car was deployed at the Madring, Mercedes immediately called Antonelli in for fresh tires. The decision was correct — he emerged in the lead and went on to win the race. Ferrari, running Leclerc in a strong position, stayed out. The team's reasoning has not been fully explained, but the consequences are clear: Leclerc restarted with older rubber, lost positions at the restart, and ultimately crossed the line in fourth.

In Formula 1, these decisions are made in seconds with imperfect information. What's certain is that Ferrari's strategy team missed the same call Mercedes got right — and at a circuit where first-time data made every decision harder, the team that executed best was not Ferrari.

The review inside Maranello will be thorough. Whether the decision was defensible with the information available at the time is a question only Ferrari's strategists can fully answer. But from the outside, it looked like another moment where the team that needed to be excellent was merely adequate.

McLaren's Momentum

While Ferrari was managing its disaster, McLaren was accumulating points. Norris finished third — his best result in several rounds — and the broader McLaren package has found consistency that Ferrari has not. With 52 points now separating the two teams and nine rounds remaining (403 points available in the Constructors'), the prospect of Ferrari finishing third in the championship is no longer hypothetical.

Third in the Constructors' Championship would be a damaging result for Ferrari's ambitions. The team arrived at 2026 believing Hamilton alongside Leclerc gave them a genuine dual-driver championship threat. That's gone. The question now is whether they can at least secure second.

What's Next

Ferrari needs both of its cars to score heavily at every remaining round while McLaren stumbles. That's a tall order for a team that just scored 12 points at Madring when the opportunity to score 27 or more was squandered. Leclerc remains competitive enough to win races, and Hamilton — once the brakes are fixed — is still one of the best drivers on the grid.

Baku is next, September 24-26. The Baku City Circuit's chaotic nature can scramble results in Ferrari's favor as easily as anyone else's. But Ferrari needs execution that matches pace. At Madring, it didn't. The clock is running down on their chance to save the constructors' season.
