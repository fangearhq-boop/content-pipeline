Antonelli 81 Clear, Three Wins in a Row: The 2026 F1 Title Is Now His to Lose

By Elena Voss | September 14, 2026

Kimi Antonelli's VSC-fueled victory at the Madring extended his championship lead to 81 points over George Russell with nine rounds remaining. Three consecutive wins. One dominant team. The 2026 title is his unless something unprecedented happens.

Dutch Grand Prix. Italian Grand Prix. Spanish Grand Prix.

Three consecutive Formula 1 victories for Kimi Antonelli, driving for Mercedes. Three rounds in which the 20-year-old Italian driver has converted his car's pace advantage into race wins under different conditions, against different pressures, at different circuits. The latest came at the Madring — Madrid's brand-new permanent circuit — where Antonelli was running second before a virtual safety car opened the door for a pitstop that gave him clean air, the lead, and ultimately his third straight victory.

The 2026 Drivers' Championship standings now read: Antonelli 292 points. George Russell 211 (-81). Lewis Hamilton 191 (-101, DNF Madring). Lando Norris 186 (-106). Nine rounds remain in the season, 233 points are available, and nobody on the leaderboard looks capable of mounting the kind of sustained run that would close an 81-point gap against a driver in this form.

The VSC That Decided the Race

The Spanish Grand Prix at the Madring did not unfold as a dominant front-to-back performance. Antonelli started from the second row, as No. 2 on the grid, and spent the early laps following the race leader. When the virtual safety car was deployed during a mid-race incident, Mercedes called him in for fresh tires. The timing was perfect — he emerged in the lead with clean air ahead.

Ferrari, running Leclerc in a position to benefit from a similar call, stayed out. That decision — and its consequences — will be analyzed and debated. But for Antonelli and Mercedes, it was textbook execution of the kind of strategic reads that have characterized their season.

Verstappen was second at +4.351 seconds. Norris third at +5.089 seconds. Hamilton retired on Lap 7 with brake failure. Leclerc fourth.

What the Math Means

Ninety-three points across nine rounds. That is what Russell — the driver closest to Antonelli — needs to find, assuming Antonelli scores zero more points. The mathematical maximum Antonelli can score in those nine rounds is 233 points. If Russell wins all nine remaining races and scores maximum points, and Antonelli scores zero, Russell would win the title by approximately 140 points in Russell's favor. That scenario is fantasy.

A more realistic framing: Antonelli needs to maintain roughly the same average points-per-race he's accumulated all season. At this rate, he doesn't need to win another race to clinch the title — he simply needs to avoid disasters. In a Mercedes that hasn't had a reliability failure at a critical moment, and with a team executing strategy at this level, that is a very low bar.

Hamilton, officially, has conceded. He said it plainly after Madring: there is no stopping Kimi Antonelli.

What's Next

Nine rounds. Four or five wins from Antonelli, barring catastrophe, likely clinches the title. Baku is next — September 24-26 — and it is historically the circuit most capable of producing unexpected results. Safety cars, crashes, and mechanical failures are endemic to the Baku City Circuit's street layout.

If Antonelli's rivals are going to manufacture an opening, they need it fast. The 81-point gap doesn't grow smaller on its own. Antonelli is 20 years old, driving with precision that belies his age, backed by a team executing at a championship-winning level. The 2026 F1 title is his to lose. And right now, he shows no signs of losing anything.
