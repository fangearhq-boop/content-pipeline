Baku on a Saturday: The 2026 Azerbaijan Grand Prix Is Unlike Any Race in the Circuit's History

By Ryan Calloway | September 14, 2026

The next Formula 1 race is the Azerbaijan Grand Prix — September 24-26 in Baku — and for the first time ever, the race takes place on Saturday. Here's why, what to expect from one of the most unpredictable circuits on the calendar, and why this weekend matters enormously for the championship.

Formula 1 is heading to Baku, and nothing about this weekend will be ordinary.

The 2026 Azerbaijan Grand Prix takes place September 24-26 at the Baku City Circuit — and in a first for the event's history, the race itself will be held on Saturday. The change comes because Azerbaijan's National Day of Mourning falls on Sunday, September 27, making a Sunday race impossible under local protocols. F1 accommodated the request and shifted the race day back by 24 hours.

A Formula 1 race on a Saturday. The phrase alone sounds unusual, and it is. But it's also an intriguing preview of how Formula 1 might adapt the race day format in future seasons, as the sport continues to grow in markets with varied cultural and governmental requirements.

The Circuit: Beautiful, Brutal, Unpredictable

The Baku City Circuit is 6.003 kilometers long and winds through the ancient streets of Azerbaijan's capital. Through the narrow Kisha section near the medieval city walls, cars squeeze between barriers with centimeters of clearance. On the main straight — nearly two kilometers — they hit speeds exceeding 340 km/h before braking hard for Turn 1 at the castle section.

Baku produces more safety car deployments per race than almost any other circuit on the calendar. It has ended championship runs and launched improbable comebacks. It rewards bravery and punishes impatience in equal measure. For drivers chasing a championship — or trying to close on one — it is both the most dangerous and the most opportunistic circuit of the year.

There is no sprint format at Baku this year. Standard race weekend: FP1, FP2, FP3, qualifying Saturday morning, race Saturday afternoon.

Championship Implications

With Kimi Antonelli leading by 81 points and three consecutive wins in the bank, Baku is exactly the circuit his rivals have been waiting for. A safety car at the wrong time, a debris strike, a mechanical failure on Lap 1 — any of these scenarios could swing the gap dramatically in a single afternoon.

George Russell, 81 points back, needs to win races and needs Antonelli to falter. Lando Norris, 106 back, faces a similar imperative. Both of them are capable of winning at Baku. Ferrari — if Hamilton's brakes are fixed and Leclerc's strategy team is on its game — could also threaten for victory.

The circuit's history of chaos means this championship is not over in practice, even if the math is becoming brutal. One Baku weekend can change everything.

What's Next

Thirteen days from now, the paddock arrives in Baku. Katy Perry and Calvin Harris are headlining entertainment events around the weekend, making this one of the most commercially high-profile rounds of the season. The racing — on Saturday, for the first time — will be watched by millions of fans who want to see whether Antonelli can be stopped.

He hasn't been stopped in three races. Baku has stopped drivers who looked unstoppable before. Watch September 24-26 closely. This is where the 2026 championship either gets decided or gets interesting again.
