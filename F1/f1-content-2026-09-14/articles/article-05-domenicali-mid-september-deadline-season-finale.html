Domenicali's Mid-September Deadline Is Today: F1's Season Finale Calendar Decision Is Now

By Elena Voss | September 14, 2026

Formula 1 CEO Stefano Domenicali promised a decision "before mid-September" on whether Qatar and Abu Dhabi would close the 2026 season. That date has arrived. Here's what we know, what Imola has to do with it, and why the finale location matters for the championship itself.

The deadline Stefano Domenicali set is today.

Formula 1's CEO committed in late July to making a decision "before the middle of September" about whether the final two races of the 2026 season — Qatar (November 29) and Abu Dhabi (December 6) — would proceed as scheduled. The commitment came after Bahrain and Saudi Arabia were cancelled due to regional instability, with Malaysia's Sepang circuit stepping in to replace Bahrain on October 2-4.

September 14. The middle of September. Domenicali's deadline is here.

What Is and Isn't Confirmed

Recent reporting suggests Abu Dhabi's place on the calendar as the season finale is likely secure. The Yas Marina Circuit has a long-standing commercial relationship with Formula 1, and organizers appear to have satisfied the conditions required to keep the December 6 date. An August report indicated Abu Dhabi was confirmed.

Qatar's November 29 slot is the more uncertain element. The Losail International Circuit faces the same regional context as the cancelled Bahrain and Saudi rounds, and F1 has been evaluating whether the situation there has stabilized sufficiently to proceed. Qatar hosts an F1 race at a circuit with strong safety records, but the broader political and logistical environment has been the deciding factor throughout 2026's calendar disruptions.

Domenicali's announcement — expected today or within the next 24-48 hours — will clarify whether both races proceed, or whether the European contingency is activated.

The European Contingency: Imola and Portimao

If Qatar cannot proceed, F1 needs a replacement for the penultimate round. The Italian Automobile Federation has positioned Imola — the Autodromo Enzo e Dino Ferrari — as the "ideal solution." The circuit hosted a Grand Prix most recently in 2024 before its original planned return was disrupted. Imola's infrastructure is ready, its fans are passionate, and its operators have maintained the venue at F1 standard.

Portimao in Portugal has also been discussed as a contingency option. The Algarve International Circuit hosted two grand prix during the pandemic years and demonstrated it can handle an F1 event at short notice.

A European finale would change the championship dynamics. Tire compounds, track temperatures, circuit characteristics — all of these differ meaningfully from a Middle Eastern desert venue. If Antonelli has wrapped up the title before the finale, it matters less. If the championship goes to the wire, the venue could be decisive.

What's Next

The announcement from F1 — through Domenicali or an official statement — should arrive today or shortly after. Nine rounds remain in the championship. Seven rounds are already confirmed and on track. The final two — Qatar and Abu Dhabi, or their replacements — need to be settled so teams can prepare their setups, logistics, and staffing plans for the closing stretch of a championship that, while appearing increasingly decided in Antonelli's favor, still has 233 points to be scored.

The calendar is F1's most fundamental administrative document. Today, Domenicali's promise comes due. The answer will set the stage for the end of one of the most extraordinary Formula 1 seasons in recent memory.
