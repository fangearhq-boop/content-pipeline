McLaren Brings New Parts to Baku — But Mercedes Has a Bigger Upgrade Waiting in Malaysia

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | September 21, 2026

The 2026 F1 Constructors' Championship is not settled — not by a long measure. After 14 rounds, Mercedes holds 503 points, Ferrari has 358, and McLaren sits at 306. That 145-point Mercedes lead looks commanding. But the remaining 403 points, and a strategic upgrade cycle that is now playing out in real time, means this battle is far from over.

This weekend's Azerbaijan Grand Prix is where the next chapter begins.

McLaren's Baku Bet

McLaren have arrived in Azerbaijan with a specific upgrade package designed for the Baku City Circuit's demanding second sector. The narrow, high-energy section of the track requires a particular aerodynamic balance — and McLaren's engineers have spent recent weeks developing hardware optimised for exactly these conditions.

Lando Norris enters the weekend as the favourite for both pole and race victory. If the new parts deliver as intended, McLaren could close the constructors' gap to Ferrari and begin building a case that the Papaya cars — not the Scuderia — are the real threat to Mercedes in the season's final nine rounds.

Mercedes Playing for Malaysia

Mercedes are doing something different. Rather than bring their development resources to Baku, the Silver Arrows are holding a major upgrade package in reserve for Malaysia — the round immediately after Azerbaijan. The decision reflects confidence that the current car can extract a strong result in Baku without the new parts, and a strategic belief that the Malaysian circuit is better suited to their upgrade's characteristics.

It is the right kind of chess move: preserve your strongest weapon for the moment it delivers the most. But it also means that if McLaren dominates in Baku and Mercedes finishes outside the points, the constructors' picture shifts in ways that make Malaysia's upgrade far more critical.

Ferrari in the Middle

Ferrari sits between the two, leading McLaren by 52 points — a gap that represents roughly two race weekends of advantage. The Scuderia's own upgrade cycle has been steady rather than explosive in recent rounds, and they enter Baku without the strategic headline that either McLaren or Mercedes brings this weekend.

Ferrari's challenge is maintaining the buffer to McLaren while not ceding further ground to Mercedes at the top. Baku is a high-variance circuit where reliability and track position matter enormously. One failure for either Mercedes driver changes the constructors' math instantly.

What's Next

403 points remain across the final nine rounds of the 2026 season. McLaren are the fastest-improving team. Mercedes are deploying a major upgrade in Malaysia. Ferrari are protecting a three-way gap on two fronts. The Constructors' Championship runs alongside the Drivers' battle — and this weekend's race in Baku could shift the entire picture before Malaysia's upgrade even arrives on the grid.
