Baku on Saturday: Norris, the New Parts, and Everything That Makes the Azerbaijan GP Different This Year

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | September 21, 2026

In five days, Formula 1 will do something it has never done before: run the Azerbaijan Grand Prix on a Saturday. The scheduling shift — made to avoid a conflict with Azerbaijan's National Day of Mourning — compresses the entire weekend by 24 hours. Practice begins Wednesday, qualifying falls on Friday, and the race itself starts Saturday at 15:00 local time in Baku.

The format is unchanged. The implications are substantial. And the championship stakes going into this race are as high as they have been all season.

Norris the Favourite, McLaren Armed

Lando Norris enters the Azerbaijan Grand Prix as the favorite for both pole position and race victory. McLaren have specifically brought new parts to Baku optimised for the circuit's most demanding stretch — the technical second sector, where walls close in and the cars must navigate a series of high-speed direction changes with millimetre precision.

McLaren's development rate has been one of the stories of the second half of the 2026 season. The Papaya cars have been consistently faster in recent rounds, and this Baku upgrade package represents a further step. If their analysis of the circuit's characteristics proves correct, Norris should have the pace to challenge for the front row from the moment practice begins Wednesday.

Mercedes Playing the Long Game

While McLaren are going all-in on Baku, Mercedes is holding their major upgrade package in reserve for Malaysia — the round immediately following Azerbaijan. It is a calculated decision that reveals confidence in the current car's ability to score well in Baku while signalling a deliberate strategic choice to deploy the larger development step at a circuit better suited to it.

George Russell, who has three podiums from his last four Baku appearances, is Mercedes' best bet for a big result this weekend. With an 81-point deficit to team-mate Kimi Antonelli in the Drivers' Championship, Russell needs to maximize every opportunity. Baku, with its wall-proximity demands and high-energy inlap characteristics that suit Russell's precise driving style, is one of his strongest circuits.

The Circuit

The Baku City Circuit stretches 6.003 kilometres through the streets of Azerbaijan's capital. Its most famous feature is the 2.2-kilometre main straight, where cars reach speeds approaching 340 kilometres per hour before braking hard for Turn 1. The narrow castle section through the old city is one of the most challenging passages of any street circuit on the calendar — walls an arm's length away, corners arriving faster than they appear.

Reliability matters here. One mechanical failure, one puncture on the straight, one moment of misjudgement entering the castle section can end a race instantly and reverse a championship calculation. Baku is where seasons have been won and lost in moments of chaos that arrive without warning.

What's Next

Practice opens Wednesday, September 24. Qualifying is Friday, September 25. Race day is Saturday, September 26 at 15:00 Baku local time. With McLaren's new package, Russell hunting points, and Antonelli aiming to extend his 81-point lead, this Azerbaijan Grand Prix — the first to run on a Saturday — could be the most significant race of the European season's closing chapter.
