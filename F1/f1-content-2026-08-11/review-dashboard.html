### Long-Form Post 🔴 Zero wins. Five tenths off the pace. A team that's reportedly already moved on to 2027. If you'd said this would describe Red Bull Racing at the summer break of 2026, nobody would have believed you. But here we are. Former Formula 1 race engineer Rob Smedley published a mid-year assessment of the 2026 field earlier this month — and he gave Red Bull a 5 out of 10. The reigning dominant team of the sport's recent era has zero wins from 11 Grands Prix. Their car, the RB22, has been described as running "one second off" the leaders at some circuits. Here's what's gone wrong — and what it means: **The chassis, not the engine.** This is the key distinction: the FIA assessed Red Bull's Ford power unit as among the strongest on the grid. The problem is the aerodynamic package and mechanical platform around it. Red Bull built the wrong chassis for 2026's 50/50 hybrid regulations, and they've now exhausted their development tokens — meaning no meaningful upgrade is coming in the second half of this season. **Already looking at 2027.** Reports from inside the paddock suggest Red Bull has effectively written off 2026 and redirected significant resources toward building a contender for 2027's rule set. It's a pragmatic decision — but it's also an admission that this season cannot be saved. **Verstappen's exit clause window.** Max Verstappen's contract runs until 2028, but a performance-related exit clause allows him to leave from 2027 onwards. That window is open right now, running through October 2026. The Dutch GP — at Verstappen's home circuit, in front of his home crowd — arrives with that question unanswered. The best results for Verstappen this season: P3 at the Canadian GP (Red Bull's first podium of 2026) and P3 at the Belgian GP (the team's historic 300th F1 podium). In an era where he won 21 Grands Prix in 2023, two podiums as highlights is a sobering number. The summer break is ending. Red Bull needs the Dutch GP badly. 📉 ---

### Long-Form Post 🔴 Lewis Hamilton calls his 2026 Ferrari campaign "worlds away" from 2025. He's not wrong. One year ago, Hamilton was fighting to adapt to a car that felt alien to him, struggling in qualifying, and watching his new teammate Charles Leclerc outperform him. The 2025 season was, by Hamilton's own standards, a difficult transition year. The 2026 season has been something different. Hamilton won the Spanish Grand Prix — his first-ever victory for Ferrari. He has scored points in all 11 races, with zero retirements, and currently sits second in the World Championship on 169 points. He has 5 podiums. And in qualifying, he has actually edged ahead of Leclerc — 0.017 seconds per lap faster on average, a reversal from a year earlier when he was 2.5 tenths slower than his teammate. So why is he still 50 points behind Antonelli? Two reasons: 1. **Straight-line speed.** Hamilton has consistently described losing 0.3-0.4 seconds per lap compared to the Mercedes on the straights. Ferrari's aerodynamic efficiency under the 2026 50/50 hybrid regulations has lagged behind Mercedes' mastery of the power deployment system. 2. **Ferrari has one more shot.** Under the FIA's Additional Development Upgrade Opportunity (ADUO) system, teams get one bonus power unit upgrade per season. Ferrari used theirs at the Austrian GP. Wait — they still have one more available. Ferrari boss Fred Vasseur has confirmed it's coming in the second half, alongside chassis updates. Hamilton's own prediction: "It will be stronger." That word choice is deliberate. He's not calling it a guaranteed title run. He's putting his team on notice that the second half will be better. With 291 points remaining and 50 to make up, better might be just enough. 🏎 ---
