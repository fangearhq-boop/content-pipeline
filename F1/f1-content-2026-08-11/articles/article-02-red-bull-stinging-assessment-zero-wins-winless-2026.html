Red Bull Racing: Zero Wins, a Stinging Assessment, and a Season Already Being Written Off

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | August 11, 2026

F1 Team Analysis / Red Bull Racing / Max Verstappen

Former Formula 1 race engineer Rob Smedley gave Red Bull Racing a 5 out of 10 at the summer break. The team that won 21 Grands Prix in 2023, dominated 2022 and 2024, and turned Max Verstappen into a four-time world champion has zero wins from the first 11 races of the 2026 season.

The score feels, if anything, generous.

What Went Wrong

The 2026 regulations rewrote Formula 1 with a 50/50 split between the internal combustion engine and electric motor — a seismic shift in how cars are designed and driven. The Mercedes team built a car that understood this new world immediately. Red Bull did not.

The diagnosis is specific: this is a chassis problem, not a power unit problem. The FIA has assessed Red Bull's Ford power unit as one of the strongest on the grid. The aerodynamic package and mechanical platform wrapped around it — the RB22 — has been described by the team itself as having "significant shortcomings." At some circuits, they have been running a full second off the leaders' pace.

Red Bull has now exhausted their available development tokens for the 2026 car. There are no significant upgrades left to bring. Whatever the RB22 is at the summer break, it will largely remain for the rest of the year.

The Pivot to 2027

Reports from inside the paddock have suggested the team has effectively made a pragmatic decision: the 2026 season cannot be salvaged, and the smartest play is to redirect resources toward 2027's rule set. It is not an admission that has been made publicly at the highest levels — but the evidence from the track is consistent with it.

The best results Verstappen has managed this season are a third place at the Canadian Grand Prix — Red Bull's first podium of 2026 — and a third at the Belgian Grand Prix, which happened to be the team's 300th Formula 1 podium in history. Bronze from a milestone event is a bittersweet way to mark the occasion.

Isack Hadjar, promoted from Racing Bulls to partner Verstappen, has contributed results including fourth and sixth place finishes — signs of a young driver performing above the car's nominal ceiling.

The Verstappen Question

Max Verstappen's contract with Red Bull runs until 2028 — but performance-related exit clauses allow him to trigger a departure from 2027 onwards. That window is currently open, running through October 2026. The Dutch Grand Prix arrives in 10 days with that question still unanswered.

Verstappen's championship position of sixth, with 109 points, would have seemed unthinkable before the season started. It is the combined product of a car that cannot win and a regulation change that erased the performance advantage Red Bull had spent years building. Whether he stays to help rebuild, or exercises his clause, is the most significant personnel decision of the 2026 silly season.

What's Next

Red Bull returns from the summer shutdown on August 17 ahead of the Dutch Grand Prix, August 21-23. It is Verstappen's home race — 70,000 orange-clad fans at Zandvoort — and the circuit that has historically been one of his strongest. A win there would change the narrative and prove the car has more in it than the season suggests. But with zero wins from 11 attempts, proving it has become the hardest thing in F1 right now.
