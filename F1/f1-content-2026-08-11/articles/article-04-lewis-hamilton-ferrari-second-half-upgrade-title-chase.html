Hamilton Says This Ferrari Is "Worlds Away" From 2025. He Has One More Weapon Still to Come.

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | August 11, 2026

F1 Championship / Lewis Hamilton / Ferrari

Lewis Hamilton's 2026 Formula 1 season looks nothing like his 2025. That was a year of difficult adaptation — to a new team, a new car, a new culture, and a regulation set that didn't suit him. He described the transition as the hardest of his career.

This year, Hamilton uses a different word for his Ferrari experience: "worlds away."

He has won the Spanish Grand Prix — his first victory in Scuderia red. He has scored points in all 11 races, with zero retirements. He sits second in the championship on 169 points. In qualifying, he is running faster than Leclerc for the first time since joining Ferrari. The transformation is real.

And yet he remains 50 points behind Kimi Antonelli. And he has one card left to play.

The Straight-Line Problem

Hamilton has been consistent and precise about where Ferrari's limitation lives. On the straights, he estimates he loses 0.3 to 0.4 seconds per lap compared to the Mercedes under the 2026 50/50 hybrid power regulations. When your rival's car deploys electrical energy more efficiently at high speed, every straight becomes a deficit that the corners must try to absorb.

The new 2026 power unit regulations — with a 350-kilowatt MGU-K versus the previous era's 120-kilowatt limit — have made straight-line energy deployment into one of the defining competitive factors of the season. Mercedes has mastered this aspect. Ferrari has not. Not yet.

The ADUO Upgrade

Under the FIA's Additional Development Upgrade Opportunity system, each team receives one permitted bonus power unit upgrade per season. Ferrari used their first upgrade at the Austrian Grand Prix — and they still have one remaining. Ferrari team principal Fred Vasseur has confirmed it is coming in the second half, alongside continued chassis development.

Hamilton's promise: "It will be stronger." He said it publicly. He meant it as a signal to his rivals as much as a prediction for his fans.

The question is whether the upgrade closes the straight-line gap enough to put Hamilton within genuine striking distance of Antonelli. A 50-point gap with 291 points remaining is not insurmountable. But it requires Ferrari's second half to be demonstrably better than their first — and it requires Antonelli to falter in a way he hasn't shown any sign of doing.

The Head-to-Head Picture

Within the Ferrari garage, Hamilton leads Leclerc in race results six to five through 11 rounds. Leclerc leads qualifying six to five. Hamilton has zero retirements; Leclerc has had two race-ending incidents that have cost him significantly in the championship standings — Leclerc sits fourth on 138 points, 31 behind Hamilton. The internal battle is closely fought, but Hamilton is winning the points that matter.

His 2025 Ferrari year produced frustration. His 2026 year has produced a victory, consistency, and a genuine second-half upgrade plan. Fifty points is a manageable deficit for a seven-time world champion who has made entire careers out of closing deficits that seemed too large to close.

What's Next

Lewis Hamilton heads into the Dutch Grand Prix on August 21-23 needing to put pressure on Antonelli from Round 12 onward. Ferrari's ADUO power unit upgrade arrives in the second half of the season. With 291 points remaining and Antonelli's lead at 50, every result from Zandvoort forward carries maximum stakes. Hamilton said the second half will be stronger. The Dutch GP is where the proof begins.
