10 Days Until the Last Dutch Grand Prix Ever — And It's a Sprint Weekend

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | August 11, 2026

F1 Race Preview / Dutch Grand Prix / Zandvoort

Formula 1 returns to Circuit Zandvoort in 10 days — and it will never return again. The Dutch Grand Prix on August 21-23 is the final edition of the race under the current contract, ending F1's relationship with the coastal Dutch circuit after this weekend. When the chequered flag falls on Sunday August 23, it marks the permanent end of an era for one of motorsport's most distinctive and beloved venues.

And to send it off properly, this is the first-ever Sprint weekend in Zandvoort's history.

The Weekend Format

The Sprint structure adds a layer of complexity and opportunity to what is already a loaded race weekend:

Friday brings Free Practice 1 followed immediately by Sprint Qualifying. Saturday morning runs the Sprint Race before GP Qualifying takes over the afternoon. Sunday is the main event — 72 laps of the 4.259-kilometer Circuit Zandvoort, starting at 15:00 CET.

For teams returning from the summer break — the shutdown ends August 17, six days from now — there is essentially no warm-up time. They arrive at the circuit, run one free practice session, and then it is qualifying for the Sprint. Every data point from that first session has to count.

The Championship Picture at Zandvoort

Round 12 of 22 arrives with Kimi Antonelli holding a 50-point lead in the championship over Lewis Hamilton. Zandvoort's 291 remaining points represent the second half of the title fight — and every round counts.

The circuit has a particular emotional weight for Max Verstappen. Zandvoort is his home track — 70,000 orange-clad fans fill the banked grandstands every year chanting his name. This year, Verstappen arrives with zero wins in 11 Grands Prix, an exit clause window sitting open through October, and a car his team has acknowledged is "one second off" the leaders on pace. The farewell weekend for Zandvoort is also, for Verstappen, the first real chance to give his home crowd something to cheer in 2026.

Why Zandvoort Matters Beyond the Championship

The circuit itself is a genuinely rare F1 venue. Its signature banking at Turns 3 and 14 creates a sensation of speed that most modern circuits simply cannot replicate. The spectacle is amplified by the natural amphitheater that the circuit's layout creates — fans can see most of the track from the grandstands in a way that flat-profile circuits deny them.

When F1 returned to Zandvoort in 2021 after a 35-year absence, it immediately felt like a race that belonged on the calendar. Five years later, it's leaving again. That fact has given this particular Dutch GP a weight and a significance that transcends the championship battle alone.

What's Next

The Formula 1 summer shutdown ends August 17. Teams return to Zandvoort for the Dutch Grand Prix on August 21-23 — the final F1 race ever at the circuit, and the first-ever Sprint weekend there. From Zandvoort, the calendar moves to the Italian Grand Prix at Monza on September 4-6. The second half of the 2026 championship — 11 rounds, 291 points — begins in 10 days.
