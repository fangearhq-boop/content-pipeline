Mercedes Vows Qatar Revival After Las Vegas Proved Their Worst Day of 2026

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | November 25, 2026

Las Vegas was everything Mercedes did not want it to be. George Russell, hunting a top-two championship finish, crashed into the barriers and limped home in ninth. Kimi Antonelli, the champion already crowned, misjudged his energy deployment on the final lap and was passed by Lando Norris for second place. The team that had already secured the 2026 Constructors' Championship left Nevada describing it as the lowest-scoring weekend of their season.

Qatar is the response. Russell has arrived at Lusail saying the circuit suits Mercedes, and given what happened in Las Vegas, the team needs results more than reassurances. With Russell on 264 points — tied with Norris, 37 behind Hamilton for the final podium spot in the championship — there is real stakes attached to what happens this Sunday and in Abu Dhabi the week after.

The Las Vegas result was not entirely without mitigating factors. Mercedes took on the ADUO power unit upgrade during that race weekend, with Antonelli accepting a grid penalty to run the new specification. The team was explicitly targeting Russell's championship battle as a priority with the new hardware, and the crash undid that plan entirely. Still, the final gap between Russell and Hamilton in the standings will likely come down to these last two races.

Antonelli's Lesson from Las Vegas

For Antonelli, Las Vegas was a blemish on an otherwise extraordinary season. The 20-year-old champion entered the final lap in second place and exited it in fourth after misreading his energy deployment and allowing Norris through. It was the kind of error that speaks to experience still being accumulated rather than natural talent running dry. Antonelli sits at 402 championship points with two races to go and a 98-point lead — there is nothing at stake for him mathematically — but the manner of the Las Vegas result left a mark.

Toto Wolff, speaking after the race, was characteristically measured while still noting the incident as a learning moment. What was clear from his comments was that Mercedes view Antonelli's ceiling as very high indeed, and the occasional misstep in an already-dominant season is treated as data rather than damage.

Qatar suits Antonelli's smooth, precise driving style as much as it suits Russell's experience. Mercedes arrive with strong pace data from previous visits to Lusail and genuine belief that they can recapture what Las Vegas temporarily removed.

What's Next

Practice at Lusail begins Thursday, with qualifying on Friday and the race on Sunday over 57 laps. Mandatory tyre regulations require a minimum of two pit stops, with Pirelli's 27-lap maximum stint governing strategy. For Russell, the goal is straightforward: finish ahead of Norris and as close to Hamilton as possible, then repeat the trick in Abu Dhabi. For Antonelli, the aim is simpler still — race without regret and add one final chapter to what has been a historic first championship year. Mercedes need both of them to deliver in the final two rounds.
