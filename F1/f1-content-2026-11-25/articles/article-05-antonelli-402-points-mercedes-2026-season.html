Kimi Antonelli: 402 Points, 9 Wins and the Beginning of What Could Be an F1 Dynasty

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | November 25, 2026

With two races still to run in the 2026 Formula 1 season, it is worth pausing to consider what Kimi Antonelli has actually built this year. The 20-year-old holds 402 championship points, a 98-point lead over the field, and nine race wins. Mercedes has secured both the Drivers' and Constructors' Championships. By almost any measure, this has been a dominant season.

That does not mean it has been without challenge. The Las Vegas Grand Prix — Antonelli's most recent race before Qatar — served as a reminder that the champion is still learning, and that Formula 1 at the highest level does not offer forgiveness for even small errors in judgment.

What Las Vegas Revealed

Antonelli had second place in hand on the final lap in Las Vegas when he misjudged energy deployment and lost momentum at the wrong moment. Lando Norris, running directly behind him, found the gap and passed him before the line. Antonelli finished fourth. The championship had already been clinched, so the points loss did not alter the season's outcome — but the manner of it was notable.

The 20-year-old was straightforward about it afterward. He acknowledged the mistake, did not look for external factors to blame, and framed it as a learning moment. Toto Wolff, speaking after the race, was characteristically direct about what it means in context: a driver who can build a 2026-caliber season at age 20, Wolff suggested, is only going to get better.

That framing captures something real about where Antonelli is. Nine wins and a 98-point championship margin are achievements by any standard. But the Las Vegas error hints at the edges that remain to be smoothed as he moves from first-year champion to multi-year title contender.

The Season by the Numbers

Nine race wins from 22 races is a strike rate of roughly 41 percent. His 98-point championship lead over second place reflects consistent scoring across the full year, not just a hot streak in the opening rounds. Mercedes also secured the Constructors' title, completing a double that underscores how thoroughly the team dominated 2026.

At 20 years old, Antonelli is among the youngest Formula 1 champions in the sport's history. The title places him in a conversation alongside the sport's fastest-emerging generational talents — a comparison that becomes easier to make with each passing race of a season like this one.

What 2027 Might Look Like

The final two races of 2026 — Qatar and Abu Dhabi — give Antonelli the opportunity to close the season on his terms. There are records within reach, points to extend, and a final impression to make on a field that will return in 2027 knowing exactly what they are up against.

Wolff's public optimism about Antonelli's development trajectory is not unusual for a team principal protecting a prized asset. But in this case, the season results provide genuine foundation for what the prediction implies: that the 2026 champion is not yet at his ceiling. What comes next may be more of what 2026 delivered — with the rough edges worn away.

What's Next

The Qatar Grand Prix runs Sunday, November 29 at the Lusail Circuit — 57 laps, mandatory two stops, and the first race of the season's final weekend pair. Abu Dhabi follows to close 2026. For Antonelli, the title is long since decided. The question is simply how he ends the year — and what kind of statement he wants to make heading into 2027.
