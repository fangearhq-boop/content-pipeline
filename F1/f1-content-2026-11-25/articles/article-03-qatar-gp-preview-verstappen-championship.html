Qatar GP Preview: Verstappen Just Needs to Stay Consistent to Lock Up Second Place

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | November 25, 2026

The 2026 Qatar Grand Prix weekend begins Thursday at the Lusail International Circuit, and for the first time since Kimi Antonelli clinched the Drivers' Championship, the focus shifts to the battle behind him. With 50 points remaining across two races, the fights for second, third, and fourth place are all mathematically unresolved.

Practice runs Thursday, qualifying Friday, and the 57-lap race takes place Sunday, November 29.

Verstappen's Math

Max Verstappen arrives at Lusail holding 304 championship points, a 37-point advantage over Lewis Hamilton for second place. The numbers strongly favor the Red Bull driver. For Hamilton to overtake Verstappen by the end of the season, he would need to outscore him by 37 or more points across Qatar and Abu Dhabi combined. The maximum available to either driver across those two races is 50 points.

That means Hamilton would need to win both remaining races while Verstappen finished outside the top four in both. The scenario is theoretically possible but practically requires Verstappen to suffer the kind of catastrophic misfortune that has not visited him during this stretch of the season. Verstappen has won four of his last five races. His late-season form has been strong.

A conservative, points-securing approach at Lusail — finishing fifth or higher while Hamilton does not win — would seal second place for the Dutchman before Abu Dhabi.

The Tighter Fight Beneath

The more urgent battle may be three places lower in the standings. Russell and Norris are tied on 264 points — just three behind Hamilton — and either could realistically finish the season third, fourth, or fifth depending on how the final two rounds play out.

Russell crashed in Las Vegas and needs Qatar to go cleanly. Norris seized his opportunity in Las Vegas by passing Antonelli on the final lap to claim second place, adding points he desperately needed. Both are within reach of Hamilton if the Ferrari driver struggles in Qatar.

Perez of Red Bull starts with a five-place grid penalty for his Las Vegas collision with Haas rookie Rafael Camara, further opening the midfield positions at the front of the second group.

Strategy Notes

Pirelli has set a 27-lap maximum tyre stint for the Qatar Grand Prix. With the race running 57 laps, every driver must make at least two pit stops — a mandatory two-stop race with no flexibility for a one-stopper. That adds strategic variation to a circuit that might otherwise favor a straightforward run.

The timing of those pit stops, the tyre compounds selected, and the management of energy deployment through the final stages will likely determine whether the race produces a predictable result or a tactical shake-up in the final order.

What's Next

Practice begins Thursday, November 27, at Lusail. Qualifying takes place Friday, November 28. The Qatar Grand Prix runs Sunday, November 29, with 57 laps around one of the most demanding circuits on the calendar.

For Verstappen, it may be as simple as staying out of trouble. For Hamilton, Russell, and Norris, Qatar is not a weekend they can afford to let slip. The championship picture will look significantly clearer by Sunday evening.
