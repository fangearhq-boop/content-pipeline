Perez Handed 5-Place Qatar Penalty as FIA Places Full Blame for Las Vegas Camara Crash

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | November 25, 2026

Sergio Perez will start the Qatar Grand Prix from five places behind his qualifying position after FIA stewards found him fully responsible for his collision with Haas rookie Rafael Camara during the Las Vegas Grand Prix. The ruling landed on Tuesday, converting the 10-second time penalty Perez received during the race — which became academic after he retired — into a grid drop for Lusail.

The incident occurred at Turn 14, where Perez's front-right tyre made contact with Camara's rear-left as the two cars competed for position. Stewards reviewed the footage and concluded that Perez was not entitled to the racing line and bore full responsibility for the contact. Perez maintained after the race that he was alongside Camara entering the corner and that the rookie had not seen him, but the stewards were unmoved by that account.

For Camara, the collision had consequences far more positive. The Brazilian rookie finished 16th at Las Vegas, then remained composed in the aftermath and earned widespread respect — including, apparently, from Haas team principal Ayao Komatsu. Within days of the incident, the team confirmed Camara for the Qatar and Abu Dhabi rounds and announced he will race for Haas throughout the 2027 season. The crash that ended Perez's race arguably cemented his adversary's future.

Penalty Lands at a Difficult Moment for Perez

The timing of the penalty compounds what has been an increasingly difficult end to the 2026 season for Perez. Red Bull have already confirmed changes to their 2027 driver lineup, and whispers about the veteran's long-term future at the team have grown louder with each difficult result. A five-place grid drop at Qatar — a circuit where track position matters — is the last thing Perez needed.

He is not the only driver penalised from Las Vegas. Kick Sauber's Gabriel Bortoleto also received a five-place grid penalty, plus two licence penalty points, for his separate first-lap collision with Lance Stroll. That incident played out at Turn 1 and was investigated independently of the Perez-Camara clash, but the two penalties serve as a reminder that the stewards reviewed Las Vegas's incident list comprehensively in the days that followed.

Perez has historically performed reasonably well at Lusail, but starting from several places behind his qualifying slot with limited points left in the season creates a tricky calculation for Red Bull. The team will want him to score where possible, but the grid drop tightens his margin for error considerably.

What's Next

The Qatar Grand Prix weekend begins Thursday with practice, with qualifying on Friday and the race on Sunday over 57 laps at the Lusail Circuit. Mandatory tyre regulations — including Pirelli's 27-lap maximum stint — will force at least two pit stops, adding strategic complexity that could either help or hinder Perez depending on how Red Bull choose to play the opening laps from his penalised grid slot.

Camara, meanwhile, begins his Qatar weekend as a confirmed Haas driver for 2027 — his best starting position of the season in every sense. For the rookie, the incident in Las Vegas closed one door but opened several more. That is a story of its own as the final two races of a remarkable 2026 campaign approach.
