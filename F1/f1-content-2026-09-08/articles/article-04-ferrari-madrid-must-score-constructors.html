Ferrari Needs a Big Weekend in Madrid: 87 Points Behind in Constructors, Clock Is Ticking

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | September 8, 2026

After a Monza nightmare — Leclerc's Lap 2 crash, Hamilton's P6 — Ferrari arrives in Madrid 87 points behind Mercedes in the Constructors' Championship with eight rounds left and a new circuit where they can reset.

Monza was supposed to be Ferrari's weekend. It became the opposite.

Charles Leclerc crashed out on the second lap. Lewis Hamilton crossed the line in sixth place. The home crowd went home disappointed, and Mercedes extended their Constructors' Championship lead to 87 points. For a team that has been competitive but not quite able to match the Silver Arrows pace across this season, Monza felt like a turning point — the moment the Constructors' title stopped looking recoverable.

Ferrari hasn't given up. But Madrid is not optional. It is urgent.

The Mathematics Are Uncomfortable

With eight rounds remaining, a theoretical maximum of 25 points per race per car means Ferrari could, in a best-case scenario, outscore Mercedes by a large margin in each remaining round. But that requires consistent Ferrari wins alongside Mercedes problems — a combination that has not materialized for any sustained stretch of the 2026 season.

The 87-point Constructors' gap translates to roughly 43 points per car. In a season where Mercedes has been the benchmark, that gap does not close unless Ferrari finds something they haven't shown all year, or unless the Silver Arrows suffer significant reliability or strategic failures.

The realistic path for Ferrari involves damage limitation — keeping the Constructors' gap from growing further, scoring on podiums consistently, and hoping the calendar's remaining circuits favor their package.

The Madring Opportunity

New circuits are equalizers. Without pre-existing baseline data, every team starts Free Practice 1 on essentially equal footing — the same blank page of setup options, the same absence of historical lap time references, the same need to react to what the circuit tells you rather than what previous experience taught you.

Ferrari has historically been stronger at high-downforce, technical circuits — the kind of layout that rewards mechanical grip and car balance over outright engine power. Monza, with its emphasis on straight-line speed, was always going to suit Mercedes' power unit advantage. The Madring circuit, with its mixed-characteristic design that includes technical sections and chicanes, could offer Ferrari a more level playing field.

It is not a guarantee. But it is an opportunity.

Hamilton and Leclerc — Full Lineup for the First Time Since Monza

Ferrari's best asset arriving in Madrid is that both their drivers will be on the grid. Leclerc's clearance after his Monza medical evaluation was the essential precondition for any Ferrari response this weekend.

Hamilton, in his first full season with Ferrari, has shown the pace to fight at the front. His sixth-place finish at Monza was a product of the circuit's power-sensitive layout rather than a reflection of his ability at Madring-type venues. He knows circuits like this — where technical execution matters as much as raw pace — and has won championships with precisely this kind of ability to find performance when it is harder to find.

The task is clear: both drivers on the podium. Anything less than that in Madrid, and the Constructors' title starts to feel like a statistical impossibility rather than a mathematical long shot.

What's Next

The Madrid Grand Prix begins Friday, September 11 at Madring. Ferrari arrives with full driver strength, a circuit that should suit them better than Monza, and the knowledge that the Constructors' Championship is not yet mathematically decided.
