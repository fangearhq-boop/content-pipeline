Leclerc Cleared to Race in Madrid After Monza Vision Scare and Medical Checks

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | September 8, 2026

Charles Leclerc has passed all required medical tests following his 23G crash at Monza and will take his place on the grid for the inaugural Madrid Grand Prix this weekend.

The wait is over. Charles Leclerc has been cleared to race in Madrid.

Ferrari's No. 16 driver passed all medical tests and received full clearance from Dr. Isabel Ornaque, the Madring circuit's medical director, ending a 48-hour period of uncertainty following his massive crash at Monza's Parabolica corner. Leclerc will take his place in the F1 grid for the inaugural Madrid Grand Prix, which begins Friday, September 11.

The news comes as a significant relief for Ferrari and for fans who had been nervously following updates since the Italian Grand Prix on Sunday.

What Happened at Monza

On the second lap of the Italian Grand Prix, Leclerc was battling Oscar Piastri's McLaren approaching the Parabolica — the fearsome sweeping final corner that feeds onto Monza's famous main straight. The Ferrari snapped sideways and Leclerc had no chance to correct. He speared into the tyre barriers at high speed, with data later confirming the impact registered 23G of deceleration.

Leclerc climbed out of the destroyed car unaided, walking to the medical car as the race was red-flagged. That was an encouraging early sign. But within minutes of the incident, reports began to emerge that he had experienced vision problems — specifically, blurred or impaired vision in his right eye in the immediate aftermath of the impact.

Under F1 medical protocols, any driver reporting neurological symptoms after a high-G impact must undergo further evaluation before being permitted to compete again. Leclerc flew to Monaco on Monday to undergo imaging and assessment.

The Clearance Process

Ferrari's medical team worked alongside the FIA's medical staff and Dr. Ornaque to assess the results of Leclerc's scans and neurological testing. The criteria for clearance are not made public in detail, but involve evaluating whether any soft-tissue, ocular, or neurological effects from the impact could impair a driver's ability to compete safely at speed.

Leclerc spoke briefly about the process, noting that his vision had improved significantly after the initial post-crash period and that the checks were precautionary but thorough. "The right eye was not showing me great vision at first, but now it's all good," Leclerc said in the immediate aftermath of the crash. By Monday, he was confident the results would return clean — and they did.

The announcement that he passed all checks came ahead of the Madrid weekend, giving Ferrari the full week to prepare Leclerc's race seat, helmet fittings, and simulator sessions for a circuit neither he nor anyone on the grid has raced at before.

What It Means for Ferrari

Leclerc's availability is not a small thing for the Scuderia. After his Lap 2 DNF at Monza — one of the most painful ways to lose points, at the team's home circuit — Ferrari desperately needs both drivers scoring heavily in Madrid. Hamilton salvaged sixth at Monza but Mercedes' lead in the Constructors' Championship now stands at 87 points with eight rounds remaining. A Leclerc replacement scenario in Madrid would have been a body blow.

Instead, Ferrari arrives in Madrid with their full lineup intact, a new circuit where nobody has a data advantage, and genuine motivation to respond after Monza's nightmare.

What's Next

The Madrid Grand Prix weekend begins with Free Practice on Friday, September 11. Qualifying follows Saturday, September 12, with the race on Sunday, September 13. The Madring circuit at IFEMA Madrid is making its Formula 1 debut — a new venue where no team has pre-existing setup data. For Leclerc, it is a chance to put Monza behind him and show what he and Ferrari can do when everything is working as it should. Follow F1 Fanrecap for full Madrid GP coverage all weekend.
