Antonelli Targets Three Straight Wins in Madrid as Championship Lead Swells to 66 Points

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | September 8, 2026

After winning the Italian Grand Prix from 19th on the grid, Kimi Antonelli leads the 2026 Drivers' Championship by 66 points over Mercedes teammate George Russell and heads into the Madrid debut with unstoppable momentum.

Stop him if you can.

Kimi Antonelli heads into this weekend's inaugural Madrid Grand Prix on the back of consecutive Formula 1 victories — the Dutch Grand Prix, then the Italian Grand Prix from 19th on the grid — with a 66-point championship lead and the kind of momentum that turns a title race from a competition into a formality.

He is 20 years old. He is driving for Mercedes. And right now, he is the best driver on the F1 grid.

Where the Championship Stands

After fourteen races, the 2026 Drivers' Championship table looks like this: Kimi Antonelli leads with 267 points. George Russell sits second on 201, a gap of 66. Lewis Hamilton is third with 191, 76 points adrift. Lando Norris occupies fourth with 184, 83 behind the leader.

With eight rounds remaining in the season — including the disputed season finale venues — the maximum points available to any driver is in the low 200s. Antonelli's lead is not insurmountable in raw mathematics, but it requires a near-perfect run for any challenger while Antonelli struggles simultaneously. That scenario is increasingly difficult to imagine.

In the Constructors' Championship, Mercedes leads Ferrari by 87 points — a gap that reflects just how dominant the Silver Arrows package has been across the second half of the season.

The Monza Performance in Context

Sunday's Italian Grand Prix win will be discussed for years. Starting from 19th position after an engine-related grid penalty, Antonelli had to overtake 18 cars while managing tyre wear, responding to a red flag after Charles Leclerc's Lap 2 crash, and finding the right moment to pit under a Virtual Safety Car while teammate Russell was leading.

He passed Russell on Lap 50 of 53 — using the fresher medium tyres from his VSC stop against Russell's worn hard compounds. The margin at the finish was 3.857 seconds. It looked comfortable at the end, but the execution required by Antonelli to set up that position was anything but.

He also became the first Italian driver to win the Italian Grand Prix since Ludovico Scarfiotti in 1966 — a piece of history that resonated far beyond the technical achievement of the drive itself.

What Russell Needs in Madrid

George Russell is the only driver on the grid with both the car and the proximity to realistically challenge Antonelli for the title. But Russell knows the arithmetic too. A 66-point gap with eight rounds remaining means Antonelli could afford to score second-place finishes in all remaining races and still be mathematically difficult to catch unless Russell wins consistently.

The Madring circuit at IFEMA Madrid presents an opportunity in theory — a new circuit where nobody has a setup advantage, where driver instinct matters more than historical data, and where the playing field is genuinely level going into Free Practice 1. But Russell needs to not just beat Antonelli — he needs to beat him by meaningful margins, consistently.

Expect another closely fought intra-Mercedes battle at Madring. The team is unlikely to impose team orders with this much of the season remaining, which means Sunday's race could come down to strategy, pace, and the same qualities that have separated Antonelli from the rest of the field all year: composure under pressure and pace when it matters most.
