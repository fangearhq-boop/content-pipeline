## Story Summaries

### STORY 1: Leclerc Cleared for Madrid
Following his massive Lap 2 crash at Monza (23G impact at Parabolica), Ferrari's Charles Leclerc underwent medical checks in Monaco on Monday. He reported temporary right-eye vision problems post-crash. Ferrari's doctor required Leclerc to undergo neurological assessment before being cleared. Update: Leclerc has now passed all medical checks and will race in Madrid this weekend. Dr. Isabel Ornaque (medical director at Madring) signed off on his return to competition.

### STORY 2: Madrid Grand Prix — Circuit Debut
F1's 14th round of the 2026 season heads to Madrid for the inaugural Madring Grand Prix (September 11-13). The Madring circuit at IFEMA Madrid is a purpose-built street/hybrid circuit making its Formula 1 debut. After Monza's power-dependent layout, Madring's mixed characteristics present a contrasting challenge for teams. This is the second Spanish race on the calendar (Barcelona was held earlier in 2026).

### STORY 3: Antonelli Championship Momentum
Antonelli leads the 2026 Drivers' Championship with 267 points — 66 ahead of teammate George Russell. He has won his last two consecutive races (Dutch GP + Italian GP). A third straight victory in Madrid would be a major championship statement. Russell, starting from P2 at Monza, knows the pressure: he must outscore Antonelli significantly at Madring to stay in mathematical contention.

### STORY 4: Ferrari's Madrid Mission
Mercedes leads Ferrari 87 points in Constructors'. Hamilton finished P6 at Monza in what was a damage-limitation result for Ferrari at their home race (made worse by Leclerc's Lap 2 DNF). Hamilton must convert in Madrid. With Leclerc cleared, Ferrari fields their full lineup at Madring. The team needs both drivers to score heavily on a circuit where they hope the power-mode mix will be more favorable than Monza's power-sensitive layout.

### STORY 5: Season Finale Calendar — Qatar/Abu Dhabi Decision
FOM chief Stefano Domenicali said a decision on the Middle East season finale would come "by mid-September." Qatar GP (Nov. 27-29 at Lusail) and Abu Dhabi GP (Dec. 4-6 at Yas Marina) remain on the calendar, but US military action near the Strait of Hormuz and regional instability make their future uncertain. Contingency options: Imola (leading candidate) or Portimao. Bahrain and Saudi GPs have already been cancelled and rerouted (Malaysia Sepang, Oct. 2-4).
