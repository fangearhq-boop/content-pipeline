The Real F1 Championship Fight: Verstappen, Russell, and Hamilton Separated by 18 Points Entering Las Vegas

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | November 15, 2026

Kimi Antonelli may be the champion, but the most compelling numbers heading into the Las Vegas Grand Prix belong to Max Verstappen, George Russell, and Lewis Hamilton — three drivers separated by 18 points with 75 still available across the season's final three races.

At first glance, a closed championship race heading into the final three rounds feels anticlimactic. But the 2026 Formula 1 season has refused to let its drama evaporate with the Drivers' title secured. Instead, it has redirected that energy into a three-way battle for second place that makes the standings table compulsive reading.

Verstappen: 279 points. Russell: 262 points. Hamilton: 261 points. Eighteen points separate first from third in a battle that will play out across Las Vegas, Qatar, and Abu Dhabi — 75 championship points still available, any of three outcomes still possible.

Verstappen's Comfort Zone

At 17 points clear of Russell entering Las Vegas, Verstappen holds the clearest path to the runner-up position. His São Paulo recovery drive — from 21st to second — delivered 18 crucial points and extended the margin from roughly two to seventeen. That is the difference between tension and control.

But in Formula 1, 17 points across three races is not safety — it is a small cushion. A retirement, a penalty, a strategy miscalculation can erase it in a single afternoon. What Verstappen needs is not a miracle. He needs a consistent run through Las Vegas that doesn't throw the door open for Russell or Hamilton.

Russell's Window

George Russell at 262 points is not out of this fight — he is one good weekend away from reopening it. The Las Vegas street circuit is technical, and Russell has shown throughout 2026 that he excels in conditions that reward mechanical sympathy and corner precision over pure top-end power. If the W17 is competitive in Las Vegas — and given the constructors' title, there is every reason to think it will be — Russell has the tools to close the gap.

The challenge for Russell is that he cannot depend on Verstappen's misfortune. He needs to generate his own points surplus — wins where Verstappen doesn't podium, podiums where Verstappen finishes lower. It is a narrow path, but it is a real one.

Hamilton's Long Shot

Lewis Hamilton at 261 — one point behind his Mercedes teammate Russell — represents one of the more unusual subplots of the season's end. The two Silver Arrows drivers are both chasing the same prize, and while team orders are unlikely to become a factor this late in a campaign where both championships are already settled, the internal dynamic between Russell and Hamilton will be worth monitoring.

Hamilton would need to gain 19 points on Verstappen while also staying ahead of Russell. It is the longest shot of the three — but Hamilton has pulled off longer odds in a career defined by improbable achievements.

What's Next

Las Vegas on November 21 is the opening act of the season's final triple-header. For Verstappen, Russell, and Hamilton, every qualifying lap and every overtaking move matters in ways that championship-clinched weekends typically do not. The three-race sprint to Abu Dhabi will determine which of them stands on the winner's podium alongside Antonelli at the final ceremony.

The Drivers' Championship is settled. The P2 fight is very much alive.
