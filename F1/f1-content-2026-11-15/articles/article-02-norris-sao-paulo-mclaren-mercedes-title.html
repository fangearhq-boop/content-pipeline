Norris Masters a Wet Interlagos as McLaren Wins São Paulo — Mercedes Clinches 2026 Constructors' Title

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | November 15, 2026

Lando Norris won the 2026 São Paulo Grand Prix from pole at a rain-soaked Interlagos on November 8, claiming McLaren's third victory of the season while Max Verstappen produced an exceptional drive from 21st to second. The result sealed the 2026 Constructors' World Championship for Mercedes.

Interlagos has a habit of producing Formula 1 stories that linger. The 2026 São Paulo Grand Prix gave the paddock three in one afternoon. Lando Norris delivered a dominant rain-master performance to win from pole. Max Verstappen produced the drive of the race from 21st on the grid to second. And through it all, Mercedes clinched the 2026 Constructors' World Championship — their second title of the season alongside Kimi Antonelli's Drivers' crown.

When the checkered flag fell at the end of a wet, gripping race at Interlagos, McLaren's third win of the season was confirmed. The margin was approximately 14.6 seconds over Verstappen at the finish. Isack Hadjar brought his Red Bull home third.

Norris in Control

From the moment the lights went out in difficult, wet conditions, Norris was in command. He read the changing track surface with the kind of instinctive pace management that separates race winners from competitors, and he never gave anyone behind him the platform to mount a challenge. For Norris, it was a performance that validated everything McLaren has been building over the last two seasons — a driver and car capable of winning in any conditions when the timing is right.

McLaren's third win of 2026 gives them 398 Constructors' points, and while the title was always Mercedes' to lose, the papaya team has demonstrated they are a genuine contender for the future.

Verstappen's Spectacular Comeback

Whatever grid slot Verstappen started from in São Paulo — 21st — was irrelevant by the time he crossed the finish line. His charge through the field on a wet Interlagos circuit was relentless: sector by sector, lap by lap, corner by corner, until second place was claimed and 18 championship points were banked. For a driver in a P2 title fight, that drive may have been season-defining.

Kimi Antonelli, whose championship was already secured, finished fifth with the low-key efficiency of a champion who understood what the race meant to others around him.

Mercedes' Constructors' Crown

The announcement arrived alongside the checkered flag: Mercedes are the 2026 Formula 1 Constructors' World Champions. Their 652 points represent a commanding lead over Ferrari's 510 and Red Bull's 415 — a margin that, over the course of a 23-race season, reflects not luck or circumstance but sustained excellence from car, strategy, and all three drivers.

Antonelli, Russell, and Hamilton each contributed throughout the year. The W17 concept delivered from the first race in Bahrain and never stopped. The double championship haul — both Drivers' and Constructors' titles — is the sport's highest honor, and Mercedes earned it comprehensively.

What's Next

Las Vegas awaits on November 19-21. Norris heads there with momentum and confidence, a driver who has reminded the paddock of his capability precisely when the season enters its final act. Verstappen heads there needing more of what he showed at Interlagos — a drive that makes the opposition look like it's standing still.

The title is gone. The race is not. When those two things are true simultaneously, Formula 1 gives you exactly what you came to watch.
