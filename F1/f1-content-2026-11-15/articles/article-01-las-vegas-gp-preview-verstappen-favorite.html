Las Vegas GP Preview: Verstappen Arrives as Race Favorite While Antonelli Races Free as Champion

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | November 15, 2026

The 2026 Las Vegas Grand Prix kicks off Thursday with Max Verstappen entering as the fan favorite and co-betting pick at +200 alongside Kimi Antonelli, whose clinched Drivers' Championship means he races with nothing to lose on the Strip.

Four days. That is all that separates Formula 1 from one of the sport's most anticipated evenings of the year. The Las Vegas Grand Prix lights up the Strip circuit on November 19-21, and for the first time since the season's opening rounds, the paddock arrives with both championships already decided — but with no shortage of storylines that give the race genuine meaning.

Kimi Antonelli, the 2026 Drivers' World Champion, comes to Las Vegas with 390 points on the board and the title secured. He and Max Verstappen are listed as co-favorites at +200 for race victory. George Russell, who needs every point in the championship battle for second place, enters at +600. The race itself is Saturday night, November 21.

Verstappen's Stakes

For Max Verstappen, Las Vegas is not a celebration weekend — it is a mission. The Red Bull driver holds second place in the Drivers' Championship with 279 points, 17 clear of George Russell's 262 and 18 ahead of Lewis Hamilton's 261. With 75 points still available across Las Vegas, Qatar, and Abu Dhabi, that lead is comfortable but far from insurmountable.

A win in Las Vegas would not just add 25 points to Verstappen's tally — it would deliver a psychological blow to Russell and Hamilton's hopes of stealing the runner-up position. The Las Vegas street circuit has been a circuit where Verstappen's car performs, and his momentum from the São Paulo Grand Prix — where he charged from 21st to second — suggests his confidence is at a season high.

Antonelli's Freedom

Antonelli arrives in a unique position. As Drivers' Champion, he carries no pressure. He can attack without fear of championship consequences, take risks that would terrify a title contender, and race for the win on pure terms. This combination of elite machinery and mental freedom has historically produced some of the sport's most commanding individual performances.

Whether he treats Las Vegas as a demonstration or a genuine race challenge is one of the weekend's defining subplots. His talent is not in question. His motivation, without a title on the line, is the intriguing unknown.

The Circuit and the Field

The Las Vegas street circuit rewards precision and mechanical grip more than raw power, which explains why Russell at +600 is not as long a shot as it looks on paper. The Silver Arrows' W17 delivered one of the most dominant constructors' campaigns in recent memory, and Russell brings the technical discipline that the circuit demands.

Lando Norris, fresh from his São Paulo victory, arrives in strong form. Isack Hadjar, who podiumed in Brazil, represents the next generation of Red Bull's depth. And throughout the field, drivers with nothing to lose in the championship race will be pushing for Vegas glory with the same desire that has made this race appointment viewing since its debut.

What's Next

Practice begins Thursday. Qualifying Friday night. And Saturday, the lights go out on a Las Vegas race that — despite Antonelli's closed title — carries real stakes for three of the sport's biggest names. When Verstappen, Russell, and Hamilton all need something from the same circuit, Formula 1 at its best is exactly what gets delivered.

This is the moment the season's final act truly begins.
