Qatar GP Weekend Opens: Verstappen Targets Third Consecutive Lusail Victory

By Ryan Calloway

November 27, 2026

The 2026 Formula 1 Qatar Grand Prix weekend is underway at the Lusail International Circuit, with Practice 1 and Practice 2 taking place Friday ahead of qualifying Saturday and the race on Sunday. For Max Verstappen, the desert night circuit carries particular significance: he has won the Qatar Grand Prix in each of the past three seasons, turning Lusail into one of his most reliable hunting grounds on the entire calendar.

A third consecutive Qatar victory would extend a streak of track dominance that speaks to Verstappen's ability to read and exploit specific circuit characteristics. The Red Bull driver arrives on the back of his Las Vegas victory — his third consecutive Las Vegas win — and enters the penultimate round as the championship's second-placed driver with 304 points.

What Makes Verstappen Exceptional at Lusail

The Lusail circuit rewards precision, tire management, and the ability to extract performance from a car under sustained heat and load. Verstappen's three consecutive wins here have come in different regulatory eras and with different car specifications, suggesting his success at Qatar is as much about his driving approach as it is about machinery advantage.

The circuit's demanding medium-to-high-speed corners, particularly in the second sector, require consistent brake balance and high-confidence entry speeds. Verstappen's ability to maintain those confidence levels late into stints — when tire degradation peaks — has been the defining factor in his Qatar performances.

Pirelli has set a 27-lap maximum stint for this weekend's race, enforcing a minimum two-stop strategy across the 57-lap distance. That tactical constraint affects all drivers, but it plays into Verstappen's comfort zone: he is exceptionally adept at managing degradation right up to the statutory limit, then maximizing the fresh rubber window after a stop.

Antonelli: The Champion Without Pressure

Kimi Antonelli arrives at Qatar as the confirmed 2026 World Champion, having sealed the title at the Mexico City Grand Prix weeks ago. The 20-year-old Mercedes driver now faces the season's final two rounds free of championship anxiety, a mental shift that some drivers describe as simultaneously liberating and oddly disorienting.

For Mercedes, the remaining races offer an opportunity to extend the constructors' championship margin, finalize internal testing objectives, and give Antonelli experience of winning seasons' closing chapters — context that will matter deeply as the team builds around their new standard-bearer for years to come.

The Weekend Ahead

Practice 1 and Practice 2 today will give each team their first meaningful data set from the weekend, particularly around tire behavior in the cooler ambient temperatures of a November Qatar evening. Qualifying on Saturday will set the grid for Sunday's race, with the full championship picture to be digested once results come in.

Several drivers arrive at Lusail with specific agendas beyond their qualifying position. Hamilton needs points to close on Verstappen in the P2 battle. Russell and Norris are tied for fourth and will be watching each other as closely as they watch the front. Sergio Perez starts with a five-place grid penalty, adding strategic complexity for Cadillac.

What's Next

Qualifying takes place Saturday, followed by the race at 16:00 local time on Sunday, November 29. For Verstappen, a win at Lusail would be his fourth consecutive Qatar victory — another layer in a legacy that is still being written. For Hamilton, Norris, Russell, and the rest, the penultimate round is a final chance to make the standings table look the way they want it to look before Abu Dhabi closes the 2026 season.
