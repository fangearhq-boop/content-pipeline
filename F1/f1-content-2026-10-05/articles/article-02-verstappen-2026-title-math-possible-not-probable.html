Verstappen Finally Won in 2026. Here's Why He Still Probably Can't Win the Title.

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | October 5, 2026

Max Verstappen got his first Formula 1 win of 2026 in Malaysia on Sunday. It was dominant, it was earned, and for a driver who had spent the entire season watching his historic run of consecutive championships come to an end, it was a moment of validation. He can still win. The car can still win. The chapter is not over.

And yet.

Verstappen heads to Singapore trailing Kimi Antonelli by more than 80 points in the drivers' championship, with only seven rounds remaining after Singapore and a maximum of 183 points still available. The path to a fifth consecutive title requires a scenario that, while not mathematically impossible, demands near-perfection from Verstappen and a catastrophic collapse from Antonelli — a collapse that the 19-year-old Mercedes driver has given no indication of producing.

The Numbers

There are 183 points left to score in the 2026 season, including Sprint points. If Verstappen wins everything, he finishes the year on approximately 371 points. For Antonelli to lose the title from 320 points, he would need to score fewer than 51 more points across seven races and a Sprint. That amounts to roughly an average of P7 or lower in every remaining round while Verstappen wins everything.

In a normal championship, that calculation would already be over. In a season involving Antonelli — who has eight wins, six poles, and 13 podiums — there is no rational basis for expecting that kind of scoring collapse. Antonelli has the team, the car, and the mental composure to manage a lead. He has done it for 16 rounds already.

The Malaysia win was Verstappen's reminder that he belongs in conversations about the greatest in the sport's history. But reminders and titles are different things, and the gap between them is currently wider than Red Bull would like to admit.

What Singapore Means for Verstappen

The Singapore Sprint format offers a compressed opportunity for Verstappen to close ground — if Antonelli has an off weekend and Verstappen takes maximum points, the gap could shrink meaningfully. That scenario keeps the mathematical conversation alive into October and November. Without it, the conversation may effectively end at Marina Bay.

For Verstappen personally, Singapore is also a chance to build on Malaysia's momentum. He has not been on the podium consistently this season, and the rhythm that defined his title runs — relentless points accumulation, mechanical reliability, and the understanding with his car that comes from confidence — has been absent for much of 2026. Recapturing it in one of the sport's most demanding environments would matter for next year, even if it does not change this one's outcome.

What's Next

Verstappen has historically been fast at Marina Bay, but the street circuit's characteristics put a premium on setup precision that Red Bull has sometimes struggled with under the new 2026 regulations. If the car that won in Malaysia translates to Singapore's lower-speed corners and high-downforce requirements, Verstappen is a genuine podium threat. Whether that is enough to materially change the title picture will depend on what Antonelli does alongside him.
