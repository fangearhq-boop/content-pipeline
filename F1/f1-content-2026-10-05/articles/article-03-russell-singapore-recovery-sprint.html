Russell Is 84 Points Down with 7 Races Left — Singapore's Sprint Is His Best Chance

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | October 5, 2026

George Russell's 2026 Formula 1 season has been exceptional in almost every way that does not involve the championship standings. He is No. 2 at Mercedes. He is second in the world. And heading into Singapore, he is 84 points behind his own teammate with seven rounds remaining — a gap that would require near-impossible circumstances to close.

The Malaysia retirement was the moment the championship calculus shifted decisively. Russell had been within striking distance for much of the second half of the season. Then the safety car came out in the closing laps at Sepang, and his car stopped behind it. No points. Antonelli scored. The lead widened to its largest margin of the year.

Why Singapore Matters More Than Any Other Round

Under normal circumstances — 25 points per race, no Sprint — Russell needs to outscore Antonelli by an average of 12 points in every remaining round. That requires him to win every race and hope Antonelli finishes outside the points consistently. The probability of that happening is low.

But Singapore is not a normal round. It is a Sprint weekend. That means Saturday offers an additional 8 points — and if Russell wins the Sprint and the race while Antonelli scores nothing in either session, Russell recovers 33 points in a single visit. Over the remaining seven rounds including the Sprint, the gap is not mathematically insurmountable — it is just brutally improbable.

Russell is one of the best drivers in Formula 1. He has the pace to win in Singapore. He has been on the podium multiple times this season and has the qualifying speed to put himself at the front of the grid. What he cannot control is what Antonelli does. And therein lies the dilemma that has defined this championship battle since July.

The Character Test

What matters now for Russell is not the odds. It is the response. Formula 1 history is full of drivers who kept competing at the highest level after their title chances evaporated — some because the math still technically allowed it, others because the character of a champion does not change based on the standings. Russell has won races this year. He has driven at a level that, in most seasons, would have been enough to win a championship. The 2026 season simply produced someone better.

Singapore is his next race. He will compete to win. He will use the Sprint to score maximum points. And he will do it because that is what he does, regardless of what the spreadsheet says.

What's Next

The Sprint Qualifying session on Friday in Singapore will tell the story early. If Russell qualifies at the front and takes the Sprint win with Antonelli behind him, the weekend becomes something to follow closely. If the gap widens further over the weekend, the mathematical conversation will effectively end before the next round. Watch closely for how Russell responds after Malaysia — the mental fortitude in adversity is the thing that separates the very best drivers from the rest.
