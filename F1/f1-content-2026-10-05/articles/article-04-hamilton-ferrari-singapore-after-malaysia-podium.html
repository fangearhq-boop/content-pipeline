Hamilton and Ferrari Head to Singapore with Malaysian Momentum — and a Podium to Build On

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | October 5, 2026

Somewhere in the rain chaos at Sepang on Sunday, Lewis Hamilton drove one of the races of the year. Starting from 18th. Picking his way through a field scattered by a wet Malaysian afternoon. Finishing third. A Ferrari double podium with Charles Leclerc taking fourth.

It was a performance that reminded anyone watching why Hamilton is still in Formula 1 at 41, and why Ferrari signed him to lead their championship challenge. The wet conditions suited everything he has spent his career building — the late braking, the tire sensitivity, the instinctive reads on when to push and when to protect. He does not do this for the nostalgia. He does it because he is still very good at it.

Ferrari's Setup Challenge at Marina Bay

Singapore presents a different kind of test. Marina Bay Street Circuit is defined by its slow corners, tight walls, and premium on mechanical grip and downforce. Ferrari's performance at high-downforce, low-speed circuits in 2026 has been mixed — they have shown race pace without always converting it into wins, and their strategy execution on a standard weekend has been inconsistent.

But the Malaysian race showed something important: Ferrari can execute strategy when the situation demands creativity. The call to bring Hamilton in during the rain transition, which dropped him to P18, was aggressive — and it paid off because Hamilton had the composure to make the most of fresh tires while others struggled on worn compounds. That kind of operational intelligence can transfer to Singapore's unique demands.

Leclerc, who took P4 in Malaysia after a difficult qualifying, adds another dimension. Two strong Ferrari cars at Marina Bay — a circuit where track position matters enormously because overtaking is so difficult — would give the team tactical options that can make the difference between a podium and a points finish outside the top five.

Hamilton's Championship Context

Hamilton sits third in the drivers' championship. His title chances are effectively over — he is more than 100 points behind Antonelli — but his motivation at Ferrari has never been purely mathematical. He came to Maranello to win a race for the most storied team in Formula 1 history and, ultimately, to add to his seven world championships. A Singapore win would be his first on a street circuit as a Ferrari driver and would add another chapter to one of the sport's most remarkable late-career narratives.

He is 41. He is still P3 in the world. And he is coming off one of his best drive performances of the season. Singapore, historically a race where experience and precision trump raw speed, may suit him perfectly.

What's Next

Ferrari will need their qualifying pace to be consistent at Marina Bay — starting at the front matters more here than almost anywhere else on the calendar. If Hamilton and Leclerc can lock out the front two rows on Saturday and translate that into a well-managed race on Sunday, Ferrari could leave Singapore with a result that keeps their constructors' championship ambitions alive for the remaining six rounds.
