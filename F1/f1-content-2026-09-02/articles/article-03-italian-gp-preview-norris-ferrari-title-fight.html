Italian GP Preview: Norris, Ferrari, and Russell All Have a Shot — The Title Race Opens Up at Monza

By Marcus Cole

September 2, 2026

The formula for this weekend's Italian Grand Prix is almost too good to be scripted. Kimi Antonelli — the championship leader — starts from the back of the grid with a fresh power unit. Lando Norris, coming off back-to-back victories at the last two rounds, starts from near the front as the favorite. Ferrari brings a tribute livery and a new engine to their home circuit. George Russell has a championship to fight for. And Max Verstappen, who won this race in 2025, is never to be counted out at Monza. Sunday's race in Italy is wide open in a way that the 2026 championship has not often been.

Norris: Momentum, Math, and an Open Door

Lando Norris arrives at Monza as the driver with the most momentum in Formula 1. He won in Hungary in late July and followed it with victory at Zandvoort in August, beating Antonelli by 11.5 seconds in a race that temporarily made the championship feel close. At 83 points behind Antonelli, Norris needs to close the gap aggressively — and this weekend presents his best opportunity. The McLaren driver is listed at 6/4 odds to win the Italian GP, reflecting both his form and the straightforward reality that his primary rival starts from position 20.

Monza suits McLaren. The circuit's long straights and low-downforce demands play to the strengths of a car that has shown its pace on power circuits all season. If Norris can take pole position — which Antonelli's penalty makes almost entirely probable — he starts the race in exactly the position he needs to maximize the potential gap swing.

Ferrari's Dual Weapon: Tribute and Technology

Ferrari brings the ADUO-2 engine upgrade to Monza — approximately 15 additional horsepower and 0.2 seconds per lap at the most power-sensitive venue on the calendar. Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc receive the upgrade without grid penalties. At a circuit where pure engine performance is a primary differentiator, Ferrari's new specification could put one or both of its cars in contention for a first victory of the season's final phase.

Hamilton, 59 points behind Antonelli, is mathematically in the fight. The Schumacher tribute livery around him adds emotional weight to a weekend where technical circumstances already favor Ferrari. Leclerc, racing in front of the tifosi, will be similarly motivated. Between the pair, Ferrari could play either a No. 1 driver strategy or give both pilots freedom to race — a decision that will become clearer in qualifying.

Russell, Verstappen, and the Rest

George Russell occupies an interesting position this weekend. He is also 59 points behind Antonelli, tied with Hamilton in the standings, and will start from a conventional grid position in what is otherwise an identical Mercedes to his teammate's — save for the fresh power unit. Russell's target is clear: if Antonelli struggles to recover from the back, Russell finishing ahead of Norris could meaningfully improve his own title position.

Max Verstappen won the 2025 Italian Grand Prix and remains a factor whenever traction and power matter more than aerodynamic load. Red Bull has struggled relative to their 2025 form, but Verstappen's ability to extract unexpected results at Monza specifically makes him worth watching in qualifying and race strategy alike.

What's Next

Practice begins Friday at 12:30 CEST (6:30 AM ET). Qualifying is Saturday at 16:00 CEST (10:00 AM ET). The race starts Sunday at 15:00 CEST (9:00 AM ET) on ESPN. Watch the gap between pole position and the championship leader's recovery pace — that number tells you how the title race evolves out of Monza.
