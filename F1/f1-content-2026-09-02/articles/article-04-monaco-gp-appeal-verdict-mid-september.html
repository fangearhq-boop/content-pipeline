Monaco GP Appeal: Verdict Still Weeks Away as F1 Heads to Monza and Madrid

By Elena Voss

September 2, 2026

Three months have passed since Formula 1 raced through the streets of Monte Carlo, and the official result of the Monaco Grand Prix is still not final. The FIA's International Court of Appeal — the sport's highest judicial body — heard arguments from McLaren and Red Bull on August 25 in Paris, but indicated it will need another two to three weeks to deliver its verdict. That timeline means the Monaco classification could remain unresolved through this weekend's Italian Grand Prix and into the inaugural Madrid Grand Prix on September 13.

The Background

The dispute traces back to an incident during Monaco race day. Pierre Gasly was penalized for speeding in the pit lane, costing him a podium finish. Alpine, Gasly's team, subsequently exercised their Right of Review — a formal process allowing teams to present new evidence not available at the time of the original ruling. The review was granted and, crucially, F1's commercial rights holder FOM admitted there had been a measurement error in the original incident data.

With the errors acknowledged, Gasly's time penalties were removed and the Frenchman was reinstated to third place. That decision demoted Isack Hadjar (Red Bull) from P3 to P4 and Oscar Piastri (McLaren) from P4 to P5 in the Monaco classification — consequences with meaningful championship points implications for both drivers and their teams. Red Bull and McLaren lodged appeals immediately on June 16.

What the Appeal Could Decide

The central question before the ICA is whether the stewards had the authority to reinstate Gasly's position in the manner they did, and whether the Right of Review process was applied appropriately given FOM's measurement error admission. McLaren and Red Bull argue the stewards exceeded their remit. Gasly and Alpine argue the reinstatement was correct given the evidence.

If the ICA rules in favor of McLaren and Red Bull, Gasly loses his Monaco P3. Hadjar moves from P4 to P3, adding championship points for Red Bull. Piastri moves from P5 to P4, adding points for McLaren. These shifts ripple through the Constructors' Championship standings as well as the Drivers' Championship, where Gasly currently holds No. 3 in the standings — a position that Hadjar would claim if the appeal succeeds.

The Waiting Game

The ICA's judges underscored the complexity of the case when they explained why an immediate ruling was not possible. The proceedings in Paris included technical testimony about the measurement protocols used at Monaco, legal arguments about the stewards' jurisdiction under the FIA's sporting regulations, and questions about the appropriate remedy when FOM itself acknowledges an error in the original data.

A ruling is expected before the end of September — most likely before or shortly after the Madrid Grand Prix on September 13. Until then, every championship calculation involving Gasly, Hadjar, and Piastri carries a footnote: these standings are provisional, pending one more court decision that Monaco is not yet finished producing.

What's Next

The Italian Grand Prix at Monza this weekend (September 6) and the Madrid Grand Prix on September 13 will both be contested while Monaco's official result remains open. Watch for the ICA verdict announcement, likely during the week of September 8-12 or immediately after Madrid. When the ruling arrives, the championship standings will either confirm what they currently show or produce a modest but potentially significant reshuffle.
