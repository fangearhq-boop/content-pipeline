Antonelli Starts Italian GP From Back: Championship Leader Takes Strategic PU Penalty at Home

By Elena Voss

September 2, 2026

Formula 1 championship leader Kimi Antonelli will start Sunday's Italian Grand Prix at Monza from the back of the 20-car grid after Mercedes confirmed he will take a full power unit penalty at his home race. It is a calculated strategic risk that has handed his title rivals their clearest opportunity of the 2026 season — and a stage set for one of the most compelling storylines in recent F1 memory.

Mercedes team principal Toto Wolff confirmed the penalty decision in the build-up to race week, calling the coming stretch "an important period" for the team. The decision to take the penalty at Monza was deliberate: the circuit is the fastest on the calendar, overtaking opportunities are plentiful compared to street circuits, and Antonelli's 59-point championship lead provides enough of a buffer that even a points-limited race in Italy would not necessarily damage his title prospects.

Why Monza, Why Now

The decision to take a fresh power unit was precipitated by a reliability failure at the Spanish Grand Prix in Barcelona, where Antonelli suffered a race-ending stoppage. Each driver is allocated four power unit components per season, and any usage beyond that allocation triggers a mandatory starting position drop. With seven races remaining after Monza and the championship still very much alive, Mercedes calculated that a fresh, reliable unit was worth the short-term cost of starting at the back.

The full scope of the change is significant: Antonelli's V6, exhaust system, MGU-H, and related components are all being replaced. This is not a partial penalty but a complete reset. The upside is substantial — Antonelli will carry those fresh components through the remaining races on the schedule, including circuits that put significantly more strain on power units than Monza. The team is playing a long game.

The Championship Math

Heading into Monza, the championship standings read: Antonelli 242, George Russell 183, Lewis Hamilton 183, Lando Norris 159. Both Russell and Hamilton are 59 points behind Antonelli; Norris is 83 points back. In a worst-case scenario where Norris wins the Italian GP and collects the bonus fastest lap point while Antonelli finishes last and scores a single point, the gap shrinks from 83 to 59 — still a sizable margin with 275 points mathematically remaining.

That cushion is why Mercedes could afford this move. Antonelli's consistent scoring across the season has built a lead that is difficult to erode in a single race, even one where he starts from position 20. At a circuit like Monza where the racing is faster and closer than most venues, a determined drive from the back can still produce points. It is not inconceivable that Antonelli finishes in the top ten.

The Rivals' Response

For Norris, this is the moment his title campaign needed. The McLaren driver has won back-to-back races at Hungary and the Netherlands and arrives at Monza with genuine momentum. A Norris victory in Italy, combined with Antonelli failing to score well, could reduce the deficit to a truly competitive range. Ferrari's Lewis Hamilton — driving at a circuit bathed this weekend in tribute to his hero Michael Schumacher — will equally not be holding back. George Russell, in the other Mercedes, starts from a conventional grid position and has his own title ambitions to serve.

The Italian Grand Prix begins with FP1 on Friday, September 4 at 12:30 CEST. Qualifying takes place on Saturday, September 5 at 16:00 CEST. Race day is Sunday, September 6 at 15:00 CEST (9:00 AM ET on ESPN).

What's Next

Watch qualifying on Saturday to see whether Norris can secure pole with Antonelli starting last regardless. Then on Sunday, the battle begins in earnest — not just for race victory, but for championship momentum going into the second half of the F1 season's final stretch. If Antonelli can recover into the points from position 20 while Norris or Hamilton wins, the title picture changes. If Antonelli ends up scoring nothing, the math gets genuinely interesting.
