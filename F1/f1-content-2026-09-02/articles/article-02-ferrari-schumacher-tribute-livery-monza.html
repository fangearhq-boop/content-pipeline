Ferrari Unveils Schumacher Tribute Livery for Monza: 30 Years of Red, Seven Stars, and a New Engine

By Ryan Calloway

September 2, 2026

Ferrari has revealed one of the most emotionally resonant liveries in recent Formula 1 history for this weekend's Italian Grand Prix at Monza — a tribute to Michael Schumacher on the 30th anniversary of his debut season with the Scuderia. The SF-26 cars that Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc will pilot through the Temple of Speed this weekend look and feel unlike anything Ferrari has brought to a race weekend in years.

The Design

The livery takes its inspiration from the 1996 Ferrari F310, the car Schumacher drove in his first season with the team. The design is predominantly red — even more so than Ferrari's usual specification, with the white tone on the engine cover removed entirely to allow an unbroken field of Scuderia red. Gold accents on the wheels echo the all-gold wheel design of the 1996 car, a distinctive visual touch that links the 2026 machine to its 30-year-old ancestor.

On the car's nose, seven gold stars are arranged — one for each of Schumacher's seven world championships. The same design element appeared on Schumacher's racing helmet following his final title in 2004, when he became the only driver at the time to claim seven drivers' crowns. Hamilton and Leclerc will wear race suits featuring those same seven stars across the weekend.

Ferrari's slogan for the livery: "Home is calling."

Schumacher and Monza

The circuit choice makes this tribute particularly fitting. Michael Schumacher won five Italian Grands Prix wearing Ferrari red: 1996, 1998, 2000, 2003, and 2006. Of his 72 Ferrari victories — out of 91 career wins total — Monza was a venue where he found consistent brilliance. Schumacher joined Ferrari in 1996, the season that provides this livery's visual template, and won his first Italian GP in that first Ferrari season. The arc from that 1996 debut to his final Monza win in 2006 spans the peak of one of the sport's most dominant dynasties.

Hamilton, now in his second Ferrari season, is acutely aware of the weight of that legacy. Ferrari's ambitions in 2026 have been centered partly on Hamilton's ability to deliver the kind of championship that would cement his own place in the Scuderia's history alongside the drivers who came before him.

The ADUO-2 Upgrade

Beneath the tribute livery sits Ferrari's most significant technical introduction of the second half of the season. The ADUO-2 (second-generation Accelerated Development Upgrade Operations) power unit makes its race debut at Monza, bringing an estimated 15 additional horsepower and approximately 0.2 seconds per lap benefit. At the fastest circuit on the calendar — where pure straight-line speed is more decisive than at almost any other venue — those gains could prove decisive.

Critically, Hamilton and Leclerc take the ADUO-2 without incurring grid penalties. This is the first stage of a planned two-phase 30-horsepower upgrade program. Ferrari chose Monza — the circuit most sensitive to power unit output — as the debut venue for maximum performance impact. At the same time, championship leader Kimi Antonelli is starting the race from the back with a fresh Mercedes PU. The combination of Ferrari's ADUO-2 debut and Antonelli's penalty makes this one of the most open Italian Grands Prix in recent memory.

What's Next

Ferrari's fastest practice laps on Friday will be the first real-world test of the ADUO-2's performance advantage. Qualifying on Saturday — with Antonelli starting from the back regardless — gives Hamilton and Leclerc their clearest shot at a front-row start this season. Race day is Sunday, September 6 at 15:00 CEST (9:00 AM ET). The tribute livery comes off the cars after Monza, but whatever happens on Sunday lives in the record books permanently.
