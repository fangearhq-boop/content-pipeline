Cadillac at COTA: America's Team Races at America's Track

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | October 20, 2026

Every Formula 1 team has a home race — the one weekend where the crowd is loudest for them, where the faces in the grandstands are familiar, and where the pressure to perform carries a different weight than every other round on the calendar. For Cadillac, Formula 1's 11th team and its newest American entry, that race is this weekend at Circuit of the Americas in Austin, Texas.

The American Dream in F1

Cadillac joined the Formula 1 grid for the 2026 season as the series' 11th constructor — the first new team to join the grid in more than a decade. With Sergio Perez and Valtteri Bottas as its drivers, the team has been building race-by-race experience through a debut season that was always going to be a development year. Their Miami race earlier in 2026 drew enormous attention: the team ran a stars-and-stripes livery and the debut on American soil felt electric.

COTA, though, is a different level. Austin's crowd comes specifically for Formula 1, with over 100,000 fans per day. They are knowledgeable, passionate, and hungry for an American story to cheer for. Cadillac provides exactly that.

The Perez and Bottas Factor

Sergio Perez brings experience — multiple race wins and years of top-team experience at Red Bull — but 2026 has been a transition year. Valtteri Bottas, a five-time podium finisher with Mercedes in a prior era, has added technical depth to the lineup. Bottas' best finish through the early rounds was No. 13 in China, and Cadillac has yet to score its first championship point.

COTA represents one of the best opportunities in their remaining schedule to change that. The circuit's combination of low-speed corners, high-speed sweeps, and the long start-finish straight rewards both raw pace and strategy — and an experienced driver like Perez knows how to manage a race when the opportunities arise.

What a Point Would Mean

COTA chairman Bobby Epstein said before the season began that Cadillac "won't be last." A championship point in Austin — P10 or better — would validate that prediction and give the team a milestone to build momentum for the remainder of 2026 and into 2027. The crowd would go berserk. The optics of scoring a point on American soil, at the most American grand prix on the calendar, would be significant for the team's commercial and sponsorship trajectory.

What's Next

Cadillac gets their first COTA laps in Thursday's new preview session — a chance to understand the circuit, dial in setup, and get both drivers comfortable on a track where they have no historical data in this car. Free practice continues Friday before qualifying Saturday and the race Sunday at 3:00 PM CT. Watch for Perez in particular: his experience reading races in real time could be the edge that puts Cadillac in the points for the first time.
