Pirelli Brings C2/C3/C4 to Austin: The One-Stop Question

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | October 20, 2026

Pirelli has selected the C2 Hard, C3 Medium, and C4 Soft compounds for the 2026 United States Grand Prix — a combination that sets up a one-stop race on paper but leaves plenty of room for strategy variation in practice. With the championship on the line and Max Verstappen coming in off back-to-back wins, tire strategy at COTA this Sunday could be decisive.

Why One-Stop Is the Base Plan

The C2/C3/C4 selection is a middle-ground choice from Pirelli — hard enough to survive 56 laps under race loads, soft enough to generate the tire temperatures needed on COTA's surface. Teams working to a one-stop plan will likely start on Mediums and switch to Hards at some point between laps 20 and 35, depending on traffic and race position.

The logic for Mercedes — who arrive as the constructors' leaders — is conservative: keep Kimi Antonelli clean, protect the tires, and nurse home the points needed for a championship clinch. For Red Bull and Verstappen, the aggressive play may be to push the Softs early and gamble on a fresher set of Mediums for the run-in.

The Heat Variable

Austin in late October still runs warm — track temperatures can exceed 50°C in full afternoon sun. Higher temperatures mean faster tire wear, which could push some teams off the one-stop plan and into a two-stop regardless of their preferred strategy. Pirelli notes that tire wear is the primary concern at COTA, not pure pace degradation.

The resurfacing of Turns 9–11 ahead of 2026 introduces an additional variable. New asphalt in those corners offers improved grip but different thermal properties than the older surface elsewhere on the circuit — and teams will use Thursday and Friday practice sessions specifically to understand how the tires behave across the full lap.

Rain and the 2026 Aero Factor

Weather forecasts show a 25% chance of rain on Sunday, which would transform the strategic picture entirely. Under wet or mixed conditions, the Intermediate and Wet compounds replace the dry selection, and the timing of the switch becomes the most important strategic call of the race.

The 2026 regulations — which removed DRS and introduced active aero with an Overtake Mode button for defensive and offensive maneuvers — add another layer. Teams managing Overtake Mode usage alongside tire life requires a careful balance, particularly for a team in Antonelli's position: you cannot afford to burn out the tires defending position with aggressive aero mode early in a stint.

What's Next

Friday's free practice sessions will be the key data-gathering exercise for tire behavior, especially at the resurfaced Turns 9–11. Teams will run multiple tire compounds in FP1 and FP2 to build their models for Sunday. Watch the long-run pace data from Friday evening — it will show which teams can commit to a one-stop and which ones may be forced to stop twice. Race day is Sunday, October 25 at 3:00 PM CT.
