If Hamilton wants to stay ahead — and finishes the season on the podium for only the second time in his career with a different constructor — he needs results like Singapore (P6 from the back of the grid) to turn into something better.

Ferrari has context working in its favor. The SF-26 has shown the fastest development rate of any car on the grid in 2026, according to McLaren's own technical director. The team brought its biggest single upgrade package to Miami. At COTA — a circuit with high energy loads and large elevation changes — Ferrari on form could challenge for the front row.

For Ferrari, the Constructors' prize is the real target. They sit 134 points behind Mercedes with six races left. A podium from both cars every weekend from here to Abu Dhabi is not out of the question.

### STORY 5: COTA Circuit Preview

**Long-Form Post** — 5:00 PM ET

If you haven't been to the Circuit of the Americas, these are the things you need to know before the 2026 United States Grand Prix weekend begins October 23.

COTA is a 5.513-kilometer circuit with 20 turns, built from scratch in Austin, Texas, and opened in 2012. It was specifically designed to be the home of Formula 1 in America, and it has earned that billing. The track features dramatic elevation changes — most notably the approach to Turn 1, where drivers crest a hill at full speed before diving into one of the most critical braking zones on the calendar.

Turn 1 is where races are made and lost. It's where Vettel and Hamilton used to battle. Where Verstappen launched his 2025 victory. Where every championship contender will be watching the car behind them in their mirrors on Sunday.

The race is 56 laps. Starts Sunday October 25 at 3 PM local time (8 PM GMT). Practice runs Thursday and Friday. Qualifying Saturday. And this year, the title might be decided right here in Texas, before the Americas swing even leaves Austin.

The contract for this race runs through 2034. Austin isn't going anywhere. Neither is the drama.
