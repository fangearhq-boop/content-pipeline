Kimi Antonelli Replaced Hamilton, Won Seven Races, and Is Eight Days From History

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | October 15, 2026

Kimi Antonelli | Mercedes | F1 2026 Championship | History

The story of the 2026 Formula 1 season was supposed to be about transition. Lewis Hamilton had left Mercedes for Ferrari after 12 years and six titles. The sport was entering a new regulatory era with the 50/50 power split and active aerodynamics. Everything was in flux.

Nobody scripted what actually happened.

Kimi Antonelli — 19 years old, born in 2006, old enough to have grown up watching Hamilton win championships at Mercedes — stepped into that seat and dominated. He won seven races. He led the championship from early in the season. He became the youngest driver ever to lead the Drivers' standings. And eight days from now, at the Circuit of the Americas in Austin, he can become the youngest Formula 1 world champion in the sport's 76-year history.

The Weight of the Seat

When Mercedes announced Antonelli would partner George Russell for 2026, the reaction was a mixture of excitement and skepticism. He was an unproven Formula 2 graduate taking the most scrutinized seat in motorsport. The pressure was enormous — not just to win races, but to justify the faith of a team that had passed over established drivers to give a teenager their chance.

He justified it from the first race. The team did not need to wait for him to find his footing. He arrived knowing how to race at this level, adapted to the new regulations quickly, and built a season that has left everyone — including, he admits, himself — in something approaching disbelief.

The Record Within Reach

The record for youngest Formula 1 world champion belongs to Sebastian Vettel, who was 23 years old when he won his first title in 2010. Before Vettel, it was Fernando Alonso at 24. Hamilton was 23 in 2008. Youngest-ever is a significant threshold.

Antonelli is 19. That gap is not small. It is the difference between a record and a transformational milestone for the sport.

To win in Austin, he needs to win the race and have Russell score two points or fewer. That is the precise condition for clinching the championship with five races remaining — one short of Schumacher's all-time record for earliest clinch.

The Competition

George Russell, his teammate, is No. 2 in the championship at 238 points — 102 behind. Russell has driven an exceptional season himself, winning in Baku and showing consistent pace throughout. He is not gifting the title to Antonelli. But he also cannot close a 102-point gap if Antonelli keeps scoring points at his current rate.

Max Verstappen won the last two races and will be dangerous in Austin. Lewis Hamilton carries Ferrari's Constructors' hopes and needs results. None of them will make Antonelli's path easier.

What's Next

Kimi Antonelli arrives in Austin on October 23 as the Championship leader, the race favourite on a circuit that suits his team's car, and a young man carrying the weight of a potentially historic moment. He "dares to dream." The sport dares with him. Eight more days until we find out if it's enough.
