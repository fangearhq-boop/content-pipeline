Ferrari's 15hp Secret Weapon: The ADUO-2 Engine Upgrade Coming to Monza for Hamilton's Title Push

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | August 15, 2026

Ferrari has confirmed its ADUO-2 power unit upgrade will arrive at Monza, the race immediately following the Dutch Grand Prix. The numbers attached to it are significant — and the timing, with Lewis Hamilton 50 points behind championship leader Kimi Antonelli, is deliberate.

The ADUO-2 delivers approximately 15 additional horsepower through a redesigned turbocharger. At Monza — the Temple of Speed, a circuit defined by long straights where raw engine output matters more than at almost any other venue on the calendar — that translates to an expected performance gain of around 0.2 seconds per lap. Over the course of a race distance, that is a meaningful advantage.

A Two-Phase Power Campaign

The ADUO-2 is the second stage of Ferrari's power unit development plan for 2026. The first upgrade — the ADUO-1 — was deployed at the Austrian Grand Prix earlier in the season, delivering approximately 7 additional horsepower. Combined, the two upgrades represent a gain of roughly 30 horsepower versus Ferrari's specification at the start of the season.

At Zandvoort this weekend, Ferrari will run on the Austrian-spec engine — the floor upgrade they are bringing to the Dutch GP is purely aerodynamic. The ADUO-2 is specifically targeted at Monza, where the performance gain will be most pronounced and the championship implications most direct.

It is worth noting that Ferrari will revert to an older engine specification at Singapore — a circuit where the demands on the power unit differ significantly from Monza's high-speed profile. The ADUO-2 is part of a circuit-by-circuit strategy, not a blanket upgrade across all remaining rounds.

Sources for the ADUO-2 specifications include GPFans, ScuderiaFans, MotorBiscuit, and Formula Live Pulse, all reporting from August 14-15. The Leclerc interview referenced is from Crash.net, also August 14.

Hamilton's Window

Lewis Hamilton sits at 169 points — 50 behind Antonelli, with 291 remaining across 11 rounds. He is in mathematical range of the title. What he needs is circuits and upgrades that favor Ferrari, and moments where the gap closes rather than extends.

Monza, with its power-sensitive layout and Ferrari's ADUO-2, could be one of those moments. A 0.2 seconds-per-lap advantage at a circuit where margins are already razor-thin is not a guarantee of anything, but it changes the competitive calculus in Ferrari's favor at exactly the right time.

Charles Leclerc, who will also benefit from the ADUO-2, described his feelings on the 2026 cars in a fresh August 14 interview as "open-minded" — a measured verdict from a driver who has at times been openly critical of this generation of regulations. Whether open-minded translates to competitive at Monza will become clearer once the upgraded engine is on track.

What's Next

Ferrari arrives at Zandvoort this weekend with a major floor upgrade for the circuit's sweeping banked layout. The ADUO-2 engine remains in reserve until Monza. For Hamilton, the Dutch GP is an opportunity to close ground before the power unit advantage arrives — and Monza, whenever it comes, will be his most favorable test of whether Ferrari's second-half plan can deliver the points the title requires.
