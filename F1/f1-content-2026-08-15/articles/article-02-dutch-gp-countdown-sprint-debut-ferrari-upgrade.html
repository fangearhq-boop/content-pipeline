Six Days to Go: The Final Dutch Grand Prix Is Almost Here — and It's Unlike Any Before It

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | August 15, 2026

Six days from now, the Dutch Grand Prix weekend begins at Circuit Zandvoort. It will be the last one ever held there. And for the first time in the circuit's F1 history, it will feature a Sprint.

The Dutch GP runs August 21-23. The promoter has confirmed they will not continue beyond 2026. Zandvoort — with its banked final corner, its orange-clad crowds, its atmosphere unlike any other on the calendar — hosts F1 for the final time this season. The farewell has been building since the calendar was confirmed. Now it is six days away.

First Sprint Weekend at Zandvoort

The 2026 Dutch GP will be the first-ever Sprint weekend at Circuit Zandvoort. The format changes the structure of the entire weekend in ways the circuit's fans have never experienced.

Friday brings FP1 and Sprint Qualifying. Saturday brings the Sprint race and then GP Qualifying. Sunday is race day, with the main grand prix scheduled for 3:00 PM local time. Every session carries more consequence than a traditional F1 weekend, and for fans who have attended Zandvoort before, the Saturday Sprint will be an entirely new kind of F1 racing to watch live.

Sky Sports confirmed the format and called it the first time the Dutch Grand Prix features a Sprint. F1.com and ESPN have both confirmed the August 21-23 dates and the circuit details.

Ferrari Arrives With Upgrades

Ferrari is bringing their third aero package of the 2026 season to Zandvoort. It is a major floor upgrade, specifically developed for the circuit's sweeping, banked layout. Ferrari is already considered to hold a chassis advantage at Zandvoort — the floor package is expected to build on that edge rather than compensate for any deficit.

The ADUO-2 power unit upgrade, which will add approximately 15 horsepower via a redesigned turbocharger, is being held for Monza. The Zandvoort package is purely aerodynamic, meaning Ferrari will arrive at this circuit with a fresh aero specification on what is already expected to be a favorable chassis platform.

That combination makes Ferrari a serious contender at Zandvoort — and potentially the team to beat for the Sprint as well as the main race.

The Wider Picture

F1's summer shutdown ends Monday, August 17, when factories reopen and the full intensity of preparation for race week begins. For teams up and down the pitlane, the Zandvoort weekend is the opening act of the second half — and the championship standings entering the break make clear how much is at stake.

Kimi Antonelli leads on 219 points, 50 ahead of Lewis Hamilton, with 291 remaining across 11 rounds. Max Verstappen — who has called this his "last home race" and is 110 points behind Antonelli — will be racing in front of his home crowd for what may be the last time at this venue. The Sprint format means more opportunities for drama, more on-track moments, and more ways for the championship picture to shift in a single weekend.

Six days. The final Dutch Grand Prix. And for Zandvoort's faithful orange army, one last chance to see F1 racing at a circuit that has given them some of the most electric weekends in recent memory.

What's Next

Factories reopen Monday and teams return to full operational mode. The Dutch GP weekend begins Friday, August 21 with FP1 and Sprint Qualifying — the first on-track action of the second half of 2026. Watch for Ferrari's floor upgrade performance in Friday practice as an early indicator of the weekend's competitive order.
