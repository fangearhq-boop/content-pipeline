Decision Day — Domenicali's Sept. 15 Deadline Arrives for Qatar and Abu Dhabi

By Ryan Calloway | September 15, 2026 | F1 Fanrecap

The calendar question that has hung over the 2026 Formula 1 season for weeks comes to a head today. F1 CEO Stefano Domenicali set September 15 as the deadline to decide whether the Qatar Grand Prix (November 29) and Abu Dhabi Grand Prix (December 6) will take place as scheduled — or whether the season's end will need to be rerouted.

The uncertainty stems from ongoing security concerns in the Middle East. Bahrain and Saudi Arabia were already removed from the 2026 calendar, with Malaysia's Sepang circuit added in their place for October 2-4. Qatar and Abu Dhabi are the two remaining Middle Eastern rounds, and both have been under scrutiny for months.

What Domenicali Has Said

Domenicali's public position has been measured: Qatar remains confirmed on the calendar, F1 is "monitoring" the situation, and a final decision on both races would come no earlier than mid-September. That mid-September target is today.

He has also stated clearly that if the Middle Eastern races cannot go ahead, F1 will end its 2026 season in Europe. That framing established Imola as the prime contingency option — the circuit has long been discussed as an emergency replacement candidate, and Portimao serves as a secondary backup.

The Championship Implications

The calendar decision carries direct consequences for the title race. If both Qatar and Abu Dhabi are cancelled, the number of remaining points opportunities shrinks significantly, which would accelerate Kimi Antonelli's path to clinching the championship.

Under the current full 23-race calendar, Antonelli — sitting 81 points clear with 9 rounds to go — cannot clinch at Azerbaijan. Singapore is the earliest mathematical possibility, and São Paulo (three races from the end) is the most likely scenario. Remove Qatar and Abu Dhabi, and those windows shift earlier.

For Russell, Hamilton, and Norris, any calendar reduction further diminishes the already slim hope of catching Antonelli.

Europe vs. the Middle East

Imola last hosted Formula 1 in 2024 before losing its calendar slot. The Autodromo Enzo e Dino Ferrari has been kept in readiness as a backup, and race promoters there have been in discussions with FOM throughout the second half of the 2026 season. Portimao, which hosted races during the COVID era, is the second choice if Imola is not available.

The preference across the paddock is clearly for Qatar and Abu Dhabi to proceed as planned — both races are significant financially, and the Abu Dhabi finale at Yas Marina has been the traditional season closer since 2009.

What's Next

An announcement from Domenicali and FOM is expected today or in the days immediately following. If Qatar and Abu Dhabi are confirmed, the final 9 races of the 2026 season proceed as scheduled through December 6. If one or both are cancelled, F1 will confirm replacement venues and revised calendar dates within days. Either way, this has been the most significant off-track question of the past month — and an answer is finally due.
