Williams Are Bringing "An Almost Entirely New Car" to Baku — Everything Riding on This Upgrade

By Ryan Calloway | September 15, 2026 | F1 Fanrecap

James Vowles has been promising a turnaround for months. At the Azerbaijan Grand Prix (September 24-26), Williams intend to deliver it.

The Grove-based team's principal confirmed the squad will bring what he describes as "an almost entirely new car" to Baku — the most significant single-event upgrade package in Formula 1 this season. For Carlos Sainz and Alex Albon, who have spent the opening stretch of 2026 fighting the limitations of a car that never should have been raced as-is, it cannot come soon enough.

Why the FW48 Has Struggled

Williams entered the 2026 season with enormous expectations. The team had spent significant resource preparing for F1's new technical regulations, believing the reset offered a genuine opportunity to climb the order. Instead, the FW48 arrived at pre-season testing both late and overweight — two fundamental problems that proved nearly impossible to recover from once the season started.

The weight issue, in particular, has been persistent. An overweight car cannot be driven at the limit of the tyres in the same way as a car at the weight limit, and the performance compromise has shown. Williams sit eighth in the constructors' championship with just 11 points after 14 rounds.

The Baku Package

Vowles has given no specific technical detail on what constitutes "almost entirely new," but the phrase itself signals the scope of what is coming. The team has been working through a staged upgrade plan since early in the season, with each race bringing components designed to address the foundational issues. Baku is where all of those development threads are expected to converge into a comprehensive redesign of the package Sainz and Albon have been managing.

The Baku City Circuit itself is not a typical test for an upgrade package — its mix of flat-out straights, heavy braking zones, and tight technical sections demands a well-balanced car. But that is precisely the point: the upgrades are designed to address the FW48's balance, not just raw performance numbers.

Sainz and Albon's Season

Both drivers have delivered performances above what the car's standings position suggests. Sainz, a two-time race winner before joining Williams, has remained professionally disciplined through a frustrating campaign. Albon has been similarly methodical, consistent in qualifying and reliable in races where the car has offered anything to work with.

If the Baku upgrade delivers even a fraction of what Vowles has suggested, Williams could jump multiple positions in the constructors standings before the season's end.

What's Next

The first signs of whether the upgrade works will emerge in free practice in Baku on September 24 and 25, with qualifying on Friday September 25 and the race on Saturday September 26. Williams' Baku Friday practice pace relative to the midfield will be the immediate measure of whether the new package delivers the "huge step" that has been promised. If it works, Williams' second half of 2026 could look very different from their first. If it does not, the team will need to regroup around a different strategy for the remaining seven rounds after Baku.
