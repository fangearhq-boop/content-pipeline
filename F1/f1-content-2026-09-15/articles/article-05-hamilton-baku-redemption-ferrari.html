Title Conceded, Baku Next — Hamilton Needs a Response and the Circuit Could Deliver One

By Marcus Cole | September 15, 2026 | F1 Fanrecap

Lewis Hamilton has never won at Baku. He has entered the Azerbaijan Grand Prix in every year the race has appeared on the Formula 1 calendar, come close on several occasions, and left without a victory. The Baku City Circuit is one of the most glaring blanks on a trophy cabinet that covers almost every other major circuit in the world.

This year, he has added motivation. After his brake failure on Lap 7 of the Spanish Grand Prix in Madrid — and his subsequent concession of the 2026 title fight — the Azerbaijan Grand Prix (September 24-26) represents Hamilton's best opportunity to write a more compelling chapter in what has been a disappointing personal campaign with Ferrari.

The Context Going Into Baku

Hamilton sits third in the drivers' championship with 191 points — 101 points behind Antonelli, with 233 available in the remaining nine races. The mathematics of a title fight no longer apply. What remains is the question of how he, and Ferrari, choose to spend the final third of the 2026 season.

His Ferrari career, which began with the promise of challenging for a first title with the Scuderia, has been interrupted by a series of misfortunes and strategic miscues that have kept both car and driver from realising their potential in 2026. The Madring brake failure was perhaps the most dramatic of those moments — a mechanical DNF when Hamilton appeared to have the pace for at least a podium finish.

Why Baku Suits Ferrari

The Azerbaijan street circuit runs for 6.003 kilometres through the streets of the Azerbaijani capital, with a main straight along the Caspian Sea waterfront where cars routinely exceed 340 kph. Power unit performance matters greatly on that straight, and Ferrari's current power unit has been one of the strongest in the field.

Hamilton has historically been competitive in qualifying at Baku even when race results have eluded him. Ferrari's ability to harness that straight-line advantage, combined with lessons learned about strategic decision-making after Madring, could produce a different outcome in late September.

The Brake Investigation

Ferrari's engineers are working through the analysis of Hamilton's brake system failure at Madring. What caused the pedal to lose pressure on Lap 7, whether the issue was isolated to that specific car on that specific day or reflects a broader system concern, and what changes are being made ahead of Baku — these are the technical questions the team is focused on in the days between races.

The reliability and safety of the brake system must be fully understood before Hamilton returns to the car in free practice at Baku. A repeat failure would raise questions well beyond one weekend's result.

What's Next

Hamilton and Ferrari head to Baku with clear priorities: understand and fix the brake issue, deliver strong results in both qualifying and the race, and narrow the gap to Russell in the race for second in the drivers' championship. A win would be the most powerful statement possible. Free practice in Baku begins September 24, with qualifying September 25 and the race on Saturday September 26. Watch Hamilton's pace from FP1 — his immediate lap times will signal whether the Ferrari reset for Baku has been completed successfully.
