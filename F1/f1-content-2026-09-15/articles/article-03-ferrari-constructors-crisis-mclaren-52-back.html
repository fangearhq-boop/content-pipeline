Ferrari's Constructors' Fight Is in Trouble — McLaren Are Now Just 52 Points Back

By Elena Voss | September 15, 2026 | F1 Fanrecap

The 2026 Formula 1 Constructors' Championship has a second-place crisis, and it belongs to Ferrari. Following the Spanish Grand Prix in Madrid, McLaren have closed to within 52 points of the Scuderia — a gap that has been shrinking with each passing round and shows no signs of stabilizing on its own.

The standings after Round 14 of 23: Mercedes lead with 503 points. Ferrari sit second with 358. McLaren are third with 306. The 52-point gap between Ferrari and McLaren looks like a comfortable cushion on paper. In context, it is anything but.

Madring Disaster: 12 Points for Ferrari, 15 for McLaren

The Spanish Grand Prix in Madrid crystallized the problem. Ferrari's race fell apart twice over: Lewis Hamilton suffered a brake failure on Lap 7 and retired, while Charles Leclerc — having qualified strongly — missed the optimal VSC pit window when Antonelli's team made the correct call and Leclerc's team kept him out, costing him likely second place and leaving him to finish fourth.

The result: 12 constructors' points for Ferrari (12 from Leclerc's P4). McLaren, with Lando Norris finishing third, scored 15. One race, three points swung, and the Ferrari-McLaren gap dropped further.

The Trajectory Is Concerning

Ferrari's 52-point advantage over McLaren must be read alongside the 403 constructors' points still available in the remaining nine rounds. That means McLaren need only to outscore Ferrari by an average of about six points per race to overtake them. At their recent form, that is entirely achievable.

The gap between Ferrari and McLaren has halved multiple times since mid-season. Ferrari's strategic miscues — the VSC miss at Madring being the most recent — have been chronic in 2026, and McLaren's development trajectory has been steadily upward.

What Baku Means for Ferrari

Azerbaijan is not a circuit where Ferrari has consistently excelled, but the Baku City Circuit's long main straight provides opportunities for a strong power unit to compensate. Ferrari also has added motivation: their brake investigation from Hamilton's Madring failure needs to deliver answers and fixes before the race weekend.

Charles Leclerc, who scored all 12 of Ferrari's Madring points, will be the team's primary weapon in Baku. A strong result from Hamilton would be a bonus — and a necessary statement from a driver looking to respond after conceding the drivers' title.

What's Next

Baku (September 24-26) is Ferrari's most immediate priority. A strong double points finish — both cars in the top five — would stabilize the constructors gap with McLaren and restore confidence after recent strategic failures. If McLaren outscore Ferrari again at the Azerbaijan GP, the gap drops below 40 points with seven races remaining, and Ferrari's hold on second place becomes genuinely tenuous. Watch both cars, both teams, and the gap closely in the Caspian streets.
