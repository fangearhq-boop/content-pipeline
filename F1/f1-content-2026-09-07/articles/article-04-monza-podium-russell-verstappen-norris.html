Italian GP Podium: Russell P2, Verstappen P3, Hamilton P6 — The Full Monza Story Beyond the Winner

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | September 7, 2026

George Russell started from the front row and led much of the race before being passed by Antonelli on fresh tyres. Verstappen charged to P3. Norris fourth, Piastri fifth, Hamilton sixth. Gasly fell from pole to P7.

The Italian Grand Prix at Monza gave the world its headline — Kimi Antonelli's miracle from 19th — but behind the winner, a full day's worth of racing unfolded that is worth examining in its own right.

George Russell started from second on the grid after Pierre Gasly's pole position on Saturday, and the British Mercedes driver led for much of the race before the tyre strategy that ultimately cost him the win. Verstappen charged from further back to claim third. And Lewis Hamilton collected sixth for Ferrari in difficult circumstances.

Russell: Unlucky or Outmanoeuvred?

Russell crossed the line in second place, 3.857 seconds behind Antonelli — a small margin that understates how decisive the final stint was. Russell had led the race and appeared in control when the Virtual Safety Car period changed the strategic picture. His team elected to keep him out on old hard tyres, expecting Antonelli's fresher mediums to struggle to close the significant gap in the laps that remained.

It did not play out that way. Russell's worn rubber degraded faster than projected, his lap times began slipping, and Antonelli's pace on the fresh compound was relentless. The gap closed, the pass was made, and the result that had appeared likely for Russell dissolved in the final metres.

Second place extends a difficult personal narrative for Russell at Monza — fast, but not quite there. The championship gap to Antonelli has now grown to 66 points, which adds urgency to the team's need for a response in Spain.

Verstappen: Maximum From the Circumstances

Max Verstappen's third place — 14.718 seconds behind the winner — is the kind of result that reflects both the Dutchman's ability and the limitations of the Red Bull package at a circuit where top speed matters enormously. Verstappen was never in contention for the win but squeezed every last point from a weekend that could have yielded less, and his championship tally continues to grow even as the title gap to the Mercedes pair widens.

Red Bull's deficit to Mercedes this season has been a recurring theme. Verstappen's brilliance has partially masked the gap. But at Monza, where aerodynamic efficiency translates directly into lap time, the distance between the cars was perhaps the clearest of the season.

McLaren and Hamilton

Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri finished fourth and fifth respectively for McLaren, a solid double points score that continues the team's push in the Constructors' standings. Monza is not McLaren's most natural territory — their car's characteristics suit circuits with more varied corner types — but both drivers extracted the maximum from their package.

Lewis Hamilton's sixth place for Ferrari rounded out the top six. It was a workmanlike result on an afternoon when his teammate's early crash dominated the attention. Hamilton has been searching all season for the Italian Grand Prix win that has eluded him throughout his career — this result, at Ferrari, on a day when the team were already broken by Leclerc's accident, was not the chapter he would have written.

Gasly's Rise and Fall

Pierre Gasly experienced the full range of Formula 1 emotion across the Monza weekend. His maiden career pole position on Saturday was a landmark achievement — career-best and one of the sport's feel-good moments. The race, unfortunately, unravelled from the front. Gasly fell to seventh by the finish, the gap between starting pole and winning a race demonstrating once again how different Saturday and Sunday demands can be.

Eighth, ninth, and tenth went to Arvid Lindblad (Racing Bulls), Franco Colapinto (Alpine), and Yuki Tsunoda (Racing Bulls) respectively, providing scattered points through the midfield on a day dominated by the events at the front.

Barcelona's demands are considerably different to Monza, and teams will be working overnight to understand the setup changes required.
