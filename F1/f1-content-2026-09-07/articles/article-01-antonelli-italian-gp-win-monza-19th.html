Kimi Antonelli Wins Italian Grand Prix From 19th — First Italian to Triumph at Monza Since 1966

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | September 7, 2026

Starting last-ish due to an engine penalty, Antonelli carved through the field at his home race to beat Mercedes teammate Russell in one of the great Italian GP drives. He leads the championship by 66 points.

Sixty years. That is how long Italian fans waited for one of their own to stand on the top step of the Monza podium at the Italian Grand Prix. The wait ended on a Sunday afternoon in September 2026, when a 20-year-old from Bologna climbed from 19th on the grid to first place in one of the most memorable drives the sport has seen in years.

Kimi Antonelli won the 2026 Italian Grand Prix.

The Mercedes driver — carrying an engine penalty that had relegated him to the back of the grid for his home race — produced an extraordinary performance at the Autodromo Nazionale Monza, picking off rivals one by one before hunting down his teammate George Russell in the closing laps and delivering a moment the Tifosi will talk about for a very long time.

The Race

The afternoon at Monza was eventful from the opening metres. A violent crash for Charles Leclerc of Ferrari on only the second lap — heavy contact with the Parabolica barriers while fighting McLaren's Oscar Piastri — brought out the red flag and halted the race for approximately 30 minutes. When racing resumed, the complexion of the afternoon was reshaped.

Antonelli, who had been working his way through traffic with relentless precision, was positioned to take maximum advantage. The key moment came when a Virtual Safety Car period appeared mid-race. Antonelli's team made the call: pit for fresh medium tyres. Russell, leading the race, gambled on staying out on his older hard compound, expecting his pace advantage to hold through to the end.

It was the decisive strategic moment of the afternoon. Antonelli emerged with new rubber and superior pace; Russell's worn hards degraded in the final stint and his lap times began to slide. Antonelli closed the gap rapidly and took the lead at the start of Lap 18 — a clean, clinical pass on his teammate at one of the world's most iconic venues.

He did not look back. Antonelli crossed the line 3.857 seconds ahead of Russell to claim his seventh win of the 2026 Formula 1 season. Max Verstappen charged through to take third place, 14.718 seconds off the leader. Lando Norris was fourth, Oscar Piastri fifth, and Lewis Hamilton sixth for Ferrari — a modest return from the team on their most emotional weekend of the year.

History Made

The statistics frame the magnitude of what Antonelli achieved. Starting from 19th position, according to Formula 1's own records, is the second-lowest starting position from which a Grand Prix has ever been won in the sport's history. That Antonelli did it at his home race, in front of Italian fans who had not seen a compatriot win this race since Ludovico Scarfiotti's victory in 1966, gives the result a weight that transcends mere sporting achievement.

Scarfiotti won in the dawn era of motorsport. Antonelli won in the most competitive field Formula 1 has ever assembled. Both results carry a permanence in the Italian Grand Prix record books that nothing can erase.

After crossing the line, Antonelli's radio message captured the emotion: "Could never have imagined this moment," he told his team, according to Formula 1's own post-race coverage. The Tifosi, those Ferrari-first devotees in the grandstands, had found themselves — perhaps to their own surprise — cheering a silver Mercedes to victory. When the history is this compelling, the colours matter less.

Championship Implications

Antonelli arrives at the title picture with even greater authority after Monza. He now leads the Drivers' Championship by 66 points over Russell, with 11 rounds and 283 available points remaining in the season. The margin is significant but not insurmountable — a handful of strong Russell results combined with an Antonelli DNF or two could rapidly tighten things.

But the psychological edge belongs completely to Antonelli. He started a race at a disadvantage and turned it into an all-time performance. Teams do not easily recover from a result like that in the championship conversation.

Mercedes also leads the Constructors' Championship, on course for a dominant season across both titles.

What's Next

The paddock moves immediately to the Circuit de Barcelona-Catalunya for the Spanish Grand Prix, which runs September 11-13. Round 14 of 22 will take place on a circuit that rewards high downforce and tyre management — very different demands to the low-drag cathedral of Monza. Antonelli will carry 66 points of lead and the momentum of one of the season's most dramatic victories into the Spanish weekend.
