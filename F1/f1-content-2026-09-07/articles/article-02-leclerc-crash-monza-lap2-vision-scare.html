Charles Leclerc's Monza Nightmare: Lap 2 Crash, Red Flag, and a Vision Scare at Ferrari's Home Race

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | September 7, 2026

The Ferrari driver lost the rear of his car at the Parabolica on Lap 2, hit the barriers hard, and triggered a 30-minute red flag. He walked away but later revealed he suffered vision problems following the impact.

It was over almost before it began.

Charles Leclerc's 2026 Italian Grand Prix ended violently on the second lap, when the Monegasque Ferrari driver lost the rear of his car at the exit of the Parabolica and was thrown heavily into the tyre barriers at one of Formula 1's most unforgiving corners. The red flag flew immediately, halting a race that had barely started and sending a wave of collective alarm through the packed Monza grandstands.

Leclerc walked to the medical car under his own power — a relief given the ferocity of the impact. But the story took a more concerning turn hours later when Leclerc revealed he had experienced vision problems following the crash, and that he would undergo further medical checks as a precaution.

What Happened

The sequence of events was fast and brutal. Leclerc was battling with McLaren's Oscar Piastri in the opening phase of the race when the incident occurred at the Parabolica, the long, sweeping final corner at Monza that leads onto the main straight. As Leclerc held off Piastri's challenge, the rear of his Ferrari stepped out — contact with the green-painted run-off area on the exit of the corner is believed to have triggered the snap.

The car rotated immediately, destroying the front end as it buried itself into the tyre barrier on the outside. The impact was significant. Safety teams arrived quickly, and after evaluation, Leclerc was able to walk to the medical car — an important early sign that he had not suffered immediately obvious serious injury.

The red flag was shown, and the race was neutralised for approximately 30 minutes while the barriers were inspected and repaired and the circuit was declared safe for a restart. Leclerc was classified as a DNF, officially completing only two racing laps.

The Vision Scare

The post-race update was the moment the sporting conversation shifted to something more serious. Leclerc confirmed that he experienced vision problems in the aftermath of the crash, and Ferrari announced he would undergo further medical checks. The nature and severity of those vision issues — and the results of any scans or examinations — had not been publicly confirmed as of post-race reporting.

Vision disturbances following a high-speed impact are a recognised concern in motorsport and can indicate anything from minor concussive symptoms to more significant head or eye trauma. The medical teams' caution in pursuing further checks is the appropriate response, and the wider F1 community was supportive across social media following the news.

Formula 1 has made significant strides in driver safety in recent years, with the HALO device — which Leclerc's car carried, as all modern F1 cars do — credited with saving lives in multiple documented incidents. Whether it played a role in the Monza crash outcome is not yet confirmed, but Leclerc's ability to walk away provides some comfort in the aftermath.

Ferrari's Difficult Afternoon

The crash was the latest difficult chapter in what has been a complicated 2026 season for Ferrari. The Scuderia has not matched the pace of Mercedes or, at times, McLaren, and the Italian Grand Prix — always a uniquely emotionally weighted weekend for the team — provided no relief. Lewis Hamilton, Ferrari's other driver, finished sixth in the race — a points finish but not the result the team or their passionate support hoped for from a home weekend.

The Tifosi in the grandstands endured the indignity of watching their champion team lose Leclerc in the opening moments, then watching a Mercedes take the historic victory that should have carried so much weight for Italy's most loved motorsport institution.

The next race weekend brings Spain. Leclerc's availability for Barcelona — scheduled September 11-13, just days away — will depend on the outcome of his medical checks.

What's Next

The Formula 1 community will be watching Charles Leclerc's medical status closely heading into the Spanish Grand Prix weekend. Ferrari is expected to provide an update on his condition before Barcelona practice on September 11. Leclerc's ability to race — and the results of his vision checks — will be the most watched story in the sport heading into Round 14.
