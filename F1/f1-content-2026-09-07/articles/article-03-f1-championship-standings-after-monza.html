2026 F1 Championship After Monza: Antonelli 66 Points Clear — Title Momentum All His

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | September 7, 2026

A victory from 19th at his home race extended Antonelli's lead over teammate Russell. With 11 rounds and 283 points still to play for, the title race is mathematically open — but the momentum belongs entirely to the 20-year-old Italian.

The 2026 Formula 1 Drivers' Championship is not officially over. But after the Italian Grand Prix at Monza, the view from outside the battle looks increasingly one-sided.

Kimi Antonelli's stunning comeback victory — from 19th on the grid to the top step of the podium at his home race — extended his championship lead over Mercedes teammate George Russell to 66 points. With 11 rounds remaining in the 22-race season and 283 points still available, the maths technically allow for a title swing. But mathematics and momentum are different things, and the momentum belongs entirely to Antonelli.

The Championship Picture

The 2026 standings entering Spain look like this: Antonelli first with a substantial lead, Russell second 66 points behind, and Max Verstappen and the McLaren pair of Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri trailing further behind in an order that reflects the season's dominant narrative — Mercedes has been the class of the field, and within Mercedes, Antonelli has been the superior performer.

For the season as a whole, Antonelli has seven victories, six pole positions, and has been on the podium more often than not. He is 20 years old, and he is producing one of the most impressive debut championship seasons in modern Formula 1 history. The Italian GP was his most dramatic and emotionally resonant win, but it was not his best display of raw pace — that has come on other weekends where the car simply dominated from the front.

Russell is a formidable opponent. The British driver has been consistently fast, a regular pole contender, and a fierce internal competitor. He will not give the title away. But for Russell to win the championship from here, he needs a combination of multiple Antonelli non-scores and his own maximum points weekends — a scenario that requires things to go wrong for the championship leader in a way that has not materialised since the season began.

Constructors' Dominance

Mercedes' lead in the Constructors' Championship is equally commanding. The Silver Arrows have had both cars scoring consistently, with Antonelli's wins bolstered by Russell's podiums and points finishes throughout the year. Red Bull and McLaren have provided the strongest opposition, but neither has matched the consistency of the Mercedes package across the season's 13 completed rounds.

The Constructors' title, barring a catastrophic reversal, appears headed back to the Brackley factory. The question for the remainder of 2026 is whether the Drivers' title goes to the same address — and if so, to which driver's name on the trophy.

What Could Still Change

Formula 1's remaining calendar includes circuits that have historically been less kind to Mercedes than their current package suggests: the street circuits of Azerbaijan, Singapore, and Las Vegas introduce different risk profiles, and the variable weather conditions in Brazil have historically produced unexpected outcomes.

Verstappen, despite trailing significantly in the standings, remains one of the sport's most dangerous racers when conditions tilt in his favour. McLaren's Norris and Piastri are not mathematically eliminated. But at 66 points with 11 rounds to play, Antonelli owns the championship. His rivals can close — but closing and overtaking are very different distances to travel.

What's Next

Round 14 of 22: the Spanish Grand Prix at Circuit de Barcelona-Catalunya, September 11-13. Barcelona is a medium-speed circuit that rewards mechanical grip and tyre management — different demands to Monza, and a venue where Mercedes has traditionally been strong.
