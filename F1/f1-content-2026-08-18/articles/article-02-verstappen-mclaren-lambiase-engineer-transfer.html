Verstappen's Race Engineer Lambiase Signs with McLaren — Transfer Speculation Intensifies

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | August 18, 2026

Gianpiero Lambiase guided Max Verstappen through four Formula 1 World Championship campaigns at Red Bull. He was the voice in Verstappen's ear through the title-winning battles that defined the early half of this decade. And now, Lambiase is switching to McLaren.

Both Red Bull and McLaren confirmed the move this week. Lambiase joins McLaren ahead of the 2028 season — a timeline that, on its surface, appears to involve no overlap with any plausible Verstappen contract window. But in a paddock attuned to every signal, the move has been read as exactly what it looks like: organizational alignment between a driver and a team that have not yet publicly acknowledged they are heading toward each other.

What Zak Brown Is Saying

McLaren CEO Zak Brown has not wavered in his public messaging. Talks with Verstappen "didn't go anywhere." McLaren is "very happy" with Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri — the reigning Constructors' Championship-winning pairing — and has no intention of breaking up what it built.

The problem for that narrative is the mounting volume of contradictory reporting from inside the paddock. PlanetF1 has reported that Verstappen is in "advanced talks" with McLaren. GPFans sources — writing on August 16 — indicated that Verstappen's relationship with Red Bull is "broken," that Red Bull made Verstappen a fresh contract offer and received no response, and that his exit clause is currently active through October.

The Exit Clause and What It Means

Verstappen's Red Bull contract included performance-based exit clauses. Reports indicate the clause was triggered when Verstappen went into the summer break outside the top two in the championship standings — a reflection of the SF-26 Red Bull's relative struggles this season compared to Mercedes and Ferrari. With the clause now active, the window from August through October represents the period during which a departure could be negotiated.

The Dutch Grand Prix — Verstappen's home race, happening in three days — is the most naturally dramatic setting imaginable for an announcement of this magnitude. Three consecutive victories at Zandvoort. A sellout orange crowd. A farewell race at a circuit being taken off the calendar. If Verstappen were to announce a future move to McLaren this weekend, the timing would write itself.

The Implications for the Grid

Verstappen's destination remains the single most consequential decision in the 2027 driver market. GPFans reported that 14 of 20 seats remain unconfirmed for 2027. Where Verstappen goes shapes everything downstream — which team gains one of the sport's greatest drivers, which seat opens up behind him, and what the cascade of moves through the midfield looks like as the ripple effects spread.

For McLaren specifically, the Lambiase signing creates a structural question: the team insists on Norris and Piastri for 2027, but has brought in the race engineer most closely associated with Verstappen's success. Those two positions are difficult to hold simultaneously if the intent is truly to maintain the current driver lineup unchanged.

What's Next

The Dutch GP weekend begins Friday, August 21. Whether Verstappen uses the occasion to make an announcement, or whether the silence continues through Zandvoort and into Monza, the Lambiase confirmation has already moved the story forward. Watch the paddock very carefully this weekend. And if Verstappen gives any interview that diverges from his usual deflection on the topic, that will be the moment the whole transfer saga breaks open.
