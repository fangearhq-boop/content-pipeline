Ferrari's Two-Phase Upgrade Plan: New Floor at Zandvoort, ADUO-2 Engine at Monza

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | August 18, 2026

Ferrari arrives at the Dutch Grand Prix with the most targeted upgrade plan of its 2026 season — and it unfolds across two circuits, two weekends, and two very different technical domains. If both phases deliver as expected, the Scuderia believes it can transform its championship prospects in the space of three weeks.

Phase one starts Friday at Zandvoort.

The Zandvoort Floor Upgrade

Ferrari has fast-tracked a new SF-26 floor for the Dutch Grand Prix after engineers identified floor correlation issues during a data review following the Austrian and British GPs. The new component is described as a direct evolution of the Barcelona floor introduced in round 7 of the championship, with the primary objective of generating increased downforce through the floor's venturi channels.

Charles Leclerc publicly teased the Zandvoort upgrade ahead of the summer break, signaling Ferrari's confidence in the package. Team principal Frederic Vasseur has confirmed the two-phase plan publicly, framing Zandvoort as the aerodynamic intervention and Monza as the power unit intervention.

For Hamilton and Leclerc, the upgrade arrives at a circuit where Ferrari has no 2026-spec baseline data — meaning the team will need Friday's sole practice session to validate whether the floor's simulated performance translates to the track. If the correlation is accurate, Ferrari could emerge Saturday already carrying a tangibly faster machine.

The Monza Engine — ADUO-2

Ferrari's second upgrade allocation under the FIA's Additional Development and Upgrade Opportunities (ADUO) system has been deliberately held back for Monza, the Italian Grand Prix on September 4-6. The ADUO-2 represents Ferrari's second engine upgrade of the 2026 season and is estimated to deliver approximately 15 additional horsepower and 0.2 seconds per lap.

Monza is the ideal circuit for an engine upgrade: the Temple of Speed rewards straight-line velocity more than any other venue on the calendar. A 15-horsepower gain at a circuit where the cars spend roughly 70% of the lap at full throttle is worth meaningfully more in lap time than it would be at a more balanced track.

The combined effect of both upgrades — aerodynamic from Zandvoort, power from Monza — is the knockout weapon Ferrari intends to have ready for Singapore and the final run of the season.

The Title Stakes

Lewis Hamilton sits 50 points behind Antonelli in the championship. Leclerc is 81 back. Both need the upgrade plan to work, and work quickly. The window from Zandvoort through Singapore represents approximately six of the 12 remaining rounds — the period in which Ferrari believes it can build back the deficit to a closeable gap heading into the season's final third.

If the floor performs as designed at Zandvoort and the ADUO-2 delivers at Monza, Vasseur's sequential plan becomes the most credible technical narrative for a Ferrari championship charge. If either phase falls short, the mathematics of the remaining rounds become considerably more challenging.

What's Next

Zandvoort is the proof of concept. Friday's Free Practice session (12:30 CEST, August 21) will be every Ferrari engineer's first chance to see real data from the new floor at this circuit under 2026 regulations. Sprint Qualifying on Friday afternoon will deliver the first comparative performance snapshot against Mercedes and McLaren. By Saturday's Sprint, the paddock will have a clear early read on whether Ferrari's upgrade deserves the attention it has received — or whether the team needs to continue its development push into Monza with more urgency.
