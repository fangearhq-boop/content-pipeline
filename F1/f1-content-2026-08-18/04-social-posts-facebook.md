### STORY 4: Ferrari's Zandvoort Upgrade and Monza Engine Plan

#### Long-Form Post — 2:00 PM ET

Ferrari arrives at Zandvoort with the most significant upgrade package the Scuderia has produced in months — and it's only half of a carefully sequenced two-race plan.

The Zandvoort package centers on a new SF-26 floor, fast-tracked after Ferrari engineers identified floor correlation issues by comparing data from the Austrian and British GPs. The component is a direct evolution of the Barcelona floor introduced in round 7 and is designed to generate meaningfully more downforce. Charles Leclerc has already teased the upgrades publicly. If the correlation hits the mark, both Leclerc and Lewis Hamilton should have a faster car for the first time since the summer break began.

But Ferrari's ambition doesn't stop there. Monza — the Italian Grand Prix on September 4-6 — brings the ADUO-2 engine upgrade that Ferrari has been holding in reserve. The second ADUO (Additional Development and Upgrade Opportunity) allocation delivers an estimated 15 additional horsepower and 0.2 seconds per lap — a substantial gain at a circuit where straight-line speed and engine power are the primary differentiators.

Team principal Frederic Vasseur has mapped the campaign clearly: Zandvoort for aerodynamics, Monza for power, Singapore for the combined effect. If both phases deliver, Ferrari's SF-26 could become a genuinely different machine in the second half of the championship — exactly when it needs to be.

Hamilton sits 50 points behind Antonelli. Leclerc is 81 back. The window is closing. The upgrades are the key.
