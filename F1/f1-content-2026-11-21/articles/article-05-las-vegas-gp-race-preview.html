Las Vegas GP Race Preview: Verstappen From Pole, Ferrari and Piastri Must Charge

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | November 21, 2026

The 2026 Las Vegas Grand Prix fires tonight under the neon lights of the Nevada Strip, and the grid that has been set by Friday's qualifying drama could not be better designed for an entertaining race. Max Verstappen leads from pole. Kimi Antonelli — the fastest man in qualifying — starts P6 with a grid penalty. Both Ferraris are stuck in the mid-field. Oscar Piastri starts deep. And the P2 championship fight between George Russell and Lewis Hamilton is running live through every lap.

Verstappen's Pole Advantage

Pole position on the Las Vegas Strip Circuit is a meaningful advantage — the ability to run in clean air through the first corner sequence and control the pace on the opening laps without the wheel-to-wheel pressure that comes from starting behind. Verstappen is a master of converting pole positions into race wins, and his 2026 car has demonstrated enough speed to hold the front through most of the season.

The challenge tonight is that George Russell starts immediately alongside him, and further back in the field, Kimi Antonelli — who was categorically faster in qualifying — will be looking to make rapid progress from P6. If the champion can close the gap in the first stint, the race becomes a Red Bull vs Mercedes duel with the fastest car potentially at the front by mid-distance.

The Circuit and Strategy

The Las Vegas Strip Circuit is defined by three long straights — the kind of circuit that rewards outright power and allows DRS overtaking in a way that many Formula 1 venues do not. Cold track temperatures in November Las Vegas mean tire warm-up is a critical factor in the opening laps, particularly for drivers starting from cooler positions on the grid who will not benefit from the heat generated by racing ahead of them.

Strategy calls will likely hinge on the balance between tire management and track position. With 50 laps to run, teams have flexibility on pit stop timing, and a well-executed undercut or overcut on the main straight can recover significant positions. For Ferrari and Piastri, strategy is their primary tool for recovering from their compromised grid slots.

The Championship Tension

Underlying every development in tonight's race is the championship fight between Russell (second, starts P2) and Hamilton (fourth, starts P12). The one-point gap between them means a single position change in race finishing order can swing the standings. If Russell takes a podium and Hamilton fights to seventh, Russell gains another buffer. If Hamilton charges to third and Russell encounters trouble, the picture changes entirely.

These kinds of parallel storylines — a race within a race — are what Formula 1 at its best delivers. Tonight, there's the race at the front with Verstappen and Antonelli, the mid-field charge for Ferrari, and the championship duel woven through it all.

What's Next

When the lights go out tonight, the Las Vegas Grand Prix will begin writing one of the final chapters of the 2026 Formula 1 season. The result will reshape the P2 championship fight and set the stage for the finale in Qatar and Abu Dhabi. After 20 dramatic races, the season is coming down to three nights. This is the first. Everything still to play for.
