Verstappen Inherits Las Vegas Pole After Antonelli's Engine Penalty

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | November 21, 2026

Kimi Antonelli was the fastest driver in Las Vegas on Friday night. His 1:33.893 on the Strip Circuit was 0.258 seconds quicker than Max Verstappen's best and would have put the 20-year-old world champion on pole position for his home race as the dominant figure of the 2026 Formula 1 season. Instead, a five-place grid penalty for using a sixth internal combustion engine — one beyond the season allocation — drops Antonelli to sixth on the starting grid.

Verstappen, whose 1:34.151 put him second in the timesheets, inherits pole and will lead the Las Vegas Grand Prix away when the lights go out Saturday night.

The Penalty That Changes Everything

Engine component penalties are a feature of Formula 1's cost-control regulations, designed to limit the number of power unit elements teams can use across a season without financial and sporting consequences. When a driver exceeds their allocation, the penalty is automatic — a set number of grid places regardless of qualifying pace.

For Antonelli, whose Mercedes machinery has been the class of the field for most of 2026, reaching a sixth ICE at this stage of the season reflects the intensity with which Mercedes have pushed their power unit. The champion who clinched the title in Mexico City on November 1 — the youngest world champion in Formula 1 history at 20 years old — will now have to work his way forward from P6.

His pace suggests he has the tools to do it. A driver who is 0.258 seconds faster than the nearest legitimate threat around the Strip Circuit's combination of chicanes and straights starts the race with a clear speed advantage. The question is whether he can translate it into a race win while navigating traffic and tire management.

Verstappen's Grid Gift — and What He Does With It

For Verstappen, inheriting pole is a fortuitous outcome after a Friday night where his Red Bull was clearly not the fastest machine. His 1:34.151 represented a solid qualifying effort — good enough to hold second in the timesheet without the penalty factor — but Verstappen knows better than anyone that starting at the front does not guarantee a Las Vegas win.

George Russell starts alongside him in second (1:34.224), with Lando Norris third and Isack Hadjar fourth in the second Red Bull. The front four are compact in time, and the race will likely come down to strategy calls and tire management on a circuit where the cold Las Vegas November asphalt makes compound choices particularly consequential.

What's Next

Tonight's Las Vegas Grand Prix is set up as one of the most compelling races of the 2026 season. Antonelli charging from P6, Verstappen leading from pole, and the Russell-Hamilton championship battle running through the entire field make for a grid where almost every result is possible. The Strip Circuit's three DRS-enabled straights provide overtaking opportunities, but clean air at the front remains the most valuable asset in Las Vegas. Verstappen has it. The champion does not. That contrast alone makes this race worth watching to the final lap.
