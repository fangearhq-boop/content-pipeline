Ferrari's Las Vegas Nightmare: Leclerc and Hamilton Both Eliminated in Q2

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | November 21, 2026

It was not the Las Vegas weekend Ferrari needed. On Friday night, both Charles Leclerc and Lewis Hamilton were eliminated in Q2, leaving the Scuderia locked out of the final qualifying session and facing a daunting pair of grid slots for Saturday's race: Leclerc 11th, Hamilton 12th. For a team that arrived at the penultimate triple-header chasing championship positions, the double Q2 exit is a serious blow.

Leclerc's Championship Hopes Take a Hit

Heading into Las Vegas, Charles Leclerc sat fifth in the drivers' championship with 249 points — 13 behind George Russell in third, 12 behind Lewis Hamilton in fourth. The P3 fight remains alive mathematically, but only just. With 75 points available across the final three races, Leclerc needed strong points hauls in Las Vegas, Qatar, and Abu Dhabi to have any realistic chance of overhauling Russell.

Starting 11th in a Las Vegas Grand Prix does not align with that plan. The Strip Circuit's long straights give overtaking opportunities, but working from the mid-field against a front pack featuring Verstappen, Russell, Norris, and world champion Antonelli requires near-perfection in strategy and execution. A points finish is achievable. A dominant result that closes the gap on Russell is a significantly harder ask from P11.

Hamilton's Title Fight Complicated

For Lewis Hamilton, the Q2 exit creates a different kind of pressure. He trails George Russell by exactly one point in the battle for second in the championship — 261 to 262 — and starts tonight's race four grid slots behind his rival. The gap is so small that a single point swing in either direction changes the narrative entirely.

Starting 12th in his first Las Vegas Grand Prix in Ferrari red is not the script Hamilton would have written. His Mercedes machinery delivered multiple victories on this circuit in previous seasons. Friday night, the red car underperformed at the worst possible moment for his championship ambitions.

What Hamilton does have in his favor is experience of racing through the field, racecraft that has never been in question, and a circuit with enough overtaking potential that a strong strategy could still deliver a podium finish. But the margin for error tonight is essentially zero.

What Went Wrong for Ferrari

The Las Vegas Strip Circuit is technical in a specific way — its combination of tight, slow corners and three long straights punishes cars that lack balance across the full lap. Ferrari arrived in Nevada having struggled in Brazil in the previous race, and Friday's qualifying confirmed that the SF-26's issues have not been resolved quickly enough.

Both drivers found the limits of what their car could deliver in Q2 and ran out of road before Q3. For a team of Ferrari's resources and ambition, that is a performance failure at a circuit where their drivers needed maximum points.

What's Next

Tonight's race is Ferrari's last realistic chance to swing the P3 championship battle for Leclerc before the season's final two rounds in Qatar and Abu Dhabi. For Hamilton, it's a critical moment in his fight to overhaul Russell and finish second in a year that has been defined by consistency rather than dominant wins. Both drivers will need flawless execution, favorable strategy, and some racing luck to make up the ground their qualifying lost them. Ferrari's season may ultimately be remembered by what happens in the next three races — and Las Vegas did not start well.
