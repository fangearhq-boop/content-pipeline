Piastri Takes 10-Place Grid Penalty, McLaren Navigates Split Strategy in Las Vegas

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | November 21, 2026

Oscar Piastri's Las Vegas Grand Prix weekend was complicated before the first lap was run. A 10-place grid penalty for a power unit component change means the McLaren driver starts well back in the field, forcing the Woking team to manage two cars in drastically different strategic positions at a circuit where points hauls are essential for their constructors' standing.

Norris Leads McLaren From the Front

With Piastri pushed back by the grid penalty, Lando Norris becomes McLaren's primary asset at the front of the Las Vegas grid. Norris qualified fourth and starts third on the actual grid after Piastri's penalty and Antonelli's five-place drop are applied. That places him alongside Isack Hadjar on the second row, directly behind championship contenders Verstappen and Russell.

Norris has been one of the more consistent performers across the second half of the 2026 season, with multiple podiums to his name. Starting third on a circuit that rewards outright speed on its three DRS straights gives him a genuine shot at a race win if either Verstappen or Russell encounters trouble in the early laps.

Piastri's Charge-Through Scenario

For Piastri, the race presents a different kind of challenge — one that the Australian has demonstrated he can handle. Starting deep in the field on the Strip Circuit is not ideal, but it is not hopeless either. Las Vegas's three long straights and multiple overtaking zones give a fast car with DRS opportunities to move forward, particularly in the first stint when the race is still open.

McLaren will likely run a different strategic approach for Piastri than for Norris — an offset on tire compounds, a deliberately timed undercut, or a longer first stint to build track position through attrition. The team has the data and the experience to find the best path through a compromised starting position, and Piastri has the racecraft to execute it.

Constructors' Context

McLaren entered Las Vegas fourth in the constructors' championship, trailing Mercedes (who have already clinched the title), Red Bull, and Ferrari. Every point from both cars matters in the battle for position, and a Piastri race starting from deep in the field means the burden falls more heavily on Norris to deliver a strong result.

Grid penalties are an unfortunate but unavoidable consequence of Formula 1's power unit allocation rules across a full season. Teams like McLaren manage the trade-offs carefully, timing component changes at circuits where recovery is most feasible. Las Vegas, with its overtaking potential, is a reasonable place to take the hit.

What's Next

Tonight's race will reveal how well McLaren's split strategy plays out in practice. If Norris can stay with the leading group and Piastri can charge into points-paying positions, the team may emerge from Las Vegas having minimized the damage from the grid penalty. The constructors' fight in the final two rounds after Las Vegas will depend on how efficiently both McLaren drivers perform on this peculiar night under the neon lights of the Strip.
