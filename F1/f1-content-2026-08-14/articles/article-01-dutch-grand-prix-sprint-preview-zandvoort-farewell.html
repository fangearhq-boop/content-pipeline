Dutch Grand Prix Preview: Zandvoort's Final Race Features First-Ever F1 Sprint — Championship Drama Awaits

By Elena Voss | August 14, 2026

Circuit Zandvoort hosts its farewell Dutch Grand Prix in seven days, and it does so with a Sprint Weekend for the first time in its history. The championship stakes couldn't be higher as the second half of the season begins.

The Dutch Grand Prix has always delivered something special, and its final edition at Circuit Zandvoort looks set to be the most action-packed yet. Round 12 of 22 on the 2026 Formula 1 calendar takes place Aug. 21-23, and it marks two historic firsts: the last ever Dutch Grand Prix at Zandvoort and the first Formula 1 Sprint weekend the circuit has ever staged. For a six-year chapter that began in 2021 and produced some of the most passionate atmospheres in modern F1, the sendoff was written with maximum drama in mind.

The Sprint Format: A Packed Weekend in Zandvoort

The weekend's format squeezes four on-track sessions into three days. Sprint Qualifying runs Friday, followed by the Sprint Race on Saturday morning — the first competitive sprint event the Dutch venue has ever hosted. GP Qualifying then takes place Saturday afternoon, and the 72-lap grand prix closes the weekend on Sunday at 15:00 CET.

The Sprint format adds a layer of strategy and consequence that a standard race weekend doesn't provide. Points are available on Saturday, which means the championship picture could look different before the main event even starts. Drivers who need to make ground — Hamilton, Norris, Russell — have an extra session to do it. The leader, Antonelli, must defend on two fronts.

Farewell to an Iconic Circuit

Circuit Zandvoort's return to the F1 calendar in 2021 was one of the most anticipated events of that season. The circuit — with its sweeping corners through the sand dunes and narrow, technical layout — delivered exactly the kind of racing the sport needed at the time. Over six years, it produced dramatic races, massive crowd energy, and a home atmosphere for Max Verstappen that became one of F1's signature annual events.

The Dutch Grand Prix will continue at a new venue from 2027. The six-year Zandvoort era ends in one week, and the circuit's fans know it. Expect the Orange Army to turn out in full force for a sendoff that will be emotional for everyone inside the fences.

Championship Context

Kimi Antonelli arrives at Zandvoort with a 50-point lead over Lewis Hamilton in the drivers' championship. Mercedes leads Ferrari by 72 points in the constructors'. Lando Norris won the last race — the Hungarian Grand Prix — to give McLaren its first victory of 2026, a reminder that the second half of this title fight is far from settled. George Russell sits third in the championship with 160 points, 59 points behind his Mercedes teammate and still searching for his first win of the season.

For Antonelli, Zandvoort is an opportunity to extend his lead or, at worst, limit the damage from the Sprint scoring opportunities. For Hamilton, it's the first step in a comeback that Ferrari's upcoming engine upgrade will need to power. For Norris and McLaren, fresh off their Hungarian momentum, it's a chance to establish themselves as genuine championship contenders rather than race-by-race threats.

What's Next

Sprint Qualifying at Zandvoort begins Friday, Aug. 21. The Sprint Race — Zandvoort's first — follows Saturday morning, with GP Qualifying that afternoon. The main event runs Sunday. After the Dutch GP, the calendar moves to Italy (Monza) on Sept. 4-6, then to Singapore, Austin, Mexico City, and beyond for the season's final stretch. This week is where the second half truly begins. The farewell to Zandvoort will be emotional — and if the championship standings are anything to go by, it will be unforgettable.
