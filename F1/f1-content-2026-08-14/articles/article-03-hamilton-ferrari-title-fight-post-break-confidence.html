Hamilton Enters F1's Second Half "More Focused Than Ever" — Ferrari's Engine Upgrade Could Change Everything

By Marcus Cole | August 14, 2026

Lewis Hamilton is 50 points behind Kimi Antonelli with 11 races to go, but the seven-time champion believes Ferrari's best is still to come. A pending engine upgrade and renewed confidence define his post-break mindset.

Fifty points is a meaningful gap. Eleven races is also a meaningful stretch of time. Lewis Hamilton knows both of these things, and heading into the second half of the 2026 Formula 1 season, his calculation appears to be: the gap is manageable, the upgrade is coming, and there's no reason not to believe. "I really believe we can fight," Hamilton said ahead of the Dutch Grand Prix weekend. He added that he's "more focused than ever" entering the post-break schedule.

The Season So Far

Hamilton's first season at Ferrari has been a study in determined progress against persistent headwinds. He has five podiums and one race victory — the Barcelona-Catalunya Grand Prix — across 11 starts. His teammate Charles Leclerc has also contributed a win (British Grand Prix). Ferrari's 307 points in the constructors' championship keeps them alive in the title race, but Mercedes' advantage — 72 points with 379 constructors' points — reflects the gap that has made Hamilton's season an uphill fight.

The most consistent frustration has been straight-line speed. Hamilton has routinely noted that Ferrari loses between 0.3 and 0.4 seconds per lap on the straights relative to Mercedes — a deficit that, over a 60-70 lap race, compounds into enormous time lost regardless of how strong the Ferrari is through the corners.

The Engine Upgrade That Could Shift the Fight

Ferrari brought a power unit upgrade to the Austrian Grand Prix earlier in the season. Under the FIA's Additional Development Upgrade Opportunity (ADUO) system — a mechanism introduced with the 2026 regulations to allow teams to bring targeted technical upgrades — Ferrari is eligible for one more power unit upgrade before the season ends. If that upgrade addresses the straight-line deficit meaningfully, it changes the strategic calculus for the final 11 rounds.

Hamilton has been careful to frame the upgrade as a possibility rather than a certainty. Ferrari's engineering department must prioritize where the gain comes from. But the direction of travel — toward addressing the power and drag issues that have hampered Hamilton all season — suggests a substantive package is being developed.

The Challenge Ahead

Kimi Antonelli has been extraordinary this season. Six wins, including five consecutive victories, and a 50-point lead entering the second half at age 19 represents a performance that Hamilton himself would have been proud of at any point in his career. Catching a driver of that quality, in a car that has been systemically faster, requires both Antonelli to falter and Hamilton to find another level.

Hamilton has closed gaps like this before — with Mercedes, in seasons where the championship came down to the final race. The conditions in 2026 are different, but the fundamental skill required is the same: execute perfectly, capitalize on every opportunity, and force errors through relentless pressure. Hamilton's post-break mindset suggests he's prepared for exactly that approach.

What's Next

The Dutch Grand Prix at Zandvoort (Aug. 21-23) is Hamilton's first chance to close the gap in the second half. A Sprint Weekend format means points are available on Saturday, giving Hamilton an additional scoring opportunity before the main race. If Ferrari's upgrade arrives at Monza or Singapore, and Hamilton can string together a run of podiums and wins, the title fight has life. The seven-time champion has earned skepticism — and belief — in equal measure. Watch what he does with 11 races and an engine upgrade.
