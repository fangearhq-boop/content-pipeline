Dutch GP Preview: F1 Says Goodbye to Zandvoort With Its First-Ever Sprint Weekend

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | August 1, 2026

In three weeks, Formula 1 will race at Circuit Zandvoort for the last time. The Dutch Grand Prix on August 21-23 is Round 12 of the 2026 season — and the final chapter of a six-year relationship between F1 and the circuit that sits in the coastal dunes of North Holland.

The race returns the sport from its summer break, resumes the championship battle that left Kimi Antonelli 50 points ahead of Lewis Hamilton after Hungary, and adds a layer of history unique to this specific weekend: Zandvoort's first-ever Sprint format in Formula 1.

The Farewell to an Era

Zandvoort's modern F1 chapter began in 2021 when the Dutch Grand Prix returned to the calendar for the first time in 36 years — driven largely by the phenomenon of Max Verstappen and a Dutch fanbase that had been waiting decades to see their champion race at home. What followed was six years of sold-out grandstands, orange-clad fans, and some of the most atmospherically charged races on the calendar.

The circuit itself is a technical challenge — narrow, high-speed, with banked turns that create visual spectacle for the fans but demand precision from drivers. It was never an easy circuit to overtake on, which made strategy and qualifying position especially important. The crowd made up for any on-track limitations.

This year's race concludes that chapter. Economic factors and circuit investment decisions ended the partnership. Zandvoort's farewell falls in a season where Verstappen's own future is uncertain, adding emotional weight to every lap he turns in front of the Dutch crowd.

First Sprint at Zandvoort

For the first time in the circuit's Formula 1 history, Zandvoort hosts a Sprint weekend format. The schedule: Sprint Qualifying on Friday afternoon, Sprint Race on Saturday morning, GP Qualifying on Saturday afternoon, and the main Dutch Grand Prix — 72 laps, 306.587 kilometers — on Sunday at 15:00 CET (09:00 ET).

The Sprint format introduces an additional race result and championship points into a weekend that already carries enormous story weight. Sprint races have occasionally produced surprising results — incidents, different tire strategies, conditions that diverge from the main race. At Zandvoort's narrow circuit, the compressed format adds further unpredictability.

Championship Stakes

Kimi Antonelli arrives at Zandvoort with 219 points and a 50-point lead over Lewis Hamilton. Russell sits third at 160 points. With 291 points still available across 11 races and 2 Sprints remaining, the title is far from decided — but Antonelli's consistency through the first half has been the defining characteristic of the season so far.

Lando Norris won the Hungarian Grand Prix in what was McLaren's first win of the 2026 season. That result, combined with McLaren's pace over recent rounds, reinforces their status as a championship contender for the constructors — and adds context to the Verstappen speculation that has swirled all summer.

What's Next

After Zandvoort, the calendar moves to Monza, Baku, and Singapore before the crucial second half of the title fight. The Dutch GP's farewell sets the tone for a second half of the season defined by the championship battle, driver market decisions, and lingering calendar uncertainty over the Middle East races in November and December.

August 21 arrives in three weeks. The last F1 race at Zandvoort starts then. It will not be forgotten.
