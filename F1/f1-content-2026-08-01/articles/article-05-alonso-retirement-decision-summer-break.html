Fernando Alonso's Last Call: Retirement Decision Expected After Summer Break as His Car Finally Improves

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | August 1, 2026

Fernando Alonso has made a promise — and Formula 1 is waiting on it. The two-time world champion said before the summer break that he would decide his F1 future once the break was over. That moment is approaching, and the signals coming from his camp and from people close to the situation suggest the announcement is not far away.

The situation is complicated in a way that is very Alonso. The news is not straightforwardly good or bad. It is layered.

The Numbers That Defined His 2026

Through 11 rounds of the 2026 Formula 1 season, Aston Martin had accumulated one championship point. That point belongs to Alonso, from his Monaco result. Cadillac — in their maiden F1 campaign, their first season as a constructor — comfortably outscored them over the same stretch. That is the hard context for any retirement decision from a driver who turned 45 this summer.

Alonso said, ahead of the Hungarian Grand Prix, that "Spa and Silverstone isn't the dream." The quote can be parsed in multiple ways. The most natural reading is that racing at the back of the field, unable to fight for positions that match his talent, does not satisfy the competitive standard he has held for himself across a career that spans more than two decades and 32 championship wins.

Hungary Changed Something

Then Hungary happened. The B-spec AMR26 arrived with 16 aerodynamic and structural modifications, and Alonso qualified into Q2 for the first time all season. For a driver who has spent months extracting everything from a car that could not fight for the midfield, finally reaching that threshold carried genuine emotional and competitive significance.

The Honda power unit upgrade coming at Zandvoort — reported at up to 30 brake horsepower — extends the positive trajectory. More chassis updates are planned for Monza and Baku. The second half of 2026 for Aston Martin looks meaningfully different from the first.

Whether that is enough to change the calculus for a 45-year-old champion who can see the finish line is a question only Alonso can answer.

What the Insiders Say

Sky Sports' Ted Kravitz, one of the best-connected journalists in the paddock, predicted that Alonso will retire at the end of 2026. A Spanish F1 insider reported that Alonso has already made his decision and that it represents "massive news for Aston Martin." The framing of that report — massive news — suggests the decision has been made and that Aston Martin's planning has shifted accordingly.

Alonso's contract at Aston Martin expires at the end of this season. No extension has been announced or reported as close. The timeline for announcing a replacement driver — should Alonso be leaving — would typically require several months of negotiation, and those conversations would already need to be underway for the team to secure a capable replacement before 2027.

What's Next

The summer break ends with the Dutch Grand Prix on August 21-23. Alonso arrives at Zandvoort in a meaningfully faster car than he has driven all season. He will almost certainly make his announcement before then, or simultaneously.

If it is retirement, Fernando Alonso will close a career that includes two world championships, 32 wins, and nearly 400 grands prix starts — spanning decades and three distinct F1 eras. One of the most celebrated careers in the sport's history ends not with a dramatic final race but with a quiet summer decision. That, too, feels very Alonso.
