F1 Calendar Crisis: Qatar and Abu Dhabi Face September Deadline as Middle East Conflict Threatens Season End

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | August 1, 2026

Formula 1 has already cancelled two grands prix in 2026. Two more may follow before the year is done.

The Bahrain Grand Prix and the Saudi Arabian Grand Prix — originally scheduled for March — were pulled from the calendar due to the ongoing conflict involving Iran, the United States, and Israel. The Middle East chapter of the 2026 calendar was written off before most fans had seen a single race lap.

Now the season-ending races in Qatar (November 27-29) and Abu Dhabi (December 4-6) are facing the same uncertainty — and F1 CEO Stefano Domenicali has set a hard deadline to decide their fate.

The September Deadline

Domenicali confirmed that the sport has a rough deadline of mid-September to determine whether Qatar and Abu Dhabi can proceed as scheduled. That deadline is not arbitrary — it reflects the logistical and commercial commitments that racing organizations, broadcasters, and hospitality providers need to honor or cancel well in advance of a November event date.

F1 has confirmed that a contingency plan is in place. If both Gulf races become impossible to stage, the 2026 season will end in Europe. Imola is among the circuits being actively considered as a replacement round. The Italian circuit previously hosted a replacement race in 2020 and has the infrastructure to host a late-season event on relatively short notice.

What Cancellation Would Mean

Losing Qatar and Abu Dhabi would reduce the 2026 season from 22 scheduled rounds to 20 — still among the longest in F1 history, but with significant commercial implications. Abu Dhabi has traditionally hosted the season finale, with its sunset backdrop and the ceremonial conclusion of the championship. Moving that moment to Imola — or another European circuit — would be a jarring change from the norm.

One report has gone further, claiming that both races are already cancelled and that Las Vegas — scheduled in late November — would become the effective season finale. That claim has not been confirmed by multiple major outlets, and F1's official position remains that a decision will come in September.

The championship context adds stakes to the calendar question. If Kimi Antonelli's 50-point lead over Lewis Hamilton holds through the second half of the season, the location of the finale matters less. If the title fight tightens dramatically, the specific circuit and conditions of the final rounds could become critically important.

What's Next

The mid-September deadline falls between the Italian Grand Prix at Monza (September 6-8) and the Azerbaijan Grand Prix in Baku. By that point, the season will have 13 or 14 rounds in the books, and the championship picture will be significantly clearer than it is today.

F1 navigated the March cancellations and delivered a competitive first half of the season across European, North American, and Asian rounds. It can adapt again if the Middle East races fall. But the identity of the 2026 season finale — whether it is Abu Dhabi, Imola, Las Vegas, or somewhere else — remains genuinely unresolved.
