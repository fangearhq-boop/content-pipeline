Aston Martin and Honda Target Dutch GP With Up to 30bhp Upgrade — Second-Half Revival Under Way

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | August 1, 2026

Aston Martin's 2026 season had a start that tested the patience of everyone involved. Through the first 11 rounds, the team had one championship point — Fernando Alonso's Monaco result. Cadillac, in their first-ever Formula 1 season, outperformed them with consistency that made the situation hard to dismiss.

Then came Hungary, and then came a filming day at Zandvoort that changed the team's entire outlook heading into the second half of the season.

The B-Spec Worked in Hungary

Aston Martin introduced a 16-part upgrade package to the AMR26 at the Hungarian Grand Prix — effectively a B-specification car. The modifications covered the sidepods, floor, rear wing, and nose, representing the most comprehensive aerodynamic overhaul of the season in a single package.

The results were immediate. Fernando Alonso qualified for Q2 for the first time all 2026 season. The car showed measurable pace improvement in both qualifying and race trim, and the team came away from Hungary with genuine optimism — a feeling that had been largely absent since the pre-season tests revealed how far behind the new regulations had left them.

Honda's Power Unit Upgrade at Zandvoort

The chassis improvement was step one. Step two is the power unit. Aston Martin and Honda conducted a filming day at Circuit Zandvoort this week to debut Honda's upgraded 2026 power unit ahead of its race introduction at the Dutch Grand Prix on August 21-23.

Honda's upgrade is reported to deliver up to 30 brake horsepower — a significant jump for a team that has been disadvantaged on power-sensitive circuits all season. The updated PU represents Honda's sole 2026 engine development allocation and was designed specifically to close the gap to the manufacturer front-runners.

Further updates to the AMR26 chassis are also planned for Zandvoort, Monza, and Baku. The team's original two-step upgrade plan — B-spec at Hungary, power unit and additional aero at Zandvoort — is now fully on track following the Budapest test.

Alonso's Decision at the Right Moment

The timing of Aston Martin's technical improvement creates a compelling subplot around Fernando Alonso's future. He promised a decision on whether to continue racing after the summer break, with his contract at Aston Martin expiring at year's end.

Multiple sources suggest the decision may already be made. Sky Sports' Ted Kravitz has predicted Alonso will retire. A well-connected Spanish insider reported that Alonso has finalized his plans. Whatever the outcome, the car that Alonso drives into the Zandvoort farewell will be meaningfully faster than the one he drove into Monte Carlo in May.

Whether that matters to a 45-year-old double world champion evaluating the end of his career is a question only Alonso can answer.

What's Next

The Dutch Grand Prix on August 21-23 will be the first genuine test of Aston Martin's upgraded package in race conditions. If the Honda power unit delivers its reported performance, and the additional chassis modifications show further progress, Aston Martin's second half of 2026 could look very different from their first.

Further updates at Monza and Baku extend the development runway into September and October. By Alonso's own timeline, his future will be clear long before those races arrive.
