Verstappen's Red Bull Exit Clause Is Now Active — And October Is the Deadline

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | August 1, 2026

The most significant driver market question in Formula 1 has entered its decisive phase. Max Verstappen's performance-based exit clause from Red Bull Racing is now officially active, and the four-time world champion has until October to decide whether he uses it.

The exit clause threshold required Verstappen to be outside the top two in the Drivers' Championship by the summer break. After 11 rounds including the Hungarian Grand Prix, he sits 7th — well clear of any zone that could have locked him in. His manager Raymond Vermeulen publicly acknowledged the clause is active in July, the first time anyone in the Verstappen camp had confirmed this directly.

Red Bull Tried to Buy It Out. Verstappen Said No.

Red Bull saw this coming. In June, the Milton Keynes-based team approached Verstappen's management about removing the exit clause from his contract that runs through 2028. The offer was rejected. Verstappen's refusal to surrender that contractual leverage spoke louder than any statement his camp could have issued.

That decision, combined with his refusal to commit to Red Bull past 2026 in subsequent media sessions, has fueled a silly season that has dominated F1 headlines throughout the second half of the calendar's first segment.

Where McLaren Stands

McLaren is the team most consistently linked to Verstappen since the British Grand Prix. Rumors of a potential 2027 move intensified through July, despite McLaren CEO Zak Brown repeatedly denying interest in disrupting a driver lineup of Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri.

Brown's most notable comment on the subject came when he acknowledged that Verstappen would be an attractive option "should one of my drivers slip on a banana peel." It was a diplomatic denial that left more questions than it closed.

The Mercedes door, by contrast, appears definitively closed. Toto Wolff activated George Russell's contract option to extend him through 2027. Ferrari is occupied with Lewis Hamilton and Leclerc. That leaves McLaren as the primary speculative destination — and a team that may face internal pressure to secure generational talent regardless of what Brown says publicly.

The Championship Context

Verstappen's 7th-place standing reflects a difficult 2026 season for Red Bull. The new technical regulations cycle has not suited their car in the way the previous formula did, and Kimi Antonelli's dominant debut season with Mercedes — 219 points after 11 rounds, 50 points clear of Hamilton in second — has underlined just how far Red Bull has been from the front in 2026.

Norris won the Hungarian Grand Prix, suggesting McLaren's pace is genuine. For Verstappen, watching a potential new employer perform at the front while his own team struggles is precisely the kind of information that shapes a trigger decision.

What's Next

October is the window. When it opens, Verstappen will either exercise the clause and enter negotiations for a 2027 move or remain silent — which would de facto commit him to completing his Red Bull contract. Whatever the outcome, it will be one of the most consequential driver market decisions in a generation.

The Dutch Grand Prix on August 21-23 will be his first race back from the summer break, and his first race in front of the home crowd that has loved him since 2021. The timing could not be more emotionally charged.
