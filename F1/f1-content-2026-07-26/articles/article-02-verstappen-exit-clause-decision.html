Max Verstappen's Exit Clause Has Been Triggered — The Summer of Decision Begins

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | July 26, 2026

For years, it was a hypothetical — a contractual safety valve that everyone in the Formula 1 paddock knew existed but assumed would never be needed. Max Verstappen's performance-based exit clause at Red Bull Racing, tied to his championship standing at the summer break, has now officially been activated.

The clause's trigger condition: if Verstappen is not in the top two of the Drivers' Championship after the Hungarian Grand Prix — the final race before the August break — the clause becomes active. As of the pre-race standings, Verstappen sat seventh in the championship with 91 points, 128 behind leader Kimi Antonelli. The gap is not bridgeable. The summer break cutoff has arrived. The clause has been triggered.

What this means in practice is that Verstappen now has the legal right to negotiate an exit from his Red Bull contract before it expires, and — if both parties agree — to join another team. The clause was designed as a protection mechanism for a four-time World Champion who had no intention of spending his peak years on an uncompetitive car. This season, Red Bull's RB26 has not matched the pace of Mercedes or Ferrari. Verstappen has won races on skill alone but cannot manufacture championship points from a car that isn't there.

McLaren Talks: Where Things Stand

Multiple sources have confirmed that Verstappen's management team and McLaren Racing have worked out "broad terms" for a potential 2027 partnership. This is not the same as a signed contract — no deal has been formally executed, and Red Bull Racing maintains Verstappen is under contract and plans to race for them through its expiration. But the existence of an agreed framework suggests that at least one serious option for Verstappen's future is concrete.

A Norris-Verstappen McLaren lineup would represent an extraordinary pairing: two of the most talented drivers of their generation, both in their peak years, sharing one of the fastest cars on the grid. Lando Norris is under contract with McLaren through 2027 and beyond. Oscar Piastri extended through 2028. If Verstappen joins, one of those arrangements must change — and the arithmetic of the McLaren driver lineup becomes its own storyline.

There have been mixed signals from Verstappen's camp. His manager Raymond Vermeulen said earlier in the summer that Verstappen "wants to finish his career at Red Bull." Racing journalist Tom Clarkson has suggested Verstappen will stay for 2027. Yet the fact that broad terms have been worked out with McLaren at the same time suggests the situation is more fluid than public statements indicate.

The Cascade Effect

What happens next affects every team with a driver contract expiring after 2026. Seventeen drivers are out of contract for 2027 — an unusually large number that makes the 2026-27 silly season one of the most complex in recent memory. Verstappen's decision, whichever way it falls, determines what happens to multiple seats simultaneously.

If Verstappen joins McLaren, a top Red Bull seat opens. Red Bull will want the best available replacement for a four-time champion, which means pursuing one of the other free-agent drivers on the market. That in turn affects the teams those drivers currently occupy. The domino chain runs through the entire paddock.

If Verstappen stays at Red Bull, McLaren's driver plans for 2027 pivot entirely. Their focus on Verstappen would end, and their attention turns to consolidating Norris and Piastri — who would themselves have alternatives to explore.

What's Next

The summer break gives Verstappen and all parties six weeks of relative quiet to finalize decisions. The expectation across the paddock is that a resolution — one way or another — will be announced before Formula 1 resumes racing. The sport's attention during August will be directed squarely at Max Verstappen's next move. For the first time in his Red Bull career, the four-time champion's path forward is genuinely uncertain. The clause was triggered. The decision is coming.
