### STORY 5: FIA Energy Management Changes Coming

#### Long-Form Post — 8:30 PM ET

The 2026 Formula 1 regulations were supposed to reset the competitive order and create closer racing. For the most part, they have. But they also created a problem that no one fully anticipated: a safety concern on straights that the FIA has been watching all season — and is now moving to address.

The issue stems from the 2026 power unit design. With a 50/50 split between internal combustion and hybrid power, and approximately 350kW of Electric Recovery System output, the energy management of these cars is far more complex than previous generations. When the ERS is depleted — during intensive deployment phases of a lap — the car's performance drops dramatically. On a straight, this creates large speed differentials between cars in different phases of their energy cycles.

Drivers have described moments of being effectively "sitting ducks" as opponents fly past on straights because their energy timing differs by a fraction. It's not just competitive — the FIA has flagged it as a safety concern. Closing-speed differentials at 200+ mph, even brief ones, create risk.

The FIA is using the summer break to finalize proposed tweaks to the Overtake Mode (formerly Manual Override Mode) regulations and potentially adjust the energy harvest algorithms that govern how quickly ERS replenishes. The goal is to reduce the amplitude of the speed differentials without fundamentally changing the character of the 2026 formula.

Changes are expected to be announced before September.
