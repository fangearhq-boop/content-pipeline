Verstappen Mathematically Out of 2026 F1 Title Race — Red Bull's Season Now Defined by Constructors Battle

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | October 27, 2026

After finishing third at COTA, the four-time champion has run out of road. 129 points down with 125 remaining, the title belongs to someone else this year.

Max Verstappen has been here before — on the wrong side of a championship fight. The difference is that in previous years, it never lasted long before he turned the tables. In 2026, there is no turning back.

Following Sunday's US Grand Prix at the Circuit of the Americas, where Verstappen finished third after a two-stop strategic gamble that did not deliver, the four-time world champion is now mathematically eliminated from the 2026 Formula 1 drivers' championship. With 125 points available across the remaining five races and a deficit of 129 points to Kimi Antonelli in the standings, the numbers are irreversible.

Verstappen's title defence — if it could even be called that in a season where he rarely threatened the front — is over. The focus now shifts entirely to a constructors' battle that may represent Red Bull's most meaningful opportunity to salvage something from a year that has exposed the limits of their new-era machinery.

A Season That Slipped Away

The COTA weekend was a microcosm of Red Bull's 2026 campaign. Verstappen tried to seize control through strategy, pitting on lap 14 for soft tyres in a two-stop gamble while rivals ran longer on their compounds. It did not work. He emerged from the pit stop unable to close the gap to the front-running Mercedes of Antonelli, who controlled the pace from pole to flag, and fell further behind Leclerc in the standings as well.

The pattern has repeated throughout the year. Red Bull's car, while fast enough to challenge on certain circuits, lacks the baseline performance to compete with the dominant Mercedes package across a full season. Verstappen has driven brilliantly in patches — extracting more from the car than anyone else on the grid could — but there is a ceiling to what even the best driver can do without the equipment beneath them.

Red Bull extended Verstappen's contract through 2030 earlier in the season, a statement of long-term commitment from both parties. Verstappen has confirmed he intends to see it through. The expectation on both sides is that the technical cycle will eventually favour Red Bull again. In 2026, that cycle has not arrived.

The Constructors' Fight Is Real

With the drivers' title settled in all but formality, the secondary championship has become Red Bull's most important target. Both Red Bull and McLaren sit on approximately 355 points in the constructors' standings — a tie that makes every remaining race genuinely consequential for both teams.

That fight is not academic. The constructors' championship carries prestige, prize money, and — critically — a reflection on the technical direction of the entire team. For Red Bull, finishing second in the constructors' would be a meaningful achievement in a year where their car fell short of Mercedes. Finishing third or lower would compound the disappointment considerably.

McLaren, for their part, bring Lando Norris and a car that has shown genuine pace throughout the season. The battle between these two teams over the next five races could be one of the most competitive storylines remaining in the 2026 season.

What's Next

Verstappen heads to Mexico City for the Grand Prix weekend of October 30 to November 1 looking to score maximum points in the constructors' fight. The Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez is a circuit where strategy plays an enormous role given the altitude challenges — and Red Bull will need to execute cleanly if they are to maintain parity with McLaren. For Verstappen personally, the task is simpler than it has been in years: drive fast, score points, and help his team finish the season with dignity.
