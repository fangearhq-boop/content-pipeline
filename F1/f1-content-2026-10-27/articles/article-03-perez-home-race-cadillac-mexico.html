Pérez Comes Home: Checo Races in Mexico City for the First Time With Cadillac

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | October 27, 2026

The most beloved F1 driver in Mexico is back at his home race, now carrying the Cadillac colours — and the expectations of an entire nation.

When Sergio Pérez drives through the gates of the Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez on Thursday morning, the welcome he receives will be unlike anything available to any other driver on the Formula 1 grid. He is not merely competing at home. He is competing as Mexico's racing hero, in the race that every one of his fans has circled on their calendar since the schedule was announced.

The difference in 2026 is the car. Pérez is no longer in the Red Bull that he drove to back-to-back runner-up finishes and numerous race victories. This weekend, he represents Cadillac — the American manufacturer that joined Formula 1 as a new constructor for the 2026 season. It is a new chapter, and the Mexico City Grand Prix is the most meaningful race on the calendar to write it in.

A New Team, an Unchanged Reception

Pérez's popularity in Mexico transcends team allegiances. He is the driver who brought Formula 1 home to a country that had been waiting decades for a race winner they could call their own. The Mexican fans who pack the Autodromo in elaborate Día de Muertos costumes — skull face paint, elaborate headdresses, the most colourful racing crowd in the world — cheer for Checo first, everything else second.

That will not change on Sunday regardless of what car he is in. Cadillac is a new team, still building its performance package and finding its feet in the sport. Expectations for race results are calibrated accordingly. But expectations for noise, emotion, and atmosphere? Those are as high as the mountains that ring Mexico City.

The Día de Muertos backdrop adds another dimension. The festival falls on the same weekend as the Grand Prix, turning the entire city into a celebration — and turning the race itself into something more than a sporting event. For Mexican fans, the two traditions overlap in a way that makes the weekend feel uniquely theirs.

What Cadillac Needs From Checo

From a performance perspective, Pérez's job this weekend is clear: extract the maximum from the Cadillac machinery and deliver a result that rewards the team's investment in their first season. A points finish would be a milestone. A strong qualifying lap would demonstrate the team's development pace. Whatever the result, the experience of competing in front of 140,000 fans who are cheering specifically for your driver is one that no amount of wind tunnel testing can replicate.

Pérez has won at Baku, Monaco, and multiple other street circuits — he knows how to read a race, manage tyres, and execute under pressure. On a circuit he has raced at many times before, with a home crowd behind him, those instincts will not suddenly disappear just because the car's livery has changed.

What's Next

The Mexico City Grand Prix weekend begins Thursday with the media day, free practice runs Friday and Saturday, and qualifying Saturday afternoon ahead of Sunday's race.
