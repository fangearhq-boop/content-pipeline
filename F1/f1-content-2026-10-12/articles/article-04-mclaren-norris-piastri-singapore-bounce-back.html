McLaren's Singapore Redemption: Norris P4, Piastri P5 After Sprint Disaster

By Ryan Calloway

Twenty-four hours after both McLaren cars were eliminated on the opening lap of the Singapore Sprint, Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri finished fourth and fifth in Sunday's Singapore Grand Prix. Together, they delivered 22 constructors' points for McLaren on a weekend that could have spiralled into something far worse.

The Sprint That Shouldn't Have Happened

Saturday's sprint collision between Norris and Piastri was ruled a racing incident. No penalties were issued against either driver. The contact happened as the pair fought for position in the early laps — an aggressive moment between two teammates who both understand that points are available and neither is willing to gift them to the other.

What happened next says everything about McLaren's team culture. Both drivers reset. No public blame. No fractured atmosphere in the garage. When Sunday came, they drove the grand prix as if Saturday had not happened.

22 Points and What They Mean

Norris's fourth place earned 12 constructors' points. Piastri's fifth earned 10. The combined 22-point haul does not close the gap to championship-leading Mercedes in the Constructors' standings significantly — that gap remains large — but it represents McLaren maintaining pressure on the teams directly behind them.

More important than the arithmetic is the demonstration of pace. Singapore is not a circuit where power units dominate or where aerodynamic efficiency is everything. It is a circuit that punishes setup errors and rewards driver skill and mechanical reliability. McLaren brought a competitive car, both drivers extracted what was available, and the result reflected it.

What the Americas Swing Means for McLaren

Formula 1 now moves to the Americas — Austin on October 23–25, Mexico City on October 30 – November 1, and São Paulo following. Historically, Circuit of the Americas and Mexico City are circuits that have suited McLaren's package. The high-speed sections at COTA reward aerodynamic efficiency, and McLaren has demonstrated that capability across 2026.

The Constructors' Championship may be mathematically difficult from here. But for a team that crashed both cars on Saturday and delivered a double top-five on Sunday, the momentum heading into the Americas swing is real. McLaren is not done yet.
