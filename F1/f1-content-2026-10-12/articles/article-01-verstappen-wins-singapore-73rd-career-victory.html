Verstappen Finally Wins in Singapore — 73rd Career Victory at Marina Bay

By Ryan Calloway

Max Verstappen has won in Singapore. After years of frustration at Marina Bay Street Circuit, the Red Bull driver finally conquered the most punishing street race on the Formula 1 calendar, leading from pole position to the chequered flag in his 73rd career grand prix victory.

Charles Leclerc crossed the line second, 7.3 seconds behind, with championship leader Kimi Antonelli completing the podium in third at +24.5 seconds. Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri finished fourth and fifth for McLaren. Lewis Hamilton was sixth for Ferrari.

A Circuit That Had Always Said No

Singapore had been the notable gap in Verstappen's record. The 62-lap street circuit — narrow, unforgiving, lit by floodlights through the night — demands a different kind of car and a different kind of race management than the open circuits where Verstappen has made his name. Before Sunday, every attempt at Marina Bay had ended in frustration.

This time was different from the very first session. Verstappen was fastest in qualifying, fastest in Saturday's sprint race, and fastest again when the grand prix lights went out on Sunday evening. Leclerc challenged at the start, but Verstappen had the pace to answer whenever he was pressed. By the midpoint of the race, the result was not in serious doubt.

The Perfect Weekend

The Singapore result was not just a win — it was a maximum-points weekend. Verstappen took sprint pole, won the sprint race on Saturday, took grand prix pole, and then won the main event. No other driver took more points from Singapore in 2026.

It is the kind of complete performance that defines a driver's résumé. Win No. 73 comes with added weight: it is the win that filled the last meaningful blank on Verstappen's record. Singapore, finally, is his.

McLaren's Recovery and the Rest

McLaren's double top-five — Norris fourth, Piastri fifth — came one day after both drivers crashed out of the sprint on lap one following a collision between the two cars. The sprint incident was ruled a racing incident with no penalties. Their ability to reset and deliver 22 constructors' points in the grand prix was a statement about the team's resilience.

Hamilton's P6 came with regret. Ferrari described his Sunday as a difficult strategic afternoon, and Hamilton himself acknowledged the outcome was not what the team had hoped.

George Russell, starting from 20th after an engine component penalty, produced one of his finest drives of the 2026 season, recovering to ninth. At Marina Bay — where passing is considered near-impossible — eleven positions gained without safety car assistance was a remarkable individual effort. It earned him six points. It did not change the championship picture.

What Comes Next

The F1 season now moves to the Americas. The United States Grand Prix at Circuit of the Americas in Austin, Texas runs from October 23–25. Verstappen will arrive in Texas with momentum, a perfect Singapore weekend behind him, and the knowledge that Marina Bay — after all this time — is no longer a circuit where he has unfinished business.

He has 73 wins. And he's not done.
