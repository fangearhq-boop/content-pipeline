Antonelli's Championship Lead Grows to 93 Points — Austin Clinch Watch Begins Now

By Elena Voss

Kimi Antonelli did not need to win in Singapore. He drove cleanly, avoided trouble, took third place, banked 15 points, and let the championship do the work. After Sunday's race at Marina Bay, the 19-year-old Mercedes driver leads the 2026 Formula 1 Drivers' Championship by approximately 93 points over his teammate George Russell with five races remaining.

The United States Grand Prix at Circuit of the Americas in Austin, Texas — October 23–25 — is the earliest possible venue for Antonelli to be crowned Formula 1 World Champion.

The Math

Before the Singapore race, Antonelli led Russell by approximately 84–89 points. Russell's P9 earned him six points. Antonelli's P3 earned him fifteen. The gap grew.

Five races remain: Austin, Mexico City, São Paulo, and two further rounds. The maximum points available are approximately 125–130, factoring in sprint races and fastest lap bonuses. For Russell to overhaul Antonelli, he would need near-perfection in every remaining round — wins, sprint wins, fastest laps — while Antonelli scored almost nothing. That is not a realistic scenario.

The clinch math for Austin: if Antonelli outscores Russell by a sufficient margin at Circuit of the Americas, history gets made in Texas. Exactly what margin is required depends on sprint results and fastest lap points, but the direction is clear.

Champion's Thinking at 19

What is most striking about Antonelli's championship campaign is not the number of wins — though there have been several — but the consistency. A driver who only turned 19 this year has managed his campaign with the composure of someone years more experienced. Singapore illustrated it precisely: the race was won by Verstappen, who had the fastest car. Antonelli's job on Sunday was to finish on the podium and not lose ground. He did exactly that.

That is how championships are won. Not by fighting for wins on circuits where the other car is quicker. By calculating the minimum acceptable outcome and delivering it.

What Russell Needs

Russell's season has been interrupted by bad luck at critical moments — engine component penalties, sprint incidents, events that cost him the ground he needed to stay in contention. He is still an exceptional driver. P9 from 20th at Singapore, without safety car assistance, proved that on Sunday.

But the gap is 93 points. Five races remain. The math does not require detailed analysis to read. Russell needs miracles; Antonelli needs to stay out of trouble.

Eleven Days

F1 travels to Austin on October 23. If Antonelli performs at Circuit of the Americas the way he has performed all season, the champagne goes out. At 19 years old, Kimi Antonelli would become the youngest Formula 1 world champion in the history of the sport.

Eleven days. The countdown has started.
