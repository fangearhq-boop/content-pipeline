Ferrari's Second-Half Plan Is Now Mapped: New Chassis at Zandvoort, ADUO-2 Engine at Monza

By Ryan Calloway | August 16, 2026

The Scuderia has a three-race upgrade sequence that defines their championship push: aero package at the Dutch GP, power unit at Monza, both combined at Singapore. The ADUO-2 will add 15 horsepower and an estimated 0.2 seconds per lap on Italy's straights. Vasseur is honest: full power parity with Mercedes isn't coming in 2026.

Ferrari's second-half strategy has been an open question since the summer break began. The team finished the first half of the season 72 points behind Mercedes in the Constructors' Championship, with Lewis Hamilton sitting 50 points behind Kimi Antonelli. The gap is real. The plan to close it is now fully in view.

The sequence is deliberate. Zandvoort: aero. Monza: power. Singapore: both.

Phase One — Zandvoort

At the Dutch GP this weekend, Hamilton receives an all-new chassis. Ferrari engineers identified specific flexure characteristics in the current chassis that were limiting performance — particularly in high-speed cornering — and the replacement is intended to address those deficiencies directly.

Alongside the new chassis, Ferrari is bringing what sources describe as a significant floor evolution to Zandvoort. The package was fast-tracked after engineers spent the summer break comparing Austrian and British GP data and identified a clear area of improvement in the floor's interaction with the 2026 cars' active aerodynamics. This is the most aggressive single aero package Ferrari has brought at any point this season.

If both the chassis and floor perform as modeled, Hamilton's qualifying pace deficit relative to Antonelli — currently measured in tenths per lap — should narrow at Zandvoort. The Zandvoort circuit, with its high-speed banking and demanding aerodynamic requirements, is a reasonable test environment for the new floor's performance.

Phase Two — Monza

The ADUO-2 is not coming to Zandvoort. Ferrari is saving it for Monza, where straight-line speed matters most.

The ADUO-2 is a complete redesign of the turbocharger. Same impeller diameter, but revised blade count and blade angle, using advanced materials throughout. The performance gain: approximately 15 horsepower over the current ADUO-1 specification. Ferrari's own simulations project this translates to approximately 0.2 seconds per lap at Monza's specific layout. On a circuit defined by 1.4-mile straights, that is a meaningful swing.

Ferrari team principal Fred Vasseur has been direct: the ADUO-2 will help, but it will not close the full power gap to Mercedes in 2026. That honesty matters. The ADUO-2 is not a magic equalizer. It's a step toward closing the gap — and closing the gap, on a circuit like Monza, creates legitimate chances for a victory.

Phase Three — Singapore

Singapore is where Ferrari expects to field their most complete package of the year: the aero upgrades from Zandvoort plus the ADUO-2 engine operating together. The Singapore street circuit rewards a combination of mechanical grip and low-drag efficiency — different from Monza's raw speed demands, but another context where a power step can compound with aerodynamic advantages.

If all three phases deliver as expected, Ferrari arrives at Singapore as the most significantly upgraded team since the summer break. Whether that translates to championship gains depends on how Antonelli and Mercedes respond — and Antonelli's 50-point lead means Ferrari needs a run of victories, not just competitive performances, to genuinely threaten the title.

What's Next

The new chassis and floor go onto Hamilton's car at Zandvoort this week. Engineers will assess their real-world performance against the simulation data in practice — one practice, given the sprint format. If the aero upgrades work as projected, the ADUO-2 at Monza becomes the most anticipated Ferrari development of the season. Three races. Three upgrade phases. Ferrari's championship push starts this weekend.
