Leclerc Grid Penalty Confirmed: Ferrari's New Engine Strategy for Baku

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | September 23, 2026

Ferrari has confirmed its strategy for the 2026 Azerbaijan Grand Prix: Charles Leclerc will use his existing engine through FP1 on Thursday, then switch to a brand-new spec power unit for the remainder of the weekend — triggering a grid penalty that will send him to the back of the grid regardless of where he qualifies on Friday.

It is a decision Ferrari had been telegraphing for weeks, and one team principal Frédéric Vasseur called calculated rather than unfortunate. The new engine is reportedly worth approximately 0.2 seconds per lap over the ADUO1 unit. At Baku, with its 2.2 kilometre main straight and the braking zone into Turn 1, Ferrari concluded this was the right place to absorb the hit.

The Plan in Detail

Leclerc's current engine was damaged in the Monza accident. Ferrari engineers assessed it and determined it was usable but not at full performance potential. The strategy is straightforward: run the compromised unit on Thursday morning to gather initial setup data in a session where grid position does not matter, then bolt on the new engine for FP2 onward.

From that point, the penalty is locked in. No matter how well Leclerc qualifies on Friday — and he remains one of the fastest qualifiers in the field at any circuit — he will line up at the back of the grid when Saturday's race begins.

The new spec unit's performance gain matters beyond this weekend. Ferrari are committed to using it through the remainder of the season, and the 0.2 second per lap advantage compounds across the remaining nine rounds. For a team fighting to preserve third place in the constructors' championship against McLaren's challenge, those tenths matter.

Why Baku Is the Right Circuit for This

The Azerbaijan Grand Prix is, by general consensus, the most overtaking-friendly circuit on the Formula 1 calendar. The 2.2 kilometre main straight accelerates cars to approximately 340 kilometres per hour before the heavy braking zone at Turn 1. Cars that are faster in a straight line can find themselves in DRS range of the car ahead from the other end of the circuit, giving drivers multiple opportunities to attack on every lap.

For a driver starting from the back with a faster engine, those characteristics are exactly what is needed. Vasseur's comment weeks ago that Baku was "quite suitable" for a grid drop was not idle confidence — it was a genuine assessment of where a recovery drive was most achievable.

The question is how high Leclerc can climb. The top teams will be running nose-to-tail at the front. Getting from the back into the points, potentially even the top six, is a realistic ambition. Getting to the podium would require significant attrition ahead of him. In Baku — where safety cars and retirements are more common than at most circuits — that is not impossible.

What's Next

FP1 begins Thursday September 24, with Leclerc using his old engine. FP2 sees the switch to the new spec power unit. Qualifying runs Friday September 25, and the race takes place Saturday September 26 — an unusual Saturday schedule moved from the traditional Sunday to avoid Azerbaijan's national day of mourning on September 27. Watch Leclerc's lap times from FP2 onward for the first data on how much the new engine has improved Ferrari's straight-line speed. The recovery drive begins Saturday morning.
