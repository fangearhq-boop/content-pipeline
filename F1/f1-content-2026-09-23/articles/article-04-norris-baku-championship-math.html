Norris Needs Baku: 106 Points Back, Running Out of Races

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | September 23, 2026

Lando Norris arrives in Baku as the defending Formula 1 world champion, ranked fourth in the 2026 standings, 106 points behind Kimi Antonelli. There are 233 points still available across nine remaining rounds. The gap is closeable in theory. In practice, it requires a sustained collapse from the most composed driver on the grid — and sustained dominance from Norris that has not consistently arrived this season.

Baku is his best opportunity in weeks. McLaren came to Azerbaijan with upgrades targeting the second sector. Norris entered the weekend as one of the race favourites. He needs to translate that into the result the championship requires.

The Standings Picture

Kimi Antonelli (Mercedes): 292 points. George Russell (Mercedes): 211 points. Lewis Hamilton (Ferrari): 191 points. Lando Norris (McLaren): 186 points.

Norris is not just behind Antonelli. He is five points behind Hamilton in third — in danger of finishing a season as defending world champion outside the top three. The battle for third place is not the one he wanted to be fighting, but it is the one the standings dictate entering Baku.

A win at Baku accompanied by a Hamilton non-score would move Norris to third place and reduce the gap to Antonelli to approximately 79 points. From there, with eight races remaining, the title would still require significant help — but it would feel alive again rather than the mathematical exercise it currently represents.

McLaren's Baku Investment

McLaren did not arrive at the Azerbaijan Grand Prix without preparation. The team brought a targeted upgrade package aimed at the second sector of the Baku City Circuit — the tighter street section where lap time can be gained or lost through mechanical grip and aerodynamic downforce rather than pure straight-line speed.

That investment signals a belief that McLaren can be genuinely competitive here. Norris has shown in previous rounds that when the MCL-40 is dialed in and the conditions suit his driving style, he is capable of wins. The question in 2026 has been consistency — the car has been at its best on circuits where mechanical grip matters, and has struggled at tracks where Mercedes' straight-line speed advantage is most pronounced.

Baku mixes elements of both. The second sector rewards downforce and precision. The main straight rewards raw speed. How McLaren's upgrade addresses the overall balance will be visible from the very first free practice session on Thursday.

What Norris Needs to See

Norris needs three things to happen at Baku for this weekend to change the championship narrative: a McLaren that is genuinely on pace from FP1 onward, a race that plays to his strengths rather than defaulting to the Mercedes-at-the-front outcome that has defined too many 2026 rounds, and a Antonelli weekend that, for the first time in months, ends without maximum points.

None of those things are within his control beyond his own driving. The championship, at this stage of the season, is largely in other people's hands.

What's Next

FP1 Thursday will give the first read on McLaren's upgrade performance relative to Mercedes and Ferrari. Qualifying Friday will tell the story of whether Norris is genuinely in contention for pole position. The race on Saturday September 26 is where the arithmetic shifts — or doesn't. If Norris takes a dominant win and Antonelli drops points, Baku becomes the weekend the 2026 championship reopened. If it follows the pattern of recent rounds, the conversation starts turning toward who finishes third in the standings.
