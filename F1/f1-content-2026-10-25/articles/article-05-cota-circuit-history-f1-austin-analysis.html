COTA by the Numbers: Why the Circuit of the Americas Always Delivers in Formula 1

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | October 25, 2026

When the Formula 1 circus arrives in Austin, Texas, it brings with it one of the sport's most beloved modern circuits. The Circuit of the Americas — COTA to everyone who loves this sport — has been a fixture on the calendar since 2012 and has delivered memorable racing year after year. Today's United States Grand Prix adds another chapter to a rich legacy.

The Circuit

COTA stretches 5.513 kilometers with 20 corners and a dramatic 40-meter elevation change across its length. The circuit's architects designed it as a tribute to the greatest corners in motorsport, and the result is a layout that rewards drivers who can master every discipline: high-speed aerodynamic balance, medium-speed mechanical grip, and late-braking precision.

The circuit runs clockwise, beginning with the spectacular uphill approach to Turn 1 — a sharp right-hander at the top of a blind crest that demands both bravery and precision. Arriving at 300 kilometers per hour, drivers begin braking while still climbing, unable to see the full apex. It is one of the most visually dramatic corners in the sport and one of the most reliable overtaking opportunities on the calendar.

Sectors That Define Winners

Sector 2 features a sequence of flowing left-right esses borrowed in spirit from Maggots and Becketts at Silverstone. These corners punish any car with excessive understeer or snappy oversteer — the best machinery finds a rhythm through them that sheds only minimal laptime. Sector 3 features a long back straight followed by a sweeping final complex that places high demands on rear-end stability and tire management.

The combination of elements makes COTA a true all-rounder's circuit. Cars that are fast everywhere tend to shine here. Drivers who rely on a particular specialty — ultra-late braking, for example — often find that COTA exposes any weakness in the package.

A Venue Built for Racing

Beyond the circuit itself, COTA has proven itself as a world-class motorsport destination. Grandstand capacity exceeds 130,000, and the American fan base has embraced Formula 1 with genuine passion. The circuit sits on the eastern outskirts of Austin — a city that has grown significantly alongside its annual F1 race — and the atmosphere through the race weekend routinely ranks among the most electric on the calendar.

What's Next

Sunday's 2026 US Grand Prix may be remembered as the day a new chapter began — or as a race that delayed one. Either way, COTA will deliver the spectacle it always does. The circuit brings out the best in Formula 1 machinery and the drivers who control it, and with championship stakes as high as they are in 2026, the 56 laps that begin at 4:00 PM ET have every ingredient to produce something special in Austin.
