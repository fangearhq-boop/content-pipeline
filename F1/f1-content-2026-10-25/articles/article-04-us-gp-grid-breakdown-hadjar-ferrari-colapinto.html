US Grand Prix Grid Breakdown: Hadjar Impresses in P3, Ferrari Locked In P4 and P5, Colapinto Penalized

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | October 25, 2026

Saturday's qualifying session at the Circuit of the Americas produced some fascinating storylines beyond Kimi Antonelli's pole position. Here is the full breakdown of the 2026 US Grand Prix starting grid and what it means for the race.

Hadjar's Impressive P3

Isack Hadjar took third place on the grid for Racing Bulls — one of the qualifying session's standout performances. The young Frenchman has been impressive throughout his first full F1 season, and a front-row-adjacent grid position at one of the season's most high-profile events underscores the talent the team has nurtured. He will start alongside the two big title contenders in the top three and will be looking to stay in touch with the leaders early.

Ferrari in P4 and P5

Charles Leclerc locked in P4 and Lewis Hamilton P5 for Ferrari, giving the Scuderia a strong presence in the top half of the grid. Ferrari's 2026 season has been defined by strong race pace and constructors' championship points, even as pole positions have eluded them — Saturday marked the team's 18th consecutive qualifying without a polesitter in 2026.

Hamilton in P5 adds an interesting subplot to the championship picture. As one of the drivers whose finishing position factors into the title-clinch math for Antonelli, the Ferrari driver's race performance carries additional weight beyond the individual result.

Russell P6 — Championship Hope Fading

George Russell qualified sixth, starting a race where he needs a near-miracle to change the championship picture. He trails Antonelli by 102 points with 162 remaining, meaning a win today only matters if Antonelli falls well down the order. Russell will race to maximize his own points total, but the Sunday mathematics are not in his favor.

Colapinto and Bortoleto Penalized

Franco Colapinto faces a challenging afternoon after a 10-place grid penalty drops him to P21. Gabriel Bortoleto takes new power unit components and starts from the back of the grid. Both drivers will be looking to race through the field on a circuit where overtaking opportunities exist, particularly at Turn 1 and along the main straight.

What's Next

The grid is set. The 56-lap race begins at 4:00 PM ET. With multiple championship-relevant storylines running through the top six, and penalty-laden drivers threatening to shake up the mid-field, Sunday's US Grand Prix has the ingredients for a compelling afternoon of racing at one of Formula 1's most celebrated venues.
