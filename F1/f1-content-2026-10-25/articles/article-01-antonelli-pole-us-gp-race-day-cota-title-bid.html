Antonelli on Pole at COTA: Race Day Preview as F1 Title Beckons in Austin

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | October 25, 2026

Kimi Antonelli lines up on pole position for the 2026 United States Grand Prix at the Circuit of the Americas, and the stage is set for one of the most anticipated Sunday afternoons in Formula 1 this season. The 19-year-old Mercedes driver clocked a 1:34.035 lap on Saturday to take his seventh career pole, edging championship rival Max Verstappen by 0.179 seconds. The race starts at 4:00 PM ET.

It was Antonelli's first pole since Belgium, a seven-race gap that he ended at exactly the moment it mattered most. He leads the 2026 Formula 1 World Championship by 102 points over George Russell and 111 over Lewis Hamilton with just six races remaining. A specific combination of results today could hand him the title in Texas.

The Grid

Verstappen will start alongside Antonelli from P2, with Isack Hadjar having produced a strong qualifying effort to claim P3 for Racing Bulls. Ferrari's Charles Leclerc locks in P4, with his teammate Lewis Hamilton just behind in P5. Russell starts P6 in the second Mercedes — a result that keeps his championship hopes mathematically alive but in need of significant help.

Further back, Franco Colapinto faces a difficult race day after a 10-place grid penalty drops him to P21. Gabriel Bortoleto starts from the back of the grid after taking on new power unit components. Both drivers will be looking to navigate through traffic and score what points they can from difficult positions.

Ferrari's Pole Drought

Saturday represented Ferrari's 18th consecutive attempt to take an F1 pole in 2026 without success. The Scuderia has been competitive in race pace throughout the year, but Saturday afternoons have eluded them. Leclerc was fourth and Hamilton fifth, leaving Ferrari with a reasonable grid position but no polesitter for another event.

Team principal Frederic Vasseur will be watching closely how his drivers manage the all-important opening laps at COTA. Turn 1 is a prime overtaking opportunity — and with both Ferraris in the top five, there is every incentive to try and move up the order at the start.

What's Next

The lights go out at 4:00 PM ET. Fifty-six laps and 308 kilometers of racing await. For Antonelli, it is an opportunity to write his name into the history books before the sun sets over Austin. For Verstappen, it is a chance to do what he has done four times in recent memory at this circuit — take the win. For the rest, it is simply another race day, with championship points and pride on the line across all 20 positions.
