Verstappen Starts P2 at COTA — The One Man Who Could Deny Antonelli's History Day

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | October 25, 2026

If anyone is going to prevent Kimi Antonelli from making history at the Circuit of the Americas today, the most likely candidate is the man lined up alongside him on the front row. Max Verstappen starts P2 for Sunday's US Grand Prix, just 0.179 seconds off Antonelli's pole lap, and his record at COTA is arguably the most formidable of any driver in recent Formula 1 history.

The COTA King

Verstappen has won four of the last five United States Grand Prix races at Austin's Circuit of the Americas. He knows every detail of this 5.513-kilometer layout — the blind crest at Turn 1, the commitment required through the Sector 2 flowing esses, the braking stability needed at the end of the long back straight. When Verstappen is comfortable at a circuit, it is extraordinarily difficult to beat him, and there are few circuits where he feels more at home than COTA.

His 2026 championship campaign has been complicated by the sheer pace of Antonelli and the Mercedes machinery. But race pace has always been one of Verstappen's greatest strengths, and on Sunday he will be the primary threat to the man who starts from pole.

The Battle at Turn 1

The opening lap at COTA typically determines more than at any other venue on the calendar. Turn 1's uphill approach creates a natural overtaking spot where drivers can brake late, dive to the inside, and potentially emerge ahead. With Verstappen just behind Antonelli as the lights go out, the first few corners of the race could establish the early narrative.

Antonelli will be acutely aware of his responsibilities on the opening lap. A collision — however unintended — would be catastrophic for his championship prospects. Racing cleanly but assertively around the outside is the priority. Verstappen, with nothing to lose in a championship sense, can afford to be more aggressive.

What's Next

Verstappen's own title bid is 119 points behind Antonelli's with 162 points available. A win today keeps his mathematical hopes alive for a few more races, while a poor result would essentially end his championship aspirations. That pressure to perform creates the perfect dynamic for an outstanding race: Antonelli protecting a lead; Verstappen attacking with nothing to lose. Formula 1 fans watching from Austin's grandstands and around the world could be in for one of the year's best races.
