Leclerc and Ferrari's Best Chance at a First Win of 2026

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | November 1, 2026

The 2026 Formula 1 season has been many things for Ferrari. A year of frustration. A year of potential not fully realized. A year where the pace was there on certain weekends but the results were not. Now, in Mexico City, on a Sunday when the world will be watching Kimi Antonelli collect his championship, Charles Leclerc has a chance to give Ferrari what they have been denied all year: a race win.

Leclerc dominated both Friday practice sessions at the Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez, setting the fastest times in FP1 and FP2. His teammate Lewis Hamilton followed that up with his best qualifying performance of the 2026 season. The data suggests Ferrari has found a setup that works extraordinarily well at altitude, and Mexico City — at 2,240 meters above sea level — may be the circuit most likely to hand them their first victory of the season.

Altitude as Ferrari's Advantage

Formula 1 at high altitude operates by different rules than at sea-level circuits. The thin air reduces aerodynamic downforce, which means higher straight-line speeds but less mechanical grip through corners. Engine cooling behaves differently. Tire heating compounds change. Teams that can optimize their car for these specific conditions — and Ferrari appears to have done exactly that — find themselves with an advantage that exists nowhere else on the calendar.

Leclerc's practice dominance suggests Ferrari's altitude-tuned setup is genuinely working. The power unit concern that hampered Hamilton in FP2 has been addressed, leaving the team with two fully operational cars and a weekend's worth of data confirming they have the pace to fight at the very front.

The Weight of a Winless Season

For a team with Ferrari's history, resources, and ambition, going an entire season without a victory would represent a significant failure. The pressure to win, especially as Antonelli approaches his coronation, is real. Leclerc understands that and has spoken about wanting to give Ferrari their first 2026 win before the season closes out.

Sunday's race is not just about the points, which at this stage of the season mean relatively little in the championship context. It is about pride, momentum, and proving that the 2026 Ferrari package — which clearly has raw pace — can be converted into results. A win in Mexico would change the narrative heading into the final four rounds.

What's Next

Leclerc starts alongside Hamilton, with the Ferrari pair in strong grid positions heading into the first chicane. Their greatest challenge will come from Antonelli, who is highly motivated from P4 and driving the championship-leading car. If Ferrari can get ahead early and manage the race strategically, they have the ingredients for a landmark result. Whether they execute is the only question that remains.
