Hamilton's Best Qualifying of 2026: Ferrari Makes a Statement in Mexico City

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | November 1, 2026

Lewis Hamilton emerged from Mexico City qualifying with three words that Ferrari fans have been waiting to hear all year: "An amazing feeling."

Starting from third on the grid, Hamilton delivered what has been acknowledged as his strongest qualifying performance of the entire 2026 season at the Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez. It is a result that, in the context of the championship, feels like too little too late — Kimi Antonelli could clinch the title in the same race on Sunday. But for Ferrari, and for Hamilton personally, it represents a moment of genuine hope that this partnership can still deliver results.

Why Mexico Suits Ferrari

The Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez sits at 2,240 meters above sea level, the highest point on the entire Formula 1 calendar. The thin air at altitude changes the aerodynamic equations for every car on the grid — less downforce, different tire behavior, altered engine efficiency. It is a circuit that rewards specific characteristics, and it appears that the 2026 Ferrari suits Mexico City in a way that most of the other 22 venues on the calendar do not.

Charles Leclerc had already signaled that this would be a strong weekend by setting the fastest times in both FP1 and FP2. Hamilton took that performance baseline and converted it into a front-three grid position in qualifying, the first time this season that Ferrari has looked genuinely capable of running with the leading teams from the opening laps of a race.

Hamilton's Race Ambitions

Starting third, Hamilton will be alongside and immediately behind the championship front-runners. Motorsport.com reported that Hamilton was plotting an "aggressive start" after finally gelling with the Ferrari setup — and an aggressive start from P3 into the tight first chicane at Hermanos Rodriguez could shake up the entire order in the opening sector.

Hamilton has won in Mexico City before, and his experience at this particular circuit — with its unusual characteristics, its high-energy run through the stadium section, and its strategic complexity — gives him a competitive intelligence that cannot be taught. A driver in his career situation, hungry for results with Ferrari, has every reason to race on the limit from the moment the lights go out.

Ferrari's power unit concern — a power issue forced Hamilton to park his car in FP2 last week — appears to have been resolved ahead of race day. That was the final piece needed to give Ferrari a genuine shot at converting their qualifying pace into a race result.

The Bigger Picture

For Hamilton personally, Sunday's race is about more than championship positioning. His Ferrari chapter has been defined, so far, by flashes of brilliance interspersed with reliability frustrations and the difficulty of adapting to a new car philosophy. A podium or victory in Mexico City — against the backdrop of Antonelli's potential coronation — would represent a meaningful milestone: proof that the move to Ferrari was not a mistake, and that the partnership still has its best moments ahead of it.

What's Next

Hamilton starts third, Leclerc alongside him, in what could be Ferrari's most important race of the 2026 season. A win today would end a winless campaign, give the team enormous momentum for the final four rounds, and establish Ferrari's altitude package as a genuine weapon for the rest of the year. Whatever happens, Mexico City has already delivered one answer: Lewis Hamilton and Ferrari can still find the feeling that makes this partnership worth watching.
