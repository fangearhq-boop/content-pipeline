Colapinto Gets 5-Place Penalty for Malaysia GP After Wrecking Norris and Gasly at Baku

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | September 28, 2026

Franco Colapinto will start the Malaysia GP five places behind his qualifying position after the FIA issued a grid penalty for the restart crash that ended both Lando Norris and Pierre Gasly at the Azerbaijan Grand Prix on Sunday.

Colapinto locked up under braking at Turn 1 on the Lap 36 restart, hit Gasly's car, and the impact collected Norris immediately after. Both drivers retired on the spot. Colapinto also retired from the race, which made the original 10-second time penalty academic. The stewards converted it to a five-place grid penalty for the next event, the Bahrain Grand Prix at Sepang on October 2-4.

Norris Calls for a Race Ban

Norris was not interested in measured language when he spoke in the paddock afterward. The McLaren driver said the penalty did not go far enough.

"If you cause a crash on a restart like this, you should have at least a one-race ban," Norris said.

It was his first retirement since the Monaco Grand Prix in early June. The timing was brutal for Norris in the championship context, though he entered Baku outside the title picture given the gap to Kimi Antonelli. Still, the points loss matters when every position counts.

Race winner George Russell offered a different view from Norris. Russell said a ban would have been too harsh a response for the incident, stopping short of endorsing the same level of punishment his McLaren rival demanded.

Alpine Leaves the Door Open on Colapinto's Future

The penalty is one issue. The bigger question hovering over the weekend was what Alpine plans to do about Colapinto's seat.

The team released a statement that stopped well short of offering him a clear vote of confidence. Alpine said it is deciding "what course of action should be taken" regarding Colapinto going forward. That phrasing, coming immediately after a crash that wiped out two drivers and triggered a public call for a ban from a world champion, drew immediate attention.

Colapinto has had incidents before. The Baku crash follows a pattern that has made his Alpine seat the subject of ongoing scrutiny this season. Whether Alpine takes further action beyond the FIA-issued penalty will become clearer before the Malaysia weekend opens.

What the Penalty Means at Sepang

A five-place grid penalty does not automatically push a driver to the back, but it does limit strategic options heading into a race where track position often proves difficult to recover. Sepang is known for its high-speed sections and demanding tyre management. Starting five places down from a qualifying result creates a real challenge, particularly early in the race when the field is compressed.

Colapinto will need a clean run through the opening laps to avoid compounding the situation further.

What's Next

The 2026 Bahrain Grand Prix at Sepang takes place October 2-4. Practice begins Friday, qualifying and additional practice sessions run Saturday, and the race starts Sunday at 15:00 local time, which is 08:00 BST and 03:00 ET. Colapinto's five-place penalty will be applied after qualifying, dropping him from his grid slot accordingly. Alpine's announcement on whether any further action follows from the Baku crash is expected before the team arrives in Malaysia.
