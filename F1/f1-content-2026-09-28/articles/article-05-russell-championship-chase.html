Russell Won at Baku and Cut the Gap to 66. Can He Actually Catch Antonelli?

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | September 28, 2026

George Russell held off Max Verstappen by 0.196 seconds at the Azerbaijan Grand Prix to take his third win of the 2026 Formula 1 season. After crossing the line in Baku, he said he is not giving up on the championship. The gap to Kimi Antonelli stands at 66 points with nine rounds remaining and a maximum of 459 points still available.

The question is whether that gap is actually closeable.

How the Season Has Gone

Russell's win rate in 2026 is strong. Three victories in a season is the kind of form that would win titles in many years. The problem is that Antonelli has been even better. Antonelli leads with 302 points. Russell is on 236. The gap peaked at 81 points after Antonelli won in Spain. Baku trimmed 15 points off that, which is the largest single-round swing Russell has produced this season.

But there is a structure problem. Russell and Antonelli drive for the same team. When Russell wins, Antonelli tends to finish in the top five. On Sunday, Antonelli started from P16 after a qualifying crash and still recovered to fifth, scoring 10 points. Russell winning the race and Antonelli taking fifth still leaves the gap at 66. A maximum result from Russell only shifts the gap when Antonelli genuinely struggles.

The Math for Russell

For Russell to win the championship, he needs to close 66 points over nine rounds while both drivers are likely competing in the same machinery. That requires Antonelli to make mistakes or face mechanical failures across multiple weekends while Russell scores maximum points. It is not impossible. Seasons can turn quickly.

The scale of the task becomes clearer when you look at what happened at Baku. Russell won the race outright. He drove one of the cleaner weekends of his season and held off the defending three-time champion in a wheel-to-wheel finish. He gained 15 points. At that rate, he would close the gap to zero across roughly four more Baku-level performances, assuming Antonelli does not score anything in those rounds. That is not how it works.

Russell is right that 459 points remain and it is not over. He is also right that he cannot afford to give up now. But closing 66 points on the driver in the faster seat at the same team requires a specific set of circumstances that have not materialized consistently this season.

What Russell Said

"I'm not giving up," Russell said after Baku. "The gap is 66 and there are 459 points left. It's not over."

That is a genuine statement of intent, not a political answer. Russell has won before under pressure, and he knows the championship table better than anyone. The math exists. The probability is low, but the 2026 season is not done.

What's Next

Russell heads to Malaysia for the Bahrain Grand Prix at Sepang on October 2-4, where he arrives with momentum from Baku. A win there, combined with Antonelli finishing outside the top three, would close the gap further and keep the title race alive heading into Singapore. The Singapore GP on October 9-11 is the earliest window for Antonelli to clinch. Russell's best path is preventing that window from opening.
