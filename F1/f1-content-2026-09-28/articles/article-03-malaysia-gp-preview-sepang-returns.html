F1 Returns to Sepang for the First Time Since 2017 -- What to Know About the Malaysia GP

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | September 28, 2026

Nine years is a long gap between visits to one of Formula 1's most iconic venues. When the lights go out at Sepang International Circuit on Sunday, October 4, it will be the first time F1 has raced there since the 2017 Malaysian Grand Prix, the final edition before the race left the calendar.

Now it is back. The 2026 Bahrain Grand Prix, hosted at Sepang under a commercial arrangement with the Bahrain GP title sponsor, runs October 2-4 at a circuit that shaped some of the sport's most memorable afternoons over its 18 years on the schedule from 1999 to 2017.

The Circuit

Sepang International Circuit stretches 5.543 kilometers and includes 15 corners across a lap. The race runs 56 laps. Hermann Tilke designed the track, which opened in 1999, and it became one of his most celebrated works. The layout features long straights that reward straight-line speed and heavy braking zones, wide corners that allow multiple lines through the mid-section, and a sweeping final sector that punishes any setup compromise.

Tyres are a constant factor at Sepang. The combination of abrasive asphalt, heat, and high lateral loads through the circuit's faster corners makes degradation difficult to manage. Rain adds another variable. Sepang is one of the few circuits where afternoon thunderstorms are not just possible but probable during the race window, and the weather has triggered safety cars, red flags, and strategy swings that altered outcomes in almost every edition the circuit hosted.

Storylines Heading In

Franco Colapinto arrives in Malaysia carrying a five-place grid penalty from the FIA following his restart crash at the Azerbaijan Grand Prix. He locked up at Turn 1 on a Lap 36 restart and triggered a collision that ended both Lando Norris and Pierre Gasly. The penalty drops him five places from wherever he qualifies on Saturday.

Max Verstappen finished second at Baku by 0.196 seconds behind George Russell after a late charge in the closing laps. Verstappen has not won a race in several rounds and Sepang represents a circuit that has historically suited Red Bull's philosophy of mechanical grip and downforce. He will push hard for a first victory in a while.

Kimi Antonelli leads the drivers' championship by 66 points over Russell heading into Malaysia. A clean points-scoring run here maintains the gap ahead of the Singapore GP, which is the first window to clinch the title.

Race Times

The Malaysia GP race start is 15:00 local time at Sepang, which is 08:00 BST and 03:00 ET on Sunday, October 4. Practice sessions run on Friday. Saturday includes an additional practice and qualifying.

What's Next

After Malaysia, the calendar moves to Singapore for the Grand Prix on October 9-11. Singapore will run its first-ever Sprint format, adding eight Sprint points on Saturday to the 25 available Sunday. Antonelli leads by 66 points and the Singapore Sprint creates the earliest possible scenario for him to clinch the 2026 drivers' title. Malaysia is the final step before that window opens.
