### STORY 1: Ferrari ADUO-2 Engine Confirmed for Monza

**Long-Form Post | 10:30 AM**

Ferrari is coming to Monza with a brand new weapon — and this time, they don't have to pay a grid penalty to use it.

Team principal Fred Vasseur confirmed ahead of the Italian Grand Prix that both Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc will run Ferrari's new ADUO-2 power unit this weekend. The upgrade features a completely redesigned turbocharger — a larger unit with a new compressor targeting more power, better efficiency, and improved drivability — delivering an estimated gain of around 15 horsepower over the previous ADUO-1 specification.

The timing could not be more significant. Monza is the fastest circuit on the Formula 1 calendar, where straight-line speed matters more than anywhere else. Ferrari has been visibly behind Mercedes in power output for much of the 2026 season — that gap has cost them pole positions and race wins on high-speed circuits. At the Temple of Speed, extra horsepower translates directly to lap time.

Crucially, both Hamilton and Leclerc receive the upgrade without triggering grid penalties, meaning they stay within their seasonal allocation. The same cannot be said for Antonelli, who is starting from the back after a full Mercedes power unit change.

Hamilton was upbeat in Thursday's press conference, saying he's "hopeful Ferrari can fight for the win" at their home race.

It's Ferrari's home weekend. They have a new engine. They have the Schumacher tribute livery. If there was ever a Monza weekend built for Ferrari magic, it's this one.

### STORY 2: Antonelli's Monza Challenge — Championship Leader Starts from Back

**Long-Form Post | 8:30 AM**

Here is the situation heading into Sunday's Italian Grand Prix: Kimi Antonelli leads the 2026 Formula 1 championship by 59 points. He is 20 years old, Italian, and racing at his home circuit — the Autodromo Nazionale Monza, just north of Milan — where the Tifosi will be filling the grandstands to cheer him on.

And he is starting from last place.

Mercedes confirmed last week that Antonelli would take a full power unit change ahead of Monza — swapping out every engine component, from the V6 to the MGU-H and beyond, pushing him to the back of the grid under F1's penalty system. The reason is strategic: with 11 races remaining after Monza, the team decided to absorb the hit now, at a track with better overtaking opportunities than almost anywhere else on the calendar, and emerge with a fresh, reliable engine for the championship run-in.

Toto Wolff set a modest but clear objective for Antonelli's race: a points-scoring finish, ideally fifth place or better, while keeping his rivals within manageable distance.

Former champion Mika Hakkinen weighed in Thursday, warning that fans would "crucify" any team that issued team orders in the 2026 title fight. There are no orders expected — Russell and Hamilton are free to race — but with Antonelli starting last, Mercedes' title defense just got a lot more complicated.

Can he charge from 20th to a points finish on race day? Monza has seen some great recovery drives in its history. Sunday could add another one.

### STORY 3: Norris Targets Three Straight

**Long-Form Post | 2:30 PM**

Two weeks ago, Lando Norris won the Dutch Grand Prix at Zandvoort. The week before that, he won in Hungary. Now he arrives at Monza as the favorite to win a third consecutive race.

The math, for once, is cooperating with the McLaren driver's title hopes. Norris sits 83 points behind championship leader Kimi Antonelli with 11 races to go — a deficit that looked insurmountable a month ago. But Antonelli is starting Sunday's Italian Grand Prix from the back of the grid, and Norris is starting from the sharp end. A maximum swing weekend — Norris wins, Antonelli finishes outside the points — would cut the gap to 58 points with ten races remaining.

Suddenly the 2026 title fight looks very different.

Monza favors McLaren's style. The circuit rewards straight-line pace, late braking, and race strategy at high speed — and McLaren has had the best race car on power-circuit tracks all season. Oscar Piastri is also a threat from the front of the grid. A McLaren 1-2 here would be a statement of intent that the team is coming for both championships.

For Norris himself, this is his moment. After finishing runner-up to Max Verstappen in 2024 and claiming his maiden title in 2025, he knows what it takes to win a championship. The question now is whether he can summon those performances at exactly the right time.

Three straight for Norris. It's possible. It might be necessary.

### STORY 4: The Schumacher Tribute — Hamilton and Leclerc Race in F310 Colors

**Long-Form Post | 12:30 PM**

Thirty years ago, Michael Schumacher put on a red suit and climbed into a Ferrari for the first time. The result was an F310 with a red, black, and white livery — not the all-red look we associate with Ferrari today — and the beginning of the most successful partnership in Formula 1 history.

This weekend at Monza, Ferrari is bringing that story back to life.

The SF-26 will carry a special livery inspired by Schumacher's 1996 F310: glossy red bodywork with a white rear wing and black detailing, finishing the look with gold-tinted wheels and Schumacher's iconic MS logo. The seven-star motif representing his seven world championships features on the helmets worn by Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc, who will also race in specially designed race suits, gloves, and shoes for the tribute weekend.

The celebration extends beyond the cars. Three Schumacher-era Ferraris — the F310 (1996), the F2002, and the 248 F1 (2006) — will be on display at the circuit throughout the weekend. On Saturday, Rubens Barrichello — Schumacher's teammate for all five of his Ferrari titles — will drive the F2002. Sebastian Vettel, the other Ferrari driver to reach Schumacher's heights (though not his numbers), will take the same car on a ceremonial lap ahead of Sunday's race.

It's a weekend that celebrates not just Schumacher, but the soul of what Monza means to Ferrari.

As for the livery itself — it has divided fans. Some wished Ferrari had gone all-red; Hamilton himself reportedly wanted a cleaner all-red look. But the F310 reference is so specific, so deliberate, that the design choice feels earned.

This weekend, Monza hosts a living museum of Ferrari greatness. Whatever happens in the race, the theatre is already extraordinary.

### STORY 5: Monaco GP Appeal — Verdict Could Land This Weekend

**Long-Form Post | 7:30 PM**

While Formula 1's finest are racing at Monza this weekend, the FIA's International Court of Appeal may be quietly delivering one of the most consequential off-track verdicts of the 2026 season.

The case involves Pierre Gasly's Monaco Grand Prix podium — and whether it will stand.

Here's the background: at the June 7 Monaco Grand Prix, Gasly received two five-second time penalties for exceeding the 60 km/h pit lane speed limit, recording 60.1 and 60.4 km/h. Those penalties dropped him off the podium. Alpine subsequently filed a successful right of review, presenting evidence that Monaco's pit lane distance measurement was incorrect — meaning the speed readings used to penalize Gasly were derived from a miscalibrated system. The stewards overturned his penalties, reinstating him to third place.

McLaren and Red Bull were not satisfied. They formally appealed the reinstatement, arguing that the right of review was applied incorrectly and raising concerns about "sporting fairness." Both teams lost points as a result of Gasly's reinstatement — Oscar Piastri dropped from fourth to fifth, affecting McLaren's constructors' standings.

The FIA International Court of Appeal heard the case on August 25 in Paris, with a four-judge panel deliberating. A verdict was expected within two to three weeks — which puts the ruling squarely during or immediately after the Monza weekend.

If the court overturns Gasly's reinstatement, he loses the P3 points, the classification reverts, and McLaren and Red Bull gain back what they lost. If the court upholds the reinstatement, Gasly keeps his podium and the June 7 result stands permanently.

It's one of F1's most unusual legal sagas, and it's coming to a close while the drivers are still racing.
