Can Norris Win Three Straight? Monza Sets Up the Championship's Most Dramatic Weekend

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | September 3, 2026

Lando Norris won the Hungarian Grand Prix. The week after that, he won the Dutch Grand Prix at Zandvoort. He arrives at Monza for the Italian Grand Prix as the outright favorite to make it three in a row — and at this particular moment in the 2026 Formula 1 season, three consecutive wins might be exactly what a title challenge requires.

The math is stark. Norris sits 83 points behind championship leader Kimi Antonelli with 11 races remaining. Under normal circumstances, that gap would be daunting but manageable over a full calendar. This weekend is not normal circumstances: Antonelli starts from the back of the grid due to a full power unit penalty, and Norris starts from the sharp end as the favorite. A maximum swing — Norris wins, Antonelli finishes outside the points — closes the gap by 25 points in a single afternoon.

Why Monza Suits McLaren

The Italian Grand Prix circuit at Monza is the fastest on the Formula 1 calendar. Its defining characteristics — long full-throttle sections, minimal technical corners, and a premium on straight-line pace — align closely with McLaren's strengths in 2026. The team has posted the best race pace on power-circuit tracks throughout the season, and the MCL60's aerodynamic philosophy in low-downforce configuration has proven effective at venues where raw speed matters most.

Norris arrives as the bookmakers' favorite at approximately 4-to-1 odds to win Sunday's race. That figure reflects both his recent form and the circuit characteristics. Ferrari's new ADUO-2 engine will make them competitive, and the Monza power requirements suit their upgraded specification. But McLaren's overall package — race pace, pit stop execution, and strategic flexibility — has been the benchmark in 2026 on circuits like this.

The Piastri Factor

Oscar Piastri provides McLaren with a second legitimate race contender from the front of the grid. The Australian has been consistently quick in 2026 and is capable of challenging for the win independently — meaning Ferrari and Mercedes must manage the threat of both McLaren drivers simultaneously rather than focusing all defensive strategy on Norris alone.

A McLaren 1-2 at Monza would be a statement of intent in the constructors' championship, where the team is fighting hard against Mercedes. It would also, crucially, maximize the points swing against Antonelli in the drivers' title fight — if Piastri finishes second and Norris wins, Antonelli faces an even steeper climb to protect his lead.

Norris Under the Microscope

Norris knows what it takes to win a championship. After finishing runner-up to Max Verstappen in 2024, he claimed his maiden drivers' title in 2025 in what was widely considered one of the most competitive seasons in the sport's recent history. He is not a driver who shrinks under pressure.

But the 2026 season has presented a different kind of challenge. Antonelli's consistency has been the story of the year — the 20-year-old has managed points-scoring finishes in all but one race, building a lead that resisted McLaren's strongest challenges until the Hungarian-Zandvoort run. Two wins in a row have shifted momentum. A third would announce that the title race is genuinely back on.

The psychological dimension matters here. A driver winning three consecutive races is in form, in confidence, and in a rhythm that can be difficult to disrupt. Norris knows this. Ferrari and Mercedes know this. The pressure on Monza is real.

The Championship Calendar Ahead

After Monza, the 2026 season heads to Singapore, Japan, Austin, Mexico City, Sao Paulo, Las Vegas, and Abu Dhabi. Of those circuits, several favor the characteristics that have given McLaren an edge in 2026: Austin and Las Vegas tend to reward high-speed and aerodynamic efficiency; Sao Paulo's changeable weather creates strategic chaos. The calendar is not uniformly favorable to any one team, which makes Monza's swing potential all the more significant.

If Norris wins on Sunday and Antonelli struggles from the back, the final ten races become a genuine championship fight rather than a managed title defense. That possibility — improbable a month ago — is now very real.

What's Next

Qualifying Saturday will establish whether Norris and McLaren can convert pre-weekend pace advantage into a front-row start. A Norris pole position would confirm the form and maximize his strategic options for Sunday's race. Practice sessions begin Friday, and every session will add clarity to whether the 2026 Italian Grand Prix delivers on its extraordinary pre-race billing.
