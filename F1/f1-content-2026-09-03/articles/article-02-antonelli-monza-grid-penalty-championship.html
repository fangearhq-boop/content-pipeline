Starting Last at His Home Race: Antonelli's Italian GP Challenge and Mercedes' Strategic Gamble

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | September 3, 2026

Kimi Antonelli is 20 years old, Italian, and leads the 2026 Formula 1 World Championship by 59 points. On Sunday, he will start the Italian Grand Prix — at Monza, the circuit closest to where he was born, in front of Italian fans who have adopted him as the sport's next great champion — from last place.

Mercedes confirmed last week that Antonelli would take a full power unit change ahead of the Italian GP, replacing every component of the hybrid system: the V6 internal combustion engine, the turbocharger, the MGU-H, the MGU-K, the energy store, and the control electronics. The penalty for exceeding the seasonal component limit is a start from the back of the grid, regardless of qualifying result.

The Strategy Behind the Sacrifice

Mercedes did not choose Monza for this penalty by accident. Team principal Toto Wolff outlined the reasoning clearly: with 11 races remaining in the 2026 season after Monza, the team wanted to absorb the grid hit at a circuit with the best overtaking opportunities on the calendar, then emerge with a fresh, reliable power unit for the championship run-in through Singapore, Japan, Austin, Mexico, Brazil, Las Vegas, and Abu Dhabi.

Monza's long straights and DRS zones create multiple genuine overtaking opportunities per lap — a recovery drive that would be nearly impossible at Monaco or Budapest becomes plausible, if demanding, here. Wolff set a modest but clear target for Antonelli's race: a points-scoring finish, ideally No. 5 or better.

The calculation is straightforward. A fifth-place finish yields ten points. If Antonelli's rivals score maximum points and he scores ten, the championship lead narrows — but he still leads. With a fresh power unit for the final 11 races, the long-term math favors the sacrifice.

The Pressure of Racing at Home

What the strategy spreadsheet cannot account for is the emotional weight of starting last at your home race. The Autodromo Nazionale Monza sits just north of Milan, and the Italian fans — the Tifosi — pack the grandstands for the Italian GP with a fervor unique in Formula 1. In 2026, those fans have a new hero: Antonelli, the 20-year-old Bolognese driver who carried Italian motorsport's hopes into the Silver Arrows.

Starting from 20th place means Antonelli will spend the first stint of Sunday's race working through backmarkers and midfield traffic while his championship rivals — Hamilton and Russell in the Ferraris and Mercedes, Norris and Piastri at McLaren — fight each other from the front. For the Tifosi, watching their man emerge from the back while Ferrari fights for the win creates a complex emotional cocktail.

The Team Orders Question

Former champion Mika Hakkinen weighed in at Monza on Thursday, issuing a warning that will hang over the weekend: fans would "crucify" any team that deployed team orders in the 2026 title fight. The comment was pointed. George Russell, Antonelli's Mercedes teammate, is tied on points with Hamilton at 183 — both 59 points behind Antonelli. Russell is racing freely this weekend, not under orders to support his teammate.

Wolff confirmed that both Mercedes drivers are free to race on Sunday, a position that protects the team from accusations of manipulation while ensuring Antonelli cannot expect assistance from the car ahead of him. Whatever position Antonelli recovers to, he will earn it on merit.

Monza's Recovery History

The Italian Grand Prix has a history of dramatic recovery drives. The circuit's long straights reward raw pace; a car with genuine straight-line speed advantage can carve through the field quickly with DRS assistance. Mercedes' power unit performance this season has been among the strongest in the field — Antonelli will not be lacking for straight-line pace as he works his way forward.

The realistic ceiling for a recovery drive at Monza, given typical traffic patterns and safety car probability, is somewhere between eighth and fifth. Scoring in the top five from 20th would be an extraordinary result. Scoring points at all would keep the championship lead largely intact. Failing to score would represent a genuine crisis, potentially allowing Norris — who has won the last two races and starts from the front at Monza — to cut the gap to 58 points.

What's Next

The Italian Grand Prix takes place Sunday, September 6. Antonelli's qualifying session on Saturday will be largely academic — he starts from the back regardless of his lap time — but the team will use it to assess setup and power unit performance ahead of the race. His race pace and ability to manage the fresh power unit in the opening laps will define how Sunday unfolds. Whatever happens, Monza 2026 is already a story worth watching.
