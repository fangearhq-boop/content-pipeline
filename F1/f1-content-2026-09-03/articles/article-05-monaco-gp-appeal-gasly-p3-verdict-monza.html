Gasly's Monaco P3 Still on the Line: What the FIA Appeal Verdict Could Mean for the 2026 Championship

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | September 3, 2026

While drivers compete at the Italian Grand Prix this weekend, a four-judge panel at the FIA International Court of Appeal in Paris may be finalizing one of the most consequential verdicts of the 2026 Formula 1 season. The ruling — on McLaren and Red Bull's appeal of Pierre Gasly's reinstated Monaco Grand Prix podium — could land during race week or immediately after, and it affects championship points in both the drivers' and constructors' standings.

How This Started

At the June 7 Monaco Grand Prix, Gasly received two five-second time penalties for exceeding the 60 km/h pit lane speed limit. The recorded readings were 60.1 km/h and 60.4 km/h — marginal violations, but violations nonetheless under the regulations as they stood. The penalties dropped him off the podium.

Alpine subsequently exercised their right of review, presenting evidence to the Monaco stewards that the pit lane distance measurement used to derive the speed readings was incorrect — meaning the speed calculations were based on a miscalibrated system. The stewards accepted Alpine's evidence, overturned the penalties, and reinstated Gasly to third place. The amended Monaco classification: Gasly P3, Isack Hadjar P4, Oscar Piastri P5.

McLaren and Red Bull, who both lost championship points as a result of the reinstatement, formally appealed to the FIA International Court of Appeal. The case was heard August 25 in Paris.

The Legal Arguments

McLaren's appeal centers on two arguments. First, they contend that the right of review was applied incorrectly — that the evidence Alpine presented did not meet the threshold of "new significant element" required to trigger a review under the International Sporting Code. Second, McLaren raises a "double intent" argument: that the appeal challenges not just the specific penalty decision but the broader mechanism of the right of review, claiming the stewards' application of it violated principles of sporting fairness.

Red Bull's position aligns with McLaren's sporting fairness argument, though their specific objections have been less publicly detailed. Both teams lost constructors' championship points when the Monaco result was amended, giving them concrete standing in the appeal.

Alpine and Gasly's defense rests on the measurement error itself — which FOM, Formula 1's commercial rights holder, has publicly acknowledged. If the speed readings were derived from a miscalibrated system, the penalties were based on incorrect data. A penalty imposed on incorrect data, the argument runs, should be overturned regardless of the procedural mechanism used to surface that fact.

What the Verdict Could Change

If the FIA Court of Appeal overturns Gasly's reinstatement, the Monaco classification reverts to the post-penalty result. Gasly loses the 15 points he received for third place, Hadjar moves back to P3, Piastri returns to P4, and McLaren regains the points they lost when the result was amended. The shift affects both the drivers' championship and the constructors' standings.

If the court upholds the reinstatement, Gasly keeps his Monaco podium and the amended classification stands permanently. McLaren and Red Bull receive no additional points. The legal process closes.

The verdict is expected within two to three weeks of the August 25 hearing, placing the likely window squarely between the Italian Grand Prix and the Singapore GP — with a genuine possibility the ruling arrives during Monza race week itself.

Why the Measurement Error Matters

The extraordinary element of this case is not the appeal itself but what triggered it: an acknowledged miscalibration in the Monaco pit lane measurement system. FOM's admission that the distance measurement was incorrect is unusual — systems that underpin race penalties are typically assumed to be reliable, and a public acknowledgment of systemic error is rare in Formula 1's technical and sporting landscape.

The broader implication is a question about whether similar measurement issues could exist at other circuits, and whether the systems used to enforce speed limits in pit lanes are subject to sufficient independent verification. That question may not be part of the appeal's formal scope, but it lingers in the background of every pit lane speed infraction in 2026.

What's Next

Until the FIA Court of Appeal issues its ruling, the amended Monaco classification stands. Gasly holds P3 from Monaco. The verdict, when it arrives, will be published by the FIA and takes effect immediately. If it lands during Monza weekend, the timing will add an additional layer of drama to an Italian Grand Prix already overloaded with storylines. Either outcome closes one of the sport's most unusual legal sagas — and the result will define how the 2026 season is remembered in Monaco's record books.
