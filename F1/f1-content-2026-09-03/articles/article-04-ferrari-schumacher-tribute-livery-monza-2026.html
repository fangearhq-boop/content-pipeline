Ferrari's Schumacher Tribute at Monza: F310 Colors, Barrichello, and 30 Years of Red

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | September 3, 2026

In 1996, Michael Schumacher climbed into a Ferrari for the first time at a pre-season test in Fiorano. He was a two-time world champion joining the most famous team in motorsport, and what followed was the greatest partnership in Formula 1 history — five consecutive constructors' championships from 1999 to 2004, five drivers' titles, and a legacy that reshaped the sport. Thirty years later, Ferrari is bringing it all back to life at Monza.

The SF-26 will carry a special tribute livery this weekend inspired by Schumacher's 1996 F310, the car he drove in his first Ferrari season. The design features glossy red bodywork, a white rear wing, and black detailing — a deliberate callback to the F310's distinctive look that differed significantly from the all-red Ferrari liveries that followed. Gold-tinted wheels complete the visual, along with Schumacher's iconic MS initials logo on the car and the seven-star motif representing his seven world championships.

The Drivers in Tribute

Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc will race in specially designed race suits, gloves, and shoes for the weekend, each incorporating Schumacher-inspired elements. Both drivers' helmets feature the seven-star design — a nod that carries particular resonance for Hamilton, who equals Schumacher's seven championship tally. The tribute is not merely visual; it is a deliberate statement about the lineage of greatness at Ferrari.

Hamilton, who wore red as Schumacher once did and shares his championship record, occupies a unique position in the tribute narrative. Leclerc, meanwhile, has spent his entire Ferrari career chasing the ghost of Schumacher's dominance at a team that measures every driver against that benchmark. For both of them, racing in F310 colors at Monza carries weight beyond aesthetics.

Barrichello and Vettel on the F2002

The tribute extends beyond the current cars and drivers. Ferrari has brought three cars from the Schumacher era to Monza for the weekend: the 1996 F310, the legendary F2002, and the 248 F1 from Schumacher's final Ferrari season in 2006. The F2002 is widely regarded as one of the greatest Formula 1 cars ever built — it powered Schumacher to the 2002 title with a dominant season that is still discussed as a benchmark of constructors' engineering excellence.

Rubens Barrichello, Schumacher's teammate for all five of his Ferrari title years, will drive the F2002 on Saturday at Monza. The sight of Barrichello back in a Ferrari — in Schumacher's car, at the circuit where he last drove it competitively — will be a significant emotional moment for fans who remember those years firsthand.

Sebastian Vettel, who won four consecutive championships with Ferrari from 2010 to 2013 and represents the other great champion who drove for the team in the Schumacher tradition, will take the F2002 for a ceremonial lap ahead of Sunday's race. Two Ferrari champions, one iconic car, one iconic circuit.

The Design Debate

The livery has not been universally embraced. Some fans, particularly those attached to the all-red identity Ferrari has maintained for decades, felt the white rear wing was an unnecessary deviation — a tribute that could have been more powerful as a clean, uninterrupted statement of red. Hamilton himself reportedly preferred an all-red look, and his candor on the subject created a minor pre-weekend discussion.

The counterargument is simpler and, arguably, stronger: the F310 reference is historically specific. Schumacher's first Ferrari was not all-red. Acknowledging that reality — that the car he arrived in looked different, that the journey to the red dynasty had a distinct visual starting point — is precisely what gives this tribute its historical weight. An all-red car would be Ferrari honoring Schumacher with a fictional version of his livery. The actual F310 colors are the truth.

What's Next

The tribute weekend formally begins with Friday practice and peaks on Sunday with the race — an Italian Grand Prix that already carries extraordinary storylines beyond the livery. Ferrari racing in Schumacher's colors, with a new engine, at their home circuit, while the championship leader starts from the back. The theatre is exceptional even before a wheel turns.

Whatever happens in the race itself, Monza 2026 has already secured its place in the history of Formula 1 tributes. Thirty years of red, condensed into one weekend, with the people who made it happen back on the track.
