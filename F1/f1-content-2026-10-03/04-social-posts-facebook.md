Red Bull's 2026 challenger has been plagued by problems from day one: reportedly running 9-10 kilograms overweight and struggling notably in high-speed corners, it simply hasn't been able to keep pace with the Mercedes, Ferrari and McLaren cars that have dominated the grid.

History is watching.

### STORY 4: Hamilton at Ferrari

**Long-Form Post:**
When Lewis Hamilton announced his shock move from Mercedes to Ferrari ahead of the 2026 season, opinion was divided. Was he chasing one more championship? Riding out his career on a romantic adventure? Leaving the team that made him a seven-time champion for a project that might take years to bear fruit?

Three months into the season, the verdict is: none of the above. Hamilton is simply racing.

He sits P3 in the 2026 Drivers' Championship with 199 points. He's 103 points behind leader Kimi Antonelli — a mountain to climb, and realistically too far back to win the title this year. But he's ahead of McLaren's Lando Norris (186 pts) and two positions ahead of Ferrari teammate Charles Leclerc (179 pts).

Ferrari, meanwhile, sits P2 in the Constructors' Championship with 378 points — 160 behind the dominant Mercedes outfit. Hamilton hasn't won yet in 2026, but his consistency has been a key part of keeping Ferrari in the hunt.

The title may not arrive in Year One at Maranello. But at 41, Hamilton is proving that the doubters were wrong: there is still plenty of fuel in the tank.

### STORY 5: Cadillac Zero Points

**Long-Form Post:**
Cadillac F1 Team made history when it entered Formula 1 in 2026 as the sport's first American constructor in the modern era. Now, 15 races into their debut season, they're making a different kind of history — 0 championship points from 30 race starts.

The team, running Valtteri Bottas and Sergio Perez in its first year, has faced the brutal reality of being a new constructor in the world's most technically demanding motorsport. Bottas' best finish has been P13 at the Chinese Grand Prix. Perez has managed P14 as his top result.

Their most agonizing moment came at Monaco. Perez crossed the finish line in P10 — what would have been Cadillac's first-ever championship point, and a huge milestone for American F1. Then came the post-race penalty: out of position on a restart, Perez was dropped five places to P15, and the point evaporated.

That Monaco moment has become something of a symbol for the team's season: promise, then heartbreak, then regrouping. There are 8 races left in 2026. The first American F1 point is still out there, waiting.
