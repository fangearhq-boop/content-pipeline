Verstappen's Zero-Win 2026: The Crisis That Changed Everything

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | October 3, 2026

In 12 seasons of Formula 1 racing, Max Verstappen had never gone a full year without winning a race. Not once. Not even close.

Fifteen races into 2026, with eight rounds remaining, that record is gone — and the four-time world champion is in territory no one expected to see him occupy.

Verstappen sits sixth in the Drivers' Championship with 163 points, 139 behind leader Kimi Antonelli. His best result of the season: second place at the Azerbaijan Grand Prix, where he pushed race winner George Russell to the final metres before being held at bay by 0.196 seconds. It was the closest Verstappen has come to the top step in a calendar year that has tested his patience, composure, and the loyalty of every Red Bull engineer working nights on the RB22.

The RB22 Problem

The story of Verstappen's 2026 season is largely the story of a car that arrived broken and has only partially been fixed.

The RB22 has been reported to be running 9 to 10 kilograms overweight — a significant handicap in a sport where tenths of a second separate champions from also-rans. The car has struggled particularly in high-speed corners, where aerodynamic balance is most critical and where Mercedes, McLaren, and Ferrari have found decisive advantages.

For a team that has built four consecutive championship-winning cars (2021-2024), the RB22 represents a painful regression. The pivot to the 2026 regulations — with their new power unit framework and revised aerodynamic rules — appears to have caught Red Bull at a moment when their development trajectory hit a wall.

Verstappen has extracted what he can from the package. There have been podiums, points, and moments of the raw brilliance that made him the most dominant driver of his generation. But the infrastructure underneath him has repeatedly failed to deliver the car he needs.

A Streak That Stood for 12 Years

Context matters here. Verstappen won his first grand prix in May 2016 at Barcelona — the youngest race winner in F1 history at the time. Every full season he competed in from that point forward included at least one victory: 2017, 2018, 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025. In some of those years, he won one or two. In his peak seasons, he won 19 in a single campaign.

There has never been a full season without a win — until now.

Red Bull sits fourth in the Constructors' Championship with 263 points, behind Mercedes (538), Ferrari (378), and McLaren (306). The gap to Mercedes is 275 points. For a team that spent three seasons looking like they could never be caught, that number is staggering.

Malaysia and Beyond

There is hope. Verstappen led first practice at Sepang International Circuit on Friday — a positive signal heading into Sunday's Malaysia GP (03:00 ET). Sepang's layout, with its long straight and multiple overtaking opportunities, should theoretically suit a driver of Verstappen's calibre if the car can deliver qualifying pace.

But 15 races of results say hope must be earned. Verstappen has led sessions before. He has had weekends where pace seemed to materialise, only to see it evaporate in race trim. The four-time champion knows this better than anyone.

The winless record is painful. It is also, ultimately, secondary. What matters to Verstappen now is building a platform for 2027, when Red Bull hopes to arrive with a corrected, competitive package. Salvaging Malaysia and Singapore would at minimum close 2026 with something other than the longest winless run of his career.

What's Next

The Malaysia GP at Sepang races Sunday, October 4 at 03:00 ET. After Sepang, F1 heads to Singapore (October 9-11), where Antonelli has his first realistic championship clinch window. Verstappen needs wins, and needs them soon. Eight rounds remain to break a 2026 drought that has already made history — for all the wrong reasons.
