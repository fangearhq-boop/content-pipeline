Cadillac F1's Zero-Point Debut: America's Dream Still Waiting

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | October 3, 2026

When Cadillac F1 Team lined up on the grid in Melbourne for the 2026 Australian Grand Prix, it was the culmination of years of effort, lobbying, legal battles, and a firm belief that American motorsport had a place in Formula 1 as a full constructor — not just a team that branded someone else's machinery.

Fifteen races later, the points column still reads zero.

Valtteri Bottas and Sergio Perez — an experienced pairing by any measure — have combined for 30 race starts and found the scoring zone precisely never. Their best finishes remain in the lower midfield: Bottas peaked at P13 at the Chinese Grand Prix, Perez at P14. They are last in the Constructors' Championship among 10 teams.

And yet, the story of Cadillac's 2026 season is not quite as bleak as those numbers suggest.

The Monaco Moment

Ask anyone on the Cadillac pit wall what they think about first when the 2026 season is mentioned, and the answer will almost certainly be Monaco.

Sergio Perez crossed the finish line at the Circuit de Monaco in tenth place. In Formula 1, the top ten finishers score championship points. P10 earns one point — a single, modest point. But for Cadillac, that point would have been historic: the first championship point scored by an American F1 constructor in the modern era of the sport. The team was preparing to celebrate.

Then came the post-race investigation. Stewards ruled that Perez had been out of position on a safety car restart — a technical infringement that carries a five-place grid drop at the next race, retroactively applied as a time penalty when the incident occurs during a race in progress. His tenth place became fifteenth. The point was gone.

The team issued a brief statement accepting the stewards' decision. What the statement didn't capture was the silence in the Cadillac garage when the verdict landed.

First Team, Tough Year

Cadillac entered F1 as the sport's 11th team and its first American constructor in the modern era. The regulatory framework for new entrants is deliberately strict: teams must meet stringent technical standards, financial guarantees, and operational requirements. There are no shortcuts, and there is no budget cap exemption for being new.

Perez and Bottas bring decades of collective experience to the cockpit — Perez has scored race wins and podiums in F1 for over a decade; Bottas raced for Mercedes during their most dominant era. Neither is a development driver. Both understand how to extract performance from an imperfect car.

The car, however, has been the constraint. Cadillac is still learning the nuances of F1 manufacturing at the highest level — the margin between a car that scores points and a car that doesn't in this field can be as small as a tenth of a second per lap. Compound that over a 56-lap race and the gap to the point-scorers grows quickly.

Qualifying pace tells part of the story: Perez and Bottas have each set a joint-best qualifying result of P18. On a 20-car grid, P18 means a very long afternoon.

Eight Races to Make History

With eight rounds remaining in 2026, the first Cadillac championship point is still out there. The Malaysia GP at Sepang, the Singapore GP, Japan, and the late-season flyaways offer circuits where midfield variability — safety cars, retirements, strategy gambles — can occasionally produce surprise results for the cars at the tail of the field.

No one inside the team is conceding defeat. Both drivers are motivated, the team is learning rapidly, and the 2027 car is already in development with 15 races of hard-won data baked into its design. The real prize — a competitive Cadillac competing at the front of the midfield — is probably 12 to 18 months away.

But the immediate prize, the one the whole team is racing for right now, is still that one point. The first American F1 constructor point in the modern era. It is waiting somewhere in the final eight races of 2026.

What's Next

Cadillac races at the Malaysia GP at Sepang on October 4 (start 03:00 ET) before the Singapore GP Sprint weekend (October 9-11). Eight rounds. Thirty drivers. One point is out there. The American dream hasn't ended — it's just taking a little longer than advertised.
