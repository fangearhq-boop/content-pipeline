Malaysia GP Race Day: F1 Returns to Sepang After Nine Years

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | October 3, 2026

Nine years is a long time in Formula 1. The last time the sport raced at Sepang International Circuit, Sebastian Vettel won for Ferrari on a sweltering afternoon in 2017. Four world championships have been won since. Cars have been redesigned from the ground up. Most of the current grid was barely old enough to hold a racing licence.

Tomorrow, October 4, 2026 — F1 comes back.

The 2026 Bahrain Grand Prix at Sepang (Round 16 of 23) goes racing at 15:00 local time, 03:00 ET on Sunday morning, and it arrives with storylines stacked high enough to fill the paddock twice over.

The Circuit

Sepang International Circuit is 5.543 kilometres of sweeping curves, tight infield sections, and one of the most demanding tyre-degradation tests on the F1 calendar. Fifteen corners. Fifty-six race laps. Punishing heat and humidity that can destroy rubber in a matter of stints if a team gets its strategy wrong.

The circuit was purpose-built for Formula 1 in the 1990s and quickly established itself as one of the sport's iconic venues. Its wide pit straight, the flowing Turns 1-4 sequence, and the slow but critical Turn 15 hairpin create a circuit where multiple racing lines exist and overtaking is genuinely possible — not just in theory.

With the 2026 cars generating different aerodynamic loads and power unit characteristics than the previous generation, Sepang's high-speed corners and long straights should reward efficiency over outright peak speed.

Verstappen and the Drought

Max Verstappen led Friday practice at Sepang. For a driver who has scored zero race victories through 15 rounds of the 2026 championship, that one-session piece of good news matters more than it normally would.

Verstappen finished P2 at the previous round in Azerbaijan — his best result of the 2026 season. He pushed eventual race winner George Russell all the way to the end, closing to within fractions of a second in the final laps, but could not find a way through. The four-time champion is averaging a level of performance that would comfortably score wins in most seasons. In 2026, something has consistently been missing at the crucial moment.

Malaysia could change that. Sepang's characteristics suit cars with strong straight-line speed, and if Red Bull can deliver a clean weekend — clean strategy, clean pit stops, clean lap in qualifying — a first 2026 win is achievable.

Championship Stakes

For Kimi Antonelli, tomorrow is about keeping the gap to George Russell intact or growing it. Antonelli leads the championship by 66 points with eight rounds remaining after Malaysia. A comfortable finish — P4, P5, somewhere in the points — while Russell wins does manageable damage. A win of his own would silence any suggestion that Singapore's sprint weekend will be anything other than a coronation march.

Russell won Baku in dominant fashion. Two weeks later, Sepang presents him with another shot at cutting the gap. He needs a swing of 67 points to draw level — and every race he doesn't win makes it harder.

Hamilton, Ferrari, and McLaren will also be in the mix. Norris in particular has shown strong race pace in warm-weather conditions, and Leclerc's Ferrari tends to thrive on circuits where rear traction and smooth power delivery are rewarded.

What's Next

Race start is Sunday October 4 at 03:00 ET. The result shapes the championship picture heading into Singapore — F1's first-ever Sprint weekend at Marina Bay (October 9-11). Verstappen needs a win, Antonelli needs points, and Malaysia's return to the calendar after nine years deserves a race that matches its history. Set the alarm. Sepang is back.
