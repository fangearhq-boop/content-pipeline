Hamilton's Ferrari Move Is Working: P3 and Keeping the Title Dream Alive

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | October 3, 2026

The question heading into 2026 was whether Lewis Hamilton had made the right call. After more than a decade at Mercedes — seven world championships, a record-equalling 103 race victories — he had taken the most significant leap of his career: signing with Ferrari for what is almost certainly the final chapter of his driving days.

Fifteen races into his debut season in red, the answer is looking increasingly like yes.

Hamilton sits third in the 2026 Drivers' Championship with 199 points, trailing leader Kimi Antonelli (302) by 103 and George Russell (236) by 37. He is ahead of McLaren's Lando Norris (186) and above Ferrari teammate Charles Leclerc (179). Ferrari holds second in the Constructors' Championship with 378 points — a full 160 behind the dominant Mercedes outfit, but firmly clear of McLaren in third (306).

What the Numbers Show

P3 in the Drivers' Championship at this stage of the season represents genuine performance, not circumstance. Hamilton has not been gifted points by retirements ahead of him. He has outscored Leclerc, one of the fastest qualifying drivers on the grid, across the first 15 rounds.

For Ferrari, Hamilton's arrival has coincided with one of their stronger seasons in recent memory. The car is fast enough to fight for pole position at certain circuits and to threaten race wins when the top two teams — Mercedes and McLaren — have operational difficulties. Hamilton's experience and tyre management expertise have helped the team extract consistent performance rather than the boom-or-bust weekends that sometimes defined their pre-Hamilton years.

The math still makes a 2026 title nearly impossible. With eight rounds remaining and 103 points separating him from Antonelli, Hamilton would need to outscore the young Mercedes driver by roughly 13 points per race — essentially winning every Grand Prix while Antonelli finishes outside the top three every time. That is not happening.

Building Toward the Future

What Hamilton is doing, however, is more important for Ferrari's longer-term trajectory. A driver of his calibre — his feedback systems, his ability to find setup solutions under pressure, his relationship with engineers — is the kind of catalyst that shapes a team's development for years, not just months.

There are eight races left in 2026. Hamilton going on a run of wins is not outside the realm of possibility; Ferrari's car has shown the raw pace to deliver results at circuits that favour their characteristics. If he finishes the season with two or three victories, he will have achieved exactly what most neutral observers hoped for: a competitive, dignified final chapter that also genuinely helped build Ferrari into a title contender for 2027.

Leclerc, for his part, is not far behind. At 179 points, the Monaco-born Monegasque driver is still mathematically in the title hunt and has outperformed expectations in qualifying. The two drivers appear to be functioning as teammates rather than rivals — a healthy sign for a team with championship aspirations.

What's Next

Hamilton and Ferrari head to the Malaysia GP at Sepang (October 4, race start 03:00 ET) before Singapore's historic Sprint weekend (October 9-11). If Hamilton can keep the podium streak intact through this Asian swing and remain within striking distance of Antonelli's 302, the final months of 2026 could produce some of the most compelling racing of the season — a seven-time champion in a Ferrari, chasing history one more time.
