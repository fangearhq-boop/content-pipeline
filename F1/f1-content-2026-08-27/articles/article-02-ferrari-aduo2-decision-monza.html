Ferrari's ADUO-2 Decision Could Change the 2026 F1 Championship This Week

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | August 27, 2026

Ferrari is days away from making one of the most consequential engine decisions of the entire 2026 Formula 1 season.

Before the Monza entry deadline, the Scuderia must confirm whether its ADUO-2 upgraded power unit will debut at the Italian Grand Prix on September 4-6. The second phase of Ferrari's season-long engine recovery program targets approximately 15 additional horsepower — and Ferrari's own simulations indicate a gain of around 0.2 seconds per lap at the Temple of Speed.

The timing could not be more significant. Kimi Antonelli will start from the back of the Monza grid with a penalty engine. Lewis Hamilton — 53 points behind the championship leader — could line up at the front with Ferrari's most powerful specification of 2026.

What ADUO-2 Changes

The ADUO program is Ferrari's structured response to the straight-line deficit that held them back in the first half of the season. ADUO-1 — the first phase — targeted combustion improvements. ADUO-2 centres on a completely redesigned turbocharger, together with further combustion chamber refinements.

Combined, the two-phase ADUO recovery targets approximately 30 horsepower across the full season. At Monza — the circuit where raw straight-line power matters more than almost anywhere else on the calendar — the additional output from ADUO-2 is expected to translate directly into lap time.

Ferrari sources described the upgrade to various outlets as potentially the biggest single-session power unit step of their 2026 campaign. One technical report described the potential as sufficient to "break" the competitive order at Monza, shifting Ferrari's qualifying threat from midfield to the front row.

The Championship Math

Antonelli currently leads on 242 points, with Russell and Hamilton tied at 183. The championship leader's Monza grid penalty — a full power unit change placing him effectively last — creates a once-in-a-season opportunity for his rivals.

Hamilton winning Monza outright, with Antonelli charging through the field to perhaps seventh or eighth, could deliver a 10-to-18-point swing in a single afternoon. Do that across two or three consecutive races and the gap closes to a genuinely nervous margin for Mercedes.

Hamilton himself has been vocal about his renewed belief in Ferrari's trajectory, pointing to the team's resilience in maintaining competitiveness despite an estimated 0.3-0.4 second straight-line disadvantage earlier in the year. ADUO-2, if confirmed, is the tangible proof that the gap has been closed — and potentially eliminated.

What's Next

The confirmation from Ferrari is expected before the end of this week. Once the entry deadline passes, the grid for Monza begins to take shape around whatever specification each team has declared.

If ADUO-2 is confirmed, the Italian Grand Prix becomes simultaneously Ferrari's biggest opportunity of the season and Antonelli's biggest challenge. Two storylines — Italian power, Italian pressure — playing out at the fastest circuit in Formula 1.

This week's decision matters. The championship may feel its effects for the rest of the season.
