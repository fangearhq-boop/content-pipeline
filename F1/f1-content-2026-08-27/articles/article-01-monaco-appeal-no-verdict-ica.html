Monaco Grand Prix Appeal Still Unresolved: FIA Court Won't Rule for Two More Weeks

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | August 27, 2026

Pierre Gasly keeps his Monaco Grand Prix podium — for now.

The FIA International Court of Appeal heard arguments from McLaren and Red Bull on Tuesday in Paris, but the four-judge panel has not reached a decision. Per multiple reports, a verdict is not expected for another two to three weeks, which puts the ruling somewhere around September 8 to 15.

That means the Monaco GP result remains officially in limbo, with Gasly classified third, Isack Hadjar in fourth, and Oscar Piastri in fifth — until the court finally speaks.

How We Got Here

At the Monaco Grand Prix, stewards penalised Gasly twice for exceeding the pit lane speed limit. His recorded speeds — 60.1 km/h and 60.4 km/h — were deemed violations, and the resulting two five-second penalties initially dropped him from P3 to P7.

The case unravelled when it emerged that the distances between timing loops in Monaco's pit lane had been measured incorrectly. Those incorrect measurements formed the basis of the speed calculations used to issue the penalties. With accurate data applied, Gasly's speeds no longer constituted infractions — and the stewards rescinded both penalties, reinstating him to third place.

That decision prompted McLaren and Red Bull to appeal. McLaren's Piastri had briefly stood on the Monaco podium during the post-race ceremony before being demoted to fifth; Red Bull's Hadjar had been elevated to fourth. Both parties argued to the ICA that the reinstatement was not handled correctly under the sporting regulations.

What the Court Must Decide

The central question is not simply whether Gasly's speeds were legal. The court must determine whether the process by which the stewards reversed their own original decision was procedurally valid. It is a nuanced legal argument, not purely a factual one — which explains why the panel needed more time than a straightforward case would require.

The outcome has championship implications beyond the Monaco podium itself. Championship points currently awarded to Gasly, Hadjar, and Piastri based on current classifications could shift if the ICA overturns the reinstatement and restores the original penalised result.

What's Next

The two-to-three-week timeline places the ruling squarely within the Italian Grand Prix weekend window, though the court is unlikely to deliberately time its judgment to coincide with a race. The verdict will be issued when the panel is ready, and all parties will be notified simultaneously.

Until then, Gasly officially has his Monaco podium. McLaren and Red Bull wait. The championship standings carry an asterisk that only a courtroom can remove.
