Las Vegas GP Preview: Norris and Verstappen Battle for P2 on the Strip

By Ryan Calloway | November 12, 2026

With Kimi Antonelli crowned 2026 Formula 1 World Champion, the Las Vegas Grand Prix on November 22 carries a different kind of pressure — a fierce battle for second place in the Drivers' standings between Lando Norris, Max Verstappen, and George Russell.

The Title Is Won — The P2 Fight Is Very Much Alive

Kimi Antonelli wrapped up the 2026 Drivers' Championship at the Brazilian Grand Prix, with Mercedes also clinching the Constructors' title. That settled the biggest question of the season. What it has done, in a way that sometimes happens in Formula 1, is redirect the entire competitive conversation toward a different fight — the battle for the runner-up position.

Lando Norris, fresh from winning the Brazilian GP in difficult wet-to-dry-to-wet conditions, leads that fight heading into Las Vegas. Max Verstappen, who delivered a stunning charge from 21st to second in Sao Paulo, is right behind him. George Russell, fourth in Brazil, remains in mathematical contention. These are three of the most gifted drivers in the world, all operating at peak intensity with something meaningful to prove.

Second place in the Drivers' Championship is not a trophy. But for Norris and Verstappen especially, the pride and prestige attached to finishing as championship runner-up — ahead of the other — is a powerful motivator across the remaining rounds.

What Las Vegas Means for Each Contender

Norris has momentum. His Sao Paulo win was the kind of performance that reminds the paddock what a fully committed Norris looks like — controlled under pressure, strategic in his decisions, clinical in execution. Las Vegas suits drivers who can manage tire life across a hot, smooth surface, and McLaren's strengths this season make them legitimate favorites on the Strip circuit.

Verstappen's approach will be to attack from the front. Red Bull have shown this season that their pace over a single lap remains exceptional, and in Las Vegas — where qualifying position often determines race outcome on a circuit where overtaking is limited — that pace could be decisive. His recovery drive in Brazil was breathtaking in its ambition; Las Vegas demands something more methodical, and Red Bull will have refined their setup accordingly.

Russell and Mercedes carry the advantage of having already secured the Constructors' title. That takes pressure off strategically, but it also raises questions about motivation. Russell is a racer — he will not cruise. But the team's focus may now shift toward data gathering and preparation for 2027.

The Circuit and What to Expect

The Las Vegas Grand Prix circuit cuts through the heart of the Strip, with its distinctive high-speed sections and the unique atmospheric pressure of racing past lit hotels and casinos in the desert night. The race has quickly established itself as one of the most visually spectacular events on the calendar, drawing enormous viewership and creating its own distinct energy in the paddock.

Technically, teams tend to run medium-to-high downforce configurations here, balancing the requirements of the long straight sections against the demanding corner sequences that separate consistent racers from adventurous ones. Tire degradation is typically moderate, which means strategy is a factor — but not necessarily the defining one.

What's Next

Las Vegas is the penultimate round of the 2026 F1 season, followed by Qatar and Abu Dhabi. With three races remaining after Sao Paulo, the P2 battle will likely come down to small margins — a qualifying lap here, a strategy call there. Norris has the momentum. Verstappen has the talent to change the narrative at any time.

The champion is crowned. The fight, though, is very much alive. Las Vegas will deliver.
