Piastri and Webber Part Ways After Seven Years in Mutual Management Split

By Marcus Cole | November 12, 2026

Oscar Piastri and long-time manager Mark Webber have confirmed they have ended their seven-year partnership in a mutual split, closing a chapter that guided the Australian from Formula 2 champion to McLaren race winner.

Seven Years Together — Now a New Chapter

The announcement came on November 9, 2026. Oscar Piastri and Mark Webber, whose management partnership began in 2019, have parted ways. Both sides described the split as mutual, and the language used — from Webber's statement to Piastri's response — carried the tone of a chapter closing respectfully rather than bitterly.

Seven years in F1 management terms is a long time. Webber, the former Red Bull Grand Prix driver, took on Piastri at a critical period of the Australian's development — when he was coming through the junior formula ranks with Alpine's driver academy and needed guidance on how to navigate a sport that is as much political as it is athletic. Under Webber's stewardship, Piastri won the Formula 2 championship, fought off an infamous contractual dispute to secure his McLaren seat, and developed into a Grand Prix winner.

That is a remarkable list of milestones. The partnership worked.

The Context Behind the Split

The groundwork for the separation was laid earlier this year. Ahead of the 2026 season, Webber stepped back from his trackside management role, shifting the emphasis of his involvement toward commercial responsibilities. The restructuring brought in former race engineer Pedro Matos and mental performance coach Emma Murray as more prominent figures in Piastri's immediate support circle.

As the season progressed, it became clear that the reorganization had effectively changed what the relationship looked like in practice. Webber's influence, once central and ever-present at the circuit, had diminished not through conflict but through natural evolution. The mutual split, in some ways, formalized what had already become a new reality.

Piastri has not yet announced what his management structure will look like going forward. That question will be answered in due course — but the focus now is on the final three races of the 2026 season.

What It Means for Piastri

At the point of this announcement, Oscar Piastri sits seventh in the 2026 Drivers' Championship standings. It has been a season of mixed results for the McLaren driver — moments of real quality undermined by reliability issues and a team that has at times been outpaced by Antonelli's dominant Mercedes.

Heading into the final three rounds of the season and then into 2027 contract negotiations in a new context, Piastri will want to perform. His talent is not in question — he has shown what he can do when the car and circumstances align. The management transition, however handled, will not change that.

What's Next

Piastri heads to Las Vegas with new management conversations ahead of him and three races to close out a complicated season. The long-term story here will be worth following: how he handles this transition, who steps in to guide his commercial and strategic interests, and how that shapes the next phase of a career that still carries enormous potential.

Mark Webber's legacy in this partnership is secure. He took a young Australian driver and helped shape him into an F1 race winner. Whatever comes next for Oscar Piastri, that foundation was built together.
