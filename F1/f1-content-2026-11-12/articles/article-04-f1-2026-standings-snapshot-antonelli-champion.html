F1 2026 Standings: Antonelli Champion, Norris and Verstappen Fight for Runner-Up

By Ryan Calloway | November 12, 2026

With three races remaining in the 2026 Formula 1 season, Kimi Antonelli is confirmed World Champion and Mercedes are Constructors' Champions. The battles that remain — particularly the fight for second between Norris and Verstappen — give the season's final rounds genuine weight.

Antonelli: Champion at 19

Kimi Antonelli's 2026 World Championship is one of the most significant results in Formula 1 in years. The Italian teenager became the second driver in history to win the title at 19 — a generational achievement that sets the tone for what could be a decade-long run of dominance if Mercedes continues to support him with a competitive car.

Post-Brazil standings place Antonelli at 390 points — a commanding lead of 111 over second-placed Max Verstappen at 279. Those are the numbers of a champion who dominated the season with consistency and nerve. His title was confirmed in Brazil when he finished fifth, letting the mathematics do the work.

The P2 Battle: Norris vs. Verstappen vs. Russell

Lando Norris enters Las Vegas leading the battle for second in the Drivers' standings. His Brazilian GP win was the kind of dominant, composed performance that makes him difficult to discount anywhere in the final three races. Norris has been the driver most capable of troubling Antonelli at his peak this season, even if the results have not always matched the pace.

Verstappen's deficit to Norris is real, but his comeback drive from 21st to second in Sao Paulo is a reminder that he will never stop fighting. With three races left across circuits that suit Red Bull's setup, he has time and opportunity. Russell, meanwhile, cannot be entirely ruled out — though the gap to close is substantial.

The P2 fight matters because these are three of the defining drivers of this era, and the final order on the championship table will be referenced for decades.

Hamilton's 400th Race — A Milestone in Difficult Circumstances

Lewis Hamilton's 400th Formula 1 Grand Prix at the Brazilian GP was a milestone that deserved a better result. The seven-time World Champion, in his second season at Ferrari following his high-profile move from Mercedes, finished a lapped ninth — his team's SF-26 lacking the pace to compete with the front-runners in Sao Paulo's demanding conditions.

That Hamilton has started 400 Grands Prix says everything about his longevity and competitive drive. That the milestone arrived in those circumstances says something about where Ferrari currently stand relative to the top teams. The investigation into the SF-26's performance issues continues, with the team hoping to address the deficit before the season ends.

What's Next

Las Vegas (November 22), Qatar (November 29), and Abu Dhabi (December 6) are the final three races. The championship is settled. The fight for every other position on the leaderboard is not. For Norris and Verstappen, this is their chance to claim the runner-up prize — and neither will give it without a fight.
