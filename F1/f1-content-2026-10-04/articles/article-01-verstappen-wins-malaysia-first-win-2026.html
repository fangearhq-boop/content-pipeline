Verstappen Wins Malaysia — First F1 Victory of 2026 in Dramatic Rain Race

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | October 4, 2026

Fifteen rounds into the 2026 Formula 1 season, Max Verstappen had done something he had never done before — gone an entire calendar year without winning a Grand Prix. The four-time world champion had podiums. He had fast laps. He had races where Red Bull looked genuinely competitive. But the win kept not coming.

Then came Malaysia.

The 2026 Gulf Air Bahrain Grand Prix at Sepang International Circuit — F1's return to Malaysia after a nine-year absence — was the kind of race that tends to favour champions. Torrential rain before the start caused a delay. Power unit failure warnings on multiple cars created formation lap chaos. Multiple safety car deployments scrambled strategy across the grid. It was, in every sense, a race where survival and judgment mattered as much as raw pace.

Verstappen delivered both. Threading through the carnage while others made mistakes, the Red Bull driver led the decisive laps to claim victory — crossing the line 2.307 seconds ahead of Kimi Antonelli in the Mercedes.

A Season-Long Drought Ends

The significance of the win extends well beyond a single race result. Verstappen had arrived in Malaysia under pressure that was unusual for a four-time champion — an entire season without visiting the top step, while a 20-year-old teammate of his title rival dominated the championship. Red Bull's RB22 had spent much of 2026 struggling with aerodynamic efficiency in the new era, overweight and underperforming in high-speed sections.

In Sepang, in the wet and the chaos, the machine limitations mattered less. Verstappen drove the race of a man determined to remind the paddock exactly who he is.

Behind him, Antonelli took 18 championship points for second place — a strong result for the 20-year-old Mercedes driver who remains firmly in command of the title race. Lewis Hamilton completed the podium in a remarkable drive for Ferrari, recovering from P18 mid-race after a strategy gamble to surge through the field. Charles Leclerc added fourth for the Scuderia, and Red Bull's Isack Hadjar rounded out the top five.

Russell's Retirement Casts a Shadow

The other major storyline from Malaysia was what didn't happen. George Russell — the man Antonelli needs to beat for the championship — stopped behind the safety car in the closing stages, collecting no points from a race where he needed them badly.

The championship gap, already 66 points before the race, has now extended to 84. With seven rounds remaining, the title fight is entering a decisive phase — and the numbers are running out for Russell.

What's Next

Formula 1 travels directly to Singapore for Round 17 — the Singapore Grand Prix, held October 9-11 at Marina Bay Street Circuit. For the first time in the event's history, the Singapore weekend will feature a Sprint race, adding eight extra championship points to a title fight that just shifted significantly toward Kimi Antonelli.

Verstappen, meanwhile, will carry momentum from Malaysia into one of the most unforgiving street circuits on the calendar. If Red Bull can bottle whatever worked in Sepang, Singapore might see the four-time champion back at the sharp end again.
