Antonelli 84 Points Clear After Malaysia — The Title Is in His Hands

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | October 4, 2026

The 2026 Formula 1 World Championship took a decisive turn in Malaysia on Sunday, and the numbers now tell a story that is very hard to write out of.

Kimi Antonelli finished second in the chaotic Gulf Air Bahrain Grand Prix at Sepang, collecting 18 points while his main championship rival, George Russell, retired without scoring. The 84-point lead Antonelli now holds over Russell — with only seven rounds remaining — puts the youngest potential world champion in Formula 1 history firmly in the driving seat.

The Numbers After Malaysia

Kimi Antonelli: 320 championship points.

George Russell: 236 championship points.

Gap: 84 points.

Rounds remaining: 7 (Singapore through the season finale).

The maximum possible points remaining for Russell — including the Singapore Sprint bonus of eight points — is approximately 183. For Russell to overtake Antonelli, he would need to win every remaining event while Antonelli scores a handful of points or fewer. That is the only mathematical path.

Antonelli, conversely, can afford to finish sixth or better in most remaining races while Russell wins everything. The margin is that significant.

How Antonelli Built This Lead

Antonelli arrived in Malaysia already holding a commanding 66-point advantage, having led the championship since the early weeks of the season. The 20-year-old Mercedes driver has been the story of 2026 — a teenager who stepped into the most pressure-filled seat in the sport at the start of the year and proceeded to dominate it.

His season has not been perfect. There have been qualifying errors, a dramatic Baku crash, and moments of the inexperience that comes with being a first-year Formula 1 driver. But his race-day composure, his tyre management, and his ability to convert strong starting positions into points have been consistent enough to build what is now the largest championship lead in the second half of the 2026 season.

Malaysia only added to it. P2 in genuine chaos, banking points while the gap grew.

What's Next

The Singapore Grand Prix — Round 17 — arrives this coming weekend, October 9-11. Marina Bay Street Circuit will host F1's first-ever Sprint weekend, with eight extra championship points available on Saturday before the Grand Prix itself on Sunday.

The title clinch scenarios become clearer in Singapore. If Antonelli wins the Sprint and the Grand Prix while Russell scores nothing, the title gap approaches a level where mathematics would be all but settled. At 20 years old, Kimi Antonelli would become the youngest Formula 1 world champion in the sport's history.

After Malaysia, that outcome looks increasingly likely. It is now, more than at any point this season, Antonelli's title to lose.
