Antonelli Leads by 81 — The Math That Could Make Singapore His Coronation

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | September 20, 2026

Drivers' Championship

Kimi Antonelli holds 292 championship points. George Russell holds 211. The gap is 81, with nine rounds remaining. The title is Antonelli's to lose — a phrase that has been said about other drivers in other seasons, not always correctly. The difference here is that the math is extraordinarily difficult for Russell to reverse, and Baku is the first of several dominoes that could make Singapore the clinching venue.

Here is how the Singapore scenario works, precisely.

The Baku + Singapore Calculation

Antonelli scores maximum points at Baku: 26 for the win, 8 for the sprint, plus the one point for fastest lap, for a total of 35. Russell scores zero at Baku — not an inconceivable result on a street circuit that has retired championship contenders before. Antonelli's lead grows from 81 to 116 points.

At the Singapore Sprint, worth 8 points maximum, Antonelli wins again. Russell again scores zero. The lead reaches 124. There are now 26 points available from the main Singapore race.

Antonelli needs a lead of 26 points or more entering Sunday to clinch regardless of what Russell does. He would be 1 point short on the most optimistic scenario after the sprint. A single point at the Singapore Sprint — 8th place or better — combined with the assumptions above, and Antonelli would be champion before the Singapore main event begins.

The realistic read from championship analysts is that this scenario — Russell scoring zero points across two race weekends — is extremely unlikely. Russell finished second in the championship in 2024 and has been Antonelli's closest consistent competitor all season. He does not retire from Baku every year. He does not score zero in Singapore every year.

The More Probable Path

F1 scenario modeling — accounting for Russell's average points scoring rate, the remaining calendar, and Antonelli's scoring consistency — puts the most probable clinch location at Interlagos, with approximately three rounds remaining in the season. That is still a championship that is functionally over. The remaining nine rounds are a formality for the purposes of any realistic reversal scenario.

What makes Baku consequential for the championship is not the mathematics of a Singapore clinch, which require a specific sequence of unlikely events. What makes Baku consequential is its ability to produce exactly those unlikely events. The circuit has no patience for assumptions about dominant leaders. Charles Leclerc's tire explosion while leading in 2021. Red Bull's puncture drama during a safety car restart. These are not isolated incidents — they are Baku's character, built into every lap of a circuit that passes a medieval fortress wall at 300 kilometers per hour.

Antonelli's Season in Context

Antonelli has eight wins in 14 rounds. His most recent victory came at the brand-new Madring circuit, where he beat Russell to extend his lead. He has six pole positions and 12 podium finishes — a statistical profile that is historically strong for a driver competing in just his second full season. At 20 years old, he would be among the youngest champions in F1 history if the title is clinched this year.

The 81-point lead reflects a season of few errors and frequent excellence. It reflects a Mercedes car that has been the class of the field in 2026, providing Antonelli the platform for a performance that has rendered the drivers' championship a procession rather than a contest. But Baku, more than any other circuit on the calendar, tests what a 81-point lead is actually worth when the safety car comes out and the field stacks behind it with five laps to go.

What's Next

The Azerbaijan Grand Prix takes place Saturday, September 26. After Baku, the Singapore Grand Prix follows on October 5 at Marina Bay Street Circuit. If Antonelli wins Baku and maintains his margin into Singapore, the championship conversation will intensify around a circuit that has its own history of unpredictable races. For now, the math is clear. The only question is whether Baku lets the season play out the way the standings suggest it should.
