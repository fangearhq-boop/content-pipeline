6 Days to Baku — F1's First Saturday Race on the Streets of Azerbaijan

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | September 20, 2026

Azerbaijan Grand Prix

Six days. The Formula 1 Qatar Airways Azerbaijan Grand Prix is this Saturday, September 26 — and for the first time in Baku's history as a Formula 1 venue, the race will not take place on a Sunday. The reason is specific and significant: September 27 is Azerbaijan Remembrance Day, the national day of mourning for military personnel who died during the Second Nagorno-Karabakh War. The republic of Azerbaijan does not hold large public events on that date, and the FIA and Formula 1 responded by shifting the entire weekend forward 24 hours.

The schedule change sounds procedural. On a street circuit where the margin between first and a wall is measured in centimeters, any variable that disrupts preparation rhythms carries meaning.

The Shifted Weekend

The weekend's structure: media day on Wednesday, FP1 and FP2 on Thursday, FP3 and qualifying on Friday, and the 51-lap grand prix on Saturday. Every team's logistics, engineer sleep schedules, simulator debrief windows, and travel plans are built around a Sunday race. A shift forward by 24 hours compresses the processing time between qualifying and race day, reduces the gap between FP2 data collection and qualifying strategy decisions, and creates a Saturday broadcast window that is unfamiliar to the European audience that has structured its F1-watching habits around Sunday afternoons.

None of that is catastrophic for any team. All of it is a marginal variable. On the Baku City Circuit, marginal variables accumulate.

What Baku Does to Championship Leads

The Baku City Circuit is 6.003 kilometers long. It contains the longest straight on any Formula 1 circuit in the world. It passes within centimeters of medieval castle walls. Its corners range from a hairpin tight enough to require full lock to high-speed sweepers where a car's rear stability is tested at over 300 kilometers per hour. It has produced some of the most chaotic races in recent F1 history, including safety cars, dramatic punctures, and championship leaders retiring from positions of apparent dominance.

Kimi Antonelli arrives with 81 points of lead over George Russell. That lead represents approximately five victories worth of cushion — a number that feels secure until the Baku safety car comes out with five laps remaining and the randomness of the restart creates an outcome nobody modeled. The circuit is not hostile to championship leaders by design. It is simply hostile to the assumption that results follow form.

Weather and Track Conditions

Forecasts for the Baku weekend show dry and sunny conditions throughout, with temperatures around 26 degrees Celsius dropping to approximately 24 degrees for the Saturday race. The drier conditions favor predictable tire behavior, though Baku's abrasive surface and long straights create their own degradation characteristics that teams spend considerable simulation time modeling.

What's Next

FP1 and FP2 begin Thursday, September 24. FP3 and qualifying are Friday, September 25. The race fires off Saturday, September 26. After Baku, the calendar moves to Singapore on October 5 — the circuit that, mathematically, could host Antonelli's championship clinch under the right conditions. Between now and Saturday, the teams set up for a weekend where the only certainty is that the Baku City Circuit will find a way to surprise someone.
