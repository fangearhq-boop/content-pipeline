Mercedes Leads Ferrari by 122 in the Constructors' — Is This Title Already Won?

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | September 20, 2026

Constructors' Championship

After the Italian Grand Prix, the Formula 1 constructors' championship looks like this: Mercedes first. Ferrari 122 points behind. McLaren 59 points behind Ferrari. Red Bull fourth with 204 points. Eight rounds remain after the Azerbaijan Grand Prix — approximately 450 points worth of scoring opportunities.

Do the mathematics. Even if Ferrari were to outscore Mercedes by 15 points every single remaining race — a scenario that would require Ferrari to consistently finish higher than a team that has dominated 2026 — the gap would close to roughly two races' worth of ground. It is, for all practical purposes, over.

How Mercedes Built the Gap

The W17 — the first car designed under F1's radical new technical regulations — proved to be exactly the kind of step forward that ended Mercedes' mid-cycle struggle from 2022 to 2025. The 2026 regulations rewrote the aerodynamic rulebook, emphasized active aerodynamics, and introduced a new power unit formula. Mercedes' early work on the new technical framework translated into a car that Kimi Antonelli and George Russell could deploy consistently at the front.

Ferrari and McLaren both had competitive cars at points during the first half of the season. Ferrari was strong at circuits favoring high downforce. McLaren, with Lando Norris as defending world champion, was a genuine threat through the opening rounds. But the consistency gap — the difference between a car that wins when it gets to a favorable circuit and a car that scores heavily everywhere — became the story of the second half.

Antonelli's eight wins from 14 rounds, combined with Russell's steady scoring in second and third positions, produced the kind of 1-2 accumulation that fills a constructors' points table quickly. Ferrari has Charles Leclerc and Lewis Hamilton — two of the fastest drivers in the field — but their pace has been inconsistent across circuit types, and the combined scoring has not matched Mercedes' rate.

The Ferrari-McLaren Battle

If the championship's first chapter belongs to Mercedes, the second chapter involves Ferrari and McLaren competing for what is now effectively the championship's runner-up position. Ferrari leads McLaren by 59 points — a gap that is significant but not insurmountable over eight rounds.

McLaren's constructors' hopes rest on Norris — now committed through 2030 after his extension signed in late August — and Oscar Piastri delivering consistent points while Ferrari's inconsistency at certain circuits creates opportunities. The next several rounds, including Baku, Singapore, and Austin, will define whether McLaren can make that gap genuinely competitive again.

For Ferrari, Baku represents a circuit that historically favors cars with straight-line speed and efficient tire management — characteristics the SF-26 has demonstrated at several earlier rounds. A strong Baku result could rebuild some of the constructors' buffer against McLaren's late-season push.

The Historical Frame

A 122-point constructors' lead with eight rounds remaining places this Mercedes season in the company of the most dominant constructors' campaigns in the modern era. The combination of a dominant driver battle — Antonelli's 81-point lead in the drivers' — and a 122-point constructors' gap represents a season that, barring extraordinary collapse, will define the first chapter of the 2026 regulation era. Whether Mercedes can sustain this advantage into 2027 is the next question. For 2026, the answer to the title question is already written.

What's Next

The Azerbaijan Grand Prix runs Saturday, September 26. The constructors' standings will update after each of the nine remaining rounds. Ferrari's Baku form — where straight-line speed matters — will indicate whether the gap against McLaren for second place is stable or eroding. For Mercedes, Baku is the next step in a season that has already reached its conclusion in everything but the paperwork.
