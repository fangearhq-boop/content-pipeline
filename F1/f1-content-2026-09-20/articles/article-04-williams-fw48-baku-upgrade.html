Williams Arrives in Baku With a New Car — But Sainz's Caution Is the Real Story

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | September 20, 2026

Williams Racing

Williams will bring what the team has described as an "almost entirely new car" to the Baku City Circuit — a comprehensive FW48 upgrade package that includes a new chassis and addresses what has been a season-long performance deficit. In six days, the package will be bolted together and Carlos Sainz and Alex Albon will take it onto one of the most demanding and unpredictable circuits on the Formula 1 calendar.

But Sainz, before a wheel has been turned in Azerbaijan, has issued a measured warning. The car will be faster. He is confident of that. The question is: faster relative to what?

The Season That Preceded This Upgrade

The 2026 season has been difficult for Williams from the moment the FW48 was unveiled. The car was significantly overweight at launch — a problem that immediately capped its performance ceiling regardless of aerodynamic characteristics. Delays in the car's development forced the team to miss the Barcelona shakedown, leaving them behind the development curve before the first race was run. In the opening rounds, Sainz and Albon scored five points finishes. Then the points stopped.

The team's best result of the 2026 season remains Albon's eighth place in Monaco — a street circuit where Williams' straight-line speed kept them competitive despite the car's fundamental limitations. Since then, the FW48 has qualified and finished between 15th and 17th at most events. The pace to match the rest of the midfield has not been there.

What the Baku Package Actually Is

The term "almost entirely new car" is Williams' own language, and it is not hyperbolic for the sake of headlines. The Baku upgrade represents a fundamentally redesigned aerodynamic concept — a B-spec car, by any reasonable definition, that addresses the overweight and aerodynamic efficiency issues that have hamstrung performance all season. The new chassis is a structural solution as much as a performance one, providing the engineers with a foundation to build a car that meets its design weight targets for the first time in 2026.

When the package was signed off, the performance models showed the kind of gains that would move Williams from the back of the midfield into a genuine Q2 and Q3 conversation. Those models were built on a version of the field that existed several months ago. Other teams have not been standing still.

Sainz's Caution and What It Means

Carlos Sainz delivering a measured pre-upgrade assessment is not a sign of pessimism. It is a sign of a driver who has been through enough Formula 1 development cycles to understand that absolute pace and relative pace are not the same number. The FW48 upgrade may represent the largest single-event performance step Williams has produced in years. If the rest of the midfield field has produced similar gains — and Aston Martin, Alpine, and Haas have all developed their 2026 cars aggressively — the grid position gain may be more modest than a clean-room comparison of the old and new car would suggest.

What Sainz is also doing, implicitly, is managing what comes after Baku. If the upgrade delivers Q1 exits instead of Q3 appearances, the expectations set by "almost entirely new car" language create a gap between narrative and result that is hard to manage. His caution protects the team from a story that reads as failure if the upgrade produces incremental rather than transformational results.

What's Next

The FW48 upgrade debuts at the Azerbaijan Grand Prix this Saturday, September 26. For Williams, the meaningful metrics are straightforward: a points finish in Baku would be the team's first score since Monaco and a validation of months of development work. With the team's focus already shifting toward the FW50 for 2027, the Baku result will define not just where the team ends 2026 but what the base concept for next year's car is built on. Sainz's caution is a reminder that in Formula 1, the car that impresses in the garage and the car that scores points on race day are evaluated by different standards.
