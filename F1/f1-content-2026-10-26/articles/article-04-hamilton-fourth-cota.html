Hamilton Finishes Fourth at COTA — The Veteran Delivers as Antonelli's Coronation Waits One More Week

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | October 26, 2026

Lewis Hamilton's composed fourth-place finish at the US Grand Prix was, perhaps inadvertently, the result that delayed his teammate's title celebration by one week.

There is a particular kind of irony in the fact that Lewis Hamilton — team principal, legend, and mentor in everything but title — was the reason Kimi Antonelli did not become Formula 1 world champion on Sunday in Texas.

Not through deliberate obstruction, but simply by finishing fourth rather than fifth. One position. Two points. The difference between Antonelli clinching the title at Circuit of the Americas and the young Mercedes driver having to wait one more race for his coronation in Mexico City.

Hamilton finished fourth at the 2026 United States Grand Prix. It was a strong, professional result. It was also, as a side effect, the reason the celebration is now scheduled for November 1 rather than October 25.

A Composed Performance From the Seven-Time Champion

Hamilton's drive at COTA was what you would expect from an elite driver at a circuit he knows intimately. He managed his tyres well, stayed out of trouble, and delivered a points finish that contributed twelve points to the Mercedes total. There was no drama, no incident, no controversy — just a polished performance from one of the sport's all-time greats.

For Hamilton, the 2026 season has been a study in supporting Antonelli while maintaining his own competitive identity. He has not won a race this season, but he has regularly finished in the top five. At his stage of career, those consistent points are still valuable contributions to the team's constructors' standing.

The Teammates Narrative

The pairing of Hamilton and Antonelli at Mercedes has been one of the more fascinating inter-team dynamics of the modern era. Hamilton is a seven-time world champion; Antonelli is a 20-year-old about to become the youngest champion in history. The contrast is generational, and yet by most accounts they have coexisted constructively within the team environment.

Hamilton has spoken publicly about Antonelli's talent in positive terms. The young driver appears to have processed having one of the sport's all-time greats as a teammate as motivation rather than intimidation. The results speak for themselves: nine wins across the season for Antonelli, steady contributions from Hamilton, and Mercedes positioned as the dominant force in 2026.

What's Next

Both Mercedes drivers head to Mexico City for the Grand Prix on November 1. Hamilton will be looking to add another top-five result to his season tally, while Antonelli will need only a ninth-place finish to clinch the championship. The Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez has hosted some remarkable Formula 1 moments over the years. It may be about to host one more. Hamilton will be part of that story, as he has been part of every major F1 story for the past two decades.
