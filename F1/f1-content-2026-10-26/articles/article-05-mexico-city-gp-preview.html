Mexico City GP Preview — Five Races Left, One Could Be Enough: Antonelli's Title Is Coming

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | October 26, 2026

The Formula 1 world title heads to Mexico City on November 1. Antonelli needs P9 at the Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez to become the youngest champion in history.

Seven days. That is how long the Formula 1 world waits before Kimi Antonelli has his next opportunity to make history. The Mexico City Grand Prix on November 1 is the next item on the calendar, and it arrives with the most straightforward storyline in years: one driver, needing one result, to claim one of sport's most prestigious titles.

Antonelli needs a ninth-place finish or better at the Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez to become Formula 1 world champion. At approximately 20 years old, he would become the youngest champion in the sport's history — obliterating the record of 23 years and 134 days set by Sebastian Vettel at the 2010 Japanese Grand Prix.

The Weekend Schedule

The Mexico City Grand Prix weekend gets underway on Friday, October 30, with two practice sessions at the Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez. Saturday, October 31 brings a third practice session and qualifying — the grid that will determine whether Antonelli enters Sunday's race from the front row or from further back. Race day is Sunday, November 1.

The Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez is a unique circuit. Located at altitude — over 2,200 metres above sea level — it places unusual demands on power units and cooling systems. The thin air means teams run lower downforce than they might elsewhere, and the race can become a strategic battle as overtaking opportunities are limited in places. For Antonelli, the goal is simple: stay in the points. How he achieves that is secondary to the outcome.

Can Anyone Stop Him?

In a word: no. Not mathematically, and probably not on track either. Antonelli leads the championship by 123-124 points. A maximum of 125 is available. His rivals would need everything to go wrong for Antonelli and perfectly right for themselves in every remaining race. The sport does produce upsets, but it does not produce championships overturned from that margin in five races.

More likely is that Antonelli finishes in the points in Mexico City and the celebrations begin on Sunday evening at the Autodromo. The crowd inside that stadium section — one of F1's most passionate atmospheres — will have something remarkable to witness.

The Rest of the Field

While Antonelli's title is the narrative, the rest of the Mexico City grid will be racing hard for their own objectives. Charles Leclerc and Max Verstappen continue their runner-up battle. Lewis Hamilton will look to extend his record of consecutive top-five finishes. And throughout the field, individual championship positions, bonus points, and team pride keep everyone pushing from lights out to chequered flag.

What's Next

The Mexico City GP is followed by four more rounds if Antonelli does not clinch next Sunday — but the expectation, shared across the paddock, is that November 1 at the Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez will be the day the history books are updated.
