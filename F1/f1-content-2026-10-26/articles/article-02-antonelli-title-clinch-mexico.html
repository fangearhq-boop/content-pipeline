How Kimi Antonelli Clinches the F1 Title in Mexico — P9 Is All He Needs for Historic Record

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | October 26, 2026

After his dominant US Grand Prix win, Antonelli's championship math is now brutally simple. Here's exactly what he needs at the Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez on November 1.

Kimi Antonelli's path to the 2026 Formula 1 world championship could hardly be clearer. After his dominant victory at the United States Grand Prix in Austin, the Mercedes driver leads the standings by approximately 123-124 points over both George Russell and Lewis Hamilton. Five races remain, carrying a maximum of 125 points available in total.

The arithmetic: finish ninth or better in Mexico City on November 1 and Antonelli is champion. That is it. No conditions. No complex calculations involving other drivers' results. Just Antonelli in the points at the Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez.

Why P9 Is Enough

A ninth-place finish in Formula 1 earns two points. Two points added to Antonelli's current total — combined with the mathematical impossibility of either Russell or Hamilton catching him even if they win every remaining race — is sufficient to seal the championship.

If Antonelli finishes outside the points (tenth or lower) in Mexico, the title remains open in theory, but he would still need to lose it across four remaining races, which is almost inconceivably difficult given his advantage. The realistic scenario is that Sunday, November 1 at the Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez is the day.

The Record That Would Fall

The championship mathematics are compelling. The age makes it historic. Antonelli is approximately 20 years old. Sebastian Vettel set the record for youngest Formula 1 world champion at the 2010 Japanese Grand Prix, claiming the title aged 23 years and 134 days. Antonelli would beat that record by over three years.

To put that in context: when Vettel won his first title, Antonelli was around seven years old. The sport has waited a long time for someone to challenge that benchmark. It is about to happen in Mexico City.

The Season That Built This Moment

Nine wins across 17 completed rounds is a dominant season by any measure. Antonelli has won with pace, won with strategy, and won under pressure. He clinched his first pole position of the season and has converted race leads into victories with a composure that belies his age. The 2026 championship is not a fluke or the product of an uncompetitive field — it is the result of an exceptional driver at the peak of his early powers.

George Russell and Lewis Hamilton have pushed him hard in the constructors' fight, and Charles Leclerc and Max Verstappen have applied pressure on specific weekends. But Antonelli has answered every challenge. Mexico City is simply the place where the accounting gets done.

What's Next

The Mexico City Grand Prix weekend begins Friday, October 30 with practice sessions at the Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez. Qualifying runs Saturday, October 31. Race day — potentially the day Kimi Antonelli enters the record books — is Sunday, November 1. The 125,000-capacity stadium section of the Autodromo will be electric. Watch every lap.
