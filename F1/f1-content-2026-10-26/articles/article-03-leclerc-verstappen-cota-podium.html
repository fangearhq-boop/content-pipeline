Leclerc 2nd, Verstappen 3rd at COTA — Ferrari and Red Bull Keep Runner-Up Fight Alive

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | October 26, 2026

While Antonelli dominated Sunday's United States Grand Prix, Charles Leclerc and Max Verstappen delivered strong results that keep their battle for championship runner-up very much alive.

Not every story at Circuit of the Americas belonged to Kimi Antonelli. As the young Mercedes driver put on a masterclass from pole, Charles Leclerc and Max Verstappen quietly did what they needed to do: finish second and third respectively, collect their points, and keep themselves alive in the battle for Formula 1's secondary honours in 2026.

For Leclerc, second place at COTA was another strong Ferrari result in what has been a consistent season. For Verstappen, third was a recovery — the Red Bull driver has faced stretches of the campaign where the car's performance has lagged, but Austin delivered a podium finish and a reminder of why Verstappen remains one of the grid's most formidable operators.

Leclerc: Ferrari's Best Foot Forward

Charles Leclerc has been Ferrari's most reliable performer across the 2026 season. His second-place finish at COTA — 13 seconds behind Antonelli but ahead of the rest — demonstrated the progress Ferrari have made with their car at high-speed circuits. COTA's blend of high-speed corners and technical sections suits a car that generates strong downforce, and Leclerc made the most of what the Scuderia gave him.

Leclerc's championship position keeps him in contention for runner-up honours. With five races remaining and points still available, he will be pushing hard across every remaining weekend. The Mexican Grand Prix is next, and Ferrari will need another strong qualifying and race performance to maintain pressure on Verstappen.

Verstappen: Third Is Not Enough — But It's Something

Max Verstappen's third place is the kind of result that keeps a season respectable when the championship gap to the leader has grown beyond reach. He continues to extract maximum performance from the Red Bull machinery, and a podium at a circuit as competitive as COTA shows the team is still a factor week to week.

Red Bull and Ferrari's competition for constructor performance is one of the subplot narratives of 2026, and Verstappen's consistent podium finishes have been central to Red Bull's position in that fight. A third-place finish in the drivers' standings would be considered underperformance relative to recent Red Bull seasons, but Verstappen has navigated the circumstances with characteristic resilience.

What's Next

Both Leclerc and Verstappen head to Mexico City with motivation and points ambitions. The Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez will host the Mexican Grand Prix on November 1 — a circuit that rewards power units and aerodynamic efficiency in equal measure. Ferrari and Red Bull will both be targeting podiums. The runner-up championship may feel like a consolation prize while Antonelli's coronation looms, but for these two drivers and these two teams, every remaining point counts.
