Antonelli Wins US Grand Prix From Pole by 13 Seconds — Title One Race Away in Mexico

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | October 26, 2026

A dominant drive at Circuit of the Americas delivered Kimi Antonelli his ninth win of the season. The championship is now his to lose in Mexico City.

Kimi Antonelli arrived at Circuit of the Americas with a chance to become Formula 1 world champion. He left Texas with his ninth win of the 2026 season — dominant, unhurried, and devastating in its clarity — and a one-race countdown to the youngest championship in the sport's history.

Antonelli led the 2026 United States Grand Prix from pole position to chequered flag on Sunday, crossing the line 13.078 seconds ahead of Charles Leclerc. It was a performance that offered no drama, no vulnerability, and no concession to the occasion. The young Mercedes driver simply drove at a level his rivals could not match.

A Race Without Drama — Or Rather, One Whose Drama Was Its Effortlessness

Antonelli's dominance at COTA was total. He built his lead methodically through the opening laps, controlled the race through tyre management phases that left rivals struggling to close the gap, and delivered fastest laps at will when the situation demanded. The 13-second margin understates how thoroughly he managed Sunday's race — there were stretches where he appeared to be driving within himself, pacing the field while keeping the machinery in optimal condition.

Behind him, Charles Leclerc gave Ferrari a strong second-place result, while Max Verstappen recovered to third for Red Bull, completing a podium that was, in its own way, a fair reflection of the competitive order in 2026. Lewis Hamilton brought the second Mercedes home in fourth.

Why the Title Didn't Come in Austin

The mathematics of championship clinching are precise and occasionally frustrating for neutral fans hoping for ceremony. Antonelli needed three things to occur simultaneously at COTA: his own victory, George Russell finishing ninth or lower, and Lewis Hamilton finishing fifth or lower. He delivered his side of the bargain. Hamilton's fourth-place finish — just one spot too high — kept the title alive for another weekend.

The difference between fourth and fifth is twelve points versus ten. Those two points were enough to prevent a coronation in Austin. The celebration has been rescheduled for Mexico City.

What's Next

The Formula 1 championship circus arrives in Mexico City this week for the Mexican Grand Prix at the Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez. The race weekend runs from Friday, October 30 through Sunday, November 1. Antonelli now leads the championship by approximately 123-124 points with a maximum of 125 remaining. He needs only a ninth-place finish to secure the title.

If he clinches in Mexico City, Antonelli will become the youngest Formula 1 world champion in history — surpassing Sebastian Vettel's record of 23 years and 134 days set at the 2010 Japanese Grand Prix. He is approximately 20 years old. The record is within his grasp, and after Sunday's display at COTA, there is no reason to believe it will escape him.
