### STORY 4: 2026 Regulation Energy Problems — FIA Review

#### Long-Form Post — 2:00 PM ET

Formula 1's 2026 regulations were designed to make the sport faster, more efficient, and more exciting. Seven months in, the FIA is already examining whether they need to be adjusted.

The problem centers on energy management. The new 2026 power unit architecture — which splits total power equally between the internal combustion engine and hybrid electric system, with the MGU-H eliminated — has created a racing environment where teams and drivers are managing energy far more aggressively than planned. Cars are "derating" (cutting power output) during straights to preserve energy reserves, disrupting the on-track racing fans expected to see.

There's also a safety dimension. The energy dynamics create large speed differentials between cars in different energy states — a car with a full battery traveling significantly faster than a depleted car through high-speed sections. The FIA has flagged this as a concern.

The F1 "Overtake Mode" system — a driver-triggered electrical boost available when within one second of the car ahead — was designed to facilitate passing. But even that system is affected by the depletion dynamic: a lead car with a full battery can match the boost and resist being overtaken.

The FIA and Formula 1 are discussing potential tweaks. Whether changes can be made mid-season, or whether this is a longer-term regulatory discussion, will become clear in the coming weeks.

For fans, the news is reassuring in one sense — the sport recognizes the issue and is working on it. For 2026 race coverage, the energy management element is the hidden subplot behind every overtake attempt and every strategy call.
