Hungarian GP Practice: Ferrari Goes 1-2 Across Both Sessions as McLaren and Aston Martin Debut Major Upgrades

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap

July 25, 2026

The 2026 Hungarian Grand Prix got underway at the Hungaroring on Friday, and Ferrari left no doubt about their intentions: Charles Leclerc set the fastest time in FP1, then Lewis Hamilton topped FP2, giving the Scuderia a clean sweep of both practice sessions heading into a critical qualifying day.

The Hungaroring has historically been one of Ferrari's more favored circuits, with its tight, twisty layout and high-downforce demands suiting their car's characteristics. Friday's 1-2 in practice suggests the 2026 season continues in a similar vein — though the full picture will only emerge in qualifying on Saturday and the race on Sunday.

Leclerc Leads FP1, Hamilton Tops FP2

Leclerc's FP1 performance was the first indicator that Ferrari had arrived in Budapest with genuine pace. The Monégasque driver, who sits third in the championship, set the benchmark time in the opening session and left the other teams with a target to chase.

Hamilton — coming off a strong recent stretch and sitting second in the Drivers' Championship, 45 points behind Mercedes teammate Kimi Antonelli — then went one better in FP2, taking over the timing sheets and completing what became an all-red Friday for the teams outside Ferrari to consider overnight.

For Hamilton personally, every point matters as he attempts to chase down Antonelli before the summer break. A strong qualifying and race in Hungary could narrow the gap before the championship reconvenes in August.

McLaren's Macarena Wing and Aston Martin's 16-Part Upgrade

While Ferrari set the pace, the most technically interesting elements of Friday came from the rival teams' upgrade packages.

McLaren debuted a new rear wing configuration during practice — quickly dubbed the "Macarena" wing in media coverage. The novel aerodynamic concept is aimed at optimizing the high-downforce demands of the Hungaroring, where teams prioritize grip and cornering speed over straight-line velocity. McLaren's design team, which has consistently iterated throughout the season, will be evaluating the practice data to determine whether the concept is ready for race specification.

Aston Martin arrived in Hungary with their most substantial upgrade of the 2026 season: a 16-part package covering aerodynamic revisions and a new rear suspension design. The package, led by Adrian Newey's design influence, represents a significant bet on reversing what has been a difficult start to the year. The update was immediately complicated by a suspension failure during practice that damaged Lance Stroll's car and required replacement parts to be flown in on an emergency basis — a costly setback heading into qualifying.

Sainz and Verstappen Called to Stewards

A practice incident between Carlos Sainz (Williams) and Max Verstappen (Red Bull) added a layer of controversy to Friday's running. During FP1, Sainz was warming his brakes on the racing line between Turns 11 and 12 when Verstappen arrived at speed and was forced to take avoiding action. Sainz's radio had failed at the critical moment, preventing him from hearing his engineer's warning about incoming traffic.

Verstappen was then seen to slow deliberately at Turn 13, obstructing both Sainz and Audi's Gabriel Bortoleto. Both drivers were summoned to the stewards. The outcome: Sainz received an official warning for the initial slow-down incident, while Verstappen was cleared — the stewards accepted that his subsequent action was not a deliberate obstruction.

For Verstappen, the weekend carries significance that extends far beyond lap times. With his Red Bull exit clause now mathematically active, this Hungarian GP represents the last race before his future — and potentially the future of the entire grid — becomes official.

What's Next

Qualifying takes place on Saturday at the Hungaroring. Based on Ferrari's Friday form, Leclerc and Hamilton will enter as the drivers to beat for pole position. McLaren and their Macarena wing will be closely watched to see whether the upgrade translates from practice data to lap times under competitive conditions.

The race is scheduled for Sunday, July 26. It is the last round before the Formula 1 summer break — making Hungary the final opportunity for championship contenders to add points before the series reconvenes in August. Ferrari's 1-2 in practice is a statement. Whether it becomes a 1-2 in the race is the question the rest of the weekend will answer.
