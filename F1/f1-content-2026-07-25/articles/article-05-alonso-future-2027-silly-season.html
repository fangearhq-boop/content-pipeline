Alonso's Future Unresolved as Aston Martin Struggles — The Most Explosive F1 Silly Season in Years Is Coming

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap

July 25, 2026

Fernando Alonso arrived at Aston Martin with a clear objective: a third world championship. The team had the budget, the ambition, and a new technical direction anchored by Adrian Newey's appointment. Honda was supplying the power unit. Everything was aligned for a genuine title challenge.

Ten rounds into 2026, Aston Martin has not scored a point. Alonso is in the final year of his contract. His decision — continue or retire — is expected during the summer break. And the moment he announces, a cascade of driver market moves will begin that could reshape every team on the grid.

Why This Year Has Gone Wrong for Aston Martin

The gap between Aston Martin's ambition and their 2026 results reflects the difficulty of the new technical regulations rather than a failure of intent. The 2026 rules changed fundamental aspects of car design — dimensions, power unit architecture, aerodynamic philosophy — and teams that had built strong performance under the previous framework found that their advantages did not always translate cleanly into the new era.

Newey's design influence is a long-term investment; his work on the 2026 car and beyond is not yet fully realized in race results. Honda's power unit is competitive but has not provided the edge that was hoped for in the opening rounds. The combination of factors has left Alonso — still one of the most technically astute drivers in the paddock — without the machinery to demonstrate it.

Lance Stroll, Alonso's teammate, suffered a suspension failure during Hungarian GP practice on Friday that required emergency replacement parts to be flown in, further complicating Aston Martin's attempt to debut their 16-part upgrade package under favorable conditions.

The Decision Alonso Has to Make

At 44 years old, Alonso is among the oldest active drivers in Formula 1 and one of only a handful who can be described as all-time greats while still competing at the top level. His decision between continuing at Aston Martin and retirement is not a simple performance calculus — it involves a genuine question about what the final chapter of one of the sport's most remarkable careers looks like.

A decision to continue means committing to a team that has not yet delivered the results it promised, with the hope that Newey's work and Honda's development will change the trajectory. A decision to retire means concluding a career that includes two world championships, 32 Grand Prix victories, and arguably the most sustained excellence in the sport's history over the longest active timeline.

Alonso has historically been resistant to the idea of retiring without competing at the front. If Aston Martin cannot credibly promise a championship-capable car for 2027, the rational case for continuation weakens.

Seventeen Drivers Out of Contract

Alonso's decision is one piece of an extraordinarily complex 2027 driver market. Seventeen drivers are currently out of contract for next season. Verstappen's exit clause at Red Bull is active, with McLaren reportedly in advanced discussions. George Russell's future at Mercedes depends on the team's planning around their current lineup. Multiple mid-field teams are searching for new pairings.

The confirmed multi-year arrangements provide some stability: Lando Norris is contracted at McLaren through at least 2027, Oscar Piastri through 2028. Charles Leclerc signed a new deal with Ferrari in June 2026 through at least 2028. Hamilton is committed to Ferrari through 2027 with an option for 2028. George Russell has a multi-year deal with Mercedes through at least 2027.

Everything else is in motion. The summer break that follows the Hungarian GP this weekend is when the calls will be made, the announcements will drop, and the grid picture will begin to clarify.

What's Next

Alonso will race at the Hungaroring on Sunday — the last Grand Prix before the summer break. Whether it is among the final laps of his Formula 1 career or just another round before a renewed 2027 campaign is a question only he can answer. The announcement will come. The summer is rarely quiet in Formula 1, and 2026's break shapes up to be one of the noisiest on record.

For the sport's fans, the weeks ahead could redefine the competitive order for years to come — one contract announcement at a time.
