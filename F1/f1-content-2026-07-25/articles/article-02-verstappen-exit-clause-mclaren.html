Verstappen's Exit Clause Is Now Active — And McLaren Talks Are Already Advanced

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap

July 25, 2026

The Hungarian Grand Prix is one of Formula 1's most scenic venues. This weekend, it may also be the backdrop for the sport's biggest driver market announcement in a decade.

Max Verstappen's Red Bull contract contains a performance-based exit clause that allows him to leave the team after the 2026 season if he is not within the top two of the Drivers' Championship at the summer break cutoff. That cutoff falls after the Hungarian GP. With Verstappen currently sitting seventh in the championship on 76 points — 128 points behind leader Kimi Antonelli — triggering the clause is a near-mathematical certainty. His manager, Raymond Vermeulen, has confirmed it is in play.

The Numbers Tell the Story

The Red Bull performance clause was written as a protection mechanism — a guarantee that Verstappen would have the option to pursue a more competitive environment if Red Bull's form declined to the point where a championship challenge became impossible. Twelve rounds into 2026, the condition has been met definitively.

Antonelli leads with 204 points. Hamilton is second at 159. Third place, Leclerc, sits at 126. Verstappen's 76 points leave him 128 back from the top. With 316 points theoretically available in the remaining 12 rounds, catching two drivers ahead of him while simultaneously fending off challengers behind him is functionally impossible. The clause was built for exactly this scenario.

McLaren: The Likely Landing Spot

Multiple sources report that Verstappen and McLaren have worked out the broad terms of a potential 2027 contract, though no deal has been signed. McLaren is described across multiple outlets as the primary destination — an arrangement that would add Verstappen's title-winning experience to a team that already has Lando Norris (contracted through at least 2027) and Oscar Piastri (contracted through 2028) on their books.

The mechanics of that arrangement — which driver Verstappen would theoretically displace and what the full team structure would look like — have not been confirmed. But the reporting indicates serious, advanced negotiations rather than exploratory conversations.

Other destinations have been discussed throughout the season. Aston Martin was linked to a reported €300 million offer that the team addressed but did not definitively deny. Ferrari already has Hamilton and Leclerc committed. Mercedes has Russell under contract. The pathway to McLaren is, by elimination, the most logical.

The 2027 Domino Effect

Verstappen's decision does not exist in isolation. Seventeen drivers are currently out of contract for 2027 — a number that makes the upcoming silly season one of the most consequential grid reshuffles in recent memory.

Fernando Alonso has yet to announce whether he will continue at Aston Martin or retire. George Russell's future is tied to Mercedes's planning. Multiple mid-field teams are actively searching for driver pairings. Wherever Verstappen ends up sets off a cascade of moves that will touch nearly every team on the grid.

The announcement is expected during or shortly after the Hungarian GP weekend. Vermeulen has stated publicly that Verstappen wants a team that gives him "a chance to win" — a standard that Red Bull's current form cannot meet. The summer break will be the occasion for the formal communication of what has apparently already been decided in substance.

What's Next

The Hungarian GP race takes place Sunday, July 26. After that, Formula 1 enters its summer break. Before the series reconvenes in August, expect Verstappen's situation to be resolved officially. The structure of the 2027 grid — and the championship battles it will produce — depends on the announcement that is, by all accounts, imminent.

Whatever happens Sunday at the Hungaroring, the most significant Formula 1 story of the weekend will be decided off the circuit.
