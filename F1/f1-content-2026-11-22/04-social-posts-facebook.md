### STORY 1: Las Vegas GP — Verstappen Wins

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 10:00 AM ET

Max Verstappen has done it again in Las Vegas.

The Red Bull driver controlled Sunday's Las Vegas Grand Prix from the front, crossing the finish line in 1:27:39.624 to claim his third victory on the iconic Strip circuit and his second win in the past three races. Lando Norris was 0.639 seconds behind in second, with Oscar Piastri completing a McLaren double podium in third.

The win moves Verstappen to 304 championship points — 37 behind confirmed World Champion Kimi Antonelli, who finished fourth. While the title is long decided, the battle for second place in the standings remains wide open with two races left.

Kimi Antonelli was fourth. Pierre Gasly fifth. Franco Colapinto delivered an impressive sixth. Lewis Hamilton managed seventh for Ferrari, while Carlos Sainz, George Russell, and Charles Leclerc rounded out the top 10.

McLaren's double podium is the bigger tactical story of the day: Norris and Piastri's combined haul leaves the team just nine points behind Red Bull in the constructors' standings, with Qatar and Abu Dhabi still to come.

### STORY 2: Ferrari — Hamilton 7th, Leclerc 10th

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 10:00 AM ET

Another race, another missed opportunity for Ferrari.

After both Charles Leclerc and Lewis Hamilton were knocked out in Q2 qualifying on Saturday — leaving them 11th and 12th on the grid respectively — Sunday's race produced similarly uninspiring results. Hamilton finished seventh. Leclerc took 10th, carrying an additional penalty that hampered any chance of a meaningful recovery.

For Hamilton, the mathematics of the championship are becoming increasingly uncomfortable. He sits third with 267 points, just three ahead of George Russell and Lando Norris (both on 264) with two races remaining. A strong result in Qatar is not optional.

Leclerc's championship fight is effectively over. Ferrari's constructors' challenge is similarly fading — their 517 points leave them 149 behind Mercedes' dominant 666.

The Scuderia needs a reset heading into the Qatar weekend, and the conversations in Maranello this week will be pointed.

### STORY 4: Qatar GP Preview

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 2:00 PM ET

The 2026 Formula 1 season moves to Qatar next weekend, and after everything that's happened in Las Vegas, the stakes have never been higher.

Round 22 takes place at the Lusail International Circuit on the outskirts of Doha — a stunning floodlit venue that has become one of the most visually striking stops on the F1 calendar. The track spans approximately 5.4 kilometers over 57 laps, runs clockwise, and features 16 turns that place significant demands on tire strategy. The extreme heat in Qatar adds an additional physical toll on drivers and places significant strain on power units and braking systems.

What to watch for:
• The Verstappen vs Hamilton/Russell/Norris battle for second in the championship
• McLaren vs Red Bull in the constructors' fight — nine points between them
• Ferrari's ability to respond after back-to-back difficult weekends
• How teams manage tire degradation in the heat

Race weekend runs November 27-29, with the race starting at 16:00 GMT on Sunday the 29th.

The calendar is almost done. Make every lap count.
