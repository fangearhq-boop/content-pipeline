Ferrari's Las Vegas Nightmare: Hamilton 7th, Leclerc 10th After Q2 Disaster

By Marcus Cole | November 22, 2026 | F1 Fanrecap

Lewis Hamilton salvaged seventh place and Charles Leclerc took 10th at the Las Vegas Grand Prix, completing a miserable weekend for Ferrari that began with both cars eliminated in Q2 qualifying on Saturday and ended with another missed opportunity to close the championship gap at the top of the table.

Leclerc was burdened by an additional race penalty that hampered any realistic chance of recovery through the field. Hamilton, starting 12th, managed to advance into the top 10 and beyond, but seventh place is significantly short of what Ferrari needed from a points-available perspective.

Hamilton's Championship Mathematics

Hamilton enters the final two rounds with 267 championship points — third place, but uncomfortably close to the drivers behind him. George Russell and Lando Norris, both on 264, are just three points adrift after Norris's second-place finish on Saturday. Hamilton's cushion is the smallest it has been at any point this season relative to the chase pack.

With 43 points available per driver in each of the remaining rounds at Qatar and Abu Dhabi, the difference between finishing second versus seventh in one of those races is the kind of swing that can rearrange the standings entirely. Hamilton cannot afford another weekend like Las Vegas.

A Season of Missed Potential

Ferrari's 2026 season has been characterized by flashes of brilliance undermined by costly errors and execution failures at critical moments. The constructors' standings tell the story: 517 points against Mercedes' 666, a gap of 149 that speaks to the consistency differential between the two teams.

Leclerc's championship fight is effectively concluded. For the Scuderia, the remaining two races represent an opportunity to restore some pride and demonstrate that the platform they have built is capable of competing at the front when everything comes together.

What's Next

Ferrari must regroup before Qatar on November 29. The Lusail Circuit offers a different set of challenges — high temperatures, a clockwise layout, and the physical demands that this venue places on driver and machinery alike. For Hamilton, a strong qualifying performance to avoid starting from the midfield will be essential. For Leclerc, the goal shifts to momentum and preparation for 2027 rather than championship points.
