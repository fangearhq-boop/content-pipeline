Antonelli 302, Russell 236: The Championship Math Heading Into Malaysia and Singapore

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | October 1, 2026

Sixty-six points. Eight rounds remaining. Two Mercedes drivers, one garage, and a championship fight that has become one of the most unusual in recent Formula 1 history — not because the gaps are dramatic, but because the rivals are teammates.

Kimi Antonelli leads George Russell by 66 points after 15 rounds. The gap was 81 before Baku, and it tightened to 66 after Russell's dominant pole-to-win performance while Antonelli recovered from a qualifying crash to fifth. The net result of Baku was a 15-point swing — meaningful, but not decisive. Not yet.

What the Numbers Say

With eight rounds remaining — Rounds 16 through 23 — the maximum points available are significant. A race win is worth 25 points. The Singapore GP (Round 17) is a Sprint weekend, adding eight more to that event's total. If every remaining weekend is a standard, non-Sprint race, the available points across eight rounds are 208 for a driver who wins every race including the bonus fastest lap. Singapore adds eight on top of that.

For Russell to overtake Antonelli mathematically, he would need to outscore his teammate by 67 points across those eight rounds. That requires Antonelli to finish off the podium multiple times while Russell sweeps wins. The statistical probability of that outcome is low — Antonelli has been consistently on the podium all season, and there is no technical reason to expect sudden unreliability.

The realistic path for Russell is narrow. He needs Antonelli to make mistakes — the kind Baku produced — while Russell himself stays clean. Two or three more bad Antonelli weekends, combined with Russell maximizing results, could close the gap to single digits heading into the final three races. But time and opportunity are both running out.

Antonelli's Position

At 20 years old, Kimi Antonelli is on course to become the youngest Formula 1 world champion in history. If he clinches the title at Singapore on October 11, he would do so at 20 years, two months, and eight days — more than three years younger than Sebastian Vettel was when he won his first title in 2010.

That milestone context matters less to Antonelli than the competitive one, but it has shaped the narrative around his season from the beginning. Every stumble — the Monza qualifying crash, the Baku qualifying crash — is amplified by the expectation that a driver of his talent should be able to avoid unforced errors entirely. His response to Baku was instructive: he drove from 16th to fifth and said he intends to "push until the last corner" in Malaysia. The self-awareness is that of a champion in waiting. The qualifying crashes are still a hole in the resume.

Singapore as the Clinch Window

Round 17, October 9-11, Marina Bay Street Circuit. The Singapore GP is Formula 1's first-ever Sprint weekend at this venue — a format change that compresses championship points into a tighter window than usual. The Sprint race itself offers up to eight points, with the Grand Prix's standard 25 on Sunday.

The clinch scenario: Antonelli wins the Sprint (8 points) and the Grand Prix (25 points), scoring 33 in total. Russell finishes outside the points both days, scoring zero. That would leave the gap at 66 + 33 = 99 points. With six rounds remaining after Singapore and a maximum of roughly 156 points available, Russell could not mathematically close to within less than zero. Antonelli is champion.

That specific scenario requires Russell to have a catastrophic weekend. More likely outcomes involve both drivers scoring, Antonelli extending his lead modestly, and the title being settled at a later round. But Singapore is the first window. The Sprint format opens it slightly wider.

What's Next

Malaysia this weekend provides the first post-Baku data point. Can Antonelli qualify from the front row and control the race? Can Russell push him throughout the weekend and close another few points? The answers, over 56 Malaysian laps, will tell us whether Singapore is a genuine clinch window or a comfortable buffer for Antonelli heading into the final eight.
