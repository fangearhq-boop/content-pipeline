Colapinto Starts Malaysia Five Places Back — and His Alpine Future Is on the Line

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | October 1, 2026

Franco Colapinto has never needed a race weekend more urgently than he needs this one.

The 21-year-old Alpine driver arrives at Sepang International Circuit carrying a five-place grid penalty for the Baku crash that ended Lando Norris' race and badly damaged Pierre Gasly's. The stewards' decision — five grid places, converted from an unserved ten-second time penalty — was a compromise that satisfied nobody. Norris wanted a race ban. Colapinto wants to move forward. Alpine is doing something more consequential: reviewing whether they want to continue with Colapinto at all.

What Happened in Baku

The Baku incident unfolded on lap 36, during a restart after a safety car period. The Azerbaijan street circuit's long straights make the first turn after a restart a pressure point — brake zones are long, following distances are compressed, and the margin for error is slim. Colapinto locked up going into Turn 1, his front wheels washing wide, and struck his own teammate Gasly. Gasly's car was deflected into the path of Norris, who had nowhere to go. Three cars retired.

The five-place grid penalty represents the FIA's judgment that the crash was avoidable but not egregiously aggressive — a racing incident at the severe end of the scale, not deliberate contact. Norris disagreed publicly: "If you cause a crash on a restart like this, you should have at least a one-race ban." Russell, characteristically measured, called a ban too harsh.

The paddock debate was essentially: what is the appropriate penalty for a young driver's restart misjudgment that took out two other cars? The stewards' answer — five places, carry it to Malaysia — is now a permanent part of Colapinto's record for this season.

The Alpine Seat Question

Alpine's post-race statement that they were reviewing their "course of action" regarding Colapinto was carefully worded. It was not a dismissal. It was also not an endorsement. In the political language of Formula 1 team communications, it was a warning — publicly addressed to a driver who has been involved in significant incidents at multiple circuits this season.

Officially, the 2027 driver line-up at Alpine lists Colapinto alongside Pierre Gasly. Contracts exist. Plans were made. But Formula 1's driver market is not bound by early planning when performance becomes a concern. If Alpine's technical leadership concludes that Colapinto's incident rate creates more problems than his raw speed solves, options exist — whether that means identifying a replacement or restructuring their plans entirely.

The complicating factor is that Colapinto, before Baku, had shown genuine promise. His car control in wheel-to-wheel situations and his qualifying pace in the middle part of the season gave Alpine reason to believe they had found a driver capable of long-term development. One terrible restart decision shouldn't erase that — but in a paddock with limited seats and a deep talent pool of waiting drivers, patience has an expiration date.

What Malaysia Represents for Colapinto

Starting five places behind his qualifying position, Colapinto's Malaysia weekend is simultaneously a sporting challenge and a character test. He will need to make clean progress through the field on a circuit he has never raced in F1, in tropical conditions that create unpredictable tire wear, with the knowledge that every decision he makes over 56 laps is being evaluated at Alpine's headquarters.

What Alpine needs to see is simple: a competitive, clean performance. Not a miraculous result — the grid penalty makes that difficult — but evidence that Colapinto can manage risk, execute strategy, and finish a race weekend without incident. That combination, delivered over two or three consecutive weekends, is what turns "under review" back into "confirmed for 2027."

What's Next

Colapinto qualifies at Sepang on Saturday and starts five places behind whatever grid position he earns. The race runs Sunday. Alpine's evaluation continues in parallel. The driver market moves slowly until it doesn't — and right now, Colapinto's position in it is dependent on what happens this weekend.

F1 Fanrecap will track his Malaysia performance across the full weekend. The five-place penalty is a known quantity. What happens with Alpine's seat review is the story that matters more.
