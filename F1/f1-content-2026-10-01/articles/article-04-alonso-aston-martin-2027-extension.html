Fernando Alonso Signs Through 2027 With Aston Martin — His 24th F1 Season at Age 46

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | October 1, 2026

Fernando Alonso has signed a new contract extension to race for Aston Martin in the 2027 Formula 1 season. The announcement came September 29, confirming that the two-time world champion will continue into what would be his 24th full-time season in grand prix racing. He will be 46 years old at the start of that campaign.

Lance Stroll also extended, keeping the Aston Martin line-up unchanged for a fifth consecutive year. The organization has chosen continuity over experimentation — a deliberate signal from a team that has been building its technical and commercial infrastructure toward a sustained front-running challenge.

The Career That Refuses to End

Fernando Alonso made his Formula 1 debut in 2001. He claimed his first world championship in 2005 and his second in 2006 — both with Renault — and spent the following decade competing at the front of the grid with Ferrari, McLaren, and Renault again, adding to an achievements list that already ranks him among the sport's all-time figures. He retired in 2018, then returned in 2023 with Aston Martin after two years of Le Mans and IndyCar work reminded him that competitive racing at the top level was still available to him.

That return had skeptics. He was already 41. Aston Martin was not, at that point, a race-winning team. The prevailing view in the paddock was that Alonso was returning for the commercial profile and the thrill — not because he genuinely believed he could contend for another championship.

What happened instead: Alonso walked back into Formula 1 and drove as if he had never left. The reflexes were sharp. The tire management was characteristically subtle. The qualifying laps were often the best on the grid when conditions suited him. Whatever biology allows some athletes to defy the expected timeline of decline, Alonso appears to have it.

What the 2027 Extension Means

Alonso's extension to 2027 extends the Aston Martin partnership into a generation of machinery that will be shaped significantly by the 2026 technical regulations — the current rulebook, now in its first year. The power unit changes and active aerodynamics of 2026 have reshuffled the pecking order, and Aston Martin is in the process of developing a car capable of challenging at the front under the new framework.

Lance Stroll's extension alongside Alonso maintains a driver line-up built around experience at the top and developmental trajectory at the bottom. Stroll, as the owner's son, was always going to be part of Aston Martin's plans. Alonso's extension is the one that signals ambition — the team is retaining a driver who could still, in theory, push for a third championship if the car reaches that level.

Whether Aston Martin's technical program can produce that car in 2027 is a separate question. The team has invested heavily in facilities, engineers, and aerodynamic development since Lawrence Stroll's ownership began. The results have been inconsistent but improving. Alonso, with one more season to push for everything, provides the incentive for the team to accelerate that progress.

What's Next

Alonso races this weekend at Sepang with the 2026 Aston Martin, bringing experience of the Malaysian circuit from his earlier F1 career. He represents one of the few grid veterans who can contribute meaningful setup insight for a circuit the newer generation has never seen. Whether 2027 brings him a championship challenge or serves as a graceful conclusion to one of the sport's most extraordinary careers, it adds another chapter to a story that conventional wisdom repeatedly predicted was over.

Fernando Alonso disagrees with conventional wisdom. He has done so for 24 years. Nothing suggests he'll start agreeing with it now.
