Colton Herta's F1 Dream Comes Down to One FP1 Session in Mexico City

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap

October 30, 2026

Colton Herta has been working toward this moment all season. Friday's opening practice session at the Mexico City Grand Prix is the American driver's final opportunity to earn the FIA Super License point that unlocks his path to a Formula 1 race seat.

Herta arrives with 39 of the required 40 points. One more FP1 run — completed over 100 kilometers without incident — delivers the 40th. Today, driving Valtteri Bottas's Cadillac at the Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez, that point is within reach.

The Road to 39 Points

Herta's super license pursuit has been one of the more unusual storylines threading through the 2026 Formula 1 season. The Cadillac test and reserve driver has appeared in four FP1 sessions this year — each one adding a point toward the 40 required by the FIA for eligibility as a full-time race driver.

The path has not been entirely smooth. At Monza in September, a hydraulic failure ended Herta's session before he could complete the required 100 kilometers. The point slipped away through no fault of his own. With Mexico City's FP1 being his final scheduled opportunity of 2026, there is no margin for mechanical misfortune this time.

The IndyCar star has demonstrated throughout this season that his raw pace is not in question — the regulatory requirement is simply the completion of distance without incident. That is both simpler and more nerve-wracking than a lap time target.

What a License Means

An FIA Super License is the prerequisite for competing in Formula 1. Without it, no driver — regardless of talent, backing, or team commitment — can start a Grand Prix. For Herta, earning the license transforms a theoretical 2027 race seat into a practical possibility.

Cadillac's driver lineup for 2027 remains undecided. Sergio Perez and Valtteri Bottas hold the current seats, and multiple reports suggest the team is weighing its options for the future. Bottas represents experience and stability. Herta represents the American dream — a marketable story for a team keen to build its U.S. fan base — but only if he holds the license that makes a race seat possible.

Clean the 100km today, and that question becomes answerable.

Mexico City: A Challenging Stage

The Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez is one of the most demanding circuits on the calendar. At 2,240 meters above sea level, the thin air reduces aerodynamic downforce and taxes engine cooling systems. The long straight rewards top speed but the technical sections require precision.

For a driver running in an unfamiliar car, completing 24 clean laps is a task that requires both technical discipline and trust in the machinery. There is no time to find a rhythm and simply lap — every lap counts toward the 100km total.

What's Next

Whether Herta completes the run or not, today closes one chapter. If successful, attention will turn to Cadillac's 2027 announcement and whether their American hopeful gets the seat. If not, the super license quest ends for 2026 and any F1 race ambitions shift to a longer timeline requiring additional steps — potentially Formula 2 or another FP1 campaign in 2027.

Herta has done the work. Today, the Cadillac needs to hold together. Mexico City, October 30 — the most important 45 minutes of an American racing driver's career.
