F1's Season Finale May Not End in Abu Dhabi — Portimão and Imola Are the Contingency

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | July 29, 2026

F1 already lost Bahrain and Saudi Arabia this season. Qatar (November 29) and Abu Dhabi (December 6) are now at risk from the same regional conflict. CEO Stefano Domenicali has confirmed backup plans — Portimão is the primary option, Imola is confirmed, Las Vegas is not the finale. The 2026 world championship could be decided anywhere.

Formula 1 entered 2026 with 24 races on the calendar. By April, that was down to 22 — Bahrain and Saudi Arabia dropped when the regional conflict made those venues untenable.

Now the sport is watching two more.

The Qatar Grand Prix (November 29) and Abu Dhabi Grand Prix (December 6) are both in the geopolitical zone that has already cost the calendar its February and March rounds. The World Endurance Championship has already cancelled its own Qatar and Bahrain events. The pattern is visible. F1's leadership is taking it seriously.

The Domenicali Confirmation

F1 CEO Stefano Domenicali addressed the situation directly. "We have alternatives," he said. Portimão — the Algarve International Circuit in Portugal — is the primary contingency. The circuit was used for F1 races in 2020 and 2021, has modern infrastructure, and is broadly accepted among teams. Imola has been confirmed by Domenicali personally as an option. A double-header in Las Vegas was floated internally before Domenicali explicitly ruled it out as a season finale.

The decision on any calendar change requires unanimous team agreement, which adds complexity to any quick resolution. But with events potentially unworkable two months before they're scheduled, the timeline for a decision is tightening. An announcement is expected before summer shutdown ends — before the Dutch Grand Prix on August 21.

What Changes if the Finale Moves

The most significant implication is for the championship narrative. Andrea Kimi Antonelli leads Lewis Hamilton by 50 points after Hungary — a gap that is comfortable but not insurmountable across the remaining races. The traditional Abu Dhabi finale, with its outdoor circuit and evening light, has hosted multiple championship-deciding moments.

Portimão is a different kind of circuit — flowing, technical, with elevation changes that create unique mechanical demands. Imola, with its narrow layout and limited overtaking opportunities, produces races that reward starting position and strategy more than raw pace. Either venue would change how teams approach the final rounds.

Antonelli, in his second season, has handled the championship pressure better than many expected for a 21-year-old. Hamilton, back at Mercedes after his 2025 Ferrari tenure, is the most experienced title-decider in the sport. A non-traditional finale venue doesn't inherently advantage either of them — but it changes the conversations about setup, strategy, and preparation that will define the final weeks of the season.

What's Next

An official calendar announcement from the FIA and F1 is expected within days. If Portimão is confirmed as the replacement finale, teams will begin circuit-specific preparation immediately. If the Middle East situation improves and Qatar and Abu Dhabi are reinstated, the original calendar holds.

The championship is already one of the most compelling in years. The venue where it ends may be decided by forces well outside the sport's control.
