Aston Martin's B-spec Works — And Alonso's Retirement Decision Just Got Harder

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | July 29, 2026

Fernando Alonso made Q2 for the first time all season at Hungary after Aston Martin debuted a 16-part upgrade package. He described the "missed" adrenaline returning. Adrian Newey says more is coming at Zandvoort, Monza, and Baku. The summer retirement decision just got complicated.

Fernando Alonso was supposed to spend this summer break deciding whether to retire.

Then Aston Martin debuted the AMR26 B-spec at the Hungarian Grand Prix, and the equation changed.

The upgrade was comprehensive: 16 components modified or replaced, covering the floor, bodywork, sidepods, engine cover, diffuser, redesigned nose, front and rear wings, cooling louvres, and rear suspension. The team stripped weight from the chassis, which required re-homologation and new crash testing — a significant investment in time and resources. The result at Hungaroring: Alonso qualified in Q2 for the first time in the 2026 season, 3.369 percent off Lando Norris' pole position, a 2.036 percent lap time improvement over the two rounds that preceded Hungary.

In the context of a year in which Aston Martin has spent most weekends at the back of the grid under the new technical regulations, this was meaningful progress.

The Adrenaline He Missed

Alonso's post-qualifying comments were telling. "That adrenaline when you close the visor and you go into the lap, it was missed in the last few events," he said. "We were on the last row of the grid, whatever we were doing on the car."

He used the word "missed." Not "recovered" or "returned." He missed it — which means it was absent long enough to notice, and its return was meaningful enough to say so in public. For a two-time world champion who has maintained across multiple difficult seasons that he drives for the love of competition, describing a sensation he had stopped feeling is a significant admission.

The Q2 result wasn't the upgrade's only positive. Alonso said the bigger gain was in direction — the team now understands which way to develop the car, something that had been elusive in the opening phase of the 2026 season under the radically new regulations.

Newey's Promise

Adrian Newey, who joined Aston Martin from Red Bull last year with championship ambitions, was characteristically precise in his post-Hungary assessment. "We will have some more steps in Zandvoort, Monza and Baku," he said, "so it is the first part of our planned upgrade."

A Honda power unit upgrade is also confirmed for the Dutch Grand Prix (August 21-23) — focused on internal combustion engine performance, lubrication system reliability, and friction reduction. The total picture: the car is getting better on multiple fronts, on a timeline that runs through the rest of the season.

Alonso is 45 years old. His Aston Martin contract expires at the end of 2026. He had told Sky Sports in May that this was probably his last race at Barcelona. Flavio Briatore reportedly gave him an Alpine offer deadline that has since passed. He has a newborn son, Leonardo, for the first time in his life — a personal reality that weighs into these decisions in ways that are difficult to quantify from the outside.

What's Next

The retirement decision was supposed to come during this summer break. It may still. But Alonso has a history of finding reasons to keep driving when the conditions align — and a car that is genuinely improving, with a technical director promising more, is a meaningful reason.

The Dutch GP in 23 days will be one of the first real tests of whether the B-spec's Hungary performance can be replicated at a different circuit. If Zandvoort confirms the improvement, the conversation around Alonso's future shifts again. He missed the adrenaline. Now it's back. Whether he chooses to keep chasing it is the question that defines his summer.
