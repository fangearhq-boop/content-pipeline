Zandvoort's Last-Ever F1 Race Is 23 Days Away — Here's Everything at Stake

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | July 29, 2026

The 2026 Dutch Grand Prix (August 21-23) is the final F1 race Zandvoort will ever host. The farewell includes the first-ever Sprint format, a sold-out Orange Army weekend, and the backdrop of Max Verstappen's unresolved future.

Formula 1 is heading to Zandvoort for the last time.

The 2026 Dutch Grand Prix, scheduled for August 21-23 at Circuit Zandvoort in the Netherlands, has been confirmed as the final Formula 1 race the iconic circuit will ever host. A planned VAT increase on event tickets in the Netherlands made the economics of the event impossible to sustain. The race, which returned to the F1 calendar in 2021 after a 35-year absence, ends its modern chapter after six seasons.

Saturday and Sunday tickets are already sold out.

Why This Race Matters

Zandvoort is not just another circuit leaving the calendar. The Dutch GP's return in 2021 was one of the most significant additions to the calendar in recent memory — driven by the phenomenon of Max Verstappen and the "Orange Army" fan base that travels in enormous numbers to watch him race. The atmosphere at Zandvoort on a Verstappen race weekend is unlike anything the sport produces elsewhere: tens of thousands of fans in coordinated orange, creating a visual spectacle that television barely captures.

Verstappen has won the modern era Dutch Grand Prix three times. The circuit, with its banked corners and flowing layout, has been one of his strongest circuits. The farewell, for both the venue and its champion, carries genuine weight.

The First-Ever Sprint at Zandvoort

Adding historical resonance to the farewell: Zandvoort's final F1 appearance will also be its first-ever Sprint weekend. The schedule runs as follows — Sprint Qualifying on Friday at 16:30 CET, the Sprint race on Saturday at 12:00 CET, Grand Prix Qualifying on Saturday at 16:00 CET, and the Grand Prix on Sunday. The Sprint adds an additional competitive result to the weekend, creating extra championship points and an extra layer of strategic complexity.

For the Antonelli-Hamilton title fight — 50 points apart after Hungary — the Sprint points matter. And for Verstappen, arriving at his home race as a driver whose future is officially unresolved, the Sprint is one more opportunity to either demonstrate what he can do in a Red Bull or to perform in front of a crowd that would erupt regardless.

The Verstappen Backdrop

The Dutch GP has additional narrative weight this year because of what it represents for Verstappen personally. His exit clause from Red Bull is active. Reports suggest Red Bull has made a major retention offer. Verstappen's camp has been quiet.

The possibility exists — and has been discussed by multiple reporters — that Verstappen's future is announced before, during, or immediately after his home race. There is no more dramatic venue for such a moment: the first time the Orange Army hears their champion tell them he's staying at Red Bull, or the first time they watch him race knowing he's headed elsewhere.

What's Next

The goodbye to Zandvoort will be well-attended, emotionally charged, and historically layered. The circuit gave modern Formula 1 one of its best venues. A VAT increase is ending it. The sport moves on — to Monza, Baku, Singapore, and eventually an Abu Dhabi or Portimão finale. Zandvoort gets a proper send-off, with a Sprint weekend, a sold-out crowd, and Max Verstappen at home one final time.
