Red Bull Offers Verstappen $80M a Year — His Camp Hasn't Responded

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | July 29, 2026

The exit clause is active. Red Bull tabled a reported $80 million per year through 2029 with the performance clause removed. According to a report from July 28, there has been no formal response from Max Verstappen's management.

Red Bull Racing has reportedly made its final offer to keep Max Verstappen. The figures being reported — approximately $80 million per year, running through 2029, with the performance clause that triggered this entire situation removed from the new contract — represent a significant escalation of the team's retention efforts.

The problem, according to a report from TheJudge13 dated July 28, is that Verstappen's management has not formally responded to the offer. The offer has reportedly been with the camp for several weeks. The silence is notable from a driver whose public statements have generally leaned toward staying — while his actions have consistently kept all doors open.

Verstappen's manager, Raymond Vermeulen, told Austrian outlet OE24 in recent weeks: "Max wants to finish his time with Red Bull. He has a contract until 2028 and would like to fulfill it." But those words haven't been followed by a signed contract, and the exit clause that Verstappen's poor championship season activated remains open for exercise until October.

Why the Clause Is Active

Verstappen entered the 2026 season as the reigning champion. The new technical regulations produced a Red Bull that struggled to match the pace of Mercedes and McLaren. At the Hungarian Grand Prix — the final race before summer break — Verstappen finished second, a result that still leaves him fifth in the championship with approximately 76 points, well outside the top two that his contract required him to hold at summer break.

That performance condition, negotiated into Verstappen's deal as a mutual protection mechanism, has now been triggered. The clause gives Verstappen the right to exit his Red Bull deal and sign elsewhere for 2027. Red Bull knew this was coming. Their response has been to table an offer that removes the clause entirely from any future arrangement.

The McLaren Question

McLaren CEO Zak Brown has spent the summer publicly insisting that Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri have their seats secured — that there is no room for a third driver regardless of circumstances. But the same summer has seen ESPN report that Brown and Verstappen held private conversations, that a three-year deal was described as "imminent" by multiple sources, and that Verstappen's race engineer Gianpiero Lambiase has already confirmed a move to McLaren in 2028.

The gap between what McLaren says publicly and what the reporting suggests is significant. Red Bull's $80 million offer is, in part, an attempt to close that gap — to make the financial cost of leaving so high that the competitive argument becomes the only remaining lever.

What's Next

The Dutch Grand Prix at Zandvoort (August 21-23) is Verstappen's home race. It is also, per multiple reports, a potential moment for an announcement — either Red Bull confirming his commitment or Verstappen triggering the clause. The October deadline gives him time to delay, but the summer will make that increasingly difficult.

For now: Red Bull has offered $80 million a year. The silence from the Verstappen camp is the most interesting thing in Formula 1. Watch the Dutch GP.
