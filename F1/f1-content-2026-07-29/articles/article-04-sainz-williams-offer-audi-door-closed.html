Sainz Gets a $30M Williams Offer — Then Bortoleto Closes the Audi Door

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | July 29, 2026

Williams reportedly offered Carlos Sainz approximately $30 million per year to extend his contract. The same week, Gabriel Bortoleto confirmed his Audi seat is sorted. Sainz is keeping his options open. The options are getting fewer.

Carlos Sainz entered this summer break with his Formula 1 future unresolved. His Williams contract expires at the end of 2026. The Audi seat — widely discussed as a potential destination for a driver of his caliber heading into a factory program — had been an open question.

In one week, two things happened.

Williams reportedly placed a retention offer on the table worth approximately $30 million per year, according to multiple reports — a figure published as both £26 million and 30 million euros across different outlets, not officially confirmed by either party. And Gabriel Bortoleto, at the Hungarian Grand Prix, confirmed publicly that his Audi seat is sorted. "My future is already sorted with Audi," Bortoleto said. The door Sainz might have been hoping to walk through closed on camera.

The Williams Offer

For a team operating in the midfield, offering $30 million a year to a driver is a substantial commitment. Williams has been through a difficult adaptation to the 2026 technical regulations, but the team's long-term investment profile under Dorilton Capital and the ongoing relationship with Mercedes engines suggests an organization building toward something rather than merely maintaining position.

The offer communicates that Williams wants Sainz specifically — not just a replacement-level driver — and is willing to compete financially to keep him. That matters to Sainz, who has been clear across his career that he wants to be treated as a team's primary asset, not a transitional placeholder.

The Narrowing Field

Martin Brundle, speaking after the Hungarian Grand Prix, said Sainz is "keeping his options open" — consistent with the Spaniard's public posture throughout the summer. He hasn't announced anything. He hasn't indicated urgency. He is managing the situation carefully.

But the Verstappen exit clause and its resolution — whenever it comes — creates a potential domino. If Verstappen leaves Red Bull, a seat opens at the fastest team in the championship. If Verstappen stays, Red Bull's lineup is set. The Verstappen situation is the upstream variable that affects everything downstream in the driver market.

What Sainz controls is his response to Williams. The offer is there. The Audi option is not. Whatever other alternatives he is exploring haven't materialized publicly. The summer break is when these decisions tend to resolve — or when the delays become their own answer.

What's Next

Sainz's decision is expected before or around the Dutch Grand Prix (August 21-23). If he signs with Williams, a significant chunk of the 2027 driver market resolves simultaneously. If he waits, the question becomes what he is waiting for — and whether the answer involves a Red Bull seat that might open in the Verstappen resolution.
