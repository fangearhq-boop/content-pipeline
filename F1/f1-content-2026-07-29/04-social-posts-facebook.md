Something has to give. The $80 million offer is Red Bull saying it will pay whatever the price. Verstappen's silence is him keeping all of his options open.

### STORY 2: Aston Martin B-spec + Alonso Retirement

#### Long-Form Post — 5:00 PM ET

Fernando Alonso was supposed to use this summer break to decide whether to retire.

Then Aston Martin debuted its AMR26 B-spec at Hungary.

The upgrade package was comprehensive: 16 components modified, including the floor, bodywork, sidepods, engine cover, diffuser, rear suspension, and both wings. The team stripped weight from the chassis, requiring re-homologation and new crash testing. The result was the best qualifying performance Aston Martin had managed all year — Alonso made Q2 for the first time in the 2026 season.

Alonso's reaction said everything: "That adrenaline when you close the visor and you go into the lap, it was missed in the last few events — we were on the last row of the grid, whatever we were doing on the car."

"Missed." He used that word.

Adrian Newey made it more complicated. The technical director confirmed the B-spec is the beginning of a planned upgrade sequence: more steps are coming at Zandvoort (August 21), Monza (September), and Baku (September). A Honda power unit upgrade focused on reliability and friction reduction is also confirmed for the Dutch GP. The car that was nowhere on the grid is becoming a car that can fight.

Alonso has won championships before. He has driven uncompetitive machinery for stretches of his career and waited. This summer break was supposed to be about whether he was done. The B-spec is asking a different question: What if the car is ready now?

### STORY 3: Dutch GP — Zandvoort's Last-Ever F1 Race

#### Long-Form Post — 12:00 PM ET

In 23 days, Formula 1 goes to Zandvoort for the last time.

The 2026 Dutch Grand Prix (August 21-23) is the final F1 race the iconic Dutch circuit will ever host. A planned VAT increase on event tickets in the Netherlands made the economics of the race unworkable, ending a revival that brought back one of the sport's most beloved venues in 2021. That original return drew more than 300,000 fans over the race weekend — a level of passion that made it one of the calendar's signature events.

The farewell comes with appropriate historic weight. It will be the first-ever Sprint weekend at Zandvoort, meaning the circuit gets an additional result and additional drama on its final F1 appearance. Saturday and Sunday tickets are sold out. The "Orange Army" — the sea of Verstappen supporters who travel from across the Netherlands and beyond — will fill the stands one last time.

And at the center of it all: Max Verstappen, whose exit clause from Red Bull is now active, racing at home with his future still officially undecided.

### STORY 5: F1 Season Finale May Move to Portugal or Italy

#### Long-Form Post — 8:00 PM ET

The 2026 Formula 1 season was already reduced in March when Bahrain and Saudi Arabia were dropped from the calendar due to regional conflict. Now, nearly four months later, the same pressure may be coming for the season's final two events.

Qatar (November 29) and Abu Dhabi (December 6) are both in the crosshairs of the same geopolitical situation that cost the calendar its February and March rounds. The World Endurance Championship has already cancelled its own Qatar and Bahrain rounds. F1 is watching.

F1 CEO Stefano Domenicali has been direct: contingency plans exist. "We have alternatives," he told reporters. Portimão, the Portuguese circuit, is the primary backup — a venue with modern facilities and a good track record from its brief F1 appearances in 2020-2021. Imola has been confirmed as an option by Domenicali personally. Las Vegas has been explicitly ruled out as the season finale.

What this means for the championship: the Andrea Kimi Antonelli and Lewis Hamilton title fight — 50 points apart after Hungary — might be decided somewhere neither of them planned. The championship has been won at Portimão before. It could happen again, under entirely different circumstances.
