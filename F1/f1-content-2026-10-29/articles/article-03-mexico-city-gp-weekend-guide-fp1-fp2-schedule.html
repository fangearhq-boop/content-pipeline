Mexico City GP Weekend Guide: Schedule, Altitude, and What to Watch

By Marcus Cole

October 29, 2026

Mexico City Grand Prix

The 2026 Mexico City Grand Prix weekend begins Friday at the Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez, and it arrives carrying more narrative weight than almost any race this season. Kimi Antonelli is poised to clinch the Drivers' Championship. The constructors' battle between McLaren and Mercedes is unresolved. And the circuit itself — sitting at 2,240 meters above sea level — creates strategic and technical conditions unlike anywhere else on the calendar.

Here is everything you need to know for the weekend ahead.

Weekend Schedule

Practice sessions begin Friday, October 30, with two sessions — FP1 and FP2 — running at the Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez. Several teams are required to field rookie drivers during FP1 as part of the current regulations, which may offer an unusual look at some of Formula 1's next generation in a high-stakes environment.

Qualifying takes place Saturday, October 31, to determine the starting grid for Sunday's race. The grid position matters at Mexico City — overtaking through the first section of the circuit is possible, but starting well always simplifies the strategy.

The race itself runs Sunday, November 1. That is also, potentially, the date on which Kimi Antonelli becomes the 2026 Formula 1 World Champion.

The Altitude Factor

Mexico City is situated at 2,240 meters above sea level — one of the highest locations on the entire Formula 1 calendar. The thin air at altitude affects nearly every aspect of how the car behaves.

Aerodynamically, the reduced air density means wings and diffusers generate less downforce than they would at sea level. Teams must run higher wing angles to compensate, which increases drag and affects straight-line speed calculations. The brakes and cooling systems behave differently because there is less air flowing over them. Engine performance shifts as well, with the power unit management programs recalibrated for the specific conditions.

The strategic implications are real. Tire degradation patterns at Mexico City differ from European circuits. The specific altitude characteristics can reward preparation — teams that have invested time understanding the peculiarities of this circuit often overperform relative to their usual level, while those who arrive less prepared can find the weekend unexpectedly challenging.

The Stadium and the Atmosphere

For fans watching on television, the Mexico City Grand Prix has one of the most distinctive visual signatures in the sport. The stadium section — a purpose-built arena that the circuit runs through in its final sector — fills with tens of thousands of spectators just meters from the track. The noise level in that section rivals a concert venue.

The race weekend also coincides with Día de Muertos, giving the event a cultural richness that sets it apart from most other rounds on the calendar. The combination of motorsport spectacle and one of Mexico's most important cultural celebrations creates an atmosphere that fans who have attended in person consistently describe as extraordinary.

What's Next

FP1 and FP2 at the Mexico City GP run Friday, October 30, giving teams their first look at how their cars perform in the altitude conditions. Watch for early pace differences between the top teams, and for Colton Herta's FP1 session with Cadillac — the American driver has a super license qualification opportunity that makes his Friday morning session particularly consequential. Qualifying Saturday will set the grid, and Sunday's race carries the possibility of crowning a world champion. Mark the whole weekend on your calendar.
