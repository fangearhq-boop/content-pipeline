Colton Herta's F1 Super License Dream Comes Down to One Session Friday

By Ryan Calloway

October 29, 2026

Formula 1 Driver News

When Friday morning's FP1 session begins at the Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez, most of the 22 drivers on the grid will be treating it as an ordinary data-gathering exercise. For Colton Herta, it is something else entirely. The American needs to complete at least 100 kilometers — roughly 24 laps — without incurring a penalty in order to earn the final super license point required for an F1 seat in 2027. This is his last chance.

The previous window was Monza in September. A hydraulic failure ended his session before he could reach the required distance. He left Italy without the points, without the license, and with everything riding on Mexico City. There are no more sessions after this one.

What Super License Points Mean

Formula 1 requires all drivers to hold a super license before they can compete, and that license requires a specific number of points accumulated through performance in other series. The FIA awards super license points for strong results in feeder series, IndyCar, and a range of other championships — but they also award points for drivers who complete significant mileage in mandatory FP1 sessions for Formula 1 teams.

Herta needs 100 kilometers Friday — the minimum threshold for the points to count. It is not a demanding target on paper. In practice, however, those kilometers require a car that runs cleanly for an extended stretch. At Monza, a hydraulic failure denied him before he reached the mark. That is the kind of mechanical circumstance over which a driver has no control, and the memory of it makes Friday's session feel considerably more fraught.

A Long Road to This Moment

Herta has been one of IndyCar's most talented drivers for several years, and his association with Cadillac's Formula 1 program represents the most serious effort by an American driver to reach the pinnacle of motorsport in the current era. The path through super license qualification is technically straightforward but logistically demanding, requiring both the right team relationships and a series of successful outings.

He has come close. He has been qualified to attempt the license. And he has watched a mechanical failure take it away once this season. Friday is not just another practice session for a young driver hoping to learn the car. It is the moment the whole effort either reaches its natural conclusion or comes up short once more.

The 100-kilometer requirement has a built-in brutality. A driver can complete 90 kilometers flawlessly and have the car fail in lap 22, and the effort counts for nothing. Herta needs the car to hold together and the session to run smoothly for a sustained period. That is a reasonable ask — but it is still an ask.

What a 2027 F1 Seat Would Mean

American drivers in Formula 1 have historically been rare. The series has been dominated by European and South American talent, and the barriers to entry for American competitors — from training pathways to team relationships — have remained high. Herta's presence in an F1 car in 2027 would represent a meaningful step for the American motorsport audience and for the sport's efforts to grow its footprint in the United States.

The sport has invested heavily in building its American fanbase in recent years, and having a competitive American driver on the grid carries significant commercial and sporting value. Herta has the talent. He needs the paperwork. He needs 100 kilometers Friday morning.

What's Next

FP1 begins at the Mexico City Grand Prix on Friday, October 30. Herta will take to the Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez in the Cadillac car, and the countdown to 100 kilometers begins from the moment he exits the pitlane. If he reaches the mark without a penalty, the super license point is his. If the car fails or circumstances intervene, his 2027 F1 plans will need a fundamental rethink. The entire American motorsport community will be watching Friday morning with an interest that goes well beyond lap times and sector splits.
