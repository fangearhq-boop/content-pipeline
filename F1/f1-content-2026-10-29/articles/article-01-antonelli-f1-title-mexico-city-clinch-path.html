Kimi Antonelli Needs One Good Result to Win the 2026 F1 World Championship at Mexico City

By Ryan Calloway

October 29, 2026

Formula 1 Championship

The 2026 Formula 1 World Championship is on the verge of being decided, and it could happen Sunday in Mexico City. Kimi Antonelli, 19 years old and in his first full season of Formula 1, leads the Drivers' Championship by 123 points over teammate George Russell and needs only a ninth-place finish or better at the Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez to secure the title — regardless of what anyone else does.

A season that began with enormous expectations for the Italian prodigy has exceeded nearly all of them. Nine race victories. A championship lead that has never seriously been threatened since the middle of the year. A cool confidence that belies his age. Sunday, November 1, could be the night it all becomes official.

The Mathematics

Antonelli sits on 365 points. Russell is second with 242 and Hamilton third with 241. The gap to Russell is 123 points, and even if Russell were to outscore Antonelli by the maximum possible margin in every remaining race, the math heavily favors the title being decided at Mexico City.

Specifically: Antonelli clinches the championship if he finishes ninth or better, regardless of how Russell and Hamilton perform. He can also clinch without scoring at all if neither rival wins the race. The tiebreak advantage belongs to Antonelli regardless — his nine race victories are more than Russell or Hamilton can match even by winning every race remaining this season.

The only scenario in which the title does not go to Antonelli Sunday is if he finishes outside the points while both Russell and Hamilton score heavily. Even then, it would only extend the decision to a later date, not genuinely put the title in doubt.

A Season to Remember

What Antonelli has done in 2026 will be discussed for years. Teams typically expect rookies — even highly regarded ones — to spend their first seasons learning the machinery, absorbing pressure, and making calculated mistakes. Antonelli did none of that. He won early, he won often, and he treated the championship race like something to be seized rather than survived.

Nine victories represents a stunning haul. He won the United States Grand Prix in Austin most recently, a result that effectively moved this title conversation from "when" to "where." Mexico City is the answer.

"First, I have to get the job done in Mexico," Antonelli said this week when asked about his choice of car number going forward. The phrasing is perfect — acknowledgment of what is expected, awareness that it is not finished, and the quiet confidence of someone who has earned the right to be confident.

Mexico City as the Stage

The Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez sits at 2,240 meters above sea level, making it one of the most unusual circuits on the Formula 1 calendar. Thin air creates different aerodynamic and engine performance characteristics than drivers and teams experience at lower-altitude venues. The legendary stadium section fills with color and noise in a way that few sporting venues anywhere in the world can match.

For Antonelli, the specific demands of Mexico City are largely irrelevant. He needs to start the race and finish in the top nine. That is a target well within his capabilities, and the team behind him is experienced enough to navigate any strategic complexity that arises.

What's Next

Formula 1 qualifying takes place Saturday at the Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez, with the race following Sunday, November 1. If Antonelli finishes ninth or better, the celebration begins immediately after the checkered flag. If circumstances conspire against him, the title fight moves to the next race — but the championship lead is so large that his coronation feels inevitable in the very near future. The whole of Formula 1 will be watching Sunday, and what they expect to see is history being made.
