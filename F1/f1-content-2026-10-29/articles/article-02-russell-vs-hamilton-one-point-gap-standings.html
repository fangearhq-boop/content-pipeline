Russell and Hamilton Are Separated by One Point — And It's About to Get Interesting

By Elena Voss

October 29, 2026

Formula 1 Championship

Everyone is watching Kimi Antonelli count down the points to the 2026 World Championship. But there is another story in the F1 standings that deserves attention, and it is considerably more tense: George Russell and Lewis Hamilton are separated by exactly one point in the battle for second place, with three races remaining and Mexico City up next.

Russell has 242. Hamilton has 241. One point between them, and the Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez on Sunday as the next venue to settle the question. Both are Mercedes teammates. Neither is willing to concede.

A One-Point Gap at This Stage

One championship point is nothing. It is the difference between finishing sixth and finishing seventh. It is a fastest lap bonus that one driver had and the other did not. It is the kind of margin that can be reversed in a single pit-stop timing decision, a single corner battle, or a single safety car phase that catches one driver in the wrong part of the circuit.

What makes the Russell-Hamilton situation particularly compelling is the layered dynamic at play. They race for the same constructor. Their points contribute to the same team tally. And yet, as drivers chasing a historic individual honor, they have every incentive to take points from each other whenever the opportunity presents itself.

Formula 1 team orders are a perpetual source of controversy, and the question of whether Mercedes will intervene in this battle — or stay out of it entirely — adds a management subplot to every remaining race. If Antonelli clinches the title Sunday, his teammates will be racing purely for their own standings position, and the intensity of that fight will only increase.

Hamilton's Remarkable Season

Perhaps the most underappreciated story of 2026 is Lewis Hamilton's consistency and form throughout the year. After seasons that carried question marks about his trajectory and whether the dominant chapter of his career had passed, Hamilton has driven with precision and aggression in equal measure.

Sitting 241 points into the championship, within one point of his teammate, is not a position that happens by accident. Hamilton has earned every point he carries into Mexico City, and the prospect of him finishing the season as championship runner-up — however unusual the circumstances — would represent a meaningful statement about where he still stands among the sport's elite.

Russell's Position

Russell entered this season widely viewed as the heir apparent at Mercedes — the driver who would carry the team's ambitions once the transition from the previous era was complete. His 242-point haul reflects a season of quality, even as Antonelli's brilliance has rather complicated the narrative of who is leading this team into the future.

Leading Hamilton by one point at this stage means Russell has done what he needed to do. But it also means one bad weekend erases his advantage entirely. Mexico City, São Paulo, and Abu Dhabi are three opportunities for either driver to pull clear — and three opportunities for the other to take the upper hand.

What's Next

Sunday's Mexico City Grand Prix is the first of three remaining races in the 2026 Formula 1 season, and every point in the Russell-Hamilton battle will matter. While Antonelli focuses on securing the title, his Mercedes teammates will be racing each other as intensely as they race anyone else. The constructors' championship adds another dimension, as every point scored by both Mercedes drivers contributes to the team's battle with McLaren. The one-point gap heading into Mexico is one of the tightest and most compelling secondary narratives in Formula 1 this season, and it is far from resolved.
