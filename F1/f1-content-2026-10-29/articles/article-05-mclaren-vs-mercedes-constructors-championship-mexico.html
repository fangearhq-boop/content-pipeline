While Antonelli Closes In on the Drivers' Title, the Constructors' Championship Is Far From Over

By Elena Voss

October 29, 2026

Formula 1 Championship

The Drivers' Championship story in 2026 Formula 1 has been Kimi Antonelli's. He has been the dominant force, the consistent winner, and he arrives at Mexico City on the verge of making it official. But the Constructors' Championship — awarded to the team whose drivers collectively score the most points across the season — remains genuinely undecided, and Mexico City is the next important chapter in that fight.

McLaren and Mercedes have been at the center of the constructors' battle all year. With three races remaining and significant points available, neither team can afford to leave Mexico City without a strong haul.

Why the Constructors' Title Matters

For teams at the highest level of Formula 1, the Constructors' Championship represents something beyond individual glory. It is tied directly to the financial distribution from Formula 1 Management — teams receive a larger share of the sport's commercial revenues based on their finishing position in the Constructors' standings. Higher finishing position means more money, which flows back into car development, driver salaries, and the infrastructure that determines whether a team can compete at the front in future seasons.

A championship win in the Constructors' classification, even in a year where the Drivers' title went to a single standout performer, represents a meaningful achievement and a meaningful financial difference. For both McLaren and Mercedes, winning this battle matters beyond the trophy.

Mexico City's Strategic Variables

The Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez at 2,240 meters elevation is one of the calendar's most technically demanding venues for car setup. The thin air at altitude forces engineering compromises that play out differently for different car philosophies. A car optimized for high-downforce circuits might need more adjustment than one already calibrated for medium-drag configurations. The tire management profile at Mexico City is specific, and teams that have data from previous visits — and know how to use it — gain an advantage.

These variables mean the constructors' battle could shift meaningfully based on which team brings the better package this weekend. A well-timed double points finish from McLaren, or a standout performance from the full Mercedes lineup, can reshape the gap between the teams heading into the final two rounds in São Paulo and Abu Dhabi.

Three Races to Decide It

Mexico City, São Paulo, and Abu Dhabi make up the remaining calendar. With 25 points available for a race win, plus bonus points for fastest lap and sprint results where applicable, the total points still in play means no team can assume the battle is over.

Even if Antonelli clinches the Drivers' title Sunday — which he is positioned to do — the constructors' outcome requires full concentration from both teams through to the end of the season. The two titles will almost certainly be won by the same constructor, given Antonelli's dominance, but the margin of victory in the constructors' fight will be what defines how the 2026 season is ultimately remembered in the team standings.

What's Next

FP1 and FP2 on Friday will give both McLaren and Mercedes their first data from this year's Mexico City configuration, and the early pace comparison will be closely watched. Qualifying Saturday and the race Sunday will deliver the actual points. By the time the Mexico City GP is over, the constructors' picture for the final two races will be clearer. Whether McLaren or Mercedes holds the edge heading into São Paulo will depend almost entirely on how this weekend unfolds.
