Verstappen's Exit Clause, His Home Race, and the Most Emotionally Loaded Grand Prix in Recent F1 History

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | August 10, 2026

F1 Driver News / Max Verstappen / Red Bull Racing / McLaren

Eleven days from now, Max Verstappen will climb into a Red Bull racing car at Circuit Zandvoort — his home circuit, in front of tens of thousands of Dutch fans who have followed him from karting to four world championships — with his future at the team completely unresolved. The exit clause is active. The Red Bull counter-offer is unanswered. The McLaren rumors are alive. The Orange Army is about to have a very complicated weekend.

How the Clause Was Triggered

Verstappen's contract with Red Bull included a performance-based exit clause that was triggered when his championship standings fell outside the top two through the British Grand Prix period. After 11 rounds of a season defined by the new regulations, Verstappen sits sixth in the Drivers' Championship with 109 points — 0 wins, 4 podiums, and a public frustration with the 2026 technical ruleset that he has barely attempted to conceal. He has called the new regulations "Formula E on steroids."

The clause gives Verstappen the right to leave Red Bull at the end of 2026. It comes with no compensation to the team. The decision window runs from August through mid-October.

The Counter-Offer Red Bull Can't Force Him to Accept

Red Bull reportedly made their counter-offer in an attempt to keep their four-time world champion: approximately €65 million per year through 2029. It is an extraordinary number — but the offer is meaningless if Verstappen doesn't want to stay. His management team, led by Raymond Vermeulen, has reportedly held the offer for weeks without formally responding.

Verstappen's public comments have been conspicuously non-committal. He has acknowledged considering his future but declined to confirm or deny the McLaren rumors or the Red Bull counter-offer. Helmut Marko has said Verstappen will fulfill his 2026 contract — which everyone agrees on — but Marko notably cannot say what happens after that.

The McLaren Dimension

Multiple outlets have reported McLaren discussions as being in "closing stages" — the rumored structure being Verstappen to McLaren and Oscar Piastri moving in the other direction to Red Bull. McLaren's Zak Brown publicly denied it. Piastri's manager Mark Webber publicly denied it. Neither denial was emphatic enough to kill the story.

McLaren won their first race of 2026 at the Hungarian Grand Prix with Lando Norris. They are a competitive team with a capable car — arguably a better car than the RB22 this season. For a driver who has gone winless across 11 races, the appeal of a more competitive package is not hard to understand.

The Home Race Dimension

Zandvoort has been one of Verstappen's personal stages. The combination of his Dutch fan base, the compact circuit that suits his driving style, and the proximity to his home country creates an atmosphere that is unlike any other race on the calendar. Racing here — for what may be the final time in the circuit's F1 history and possibly the final time with a Red Bull on his car — is a moment with layers of significance that extend beyond the pure sporting result.

What's Next

The actual decision deadline is mid-October — not August. Verstappen is not required to announce anything at Zandvoort. But the Dutch Grand Prix, running August 21-23, will be the most intense period of scrutiny yet. Every interview answer, every radio transmission, every paddock interaction will be analyzed for signals. The exit clause decision window remains open. The Orange Army will be watching. So will every team principal in the paddock.
