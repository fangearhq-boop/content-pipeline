Two New Teams, One Midseason: Audi Commits Long-Term While Cadillac Already Moves On From 2026

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | August 10, 2026

F1 Team News / Audi / Cadillac / Gabriel Bortoleto / Valtteri Bottas

Two teams entered the 2026 Formula 1 season for the first time. Six months in, their approaches to the second half of their debut year look completely different — and both responses make complete sense given where each team stands.

Audi: Locking In the Future

Gabriel Bortoleto has signed a multi-year contract extension with the Audi Revolut F1 Team. The 22-year-old Brazilian, who won back-to-back championships in Formula 3 in 2024 and Formula 2 in 2025 before joining F1 alongside Nico Hulkenberg, will stay with the project for "many years" — the specific term undisclosed, but the commitment made explicitly public during the summer break.

Bortoleto's retention is a significant vote of confidence in Audi's F1 project. He is one of the most highly regarded young drivers to enter the sport in recent seasons, and his willingness to commit long-term signals belief that the Audi program has the infrastructure and ambition to compete at the front of the grid eventually. McLaren, Bortoleto's former junior team, had loaned him to the Audi/Sauber project; the extension locks in his Audi future regardless of any McLaren interest.

Bortoleto has also taken a notably different public stance on the 2026 regulations compared to veterans like Max Verstappen. Where Verstappen has described the new rules as frustratingly software-dependent, Bortoleto argued during the summer break that fellow drivers "miss the point about software's influence," adding: "We're developing the technology of the future." The perspective of a driver who has never raced under any other F1 regulations stands in instructive contrast to the complaints of those who miss what came before.

Audi has scored points and climbed the Constructors' standings. The team's Barcelona performance — where a power unit upgrade delivered a significant step — suggested the project is ahead of the development curve most new entrants face in year one.

Cadillac: Honest at Midseason

Valtteri Bottas was candid. Cadillac, he said in a statement during the summer break, will "imminently" cease development of the 2026 car and redirect all engineering resources toward the 2027 challenger.

"We still have something coming performance-wise," Bottas acknowledged, "but no magic, nothing huge. So obviously there is a point that we need to shift our focus 100 per cent for next year, which is coming soon."

Cadillac arrived in F1 as the 11th team on the grid — pairing Bottas and Sergio Perez, who combine for more than 500 career race starts and significant winning experience between them. They are competing as a midfield-to-backmarker outfit in their debut season, which is entirely consistent with what new teams have historically delivered in year one. The decision to acknowledge that the 2026 car has reached its development ceiling and commit fully to 2027 is not an admission of failure. It's a strategic choice that prioritizes long-term competitiveness over marginal short-term gains.

What These Stories Tell Us

Two new teams, two very different positions. Audi is building toward something and locking in the people who will build it. Cadillac is being transparent about where the first car stands and redirecting accordingly. Neither is a story of failure — both are, in different ways, stories of a team at a crossroads making a clear-eyed decision about how to navigate it.

Both stories also arrive at a moment when F1's newest entrants are defining what their long-term presence in the sport looks like. The decisions made in the summer of 2026 — who stays, what gets built, where resources go — will shape the 2027 season and beyond.

What's Next

Audi and Cadillac both return to competition at the Dutch Grand Prix, August 21-23 at Circuit Zandvoort. For Bortoleto, the second half of the season is the first opportunity to demonstrate his multi-year commitment in race results. For Cadillac, the remaining 2026 races are now effectively a long audition for 2027 — whatever data and development insights the team can extract from the current car before pivoting will inform the next generation chassis that Bottas and Perez are counting on.
