11 Days to the Final Zandvoort Race — and This Dutch Grand Prix Carries More Weight Than Any That Came Before It

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | August 10, 2026

F1 Race Preview / Dutch Grand Prix / Zandvoort

Eleven days. Circuit Zandvoort hosts the Dutch Grand Prix on August 21-23, and when the checkered flag falls on Sunday the 23rd, it will be the last time Formula 1 ever races there. Six years of the Orange Army. Six years of the most atmospheric, most passionate, most distinctly Dutch motorsport experience on the calendar. And it ends this month.

But the farewell is only one of several reasons this race stands apart from everything else on the 2026 schedule.

The Final Race at a Beloved Circuit

Zandvoort rejoined the Formula 1 calendar in 2021 after a 35-year absence, and it immediately became one of the most beloved venues in the sport. The compact, challenging circuit winds through the dunes of the Dutch coast, with banked corners that create overtaking opportunities unlike anything else on the calendar. The grandstands sell out the moment tickets go on sale. The Dutch fan base — the "Orange Army" — travels in numbers that turn the surrounding area into a sea of orange for an entire week.

August 23 is the last chapter of that story. F1 is moving on. The circuit that welcomed the sport back after decades away will not be on the 2027 calendar. Racing in the Dutch dunes ends this month.

A First-Ever Sprint at Zandvoort

Adding to the significance: this is the first time Zandvoort has hosted a Sprint format. The weekend structure runs Friday Practice 1 and Sprint Qualifying, Saturday Sprint Race and Qualifying for the Grand Prix, and Sunday the race itself. That means additional competitive sessions, more points available, and more chances for the championship picture to shift before the Sunday main event. In a season where 50 points separate Kimi Antonelli from Lewis Hamilton at the top of the standings, every Sprint result matters.

The Honda Power Unit Debut

During the mandatory summer factory shutdown, Aston Martin Honda ran a significantly updated power unit at the Hungaroring on a permitted filming day. The changes are reported to include revised combustion chamber and pre-chamber geometry, along with updates to the lubrication system. The estimated performance improvement: 30 to 50 horsepower.

That upgrade makes its competition debut at Zandvoort. For Fernando Alonso — who famously described the current unit as "less powerful than an F2 car" — the Dutch Grand Prix is potentially the first race where the equipment matches his expectations. The Honda upgrade is also the variable that determines whether Alonso announces his retirement or continues into 2027.

The Verstappen Factor

Max Verstappen's exit clause is active. His future at Red Bull is unresolved. His home race — at the circuit where his dominance has been on full display for years, in front of a crowd that treats him as a national hero — is 11 days away. Whatever the paddock looks like at Zandvoort will tell a significant part of the story about where the sport's most recent dominant force is headed.

What's Next

F1 factories reopen August 17, seven days from now. Teams return to work and begin race preparation for Zandvoort. The Dutch Grand Prix weekend runs August 21-23, with the Sprint on Saturday and the Grand Prix on Sunday. When it ends, Circuit Zandvoort closes its Formula 1 chapter. The second half of the 2026 season begins. Everything that has been building through the summer break arrives at once.
