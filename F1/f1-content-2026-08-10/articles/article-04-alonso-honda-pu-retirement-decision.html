Fernando Alonso Said His Retirement Decision Comes After the Summer. The Summer Ends in Seven Days.

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | August 10, 2026

F1 Driver News / Fernando Alonso / Aston Martin / Honda

Fernando Alonso has given the F1 world a clear timeline: his decision about whether 2026 is his final season in Formula 1 will come "after the summer." The summer — for F1 purposes — ends August 17, when the mandatory factory shutdown concludes and teams return to work ahead of the Dutch Grand Prix. That is seven days from now.

The Honda power unit that could tip his decision debuts in competition at Zandvoort. The announcement window is open.

Why Alonso Has Been Waiting

Alonso, 45, has not hidden his frustration with the Aston Martin Honda package in 2026. At the Belgian Grand Prix at Spa-Francorchamps, he made his feelings explicit on team radio, describing the power unit as performing below Formula 2 standards on the straights. The comment was pointed, public, and characteristic of a driver who does not soften his assessments for the cameras.

The 2026 regulations introduced the most significant technical overhaul in Formula 1 history — a new 50/50 split between internal combustion and electric power, with a dramatically more powerful motor generator unit deployed at all times rather than only under specific conditions. The new architecture has produced massive performance differentials between power unit suppliers. Mercedes is dominant. Honda — Aston Martin's supplier — has struggled to keep pace in the early part of the season.

Alonso's calculation has been straightforward: if the car is not competitive enough to fight for wins, there is no meaningful reason to continue. If the upgrade closes the gap, the calculus changes.

The Honda Upgrade

During the mandatory summer shutdown, Honda ran an updated power unit at the Hungaroring using Aston Martin's permitted filming day allocation. The changes — involving revised combustion chamber and pre-chamber geometry along with lubrication updates — are expected to produce a meaningful improvement in straight-line performance. Paddock estimates suggest 30 to 50 additional horsepower at race deployment levels.

Aston Martin's sporting director has described the team as "bullish" about the second half of the season. Adrian Newey, who joined the team as chief technical officer this year, is leading what sources describe as an ambitious 2027 car development program. The pieces for a genuinely competitive Aston Martin next year are, at least on paper, coming together.

What Newey's Presence Means

Newey's decision to join Aston Martin — the most sought-after engineering mind in modern motorsport — was itself a signal about the team's trajectory. He would not have moved to a project without confidence in its direction. His presence arguably strengthens the case for Alonso continuing more than any single power unit upgrade could.

Ted Kravitz and others in the F1 media community have publicly predicted Alonso's retirement announcement as imminent. Aston Martin's own Adrian Newey, however, has described himself as "pretty confident" Alonso will stay. Both assessments acknowledge the same thing: the decision has not been made publicly yet, but it is coming very soon.

What's Next

The factory shutdown ends August 17. Alonso's Aston Martin team returns to work immediately. The Dutch Grand Prix begins August 21. If the Honda power unit delivers — if Zandvoort produces the first real evidence that Alonso's car can compete at the front — the argument for continuing into 2027 grows considerably stronger. The Dutch GP paddock is the most likely setting for the first concrete news. Seven days until the question begins to get answered.
