Max Verstappen arrives at Circuit Zandvoort carrying more uncertainty than he has at any point in his dominant F1 career. His exit clause is active. His future at Red Bull is genuinely unresolved. And the Orange Army — tens of thousands of Dutch fans who turned this circuit into one of the most electric atmospheres in motorsport — will be watching every move. Here's where things stand: **The clause:** Verstappen's contract included a performance-based exit clause that was triggered after the British Grand Prix, when his results left him outside the top two in the championship standings. The clause gives him the right to leave Red Bull at the end of 2026. The decision window runs from August through mid-October. **Red Bull's counter-offer:** According to reports, Red Bull offered Verstappen approximately €65 million per year through 2029 — an extraordinary sum to waive the clause and stay. His management team (Raymond Vermeulen) has reportedly had the offer for weeks. No formal response has emerged publicly. **McLaren:** Multiple sources describe McLaren talks as being in "closing stages." The rumored structure involves Verstappen to McLaren and current McLaren driver Oscar Piastri moving to Red Bull. McLaren's Zak Brown and Piastri's manager Mark Webber have publicly denied. Verstappen himself has declined to commit publicly in any direction. **The home race dimension:** Verstappen has won at Zandvoort multiple times. The circuit is essentially his backyard — Verstappen Nation turns out in full force for the Dutch GP in ways that create an atmosphere unlike anything else on the calendar. Racing here, in front of that crowd, in his likely final season as a Red Bull driver, is a moment that will define how the Verstappen era at the team ends. The decision doesn't have to come at Zandvoort. But the Dutch GP is where the emotional weight of it all arrives. ⏳ ---

### Long-Form Post 🆕 Two new F1 teams entered the 2026 season. Six months in, their trajectories couldn't look more different. **Audi: Long-Term Committed** Gabriel Bortoleto has signed a multi-year contract extension with the Audi Revolut F1 Team. The 22-year-old Brazilian rookie — who won back-to-back championships in F3 (2024) and F2 (2025) — will stay with the project for "many years." Bortoleto declined to reveal the specific contract length, but the message is clear: Audi is building around him. Bortoleto has also taken an interesting public stance on the 2026 regulations. While established stars like Max Verstappen have publicly criticized the new rules as overly software-dependent, Bortoleto has pushed back: "We're developing the technology of the future," he said. "People miss the point about software's influence." As a first-year driver entering the sport under the new rules, he has no previous standards to compare against — perhaps the ideal mindset for adapting to a new era. Audi has scored points and jumped Williams in the Constructors' standings. The project is still early — but it's pointed in the right direction. **Cadillac: Honesty at Midseason** Valtteri Bottas told it straight: Cadillac will stop developing the 2026 car "soon," he said, adding: "There is a point that we need to shift our focus 100 per cent for next year, which is coming soon." The Cadillac F1 Team — in its debut season with Bottas and Sergio Perez — is a midfield/backmarker operation in 2026. That assessment isn't a critique; it's the reality for a team that entered F1 in its first year of existence. The decision to stop developing the 2026 car and commit fully to 2027 is strategically sound — and unusually candid. Two teams. Same year. One building for the long term and saying so clearly. One acknowledging the first car has hit its ceiling and pivoting already. Both choices make sense. Both tell you exactly where these programs stand. 🏁 ---
