Norris Looks to Carry Brazil Momentum into Las Vegas After McLaren's Race-Winning Drive

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | November 17, 2026

Lando Norris walked away from the São Paulo Grand Prix with one of the drives of the 2026 season.

In wet, tricky conditions at the Brazilian Grand Prix on November 8, the McLaren driver put together a commanding performance to take the race win ahead of Max Verstappen and Isack Hadjar. It was the kind of masterclass that separates the very best drivers from the rest — patient when patience was required, aggressive when the moment arrived, and ultimately decisive when it mattered most.

Now Norris heads to Las Vegas carrying that momentum, with McLaren's MCL40 looking as sharp as it has all season.

What Brazil Showed

Wet-weather racing is often considered the truest test of a driver's ability. The reduced grip, the reduced visibility, and the heightened risk of terminal mistakes demand a combination of feel, technical precision, and composure under pressure. Norris delivered all three in Brazil.

His win also confirmed that McLaren remain a genuine race-winning force in 2026, even with the Drivers' and Constructors' championships already secured by Mercedes. The Papaya operation has built a car capable of defeating anyone on the right day, and Norris has consistently delivered those days throughout the season.

Las Vegas Suits McLaren

The Las Vegas Strip Circuit's high-speed sections and long straights should theoretically suit a car with strong straight-line performance and efficient aerodynamics — a description that fits the MCL40. McLaren have shown pace in power-sensitive environments this year, and the team will be optimistic about their prospects heading into Thursday's practice sessions.

Norris himself is a driver who thrives when his car is working, and when his confidence is high. After Brazil, both conditions appear to be met.

No Title Pressure, All Motivation

With Antonelli having claimed the championship at Mexico City, Norris races the final three rounds purely for wins, glory, and legacy. That freedom from title pressure can liberate a driver to take calculated risks and chase pace without conservative strategy management. For McLaren, the final three races are an opportunity to demonstrate that the development trajectory heading into 2027 is upward.

What's Next

Las Vegas is next, and the Norris who showed up in Brazil has every reason to be confident. The question is whether the McLaren package translates from wet São Paulo to the unique demands of a dry Saturday night on the Strip. If it does, the Papaya car could be a frontrunner once again. After a weekend like Brazil, it would be unwise to bet against Lando Norris doing something special in the neon lights of Nevada.
