Mercedes on the Brink: A Double Championship Clinch at Austin Is Possible

The drivers' championship could end Sunday at Circuit of the Americas. The constructors' title might not be far behind. Mercedes is four days from potentially sweeping it all.

By Marcus Cole | October 19, 2026 | F1 Fanrecap

The Numbers

Mercedes leads the Formula 1 Constructors' Championship with 578 points. Ferrari is second with 444 points. The gap is 134 points. There are six races remaining in the 2026 season, which means a maximum of 150 points are available per car — roughly 175 total per team if both drivers score maximum.

For Ferrari to close a 134-point constructors' gap, they would need to outscore Mercedes significantly in every remaining race while Mercedes has mechanical failures, collisions, or strategic disasters. That combination is not impossible, but it has not been the pattern of this season.

The constructors' title, like the drivers', is on its way to Mercedes.

Austin as the Double

The possibility of a double championship clinch at Circuit of the Americas is real. If Kimi Antonelli wins the race and George Russell finishes ninth or lower, the drivers' championship is sealed. The constructors' math is separate, but if Mercedes delivers a strong points haul from Austin while Ferrari does not, the gap could reach a level where the constructors' title becomes mathematically secure.

The exact constructors' clinch scenario depends on lap-by-lap results that cannot be predicted before Thursday's practice opens. But the broader point is clear: Mercedes is positioned to end both championships in the next two or three races, and Austin is the first opportunity.

The Post-Hamilton Story

When Lewis Hamilton announced his move to Ferrari ahead of the 2026 season, the questions about Mercedes were immediate. Was this the end of an era? Could the team maintain its competitiveness without the driver who had led seven of its eight constructors' championships?

The 2026 season has answered those questions decisively. Kimi Antonelli, in his second full Formula 1 season, leads the drivers' championship by 102 points. George Russell, his teammate, is second overall. Mercedes has run one-two in the standings for most of the year. The car has been quick, the strategy has been sound, and Antonelli has delivered performances that go beyond anything a team could reasonably have projected for a 20-year-old in his second season.

Mercedes did not rebuild around Hamilton's departure — they were already building around what came next. That what-came-next is Antonelli, and he is four days from delivering a championship that would stand as one of the most remarkable in the sport's history.

Ferrari's Position

Ferrari enters Austin with 444 constructors' points and two strong drivers in Hamilton and Charles Leclerc. They are not out of the championship — they simply do not have a viable path to close 134 points in six races unless Mercedes collapses. That has not happened and there is no reason to expect it.

What Ferrari can do is race for wins. Leclerc finished second in Singapore. Hamilton has five US Grand Prix victories at COTA in his career. The team has the pace to challenge at the front, and Austin is a circuit where their car characteristics apply.

This is not a story about Ferrari failing. It is a story about Mercedes being better, for longer, at the moments that mattered most.

What Sunday Represents

If both championships close in Austin on October 25, it would be the culmination of a season that has been, by almost any measure, a Mercedes statement year. The Silver Arrows led from the front, developed through the season, and kept their two drivers — one an established frontrunner, one a 20-year-old who arrived as a prospect — in first and second place for the entirety of the championship fight.

Four days from now, the Circuit of the Americas could host the end of both titles. It would be a remarkable Sunday in Austin.
