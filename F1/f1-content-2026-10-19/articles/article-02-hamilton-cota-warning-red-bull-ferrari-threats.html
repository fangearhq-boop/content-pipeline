Hamilton's COTA Warning: Red Bull and Ferrari Are the Threats at Austin

Lewis Hamilton knows Circuit of the Americas better than almost anyone. Before his first Austin weekend in Ferrari red, he has already identified the competition.

By Marcus Cole | October 19, 2026 | F1 Fanrecap

The Warning

Lewis Hamilton headed into the Austin preview week with a clear assessment: Red Bull and Ferrari are the teams to beat at Circuit of the Americas. The statement is notable partly for what it reveals about Hamilton's read of the competitive landscape at this specific circuit — and partly because, for the first time, Hamilton is on one of the teams he's flagging as a threat.

He drives for Ferrari now. He's warning about Red Bull. And somewhere in that geometry is the full complexity of Hamilton's 2026 season.

Why COTA Suits Red Bull

The basis for Hamilton's concern about Red Bull is not abstract. Max Verstappen won the US Grand Prix three consecutive years — 2021, 2022, and 2023. He holds the COTA lap record at 1:36.169, set in 2023. Red Bull's car characteristics — strong traction out of slow corners, effective aerodynamic balance at high speed — suit COTA's demands precisely.

Red Bull has not won at Circuit of the Americas since that 2023 result. The team is fourth in the constructors' standings, down from the dominance of previous seasons. But COTA remains a circuit where their setup philosophy historically clicks, and Verstappen knows every meter of it.

Hamilton has been at COTA many times in the years when Red Bull was dominant. He understands what it looks like when their car works there. What he's saying is that it might work there again.

Ferrari at COTA

The second half of Hamilton's warning concerns his own team. Ferrari runs well at Circuit of the Americas. Charles Leclerc finished second in Singapore. The Ferrari has been a quick car in 2026 — third in the constructors' standings with 444 points — and COTA's technical demands do not obviously disadvantage it.

The irony of Hamilton warning about Ferrari from inside Ferrari is real, but not strange when you understand what he's actually doing: he's acknowledging that the car he's driving has the capability to be a front-runner at Austin, while also recognizing that other cars do too. It's competitive honesty about what the grid looks like.

Singapore's Shadow

Hamilton arrives at COTA carrying the aftermath of Singapore. Two weeks ago at Marina Bay, he was called to the pit lane under a Virtual Safety Car. He stayed out instead, overruling his Ferrari engineers. He lost a podium. Fred Vasseur said post-race that Hamilton would have finished second or third if he had followed the team's call. Hamilton apologized.

It is the kind of moment that follows a driver into the next race weekend. Whether Hamilton and Ferrari have fully processed it — whether the dynamic in the garage has fully normalized — is one of the more interesting subplots heading into Austin.

This is Hamilton's first Formula 1 season at Ferrari. COTA is a circuit he dominated in other colors for years. How he performs there in red, and whether he and the team are on the same page, will say something about where this partnership stands with six races remaining.

Five COTA Wins. Zero With Ferrari.

Hamilton has won the US Grand Prix five times at Circuit of the Americas — in 2012, 2014, 2015, 2017, and 2019. All five came with Mercedes. He knows the circuit intimately, from entry points to braking zones to the specific weight transfer characteristics through the first sector.

Sunday, October 25, is the first time he will attempt to win at COTA in a Ferrari. He has already told you who he thinks stands in the way.
