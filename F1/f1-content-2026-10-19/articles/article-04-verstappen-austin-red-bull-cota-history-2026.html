Verstappen at Austin: Red Bull's Best COTA Shot in Two Years

Three consecutive US Grand Prix wins. The current lap record. Max Verstappen owns Circuit of the Americas the way few drivers own any circuit. Austin 2026 is his best chance to remind the paddock why.

By Ryan Calloway | October 19, 2026 | F1 Fanrecap

The Streak

From 2021 to 2023, Max Verstappen won the United States Grand Prix three years running. Each win was a statement: his 2021 victory over Hamilton in a championship battle, his 2022 win as Red Bull reached peak dominance, his 2023 victory in which he set the current lap record of 1:36.169.

Red Bull has not won at Circuit of the Americas since that 2023 result. Two editions of the US Grand Prix have come and gone without a Red Bull on the top step. For a team and driver that consider this one of their circuits, that absence carries weight.

Where Red Bull Stands in 2026

Red Bull is fourth in the constructors' standings with 306 points. Verstappen is fourth in the drivers' championship with 221 points, 119 points behind championship leader Kimi Antonelli. The title is mathematically closed for Verstappen in practical terms — there are only 150 points left across six races, and the gap is too large.

That changes the dynamic at Austin in an interesting way. Without championship pressure, Verstappen races COTA on its own terms: to win, because winning is what he does, and because this specific circuit is one where he has proven he is the best.

Red Bull has spent the 2026 season repositioning. They have not been the car they were in 2022 or 2023. But COTA's specific demands — traction, high-speed balance, bravery at Turn 1 — have historically aligned with their setup philosophy. Austin is a genuine opportunity for them.

Recent Form and the Off-Weekend

Verstappen's form heading into Austin is encouraging. He won the Singapore Grand Prix two weeks ago — his 73rd career Formula 1 victory at Marina Bay Circuit. The win was complete: pole position, race control, the result he needed.

He spent the off-weekend between Singapore and Austin doing what Verstappen does: racing something. On October 17, he drove a Mercedes-AMG GT3 at Portimão in the GT World Challenge, finishing ninth in a 61-car field. His co-driver picked up a drive-through penalty during the race, which impacted the final result. None of it matters for Austin, but it speaks to the way Verstappen approaches motorsport as a whole — he does not stop.

Coming into Austin off a race win, with a clear mind and no championship stress, Verstappen may be in the ideal headspace for a COTA victory.

The Lap Record

The current COTA lap record — 1:36.169, set during the 2023 race — belongs to Verstappen. Lap records in Formula 1 are often set on fresh tyres late in a race, a combination of light fuel load and new rubber. His record has held through two subsequent US Grand Prix weekends.

Whether he challenges it in 2026 depends on how Red Bull's car performs across the weekend. If the RB feels comfortable through the high-speed sectors and generates the traction needed out of the slow corners, Verstappen will be deep into contention. He knows the circuit. The question is always whether the car is good enough to let him show it.

What Sunday Could Look Like

If Red Bull works well at COTA — as they have in recent memory — Verstappen becomes a serious threat for the win. He will be racing Antonelli, who has his own reasons to push hard in Austin. He will be racing Ferrari's two drivers, one of whom has Hamilton's COTA experience. And he will be racing in a context where a win here, at a circuit he has dominated, would be the statement Red Bull most needs to make heading into the final five races.

Red Bull hasn't won at Circuit of the Americas since 2023. Verstappen would like to change that on Sunday.
