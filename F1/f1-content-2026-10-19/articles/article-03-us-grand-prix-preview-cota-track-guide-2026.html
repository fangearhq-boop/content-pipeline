2026 US Grand Prix: COTA Track Guide for the Austin Weekend

Before Thursday's practice, here's everything you need to understand what Circuit of the Americas demands from every car on the grid.

By Elena Voss | October 19, 2026 | F1 Fanrecap

The Basics

Circuit of the Americas is located in Austin, Texas. It opened in 2012 and has hosted the United States Grand Prix every year since. The 2026 race is a standard weekend — no sprint race — with practice sessions on Thursday and Friday, qualifying on Saturday the 24th, and the race on Sunday, October 25.

The circuit is 5.513 kilometers long with 20 corners. The race distance is 56 laps, for a total of 308.405 kilometers. The current lap record is 1:36.169, set by Max Verstappen in 2023.

Turn 1: The Circuit's Signature

The defining feature of Circuit of the Americas is its opening corner. Turn 1 is approached on a long uphill straight — blind, meaning drivers cannot see the apex until they are almost upon it — and leads into a tight hairpin at the crest of the hill. The approach requires precise braking point selection, and the elevation change makes it one of the most visually dramatic corners in Formula 1.

Turn 1 is where races begin and sometimes where races end. On the opening lap, the combination of full fuel loads, 20 cars converging, and the uphill approach makes it a regular flashpoint for contact and position changes. Getting it right in practice and qualifying matters. Getting it wrong on Sunday can define a race.

Sector by Sector

After the Turn 1 hairpin, the circuit descends and opens into a series of high-speed sweeping corners through Sector 1. This section, loosely inspired by the Maggotts-Becketts complex at Silverstone, requires strong aerodynamic balance and driver confidence at sustained lateral loads. Cars that handle well at high speed gain time here.

Sector 2 runs through the infield section, including a series of slower corners that reward traction and mechanical grip. The back straight provides the main DRS overtaking zone, with a heavy braking zone into a tight hairpin at the end.

Sector 3 is a flowing series of medium-speed corners leading back to the start-finish straight. It puts a premium on overall car balance and the ability to carry exit speed out of the final corner.

What the Circuit Rewards

COTA is an all-arounder — there is no single characteristic that dominates lap time. Strong traction from the slow hairpins, aerodynamic stability through the fast sections, and good braking performance under heavy loads all contribute. This makes it a circuit where genuinely quick cars reveal themselves, and cars with specific weaknesses tend to find them exposed.

Historically, Red Bull's setup philosophy has suited COTA well, which explains Verstappen's dominance here from 2021-2023. Mercedes-powered cars have also run strongly at the circuit. Ferrari's fortunes have varied, but the team has shown the pace to contend here in recent seasons.

What to Watch

Thursday's practice sessions will tell us a great deal about where the teams stand. Pay attention to how Red Bull performs in the high-speed sectors — that will indicate whether Verstappen can genuinely challenge for the win. Watch how Ferrari responds after Singapore, and whether Hamilton and Leclerc find consistent pace together. And watch Antonelli. He needs no specific lap time on Thursday. He needs to do on Sunday what he has done all year: be there at the end.

The 2026 US Grand Prix starts Sunday, October 25. Circuit of the Americas, Austin, Texas. Twenty turns, fifty-six laps. One of the most consequential race weekends in recent F1 history begins in four days.
