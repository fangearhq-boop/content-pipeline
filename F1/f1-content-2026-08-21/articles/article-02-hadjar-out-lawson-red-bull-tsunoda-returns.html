Hadjar Out With Wrist Injury: Lawson Races Red Bull, Tsunoda Makes Surprise Return at Dutch GP

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | August 21, 2026

Formula 1 rarely allows summer breaks to pass without incident. This year, the drama arrived in the form of a boxing gym, a fractured wrist, and one of the sport's more remarkable driver merry-go-rounds in recent memory.

Isack Hadjar has been ruled out of the Dutch Grand Prix after sustaining a wrist injury during the summer shutdown — reportedly while boxing in a gym. Red Bull Racing confirmed the news ahead of the Zandvoort weekend, triggering a two-team reshuffle that brings Liam Lawson into the senior Red Bull seat and sends Yuki Tsunoda back onto the Formula 1 grid, racing for Racing Bulls alongside Arvid Lindblad.

Lawson's Opportunity

Liam Lawson has been operating within the Red Bull programme for years, waiting for his chance in the senior car. This weekend at Zandvoort, on a Sprint format, at one of the most watched races of the season, that chance has arrived.

The circumstances are imperfect — stepping in at a Sprint weekend on a circuit where teams have zero 2026-car data is a challenge even for drivers who've been in the seat all season. But Lawson is no stranger to Red Bull's systems, and the talent is not in question. This weekend is about execution under pressure, and the entire paddock will be watching closely.

Whether Red Bull retains Lawson in the seat for subsequent races depends partly on this performance and partly on Hadjar's recovery timeline. For now, Lawson has a Grand Prix to race.

Tsunoda's Poetic Return

The most compelling subplot of this development is Yuki Tsunoda's.

The Japanese driver was displaced from Red Bull's junior team — Racing Bulls — at the start of 2026 specifically to make way for Hadjar, who had been earmarked for the path toward a senior Red Bull seat. Tsunoda found himself without a Formula 1 drive after years of consistent results with Alpha Tauri and then Racing Bulls.

Now, Hadjar's injury has inverted the situation. Tsunoda is back in an F1 car at Zandvoort, racing for the same team from which he was removed, while the driver who replaced him races the senior Red Bull alongside Lawson. The symmetry is almost too neat to be real.

For Tsunoda, this race is more than a one-off opportunity — it's a chance to remind Red Bull's management what they moved on from. The Dutch Grand Prix crowd, notoriously partisan and wildly enthusiastic, won't be thinking about Tsunoda's storyline. But the paddock will.

The Bigger Picture

Driver injuries during summer breaks are a reminder that Formula 1 operates on a knife edge even when cars aren't on track. The physical demands of the sport are significant; the off-season activities drivers pursue carry real risk. A single session in a boxing gym during what should have been a recovery period has reshuffled two teams' weekend plans and altered the competitive picture at a race that carries championship implications.

For the championship, the Hadjar-to-Lawson swap doesn't fundamentally change the points battle at the top — Red Bull are not in Antonelli or Hamilton's league right now in terms of outright pace. But the human drama adds layers to a weekend that already had plenty: the final Dutch GP, the orange army's farewell, a Sprint format debut at Zandvoort.

What's Next

Lawson lines up in the senior Red Bull for Sprint Qualifying on Friday afternoon and the Sprint Race Saturday morning. Tsunoda returns to the Racing Bulls seat for the same schedule. The Grand Prix runs Sunday, August 23. Hadjar's return date depends on medical clearance for his wrist injury — no timeline has been confirmed by Red Bull Racing.
