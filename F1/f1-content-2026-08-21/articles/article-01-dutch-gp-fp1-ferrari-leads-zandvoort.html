Dutch GP FP1: Ferrari Goes 1-2 at Zandvoort in the Final Dutch Grand Prix

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | August 21, 2026

The final Dutch Grand Prix weekend at Zandvoort opened on Friday with Ferrari topping Free Practice 1, setting the tone for what promises to be an intense Sprint weekend at one of Formula 1's most beloved circuits.

Ferrari went 1-2 in the only practice session of the weekend, making the most of a 60-minute FP1 window that is all teams get before Sprint Qualifying locks in the grid. Mercedes was also in the mix, keeping the championship fight close as Kimi Antonelli looks to extend his points lead over Lewis Hamilton entering Round 12.

The Sprint Format Challenge

This weekend marks the first time the Dutch Grand Prix has operated under the Sprint format — and the first time since the 2026 cars were introduced that any team has run at Zandvoort under the new technical regulations. That combination creates a unique challenge.

Teams have zero historical data with 2026-spec cars at this circuit. Setup decisions made in FP1 must carry through Sprint Qualifying, the Sprint Race, full qualifying, and the Grand Prix. There is no second practice session to correct mistakes, no opportunity to try a different setup direction and compare notes. Whatever teams learn in 60 minutes on Friday afternoon is what they work with for the rest of the weekend.

Ferrari's FP1 performance — going 1-2 on the timing sheets — means they extracted maximum value from the session. Whether that pace translates to Sprint Qualifying and beyond depends on how well the car is optimized for Zandvoort's distinctive high-speed, banking-heavy layout.

Sprint Qualifying Ahead

Sprint Qualifying was scheduled for 16:30 CEST on Friday afternoon, following the close of FP1. The six-segment shootout format compresses what would normally be a 60-minute qualifying session into a faster, higher-stakes battle for 20 grid positions ahead of Saturday's Sprint Race.

Ferrari's FP1 advantage gives their drivers confidence heading into Sprint Qualifying, but the Sprint format is notoriously unpredictable — small errors on a single flying lap carry enormous consequences when there's no time to reset and recover.

The Final Farewell

Beyond the championship implications, this weekend carries the weight of history. The Dutch Grand Prix returned to the Formula 1 calendar in 2021 after a 35-year absence and immediately became one of the season's most atmospheric events — the orange army of Verstappen fans transforming Zandvoort into something closer to a concert venue than a racing circuit.

This is the last time that will happen. The circuit's contract with Formula 1 was not renewed, and the 2026 Dutch Grand Prix is confirmed as the final edition at Zandvoort. For tens of thousands of fans who have made the pilgrimage to the North Sea coast every summer since 2021, Friday's FP1 was the beginning of a weekend they'll remember for decades.

What's Next

Sprint Qualifying results will determine Saturday's Sprint Race grid. The Sprint Race itself runs Saturday morning, followed by full qualifying in the afternoon. The Dutch Grand Prix — the last one at Zandvoort — takes place Sunday, August 23. For the championship, the weekend offers 12 Sprint points and 26 Grand Prix points for the respective winners, giving both Antonelli and his rivals meaningful opportunities to shift the title battle.
