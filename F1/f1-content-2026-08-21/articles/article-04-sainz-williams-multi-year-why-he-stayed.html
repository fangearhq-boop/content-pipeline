Carlos Sainz Signs Multi-Year Williams Extension — Why He Chose Grove Over Audi and Aston Martin

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | August 21, 2026

When Carlos Sainz left Ferrari at the end of 2024, he landed at Williams — a team that, under new leadership from James Vowles, was in the middle of a deliberate and visible rebuild. His first season in 2025 produced two Grand Prix podiums and helped Williams finish 5th in the Constructors' Championship, their best result in many years.

This week, Sainz confirmed he's not going anywhere. Williams announced a multi-year extension for the Spanish driver, completing their 2027 lineup alongside Alex Albon, who extended one day earlier. The exact length of Sainz's new deal was not disclosed.

The decision, though, is its own story.

The Alternatives Were Real

Sainz wasn't without options. According to multiple reports, Audi — who are building toward an F1 manufacturer entry with significant resources behind them — was one alternative. Aston Martin, despite their own challenges, was another. Both represented paths that, on paper, could have offered different forms of upside: Audi's long-term investment proposition, Aston Martin's ambitions under the Stroll ownership structure.

Sainz passed on both. The choice reflects something genuine about what he sees in Williams under Vowles.

The Case for Staying

James Vowles arrived at Williams from Mercedes, where he had served as chief strategist. His track record gave him instant credibility, and the changes at Williams since his arrival have been substantive — not just personnel reshuffles but structural, operational improvements that take time to show up in lap times and results, but do eventually show up.

Sainz's 2025 season gave him first-hand experience of what Vowles is building. Two podiums. A 5th-place Constructors' finish. A team culture that felt, according to various Williams insiders over the past 18 months, genuinely different from the drifting organization that had been treading water in the midfield for much of the previous decade.

The 2026 season, under the wholesale regulation reset, has been harder. Williams sits 9th in the Constructors' standings. But the trend line that preceded 2026 pointed upward, and Sainz is betting that trend resumes once teams adjust to the new technical environment.

Stability as a Strategy

There's a case for continuity in Formula 1 that often gets underappreciated. Drivers who stay with a team for multiple seasons develop institutional knowledge — an understanding of how the car behaves, where its limitations are, which engineers read data which way, what the team responds to in terms of driver feedback. That knowledge compounds. A driver parachuting into a new team, even a well-resourced one, spends the first season learning rather than optimizing.

Sainz, 32, understands this. His decision to extend with Williams is partly a bet on Vowles' project and partly a recognition that he is already the most informed critic and advocate for this car's development. Walking away from that — even to a better-funded team — resets the clock.

What's Next

Sainz races this weekend at the Dutch Grand Prix as Williams competes to find points in what has been a difficult 2026 season at Zandvoort. The longer-term prize is the 2027 championship, when the team hopes to be back in competitive territory with a fully mature version of Vowles' rebuild. The Williams 2027 lineup — Sainz and Albon — is now confirmed. The work to make that lineup competitive continues.
