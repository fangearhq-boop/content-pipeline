Monza Preview: Ferrari's ADUO-2 Engine, Norris Chasing Three in a Row, Championship at a Crossroads

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | August 25, 2026

Formula 1 arrives at the Temple of Speed in ten days. The Italian Grand Prix at Autodromo Nazionale Monza runs September 4-6 as the 15th race of the 2026 season — and it arrives at a moment when the championship, the constructor battle, and the question of team allegiances are all alive at once. This is the kind of Grand Prix the Italian GP was built for.

The most significant pre-race development: Ferrari will introduce the ADUO-2 engine upgrade at their home race. Monza is one of the most power-sensitive circuits on the calendar, with its long straights, flat-out sections, and minimal downforce requirements creating an environment where raw engine performance matters more than almost anywhere else. If the ADUO-2 delivers the gains Ferrari expects, this could be the weekend that proves their genuine championship credentials — not just for the constructors' title, but for demonstrating that the McLaren-Mercedes dominance of the first half has real competition ahead.

Can Norris Win Three in a Row?

Lando Norris won Hungary. Lando Norris won Zandvoort. Lando Norris arrives at Monza as the form driver of the field — a status that changes every season and means something, even if history and car performance matter more. Three consecutive victories in Formula 1 is not unprecedented, but it is uncommon. The last driver to achieve it in recent memory is Verstappen, whose run of dominance peaked at four straight in 2023.

For McLaren, the question is whether their car's characteristics translate well to Monza. The circuit rewards straight-line speed above all else. McLaren has shown competitive top-end performance all season, but Ferrari's home-race engine upgrade introduces a specific variable that was not present at Hungary or Zandvoort. If the power delta shifts toward Maranello, Norris will need strategy, positioning, and perhaps some fortune to maintain his streak.

The Championship Picture

Kimi Antonelli leads on 242 points with a 59-point cushion over George Russell and Lewis Hamilton. He is the favorite to win the 2026 title by any reasonable metric. But 59 points across 11 races is not insurmountable — particularly not if Antonelli experiences mechanical failure, collects damage in an incident, or if a rival strings together a run of three or four wins.

Monza's qualifying format will matter enormously. Slipstreaming on the long straights — a Monza tradition — can elevate drivers who might otherwise not qualify near the front. Strategy from the DRS zones and the undercut can scramble positions race. The circuit is one of the few where genuine surprise outcomes remain genuinely possible without depending on weather or safety car chaos.

Race start: Sunday, September 6 at 14:00 BST (9:00 AM ET). The Tifosi will be in the grandstands, the ADUO-2 will be in the Ferrari power unit, and the championship will be watching. Ten days until the Temple of Speed delivers its verdict.

What's Next

Practice and qualifying begin September 4. Ferrari's upgrade timing will be confirmed when the cars hit the track in FP1. Expect significant media attention on the power-unit performance numbers in practice — this is one of the few weekends where straightline speed data from the speed trap is immediately actionable in understanding the competitive order before qualifying even begins. After Monza, the European leg of the season continues with the Madrid Grand Prix and then a run of consecutive flyaway races in the final stretch. The gap on September 7 will tell us whether this championship is decided or still genuinely open.
