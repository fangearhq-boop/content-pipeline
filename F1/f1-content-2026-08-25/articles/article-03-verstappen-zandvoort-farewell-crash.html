Verstappen's Zandvoort Farewell: Crashed Out on Lap 1, the Dutch GP Era Ends Without Its Hero

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | August 25, 2026

The script for Max Verstappen's final Dutch Grand Prix wrote itself long ago. The home hero, the orange army, the circuit rebuilt to echo his own brilliance — it was supposed to end in victory, or at minimum a front-running display in front of 100,000 of his people. What actually happened was a pre-race shower, a damp patch on the banking of the final corner, and a heavy crash on lap 1 that sent the red flag out and eliminated the world's most dominant driver of the past four seasons before the race had even properly started.

Verstappen was unhurt. His car was not. One wheel came away in the impact. The race was red-flagged and restarted. By the time the field formed on the grid again, Verstappen was already in a Zandvoort that existed only in the past tense for him — in the garage, watching Lando Norris go on to win the final edition of a race that was, in every real sense, meant for him.

His Words, Careful and Raw

Verstappen spoke to Sky Sports after the race and chose his words with the same precise control he typically brings to a qualifying lap. "It caught me out," he said of the corner. "It's as simple as that." On the weekend as a whole: "Disappointing pace, and a disappointing end to the weekend."

He acknowledged that the banking was still wet in places from pre-race showers and that he misjudged the transition from wet to dry. He could have said more. He could have raged. He didn't. That restraint was, in its own way, as telling as anything he might have said in anger. Verstappen has always processed the worst outcomes by looking forward — a function, perhaps, of winning often enough to know the next opportunity is usually not far away.

The problem here is that the next Dutch GP opportunity is never. Zandvoort is off the F1 calendar permanently after 2026. The circuit that was rebuilt to host him, that sold out from the moment tickets went on sale every year he was dominant, will not host another Formula 1 race. His final appearance there lasted one corner.

What's Next

Verstappen moves to Monza for the Italian Grand Prix on September 4-6. Red Bull has been in a difficult stretch; the team that won four consecutive constructors' championships has not won a race since the opening rounds of the season, and Verstappen's lap-1 crash at Zandvoort added another zero to his weekend tally. The next circuit on the calendar is entirely different — long straights, minimal downforce, power-sensitive — which means no natural advantage for anyone except possibly Ferrari and their incoming ADUO-2 engine upgrade. Verstappen will need a significant step from the Red Bull engineers to make Monza better than Zandvoort. The gap between expectation and reality, for the moment, remains uncomfortably wide.
