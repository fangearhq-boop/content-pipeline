Lando Norris Wins Final Dutch Grand Prix as Verstappen Crashes Out — End of the Zandvoort Era

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | August 25, 2026

Lando Norris claimed the final Dutch Grand Prix in history on August 23, winning the last Formula 1 race ever held at Circuit Zandvoort by 11.536 seconds ahead of Kimi Antonelli, with George Russell completing the podium. It was Norris's second consecutive victory of the 2026 season — a statement of form at precisely the moment the championship enters its decisive phase.

The race should have been a celebration of Zandvoort and of the man who made it special: Max Verstappen. Instead, it became something far more complicated. On the opening lap, Verstappen lost control in the damp banking of the final corner, slamming into the barrier and bringing out the red flag. He was unhurt. But his race — and Zandvoort's final chapter — was over before it had properly begun.

After the red flag restart, Norris led from the front. Antonelli made a flying start and briefly challenged for the lead, stretching a gap in the race's middle third. But Norris composed himself, retook the position, and pulled away to win comfortably. McLaren's pace advantage on this circuit was clear throughout the weekend — and Norris executed perfectly in conditions that demanded both speed and patience.

Zandvoort's Last Chapter

Zandvoort was restored to the Formula 1 calendar in 2021 primarily because of Verstappen's presence and the enormous Dutch fan base he had created. Over five seasons, the orange army turned it into one of the most atmospheric race weekends on the schedule — 100,000 fans in the stands, the trademark banking on the final corner, a noise level that rivaled any venue in motorsport.

The circuit will not return. The 2026 race was always going to be the farewell edition, and the paddock knew it. Under normal circumstances, the final Dutch GP would have been a victory lap for Verstappen — one last dominant performance in front of his people at a track built, in part, for him. What actually happened was the opposite: a lap-1 crash, a long walk back to the garage, and the sound of 100,000 fans watching their hero depart on a flatbed.

Norris wins, Zandvoort closes, and the 2026 season moves on. Racing rarely delivers the ending the script demands — and that is precisely what makes it fascinating.

What's Next

The Formula 1 paddock now turns toward Monza, where the Italian Grand Prix takes place September 4-6. Norris arrives with back-to-back victories, McLaren in excellent mechanical form, and a championship gap of 83 points to Antonelli that is substantial but not insurmountable across eleven remaining races. Ferrari will introduce their ADUO-2 engine upgrade at their home race — and Monza's power-sensitive layout gives them every incentive to believe it can make a difference. The second half of 2026 just got significantly more interesting.
