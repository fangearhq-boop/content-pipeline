Antonelli Extends to 59 Points Clear — Russell and Hamilton Now Tied for Second

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | August 25, 2026

The 2026 Formula 1 World Championship has never looked more complicated — or more compelling. Kimi Antonelli moved to 242 points following his second-place finish at Zandvoort, extending his lead to 59 points over George Russell and Lewis Hamilton. Crucially, those two Mercedes drivers are now level on 183 points, with Russell holding the tiebreaker: two race wins to Hamilton's one. Behind them, a resurgent Lando Norris sits on 159 points after back-to-back victories at Hungary and Zandvoort.

Antonelli's advantage is formidable. At 21 years old, having scored six wins, six poles, and nine podiums across the first 14 races of the season, he has driven with a consistency that belies his age. He has not retired from a race all season. He has not made the kind of costly error under pressure that typically catches young drivers in their first championship battles. He looks, to put it bluntly, like a champion who knows he is going to win — without taking anything for granted.

But 59 points across 11 remaining races is not a sealed deal. Mathematics allows it to evaporate quickly. A DNF — or two — and suddenly the conversation changes entirely. And the field, as the second half of the season has shown, is not inclined to make things easy for any leader.

The Battle Within the Battle

The Russell vs. Hamilton subplot is one of the most fascinating dynamics in a championship already full of them. Both drivers are in their most experienced years, both are technically capable of winning this title, and both know that any misstep between them is points gifted directly to Antonelli. The team will push them toward collaboration. Their competitive instincts will push the other direction.

Norris, meanwhile, proved at Hungary and Zandvoort that McLaren can win on fast-twitch circuits. If Ferrari's ADUO-2 engine upgrade delivers at Monza — a circuit where raw power matters enormously — and if Norris can find a way to win there too, the championship conversation will shift noticeably in McLaren's direction. Eleven races is enough time for almost any outcome.

The season also has a wild card: Verstappen. He is off the pace by championship standards, sitting outside the top four in points. But Verstappen is never truly out of any weekend, and his ability to harvest strong results even when the car is not at its peak means he remains a factor in how points are distributed, if not a realistic title challenger.

What's Next

Monza on September 4-6 is the first critical data point of the championship's second half. A Norris victory there would reduce Antonelli's lead to below 50 points — territory where the pressure would start to mount noticeably. A Ferrari victory with the ADUO-2 upgrade would announce that the constructors' championship is still alive and inject genuine uncertainty into the drivers' standings. And an Antonelli win would essentially end the suspense well before the season's final stretch. The next ten days will tell us a great deal about which direction this title is headed.
