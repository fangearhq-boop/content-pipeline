Five Drivers, 70 Combined Grid Places Lost: Las Vegas GP's Pre-Race Penalty Chaos

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | November 20, 2026

Before a single qualifying lap has been set at the Las Vegas Grand Prix, the starting grid has already been scrambled. Five drivers will drop grid positions from wherever they qualify — a combined total of 70 places swallowed by component penalties — and the chaos those penalties create could have implications throughout the field.

The Penalty List

Oliver Bearman is the most dramatic case: a 35-place grid drop will push him to the back row of the grid no matter how strong his qualifying performance. When a 20-car grid is 20 cars long and you are dropping 35 places, the mathematics are unambiguous.

Oscar Piastri (McLaren), Carlos Sainz, and Lance Stroll (Aston Martin) all carry 10-place drops. For Piastri, who sits sixth in the Drivers' Championship with approximately 152 points and is trying to consolidate his position in the standings, a 10-place drop is a significant setback. A strong qualifying result of fifth or sixth becomes a 15th or 16th starting position — a hole that is very difficult to dig out of on a circuit where overtaking is not always straightforward.

Kimi Antonelli, the newly crowned world champion, takes a 5-place drop. For the 20-year-old, who has the luxury of racing without championship pressure, the penalty is an inconvenience rather than a disaster. He may simply decide to push aggressively from wherever he starts.

What It Means for the Race

Grid penalties in Formula 1 do not just hurt the penalized drivers — they reshuffle the grid behind them, promoting drivers who didn't earn those positions through pace and complicating strategic calculations for everyone. When five cars that qualified near the front of the midfield are suddenly starting from the back half of the grid, the first-lap dynamics change entirely.

For the drivers who avoid penalties — and notably, Verstappen and Russell have no drops to serve — the penalty chaos is a free gift. If Bearman, Piastri, Sainz, Stroll, and Antonelli all qualify ahead of them, those clean-grid drivers effectively move up five positions on the starting grid before the formation lap begins.

Strategy and the Unknown

Las Vegas racing tends to produce significant strategy variation. The night temperatures create unpredictable tire degradation, and teams running from the back of the grid with fast cars may choose aggressive strategies — running longer on their first stint, trying to undercut rivals on later stops — that could shake up the race order dramatically.

Piastri, in particular, has the pace to carve through the field if his car is performing well. Getting from 15th to the points is not simple, but it is not impossible on a track where the safety car occasionally compresses the field.

What's Next

Qualifying tonight (8 PM PST / 11 PM ET on Apple TV+) will establish the benchmark positions. After penalties are applied, the full strategic picture for Saturday's race becomes clear. With 50 laps on the Strip Circuit, the teams starting from the back have time to recover — but they will need a good strategy, some fortune with safety car timing, and a clean race to do it.
