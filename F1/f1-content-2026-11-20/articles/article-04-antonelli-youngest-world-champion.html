Italy's First Champion in 73 Years: How Kimi Antonelli Can Shape the Las Vegas GP

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | November 20, 2026

At 20 years and 68 days old, Kimi Antonelli clinched the 2026 Formula 1 Drivers' Championship at the Mexico City Grand Prix on November 1 — the youngest world champion in the sport's history, breaking Sebastian Vettel's long-standing record. He also became the first Italian world champion since Alberto Ascari claimed the title in 1953, ending a 73-year wait for the country that invented the sport's most storied marque.

Now, in Las Vegas, Antonelli arrives for the penultimate stretch of his title season as a free man. No championship math. No protective instincts. Just a 20-year-old in the fastest car on the grid, racing without consequence.

What He Achieved

Antonelli's championship is extraordinary by any measure. This is only his second season in Formula 1. He made his debut in 2025 with Mercedes and produced a strong rookie year, but nothing that necessarily forecast a title campaign at 20. The 2026 season changed that calculation entirely.

His 390 championship points represent a dominant haul. The Mercedes team also secured the Constructors' Championship, confirming this was a package that delivered at every level. For the sport, an Italian driver winning the title in what was broadly expected to be a continuation of Red Bull or McLaren domination is a seismic storyline.

For comparison, Vettel's record was 23 years old when he won his first title in 2010. Antonelli is three years younger. The historical weight of what he has done will only grow with time.

What It Means for Las Vegas

For the Las Vegas Grand Prix, Antonelli's champion status creates a fascinating subplot. He will start from somewhere behind his qualifying position after absorbing a 5-place grid penalty. But starting from eighth instead of third is an annoyance, not an obstacle, for a driver of his caliber in a Mercedes.

What is different is his mindset. When a driver is fighting for a championship, every decision is filtered through mathematics — the right tire strategy, avoiding contact with rivals, bringing the car home for points. Antonelli has none of that. He can try to win. He can take risks. He can push on cold tires in the opening laps when champions protecting standings would back off.

In a race where Verstappen and Russell are locked in a 17-point battle, a free-driving champion in a fast car could change everything.

The Legacy Tour

Three races remain in Antonelli's title season: Las Vegas, Qatar, and Abu Dhabi. For each of them, he has the opportunity to add wins to a championship that already stands alone historically. A dominant final three races would leave little doubt about whether the title was earned or inherited.

He took fourth in a rain-disrupted FP2 (before grid penalties apply). The pace is there. The intent to win, in the absence of anything left to protect, seems guaranteed. Las Vegas should brace itself.

What's Next

Qualifying tonight determines Antonelli's starting position before the five-place penalty is applied. If he qualifies on the front row, he would start from sixth — well-placed for an aggressive charge to the front. For a driver who has nothing to lose in the final act of a historic season, that starting position is not a setback. It is an invitation.
