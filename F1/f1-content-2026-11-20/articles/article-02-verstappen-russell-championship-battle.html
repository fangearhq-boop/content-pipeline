17 Points, 3 Races, 1 Chance: Russell and Verstappen's Las Vegas Championship Duel

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | November 20, 2026

Kimi Antonelli's name is on the 2026 Drivers' Championship. That was settled at Mexico City three weeks ago. But the battle that is consuming Las Vegas this week is a different one: Max Verstappen versus George Russell for second place in the standings, with 17 points separating them and three races remaining to decide who finishes the year on top.

Verstappen leads with 279 points. Russell sits at 262. A maximum of 78 points are available across Las Vegas, Qatar, and Abu Dhabi. Russell can still overtake him. The math is very much alive.

The Standings Picture

After Lando Norris's victory in the Brazilian Grand Prix on November 8, the standings shook up considerably. Hamilton dropped to fourth (approximately 261 points), Leclerc fifth (around 249), and Norris sixth (around 246). But the most significant narrative remains the fight directly above them: Verstappen and Russell, separated by 17 points going into what could be a decisive weekend.

To close the gap entirely in Las Vegas, Russell would need to win the race while Verstappen finishes outside the top 10. That is a specific outcome, but not an inconceivable one — especially after Verstappen's power unit scare in Thursday's practice. Even a podium for Russell while Verstappen struggles in the midfield would meaningfully tighten the battle heading into Qatar.

Russell's Advantage on This Circuit

The Las Vegas Strip Circuit has a history that favors George Russell. He won here in 2024. The circuit's mix of high-speed sections and technical complexes suits the Mercedes package. And after leading Friday's wet FP2 session, the trajectory of his weekend has started promisingly.

Verstappen won here in 2023 and 2025 — so this is not foreign territory for the Red Bull driver either. When both cars are running cleanly, Verstappen has the technical ability to match Russell anywhere on the calendar. But "when both cars are running cleanly" is the operative phrase, and Thursday raised legitimate questions about the health of his power unit.

The Stakes Beyond the Numbers

The fight for runner-up in the Drivers' Championship is not just about prestige. It reflects the arc of a season, the relative competitiveness of teams, and the foundation for the following year's development. Finishing second behind a historic champion is a far different story than finishing third.

Russell is fighting to validate what has been a strong second half of the season. Verstappen is fighting to confirm his dominance even in a year when his car was not championship-winning machinery. Both narratives matter. Las Vegas is where they intersect most dramatically.

What's Next

Qualifying tonight (8 PM PST / 11 PM ET) will determine where each driver starts the race. A front-row lockout for Mercedes — or a Verstappen starting from the fourth row after a messy qualifying — could effectively resolve this championship before the race begins. Conversely, a strong Verstappen qualifying effort would reset the tension heading into Saturday's showdown. Las Vegas rarely delivers a dull result.
