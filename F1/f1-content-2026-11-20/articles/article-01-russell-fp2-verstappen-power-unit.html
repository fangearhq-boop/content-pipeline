Russell Tops Soggy Las Vegas FP2 as Verstappen's Power Unit Clouds Championship Picture

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | November 20, 2026

George Russell led the Las Vegas Grand Prix practice sessions in the most telling way possible: quickly, while his title rival spent the session dealing with a power unit problem. Russell posted a time of 1:47.272 to top a damp, disrupted FP2 at the Las Vegas Strip Circuit — with Max Verstappen finishing 13th, nearly three seconds behind, after a mechanical issue curtailed his running.

With qualifying scheduled for tonight (11 PM ET on Apple TV+), and 17 championship points separating Russell from Verstappen, Thursday's wet sessions may have been more significant than they looked.

The Session in Numbers

FP1 never really happened. Torrential rain descended on the Strip and washed out the opening practice session entirely, leaving teams with almost no dry-weather data heading into what could be a pivotal qualifying session. When FP2 began, the track was still damp — full wet tires to start, a switch to intermediates roughly 20 minutes in, and a late red flag that compressed the final flying laps into a tense window.

Russell's fastest time of 1:47.272 set the benchmark. Pierre Gasly (Alpine) was second, 0.071 seconds behind — a surprisingly strong result for the Frenchman. Charles Leclerc was third for Ferrari, 0.333 seconds off Russell. Isack Hadjar (Red Bull) was fourth, Oscar Piastri (McLaren) fifth, Lewis Hamilton (Ferrari) sixth, and Lando Norris seventh.

Then came Verstappen, in 13th place, 2.869 seconds adrift of Russell.

Verstappen's Power Unit Concern

The gap to Russell is not fully reflective of the competitive order — wet conditions create noise, and Verstappen's power unit issue clearly cost him track time. But a 2.9-second deficit to your championship rival, even in abnormal conditions, is not the ideal way to begin a weekend where you need a strong result.

Red Bull will work overnight to resolve the power unit problem before qualifying. If the car is healthy, Verstappen should be much more competitive on a circuit that suits his driving style. But heading into the most important qualifying session of the season's final stretch with lingering mechanical uncertainty is not how the champion constructor planned to arrive in Nevada.

Russell's Momentum

Russell won the 2024 Las Vegas Grand Prix. Mercedes' high-downforce, efficient package tends to suit the Strip Circuit's combination of long straights and technical sections. And coming into this weekend on the right side of a 17-point deficit, he has every incentive to attack.

The conditions in FP2 do not fully translate to dry qualifying performance, and Thursday night was more about gathering data than setting representative lap times. But leading any practice session in Las Vegas carries psychological weight — and Russell appears to have that going into qualifying.

What's Next

Qualifying tonight (8 PM PST / 11 PM ET) on Apple TV+ will tell a clearer story. If the track is dry and Verstappen's power unit issue is resolved, expect a much tighter session at the front. If it stays damp, Russell's wet-weather advantage becomes even more significant. The race follows on Saturday at 8 PM PST — and after one rain-soaked practice, the forecast for race day will be watched closely by every team on the grid.
