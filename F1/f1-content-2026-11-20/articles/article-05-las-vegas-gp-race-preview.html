Race Day Preview: What to Know Before the 2026 Las Vegas Grand Prix

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | November 20, 2026

The 2026 Las Vegas Grand Prix is almost here. After a rain-soaked practice week that washed out FP1 entirely and produced a compressed, chaotic FP2, the stage is set for qualifying tonight and the race on Saturday. Here is everything you need to know before lights out on the Strip.

When and Where to Watch

Qualifying runs tonight — Friday, November 20 — at 8:00 PM PST / 11:00 PM ET. The race follows on Saturday, November 21, at the same time: 8:00 PM PST / 11:00 PM ET.

American fans are watching on Apple TV+, which holds the exclusive US broadcast rights for the 2026 season. This is the first Las Vegas Grand Prix under Apple TV's deal — a showcase event for the platform's F1 coverage. In the UK, Sky Sports carries the race live; Channel 4 offers highlights.

The Circuit

The Las Vegas Strip Circuit is 6.201 kilometers long, features 17 turns, and runs for 50 laps. It is a street circuit that cuts through some of the most recognizable real estate in the world — hotel facades, neon signs, and wide boulevards that the engineers have turned into one of F1's fastest layouts.

Cold desert nights are the defining characteristic of Las Vegas racing. When the temperature drops, asphalt cools rapidly, and tire warm-up becomes a unique challenge. Teams that figure out tire preparation early tend to thrive here; teams that struggle to get heat into their rubber spend the first 10 laps fighting understeering cars and graining tires.

Verstappen won here in 2023 and 2025. Russell won in 2024 — the last man to take the checkered flag on the Strip.

The Storylines

The championship battle between Verstappen (279 points) and Russell (262 points) is the most compelling thread. Seventeen points separate them with 78 available across the final three races. Russell leading FP2 on Thursday — while Verstappen dealt with a power unit issue — has set up qualifying as the first critical moment of the weekend.

Grid penalties add another layer. Five drivers absorb combined drops of 70 places from their qualifying results: Bearman (−35), Piastri (−10), Sainz (−10), Stroll (−10), and Antonelli (−5). Bearman will almost certainly start from the back regardless of his qualifying performance.

Then there is Antonelli himself — world champion, no pressure, potentially running free for the last three races of a historic season. He has the machinery to win and no reason to hold back.

What's Next

Qualifying tonight will determine the starting order, after which grid penalties will be applied to produce the final grid for Saturday. After a practice week shortened by rain, qualifying will offer teams their clearest look yet at the competitive order on dry asphalt. By midnight ET, the picture for Saturday's race should be considerably clearer — including whether Verstappen's power unit concern has been resolved and where the two championship contenders will start their most consequential race of the final stretch.
