F1 Returns to Africa: Rwanda Signs 10-Year Deal for Grand Prix in Bugesera

A 33-year absence from the continent ends with a new circuit, a new country, and a 10-year commitment.

By Ryan Calloway | October 18, 2026 | F1 Fanrecap

The Deal

Formula 1 and the government of Rwanda signed a 10-year agreement for a Grand Prix to be held at a new circuit in Bugesera, a district approximately 40 kilometers southeast of Kigali. The deal was announced on October 13.

The circuit does not yet exist. It will need to be designed, built, and certified before a race can be held. The target window for the first race is 2030 to 2032 — giving F1 and Rwanda four to six years to deliver the infrastructure.

Why 33 Years

The last Formula 1 race on African soil was the South African Grand Prix at Kyalami in 1993. The race was dropped from the calendar the following year and Africa has not appeared on the schedule since.

The reasons are complicated and include political, economic, and logistical factors spanning three decades. South Africa hosted the race for years during the apartheid era, then after its end, and when the race was finally removed, no other African nation with the infrastructure and commercial backing to step in stepped in.

Rwanda is a different kind of bet. The country has not hosted major international motorsport. It is building a circuit from scratch. But it has demonstrated a capacity for ambitious international sporting investment — the Tour du Rwanda cycling race, expanded conference tourism, and a deliberate strategy of using sports events to build the country's global profile.

What F1 Gets

Formula 1's calendar expansion under recent commercial leadership has followed a clear pattern: new markets, longer-term deals, cities with growth ambitions. Las Vegas, Miami, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, and now Rwanda fit that template. Each brings a different audience demographic and geographic reach.

Africa is a continent of over 1.4 billion people with growing motorsport interest and limited premium live sporting events to attend. A Formula 1 race in Kigali would be the first major international motorsport event on African soil in over three decades. The commercial case is clear.

What's Next

The next four to six years are the delivery window. The circuit design needs to begin. The construction timeline needs to be set. FIA certification needs to follow. F1 has been here before with other calendar additions — the planning phase for a new circuit is not a guarantee that the race happens on schedule.

But the deal is signed. Africa is on the calendar in pencil. The question is whether by 2032 it is written in ink.
