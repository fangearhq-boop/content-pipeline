Hamilton Ignored Ferrari's Pit Call in Singapore and Lost a Podium

Ferrari told him to come in. Hamilton said no. Vasseur said he would have been P2 or P3. The trust question at Ferrari got louder.

By Marcus Cole | October 18, 2026 | F1 Fanrecap

What Happened

During the Singapore Grand Prix at Marina Bay Street Circuit, a Virtual Safety Car period opened a window for tyre strategy. Ferrari's pit wall called Lewis Hamilton in for fresh rubber. Hamilton chose to stay out.

The decision was consequential. Without the fresh tyres, Hamilton could not match the pace of drivers who had stopped, and he finished outside the podium positions.

Fred Vasseur, Ferrari's team principal, spoke plainly after the race. Hamilton "would have been P2 or P3" if he had taken the stop.

The Outside View

Martin Brundle, watching from the Sky Sports broadcast, described the call as "bizarre." Brundle is one of the most experienced voices in F1 broadcasting, and calling a strategy decision "bizarre" is not a word he uses carelessly. The word reflected a genuine confusion about why a driver would override explicit team guidance during a race with a podium on the line.

Hamilton has been one of the most strategy-intelligent drivers of his generation. The decision to stay out was not ignorance of what a pit stop could do — it was a choice to override the team's read of the situation in real time. That choice cost him.

The Apology

Hamilton issued an apology to Ferrari after the race. The apology matters beyond its face value: it acknowledges that Hamilton understood, in retrospect, that he had made a mistake and had done so by going against the team's instruction.

In a driver-team relationship, the question is not just whether mistakes happen. It is whether the process that produced the mistake is likely to repeat. An apology is one signal. What happens in Austin next weekend is the data point that follows it.

Hamilton-Ferrari in Context

This is Hamilton's first season at Ferrari. He joined from Mercedes in the off-season after more than a decade at the team that gave him six of his seven world championships. Moving to a new team at any age requires rebuilding trust — between driver and pit wall, between driver and engineers, between the reflexes of a driver trained in one system and the calls coming from a different one.

Hamilton sits third in the championship at 229 points, 111 behind Antonelli. The title race is effectively over for him this year. What remains is the project of building something sustainable at Ferrari for 2027 and beyond. Singapore was not a useful data point in that direction.

What's Next

Austin is next. Hamilton will race at Circuit of the Americas while the championship fight plays out around him. How he responds to Singapore — in race setup, in communication with the pit wall, in his own decisions under pressure — will define the story of his first Ferrari season heading into its final stretch.
