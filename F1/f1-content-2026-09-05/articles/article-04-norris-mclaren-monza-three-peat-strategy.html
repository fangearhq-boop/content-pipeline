Norris Eyes the Three-Peat at Monza — But McLaren Has a Straight-Line Problem

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | September 5, 2026

Lando Norris isn't hiding from reality. Ahead of the Italian Grand Prix qualifying session on Saturday, the defending 2025 Formula 1 world champion made an admission that few race favorites would be willing to offer: "McLaren is the slowest on the straight by quite a long way."

That kind of candor is characteristic of Norris. It's also a useful data point for understanding the challenge he faces at the circuit they call the Temple of Speed.

The Monza Problem

Monza is unique on the Formula 1 calendar. Its layout — long straights, minimal technical corners, famous Lesmo and Parabolica sequences — punishes teams that lack raw straight-line speed. DRS zones are long and powerful. Slipstreaming is a genuine tactical weapon. And lap times at Monza are set more by engine power than by aerodynamic downforce.

McLaren's car is not built for this environment. The MCL concept is an exceptional package in terms of mechanical grip, aerodynamic efficiency in corners, and overall balance — but on pure straight-line speed, they trail the leading cars. At circuits like Monza, where straightaway velocity translates directly into lap time, that gap is exposed.

Yet here is Norris, entering the weekend as the 6/4 race favorite after winning back-to-back grands prix in Hungary and the Netherlands. The betting market is telling a different story than the car's Monza limitations — and with good reason.

The Case for Norris Winning Anyway

McLaren's race pace has been exceptional all season. In races where they have qualified toward the front — which is most races — their tyre management, pit strategy, and execution have consistently matched or exceeded whatever their one-lap pace suggests is achievable. Norris himself has been near-flawless, extracting every last tenth from a package that rewards commitment and precision.

At Monza, a DRS train in the early laps can mitigate the straight-line deficit. Tyre degradation on a circuit that doesn't look after rubber the way others do can open up strategy gaps that have nothing to do with top speed. And if Norris qualifies in the top three — which seems plausible given his overall pace — he gives himself every chance to be in the right position when it matters.

What Three in a Row Would Mean

Norris currently trails Kimi Antonelli by 83 points in the 2026 drivers' championship. A third consecutive victory — combined with minimal points for Antonelli from the back of the grid — would be the single biggest swing in the championship since the season turned. It wouldn't make Norris the outright favorite, but it would fundamentally change the conversation about whether this title fight is genuinely over.

The defending champion arriving at a circuit where his car is structurally disadvantaged and winning anyway would also say something powerful about the quality of his driving. Norris has that kind of race on his resume. The question is whether Monza 2026 becomes another one.

What's Next

Qualifying determines everything about Norris's race strategy. A front-row start gives him options — track position, the ability to control the pace, and the tactical flexibility to respond to whatever happens behind him. A mid-grid qualifying result means a more aggressive approach on Sunday. The session starts at 16:00 local time. Whatever happens, Monza's race will be Norris's biggest test yet of whether this championship is truly over.
