Italian GP Qualifying Day: Russell Leads, Hamilton Dreams of Pole, Antonelli Starts Last

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | September 5, 2026

The Italian Grand Prix at Monza has delivered one of the most compelling build-ups of the 2026 Formula 1 season. Qualifying on Saturday afternoon at 16:00 local time is set to answer the questions that two days of practice have raised — but the most fascinating subplot requires no answer at all: Kimi Antonelli, the championship leader, is starting Sunday's race from the back regardless of what happens in Q3.

Russell: The Form Driver of the Weekend

George Russell has been the fastest Mercedes — and the fastest car overall — through Monza's practice sessions. He topped FP2 with a time of 1:22.559, 0.120 seconds ahead of Ferrari's Charles Leclerc. Antonelli was third in that session, Lando Norris fourth, and Lewis Hamilton fifth.

For Russell, who sits 59 points behind his teammate Antonelli in the drivers' standings, this weekend is a genuine opportunity. With Antonelli absorbing a grid penalty for exceeding power unit component limits, Sunday's race will be run without the championship leader threatening from the front row. Russell needs to capitalise.

Hamilton's Dream

Lewis Hamilton is 59 points behind Antonelli in the championship — tied with Russell in third position, both needing to close ground. And at Monza, a circuit where Ferrari's history is inseparable from the grandeur of Italian motorsport, Hamilton has spoken candidly about what he wants.

"We really need to knuckle down tonight," Hamilton said after Friday's practice, evaluating where Ferrari stands heading into qualifying. His comments were measured, but his goal was clear: a dream pole position at the circuit that matters most to Ferrari's tens of millions of Italian fans.

Ferrari was second fastest through practice. The gap to Russell's Mercedes was small. In qualifying trim — where engine modes are turned up and slipstream effect can be maximized on Monza's long straights — anything can happen.

Norris and the McLaren Straight-Line Problem

The defending 2025 world champion enters Monza as the race favorite at 6/4 odds, coming off back-to-back victories in Hungary and the Netherlands. Norris has been one of the most consistently brilliant drivers on the grid this season — but at Monza, he's been candid about McLaren's limitations.

"McLaren is the slowest on the straight by quite a long way," Norris admitted ahead of qualifying. It's the kind of honesty that defines his approach: acknowledge the problem, then solve it. Whether McLaren's extraordinary mechanical grip and downforce can offset a straight-line deficit at the Temple of Speed is the central question of their race strategy.

Antonelli: Starting Last, Leading the Championship

The backdrop to all of it is Antonelli's remarkable situation. At 20 years old, he leads the 2026 drivers' championship with 242 points — 83 ahead of Norris and 59 ahead of both Russell and Hamilton. His engine failure at the Spanish Grand Prix forced Mercedes to install new power unit components beyond the season allocation, triggering an automatic back-of-grid penalty.

Toto Wolff confirmed Mercedes chose to take the penalty at Monza deliberately. Of all the circuits remaining on the calendar, Monza's layout — long straights, genuine overtaking zones, famous slipstreaming effect — gives a driver starting from the back the best possible chance to recover positions. Even from 20th, Antonelli can realistically score championship points on Sunday.

What's Next

Qualifying determines the grid order for all drivers except Antonelli, who starts from the back. The Italian Grand Prix race begins Sunday September 6 at 14:00 local time. A Norris win combined with an Antonelli recovery drive that scores no points could cut the championship gap by 25 points — the difference between first and not finishing. The title fight is alive, and Monza is the weekend it gets its sharpest test yet.
