F1's Qatar and Abu Dhabi Decision Is Coming — FOM Sets Mid-September Deadline

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | September 5, 2026

The Italian Grand Prix weekend at Monza is happening against a backdrop of uncertainty that extends well beyond the championship standings. Formula 1's 2026 season may not end where anyone expects.

F1 CEO Stefano Domenicali has confirmed that a decision on the Qatar Grand Prix (November 27-29, Lusail Circuit) and the Abu Dhabi Grand Prix (December 4-6, Yas Marina) will be made by mid-September — a deadline that places the announcement in the days immediately following the Italian GP.

The Middle East Situation

Both races remain on the published 2026 calendar. But the conflict situation in the Middle East has already forced Formula 1 to make significant schedule changes this season.

The Bahrain Grand Prix and the Saudi Arabian Grand Prix — originally scheduled to open the 2026 season — were both cancelled due to security concerns stemming from the regional conflict, including US strikes on Iranian targets near the Strait of Hormuz. Rather than lose the Bahrain race entirely, organizers arranged to host it under the same name at the Sepang Circuit in Malaysia, with that rescheduled race taking place October 2-4.

Qatar and Abu Dhabi now face a similar review. While both countries have not been directly affected in the same way as Bahrain and Saudi Arabia, the broader regional instability prompted FOM to delay a firm decision. Domenicali said no announcement would come before mid-September.

The Contingency Plan

Formula 1 has not been sitting idle. A contingency plan for a European season finale is understood to have been prepared, with Imola as the leading candidate to host a replacement round. Portimao in Portugal has also been mentioned as an alternative venue.

Imola has the infrastructure, the FIA certification, and the recent experience of hosting F1 grands prix. The Emilia-Romagna Grand Prix is a regular fixture on the modern calendar, and the circuit has demonstrated the operational capacity to host a late-season race. It would be a logistically sensible choice if the Middle East legs are cancelled.

A European season finale would represent a dramatic change to the feel of the championship conclusion. The Yas Marina Circuit in Abu Dhabi has become synonymous with F1 season endings — its twilight race under the lights has framed championship-deciding moments for a generation of fans. Replacing it with a cold European October or November would be jarring, but given what's at stake in the 2026 championship, it might also be exactly the kind of neutral ground the title fight deserves.

What It Means for the Championship

If Qatar and Abu Dhabi proceed as planned, Kimi Antonelli has seven remaining races (including this weekend's Italian GP) to extend his championship lead or defend it. If one or both are cancelled and Imola substitutes, the schedule changes — but the mathematics don't: someone still has to beat Antonelli, and time is running short.

The mid-September announcement window is tight enough that both teams and fans will know before the next race weekend what the end of the season looks like. Until then, the uncertainty is just another variable in a 2026 season that has been shaped by factors both on and off the track.

What's Next

Domenicali's mid-September deadline means an announcement is expected within days of the Italian Grand Prix. Formula 1 has the contingency plans in place. What remains is the decision — and whatever it is, it will shape how the 2026 championship concludes.
