Barrichello Returns to Monza in the F2002 — The Car He Won the 2002 Italian GP In

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | September 5, 2026

There are moments in Formula 1 that transcend the race weekend itself. On Saturday at Monza, Rubens Barrichello climbed into a Ferrari F2002 and drove at the Temple of Speed — the same car, the same circuit, and the same team that gave him one of the most celebrated victories of his career.

In 2002, Barrichello won the Italian Grand Prix in this exact chassis. He crossed the finish line at Monza driving for Ferrari, with Michael Schumacher — his teammate and one of the greatest drivers in the history of the sport — in the car right alongside him in spirit if not in position. Today, 24 years later, the same car returned to the same circuit. And Barrichello was in it again.

Ferrari's Landmark Tribute

The Schumacher demonstration is the centerpiece of Ferrari's larger tribute at the 2026 Italian Grand Prix — a weekend marking two major anniversaries. This year is the 30th anniversary of Michael Schumacher joining Ferrari in 1996, arriving with five world championships already to his name and a mission to deliver Ferrari's first title in nearly two decades. It is also the 20th anniversary of his final Ferrari season in 2006.

Ferrari unveiled a special livery for this weekend's cars — glossy red with a white rear wing, black detailing, and gold wheels, inspired by the 1996 F310 that carried Schumacher in his first Ferrari season. The cars also bear an MS logo and a seven-star emblem representing his seven world championships. Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc are wearing Schumacher-inspired race suits for the entire weekend.

Three Ferrari cars from the Schumacher era are on static display: the 1996 F310 (his first Ferrari, the car that inspired the livery), the 2002 F2002 (the machine that dominated that season and won Barrichello's Italian GP), and the 2006 248 F1 (his final Ferrari).

Vettel on Sunday

The tribute extends into Sunday's race day. Sebastian Vettel — four-time world champion and a driver who grew up idolizing Schumacher — will take the F2002 for a demonstration lap around the Monza circuit before the Italian Grand Prix begins. Vettel's connection to Schumacher is deeply personal: he has spoken throughout his career about how much Schumacher shaped his approach to racing and his understanding of what it means to be a Formula 1 champion.

Sabine Kehm, Schumacher's long-time manager and spokesperson for the Schumacher family, gave an emotional response when shown Ferrari's tribute plans. The acknowledgment from those closest to the Schumacher family gives the entire weekend an added layer of meaning.

Why the F2002 Matters

Of all the cars in Ferrari's storied history, the F2002 holds a special place. It was built to a level of dominance that few racing cars achieve — Schumacher and Ferrari won 15 of the 17 races in the 2002 season, securing the constructors' and drivers' championships by mid-July, the earliest in the sport's history at that point. The car was fast, reliable, and technically ahead of its time.

Barrichello's connection to it is more than statistical. He raced alongside Schumacher in that chassis, experienced the car's extraordinary abilities from the inside, and won races in it. Having him return to Monza — in the same car, on the same circuit — creates a loop of history that no other driver could provide.

What's Next

The demonstrations on Saturday and Sunday are the final chapter of what Ferrari has described as a comprehensive tribute weekend. The race begins Sunday at 14:00 local time. Whatever happens in the championship fight between Antonelli, Russell, Hamilton, and Norris will be written into F1 history at Monza. But the history that Barrichello made in an F2002 at this circuit was already written — and today, for a few extraordinary minutes, it came alive again.
