Russell's Azerbaijan Grand Slam: The Win That Kept the 2026 Title Fight Alive

By Marcus Cole | October 2, 2026

George Russell completed the rarest feat in Formula 1 at Baku — pole, led every lap, fastest lap, and victory by just 0.196 seconds — reminding the championship leader that the fight isn't over.

When George Russell crossed the line at the Baku City Circuit to win the 2026 Azerbaijan Grand Prix, he did so having achieved a Grand Slam — the rarest single-race achievement in Formula 1. Pole position, led every one of the 51 laps, set the fastest lap, and won the race. All four. In the same afternoon.

The margin of victory was 0.196 seconds over a charging Max Verstappen, who had pushed Russell to the absolute limit over the closing laps around the streets of Baku. Isack Hadjar completed the podium in third, 10.704 seconds back.

What a Grand Slam Means

A Formula 1 Grand Slam — achieving all four elements of pole, led every lap, fastest lap, and victory in the same race — is one of the most difficult single-race accomplishments in motorsport. It requires both pace over a full qualifying session and the ability to manage a lead through various pit stop strategies and traffic conditions while still finding time for a fastest lap push.

For Russell, the Baku Grand Slam was his first of his career and his third victory of the 2026 season. It reduced the championship gap to Antonelli from 90-plus points to 66 — not decisive, but meaningful entering the final stretch of races.

The Chase

Russell's path to the championship requires something like perfection over the final three rounds. He trails Antonelli by 66 points — meaning he needs to outscore his Mercedes teammate by 67 points across Malaysia (Bahrain GP), and the two rounds that follow.

That's possible but not probable. Yet Baku showed something important: when Russell is in the zone, he is faster than anyone on the grid on a given day. The Azerbaijan result served notice that the Englishman is not treating the remaining rounds as a coronation ceremony for his teammate.

The Teammate Dynamic

Mercedes faces a genuinely unusual situation: both drivers are in the top two of the championship with three rounds to go. The team has so far avoided explicit team orders, allowing the racing to play out naturally. Russell will need victories — not just podiums — and Antonelli will need to either match him or finish high enough to absorb the gaps.

Friday's FP1 at Sepang, where Verstappen led and Antonelli finished fifth while Russell ran second, suggests the championship will not be decided gently this weekend.

What's Next

Russell enters the Bahrain GP weekend at Sepang in form — second in FP1, second in the championship, with a Grand Slam in his recent form guide. A victory at Sepang combined with Antonelli dropping points could close the gap further heading into the final two rounds. Whether the momentum from Baku translates to Malaysia is the key question heading into Saturday qualifying.
