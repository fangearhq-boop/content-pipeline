Lewis Hamilton at 41: Ferrari's Veteran Still Chasing One More Championship

By Ryan Calloway | October 2, 2026

Third in the standings, sixth-fastest in FP1 at Sepang — Lewis Hamilton's 2026 campaign at Ferrari has been a study in sustained excellence from a man who refuses to acknowledge his own era is ending.

At 41 years old, driving for Scuderia Ferrari in his second season with the Italian team, Lewis Hamilton opened the Bahrain Grand Prix weekend in Malaysia sixth-fastest in FP1 — within a tenth of the championship leader. Third in the World Championship standings. Still here. Still competitive.

The story of Hamilton at Ferrari was supposed to be one of legacy — the greatest driver of his generation choosing to cap his career at the sport's most iconic team. The first year was exploratory. The second year has been a genuine title challenge. With 199 points and three rounds to go, Hamilton is 103 points behind leader Antonelli, but the quality of his season cannot be questioned.

The Math: Steep But Not Impossible

Hamilton trails Antonelli by 103 points with three rounds remaining. A maximum of 75 points is available to each driver in a standard three-race scenario (25 per win, excluding fastest lap bonuses). For Hamilton to win the championship, he would need to win all three remaining races and have Antonelli score zero points — a scenario that falls outside realistic expectations.

But finishing on the podium in the final three races and claiming third in the championship overall would be a significant achievement for a driver in his 20th Formula 1 season, and one that would cement his legacy at Ferrari beyond the single-year trial his move initially represented.

What FP1 at Sepang Showed

Hamilton's sixth-place time in FP1 at Sepang was separated from championship leader Antonelli (fifth) by just 0.010 seconds. Ferrari has shown strong single-lap pace at various circuits this season, with Leclerc taking pole positions earlier in the year. A front-row qualifying performance at Sepang would put Hamilton in a position to fight at the front on Sunday.

For a driver his age who has already achieved everything the sport has to offer, Hamilton's motivation level at this stage of the championship is itself worth noting. He is not here to finish respectably. He is here to win races.

The Mentorship Question

Hamilton's presence at Ferrari has also shaped how Leclerc — his younger teammate, 179 points in the championship — approaches the remainder of the season. Their dynamic has been professionally respectful and occasionally competitive. With both Ferrari drivers in the top five of the standings, the team's constructors' championship position has benefited from Hamilton's consistent points accumulation even when victories have not come.

What's Next

Hamilton enters the Bahrain GP qualifying session Saturday as a genuine threat for a front-row start. Ferrari's pace at Sepang looked strong in FP1, and a podium or victory Sunday would close the gap to Russell in second while keeping the final two rounds meaningful from a personal achievement standpoint. At 41, making the final three rounds matter — that's the Hamilton story in 2026.
