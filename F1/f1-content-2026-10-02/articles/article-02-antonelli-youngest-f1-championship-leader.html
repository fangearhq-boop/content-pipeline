Kimi Antonelli: The 19-Year-Old Rewriting F1 History

By Elena Voss | October 2, 2026

With 302 points and a 66-point championship lead heading into the final three rounds, Kimi Antonelli is on the brink of becoming the youngest Formula 1 world champion ever.

Before the 2026 Formula 1 season began, the question about Kimi Antonelli was whether the 19-year-old could maintain the form of his debut season and challenge for victories. No one seriously expected the Italian to be leading the championship by 66 points with three races to go, having already surpassed one of the sport's all-time records.

When Antonelli won the Japanese Grand Prix in March 2026, he became the youngest driver in Formula 1 history to lead the World Championship, breaking a record set by Lewis Hamilton at age 22 in his debut season in 2007. Hamilton himself is currently Antonelli's closest championship rival — sitting third, 103 points behind his Mercedes successor.

The Numbers Behind the History

After 15 of the 2026 season's 18 scheduled rounds, Antonelli sits on 302 championship points — the product of eight race victories, including the Italian Grand Prix at Monza, which delivered arguably the most emotional celebration of the season. He has also taken multiple pole positions and fastest laps throughout the campaign.

The championship standings tell the story of a season that has been dominated by Mercedes, with Antonelli and his teammate George Russell combining to put both Silver Arrows cars at the front of the field on a consistent basis:

  1. Kimi Antonelli (Mercedes) — 302 pts
  2. George Russell (Mercedes) — 236 pts
  3. Lewis Hamilton (Ferrari) — 199 pts
  4. Lando Norris (McLaren) — 186 pts
  5. Charles Leclerc (Ferrari) — 179 pts
  6. Max Verstappen (Red Bull) — 163 pts

What Antonelli Needs to Win the Title

With three rounds remaining (Bahrain GP at Sepang, plus two more), Antonelli needs 26 points more than Russell to clinch the championship mathematically. A race victory earns 25 points, meaning a single win combined with a Russell non-score would seal it. Even a string of podiums while Russell wins could see the title decided before the final round.

FP1 at Sepang was not ideal — Antonelli finished fifth, over a second behind Verstappen. But practice sessions carry no championship points, and the Italian has shown a remarkable ability to build through a race weekend.

The Weight of History

Should Antonelli win the 2026 championship, he would become the youngest world champion in the sport's history. The current record is held by Sebastian Vettel, who won the 2010 championship at age 23. Antonelli, born in late 2006, will turn 20 before the season's end — but would still claim the record by a significant margin.

The Italian press has already begun framing his career in terms of Michael Schumacher's heritage and Niki Lauda's tactical intelligence. Whether those comparisons prove apt, Antonelli has earned them through results, not projections.

What's Next

Qualifying at Sepang on Saturday will be the first meaningful indicator of where Antonelli sits heading into Sunday's race. A front-row start would put him in control; a mid-pack start with Red Bull and Ferrari looking quick in FP1 would add drama. After 15 rounds of sustained excellence, the 19-year-old's title is his to lose — but in F1, nothing is over until the chequered flag falls on the final race.
