Baku Grand Prix Preview — 9 Days to Round 17's Saturday Race

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | September 17, 2026

Azerbaijan Grand Prix

Nine days from now, the 2026 Azerbaijan Grand Prix runs on a Saturday for the first time in its history — the race shifted from its traditional Sunday slot to avoid a clash with Azerbaijan's national day of mourning. It is a small logistical change with a significant structural effect: teams are running on a compressed schedule, with FP1 and FP2 on Wednesday September 24, FP3 and qualifying on Thursday September 25, and the race itself on Saturday September 26.

The Baku City Circuit has never cared about schedules. It runs on chaos.

The Circuit

The 6.003 km layout is one of Formula 1's most distinctive. Starting along the Caspian Sea promenade, the circuit runs the length of Neftchilar Avenue — approximately 2.2 km of wide, flat tarmac where cars reach 340 kph and slipstreaming turns two-car duels into three-abreast approaches into Turn 1. From there, the track funnels into the old city of Baku's medieval streets, where the walls are close and the margin for error is approximately zero.

The setup tension this creates is fundamental and unresolvable. Teams that load up on downforce for the technical section will lose time on the straight. Teams that trim downforce for top speed will struggle for grip through the old city. The 2026 regulations' active aerodynamics allow some dynamic adjustment, but the underlying compromise cannot be engineered away entirely.

Key Storylines

Williams' major upgrade: Williams technical director Pat Symonds and team principal James Vowles have described the car arriving at Baku as representing a near-total overhaul of the FW48. The original car arrived overweight and underperforming. If the upgrade package delivers on its promise, Carlos Sainz and Alex Albon could be factors in a race where track position matters enormously.

Intra-Mercedes battle: George Russell sits 81 points behind championship leader Antonelli — his Mercedes teammate. In a car capable of winning at any circuit, Russell needs race wins to stay in any mathematical picture. Baku, where Mercedes' straight-line speed should be a significant advantage, is a genuine opportunity. Antonelli needs points; Russell needs wins. The team dynamic will be worth watching.

Ferrari and Hamilton: Lewis Hamilton's Madring (Spanish GP) was ended by a brake failure on Lap 7. Ferrari have been working on the brake system in the weeks since. Baku's main straight requires strong braking confidence into the Turn 1 chicane — if Hamilton's faith in the car's braking is not fully restored, it will show. If it is, Baku offers him the kind of chaotic circuit where he and Leclerc can take points and pressure the constructors' gap between Ferrari and McLaren.

McLaren's window: Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri have the pace to compete anywhere on the calendar. Baku's street circuit nature introduces variability — Safety Car timing, mechanical incidents, wall contact — that can compress a field that might be spread across 30 seconds in normal racing conditions. For McLaren, closing the 52-point gap to Ferrari in the constructors' requires picking up the windfall events as they come. Baku produces them.

Weather and Conditions

The Caspian Sea breeze is a consistent feature of Baku weekend conditions, affecting tire behavior and aerodynamic stability on the main straight. September temperatures in Baku are moderate by F1 standards — warm enough that tire management matters, cool enough that overheating is less of a factor than in the summer flyaway rounds.

What's Next

FP1 and FP2 run on Wednesday September 24. FP3 and qualifying are Thursday September 25. The race is Saturday September 26 — a first in Azerbaijan GP history. After Baku, the Singapore Grand Prix (October 2-4) is the first chance Antonelli can mathematically clinch the world championship.
