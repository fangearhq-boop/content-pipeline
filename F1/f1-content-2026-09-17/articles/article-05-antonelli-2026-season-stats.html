Kimi Antonelli's 2026 Season in Numbers — 8 Wins, 19 Years Old, One Record in Sight

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | September 17, 2026

F1 Drivers' Championship

Eight wins. Six pole positions. Twelve podiums in fourteen races. A championship lead of 81 points. An average of 20.9 points per grand prix.

And a single race result — Monza, September 6 — that tells you more about Kimi Antonelli than any number alone can convey: a victory from 19th place on the starting grid at one of the fastest circuits on the Formula 1 calendar.

The statistics are impressive. The Monza story is something else.

The Numbers

Through 14 rounds of the 2026 Formula 1 season, Antonelli has built the most dominant championship position in recent F1 memory. His eight wins represent more victories than any driver has managed in a 14-race stretch since the hybrid era's peak. His 12 podiums mean he has finished on the podium in all but two races — an extraordinary consistency rate for a driver who was considered an exciting but unproven talent entering his second full season.

The six pole positions add a different dimension. Antonelli is not merely fast in race trim — he is fast in qualifying, under the particular pressure of a single timed lap with no room for error. Pole position in Formula 1 is a more concentrated measure of pure speed than race performance, which layers strategy, tire management, and traffic into the equation. Six poles say: when it comes to raw pace, Antonelli is where the numbers say he is.

The 20.9-point average deserves its own emphasis. Maximum points per race in Formula 1 is 25 (for a race win plus fastest lap). Averaging 20.9 across 14 races means Antonelli has scored close to the maximum possible output in nearly every event — even accounting for the rounds where he did not win or did not lead, his scoring was not merely competitive but optimal.

The Monza Race

Starting 19th at Monza — a circuit where the slipstream effect is significant but starting position still matters enormously, where passing requires drafting and precision and the cooperation of others getting out of your way — Antonelli won. He did not merely survive to a points finish. He won the race.

The Italian Grand Prix was September 6. The circumstances of his qualifying position have been well-documented — a grid penalty dropped him from a front-row qualifying effort. But the nature of the recovery, the overtaking moves required, the tire strategy executed across 53 laps, and the final winning margin all required a performance that most drivers — at any age — would be proud to produce once in a career.

He produced it on September 6. He had already won seven races before that.

What 19 Years Old Means

Antonelli turns 20 in November 2026 — potentially after the championship is already secured. He has been 19 years old for the entirety of the 2026 season to date. In that time, he has built an 81-point championship lead, won at eight different circuits, and positioned himself to become the youngest Formula 1 world champion in the history of the sport.

The youngest previous champion, Sebastian Vettel, was 23 when he sealed his first title. Vettel had been in Formula 1 for three seasons before that, had driven for Red Bull for three years, and had the benefit of a dominant car in a period where one team's technical advantage was near-absolute. Antonelli has a Mercedes that is clearly the best car — but the 2026 regulations brought more competitive midfield pressure than any season since the early hybrid era, and navigating it has required more than just pressing the throttle.

What's Next

The Baku Grand Prix on September 26 is Round 17. Nine rounds remain. If the mathematics play out as currently projected, Kimi Antonelli becomes world champion at Mexico City on November 1 — or possibly sooner at Singapore. His season in numbers is already remarkable; its final chapter has not yet been written. Follow F1 Fanrecap through every round as history inches closer.
