Domenicali's Deadline Has Passed — F1 Still Silent on Qatar and Abu Dhabi

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | September 17, 2026

F1 Calendar

Stefano Domenicali said mid-September. Mid-September is here. Formula 1 has said nothing.

Earlier this summer, after cancelling the Bahrain and Saudi Arabian Grands Prix due to the ongoing conflict across the broader Middle East region, the Formula 1 president and chief executive told the world that he would have an answer on the remaining Middle Eastern races — Qatar on November 29 and Abu Dhabi on December 6 — by mid-September. The message was clear: watch this space. By the time teams arrived in Baku, fans would know how the season ends.

September 17 has arrived. Teams are nine days from Baku. There has been no announcement.

What Is Still Unresolved

Qatar and Abu Dhabi remain officially on the 2026 Formula 1 calendar. Both remain in question. Bahrain and Saudi Arabia were removed in the spring, citing the conflict and security concerns across the region. Qatar and Abu Dhabi present a different profile — Qatar's venue, the Lusail International Circuit, has hosted multiple grands prix without incident, and Abu Dhabi's Yas Marina Circuit is the sport's flagship season finale location. But the broader regional situation remains the concern.

F1 has confirmed that a contingency plan exists. Imola has been described as the leading candidate for a European replacement if one or both Middle Eastern rounds cannot proceed. Portimao has also been mentioned as an alternative. If both Qatar and Abu Dhabi are cancelled, the season concludes in Europe — and with 25 fewer points available than the current calendar provides, the championship timeline shifts forward accordingly.

The Championship Consequence

This is not an administrative question. It is a question that affects when Kimi Antonelli wins the title.

With the current 23-race calendar (two of which have already been cancelled, so 21 remaining including Baku), Antonelli leads by 81 points with 233 points still available across nine rounds. The earliest he can clinch is Singapore. The most likely scenario is Mexico City on November 1 or São Paulo in November.

If Qatar and Abu Dhabi are cancelled — reducing the available points from 233 to 208 — the clinch timing shifts. Mexico becomes mathematically feasible under more scenarios. The season ends sooner, and with it, the window for Antonelli to make history closes earlier.

For every team's commercial planning, for broadcast partners, for ticketholders, and for the teams themselves, the uncertainty is a practical problem with real costs.

What Comes Next

F1 is "still monitoring," according to the most recent public statements from the sport's leadership. The Azerbaijan Grand Prix is September 26. Singapore follows in early October. If F1 intends to give teams and promoters adequate notice for any changes to the final two races, the announcement window is narrowing rapidly.

The silence is not necessarily alarming — F1 may be holding a decision until new information becomes available — but it is notable. The deadline passed, and the news cycle is waiting.

What's Next

Watch for any communication from the FIA or FOM on the Qatar and Abu Dhabi situation in the coming days. The Baku race weekend begins September 24. Singapore is October 2-4. If an announcement is going to come in time to matter for logistics and planning, it is likely within the next two weeks. Until then, both races remain on the calendar — and the season's ending remains unwritten.
