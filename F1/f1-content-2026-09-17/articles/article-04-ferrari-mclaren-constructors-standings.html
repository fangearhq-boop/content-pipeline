Ferrari Leads McLaren by 52 in Constructors' — The Real Fight of the 2026 Season

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | September 17, 2026

F1 Constructors' Championship

The 2026 Formula 1 Constructors' Championship has a clear leader. Mercedes has 503 points, 145 clear of second-place Ferrari, and the numbers make their title effectively a matter of formality. With 403 constructors' points remaining across nine rounds, the Mercedes trophy is not in doubt.

But the fight for second place very much is.

Ferrari (358 points) leads McLaren (306 points) by 52 points after the Spanish Grand Prix. That gap is meaningful but not insurmountable. More importantly, it is active — the swing potential at Baku in nine days is real, and the remaining calendar has enough unpredictability to keep the chase alive through November.

The Mathematics for McLaren

To overtake Ferrari for second in the constructors', McLaren needs to average more than six additional constructors' points per race than the Maranello team over the remaining nine rounds. Six points per race — the gap between a sixth and a fourth, roughly — sounds manageable. But sustaining that gap across nine rounds against a Ferrari team with two proven race-winning drivers is a different matter.

The Spanish GP illustrated the volatility. Hamilton's Lap 7 DNF cost Ferrari significantly — 25 points that should have been in the mix were lost before the race was half done. McLaren scored 15 points (Norris third, Piastri sixth). Ferrari scored 12 (Leclerc fourth, Hamilton zero). The gap moved, but not enough for McLaren to close it below 50.

Why Baku Matters

Baku City Circuit is one of the highest-variance rounds on the calendar. Safety Cars are frequent. Wall contact removes cars from contention without warning. The slipstream dynamics on the long main straight create first-lap drama that can reshuffle the entire grid within the first few kilometers. A race that begins with Mercedes, Ferrari, and McLaren at the front can end with a completely different points distribution if the chaos touches the wrong cars.

For McLaren, Baku is one of the better remaining opportunities to pick up the kind of swing they need. If Ferrari loses both cars to incidents while Norris and Piastri score in the top five, the gap compresses significantly. For Ferrari, the objective is to manage both cars to the finish — avoid the kind of Hamilton DNF that cost them in Spain.

The Remaining Calendar

After Baku: Singapore, where the tight street circuit also creates variance. Then Japan, Malaysia, Mexico, São Paulo, Las Vegas, and potentially Qatar and Abu Dhabi — both still uncertain. Six of the nine remaining rounds are on tracks where incidents are common and gaps can close quickly.

Ferrari's 52-point lead is not comfortable. It is the kind of lead that requires consistent scoring to maintain — and Ferrari's 2026 record has shown that consistency is not guaranteed when one of their drivers is having mechanical issues.

What's Next

Baku on Saturday September 26 is the next major data point for the constructors' fight. Watch how both Ferrari and McLaren manage their cars in a high-risk environment, and watch for any technical failures that shift the points balance. The gap entering Baku is 52 points — the gap exiting Baku could be anywhere from 80 to 20, depending on what the circuit decides.
