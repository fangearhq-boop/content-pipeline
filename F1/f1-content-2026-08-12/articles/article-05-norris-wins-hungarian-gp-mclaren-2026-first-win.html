Norris Ends McLaren's Winless Streak With Dominant Hungarian GP — 15 Seconds Clear at the Flag

By Ryan Calloway | August 12, 2026 | F1 Fanrecap

The last race before the Formula 1 summer break had a clear message: the defending world champion is still very much in this sport, even if the championship table doesn't show it yet.

Lando Norris won the Hungarian Grand Prix on July 26 by approximately 15 seconds, ending McLaren's winless streak in a season dominated by Kimi Antonelli and Mercedes. It was Norris's first victory of the 2026 campaign and McLaren's first win of the year — both statements delivered at the best possible moment, heading into the summer break.

The Race

The Hungaroring result unfolded with some early drama. Norris and Oscar Piastri had a brief internal team disagreement on strategy in the opening stages of the race — the kind of inter-team tension that McLaren has managed carefully throughout the 2025 and 2026 seasons. It was resolved before Norris built his winning gap, and he pulled clear of Max Verstappen's Red Bull to take the win by a comfortable margin.

The full podium: Norris first, Verstappen second, Antonelli third. Charles Leclerc was fourth. Lewis Hamilton was fifth after a five-second pit lane speeding penalty dropped him behind Leclerc at the finish. George Russell finished seventh for Mercedes. Isack Hadjar took sixth for Racing Bulls.

What It Means for McLaren

McLaren spent the first half of 2026 watching Antonelli and Mercedes collect victories. Six of the first 11 races went to the No. 6 car. McLaren's MCL60 was competitive in places — Norris scored podiums — but victories remained out of reach until Budapest.

A 15-second winning margin is not a fluke. It is a statement about performance. Whether the Hungaroring's technical, lower-speed layout particularly suited McLaren's package, or whether the team has genuinely closed the gap to Mercedes in raw performance, will become clearer at Zandvoort on August 21-23. McLaren's existing lineup — Norris through at least 2027, Piastri through 2028 — gives them stability around which to build. The win in Hungary gives them momentum.

What's Next

Norris carries the momentum of a dominant win into the Dutch Grand Prix, the opening round of the second half. He remains outside the top five in the championship after a difficult first half, but a 15-second Hungarian GP victory changes the conversation about where McLaren stands relative to the field. Whether he can follow it up at Zandvoort — and close the enormous gap to Antonelli — is the first test of the season's second chapter.
