Verstappen's Exit Clause Is Active — McLaren, Lambiase, and an October Deadline

By Ryan Calloway | August 12, 2026 | F1 Fanrecap

Max Verstappen's Red Bull exit clause is now officially active. The four-time world champion entered the Formula 1 summer break 6th in the championship standings — roughly 110 points behind Kimi Antonelli — triggering the performance threshold written into his contract. He has until October to formally decide whether to trigger the clause and leave Red Bull Racing.

The clause was negotiated specifically because of Verstappen's apprehension about the 2026 technical regulations, which dramatically altered the sport's power unit configuration and the aerodynamic balance of the cars. Red Bull has struggled in the new era; Verstappen sits sixth, not second. The clause exists because he insisted on an exit route if the results didn't follow.

McLaren as the Frontrunner

If Verstappen triggers the clause, McLaren are the team most frequently linked with signing him for 2027. Multiple factors point in that direction. Most significantly: Gianpiero Lambiase, Verstappen's race engineer throughout his championship years at Red Bull, signed with McLaren in April 2026 as Chief Racing Officer. His departure to Zak Brown's team was noted in paddock circles as meaningful. Engineers of that seniority do not move to teams they do not believe in.

More recently, Verstappen's personal racing brand — Verstappen Racing — announced joint support with McLaren for Belgian junior driver Dries van Langendonck. The arrangement brought together Verstappen's own brand and McLaren in a commercial collaboration. In a sport where signals often precede announcements, paddock observers are reading it carefully.

Competing Signals

McLaren CEO Zak Brown told media publicly that talks with Verstappen "didn't go anywhere" and that a move "will not happen any time soon." ESPN reported that British media claims Verstappen is "close" to a McLaren deal are "wide of the mark." Verstappen himself has declined to publicly commit to Red Bull beyond the 2026 season.

The complicating factor, as analysts at Crash.net noted, is McLaren's existing lineup. Lando Norris is contracted through at least 2027. Oscar Piastri is locked in through 2028. Adding Verstappen requires moving one of them — which neither McLaren nor the drivers have indicated any appetite for doing.

What's Next

Verstappen has until October to formally notify Red Bull of his decision. The Dutch Grand Prix at Zandvoort on August 21-23 — his home race — arrives nine days from now, with the clause active and the October deadline framing every conversation about his future. Whether an announcement comes before, during, or after Zandvoort, the second half of 2026 will produce an answer.

Red Bull, for their part, has no financial protection if the clause is triggered. The team receives zero compensation under the terms. Their best case is that Verstappen's form improves dramatically in the second half, making the clause irrelevant because he's competing for a championship. There are 291 points remaining across 11 rounds — not enough to catch Antonelli without a near-perfect run, but enough to change the picture significantly.
