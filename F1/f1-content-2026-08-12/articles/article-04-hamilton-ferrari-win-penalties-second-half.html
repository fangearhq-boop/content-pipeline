Hamilton at Ferrari: The Wins Are Real, So Are the Penalties — Four in Three Weekends

By Elena Voss | August 12, 2026 | F1 Fanrecap

Lewis Hamilton's first year at Ferrari has been everything and nothing like what the sport anticipated. He's second in the world championship with 169 points. He scored an emotional maiden victory in the scarlet car at the Spanish Grand Prix in Barcelona. He is outpacing teammate Charles Leclerc by 31 points heading into the summer break and now qualifies 0.017 seconds per lap faster — a reversal from 2025 when Leclerc held a 2.5-tenths advantage over him.

And across his last three race weekends, he collected four penalties.

The Penalty Pattern

At Silverstone: a five-second time penalty for a false start. At Spa-Francorchamps: a five-second penalty for causing a first-lap collision with George Russell, who subsequently retired from the race. At the Hungarian Grand Prix: two separate penalties, including one for speeding in the pit lane that cost him a position at the finish — he dropped behind Leclerc on the road and ultimately finished fifth.

Four penalties in three weekends is a pattern that cannot be dismissed as bad luck. Hamilton, at 42 years old, is adapting to a new car, a new team, and a new set of expectations — all while competing for a world championship. The margin for error in a 50-point gap is not large. Each penalty that costs him positions is a points transfer to the championship leader.

Ferrari's Power Gap

The structural challenge for Hamilton and Ferrari is speed. Hamilton himself has estimated that Ferrari's SF-26 loses roughly 0.3 to 0.4 seconds per lap compared to Mercedes on straights — a deficit rooted in power unit output. Ferrari are chasing an update for the second half. Until it arrives, Hamilton must extract more from a car that is, on pure numbers, behind its primary championship rival.

That Ferrari managed 138 points from Leclerc and 169 from Hamilton through 11 rounds is a credit to both drivers and the team's race craft. Motorsport Week, in a piece published August 6, described Hamilton's title bid as "gathering power" heading into the second half — noting that his adaptability and experience give him tools that raw pace alone cannot provide.

What's Next

Hamilton trails Antonelli by 50 points with 291 remaining. A second world championship with Ferrari is not a mathematical impossibility — it requires outscoring Antonelli consistently across 11 rounds. The Dutch Grand Prix at Zandvoort on August 21-23 is where the second half begins. Ferrari will be looking for a clean weekend without penalties. Hamilton will be looking to prove that the Spanish GP was a preview, not an anomaly.
