Ferrari's Michael Schumacher Tribute at Monza: A Breathtaking Salute to the Legend

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap

September 4, 2026

Ferrari has done something truly special for the 2026 Italian Grand Prix — and the reaction from the F1 world has been overwhelmingly emotional.

The Scuderia unveiled a stunning Michael Schumacher tribute livery for this weekend's race at Autodromo Nazionale Monza, honoring one of the greatest drivers in the history of the sport — and the most successful in Ferrari's own storied history. The 2026 Italian GP marks 30 years since Schumacher first joined Ferrari in 1996 and 20 years since his final season with the team in 2006.

The Livery

The SF-26 has been given a glossy red finish with a white rear wing and black detailing intended to recall the F310 — the car with which Schumacher began his Ferrari career in 1996. The wheels have been given a distinctive gold tint, while the car carries Schumacher's iconic MS logo and the seven-star symbol long associated with his helmet design — one star for each of his World Championships.

Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc will both wear specially designed Schumacher-inspired race suits for the entire Monza weekend. Sabine Kehm, Schumacher's manager of many years, gave an emotional response to the tribute upon seeing the car for the first time.

Historic Cars on Track

Ferrari is not limiting the tribute to the SF-26's livery. The team is bringing three iconic cars from Schumacher's Ferrari era to Monza for demonstration runs that will rank among the most memorable moments of the weekend.

The trio of cars: the 1996 F310 (Schumacher's first Ferrari, also the car the livery is based on), the legendary 2002 F2002 (arguably the most dominant Ferrari ever built, which Schumacher used to obliterate the competition in his fifth title year), and the 248 F1 (his car in 2006, his final season with the Scuderia).

Rubens Barrichello — Schumacher's closest teammate and partner at Ferrari from 2000 through 2005 — will drive one of the historic cars on Saturday. Four-time World Champion Sebastian Vettel, who grew up idolising Schumacher and won his first championship with Ferrari, will then take over for a demonstration lap before Sunday's race.

The Legacy

Schumacher won five of his seven World Championships with Ferrari, cementing the bond between driver and team that is unmatched in F1 history. He won at Monza multiple times, including victories that moved him closer to his record titles, and the circuit has always held a special place in his story.

The tribute comes at a time when F1 has a new generation of stars — including Antonelli, a fellow Italian racing at his home circuit this weekend — but the shadow Schumacher cast over the sport has never diminished.

What's Next

Qualifying on Saturday will determine Sunday's grid, with the demonstration runs adding an extraordinary emotional backdrop to the race weekend. Whatever the outcome on track, the Schumacher tribute ensures the 2026 Italian Grand Prix will be remembered long after the chequered flag falls.

For Ferrari fans old enough to remember Schumacher in red, this weekend is a gift. For those who discovered the sport later, it is an introduction to a legend.
