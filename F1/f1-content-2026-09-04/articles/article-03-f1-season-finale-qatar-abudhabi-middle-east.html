F1 Season Finale in Doubt: Qatar and Abu Dhabi GPs May Be Cancelled Due to Middle East Conflict

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap

September 4, 2026

The 2026 Formula 1 season may not end in the Middle East after all.

Formula One Management has until mid-September to decide whether Qatar (November 27-29 at Lusail) and Abu Dhabi (December 4-6 at Yas Marina) can safely host the final two rounds of the World Championship amid ongoing conflict in the region. If FOM concludes that those venues cannot safely stage races, the season will instead finish in Europe — with Imola as the leading candidate to host a replacement finale.

A Season Already Disrupted

The 2026 season has already been reshaped by the conflict. Both the Bahrain Grand Prix and Saudi Arabian Grand Prix were cancelled before the season began, creating a gap in the early calendar. Bahrain's race was salvaged through an unprecedented arrangement — the event was moved to Malaysia's Sepang International Circuit and is now scheduled for October 2-4.

The situation has continued to develop, with recent US strikes on Iranian targets around the Strait of Hormuz and subsequent Iranian threats of retaliation keeping the region unsettled. FOM has so far maintained Qatar and Abu Dhabi on the calendar, but the mid-September deadline represents the point at which contingency plans must crystallize into decisions.

What Cancellation Would Mean

The stakes extend well beyond logistics. With the championship battle potentially tightening as the season progresses, the total points available could determine who wins the title. If Qatar and Abu Dhabi are cancelled and the season ends at Imola or Portimao instead, teams and drivers would need to reassess their strategies for the remaining rounds.

For a championship fight already featuring compelling storylines — Antonelli's large but not unassailable lead, Norris's recent surge, Hamilton and Russell in the mix — fewer rounds means less margin for error and fewer opportunities for comebacks.

The Imola Option

Imola's Autodromo Enzo e Dino Ferrari is the frontrunner to serve as a replacement venue if needed. The circuit held its own dramatic return to the F1 calendar in recent years and has the infrastructure to host a Grand Prix at relatively short notice. Portimao in Portugal is also being considered.

Either European circuit would provide a very different end to the season from the usual Abu Dhabi twilight finale — potentially more unpredictable conditions and a race environment less suited to strategic domination, which could favor drivers willing to take risks.

What's Next

The mid-September deadline arrives within days. Teams will be monitoring developments closely as the Italian GP, Spanish GP and Azerbaijan GP play out — and as FOM communicates with authorities in Qatar and the UAE about the feasibility of hosting the final rounds.

For now, Qatar and Abu Dhabi remain on the calendar. But in a year that has already rewritten the F1 schedule multiple times, nothing can be taken for granted.
