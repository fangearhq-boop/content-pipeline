Norris Enters Monza as Favourite: Can the 2025 Champion Win Three in a Row and Revive the Title Fight?

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap

September 4, 2026

Lando Norris has done it before. He has won the World Championship. He knows what a title run feels like, the momentum that builds when everything clicks and the wheels cannot stop turning.

That momentum is back. And this weekend at Monza, with championship leader Kimi Antonelli starting from the back of the grid, Norris has his best shot yet at making the 2026 Formula 1 title fight something truly worth watching.

Back-to-Back Wins

Norris arrives at the Italian Grand Prix on a streak. The McLaren driver won the Dutch Grand Prix to kick off the second half of the season, then backed it up with another victory — two consecutive wins that have rebuilt his confidence and reminded the grid what a motivated, focused Norris looks like.

The bookmakers have taken notice. Norris is listed as the favourite for Sunday's race at odds of 2.50, ahead of George Russell (3.25) and Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc (both at 7.50). Max Verstappen is an outsider at 13.00.

The Championship Mathematics

Norris sits 83 points behind Antonelli with a number of rounds remaining. In normal circumstances, that gap would feel significant — a gap requiring not just wins but Antonelli misfortune. This Sunday provides exactly that misfortune, served up by Mercedes' own strategic decision.

If Norris wins and collects 25 points while Antonelli scrapes together, say, five or six points from a difficult recovery drive, the gap shrinks to the low 60s. If the remaining schedule includes the Italian GP, Spanish GP, Azerbaijan GP and further rounds before a potential early European finale, every point matters.

Three consecutive wins would also represent a psychological statement — a declaration that McLaren's MCL40 is as fast as anything on the grid and that Norris, as defending champion, has not given up his title without a fight.

Why Monza Suits Norris and McLaren

McLaren have built their season around raw pace, and Monza rewards raw pace more than any other circuit on the calendar. The low-downforce setup requirements and the emphasis on power and aerodynamic efficiency play to the MCL40's strengths. Norris has looked comfortable in the car since the summer break, and McLaren's strategy team has executed well in recent rounds.

The competition remains fierce. Russell and Hamilton in the Mercedes will be quick, and Ferrari's Hamilton hopes to deliver the Scuderia a home victory in front of the passionate Tifosi. But if the timing of the weekend's conditions and strategy align in Norris's favour, the outcome could be spectacular.

What's Next

Free Practice today sets the baseline. Qualifying Saturday. The race — 2:00 PM local time Sunday — decides everything.

If Norris wins, this championship is alive in a way it hasn't felt since the summer break. If he doesn't — if Antonelli recovers points or if another driver breaks through — the maths become very daunting indeed.

Lando Norris is ready. The Temple of Speed is ready. Sunday cannot come soon enough.
