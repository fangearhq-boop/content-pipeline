Red Bull Offers Verstappen €65M a Year. McLaren Talks Are in "Closing Stages." No Answer.

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | August 9, 2026

F1 Driver News / Max Verstappen / Red Bull Racing / McLaren

The Max Verstappen exit clause story has escalated on two fronts simultaneously, and neither has produced an answer. Red Bull reportedly tabled a new contract worth €65 million per season through 2029, structured to eliminate the exit clause entirely. The offer has been sitting with Verstappen's management for weeks. No formal response. At the same time, multiple sources report that McLaren negotiations are in "closing stages" — a characterization that McLaren's Zak Brown publicly denied, that Piastri's manager Mark Webber dismissed, and that has done nothing to end the speculation.

The clause is active. The deadline is not August. And Formula 1's most consequential driver situation in years is moving toward resolution — on a timeline that will shadow the Dutch Grand Prix, the Italian Grand Prix at Monza, and the Singapore Grand Prix.

Red Bull's Counter-Offer: €65M to Stop the Bleeding

Red Bull's reported counter-offer is a number worth pausing on. At €65 million per season — equivalent to roughly $70-72 million at current exchange rates — Verstappen would become the highest-paid driver in Formula 1 history, by a considerable margin. The ask in return: eliminate the exit clause entirely, extending Verstappen's commitment through 2029.

That offer is, in its own way, an admission of where the leverage sits. The exit clause, if triggered, requires no compensation from Verstappen's side. Red Bull cannot demand payment. They cannot seek damages. If Verstappen invokes the clause and moves to another team, Red Bull receives nothing. The only thing preventing that outcome is Verstappen choosing to stay — and Red Bull is apparently willing to pay €65 million per year for that choice.

Verstappen's camp has had the offer for several weeks. There has been no formal response.

McLaren: "Closing Stages" and Public Denials

SportBible and PlanetF1 both reported in early July that Verstappen's camp initiated contact with McLaren and that negotiations were at an advanced stage. The rumored structure was a straight swap: Verstappen to McLaren, Oscar Piastri to Red Bull. Zak Brown denied any such talks when asked directly. Piastri's manager Mark Webber pushed back, suggesting it was no more than Verstappen "exploring options."

In the F1 silly season, public denials are part of the game. What is notable is that both the denial and the original report came from credible sources — and that neither side's public statements have actually settled whether contact occurred. The saga continues.

The Actual Deadline: Mid-October

A widely held misconception about the exit clause is that the window is August only. It is not. The clause window runs from August to October 2026, with a formal invocation deadline of approximately mid-October. That means Verstappen can legally take until around October 15-20 to make his decision — and there is no legal obligation to say anything before then.

The Dutch Grand Prix on August 23 is Verstappen's home race. Zandvoort is his circuit, built for his fanbase, with the Dutch crowd behind him on every corner. Whether he announces, hints, or says nothing — all three outcomes are possible at Zandvoort. What is not possible is the saga being over before then.

What's Next

The Dutch Grand Prix is August 21-23 — Verstappen's first race since the exit clause became active. Watch for any pre-weekend media statements from Verstappen's camp or Red Bull. A formal announcement — if it comes — would logically land either before the Dutch GP (to clear the air for his home race) or at Monza in early September (traditional silly season announcement timing). The mid-October deadline is the hard limit. Everything before it is choice.
