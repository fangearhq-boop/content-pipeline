F1's 2026 Season Finale in Doubt: Qatar and Abu Dhabi Face Replacement by Imola

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | September 26, 2026

The end of the 2026 Formula 1 season has been thrown into genuine uncertainty, with the Qatar Grand Prix on November 29 and the Abu Dhabi Grand Prix on December 6 both at risk of being cancelled or replaced. Two team principals at this week's Baku press conference confirmed that a new decision deadline — mid-October 2026 — has been set after the original September deadline passed without resolution.

Ayao Komatsu of Haas and Mattia Binotto of Audi both acknowledged the updated timeline on Thursday, telling reporters that the sport expects clarity on the final two rounds within the next few weeks. F1 CEO Stefano Domenicali had originally indicated a September deadline when the issue was first raised in July; that date came and went without announcement.

What Is Behind the Uncertainty

The situation affecting Qatar and Abu Dhabi is linked to ongoing regional conflict, though the precise details of what has triggered F1's contingency planning have not been publicly specified by the sport's governing bodies. The implication from team principals and paddock sources is that the situation remains fluid, and that staging major international events in the region later this year carries logistical and political complications that need resolving.

Both Qatar and Abu Dhabi have long-standing contracts with Formula 1 and have invested heavily in their race infrastructure. Neither venue is believed to be happy about the possibility of being replaced on the calendar.

Imola Steps Up

The primary backup venue identified by F1 is Imola — formally the Autodromo Enzo e Dino Ferrari, located in the Emilia-Romagna region of Italy. The circuit hosted the San Marino Grand Prix for decades before rotating off the calendar, and has previously returned as a host during times of calendar disruption.

Imola is not waiting passively. The circuit is already constructing grandstands in preparation for a potential return. That level of preparation suggests the F1 organisation has treated the Imola option as more than theoretical, with serious logistical planning already underway.

Portimao in Portugal has also been mentioned as a secondary contingency, though Imola appears to be the first call option.

What It Means for the Championship

The decision on these final rounds matters for multiple reasons beyond pure calendar administration. The championship will still be alive heading into the final two rounds — Kimi Antonelli's 81-point lead is significant but not clinching. Where those races are held, and who races at home or in their comfort zone, can carry marginal but real effects on the title outcome.

For fans and media who have already made travel arrangements to Qatar or Abu Dhabi, the uncertainty is also a practical concern. Mid-October gives about six weeks of lead time before the November 29 Qatar date — barely enough to organise alternatives.

What's Next

The mid-October deadline for a decision means the situation will likely be resolved — or escalated — well before the next race weekend. F1 Fanrecap will report as soon as the official announcement is made. In the meantime, Imola's grandstand construction continues. Whether those seats will be filled for a 2026 F1 race remains to be confirmed.
