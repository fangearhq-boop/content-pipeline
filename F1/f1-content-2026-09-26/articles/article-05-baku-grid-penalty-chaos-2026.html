Four Penalties and a Championship Crash: The 2026 Baku Grid Explained

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | September 26, 2026

The 2026 Azerbaijan Grand Prix starting grid tells almost no coherent story from qualifying. Four separate grid penalties — issued before a single lap of racing — combined with championship leader Kimi Antonelli's Q1 crash have produced one of the most scrambled starting orders of the year. If you are watching the race and wondering why certain drivers are nowhere near where you expected them, here is the full explanation.

Carlos Sainz: Minus 5 Places

The stewards handed Carlos Sainz a five-place grid penalty for failing to reduce speed sufficiently under yellow flags during qualifying. Lando Norris went off late in the Q3 session, triggering yellow flags in a sector. Sainz was found to have not slowed enough as required under the regulations. A five-place drop is one of the more significant routine penalties available to the stewards and reflects the seriousness with which yellow flag violations are treated.

Sergio Perez: Minus 3 Places

Sergio Perez received a three-place grid penalty for impeding Oscar Piastri during a qualifying run. The McLaren driver was on a hot lap when Perez moved in a manner the stewards judged to have caused unnecessary interference. Three-place impeding penalties have become increasingly common as the FIA attempts to manage track crowding during qualifying sessions.

Fernando Alonso and Lance Stroll: Both from the Back Row

Aston Martin's decision to take new power unit elements at Baku has sent both of their drivers to the back row of the grid. Fernando Alonso and Lance Stroll both received major power unit penalties, which under the current regulations means an automatic start from the back.

This appears to be a strategic decision by the Silverstone-based team: Baku is one of the lower-priority circuits for championship ambitions at this stage, and burning through engine allocations here makes sense if it allows Alonso and Stroll to have fresh power units available for the remaining rounds. It is a pragmatic sacrifice now for a potential advantage later.

Kimi Antonelli: Q1 Crash, Starts ~P15

The wildcard in all of this is Antonelli. The championship leader was eliminated in Q1 after hitting the inside barrier at Turn 1, breaking his front suspension. He did not post a representative lap time and will start from approximately P15 once all penalty adjustments are applied. This is by far the worst starting position he has occupied in the 2026 season.

The Natural Qualifying Order vs. Reality

The natural qualifying result had George Russell on pole, Charles Leclerc P2, Oscar Piastri P3, Isack Hadjar P4, Lando Norris P5. Verstappen qualified eighth. With the penalties applied and Antonelli's crash factored in, the actual starting grid is significantly different from that picture.

The Baku City Circuit is already one of the most chaotic and unpredictable venues on the F1 calendar. Safety cars, crashes, and strategic chaos are standard features of the Azerbaijan Grand Prix. A scrambled starting grid only amplifies that unpredictability.

What's Next

The race begins at 3:00 PM local time in Baku. Antonelli will be attempting one of the most dramatic recovery drives of the 2026 season from P15, while Russell leads from pole position.
