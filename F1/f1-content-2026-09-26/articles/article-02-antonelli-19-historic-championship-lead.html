At 19, Kimi Antonelli Is Rewriting F1 History — One Race at a Time

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | September 26, 2026

Before Friday morning's sessions at the Baku City Circuit, Kimi Antonelli owned the 2026 Formula 1 season. Eight race wins. Six pole positions — more than any other driver. Fifteen podiums from fourteen starts. An 81-point championship lead over the second-best driver on the grid. And all of it accomplished at 19 years of age, making him the youngest championship leader in the history of the sport.

Then Saturday happened.

Antonelli clipped the barrier at Turn 1 during Q1, damaged his front suspension beyond repair, and was eliminated from qualifying before he had posted a competitive time. He will start Sunday's Azerbaijan Grand Prix from approximately P15 — the worst grid position of his debut season in Formula 1. For the first time all year, the championship feels even slightly less certain than it did the day before.

A Season That Belongs in the Record Books

Put the Baku qualifying to one side for a moment and consider what Antonelli has already achieved in 2026. At 19 years, 7 months, he became the youngest driver ever to lead the Formula 1 World Drivers' Championship — a record that superseded names stretching back decades. He is also the youngest driver to claim a pole position in F1 history, at 19 years and 6 months. He has scored consecutive wins three times in a season, the youngest driver ever to achieve that feat.

Through 14 of 22 rounds, Antonelli has 292 points to runner-up George Russell's 211. Mercedes as a constructor leads Ferrari by 145 points. This has been a team and a driver performing at a level that recalls some of the sport's great dominant eras — and yet there is something deeply compelling about Antonelli doing it at this age, in his first full F1 season.

The Two Blanks That Weren't His Fault

Antonelli's record is not without blemish — but context matters. His two rounds without a points finish in 2026 came at Barcelona and Britain, and both were attributed to mechanical failures. Neither DNF was caused by a driving error. Saturday's Baku crash is genuinely the first significant mistake Antonelli has made all season.

That degree of consistency from a 19-year-old in his first year of F1 is almost unprecedented. The sport does not reward rookies this generously. Antonelli is different: composed, technically precise, and clearly driving with total confidence in the machinery beneath him.

The Championship Math Still Favours Antonelli

Even if Sunday's race goes poorly, the numbers still protect Antonelli. Nine rounds remain after Baku, covering 225 driver points. His 81-point advantage means Russell would need to outscore him by an average of nine points per race for the rest of the year just to level things up. That is possible — but it requires sustained perfection from Russell and ongoing misfortune for Antonelli.

The worst scenario from Baku — a Russell win at 26 points, Antonelli scoring zero — still leaves the gap at 55 points with 8 races to go. Significant still.

What's Next

Antonelli starts Sunday's Azerbaijan Grand Prix from approximately P15. The question is how many points he can salvage from a compromised position — and whether Russell can capitalise from pole position. After Baku, the championship continues across nine more rounds through to December. At the pace Antonelli has set this season, writing him off after one bad qualifying session would be a significant mistake.
