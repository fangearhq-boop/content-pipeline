Lando Norris Takes Historic First Madring Pole by 0.011 Seconds — McLaren's New-Circuit Mastery on Display

By Elena Voss | September 13, 2026

In a qualifying session without historical data as a reference, Norris delivered a lap he called a "masterpiece" to claim his 19th career pole — and McLaren's first at the brand-new Madring circuit.

Nobody had ever driven a competitive Formula 1 car around the Madring in qualifying conditions before Saturday. That blank slate was, for Lando Norris, an invitation.

The reigning world champion and McLaren driver produced a lap of 1m31.824s in Q3 to claim pole position for the inaugural Madring Grand Prix — pipping championship leader Kimi Antonelli of Mercedes by just 0.011 seconds. It was Norris's 19th career pole position and the first ever at Madrid's new Formula 1 circuit.

Norris described the lap as a "masterpiece," a word that captures both the quality of the effort and its context. On a circuit that offered no historical baseline — no data from previous seasons, no qualifying records to chase, no knowledge of where the rubber would go down — Norris trusted his instincts and delivered when it counted.

The 0.011-Second Margin

The margin between pole and second place at the Madring was 0.011 seconds — 11 thousandths. In Formula 1, that is essentially a rounding error across a 57-lap race distance. It reflects just how closely matched the top cars were in conditions where nobody had a clear development advantage.

Antonelli, who starts his qualifying sessions with the weight of championship expectations and team pressure that Norris operates with slightly more freedom from, was 0.011 seconds away from claiming the historic first Madring pole himself.

Max Verstappen in the Red Bull was third, 0.140 seconds behind Norris — a gap that suggests Red Bull found a good race setup but perhaps sacrificed some one-lap performance. Lewis Hamilton recovered from his dramatic FP3 crash to qualify fourth (+0.189s), while Charles Leclerc was fifth — remarkably close to his teammate despite a chassis that had been through a significant impact just hours earlier.

What the Qualifying Tells Us About McLaren

McLaren's ability to put Norris on pole at a brand-new circuit is significant. The team spent 2025 locked in a championship fight that required absolute pace at every venue on the calendar. Their development programme for the 2026 regulations — which brought wholesale technical changes to every car on the grid — appears to have delivered a car that can adapt quickly to unknown conditions.

Norris signed a contract extension in August 2026 to stay with McLaren through at least the 2030 season, committing to what will be a 12-year stint with the team if the deal runs its full course. The Madring pole is the kind of result that reinforces why both sides see a long-term future in the partnership.

The Antonelli FIA Warning

Antonelli received a warning from the FIA following the qualifying session after a breach was confirmed during the session. Importantly, the warning carried no grid penalty, leaving Antonelli in second position for the race start. Whether the incident affected his final lap or represented a separate technical matter did not change his grid position — but it added a footnote to a qualifying session that already had plenty of storylines.

What's Next

Norris started the Madring Grand Prix from pole — the first time any driver led the grid at the new Madrid circuit. The ultimate measure of McLaren's pace at the Madring is the race result, with the team's strategy from P1 determining whether Norris could convert the fastest qualifying lap into a race win. With 10 rounds remaining in the 2026 championship, every result that closes the gap to Antonelli matters for the title fight Norris remains mathematically alive in.
