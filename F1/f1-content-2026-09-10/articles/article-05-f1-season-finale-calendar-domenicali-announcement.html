Domenicali's Mid-September Calendar Decision Is This Week — Here Is What the 2026 F1 Finale Looks Like

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | September 10, 2026

FOM CEO Stefano Domenicali committed to announcing the revised 2026 Formula 1 season calendar "by mid-September." That deadline arrives this week. Malaysia is confirmed. Qatar and Abu Dhabi remain scheduled. The Imola or Portimão question will be resolved within days.

Formula 1 is in an unusual position heading into the final third of its 2026 season: the schedule is not entirely finalized.

The reason is straightforward. The Bahrain Grand Prix and the Saudi Arabian Grand Prix were removed from the 2026 calendar following regional security concerns that arose from US military activity near the Strait of Hormuz. The cancellations, announced in early summer, left two vacant spots in the schedule that FOM committed to filling.

FOM CEO Stefano Domenicali provided that commitment with a specific deadline: "by mid-September." That deadline is approximately five days away.

What Is Already Confirmed

Malaysia's Sepang International Circuit has been confirmed as a replacement event, slotted for October 2-4. Sepang was a Formula 1 venue until 2017 — a beloved circuit among drivers for its technical demands and its atmosphere — and its return as an emergency calendar addition has been broadly welcomed. The logistics were reportedly straightforward because Malaysia's circuit infrastructure remained in maintained condition and the relevant regulatory approvals moved quickly.

Qatar (November 27-29) and Abu Dhabi (December 4-6) remain on the calendar as of this writing. Abu Dhabi has been the traditional season finale for several years; the Yas Marina circuit is purpose-built for Formula 1 and is not in a region directly affected by the security concerns that removed Bahrain and Saudi Arabia from the schedule. Qatar's Lusail circuit has similarly remained on the plan.

If both those events proceed as scheduled, the season concludes with back-to-back races in the Gulf. The 2026 World Championship could be decided in Qatar or Abu Dhabi, depending on how the remaining rounds play out.

The Imola and Portimão Question

The outstanding variable is whether FOM adds additional rounds to fill the full season slate. Reports throughout the summer have identified Imola in Italy and Portimão in Portugal as the leading contingency venues — circuits that have hosted Formula 1 recently, whose infrastructure is ready, and whose local regulatory frameworks can accommodate racing events on relatively short notice.

Imola has been the more frequently cited option. It hosted the Emilia Romagna Grand Prix until the event was removed from the permanent calendar following 2024. Its facilities, race control infrastructure, and track surface are all in condition. The circuit's owners have been in contact with FOM throughout the summer. If an additional European slot is added to the final-stretch calendar, Imola is the most obvious candidate.

Portimão hosted two Formula 1 races in 2020 and 2021 during the COVID-disrupted calendar period, and its oceanside layout produced memorable racing on both occasions. As a contingency option, it offers similar readiness to Imola with a different circuit character.

Whether either venue is actually added depends on whether FOM determines that the overall race count for 2026 is sufficient as is, or whether the commercial and sporting logic of adding European dates outweighs the logistical challenge of short-notice organization.

Why the Announcement Matters for the Championship

Teams planning for the final stretch of a championship season need to know their schedule weeks in advance. Logistics — freight, personnel deployment, hospitality, press operations — require lead time that a last-minute calendar addition can make extremely difficult. By committing to a mid-September announcement, Domenicali gave teams a planning horizon that, with the Madrid Grand Prix running this weekend, is now arriving.

For the championship contenders, the total remaining round count matters mathematically. More rounds mean more opportunities for gap-closing results. Fewer rounds compress the window and make Antonelli's current lead harder to overcome. An additional European race or two favors the chasers more than the leader, purely because leaders need fewer opportunities to clinch while contenders need more occasions to produce swing results.

What's Next

Watch for a formal FOM calendar statement between now and approximately September 15. The announcement will confirm whether Qatar and Abu Dhabi proceed as scheduled, and whether any additional rounds — most likely Imola or Portimão — are added to the 2026 calendar.
