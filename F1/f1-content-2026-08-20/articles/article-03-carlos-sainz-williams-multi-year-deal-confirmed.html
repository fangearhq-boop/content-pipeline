Carlos Sainz Signs Multi-Year Deal With Williams — 2027 Lineup Complete Alongside Albon

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | August 20, 2026

Carlos Sainz has made his future clear. The former Ferrari driver has signed a new multi-year contract with Williams Racing, committing to the team for 2027 and beyond. The announcement completes Williams' driver lineup alongside Alex Albon, who extended his own deal one day earlier.

The signing ends months of speculation about Sainz's future — with Audi and other teams reportedly in the picture — and represents a significant vote of confidence in Williams' long-term project.

The Context: A Difficult Season

Williams is not having an easy 2026. The team sits ninth in the Constructors' Championship after 11 rounds, with just 11 points — a difficult position for a team with two highly regarded drivers. Sainz, who won races with Ferrari and consistently challenged for top finishes throughout his career, is operating well below his potential results in the current Williams car.

And yet he stayed. The multi-year deal reflects a belief — shared by Sainz and Williams leadership — that the current situation is a trough, not a destination. The team has investment, technical ambition, and a driver lineup that would be competitive at any team in the paddock on pure talent.

The Albon Partnership

Williams announced Albon's extension just one day before Sainz's deal, making this a calculated sequence of announcements that signals organizational confidence. Both drivers now have long-term clarity, and the team can focus on development without the distraction of driver contract negotiations.

Albon and Sainz have been competitive teammates — both capable of extracting the maximum from a car that hasn't always given them much to work with. When Williams does eventually produce a competitive package, they'll have two proven drivers ready to capitalize.

What About Audi?

Audi's entry into Formula 1 — taking over the existing Sauber chassis for the 2026 season — has been one of the most anticipated developments in the sport's recent history. Sainz was reported to be on Audi's shortlist as they built their driver program. That he chose to stay at Williams rather than join a manufacturer-backed entry at the ground floor says something about either the Williams project's appeal or concerns about Audi's development trajectory, or both.

Sainz has not publicly discussed his deliberations in detail. Williams has said the team's long-term vision and investment convinced him to extend.

The Bigger Picture

With Verstappen's Red Bull extension announced Thursday and Sainz's Williams deal confirmed Wednesday, the 2027 grid picture is becoming remarkably settled for this early in the process. The movement of marquee drivers — which typically generates the most interest in F1's "silly season" — appears to have concluded without the blockbuster reshuffling many expected.

What remains uncertain is which teams will make the biggest steps forward by 2027. If Williams delivers improved machinery, Sainz and Albon could make a serious case for relevance further up the grid. If Red Bull can rebuild around Verstappen and their new technical direction, the championship narrative could shift dramatically.

What's Next

For this weekend, Williams will be racing at Zandvoort in the Dutch Grand Prix Sprint weekend. Sainz's announcement doesn't change the immediate competitive picture, but it settles the atmosphere in the Williams garage heading into the second half of a challenging season.

For the longer term, the team now has exactly what it needed: two committed drivers and a defined lineup to build around. The results will follow — eventually. Sainz believes it. So does Williams.
