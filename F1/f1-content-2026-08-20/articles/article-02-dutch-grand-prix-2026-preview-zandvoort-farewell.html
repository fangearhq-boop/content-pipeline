Dutch Grand Prix 2026 Preview: Zandvoort's Final Farewell — Everything You Need to Know

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | August 20, 2026

Formula 1 returns from the summer break at one of its most atmospheric venues — and one it will never visit again. The Dutch Grand Prix at Zandvoort, nestled among the coastal dunes near Haarlem, hosts its final Formula 1 race this weekend. After decades away and a triumphant return beginning in 2021, the circuit closes its F1 chapter this Sunday.

Adding to the occasion: this is the first time Zandvoort hosts a Sprint weekend. For fans expecting the familiar three-day format, everything is compressed, accelerated, and higher-stakes from the very first session.

Full Weekend Schedule

Friday August 21: FP1 (Practice 1) and Sprint Qualifying — the only practice session teams get before competitive action begins. Sprint Qualifying sets the Sprint grid.

Saturday August 22: Sprint Race at 10:00 UTC, followed by main race Qualifying at 14:00 UTC. The Sprint race awards championship points. Qualifying determines Sunday's grid.

Sunday August 23: The main Dutch Grand Prix — the final Formula 1 race at Zandvoort.

The Sprint format means teams arrive with almost no setup data for the circuit. One practice session before Sprint Qualifying is a brutal constraint — it rewards teams with strong simulation tools and experience at similar circuits. Small setup errors that would normally be corrected in FP2 or FP3 carry all the way to the Sprint race.

Championship Context

The 2026 title race arrives at Zandvoort at a pivotal moment. Kimi Antonelli leads with 219 points, 50 ahead of Lewis Hamilton and 59 ahead of George Russell. Hamilton won the Belgian Grand Prix before the summer break, demonstrating that Ferrari still has race-winning capability. Russell has been consistent throughout the season, never far off pace.

Lando Norris dominated at Hungary with McLaren's upgraded car, raising the possibility that the reigning world champion could re-enter the championship conversation if the upgrade translates to different circuit types. And Verstappen — who announced today he's staying at Red Bull through 2030 — will race in front of his home orange army with a settled mind for the first time in months.

With 316 points still available across the remaining rounds, Antonelli's lead is comfortable but far from insurmountable. This weekend could cement his advantage or tighten the field.

The Zandvoort Challenge

Zandvoort is one of the most distinctive circuits on the F1 calendar. The steeply banked final sector, known as the Arie Luyendyk corner, allows drivers to carry extraordinary speed without the usual grip penalty. The tight, flowing layout rewards mechanical grip, precise entry-speed management, and confidence through the high-speed banked sections.

Weather is always a wildcard here. The circuit sits near the North Sea coast, and conditions can change rapidly. Rain is not uncommon even in August, and coastal winds affect aerodynamic balance in ways that don't appear on driver data sheets. Teams running Sprint-format weekends have precious little time to adapt.

The Emotional Weight of a Farewell

Zandvoort's return to the F1 calendar from 2021 onwards was driven significantly by Verstappen's rise to global stardom and the explosion of Dutch F1 fandom that followed. The orange army — fans in Verstappen's signature color — transformed the atmosphere at Zandvoort into something unique in modern motorsport: part sporting event, part national festival.

This weekend, that army will be there one final time at their home circuit. Verstappen's Red Bull extension announcement today adds an extra layer of emotion: he's not going anywhere, even if Zandvoort's F1 chapter is closing.

What's Next

Practice begins Friday. Sprint Qualifying follows. The orange flags go up. And by Sunday evening, the Dutch Grand Prix will have a result — and Zandvoort will have hosted its last Formula 1 race.

Whatever the outcome, this is one worth watching. Championship stakes, farewell emotions, a Sprint format debut, and Verstappen's home crowd. It doesn't get much richer than this.
