Ferrari has confirmed that its second major power unit upgrade of 2026 — internally designated ADUO-2 — will make its debut at the Italian Grand Prix at Monza.

Here are the details:

🔧 WHAT CHANGED: The upgrade centres on the turbocharger and combustion chamber. Ferrari has fitted a larger impeller to the turbocharger while redesigning the combustion architecture. The original 2026 Ferrari power unit was built with a smaller turbo to minimize lag — a benefit that disappeared when the FIA introduced a new five-second pre-start procedure, leaving Ferrari with a significant straight-line speed deficit.

🔧 MERCEDES RESPONDS: Reports indicate Mercedes has been forced to accelerate its own upgrade timeline to counter the Ferrari threat at Monza.

### STORY 3: Monaco GP Appeal Pending

**Long-Form Post**
⚖️ THREE MONTHS LATER — THE MONACO GP RESULT IS STILL NOT OFFICIAL ⚖️

The 2026 Monaco Grand Prix was run on June 7. Today is August 28 — and F1 fans still don't know the final, official podium.

Here's where things stand:

During the race, six drivers across five teams received pit-lane speeding penalties. Pierre Gasly of Alpine received two five-second penalties — dropping him from P3 to P7. But the FIA subsequently acknowledged that the pit-lane speed limit had been incorrectly calculated due to a measurement error by Formula One Management.

Gasly successfully appealed and was restored to P3. Isack Hadjar was demoted to P4. Oscar Piastri was demoted to P5.

Red Bull and McLaren — whose drivers were pushed down in the standings — appealed Gasly's reinstatement. The FIA International Court of Appeal heard those arguments in Paris on August 25.

The verdict? Could take up to three weeks. That means the final Monaco GP result might not be confirmed until mid-September.

In the meantime: the points for Gasly, Hadjar, Piastri — and everyone around them — remain provisional.
