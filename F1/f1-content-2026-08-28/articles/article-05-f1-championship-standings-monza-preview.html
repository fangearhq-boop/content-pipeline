2026 F1 Championship Standings: Antonelli Leads by 59 Points — But Monza Could Change Everything

By Elena Voss | August 28, 2026

After the Dutch Grand Prix at Zandvoort, the 2026 Formula 1 World Championship enters its most consequential phase with the following standings: Kimi Antonelli leads with approximately 242 points, 59 ahead of George Russell. Lewis Hamilton is third on tiebreaker also at 183 points, 53 back from the leader. Lando Norris, winner of the last two races, sits fourth at roughly 159 points, 83 behind Antonelli with 11 races remaining.

The numbers look like an Antonelli runaway. The circumstances entering Monza suggest something far more complicated.

The Antonelli Situation

Kimi Antonelli — who has led the championship for the majority of the 2026 season, won five consecutive races in one stretch, and averaged the points haul of a genuine champion — will start the Italian Grand Prix from the back of the grid. His Mercedes team has exceeded the season's power unit allocation, triggering a mandatory penalty at a circuit that will be overwhelmingly hostile to his cause: a Monza crowd that cheers red, running against a Ferrari that just upgraded its engine.

A 59-point championship lead is substantial. But a poor weekend at Monza — where Norris, Hamilton, and Russell all have the potential to score well ahead of him — could trim it to something more manageable for his rivals in a single afternoon.

Russell vs Hamilton: The Tiebreaker

The current second-place position is settled by tiebreaker in Russell's favour over his Mercedes teammate Hamilton. Both sit on the same points total, separated only by head-to-head race wins this season.

This teammate rivalry adds an internal dimension to the Mercedes strategy at every race. Favouring one driver over another in strategy has its own calculus when both are theoretically in championship contention — though Antonelli, as the clear leader, has been and remains the team's primary focus.

Norris's Momentum

Two consecutive wins for Lando Norris have given McLaren and the former champion genuine momentum heading into the second half of the season. The gap — 83 points — is mathematically steep, but not closed. In a season where the gap has fluctuated significantly and Antonelli faces a penalty race at Monza, Norris's position is better described as a long shot than an impossibility.

McLaren needs Norris to win at Monza — or at least score maximally — while Antonelli has a poor day. Those are two conditions that look more achievable this particular weekend than they have at any point since midsummer.

What's Next

The Italian Grand Prix at Monza (September 4-6) is the immediate priority. After that, the schedule includes Singapore, Austin, Mexico City, São Paulo, and Las Vegas before the season concludes in December. There are 11 races, 286 potential points, and at least four drivers with a mathematical case to make.

The standings say Antonelli is in control. The situation says Monza is going to be very, very interesting.
