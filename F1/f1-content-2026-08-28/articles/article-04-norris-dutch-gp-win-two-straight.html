Norris Wins Dutch GP for Second Consecutive Victory — Antonelli Holds On, Verstappen Out

By Marcus Cole | August 28, 2026

Lando Norris won the 2026 Dutch Grand Prix at Circuit Zandvoort, crossing the line 11.5 seconds ahead of championship leader Kimi Antonelli to claim his second consecutive Formula 1 race victory. The win reduces Norris's deficit to Antonelli to 83 points with 11 races remaining — and arrives with Antonelli facing a grid penalty at the Italian Grand Prix next weekend.

A Dramatic Start

The race began with immediate chaos. Max Verstappen, driving in front of his home crowd at Zandvoort, lost the rear of his Red Bull on the opening lap and hit the barriers heavily, triggering a red flag before the race had barely gotten underway. The Dutch crowd watched in stunned silence as Verstappen retired before having a meaningful chance to race.

From the red flag restart, Norris asserted himself immediately. The reigning world champion showed the pace and consistency that has characterised his best races this season, managing tyres, gap control, and strategy execution with the kind of precision that wins championships — or keeps the pressure on those who are trying to.

Antonelli Limits the Damage

To Antonelli's credit, the 20-year-old championship leader finished second. When your rival wins and you still finish on the podium, you limit the damage as well as circumstances allow. George Russell completed the Mercedes one-two-three in a sense, finishing third and reinforcing the team's dominant constructor position.

Antonelli's lead over Russell now stands at 59 points — a tiebreaker edge. His lead over Hamilton is also significant. But Monza next weekend, with the penalty forcing him to start last, could erase a meaningful portion of that advantage in a single afternoon.

Norris's Championship Calculation

At 83 points back with 11 races to go, Norris's mathematical path to the championship remains open but genuinely difficult. Each race at maximum point value is 26 points (win plus fastest lap). To win the title, Norris needs to outscore Antonelli by an average of more than 7.5 points per race — a tall order, but not impossible in a season where Antonelli is about to face one of the most difficult races of his young career.

Two consecutive wins have shifted the momentum narrative. Norris is the driver in form. Antonelli is the driver with the grid penalty. Monza is the race that sits between them.

What's Next

The Formula 1 calendar moves immediately to Italy. The Italian Grand Prix at Autodromo Nazionale di Monza runs September 4-6, with qualifying on Saturday and the race on Sunday. Antonelli will start from the back of the grid. Ferrari debuts its ADUO-2 engine upgrade. Hamilton, currently third in the championship, has his clearest title opportunity of the season.

Norris arrives at Monza as the driver who most recently held the top step of the podium. The question is whether he can make it three in a row.
