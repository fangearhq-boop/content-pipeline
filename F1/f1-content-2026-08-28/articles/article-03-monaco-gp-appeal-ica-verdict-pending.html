Monaco GP Appeal: The ICA Heard Arguments on August 25 — Verdict Could Take Weeks

By Ryan Calloway | August 28, 2026

The 2026 Monaco Grand Prix was held on June 7. Today is August 28 — and the official result of that race is still not confirmed. The FIA's International Court of Appeal heard arguments from McLaren and Red Bull on August 25 in Paris, but a verdict could take up to three weeks to be issued, leaving the Monaco podium and associated championship points in provisional status well into September.

How We Got Here

The Monaco story began during the race itself, when six drivers across five teams received pit-lane speeding penalties. Among those most severely affected was Pierre Gasly of Alpine, who received two five-second penalties — the cumulative effect of which dropped him from a podium finish of third all the way to seventh.

Shortly after the race, the FIA and Formula One Management acknowledged that the pit-lane speed limit at Monaco had been incorrectly calculated, the result of an erroneous distance measurement in the pit lane. Alpine successfully appealed, and Gasly was restored to third place. Isack Hadjar of Red Bull was demoted to fourth. McLaren's Oscar Piastri was pushed to fifth.

Red Bull and McLaren — whose drivers lost ground in the reshuffled results — then filed their own appeals against Gasly's reinstatement, arguing that the process for restoring the Alpine driver to the podium had not followed proper procedure.

The ICA Hearing

The FIA's International Court of Appeal, held in Paris on August 25, heard full arguments from both appellant teams. The FIA's own position, along with Alpine's defence of the current result, was also presented.

The ruling is expected to take up to three weeks given the complexity of the legal arguments involved — centring not only on the factual question of the incorrect distance measurement but on the procedural questions about how corrected results can be applied and what evidence can be used in such appeals.

What Hangs in the Balance

The current provisional Monaco result has Gasly in P3, Hadjar in P4, and Piastri in P5. If the ICA upholds Gasly's reinstatement, the current order stands. If McLaren and Red Bull prevail, the results would revert to some version of the post-race classification — though exactly what that would look like depends on how the court interprets the procedural questions at issue.

The championship points for each of these drivers remain provisional until the ruling is issued. In a year as tight as 2026, those points matter: Piastri is not among the front-runners for the title, but a swing of several points in either direction has downstream effects on constructor standings and head-to-head positioning.

What's Next

The ICA's verdict is expected sometime in the two-to-three-week window following the August 25 hearing, placing the likely announcement in mid-September — potentially coinciding with the aftermath of the Italian Grand Prix on September 6.

Until then, the Monaco GP result remains one of the most unresolved stories in modern Formula 1. Nearly three months after the chequered flag fell on the streets of Monte Carlo, the official podium is still a question mark.
