Monza Preview: Antonelli Starts Last, Ferrari Upgrades, and Hamilton Has His Shot

By Marcus Cole | August 28, 2026

The Italian Grand Prix is one week away, and the setup for one of the most consequential races of the 2026 Formula 1 season has never been more dramatic. Kimi Antonelli — the championship leader — will start from the back of the grid at Ferrari's home race. Ferrari will debut its biggest engine upgrade of the season. And Lewis Hamilton, 53 points back in the standings, will go to the most power-sensitive circuit on the calendar with the clearest title opportunity he has had all year.

The Penalty That Changes Everything

Toto Wolff confirmed earlier this week that Mercedes has performed a full power unit change on Antonelli's car, exceeding the season's allocation and triggering a mandatory grid penalty at the Italian Grand Prix. Antonelli, 20 years old and leading the championship by 59 points, will line up at the very back of the grid at Monza on September 6.

Monza offers more recovery potential than almost any circuit on the calendar. Its long straights, minimal chicane disruption, and reliance on slipstreaming rather than pure mechanical grip make it theoretically possible to charge through the field. But starting last and finishing anywhere near the podium in a competitive championship field remains an enormous challenge.

Ferrari's ADUO-2 Arrives

The Scuderia is bringing its second major power unit specification of 2026, internally designated ADUO-2, to Monza. The upgrade involves a redesigned turbocharger with a larger impeller and modifications to the combustion chamber. Ferrari's own simulations project approximately 15 additional horsepower and a gain of roughly 0.2 seconds per lap at Monza — a circuit where raw power translates more directly to lap time than anywhere else on the schedule.

The timing is perfect for Ferrari and Hamilton. The original 2026 power unit was designed around a smaller turbocharger intended to minimize lag off the line — but when the FIA introduced a new pre-start procedure, that advantage evaporated, leaving Ferrari at a straight-line deficit. ADUO-2 is the correction, and Monza, the fastest circuit in the championship, is the ideal venue to measure its impact.

Hamilton's Window

Hamilton currently sits 53 points behind Antonelli in the championship standings — third overall on tiebreaker behind George Russell. Every race has featured the same calculation: Antonelli needs to be beaten convincingly, and consistently, for the gap to close at a meaningful pace.

Monza provides a scenario where both conditions might be simultaneously satisfied. Antonelli working through the field from the back. Ferrari running its most competitive power unit of the season. The pressure of the Italian crowd, the emotion of the Ferrari garage, and Hamilton's decades of experience in high-stakes moments.

It will not be a certain opportunity. But it is the clearest one Hamilton has had all season.

What's Next

The Italian Grand Prix begins September 4 at Autodromo Nazionale di Monza. Qualifying is Saturday, September 5, with the race on Sunday, September 6. It is the 97th edition of the event and one of the oldest and most beloved races on the Formula 1 calendar.

With the championship in its most delicate phase — 11 races remaining, four drivers within theoretical striking distance — Monza has the potential to be the turning point of the entire 2026 season. Seven days from now, we find out.
