Norris Calls for FIA Bans After Colapinto Triggers Three-Car Baku Retirement

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | September 27, 2026

Lando Norris was not diplomatic about it. After retiring from the 2026 Azerbaijan Grand Prix as the innocent victim of a collision he had no part in creating, the McLaren driver delivered one of the most pointed post-race statements of the F1 season — and called directly on the FIA to issue race bans for incidents like the one that ended his race.

What Happened

At a safety car restart, Franco Colapinto braked too late and collided with his Alpine teammate Pierre Gasly. Gasly, unable to control his car, was then collected by Norris. All three drivers retired on the spot.

The incident was a particularly brutal blow for Norris, who had no opportunity to avoid the collision. He was a passenger in someone else's mistake.

Colapinto accepted responsibility over team radio — an acknowledgment that while important, did nothing to restore the points Norris lost.

Norris's Reaction

"Some drivers shouldn't be in F1," Norris said after the race. "I think the FIA should be more strict and start handing out bans for this kind of stuff. It's just careless."

The words are notable for their directness. Formula 1 drivers rarely call explicitly for rival drivers to be banned — even when frustration runs high. Norris's willingness to say it publicly reflects how significant the impact of the collision is to his championship position.

The Championship Damage

Norris entered Baku 106 points behind Kimi Antonelli in the Drivers' Championship. After the retirement, he sits 116 points back — with only 8 rounds remaining. The mathematical path to the title, already narrow, has become even more dependent on Antonelli suffering a catastrophic run of results.

That is not what happens to drivers like Antonelli in 2026. But Norris's frustration is legitimate: he was the victim of recklessness, not the author of it.

Colapinto Under Scrutiny

This incident places Franco Colapinto under significant scrutiny. The Alpine driver accepted fault immediately on team radio, but the consequences for three separate drivers — himself, his teammate, and a championship contender — will intensify questions about whether the FIA's current penalty framework is sufficient.

Race stewards may issue a post-race penalty. Whether that satisfies Norris, who is watching his title hopes diminish through no fault of his own, is another matter entirely.

What's Next

Norris needs a near-perfect run of results from the remaining eight rounds to mount any realistic challenge. Singapore (October 9-11, sprint weekend) is the next opportunity — and for Norris, it is less about title hopes now and more about salvaging pride and proving he can still win races when the machinery allows it.

The FIA ban debate, meanwhile, will likely continue through the Singapore week. Whether the governing body takes Norris's demand seriously is a storyline that could define the final stretch of the 2026 season.
