Aston Martin's Fourth Double DNF of 2026: The Season From Hell Continues in Baku

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | September 27, 2026

Fernando Alonso and Lance Stroll retired from the 2026 Azerbaijan Grand Prix — the fourth time in this season that Aston Martin has failed to see both cars reach the finish line. In China, Barcelona, Italy, and now Baku, the team has walked away without a single championship point from either car.

For a team with the resources and ambitions of Aston Martin, the pattern is alarming.

Another Penalty-Laden Start

Both Alonso and Stroll came into the Baku race already at a significant disadvantage. Combined engine change penalties of 45 grid places — Alonso receiving a 25-place penalty, Stroll receiving a 20-place penalty — meant both drivers started at the very back of the field.

Starting from the back in Baku is not impossible. The 2.2-kilometre main straight and the circuit's long DRS zones give cars pace advantages that can theoretically make up ground. But Aston Martin never got the chance to find out.

Stroll Out on Lap 7

Lance Stroll's race lasted seven laps. A water pressure failure at Turn 15 brought his afternoon to an end before he had any real opportunity to begin the recovery.

Stroll's Baku DNF reportedly set an 18-year Formula 1 record for DNF frequency — a statistic that speaks not just to Baku, but to a full season of mechanical misfortune that has followed the Canadian driver throughout 2026.

Alonso Fights Longer, Falls the Same Way

Fernando Alonso lasted until Lap 21 before a power unit issue ended his race. The problem was symptomatic — a power loss that was costing Alonso approximately 1.5 seconds per lap down the main straight, making meaningful progress toward the points impossible.

For a 45-year-old who has publicly stated his desire to remain competitive until the new generation of regulations reward teams like Aston Martin, the second half of 2026 has been a study in frustration.

A Pattern That Cannot Be Ignored

Four double DNFs. China. Barcelona. Italy. Baku. In each instance, both cars failed to score points. The pattern spans different circuits, different race formats, and different mechanical failure modes — which suggests the problems are not isolated to a single component or circuit type.

The deeper concern is that Aston Martin brought significant engine penalties to Baku precisely because they are already consuming power unit allocations at an accelerated rate. That suggests underlying reliability issues that are systemic, not incidental.

What's Next

With eight rounds remaining, Aston Martin's constructors' championship position continues to slide. Singapore (October 9-11) offers the team a fresh start on a street circuit where — in theory — anything can happen.

In practice, a fourth double DNF in 2026 raises serious questions about whether "anything happening" is more likely to mean another retirement than a points finish. The team needs answers — and quickly.
