Ferrari's Schumacher Tribute at Monza Is More Than a Livery — It's a Love Letter to a Legend

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | August 29, 2026

Ferrari has done tributes before. But what the Scuderia has revealed for next weekend's Italian Grand Prix at Monza stands apart — a design that honors not just a driver, but the era that defined an entire generation's relationship with Formula 1.

The SF-26 will run in a special Michael Schumacher tribute livery at Monza on September 4-6, drawing inspiration from the F310, the car Schumacher drove in his very first season with Ferrari back in 1996. The design features red and black detailing with gold wheels — a bold, striking departure from the team's standard 2026 appearance that will turn heads the moment the cars roll out of the garage.

Seven Stars, One Legacy

The tribute extends beyond the cars. Race suits worn by Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc will feature seven stars on the back — one for each of Schumacher's seven world championship titles. His personal logo is also present on each suit, positioned beneath the race numbers of both drivers.

The timing is deliberate and significant. This year marks 30 years since Schumacher first took the wheel of a Ferrari, and 20 years since his final season with the Scuderia in 2006. That final season ended, fittingly, at Monza — where he won his penultimate race for the team before announcing his retirement at the very same circuit.

Schumacher won 72 of his 91 career victories with Ferrari. He claimed five consecutive world championships between 2000 and 2004, a run of dominance that will likely never be replicated. Honoring that legacy at Monza, the spiritual home of Italian racing, is the only way to do it justice.

The Strategic Layer

Ferrari's tribute weekend is also loaded with sporting significance. The team is expected to debut its ADUO-2 power unit at Monza — the largest single upgrade of the 2026 season, delivering approximately 15 additional horsepower and an estimated 0.2 seconds per lap. At a circuit where straight-line speed is the dominant factor in lap time, that gain is not a small thing.

Championship leader Kimi Antonelli carries a grid penalty and will start from the back. That creates a rare opportunity for Hamilton and Leclerc at Ferrari's home race. Combine the new power unit with the circuit's natural overtaking opportunities, and Ferrari has constructed a weekend where performance and emotion intersect at exactly the right moment.

What's Next

The Italian Grand Prix begins with practice sessions on Friday, September 4. Qualifying follows on Saturday, and the race runs on Sunday, September 6. Watch for the first reveals of the tribute livery in full during the paddock walkabout and pit lane cameras. This is one weekend where the images will tell as much of the story as the lap times.
