Monaco GP Appeal: No Verdict Yet, and the Championship Could Still Shift When One Arrives

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | August 29, 2026

It has been months since the Monaco Grand Prix. The controversy over Pierre Gasly's third-place finish — and the FOM measurement error that sat at the heart of the dispute — has still not found a final resolution. The International Court of Appeal heard arguments from McLaren and Red Bull on August 25 in Paris, but no verdict was issued that day. The decision is expected around mid-September.

In the meantime, Gasly holds P3 in the Monaco results. The championship points that flowed from that result — to Gasly and to Alpine — remain provisional in the most literal sense: subject to change by a ruling body with the authority to alter them retroactively.

What the Appeal Is About

The Monaco controversy stems from a penalty issued to Gasly during the race for what was adjudged a violation in the pit lane. FOM, the commercial rights holder for Formula 1, subsequently admitted that the measurement of the relevant pit lane distance contained an error. That admission formed the cornerstone of McLaren's and Red Bull's appeal — if the measurement was wrong, the penalty derived from it should not stand.

The ICA's task is to determine whether the error materially affected the penalty, and if so, whether the Monaco race results should be revised. This is not a simple administrative correction. Changing the results of a completed Grand Prix involves redistributing points already counted in the championship standings.

Championship Implications

In a season as tight as 2026 at the midpoint, every set of points matters. Kimi Antonelli leads the championship by 59 points, a margin that has felt comfortable but becomes less so with every race remaining. Any retroactive shift in Monaco's results — affecting Gasly's classification, and potentially others in the points — would ripple through the standings in ways that are difficult to predict without knowing what the revised result would look like.

McLaren's Lando Norris, who is third in the championship 83 points behind Antonelli, is among those with potential interest in the outcome. Red Bull's involvement in the appeal suggests their cars were also affected by the original ruling in ways they contest.

The Timing

A mid-September verdict arrives during what will be a concentrated stretch of racing after Monza. Formula 1 typically runs back-to-back rounds in September and October, which means the championship will be in an intensely active phase when the ICA rules. The distraction of a controversial ruling — and potentially acrimonious reactions from whichever side loses — lands at the worst possible moment for the teams and drivers affected.

For now, all parties wait. Pierre Gasly keeps his podium. The championship standings hold as they are. And somewhere in Paris, a legal process unfolds that could rewrite a small but meaningful piece of the 2026 Formula 1 season.

What's Next

Watch for the ICA verdict between now and mid-September. If it arrives before Monza, it becomes part of the conversation heading into the Italian Grand Prix. More likely it drops in the weeks after, during the post-Monza racing calendar. Either way, when the ruling comes, the championship standings will need to be re-read in light of whatever the court decides.
