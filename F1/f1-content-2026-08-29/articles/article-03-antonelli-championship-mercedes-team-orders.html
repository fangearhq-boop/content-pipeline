Antonelli Leads by 59 Points — And Mercedes Refuses to Play Favorites

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | August 29, 2026

Kimi Antonelli is 59 points clear at the top of the 2026 Formula 1 Drivers' Championship, and the team that put him there has just made clear it will not put its thumb on the scale to protect him.

Mercedes confirmed this week that it will not impose team orders to favor Antonelli's title bid. In a season where the 20-year-old Italian — who turned 20 on August 25 — has already made history as the youngest driver ever to lead the World Championship, the decision to keep the internal battle fair is both principled and potentially consequential.

Where the Championship Stands

Antonelli sits on 242 points. Teammate George Russell is second on 183. Lewis Hamilton, driving for Ferrari, is also on 183 points with Russell holding the advantage on countback. Lando Norris — who just won his second consecutive race at the Dutch Grand Prix — sits on 159 points, 83 behind Antonelli with 11 races remaining.

In a season where the maximum available points are well north of 300, nobody is mathematically eliminated. But the 59-point gap Antonelli holds over his nearest rival is real. He has been consistently the fastest and most complete driver on the grid in 2026.

The Monza Wildcard

Antonelli's grip on the championship faces its sharpest test yet at Monza next weekend. He will start the Italian Grand Prix from the back of the grid after exceeding his power unit allocation, triggering a penalty that places him last on the starting grid.

Monza is one of the few circuits where a last-place start does not guarantee a last-place finish. The Temple of Speed's long straights and minimal corner count create genuine overtaking opportunities that don't exist at most circuits on the calendar. Antonelli is capable of charging through the field. But how far he gets — and how many points Russell, Hamilton, and Norris take — will determine whether this weekend closes the championship picture or reopens it.

Mercedes has been clear: Russell will race for himself. If Russell wins at Monza and Antonelli struggles to the points, the gap narrows significantly without a single team order being issued.

The Bigger Picture

The no-team-orders policy is consistent with how Mercedes has managed intra-team battles in recent memory. It protects both drivers' motivation and avoids the kind of internal resentment that team orders, when they go wrong, can produce. For Antonelli — a first-year driver in the full championship sense — earning the title on his own terms would carry more weight than inheriting points from a compromised teammate.

He has the car, the talent, and the points lead. What he needs now is a clean run through Monza's chaos and the mental clarity to manage the pressure of a title fight that is only getting more intense.

What's Next

All eyes turn to Monza and Antonelli's ability to recover from last place. A strong result — top five or better — would be a statement of resilience in the face of a penalty that would derail less gifted drivers. The championship picture following September 6 will either look like a formality or a genuine race. Right now, both outcomes are on the table.
