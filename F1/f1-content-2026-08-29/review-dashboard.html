### Long-Form Post FERRARI REVEALS MICHAEL SCHUMACHER TRIBUTE FOR MONZA — AND IT'S STUNNING One week before the Italian Grand Prix, Ferrari has pulled back the curtain on what might be the most emotionally powerful livery in recent Formula 1 memory. The SF-26 will run in a special Michael Schumacher tribute design at Monza, inspired by the iconic F310 — the car Schumacher drove in his first season with Ferrari back in 1996. The color scheme features red and black detailing with gold wheels, a dramatic departure from the team's standard 2026 appearance. The tribute doesn't stop at the car. Race suits worn by Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc will feature seven stars on the back — one for each of Schumacher's world championship titles — along with his personal logo on the waist beneath the race numbers of both drivers. This year carries double significance: 2026 marks 30 years since Schumacher first drove for the Scuderia, and 20 years since his final season in red. He won 72 of his 91 career victories at Ferrari, claimed five consecutive world titles between 2000 and 2004, and memorably announced his retirement at Monza in 2006 following his penultimate win for the team. Ferrari also has a strategic dimension to this weekend: the team is expected to debut its ADUO-2 power unit, delivering approximately 15 additional horsepower and an estimated 0.2 seconds per lap — the biggest upgrade of the 2026 season. At Monza, the most power-sensitive circuit on the calendar, that could matter enormously. Emotionally charged. Strategically loaded. This is going to be a week to remember. ### Long-Form Post HAMILTON APOLOGIZES AFTER DUTCH GP RADIO FURY — FERRARI NEEDS A RESET Lewis Hamilton knows he crossed a line at Zandvoort, and he's said so publicly. After finishing P4 at the Dutch Grand Prix — just off the podium in a race where the top three was Lando Norris, Kimi Antonelli, and George Russell — Hamilton was furious with Ferrari's strategy. The root of the frustration: Charles Leclerc and Hamilton were on different tyre strategies, and Leclerc refused a team order to let his teammate through. Hamilton lost critical time behind Leclerc on track, costing him a realistic shot at third place. The radio messages that followed were damning. "Great way of wasting time guys, good job," Hamilton said sarcastically on team radio. He went further in the media afterward, criticizing Ferrari's driver management and invoking his former team Mercedes as a comparison point. In his apology, Hamilton was direct: "In the heat of the moment frustration can come out, especially when you care as deeply as I do. But that wasn't the best version of me. I take responsibility for that, and I'll be better." It's the right message. But it raises real questions heading into Monza — Ferrari's home race, a weekend loaded with the Schumacher tribute and the ADUO-2 power unit debut. The team needs Hamilton and Leclerc pulling in the same direction. A P4 at Zandvoort with more friction than results is not the momentum Ferrari wanted. Monza is six days away. The reset starts now. ### Long-Form Post THE MONACO APPEAL VERDICT IS STILL COMING — AND IT COULD CHANGE EVERYTHING More than two months after the Monaco Grand Prix, the controversy over Pierre Gasly's third-place finish still has no final resolution. The International Court of Appeal heard arguments from McLaren and Red Bull on August 25 in Paris, but no verdict was issued. A decision is expected in approximately two to three weeks from the hearing date — placing the likely ruling around mid-September, as F1 heads into the run of back-to-back grands prix following Monza. The core of the dispute: FOM (Formula One Management) admitted to a pitlane distance measurement error that formed the basis of Gasly's original penalty during the Monaco race. Gasly — driving for Alpine — was penalized and then reclassified, ultimately holding onto P3. McLaren and Red Bull believe the ruling was incorrect and that the error-based penalty shouldn't stand. If the appeal succeeds, Monaco race results could be adjusted, which means championship points shift retroactively. In a season where 59 points separates the leader from second place, any redistribution matters. Gasly holds P3 for now. The ICA holds the pen. And the verdict could land right in the middle of F1's most critical stretch of the season.
