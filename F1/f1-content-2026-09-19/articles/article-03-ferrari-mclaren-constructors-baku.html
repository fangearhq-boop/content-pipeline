Ferrari Leads McLaren by 52 in the Constructors' — And Baku Should Help

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | September 19, 2026

Ferrari / McLaren / Constructors' Championship

The 2026 Formula 1 Constructors' Championship is heading into its defining phase. Ferrari holds second place, 52 points ahead of McLaren with nine rounds remaining. The gap has been narrowing — it was 59 points after Monza — and McLaren's technical director has indicated that additional upgrades are likely to arrive after the calendar leaves Europe at the end of September. Baku, the last European round, is in seven days.

The circuit characteristics of Baku matter more than usual in this context. The Baku City Circuit's 2.2-kilometer straight along the Azerbaijani coastline is the longest sustained full-throttle section in Formula 1. Historically, races on street circuits with long straights have rewarded teams with stronger power-unit output — and in 2026, Ferrari's power unit has been among the field's more competitive packages. That is not a guarantee of a strong result; street circuits introduce contact risk, safety car variables, and the kind of incident-driven chaos that scrambles planned strategies. But the underlying circuit DNA plays more favorably for Ferrari than for McLaren.

McLaren's Closing Pattern

The seven-point swing since Monza is not random. McLaren has been progressively better in the second half of the season — improving at circuits that suit their car's characteristics while Ferrari has found form inconsistent when their car's needs aren't precisely met. Their technical director Neil Houldey described there being a "good chance" of further bolt-on upgrades arriving after Europe, suggesting McLaren believes they have identified areas for improvement that the current car can accept without major architectural changes.

If those upgrades arrive at Singapore and beyond, the constructors' battle in the flyaway rounds could look different from what the current 52-point gap implies. McLaren's trajectory has been upward. The question is whether Baku interrupts that trajectory, maintains it, or — if Ferrari performs well on the long straight — reverses it temporarily.

The Championship Picture at Baku

Ferrari needs two things from Baku: score well themselves, and limit McLaren's haul. The constructors' points system means both halves of the equation matter simultaneously. Ferrari's Carlos Sainz and their second driver need strong results; so do McLaren's two drivers for the gap to move meaningfully. A scenario where Ferrari scores 30 points and McLaren scores 10 would add 20 points to the cushion and make the remaining eight rounds feel very different from a scenario where both teams score 20.

With 403 Constructors' points still available across nine rounds, the 52-point lead is significant but not safe. Historical evidence suggests gaps in this range are regularly overturned or extended depending on whether the final rounds include circuits that strongly favor one car over another. Baku is round 17 of 23. The gap at the end of this weekend will set the tone for how much pressure McLaren can realistically exert in the flyaway phase.

What's Next

The Azerbaijan Grand Prix runs Saturday September 26. After Baku, the calendar moves to Singapore on October 5 — which is also a Sprint weekend. Following Singapore: Japan, Austin, Mexico City, São Paulo, Las Vegas, Qatar, and Abu Dhabi. The season ends December 6. The constructors' title could go down to the final round, or Baku could be the moment it begins to feel decided. Seven days from now, we start finding out.
