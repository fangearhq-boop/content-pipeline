Williams' "Almost Entirely New Car" Arrives at Baku in 7 Days — But Sainz's Warning Still Stands

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | September 19, 2026

Williams Racing

Seven days from now, Williams arrives in Baku with the most significant upgrade package it has brought to a circuit in the 2026 Formula 1 season. Team principal James Vowles has described the update as "almost entirely new car" in the form of the FW48 overhaul — language that signals how substantial the changes to the car's fundamental architecture are, not just incremental bodywork revisions.

The warning label, though, has not changed since the upgrade was first announced. Carlos Sainz delivered what he called a "stark warning" about expectations: this is not a rescue package. And Vowles himself has been equally direct in saying the team's remaining 2026 upgrades will not be enough to consistently compete for championship points. The upgrade that arrives in Baku is real, and it is significant. But the context around it is essential.

Where Williams Actually Stands

Williams sits ninth in the 2026 Constructors' Championship with 11 points. They have not scored a single point since the Monaco Grand Prix in June — a run of scoreless rounds that stretches back far enough to have reshaped the team's narrative for the year from "surprise points scorer" to "struggling to make the top ten."

The FW48 started the season behind the pace curve. Williams missed a January shakedown test at Barcelona — a loss Vowles later acknowledged cost the team valuable early development data. By the time the car was being raced competitively, the rest of the field had refined setups and understood their cars in ways Williams was still working to catch up on. The chassis was not a bad design; it was an immature one that needed development time the race calendar was not going to provide.

The Purpose of the Baku Upgrade

The "almost entirely new car" language from Vowles is both accurate and somewhat clarifying. The FW48 upgrade at Baku is not a cosmetic refresh — it represents substantial changes designed to understand what the car should have been doing from the start of the season. But the team is running it in competitive races with limited pre-event mileage, which means Baku and the flyaway rounds that follow are as much a data-gathering exercise as they are a points push.

The honest assessment from Sainz and Vowles is that the car will improve. But "improved Williams" does not automatically mean "points-scoring Williams" when the midfield is as close as it is in 2026. There is a difference between a car that is 0.8 seconds per lap off the pace and one that is 0.5 seconds off. The upgrade should help close that gap. Whether it closes it enough for consistent Q3 appearances and point-scoring finishes on race day is what the data from Baku, Singapore, and Japan will start to tell them.

What's Next

Williams runs its upgraded FW48 in free practice for the first time at Baku on Thursday September 24. Qualifying is Friday September 25. The 51-lap race takes place Saturday September 26. The data collected over the Baku weekend will directly inform the team's final development decisions for the remainder of 2026 and the setup going into their 2027 car programme. For Williams, that may be the more important result from this weekend than whatever points, if any, they score.
