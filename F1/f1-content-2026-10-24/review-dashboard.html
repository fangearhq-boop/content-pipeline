NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Window: 8:00 AM ET When Lewis Hamilton straps into his Ferrari and rolls out for COTA qualifying this afternoon, he'll be chasing more than just a good starting position. Hamilton has taken four pole positions at the Circuit of the Americas — tied with Michael Schumacher for the most all-time at this circuit. A fifth pole today would give Hamilton sole possession of that record, one more piece of history for a career that already has more than most drivers could imagine. But the context is complicated. Hamilton finished P4 in Friday's second practice session, 0.615 seconds behind his own teammate Charles Leclerc. Closing that gap in a single qualifying hour, against Leclerc, Antonelli, and Verstappen all at their best, is a meaningful task. Hamilton has done it before at COTA — he knows this track as well as almost anyone alive. The question today isn't whether Hamilton is motivated. He is. The question is whether Saturday morning's FP3 session gave him what he needed to find those missing tenths.

NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Window: 2:00 PM ET Max Verstappen cannot win the 2026 Formula 1 World Championship. That title belongs to Kimi Antonelli now — the points gap is too large with too few races left, and Verstappen's title defense has gradually dissolved over the second half of the season. But Max Verstappen can absolutely win the United States Grand Prix on Sunday. That distinction matters more than it might seem. Verstappen has won four of the last five races at the Circuit of the Americas. His connection with this circuit runs deep — the bumpy, flowing layout suits Red Bull's chassis philosophy, and Verstappen's ability to extract performance from a difficult track is unmatched in the current grid. In qualifying this afternoon, Red Bull was third in Friday's sessions. That puts them in striking distance of the front row. And in the race, one safety car, one strategic call, one moment where Leclerc or Antonelli makes a mistake, and Verstappen will be right there. He won't be the champion. But he can still be a force. And at COTA, he almost always is.

What Ferrari's COTA Form Means for the Constructors' Title
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Window: 10:00 AM ET With Kimi Antonelli almost certain to deliver Mercedes a second consecutive drivers' championship, the battle that matters most on the constructors' side could come down to Austin. Ferrari enters the US Grand Prix as the fastest team on Friday's timesheets — Leclerc fastest in both FP1 and FP2, Hamilton P4 — and a strong COTA weekend would deliver crucial points in the team standings. Mercedes leads the constructors' championship, and the gap is significant, but every race that Ferrari can score heavily narrows it. The constructors' championship doesn't carry the same headline glamour as the drivers' title. But for Ferrari, it represents team legacy, resources, and vindication of the engineering direction they've committed to this season. A race where both Leclerc and Hamilton finish on the podium would be a major constructors' swing. COTA appears to suit the current Ferrari package. This weekend's result will say a great deal about where the Scuderia stands in the final six races of the season.
