Hamilton's COTA Legacy: Four Poles, One Record to Break, and a Different Kind of Saturday Mission

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | October 24, 2026

The Circuit of the Americas has been one of Lewis Hamilton's favorite hunting grounds throughout his Formula 1 career. Four pole positions at COTA — matching Michael Schumacher's all-time record for the most poles at this specific circuit — is just one part of his history in Austin.

Today, in a Ferrari for the first time at this track, Hamilton has a chance to break that record outright. And then he has a full Sunday race to navigate, carrying Ferrari's constructors' ambitions and his own desire to finish this season with individual wins.

The context is different this year. He's not chasing the championship — that's Antonelli's race to lose. He's not trying to prove himself at a new team anymore — the Ferrari move has settled into something that works. He is, instead, trying to be the fastest man on the track when it counts.

Closing the Gap to Leclerc

The challenge today is specific and quantifiable. In Friday's second practice session, Hamilton was 0.615 seconds slower than Charles Leclerc, his own teammate. That gap is the story of Hamilton's Saturday — not a rivalry with Antonelli or Verstappen, but a technical reckoning with the fact that, on this Friday at least, his car delivered less than Leclerc's.

Hamilton has recovered larger Friday gaps in qualifying before. He is one of the most technically thorough drivers in the history of the sport, and a Saturday morning practice session will give him and his engineers data to close what remains between his Friday setup and a Q3-ready package.

If Hamilton takes pole today, it's one of the stories of the season — a record broken, history made, in front of an American crowd that has watched him drive in Austin for over a decade. It would also be the clearest possible sign that Ferrari's Saturday-Sunday pace gap could open the door for a Hamilton race win on Sunday.

COTA's Meaning for Hamilton

Austin has delivered some of Hamilton's most memorable moments in Formula 1. His US Grand Prix victories here were defined by consistency under pressure — managing tires, reading traffic, converting pole positions into race wins with the kind of disciplined execution he built his title campaigns around.

Now, at 41, those championships are in the rearview mirror. What remains is the daily pleasure of extracting performance from a race car, and COTA is a circuit that rewards exactly the kind of technical precision that has always defined Hamilton's best laps.

That he's doing it in red instead of silver doesn't change the satisfaction of a perfect Q3 lap. The record is real. The crowd is real. The history is there to be made.

What's Next

Qualifying at 5:00 PM ET will tell us whether Hamilton can bridge the gap to Leclerc and fight for pole. If he does, it sets up a compelling Sunday dynamic where both Ferrari drivers start near the front, maximizing the constructors' championship opportunity. If he doesn't, Hamilton will race from further back — which, at a circuit where overtaking is possible, doesn't eliminate his race win potential.

Either way, Lewis Hamilton at COTA in a Ferrari is one of the visual signature moments of the 2026 season. And if he adds a fifth COTA pole to the four already on his career résumé, it'll be one of the most quietly meaningful records he's ever claimed.
