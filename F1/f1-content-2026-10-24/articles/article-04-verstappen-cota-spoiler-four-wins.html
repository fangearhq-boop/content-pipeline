Verstappen at COTA: The Champion Who Can't Retain His Title Can Still Dominate His Favorite Circuit

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | October 24, 2026

The three-time Formula 1 World Champion sits more than 100 points behind Kimi Antonelli in the 2026 standings. The championship is gone. Max Verstappen will not be a four-time champion this year — the mathematics of the season made that clear weeks ago.

What Verstappen can still do is win the United States Grand Prix at the Circuit of the Americas. And given his recent record at this specific circuit, that outcome remains as likely as any other.

Four wins in the last five years at COTA. The circuit's bumpy, technical layout has consistently rewarded the Red Bull car and the driving style that Verstappen has refined into arguably the most complete skill set in the current field. Even in a season where the championship has escaped him, COTA represents something different — a circuit where he is statistically the most likely winner.

Why COTA Suits Verstappen

The Circuit of the Americas presents challenges that Red Bull's car has managed better than the competition over the past several seasons. The bumps — COTA's perpetual surface issue — load the suspension in ways that other circuits don't, and Red Bull's chassis philosophy has consistently absorbed those loads more effectively than Mercedes and Ferrari.

Verstappen's specific driving gifts amplify that advantage. He is one of the fastest drivers through high-speed corners in Formula 1 history, and Sectors 1 and 2 at COTA are defined by high-speed sweepers that separate good lap times from great ones. In Friday's second practice session, Red Bull was third — behind Leclerc and Antonelli — but the gap was fractions of a second.

In qualifying, those fractions often come from the single perfect lap that Verstappen has shown the ability to produce on demand. His Q3 laps this season have been consistently better than his practice pace suggests they should be.

The Spoiler Role

Verstappen as championship spoiler is an interesting narrative. If he wins Sunday and Antonelli finishes outside the top two, the title stays unresolved for another race. That's good for Formula 1's storyline. It's potentially frustrating for Antonelli, who has earned the championship through consistency and speed.

But Verstappen isn't racing to be a spoiler. He's racing to win, because that's what he does. The distinction matters: a driver who races to block someone else eventually makes mistakes. A driver who races to win, and happens to be at a circuit where he has won four of the last five years, is simply doing what he always does.

The championship outcome doesn't change Verstappen's Sunday approach. He will start from whatever grid position qualifying delivers, manage his tires, and attack from the front if he has the pace to do so. Whether that complicates Antonelli's math is a consequence, not a goal.

What's Next

Qualifying this afternoon will set the stage. If Verstappen takes pole — a genuine possibility given his track record at this circuit and Red Bull's Friday pace — Sunday's race dynamic shifts immediately. An Antonelli championship clinch becomes harder from second place than from first, and Verstappen's race management from the lead at COTA is something the championship leader will need a plan to handle.

If Verstappen qualifies third or fourth, the race becomes a multi-car battle where anything can happen. That's arguably where this US Grand Prix is headed regardless — too much talent at the front, too many championship storylines, and one of the most competitive qualifying fields of the season.

COTA on a Sunday afternoon has delivered some of the best racing of recent years. This one carries stakes that make it worth clearing your calendar for.
