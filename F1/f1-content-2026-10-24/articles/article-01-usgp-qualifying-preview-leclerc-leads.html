US Grand Prix Qualifying Preview: Leclerc Heads Into COTA Shootout as Fastest Man on the Grid

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | October 24, 2026

Charles Leclerc has done the hard part. Two Friday practice sessions at the Circuit of the Americas, two fastest times. When the lights come on for qualifying this afternoon — 5:00 PM Eastern — Leclerc will carry Ferrari's momentum and the weight of the most competitive front-running field in recent memory.

The gap was minuscule. Kimi Antonelli's Mercedes was just 0.042 seconds behind in FP2, recording a time of 1:35.291 against Leclerc's benchmark. Max Verstappen was third. Lewis Hamilton, Leclerc's Ferrari teammate, sat fourth — 0.615 seconds off the pace, a gap that sounds small but represents meaningful ground to recover in a single qualifying hour.

The Battle for Pole

Mercedes acknowledged what the timing sheets already showed: no team can be ruled out for pole position at COTA. That kind of statement — unusually candid from a team that typically downplays expectations — speaks to how tight this qualifying field truly is.

Leclerc will enter Q3 as the driver to beat, but his FP2 advantage is a half-lap's margin, not a statement. Antonelli will have analyzed the Ferrari data extensively overnight, looking for the tenths that Friday's practice took from him. Verstappen, who knows COTA better than almost anyone in the paddock, will arrive in Q3 with a setup built for the circuit's signature high-speed complexes.

For Hamilton, the challenge is internal as much as external. Bridging 0.615 seconds to his own teammate is the qualifying narrative of the afternoon. Hamilton at this circuit has been elite historically — four poles at COTA put him level with Michael Schumacher for the all-time record. Today could be the day he breaks it. Or it could be the day the Ferrari shows that Leclerc simply has the edge in this package on this circuit.

What the Pole Means

Track position at the Circuit of the Americas is meaningful but not defining. The circuit's Turn 1 hairpin, approached by a massive uphill run from the start-finish line, creates genuine overtaking opportunity. A car that qualifies fourth can legitimately target a podium finish. In 2023, a driver who qualified third won the race.

But pole position matters for a different reason on Sunday: Antonelli's championship. If the 19-year-old starts from the front of the grid, he controls his own destiny in a way that no other starting position offers. A Ferrari pole — either Leclerc or Hamilton — means Antonelli begins the race reacting rather than leading, and in his championship-clinch scenario, reactive racing introduces variables that the points table can't absorb.

Weather and Conditions

Austin's October weather is typically hot and dry. No rainfall is expected for qualifying, which means the tire strategies developed through practice should translate cleanly. Both Ferrari and Mercedes completed extensive Pirelli compound data on Friday, and the decision-making in Q2 — which tire to use to guarantee a Q3 berth — will show which team did the better homework.

COTA's bumps remain a factor. The circuit's surface has been notorious for the kind of setup compromises that suit Red Bull's suspension philosophy, which partly explains Verstappen's historical success there. No resurfacing occurred before this race weekend, so all four top teams will manage the same challenge.

What's Next

Qualifying begins at 5:00 PM ET. The grid for Sunday's US Grand Prix — and Kimi Antonelli's potential championship-clinching race — will be set by 6:00 PM.
