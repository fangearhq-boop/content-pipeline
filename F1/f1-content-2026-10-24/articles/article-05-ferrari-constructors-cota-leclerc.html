Ferrari's COTA Opportunity: Leclerc's Practice Dominance Points to a Crucial Constructors' Weekend

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | October 24, 2026

While Kimi Antonelli's championship narrative dominates the US Grand Prix weekend storyline, there is a parallel battle at COTA with its own high stakes: the Formula 1 Constructors' Championship.

Mercedes leads the standings, and Ferrari is their primary challenger. The gap is significant but not insurmountable with six races remaining, and the Circuit of the Americas has produced meaningful scoring opportunities all season for teams willing to maximize both cars in a single afternoon. This weekend, the evidence from Friday practice suggests Ferrari is positioned to do exactly that.

Charles Leclerc was fastest in both FP1 and FP2 on Friday. Lewis Hamilton was fourth in FP2, 0.615 seconds back from his teammate — not ideal, but within a range that solid Saturday preparation can address. A front-row qualifying result from Leclerc paired with a top-five from Hamilton would represent a constructors'-points-maximizing Saturday, and it is a realistic outcome given Friday's pace.

What the Constructors' Championship Means for Ferrari

The constructors' title is Formula 1's team championship, and it carries weight that goes beyond trophies. Constructors' points determine prize fund allocations, which directly fund the following year's development budget. A higher constructors' finish means more resources, more aerodynamic testing capacity, and more ability to develop the car into a championship contender the following season.

For Ferrari — a team that has been pursuing its first constructors' title in years — closing on Mercedes at COTA would be a significant statement. The Scuderia has shown improvements throughout the 2026 season, and Leclerc's qualifying pace has been one of the strongest individual performances on the grid in recent rounds.

Leclerc's Season Arc at COTA

Charles Leclerc at COTA has historically been one of his best circuits. The flowing high-speed layout of Sectors 1 and 2 aligns with his natural driving preference — smooth, high-commitment inputs through corners where backing out costs lap time. Ferrari's current aerodynamic package is optimized for exactly the kind of downforce-heavy, high-speed configuration that COTA demands.

His 0.042-second margin over Antonelli in FP2 was built with apparent ease — no visible setup compromise, no indication that Friday's times were extracting anything close to the car's maximum. Qualifying and race pace are different challenges, but a driver who is fastest in practice at a circuit he enjoys is a driver who arrives in Q1 with genuine confidence.

Hamilton's Role

Lewis Hamilton's constructors' contribution will be determined by how much ground he makes up on Saturday. His Friday pace was 0.615 seconds off Leclerc — a gap that, if it persists to qualifying, puts him in a midfield rather than front-row position.

But Hamilton has repeatedly shown the ability to close Friday-to-Saturday gaps through technical preparation. He is methodical, detail-focused, and experienced enough to identify the specific setup changes that his car needs on a circuit he knows thoroughly. A top-five qualifying result from Hamilton, combined with Leclerc's expected front-row pace, would give Ferrari their best possible Sunday starting position for maximizing constructors' points.

What's Next

Qualifying at 5:00 PM ET sets Ferrari's Sunday grid positions. For the constructors' championship, the race result is what matters — both cars finishing well, absorbing points from Mercedes and delivering on the practice pace that Friday's sessions suggested.

The drivers' championship belongs to Antonelli. But for Ferrari and Scuderia supporters, the constructors' fight at COTA is the weekend's other story — quieter, more technical, but equally significant for where the team goes next. Leclerc's Friday pace has done the groundwork. Qualifying and race day will say whether Ferrari converts it.
