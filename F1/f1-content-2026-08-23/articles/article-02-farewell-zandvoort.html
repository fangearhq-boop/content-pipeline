Farewell Zandvoort: Formula 1 Says Goodbye to the Dutch Grand Prix

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | August 23, 2026

Today is the last Formula 1 race ever at Circuit Zandvoort. The Dutch Grand Prix — revived in 2021 after a 36-year absence from the calendar — is holding its sixth and final edition, a farewell shaped by banked corners, coastal dunes, and tens of thousands of orange-clad fans who showed up to say goodbye to one of the sport's most atmospheric venues.

The Dutch GP was dropped from the 2027 F1 calendar amid commercial pressures and competition for calendar slots. The decision was met with disappointment across the paddock. Motorsport.com captured the mood in their pre-race headline: "F1 drivers mourn old-school Zandvoort loss." The circuit carved into the North Sea dunes, with its banked Hugenholtz corner and tight, technical layout, was genuinely unlike anything else on the Formula 1 schedule.

A Circuit That Became a Kingdom

When the Dutch GP returned in 2021, it immediately had a home hero ready to turn it into his personal fortress. Max Verstappen won three of the revived circuit's first three races — 2021, 2022, and 2023 — each victory celebrated by tens of thousands of orange-dressed fans filling the dunes and grandstands in scenes that rivaled any sporting event in the world. Lando Norris won the 2024 edition and Oscar Piastri took the 2025 race, but the Orange Army never dimmed.

Verstappen spoke for drivers and fans alike ahead of Sunday's race: "It's absolutely the end of an era. To have had your own home race and to have been able to win it a few times is very special. Of course, it's a shame that it's going away, but it has also given me a lot of great moments and memories."

A Sprint Farewell Weekend

The farewell weekend itself produced a historic footnote: Zandvoort hosted its first-ever Sprint race on Saturday, August 22. George Russell won the Sprint ahead of Charles Leclerc and Norris, with Antonelli fourth and Piastri fifth. It was one more chapter in a circuit that always delivered.

The Orange Army turned out in force for race day despite Verstappen starting seventh with a flu-like illness and a car hampered by Red Bull's ongoing 2026 performance struggles. The fans in the dunes, on the banking, and in the grandstands did not come for the result — they came to say goodbye to a venue that gave Dutch motorsport five years of unforgettable memories.

What's Next

Formula 1 leaves the Netherlands and heads to Autodromo Nazionale Monza for the Italian Grand Prix on September 6. The Temple of Speed offers a different kind of atmosphere — roaring Tifosi rather than the Orange Army — but the emotional intensity will be no less real. For now, Zandvoort takes its place in F1 history: six races, three Verstappen wins, and a farewell that the sport will not soon forget.
