Verstappen's Last Home Race: A Farewell to Zandvoort in Orange

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | August 23, 2026

Max Verstappen will start seventh at the final Dutch Grand Prix, battling a flu-like illness and a Red Bull car that has struggled with fundamental performance problems throughout 2026. None of that changed the reception he received at Zandvoort. The Orange Army showed up in full force — tens of thousands of fans in the dunes, on the banking, and in every grandstand — to say farewell to the driver who turned this circuit into his personal kingdom.

Verstappen won three Dutch GPs at Zandvoort in the circuit's modern era: 2021, 2022, and 2023. Each victory was celebrated in scenes unlike anything else in Formula 1 — a sea of orange, a driver from the region lifting his home crowd to something approaching euphoria. Those moments belong to him and to the Netherlands in a way that will not be replicated when the Dutch GP falls off the 2027 calendar.

A Weekend Built on Grace

Verstappen has handled this final Zandvoort weekend with the composure of a four-time world champion who understands what he cannot control. Red Bull's 2026 car has been hampered by what both the team and driver describe as fundamental problems. P7 in qualifying was not the result anyone hoped for, but it reflected the reality of where the car stands relative to McLaren and Mercedes this season.

He arrived at the circuit with a special one-off helmet designed in the style of Delftware — the traditional Dutch ceramic art form, known for its distinctive white and blue patterns. Red Bull dressed the car in special livery as well. It was a tribute to his homeland and to the circuit that gave him some of the best days of his career. Verstappen signed a Red Bull contract extension to 2030 this week, announced on August 20, ensuring his future is settled even as this chapter closes.

When asked what he wanted from today's race, his answer was simple: "I just want to enjoy it with the fans."

The Crowd That Never Left

The Orange Army's loyalty to Verstappen has never been conditional on results. That much was clear again on Sunday. Even with their hero starting seventh and the championship battle centered on two Mercedes drivers at the front of the grid, the orange-clad crowd remained present and vocal. It is that kind of devotion that made Zandvoort unique — and that makes its departure from the calendar a genuine loss for the sport.

What's Next

After today's farewell race, Red Bull and Verstappen travel to Monza for the Italian Grand Prix on September 6. The team has been working on their 2026 car throughout the season, and team principal Laurent Mekies has praised the car's developmental trajectory. For Verstappen, the focus now shifts to rebuilding toward the level of performance that made his career record what it is — starting from the Temple of Speed.
