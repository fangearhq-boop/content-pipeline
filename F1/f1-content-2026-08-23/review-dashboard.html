Farewell Zandvoort — Orange Army Witnesses F1's Last Dutch Grand Prix — Tier 1
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Window: 10:00 AM ET Today is the last Formula 1 race ever at Circuit Zandvoort. Let that sink in. 🧡 When the Dutch Grand Prix returned to the calendar in 2021 after a 36-year absence, it felt like something special had been restored to the sport. A circuit carved into the coastal dunes of the Netherlands, with banked corners, a passionate crowd dressed head to toe in orange, and a local hero who immediately turned it into his own kingdom — Max Verstappen won three of the first three Dutch GPs on its return (2021, 2022, 2023), cementing Zandvoort as one of the most atmospheric venues on the entire calendar. But the contract was not renewed. Costs, competition for calendar slots, and commercial realities ended the run at six editions. The 2026 Dutch GP — run as a Sprint weekend, another first for this circuit — is the finale. Verstappen captured the feeling perfectly ahead of today's race: "It's absolutely the end of an era. To have had your own home race and to have been able to win it a few times is very special. Of course, it's a shame that it's going away, but it has also given me a lot of great moments and memories." The Orange Army arrived in force today despite Verstappen starting seventh. Tens of thousands of fans in the dunes, in the grandstands, and on the hillside banking are there to say goodbye. Motorsport.com's headline summed up the feelings of drivers and fans alike: "F1 drivers mourn old-school Zandvoort loss." Farewell, Zandvoort. You earned every orange scarf.

Kimi Antonelli leads the 2026 Drivers' Championship by 56 points over George Russell heading into today's Dutch Grand Prix. Those 56 points sound comfortable — until you realize that up to 25 points are available in a single race. A Russell victory combined with Antonelli finishing outside the top six could slash that gap to just 31 points. With at least 316 points still available in the season, this championship is very much alive. The qualifying positions make today especially interesting. Russell qualified second and Antonelli third — separated by 0.031 seconds on the timing sheet, separated by 56 championship points in the standings, and separated by barely more than one car length at the start of a 72-lap race. Antonelli, Mercedes' rookie sensation in 2026, has been one of the most dominant forces the season has seen: six wins, six poles, nine podiums. But Russell's sprint victory on Saturday showed he has found another gear. His charge in the second half of the season has been real, and he is not going away. Lewis Hamilton sits third in the championship, roughly 53 points behind Antonelli, but with Hamilton starting fifth today, his best hope is maintaining position in the points while the two Mercedes teammates battle. One race at Zandvoort. The championship picture could look very different this evening.

### Long-Form Post Window: 2:00 PM ET Max Verstappen's final home race at Zandvoort is not going the way anyone hoped — and he is handling it with the grace of a four-time world champion. Qualifying seventh after battling a flu-like illness all weekend, Verstappen acknowledged that Red Bull continues to struggle with the "fundamental problems" that have defined their 2026 season. The car that won eight consecutive Constructors' Championships looked sluggish and off the pace against the front-running McLarens and Mercedes, and no amount of determination from the driver's seat can fully close that gap. But Verstappen is choosing to focus on what matters most today. He said: "I just want to enjoy it with the fans." He unveiled a special one-off helmet inspired by Delftware — the traditional white and blue Dutch ceramic art form — as a tribute to his homeland for this final home race. Red Bull dressed their car in special livery as well. Verstappen won three Dutch GPs at Zandvoort in its modern era (2021, 2022, 2023). Each victory was celebrated with tens of thousands of orange-clad fans in a way that felt unlike anything else in Formula 1. Those moments belong to him. Today he starts seventh. But the reception he will receive from the Orange Army when he passes those dunes for the last time as a home-race competitor — P7 or not — will be one of the most emotional scenes Zandvoort has ever produced.

### Long-Form Post Window: 5:00 PM ET As the final Dutch Grand Prix concludes at Zandvoort, Formula 1 turns toward one of its most sacred venues: Autodromo Nazionale Monza, home of the Italian Grand Prix on September 6. The Temple of Speed is everything Zandvoort was not. Where Zandvoort has banked corners and dune scenery and a home hero beloved by his country, Monza has pure velocity — top speeds above 350 km/h, low downforce, the famous Lesmo curves and Ascari chicane, and a passion from the Tifosi that rivals anything in sport. For Ferrari, Monza is the race of the year. And in 2026, it carries particular weight. First, the technical angle: Ferrari has been deliberately saving their ADUO-2 power unit upgrade for Monza, where engine power is the primary performance differentiator. A circuit that demands maximum straight-line speed is exactly where an engine upgrade matters most. If the ADUO-2 delivers what Ferrari hopes, the Scuderia will arrive for their home race with a genuine performance weapon. Second, the emotional angle: Lewis Hamilton is racing for Ferrari for the first time. The greatest driver of his generation, in red, at the track the Tifosi treat as holy ground. Hamilton's debut Monza in Ferrari colors — whatever the result — will be one of the most talked-about moments of the season. The paddock moves south from the Netherlands. The orange scarves stay behind. The red flags come out. Monza awaits.
