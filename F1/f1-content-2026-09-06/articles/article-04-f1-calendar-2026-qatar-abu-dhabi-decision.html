F1 Season Finale Decision Coming by Mid-September — Qatar and Abu Dhabi Under Review

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | September 6, 2026

Formula 1 will have a clearer picture of its season-ending schedule within weeks. FOM CEO Stefano Domenicali has confirmed that a final decision on the status of the Qatar and Abu Dhabi Grands Prix — both currently scheduled to close the 2026 season — will be announced by mid-September.

The uncertainty follows a challenging second half of the calendar year for F1 in the Middle East. Bahrain and Saudi Arabia have already been cancelled due to regional conflict, with the Abu Dhabi and Qatar rounds still under active review.

The Calendar as It Stands

Currently, Qatar is scheduled for November 27-29 at the Lusail International Circuit, followed by the Abu Dhabi Grand Prix at Yas Marina on December 4-6 as the traditional season finale. Both remain on the calendar, but teams and broadcasters have been told to prepare for changes.

To replace the cancelled Bahrain Grand Prix, Formula 1 has added Malaysia's Sepang Circuit to the calendar, with the race scheduled for October 2-4. It marks the first Malaysian Grand Prix since 2017 and a popular return to a beloved venue for the sport's long-time fans.

Contingency Plans

Should Qatar and Abu Dhabi both be cancelled, Formula 1 has contingency circuits ready. According to reports, Imola — the historic Italian circuit that returned to the F1 calendar in recent years — is the leading alternative for a European season finale. Portimao in Portugal is also listed as a secondary option.

The backdrop to all of this is ongoing US military activity near the Strait of Hormuz, which has created logistical and security concerns for international sporting events in the region. F1's governance team is working closely with relevant authorities to assess risk, but Domenicali has been clear that fan safety and operational security are the deciding factors, not commercial pressure to keep the rounds in place.

What's Next

A mid-September announcement means teams, television broadcasters, and travel-planning fans will know the season's final shape within the next two weeks. The most likely outcome, according to multiple reports, is that at least one of the two Middle East rounds will be replaced. An Imola finale would be an unusual but crowd-pleasing alternative, returning F1's championship-defining moment to European soil and a historically significant venue. Watch for the announcement before the end of the month.
