Hamilton's Monza Dream: The Italian Grand Prix Is the One Win He's Never Had

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | September 6, 2026

Lewis Hamilton's Formula 1 record is almost beyond comprehension: 103 victories, seven world championships, pole positions at circuits that define the sport's geography. He has won at Monaco, Silverstone, Spa, Suzuka, and Interlagos — at tracks across six continents.

He has never won at Monza.

The Italian Grand Prix is the one major race on the calendar still missing from Hamilton's win list. He starts today's race fifth on the grid at the Temple of Speed, wearing a Schumacher-inspired Ferrari suit on a weekend that has already been heavy with symbolism, memory, and motorsport history.

The Weight of the Weekend

Hamilton idolized Michael Schumacher growing up. He grew up watching a man in red dominate this circuit — winning five Italian Grands Prix in Ferrari machinery, the crowd roaring at each lap. Now Hamilton is that man in red. He drives for the same team, at the same circuit, on a weekend Ferrari has dedicated entirely to the Schumacher legacy.

The F2002, the F310, and the 248 F1 were on display in the paddock all week. Sebastian Vettel completed a demonstration lap in the F2002 before Sunday's race. Barrichello ran it on Saturday. And Hamilton and Leclerc raced in suits carrying Schumacher's initials, seven stars, and gold details drawn from the 1996 Ferrari livery.

It is, by any measure, the most atmosphere-soaked Italian Grand Prix weekend in recent memory. And it is the backdrop for a driver chasing his greatest remaining prize.

What the Race Requires

Starting fifth at Monza is not a disadvantage in the same way as starting fifth at Monaco. The long straights, DRS zones, and slipstream effect mean overtaking is possible — and with championship leader Antonelli starting from the back, the field dynamics ahead of Hamilton are fluid.

Pole-sitter Gasly for Alpine represents an unusual obstacle. Russell (P2) and Piastri (P3) are the more likely race leaders after the first lap given their race pace throughout the weekend. Leclerc, starting P4, is Hamilton's closest competitor in raw grid position. For Hamilton to win, he needs both Ferraris to outperform the Mercedes and McLaren runners — which is exactly the kind of race where Ferrari's Monza-specific pace, shown in FP1 and FP2 where they ran one-two, could make the difference.

What's Next

Hamilton is 41 years old. He is in his first season at Ferrari. Whether or not today's Italian Grand Prix becomes his day at Monza, the question itself carries the kind of narrative weight that follows a driver through his career. The gap in his record is one of Formula 1's enduring facts. The circuit, the team, the weekend, and the moment are all aligned. Whatever happens, the Italian Grand Prix of 2026 will be remembered — for Gasly's pole, for Antonelli's recovery drive, for Schumacher's tribute, and for whether a man in red finally found what he had been missing all along.
