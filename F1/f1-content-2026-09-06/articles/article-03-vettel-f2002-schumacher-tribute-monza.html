Vettel Drives F2002 at Monza — Ferrari's Schumacher Tribute Reaches Its Emotional Peak

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | September 6, 2026

Before the 2026 Italian Grand Prix started, the Temple of Speed paused for something no race result could replicate.

Sebastian Vettel, four-time world champion, climbed into the Ferrari F2002 — one of the most celebrated and dominant Formula 1 cars ever built — and completed a demonstration lap at Monza. The crowd erupted. In the stands, Ferrari flags and Schumacher memorabilia appeared as thousands of tifosi watched history drive past them one more time.

A Weekend-Long Tribute

Sunday's Vettel demonstration brought the curtain down on Ferrari's most comprehensive tribute to Michael Schumacher in recent memory. On Saturday, Rubens Barrichello — Schumacher's partner through Ferrari's five consecutive Constructors' Championship titles — drove the same F2002 ahead of qualifying. It was the car in which Barrichello won the 2002 Italian Grand Prix on this very circuit.

Ferrari brought three cars from the Schumacher era to Monza: the F310 (his first Ferrari, 1996), the F2002 (the year he won the championship with a record gap), and the 248 F1 (his final Ferrari, 2006). All three were displayed in the paddock through the race weekend.

The weekend marks two significant anniversaries: the 30th anniversary of Schumacher joining Ferrari in 1996, and the 20th anniversary of his final Ferrari season in 2006. The convergence of both milestones at Monza — Schumacher's most beloved circuit outside of Germany — made the tribute particularly meaningful.

Hamilton, Leclerc, and the Living Legacy

Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc wore Schumacher-inspired race suits on race day, featuring gold wheels, the MS logo, and seven stars representing Schumacher's seven World Championship titles. The special livery was based on the 1996 F310 — a car that, while not his most successful, represented the beginning of the most legendary partnership in Ferrari history.

For Hamilton specifically, wearing a suit that honours Schumacher while driving for Ferrari carries particular weight. The seven-time world champion — equal to Schumacher's record — is now a Ferrari driver for the first time, carrying the weight of both his own legacy and the team's deepest traditions.

What's Next

The Schumacher tribute weekend closes after today's Italian Grand Prix. Ferrari will carry forward the inspiration of a 30-year legacy as the season moves toward its final third. The cars — the F310, F2002, and 248 F1 — return to the Ferrari Museum in Maranello. But for anyone who witnessed Vettel in the F2002 at Monza on Sunday morning, the memory will linger long after the race results are forgotten.
