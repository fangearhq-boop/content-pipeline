Pierre Gasly Claims Maiden F1 Pole at Monza — First French Pole at the Temple of Speed Since 1997

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | September 6, 2026

The greatest qualifying shock of the 2026 Formula 1 season arrived on a Saturday afternoon at Monza, and the man who delivered it was Pierre Gasly.

The Alpine driver produced a stunning final lap of 1:21.786 seconds in Q3 to claim the first pole position of his career, edging George Russell's Mercedes by just 0.060 seconds. Oscar Piastri completed the top three for McLaren, ahead of Charles Leclerc and Lewis Hamilton in the two Ferraris.

Nobody had predicted this. Alpine has not been a pole-threatening team in 2026. But Gasly — calm, focused, and perfectly in tune with Monza's high-speed demands — found something no one else could match at the end of qualifying.

A Moment 14 Seasons in the Making

Gasly made his Formula 1 debut in 2017 and has driven for Red Bull, AlphaTauri, and Alpine across 14 seasons of competition. He has won a race — a famous, emotional victory at Monza in 2020 when the race fell to chaos — but a pole position had always eluded him until Saturday.

The French driver now holds the distinction of delivering France's first pole position at the Italian Grand Prix since Jean Alesi put a Benetton on top of the Monza grid in 1997. Nearly three decades had passed between that achievement and Gasly's. For a driver who has spent much of his career in the shadows of more celebrated teammates, this moment belongs entirely to him.

It also represents Alpine's first pole since the team rebranded from Renault — a milestone for a constructor that has operated largely without the headlines of Ferrari, Mercedes, or Red Bull in the hybrid era.

The Qualifying Context

The session produced a starting grid that will define the Italian Grand Prix narrative. Championship leader Kimi Antonelli qualified seventh fastest in pure pace but will start from the back of the grid after Mercedes elected to fit a complete new power unit — triggering engine grid penalties for exceeding maximum component allowances across multiple elements.

That decision creates a dramatic scenario for Sunday's race: Antonelli, holding a 59-point lead over Russell and Hamilton in the championship, must fight from last to score meaningful points, while Russell — starting second behind Gasly — has a clear opportunity to cut the gap.

McLaren's Piastri in third represents a complication for any team following an early-race strategy. And the two Ferraris in fourth and fifth mean Hamilton and Leclerc are well-positioned to challenge if the race develops favourably.

What's Next

The 2026 Italian Grand Prix race starts at 15:00 local time. Gasly's task is straightforward in theory — defend from pole and manage the race — but the pressures of leading a Formula 1 grand prix at a circuit this demanding make execution anything but simple. A maiden pole can become a maiden victory, or it can become a cautionary tale about pace versus racecraft. Either way, Pierre Gasly has already written his chapter in Monza history.
