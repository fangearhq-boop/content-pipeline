Verstappen Arrives at Qatar Chasing Fourth Consecutive Grand Prix Victory at Lusail

By Elena Voss | November 24, 2026 | F1 Fanrecap

Max Verstappen heads into the Qatar Grand Prix this weekend with a Las Vegas victory in his recent memory and a circuit record that is as clean as any in the modern Formula 1 era. He won the Qatar Grand Prix in 2023, in 2024, and again in 2025. If he wins at Lusail on Sunday, he will have taken four consecutive victories at the same venue — a streak that would underscore just how well-suited he is to the challenges the 5.419-kilometre circuit presents.

Verstappen sits second in the 2026 championship standings with 304 points, holding a 37-point advantage over Lewis Hamilton with two rounds remaining. The title went to Kimi Antonelli weeks ago, but the battle for second place is very much alive and Verstappen's form at Qatar has been one of the most reliable storylines in recent seasons.

What Makes Lusail Work for Verstappen

The Lusail International Circuit is a high-speed venue — sweeping corners, demanding commitment levels, and an abrasive surface that punishes both tyres and drivers who do not manage the balance carefully. Verstappen has consistently handled all of those challenges with precision.

His Qatar victories have come in different conditions and against different levels of competition. He has never appeared uncomfortable here, and his preparation for each of the three wins has followed a consistent pattern: solid qualifying, clean race management, and the ability to respond when other drivers attempt strategy-based challenges.

With Pirelli introducing a mandatory 27-lap tyre limit for the 2026 event — forcing at least two pit stops in the 57-lap race — the strategic element is more complex than in prior years. That should not necessarily disadvantage Verstappen, who has historically been effective at managing pit windows and coming through traffic, but it removes the option to run a conservative single-stop where he might have preferred that approach.

The Championship Picture

Verstappen's 37-point lead over Hamilton is comfortable in the context of two races. Even if Hamilton wins both remaining events and takes the fastest-lap bonus each time, he would need Verstappen to score poorly in both races to overturn the gap. The mathematical scenarios exist, but they require a combination of outcomes that has not been characteristic of this season.

What Qatar represents for Verstappen is an opportunity to extend the gap further and effectively close out second place before Abu Dhabi. If he wins and Hamilton finishes outside the top five at Lusail, the championship battle for the runner-up spot is over before the final weekend even arrives.

What's Next

Practice sessions begin at Lusail on Thursday, November 27, with qualifying scheduled for Saturday and the race on Sunday, November 29. Verstappen will be the driver to beat on a circuit where his record speaks for itself. Whether the Pirelli tyre limits and the strategy complexity they introduce can create an opening for his rivals remains to be seen — but history at this venue says backing him here is not a leap of faith.
