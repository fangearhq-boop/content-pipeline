Hamilton and Leclerc Carry Las Vegas Baggage into Qatar After Contact Costs Both Drivers

By Ryan Calloway | November 24, 2026 | F1 Fanrecap

The Las Vegas Grand Prix left two of Formula 1's most high-profile drivers with questions to answer. Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc made contact during the race, with Hamilton finishing seventh and Leclerc tenth — results that neither driver could use as championship currency heading into the final two rounds of the 2026 season.

Hamilton remains third in the standings on 267 points, 37 behind Max Verstappen. Leclerc is fifth on 250. With 50 points still available across Qatar and Abu Dhabi, both drivers retain mathematical hope, but the margins are tight and there is no room for a repeat of Las Vegas. Qatar comes first, beginning this Thursday at Lusail.

The Las Vegas Incident

Hamilton and Leclerc racing hard in the same part of the track is not unusual — they have been rivals across multiple seasons and multiple teams, and their wheel-to-wheel moments have produced some of the sport's more compelling racing. What Las Vegas provided, however, was an outcome that hurt both men.

Hamilton, who joined Ferrari at the start of 2026 and has been in search of a final championship statement in the closing chapters of his career, found himself in a point-scoring but far-from-ideal position after the contact. Leclerc, his teammate, was even further back.

The dynamics of two drivers on the same team making contact — regardless of blame — creates internal pressure that neither driver welcomes. Ferrari will want Qatar to be clean, cooperative where possible, and maximally productive for the team's standings across both championships.

What Qatar Means for Hamilton

Hamilton won the first Qatar Grand Prix in 2021. The venue has not always returned to the calendar consistently since — it was absent in some seasons and returned in others — but his history here is positive and the circuit's high-speed, flowing characteristics have historically produced performances he is comfortable with.

At 267 points, he needs Verstappen to have a difficult weekend to make serious inroads. A win in Qatar combined with Verstappen finishing outside the points would significantly alter the picture, but that combination has rarely materialised this season when it mattered most.

What is more achievable is a podium — perhaps a win — that keeps the final race at Abu Dhabi meaningful. Closing to within 25 points of Verstappen would mean the Abu Dhabi finale settles second place, which is at least a scenario that keeps everything alive.

What's Next

Both Hamilton and Leclerc will spend the days before Thursday's first practice session reviewing what happened in Las Vegas and preparing for Lusail. The circuit is demanding and the tyre compound regulations — Pirelli's mandatory 27-lap limit — will add strategic complexity. For two drivers who need a strong result, the first requirement at Qatar is simply to stay out of trouble and let the racing do the work.
