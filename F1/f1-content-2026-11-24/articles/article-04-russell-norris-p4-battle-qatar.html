Russell and Norris Perfectly Level at 264 Points as P4 Battle Enters Its Final Two Rounds

By Elena Voss | November 24, 2026 | F1 Fanrecap

The closest championship battle of the 2026 Formula 1 season has nothing to do with the title. George Russell and Lando Norris head into the Qatar Grand Prix with identical championship tallies — 264 points each — as the fight for fourth place in the standings enters its final two rounds. With 50 points remaining across Qatar and Abu Dhabi, the final position between them will be decided on the track.

They represent different teams — Russell drives for Mercedes, Norris for McLaren — and their cars have different characteristics and different areas of strength. At Lusail, both teams have shown the ability to compete at the front when conditions align. This weekend, those conditions will be shared by two drivers who cannot afford to watch the other pull away.

What P4 Means

Championship position in Formula 1 carries financial and reputational weight that extends beyond the driver's standing. Fourth place in the constructors' and drivers' standings affects prize money distributions, team budgets, and the year-end narrative that shapes contracts, sponsorships, and public perception heading into the following season.

For Norris, finishing fourth would represent a meaningful step in a 2026 season where his McLaren team has been competitive but not dominant in the way some pre-season projections suggested. For Russell, fourth would underscore his consistency in a year where his teammate took the title and his own contribution to Mercedes' success has been substantial even without the championship spotlight.

Qatar as the Separator

The likely outcome of two drivers entering the same event at the same points total is that Qatar separates them. How it separates them depends on what each team brings in terms of setup, how their strategies under Pirelli's mandatory two-stop format unfold, and whether either driver gets caught in the kind of incident that removes their result entirely.

Russell has recent form at Lusail from previous seasons that is positive. Norris is the qualifying force on the grid this year — his pole position total and one-lap performance have been outstanding — which gives McLaren the ability to position him for optimal race strategy.

Both drivers will be fully aware of what the other is doing during the race. That awareness is a factor in itself — where normal racing instincts might suggest patience, the championship picture may demand aggression. The challenge for both is knowing when each is appropriate.

What's Next

If Qatar separates them by even a small margin, Abu Dhabi becomes a genuine decider. If the gap is small enough — within a few points — the final race of the season at Yas Marina will carry that extra layer of championship stakes. At 264 each, the sport has set up a genuinely compelling subplot that runs alongside the main show of Verstappen protecting second from Hamilton. Two races, zero points between them. Something has to give.
