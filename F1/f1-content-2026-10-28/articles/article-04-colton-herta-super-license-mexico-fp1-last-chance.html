Colton Herta's F1 Dream Comes Down to One Practice Session in Mexico City

By Ryan Calloway

October 28, 2026

Colton Herta | Cadillac | Super License | Mexico City GP

Colton Herta's path to a 2027 Formula 1 race seat with Cadillac runs through one practice session on Friday morning in Mexico City. The American driver needs to complete at least 100 kilometers — 24 clean laps — in FP1 at Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez without incurring a penalty. Do that, and he earns the final super license point that makes him eligible to race in Formula 1. Fall short again, and his window may close entirely.

This is not a new story. Herta has been the most talked-about American driver in F1 circles for years, a genuine talent who has spent his career building toward the moment where his license paperwork matches his ability. Mexico City is the last realistic opportunity he has this season — and the weight of that reality will be with him from the moment he puts on his helmet Friday morning.

The Monza Setback

Herta was in exactly this position at the Italian Grand Prix in early September. The setup was identical: a mandatory rookie FP1 appearance, 100 kilometers required, a super license point waiting at the end of a clean session. He was on pace, completing laps without incident, when a hydraulic failure forced his car into retirement before the distance threshold was met.

One mechanical issue. One point lost. One dream delayed by two months. That is the ruthless arithmetic of the super license system, and Herta has lived it in its cruelest form.

Cadillac worked through September and October to ensure the hydraulic issue was fully resolved. The team has run Herta in multiple FP1 sessions across the 2026 season as part of a coordinated effort to accumulate the points he needs, and Mexico is the last item on that checklist.

What 100 Kilometers Means

The threshold sounds simple. 24 laps of the Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez — a circuit that runs 4.304 kilometers per lap — is not a physical challenge. Under normal conditions, with a reliable car and clean air, an experienced driver like Herta would clear the distance without drama.

But Mexico City adds complexity. The altitude stresses every component on the car differently than at sea level. Hydraulic systems, which failed Herta at Monza, operate in thin air that changes pressure dynamics throughout the session. Cadillac's engineers will be watching their telemetry with particular attention this Friday morning.

The FIA's rules also require that no penalty be issued during the session — not for technical infringements, not for track limits, not for anything. Herta must complete the distance cleanly.

What's Next

FP1 at Mexico City runs Friday morning local time. If Herta completes the 100-kilometer requirement without a penalty, the super license point is awarded, and Cadillac's 2027 driver lineup takes a significant step toward becoming official. If he falls short, Cadillac would need to explore alternative mechanisms — a process that would likely push any 2027 announcement past the end of the season. Every lap Friday matters.
