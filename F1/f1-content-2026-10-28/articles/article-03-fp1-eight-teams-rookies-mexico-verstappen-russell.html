Eight Teams Field Rookies in Mexico City FP1 — Verstappen and Russell Among Those Sitting Out

By Marcus Cole

October 28, 2026

F1 Practice | Mexico City GP | Rookie Drivers

The opening practice session at the Mexico City Grand Prix will look very different from a typical Friday morning. Eight of the 10 Formula 1 teams have chosen to field rookie drivers in FP1, fulfilling the mandatory young driver practice requirement and giving the next generation of talent their most concentrated track time of the season in a single session.

Among the notable changes: Max Verstappen will not drive his Red Bull in FP1, with Ayumu Iwasa taking his place. George Russell's Mercedes will be piloted by Frederik Vesti. Alpine will field Gabriele Mini in place of Franco Colapinto, and Cadillac will run Colton Herta — the American driver whose entire 2027 F1 plans may hinge on this session.

The Rookie Lineup

Ayumu Iwasa has been Red Bull's primary development driver and brings serious junior category credentials to the session. His time in the senior machinery will be closely watched by the team's hierarchy as they evaluate options for future race seats.

Frederik Vesti has been a fixture in Mercedes' development program and has already accumulated free practice experience across multiple circuits this season. Mexico City's altitude and layout present a different challenge than most European venues, making this one of the most data-rich practice sessions available.

Gabriele Mini's FP1 appearance at Mexico is Alpine's third rookie practice outing of the season — a sign of the team's commitment to preparing its next driver cohort. Colapinto, who gives up his seat for the session, has been one of Alpine's most consistent performers this year.

Colton Herta's inclusion at Cadillac carries the most individual stakes. Every other rookie in the session is gaining experience. Herta is racing for his F1 future.

Why FP1 Matters More in Mexico

At most circuits, FP1 is primarily a data-gathering exercise that has limited impact on race outcomes. Mexico City is different. The altitude creates conditions that teams can only fully simulate once they are on-site — wind tunnel and CFD work at sea level does not replicate 2,240 meters of thin air, and getting car setup data from real laps in the actual conditions is valuable for everyone, veterans included.

That reality makes the rookie FP1 decision especially interesting in Mexico. Teams are sacrificing setup data from their regular race drivers in a session that carries genuine strategic value. The tradeoff reflects how seriously teams take the young driver program requirement — and how much they trust their simulator preparation heading into a unique venue.

What's Next

FP1 at Mexico City takes place Friday morning. Regular race drivers return for FP2 in the afternoon and FP3 Saturday morning, with qualifying following Saturday. The race is Sunday, November 1. For Herta specifically, Friday morning's practice session is the most important hour of his professional career to date — the full story is covered separately.
