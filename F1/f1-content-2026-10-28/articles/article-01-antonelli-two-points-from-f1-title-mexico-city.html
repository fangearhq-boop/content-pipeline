Antonelli Needs Just Two Points to Win the 2026 F1 Title at Mexico City

By Ryan Calloway

October 28, 2026

F1 Championship | Kimi Antonelli | Mexico City GP

Kimi Antonelli arrives at the Mexico City Grand Prix carrying 365 championship points and a 123-point lead over George Russell — and he needs just two more points, or a No. 9 finish or better, to clinch the 2026 Formula 1 Drivers' Championship. With the race set for Sunday, November 1 at Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez, history could be made this weekend.

The permutations are straightforward. A ninth-place finish or higher locks up the title regardless of what Russell or Lewis Hamilton do. If Antonelli finishes 10th and neither Russell nor Hamilton wins the race, that is also enough. The math is not complicated — and after a season in which Antonelli has been consistently the fastest driver over a race distance, executing a top-nine finish should be within his reach.

A Season Built on Dominance

Antonelli's championship campaign has been remarkable. He won the United States Grand Prix at COTA last weekend with a drive that the season, leaving Leclerc second and Verstappen third. That victory, added to a consistent string of points finishes and race wins, has given him a cushion that makes the title feel like a formality — but Mexico City's unique challenges demand respect.

At 2,240 meters above sea level, the altitude at Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez strips aerodynamic downforce and reduces engine output. The track's layout, with its long straight into the hairpin and the iconic stadium section at the end of the lap, rewards different car characteristics than circuits like COTA. For Antonelli and Mercedes, the goal this weekend is simple: start cautiously, manage the race, and take the points that end the championship fight early.

Russell and Hamilton Still in the Hunt — For Second

Russell, on 242 points, and Hamilton cannot mathematically catch Antonelli unless he fails to finish at all — which would still require them to win with maximum points to keep the title alive past Mexico. Their race is about second place in the standings, not the championship, and both drivers will push hard knowing that a strong finish here sets up a stronger position for the remaining rounds.

Hamilton has been Antonelli's most compelling rival over the last five races, and their history as teammates at Mercedes adds an unmistakable layer of narrative to this weekend. Whether Hamilton can find something to deny Antonelli's coronation — even briefly — will be one of the subplot storylines to watch.

What's Next

FP1 at Mexico City begins Friday morning, with qualifying on Saturday afternoon and the race Sunday, November 1. The 2026 Formula 1 season has 19 rounds completed after this weekend, with Qatar and Abu Dhabi still to come — but if Antonelli executes as he has all year, none of those remaining races will carry championship significance. A No. 9 finish Sunday ends it all.
