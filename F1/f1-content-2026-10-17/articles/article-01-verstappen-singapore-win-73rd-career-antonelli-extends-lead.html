Verstappen Finally Wins Singapore: 73rd Career Victory as Antonelli Extends Championship Lead to 102 Points

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | October 17, 2026

Singapore GP | Max Verstappen | Kimi Antonelli | Race Result | F1 Championship 2026

Max Verstappen finally has his Singapore victory. He took pole position, led from the front, and crossed the line first at Marina Bay on Sunday — his 73rd Grand Prix win and his second of the 2026 season. It was also the first time in his career he had won at the Singapore street circuit.

Charles Leclerc finished 7.3 seconds back in second place, continuing his strong late-season form for Ferrari. Kimi Antonelli completed the podium in third. And George Russell — whose entire weekend became a story of damage limitation — managed to recover from a 21st-place starting position to ninth, taking 2 championship points from a race he urgently needed to have gone much better.

Verstappen's First Marina Bay Win

Of the 73 races Verstappen has now won, Singapore had been a conspicuous absence from his list. The Marina Bay street circuit, with its walls, its night-race atmosphere, and its tendency to reward patience over aggression, had not previously suited his approach. Sunday was different.

He qualified on pole, took the lead at the start, and managed it throughout 62 laps. The strategy called was executed cleanly. Leclerc pushed but could not close what grew into a comfortable margin by the end. Verstappen crossed the line first and added another circuit to a list that is growing with every season.

It was his second victory of 2026. He now sits fourth in the championship with 221 points — still mathematically involved, but 119 behind Antonelli.

The Championship Picture Sharpens

Antonelli did not win the race. He did not need to. His third-place finish, combined with Russell's ninth, extended his championship lead from 89 points going into Singapore to 102 afterward.

The mathematics of the 2026 title fight are stark. Antonelli has 340 points. Russell has 238. Six races remain; 150 points are still available. The gap is larger than a race win is worth. Even if Russell won the next six races and Antonelli finished second in all of them, he would still not overtake his teammate.

The championship belongs to Antonelli barring something extraordinary. The question is no longer whether he will win it — it is where and when.

Russell's Weekend of Damage Limitation

George Russell entered Singapore weekend needing a strong result to keep any title conversation alive. He left having had arguably the worst race weekend of his season.

The penalty came before a wheel had turned in anger: a fifth internal combustion engine, fifth turbocharger, fifth exhaust set, and fourth MGU-K pushed him 40 positions back on the starting grid. He would begin the grand prix from 21st place. Then in Saturday's Sprint race, he crashed out on lap 2 — pointless and with more statistical damage accumulating before Sunday.

His recovery to ninth from 21st on Sunday was genuinely skilled. It was simply not the kind of result a championship campaign survives on.

What's Next

The Formula 1 calendar moves to Austin, Texas, for the United States Grand Prix on October 23-25 at the Circuit of the Americas. Six rounds remain in the 2026 season. Kimi Antonelli arrives in Austin with a 102-point lead and the possibility of clinching the world championship at the race itself. The next story in one of the most remarkable title fights in recent years will be written at COTA next Sunday.
