Hamilton's Singapore Regret: A P3 Start, a Failed One-Stop, and an Apology to His Team

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | October 17, 2026

Lewis Hamilton | Singapore GP | Mercedes | F1 Championship 2026

Lewis Hamilton qualified third for the Singapore Grand Prix. He left Marina Bay in sixth place, apologizing to his team over the radio. In between those two moments was a one-stop strategy call that backfired, and another afternoon that underlined why 2026 has been a year of near-misses for the seven-time world champion.

The Race That Could Have Been

Starting third on the grid at Marina Bay is a strong position. The street circuit punishes overtaking attempts and rewards track position — a driver starting third has a genuine chance of a podium if things go to plan. Hamilton needed things to go to plan. They did not.

The strategy team called a one-stop approach, which required Hamilton's tires to last longer than they ultimately did under the pressures of Singapore's abrasive surface and technical layout. The degradation came too quickly, the timing of the stop left him exposed, and the positions he had built evaporated. He crossed the line in sixth place.

After the race, Hamilton spoke to his team. He told them he was sorry for how the weekend had gone. In the context of a championship year, a driver who qualifies third and finishes sixth has every reason to be disappointed.

Where Hamilton Stands

Hamilton is third in the 2026 Drivers' Championship with 229 points — behind both of his Mercedes teammates. Antonelli leads with 340. Russell is second with 238. Hamilton is 111 points from the lead with 150 available across six remaining races.

Mathematically, Hamilton is still in the championship. In practice, the title would require Antonelli to have four or five non-scoring results while Hamilton won everything, which is not a scenario grounded in the form either driver has shown in 2026.

What Singapore represented for Hamilton was not a fatal blow to a title charge — that had effectively already ended — but rather a missed opportunity to take points away from his rivals and record a strong result in his own right. Sixth place from third on the grid is a result he will not be satisfied with.

A Season in Context

Hamilton's 2026 season has been good without being dominant. He has shown pace in qualifying, delivered some strong race results, and demonstrated that even at his stage of career he can compete with the very best in the field. But the championship has not been his story this year. Antonelli's rise has been the story. Russell's near-miss has been the story. Hamilton has been a compelling subplot — a champion among champions, still fighting, still fast, but not quite at the right end of the results when it mattered most.

Austin gives him one of his last chances to change the narrative of this season's final chapters.

What's Next

Hamilton and Mercedes head to Austin for the United States Grand Prix on October 23-25. The Circuit of the Americas is a circuit Hamilton has won at before. A strong result — or a victory — would not change where he sits in the championship, but it would be a statement going into the final five rounds of the 2026 season.
