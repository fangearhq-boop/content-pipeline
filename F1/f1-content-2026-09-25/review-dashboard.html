Azerbaijan GP Qualifying Day — Verstappen Tops FP3, Grid Set for Saturday Race
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Friday in Baku brought FP3, qualifying, and the full picture of who is fast heading into Saturday's Azerbaijan Grand Prix. Max Verstappen ended George Russell's practice dominance — Russell had led both sessions on Thursday — by going fastest in FP3 with a 1m43.922s lap. Russell ended up second, 0.099s back. Lewis Hamilton was third, only 0.111s off Verstappen's time. Kimi Antonelli slotted in at P4, Charles Leclerc fifth. McLaren continue to struggle. Lando Norris (P8) and Oscar Piastri (P7) showed some improvement from their crash-disrupted Thursday sessions but remained well off the front of the field. Norris publicly ruled McLaren out of a pole position fight. Their deficit in the power-sensitive street circuit is a product of both the floor damage sustained Thursday and a more fundamental setup challenge on this layout. Qualifying ran at 4pm local time (1pm UK) — a day earlier than usual because the full race schedule was moved forward to Saturday to avoid Sunday's Remembrance Day observance in Azerbaijan. The grid will set the stage for what could be one of Baku's more unpredictable races: championship leader Antonelli expected at the front, Leclerc expected at the back with a fast engine, and Norris needing to recover whatever points are available. Race day: Saturday September 26. ---

Leclerc Confirmed in Engine Penalty — Starts Near Back in Baku
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Charles Leclerc will start near the back of Saturday's Azerbaijan Grand Prix grid. Ferrari has officially confirmed the engine penalty that's been expected since Leclerc's heavy crash at Parabolica on the second lap of the Italian Grand Prix on September 6. That incident destroyed the power unit Ferrari had just introduced under the ADUO2 scheme — their second upgrade of the season. Ferrari reverted to the previous specification for the Madrid Grand Prix while engineers assessed the damage. Now in Baku, they've made the decision: take the penalty here rather than later. The logic is simple. Baku's 2.2km main straight — the longest in F1 — and the Turn 1 heavy braking zone create more overtaking opportunities per lap than virtually any other circuit on the calendar. Team principal Fred Vasseur described Baku as "quite suitable" for absorbing a grid drop. He's right. The track's unique combination of high-speed straights and narrow technical sectors allows faster cars to recover positions quickly. Leclerc's FP3 time (P5) came with the new engine spec, which is reported to be around 0.2 seconds per lap faster. Starting from near the back, he'll have a considerable pace advantage over midfield machinery. The question isn't whether he'll move forward — it's how many positions he can gain, and whether the safety car lottery works in his favor. ---

McLaren's "Knife in the Heart" — Norris Rules Out Pole Fight as Deficit Holds
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Lando Norris did not mince words. Asked about McLaren's pace in Baku, he described the situation as "a knife in the heart" and added that they were "miles off" at a circuit that was supposed to suit their car better than Thursday's practice sessions suggested. McLaren's troubles began in FP1, when both cars sustained floor damage — Norris from bouncing over Baku's notorious surface bumps, Piastri from a T15 wall impact. Despite an overnight fix and a more comprehensive repair plan heading into Friday, the underlying delta remained. Norris ended FP3 in P8. Piastri was P7. Both are roughly where they expected to be: ahead of the midfield, well behind the front-runners. For the championship, the timing could not be worse. Norris sits 106 points behind Antonelli with six races remaining. A maximum of 120 points are available across Baku through Abu Dhabi. For Norris to win the championship, he essentially needs to outscore Antonelli by nearly 18 points per race — while Antonelli, starting at or near the front of every grid, has every structural advantage. Piastri, for his part, sees upside. He told media that the car isn't fundamentally broken — the deficit is circuit-specific — and that there are things the team can still improve for qualifying. We'll find out if that optimism was warranted when the grid drops for Saturday's race. ---

Antonelli Escapes FP1 Penalty — Engine Change Uses Existing Pool
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post The Thursday drama was real, but Friday brought relief for Kimi Antonelli and Mercedes. Antonelli's FP1 at Baku ended abruptly when a hydraulic failure forced him to stop at Turn 7 after just nine laps. The resulting engine change sparked concern — if Mercedes introduced a new power unit, Antonelli would face a grid penalty at the worst possible point in the championship. Instead, the team confirmed the replacement was drawn from Antonelli's existing engine pool. No new power unit means no FIA grid penalty. The change resolved the hydraulic issue without triggering any additional regulatory consequences. The yellow flag investigation from FP1 — when Antonelli was placed under scrutiny for his behavior near the marshaling flags following the incident — was also resolved without penalty. One penalty point remains on his Super License from an earlier 2026 season incident, but that doesn't affect his Baku starting position. Antonelli returned for FP3 and finished P4, 0.111s behind Verstappen. He enters qualifying as the championship leader (292 points, 81 ahead of teammate Russell) with clean regulatory status. ---

Championship Snapshot — Antonelli's 81-Point Cushion at Round 15 of 20
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Six races remain in the 2026 Formula 1 World Championship, and the standings entering Round 15 at Baku tell a clear story. Kimi Antonelli leads at 292 points. George Russell is second at 211 — 81 points behind. Lewis Hamilton sits third at 191 points. Lando Norris is fourth at 186. Mercedes leads the constructors' championship at 503 points. The title math is straightforward on paper, and it heavily favors Antonelli. With a maximum of 120 points available across the final six races (Baku, Singapore, USA, Mexico City, Brazil, Abu Dhabi), Norris's 106-point deficit is theoretically closeable only if he outscores Antonelli by an average of nearly 18 points per race — roughly the difference between winning and finishing fifth every round. Russell has a more realistic arithmetic case: at 81 points back with six rounds left, he needs to outscore Antonelli by roughly 14 points per race. In a world where both are starting near the front — as they typically have been at Mercedes — that kind of separation requires Antonelli retirements or mechanical failures. Baku's unpredictability — the safety car frequency, the wall risk, the engine stress of the longest straight in F1 — makes it one of the few remaining circuits where a big points swing is possible in either direction. Saturday's race won't decide the championship. But it could narrow the field in ways that matter. ---
