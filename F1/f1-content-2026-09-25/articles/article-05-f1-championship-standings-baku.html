F1 Championship Standings at Round 15: Antonelli's 81-Point Cushion, Six Races to Go

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | September 25, 2026

The 2026 Formula 1 World Championship heads into Round 15 at the Baku City Circuit with a clear leader and a shrinking window for the rest of the field. Kimi Antonelli sits atop the drivers' standings at 292 points — 81 clear of teammate George Russell in second, 101 ahead of Ferrari's Lewis Hamilton, and 106 ahead of McLaren's Lando Norris in fourth.

Mercedes leads the constructors' championship at 503 points, with Ferrari and McLaren behind at 358 and lower respectively.

The Title Math

Six rounds remain: Baku, Singapore, USA, Mexico City, Brazil, and Abu Dhabi. The maximum available in each is 25 points for a race win plus fastest lap — giving each driver a theoretical ceiling of approximately 150 remaining points, though the realistic maximum is closer to 120 given that no driver leads every race wire to wire.

For Lando Norris to win the championship, he must close 106 points on Antonelli across six rounds. That requires outscoring him by an average of nearly 18 points per race — roughly equivalent to winning while Antonelli finishes sixth or lower, consistently. Given Antonelli's pace this season and Mercedes's structural advantages at most circuits, that scenario requires a level of misfortune that hasn't materialized over the first 14 rounds.

Russell's case is more plausible in arithmetic terms. At 81 points behind with six races remaining, he needs to outscore Antonelli by roughly 14 points per race. They drive for the same team, typically receive similar strategic treatment, and generally qualify near each other. Russell winning while Antonelli finishes third would deliver roughly 7 points per race — not enough on its own, but a realistic building block.

The Baku Variable

The Azerbaijan Grand Prix is one of the championship's most chaotic events. Safety cars appear at nearly every Baku race. Wall impacts eliminate contenders. Mechanical failures on the brutally long straight take out cars that seemed certain to score. These unpredictable elements are, in theory, the best opportunity for Norris and Russell to swing the standings significantly in a single round.

Antonelli's challenge for Saturday is straightforward: start near the front, avoid the wall, take his points. He has the pace, the car, and the team infrastructure to execute that plan. A win at Baku would push his lead to over 100 points — one retirement and a race win away from a mathematical clinch.

What's Next
