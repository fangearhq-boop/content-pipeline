Ferrari Confirms Leclerc Engine Penalty: He Starts Near the Back in Baku — With the Fastest Power Unit

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | September 25, 2026

Ferrari has confirmed what the paddock anticipated for two weeks: Charles Leclerc will take an engine penalty at the 2026 Azerbaijan Grand Prix and start near the back of Saturday's grid. The decision follows Leclerc's retirement from the Italian Grand Prix on September 6, when a heavy crash on the second lap at Parabolica destroyed the power unit Ferrari had just introduced as part of their ADUO2 scheme.

Team principal Fred Vasseur described Baku as "quite suitable" for absorbing the grid drop — and the reasoning is hard to argue with.

Why Baku?

The Baku City Circuit features the longest straight in Formula 1 — 2.2 kilometers — followed by one of the sport's heaviest braking zones at Turn 1. This combination creates more overtaking opportunities per lap than virtually any other venue on the calendar. A driver starting at the rear with a pace advantage can carve through the field methodically in ways that would be impossible at a high-downforce circuit like Monaco or Hungary.

Ferrari had the option to hold the penalty for a later race, but Baku was identified as the best recovery circuit available in the remaining schedule. The team also had competitive business reasons to use the new, faster power unit now rather than continue with the older spec that Leclerc used at the Madrid Grand Prix.

The Engine's Advantage

The new unit that triggered the penalty was Ferrari's second ADUO upgrade. Data from FP2 on Thursday suggested it is approximately 0.2 seconds per lap faster than the previous spec. On a 21-lap circuit like Baku, that differential is meaningful — particularly when Leclerc will be passing backmarkers and midfield cars who are running the standard pace of their respective packages.

Leclerc finished P5 in FP3 with the new engine. He spent the session on qualifying simulation runs to understand the car's behavior and confirm that the unit is functioning properly before Saturday's race.

What's Next

Saturday's race starts from the grid set by Friday qualifying. Leclerc's exact starting position depends on how many other drivers also take penalties and where they fall, but near-last is the expected range. He will need a safety car period or two, clean passes on the long straight, and a strategy that keeps him out of unnecessary traffic. Whether the Baku lottery works in his favor is something no amount of preparation can guarantee — but Ferrari has done everything in their power to maximize the conditions for a recovery drive.
