Azerbaijan GP: Verstappen Ends Russell's Baku Practice Streak as Qualifying Day Arrives

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | September 25, 2026

George Russell had led every timed session at the Baku City Circuit until Friday morning. Max Verstappen ended that run in FP3, setting the fastest time with a 1m43.922s — 0.099 seconds ahead of Russell and 0.111 seconds ahead of Ferrari's Lewis Hamilton in third.

The session was Verstappen's first FP3 top position of the 2026 season. Kimi Antonelli finished fourth, Charles Leclerc fifth. For McLaren, Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri ended up eighth and seventh respectively — improved from their crash-disrupted Thursday but still well off the front.

The Schedule Shift

This Azerbaijan Grand Prix runs on an unusual timetable. The race is Saturday September 26, not Sunday — the schedule moved forward one day to avoid a clash with Azerbaijan's Remembrance Day. That means qualifying ran Friday at 4pm local time (1pm UK), and the grid set by those results determines Saturday's starting order.

The compressed timeline affects everything from tire strategy to pit window calculations. Teams that anticipated a Sunday race set their plans accordingly during the week; the one-day shift creates minor but real adjustments in how long tires need to last and when safety car periods statistically tend to fall.

The Pole Contenders

Heading into qualifying, three drivers held realistic pole ambitions: Russell, Antonelli, and Verstappen. All three were within 0.111s of each other in FP3. Ferrari's Hamilton was close too, though the team's attention was partly split toward Leclerc's back-of-grid situation.

Norris stated outright that McLaren couldn't compete for pole. Their Baku deficit — visible across all three practice sessions — is a combination of circuit characteristics (the long straight amplifies Mercedes's engine advantage) and the floor damage that disrupted their Thursday setup work.

What's Next

The grid from Friday qualifying determines Saturday's race start. Leclerc's engine penalty means he starts near the back regardless of qualifying speed, creating an unusual dynamic where Ferrari has two drivers at very different grid positions — Hamilton near the front, Leclerc near the rear. Watch for whether that shapes Ferrari's race strategy in a way that benefits one driver over the other.
