McLaren's Baku Reality Check: Norris Says "Miles Off" as Championship Window Narrows

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | September 25, 2026

Lando Norris did not sugarcoat McLaren's situation in Baku. After Friday's FP3, he told media the car was "miles off" the pace and described the feeling as "a knife in the heart." It was the kind of candor that underlines how badly McLaren needed this weekend to go differently.

Norris finished eighth in FP3. Oscar Piastri was seventh. Both results mark improvement from Thursday's crash-disrupted sessions — Norris had hit the wall in FP2, Piastri made similar contact at Turn 15 — but neither performance suggests McLaren is in a position to challenge the front of the grid.

The Problem Has a Name: Baku's Characteristics

The Baku City Circuit punishes cars that are slow on the main straight. Its 2.2km straight is where Mercedes's engine advantage becomes visible in the data, and it's also where cars with floor damage — as both McLarens sustained Thursday — lose time they cannot recover through mechanical grip or aerodynamic efficiency elsewhere on the track.

McLaren engineers implemented an overnight fix for the floor damage and had a more comprehensive solution planned for FP3. The improvement showed in their positions, but not enough. Norris estimated the team was around 1.5 seconds off pace-setting levels when discussing qualifying prospects — a gap that puts pole position in a different zip code.

The Championship Math Is Getting Uncomfortable

Norris sits 106 points behind championship leader Kimi Antonelli with six races remaining after Baku. The maximum available points across those six events — Baku, Singapore, USA, Mexico City, Brazil, and Abu Dhabi — represent 120 points. For Norris to win the championship, he needs to outscore Antonelli by nearly 18 points per race on average. That means winning while Antonelli finishes outside the top five, repeatedly.

Piastri told media he sees upside in the weekend and believes there are circuit-specific explanations for the deficit that don't reflect the car's fundamental performance level. That may be accurate. But for Norris, the score that matters is the championship one, and Baku may end as another round where he falls further behind rather than closing the gap.

What's Next

Qualifying Friday afternoon sets McLaren's grid position for Saturday's race. A strong qualifying result — even P5 or P6 — would help Norris salvage useful points from a difficult weekend. The race itself is one of the least predictable on the calendar, with safety cars frequent and wall incidents capable of reshuffling the order. McLaren's best hope for a strong result is probably chaos, not pace.
