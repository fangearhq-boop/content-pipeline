Norris Wins Hungary — Verstappen's Red Bull Exit Clause Is Now Officially Active

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | August 4, 2026

Lando Norris won the 2026 Hungarian Grand Prix from pole position, with Max Verstappen second and Kimi Antonelli third. It was the final race before the Formula 1 summer break, and while the podium celebration was McLaren's to enjoy, the most significant consequence of the afternoon at the Hungaroring belonged to someone else entirely.

Max Verstappen ends the first half of the 2026 season outside the top two in the Drivers' Championship. His contract with Red Bull contains a performance-based exit clause that activates if precisely that condition is met at the point of the summer break. The condition has been met. The clause is now officially active.

How Norris Won

The Hungarian Grand Prix produced its drama early. Norris started from pole but briefly lost the lead to teammate Oscar Piastri as the opening laps unfolded. McLaren's strategy was in competition with itself — a position of strange luxury for a team that had been outside the championship conversation for years before the 2026 regulation changes reset the competitive order.

Piastri's race ended with a retirement that handed Norris the lead back. From that point, he drove the McLaren home without incident, crossing the line ahead of Verstappen and Antonelli to claim his and McLaren's first victory of the season. The win was well-earned. It also happened to trigger the most consequential contractual clause in recent Formula 1 history.

What the Exit Clause Means

Verstappen's contract includes a specific performance threshold clause that was reportedly inserted due to his concerns about the new 2026 technical regulations. The clause gives him the right to leave Red Bull if he is not within the top two in the championship standings at the time of the summer break — a provision that was always understood to be a real possibility given the upheaval the new rules have brought.

He is not in the top two. The clause is active. The October deadline, when he can formally exercise it, is approximately two months away.

Red Bull did not stand still. The team reportedly offered Verstappen €8 million to buy out the clause — to make it financially worthwhile for him to remain committed to Red Bull through the end of 2026 and whatever comes next. Verstappen refused the offer.

McLaren have emerged as the most prominently linked destination. Zak Brown denied specific interest in a way that Formula 1 veterans have learned to decode — he noted that Verstappen would be good for the team "if one of my drivers slips on a banana peel," which is not quite a denial. Neither Norris nor Piastri has slipped on anything. But the door is cracked, and Verstappen's refusal to commit to Red Bull suggests he believes there is something worth waiting for.

What Red Bull Needs to Figure Out

Red Bull's situation heading into the summer break is one of the most uncomfortable in any team's recent history. They are the four-time consecutive constructors' champions of the most recent dynasty, and they have zero victories in the 2026 season. They entered the year as the dominant power. They leave the first half of it having been described, by their own sources, as effectively a fourth-tier team in the new regulation framework.

The combination of a new power unit architecture that has caught multiple teams off balance and chassis changes that have altered the competitive landscape has fallen particularly hard on Red Bull. Verstappen's contract clause was, in retrospect, a hedge against exactly this scenario. He was not wrong to insist on it.

Whether he ultimately triggers it depends on what opportunities are available and whether he believes the second half of the season — starting at Zandvoort on August 21-23 — might produce a Red Bull revival. Those are complex calculations for a driver whose default setting is to drive the fastest car available.

What's Next

The summer break runs until the Dutch Grand Prix at Zandvoort on August 21-23 — a race that will see Verstappen face his home crowd with his Red Bull future officially unresolved. The October deadline for exercising the clause gives him the first three rounds of the second half to evaluate: Zandvoort, Monza, and Madrid. If Red Bull improves, he stays. If the gap to McLaren and Mercedes remains what it was through Hungary, the clause gets triggered and a seismic driver move reshapes the 2027 grid. Few sub-plots in Formula 1 have carried this much weight in years.
