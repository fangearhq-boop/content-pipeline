The Last Race at Zandvoort — Why the 2026 Dutch GP Is Bigger Than Any Race Weekend in Years

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | August 4, 2026

Some race weekends are just race weekends. August 21-23 at the MASCOT Circuit Zandvoort is not one of those.

The Dutch Grand Prix — Round 12 of the 2026 Formula 1 World Championship — will be the last Formula 1 race ever held at Zandvoort. The circuit that originally hosted the Dutch Grand Prix from the mid-20th century through 1985, then returned triumphantly to the calendar in 2021, is leaving Formula 1 after this season. What was built as a modern facility to welcome back a historic race will close its F1 chapter with a weekend that carries more narrative weight than almost any race in recent memory.

The Sprint Debut

The 2026 Dutch Grand Prix will be Zandvoort's first-ever Sprint weekend — and its last. Sprint Qualifying runs Friday. The Sprint itself takes place Saturday morning. Regular grid qualifying follows on Saturday afternoon. The Grand Prix fills Sunday, August 23.

The Sprint format adds a second competitive session to the weekend, with its own points allocation and its own set of strategic decisions. At Zandvoort — a circuit where track position is valuable and overtaking requires patience — the Sprint will be a different kind of challenge from the race itself. Teams must manage their tire allocation across both sessions without the usual testing freedoms of a conventional Friday practice.

For a track saying goodbye to Formula 1, the Sprint adds another layer to an already loaded weekend.

Verstappen's Homecoming

No driver carries more weight into Zandvoort than Max Verstappen. The Dutch Grand Prix returned to the calendar in 2021 in no small part because of him — because Orange Army fan demand, amplified by his championship success, made the business case for reviving the circuit irresistible to Formula 1 promoters.

He has won at Zandvoort three times since the circuit's return. The crowd that fills every available space in the grandstands — and sometimes the surrounding dunes — is there as much for him as for the racing.

This year, Verstappen arrives with his Red Bull future completely unresolved. His performance-based exit clause is officially active. He refused an €8 million buyout. The October deadline to exercise the clause falls after three more races. McLaren is the most prominently linked destination but nothing is confirmed. The Dutch crowd, who have chanted his name at every corner for four years, will be watching him race under a cloud of contractual uncertainty that he has not addressed publicly in specific terms.

Whether Verstappen uses the Zandvoort weekend to announce anything — a decision, a direction, even a timeline — is the subtext beneath every lap he runs from August 21 onward.

The Championship Stakes

Kimi Antonelli enters the Dutch GP 50 points ahead of Lewis Hamilton in the Drivers' Championship. Zandvoort is not a track historically associated with either driver — Antonelli is in his first full season, and Hamilton's Zandvoort record across the circuit's recent years has been competitive without being defining.

The first race of the second half is where the narrative of a championship chase either opens up or narrows. If Antonelli takes pole, wins, and extends his lead to 75 points, the championship effectively becomes his to lose. If Hamilton gets through the weekend without penalties and takes a victory, the deficit is suddenly manageable in a way the first half of the season has not made possible.

Every point from Round 12 onward carries roughly twice the urgency it did in April, because there are half as many left to claim.

What's Next

The summer break ends August 21. Zandvoort opens its gates for the final time as an F1 venue, under lights and Orange flags, with a Sprint weekend format it has never hosted before, featuring a driver market's most compelling storyline — Verstappen versus Red Bull versus McLaren — playing out in front of the crowd that loves him most. After Round 12, the circuit goes quiet. Formula 1 moves on to Monza, Madrid, and the remainder of a season that has already produced more compelling storylines than most years manage across a full calendar. Zandvoort deserves a send-off worthy of its history. The sport is delivering exactly the circumstances for one.
