FIA Admits Leclerc Should Have Been Penalized at Spa — But the Call Stands

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | August 4, 2026

The FIA stewards have acknowledged that they got it wrong at the Belgian Grand Prix.

At Spa-Francorchamps, Charles Leclerc squeezed Oscar Piastri into Les Combes. Contact was made. Piastri's McLaren sustained floor damage — the kind of aerodynamic compromise that affects downforce, stability, and lap time for the rest of a race. The stewards reviewed the incident at the time and took no action against Leclerc.

They have since stated publicly that they would "judge the incident differently now" — an unusually candid admission from a stewarding body that rarely comments on decisions after the fact. Per reporting from GPFans, the stewards acknowledged that Leclerc should have received a 5-second time penalty.

No retroactive action has been taken. The race result stands as it was. Ferrari's driver came out of Spa unpunished, and the stewards themselves have now confirmed that was an error.

What Happened at Les Combes

Les Combes is one of the defining corners at Spa — a chicane complex after the long Kemmel Straight where cars arrive at high speed and drivers must decide quickly who has the right to the track. On this occasion, Leclerc was on the outside and Piastri was on the inside. Leclerc did not give Piastri enough room. The contact that followed was the consequence.

In Formula 1, the protocols around "squeezing" another car — pushing a rival toward the edge of the track or into contact — have evolved significantly over the years. The general principle is that the car ahead or the car with track position cannot simply close the door on a car that has established a legitimate racing line. Whether Piastri had sufficiently established his position is a question the stewards, at Spa, decided in Leclerc's favor. They have since indicated that judgment was incorrect.

Why the Admission Matters

The FIA does not often acknowledge mistakes in real time or retrospectively. The administration of Formula 1 officiating has historically operated in a way that treats each decision as final once the protest period has closed. That the stewards would comment on the Spa decision in a way that amounts to "we were wrong" is notable, regardless of what consequences follow.

It matters for the sport's broader credibility question: if the stewards can admit a mistake but are unwilling or unable to take any corrective action, what is the purpose of the admission? The answer — transparency, the acknowledgment that the system is fallible, perhaps an implicit signal to be more consistent in similar situations going forward — is reasonable. But it does not repair the lap time Piastri lost to floor damage, or the points that may have moved between him and Leclerc as a result of an incident that the officiating body now considers a penalizable offense.

The Broader Context

The 2026 season has already been marked by controversy around stewarding consistency. Hamilton's 4 penalties in 3 race weekends — including the Monaco pit lane speed limit call at 0.1 km/h over the limit — have raised questions about proportionality on one end of the spectrum. The Leclerc-Piastri non-penalty at Spa, now acknowledged as an error, raises questions about under-enforcement on another.

Consistency is the quality that the racing community asks for above almost anything else. Drivers, teams, and fans can accept calls that go against them if they are applied evenly and predictably. When the record shows Hamilton penalized for a near-undetectable speed transgression and Leclerc unpunished for clear contact damage in the same season, the consistency argument becomes harder to sustain.

McLaren, watching their driver absorb floor damage without a remedy, have their own view of how the 2026 stewards' room has handled the season.

What's Next

No appeals or retroactive processes are expected to change the Spa result. The FIA's comment closes the matter officially, even as it opens the debate about how stewards approach similar incidents in the second half of the season. The remaining 11 rounds will bring more close racing, more borderline contact situations, and more moments where officiating decisions affect the championship. The stewards enter the second half of the year having publicly set a precedent — Leclerc-Piastri-type incidents at Les Combes, going forward, should receive a penalty. Drivers, teams, and fans will be watching closely to see if the lesson has been learned.
