Verstappen Eyes a Fourth Straight COTA Win — History, Bumps, and Spoiler Duty

By Ryan Calloway | October 23, 2026

Max Verstappen won the United States Grand Prix at the Circuit of the Americas in 2021. He won it again in 2022. He won it a third consecutive time in 2023. No driver in the modern era has won four straight races at COTA, and Verstappen arrives in Austin this week with every reason to try to become the first.

His championship situation is settled. At 221 points, Verstappen trails Kimi Antonelli by 119 with 150 remaining — mathematically alive in the most technical sense, practically out of contention unless the season collapses in a way it has shown no signs of doing. What remains for Verstappen is to race. And racing at COTA, for Verstappen, has historically meant winning.

What Makes COTA Verstappen's Track

The Circuit of the Americas was designed with wide reference corners, high-speed sweeps, and a punishing first-sector complex that rewards drivers willing to commit early. Verstappen's aggressive braking style and his ability to rotate the car quickly through medium-speed corners — Turn 1 downhill, the Turns 3-5 sequence, the long Turns 16-18 hairpin complex — have all been strengths at COTA over the years.

His lap record of 1:36.169 at the circuit stands as evidence that the combination of Verstappen's approach and his Red Bull machinery has been as efficient here as anywhere on the calendar. The 2026 regulations have changed the cars significantly — active aero replaces DRS, the power unit architecture is different — but Verstappen's instincts for this circuit have not changed.

The Bump Problem

Not everything at COTA is settled. Verstappen has voiced concerns publicly about the circuit's surface quality. Despite the resurfacing of Turns 9-11 ahead of this event, much of COTA's layout remains bumpy by modern circuit standards — a characteristic that has drawn complaints from drivers and teams across multiple visits.

How those bumps interact with 2026's new active aerodynamic systems remains to be seen. The previous generation of DRS cars had specific sensitivity patterns under floor loading changes; the 2026 ground-effect and active aero cars may respond differently to the same imperfections. Red Bull will spend significant time in Friday's practice sessions mapping the surface and adjusting the suspension setup accordingly.

If the bumps cause handling instability that Red Bull cannot fully tune out, Verstappen's advantage on this circuit narrows. If the team solves it quickly — as they typically do — he will likely be fighting for pole position on Saturday.

The Spoiler Role

The most interesting dimension of Verstappen's weekend at COTA is the one he has no direct control over: if he wins, Antonelli cannot clinch the championship. A Verstappen victory pushes the title fight to Qatar, regardless of how Russell or Hamilton finish.

That makes Verstappen the single most powerful factor in the championship mathematics without having any personal stake in the outcome. He is not trying to prevent Antonelli from winning the title — he is simply racing. But the effect of his performance lands directly in the championship battle between his competitors.

For Russell and Hamilton, a Verstappen pole position on Saturday is genuinely good news. For Antonelli, starting Sunday knowing Verstappen is directly ahead presents a problem that team strategy alone cannot solve.

What's Next

Verstappen begins his COTA weekend in FP1 today at 12:30 PM CT, with FP2 following at 4:00 PM CT. Saturday brings FP3 and qualifying, where his record at this circuit makes him one of the serious contenders for the front row. The race is Sunday at 3:00 PM CT. A fourth consecutive COTA win would be one of the defining records of his era — and this weekend, it would also be the result that keeps the world championship alive for at least two more races.
