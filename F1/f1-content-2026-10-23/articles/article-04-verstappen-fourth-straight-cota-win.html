Verstappen Eyes a Fourth Straight COTA Win: History, Bumps, and Spoiler Duty

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | October 23, 2026

Max Verstappen has arrived at the Circuit of the Americas in 2024, 2022, and 2021, and each time he has left with the winner's trophy. The United States Grand Prix at COTA has become something close to a personal territory for the Dutch driver — a venue where his car setup instincts, his bravery through the bumpy first sector, and his late-race management have combined to produce results that other drivers have consistently failed to match.

This year, Verstappen arrives at COTA for a different kind of race. At 221 points, he is 119 behind Antonelli and mathematically out of the drivers' championship. What he can do, however, is win. And a Verstappen win on Sunday prevents Antonelli from clinching the title in Austin.

The COTA Record

Verstappen's run at the Circuit of the Americas began in 2021, when he took the victory in a closely-fought race that also featured Hamilton. In 2022, he dominated from pole position. In 2023, despite challenges, he converted again. Three consecutive wins at the same circuit places him among the most dominant drivers in COTA's history.

He also holds the circuit's outright lap record at 1:36.169 — a mark set under previous regulations that gives some sense of the raw pace he has brought to Austin in past years. The 2026 aerodynamic regulations will produce different lap times, but Verstappen's reading of the circuit is unlikely to have diminished.

The Bumps and the New Regulations

One factor that Verstappen has raised publicly ahead of this weekend is the condition of the circuit's surface. COTA is famously bumpy through the opening sector — a feature that has tested drivers and setup engineers since the circuit opened. Verstappen has indicated that the bumps remain a significant factor in how Red Bull approaches setup for the race.

The challenge is more complex in 2026 than in previous years. Turns 9–11 have been resurfaced, creating a new grip and degradation profile in that section that no team has raced on under current regulations. Additionally, the replacement of DRS with the active aero Overtake Mode means that setup decisions affecting aerodynamic efficiency across COTA's various corner speeds carry different downstream consequences than they did under the old system.

Verstappen and the Red Bull engineers have navigated novel circuit conditions before. They will need to read the resurfaced section quickly in practice to have any chance of maximizing the setup window before Saturday qualifying.

The Spoiler Role

For all the historical context and personal achievement attached to a fourth consecutive COTA win, there is also a simpler motivation this weekend: Verstappen can change the story of the championship by winning.

If he crosses the line first on Sunday, Antonelli cannot clinch the title in Austin regardless of where Russell or Hamilton finish. The fight moves to Qatar. The cameras that were pointed at the Mercedes garage for the coronation point elsewhere. And Verstappen, who has competed at the highest level of the sport for years and has never been content simply to finish second, gets another moment on the top step at a circuit that has become his own.

That is not a small thing, even at the tail end of a championship he cannot win.

What's Next

Friday practice at COTA runs in two sessions: FP1 at 12:30 PM CT, FP2 at 4:00 PM CT. Qualifying is Saturday, with the race at 3:00 PM CT on Sunday. If Verstappen wants a fourth straight COTA win, the work begins today — reading the bumps, finding the setup, and preparing to do in 2026 what he has done in each of the last three years at this circuit.

No one at COTA this weekend has a better record of doing exactly that.
