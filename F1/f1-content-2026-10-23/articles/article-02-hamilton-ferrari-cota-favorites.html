Hamilton and Ferrari Arrive as COTA Favorites: Friday Practice Will Reveal Everything

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | October 23, 2026

The question Formula 1 has been circling for weeks arrives at its sharpest point this Friday afternoon in Austin, Texas: does Lewis Hamilton and Ferrari have the pace to slow Kimi Antonelli's march to the championship?

According to pre-race analysis and Ferrari's recent form, the answer is a qualified yes. Whether that pace materializes into a result that actually delays the coronation is what Friday's two practice sessions at the Circuit of the Americas will begin to reveal.

Hamilton at COTA

Lewis Hamilton has a record at the Circuit of the Americas that demands respect. He has won here multiple times across his career, turning the Texas circuit into something close to a personal stronghold. COTA rewards the qualities Hamilton brings to a race weekend — attention to detail in setup, tire management through the long second sector, and the kind of late-race composure that makes up for positions lost in the opening laps.

His first year at Ferrari has been remarkable for a different reason: Hamilton has proven that the transition from Mercedes to Maranello has not diminished his pace. At 229 points in the championship, he remains mathematically alive — dependent on substantial chaos — but he has given Ferrari the kind of sustained performance the team needed to mount a serious constructors' campaign.

Ferrari's Position in the Fight

In the Constructors' Championship, Ferrari sit second at 444 points, trailing Mercedes at 578. The deficit is significant but the gap has been closing. Ferrari's car has shown strong qualifying pace in recent rounds, and their race day execution has improved markedly across the second half of the season.

At COTA specifically, Ferrari arrive with the expectation — and some external analysis — that the circuit's characteristics suit their car. The smooth sections through the stadium complex, the traction requirements out of the hairpin, and the high-speed sweeps of the back half of the circuit all align with Ferrari's aerodynamic philosophy under the 2026 regulations.

If that translates to a front-row qualifying position on Saturday, the United States Grand Prix becomes a genuine three-car fight at the front — exactly the scenario that could prevent Antonelli from clinching on Sunday.

The Championship Angle

Hamilton's role this weekend is complicated. He is, on paper, competing for himself and for Ferrari's constructors' fight. But the outcome of his race also directly determines whether Antonelli can clinch the drivers' title in Austin.

For Antonelli to clinch on Sunday, Hamilton must finish fifth or lower. If Hamilton finishes fourth or higher, the championship cannot be decided at COTA — regardless of what Antonelli does. That makes Hamilton, in some ways, the most pivotal driver of the entire weekend, even if his own title chances are slim.

It is the kind of scenario Formula 1 produces uniquely: a seven-time world champion who can shape history on Sunday simply by driving well.

What's Next

FP1 runs at 12:30 PM CT on Friday, followed by FP2 at 4:00 PM CT. The sessions will establish the baseline pecking order and give Ferrari their first look at race-pace tire behavior on the resurfaced Turns 9-11 section of COTA. Under the 2026 active aero regulations — no DRS, replaced by Overtake Mode — long-run pace and tire preservation matter more than one-lap time attack.

By Friday evening, the shape of the United States Grand Prix will be much clearer. Whether Hamilton and Ferrari are genuinely in the fight, or whether Antonelli's coronation arrives on schedule, the practice sessions will tell.
