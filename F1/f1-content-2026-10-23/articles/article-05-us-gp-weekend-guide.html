US Grand Prix Weekend Guide — Schedule, Stakes, and What to Watch at COTA

By Marcus Cole | October 23, 2026

The United States Grand Prix weekend is here. Formula 1 has come to the Circuit of the Americas in Austin, Texas, and the stakes are as high as they have been all season. A world championship could be decided on Sunday afternoon. The circuit's most dominant recent driver is gunning for a historic fourth consecutive victory. And one of the sport's most celebrated champions arrives in a red car that has suddenly become the car to beat.

Here is everything you need to follow the 2026 US Grand Prix from practice through the checkered flag.

Championship Standings

Kimi Antonelli (Mercedes): 340 points
George Russell (Mercedes): 238 points
Lewis Hamilton (Ferrari): 229 points
Max Verstappen (Red Bull): 221 points

Points remaining in the season: 150 (United States GP, Qatar, Abu Dhabi).

Antonelli can clinch Sunday if he wins the race, Russell finishes ninth or lower, and Hamilton finishes fifth or lower. If that happens, he becomes the youngest world champion in Formula 1 history at 20 years and 61 days old — three years younger than Sebastian Vettel's 2010 record.

The Key Storylines

Antonelli's Coronation: Can the championship leader take the title in Austin? Friday's practice sessions begin to answer the question. If he goes quickest and carries that pace to Sunday, the pressure on those around him becomes immense.

Hamilton and Ferrari: Lewis Hamilton arrives as one of the favorites at a circuit where he has performed well throughout his career. Ferrari has shown strong pace recently. Today's practice sessions will reveal whether that pace translates to the COTA layout and the resurfaced Turns 9-11 section.

Russell's Last Stand: George Russell needs to win. There is no points accumulation strategy that stops Antonelli at this point — only victories. Front-row qualifying on Saturday is effectively mandatory for Russell's race plan.

Verstappen's COTA Record: Three consecutive US GP victories. Verstappen holds the circuit lap record and has been dominant at COTA since it returned to the calendar under its current format. A fourth win would be historic — and would guarantee the championship fight continues beyond this weekend.

What the 2026 Rules Change at COTA

This is the first COTA event under the 2026 aerodynamic regulations. The elimination of DRS and its replacement with active aero — drivers deploy "Overtake Mode" for increased straight-line speed — changes the overtaking picture significantly. Under the old DRS system, COTA's back straight was a reliable passing zone. In 2026, wheel-to-wheel battles may look different.

The Turns 9-11 section has been resurfaced ahead of this event. New tarmac means different grip characteristics and unknown degradation rates — information that teams will be gathering urgently in both FP1 and FP2 today.

One Extra Story to Watch

American driver Colton Herta will get behind the wheel of the Cadillac car in Friday's opening practice session. With the US Grand Prix at home in Austin, Texas, Herta's FP1 appearance gives American fans a domestic storyline to follow alongside the championship drama at the front of the field.

What's Next

FP1 starts today at 12:30 PM CT. Every piece of information from both practice sessions sets the table for Saturday qualifying, which will shape the race grid and, potentially, the championship itself. By Sunday afternoon at COTA, Formula 1 may have its 2026 world champion — or it may have three more weeks of racing left to decide.
