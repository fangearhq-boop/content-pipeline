- **Posting window:** 10:00 AM ET
- **Type:** Long-form feature

**Long-Form Post:**
Here is a number that tells the 2026 Formula 1 story better than anything else.

Mercedes' combined Constructors' Championship total: 578 points.
Kimi Antonelli's Drivers' Championship total: 340 points.
George Russell's Drivers' Championship total: 238 points.
Antonelli + Russell: 578 points.

The numbers match exactly because Antonelli and Russell have driven virtually every point Mercedes has scored this season. It is a complete team performance — two world-class drivers at the top of the standings, a constructors' lead of 134 points over Ferrari, and a technical package that has delivered wins at some of the most demanding circuits on the calendar.

The last time a Mercedes lineup went 1-2 in the Drivers' Championship was in 2016 — a year that ended in Nico Rosberg's dramatic title win over Lewis Hamilton and Rosberg's immediate retirement. That year had tension and controversy in equal measure. This one has been more settled at the top: Antonelli has built his lead methodically, while Russell has been competitive but plagued by bad luck.

Six races remain. The championship is not technically over — but Mercedes leads both titles, has the fastest car most weekends, and has two drivers capable of winning any race. It is beginning to look like their finest season since that turbulent 2016 campaign.

### STORY 5: COTA Preview

- **Posting window:** 5:00 PM ET
- **Type:** Long-form preview

**Long-Form Post:**
Ten days from today, Formula 1 arrives in Austin, Texas for the United States Grand Prix — and the circuit, the timing, and the championship storyline all conspire to make it one of the essential races of the 2026 season.

The Circuit of the Americas is a 5.513-kilometer track built in 2012 that blends elements from the best circuits in the world. The Turn 1 blind summit — where cars crest a hill and dive left at over 180 mph — has produced some of the most memorable passing moves in F1's American history. The back section mixes slow chicanes with high-speed sweeps. Getting the setup right requires compromise, and not every car or driver philosophy works the same way here.

Verstappen won the 2025 United States Grand Prix, with Norris second and Leclerc third. He arrives in 2026 fresh off his Singapore victory — arguably the best form he's shown all year. COTA has favored Red Bull's car in recent seasons, but the 2026 technical regulations have reshuffled the grid significantly.

For Antonelli, the calculus is simple: score points, keep the lead, get closer to a clinch. His Mercedes is generally one of the strongest packages at power and high-speed circuits. COTA's mix of corners should suit it.

For Russell, it is a must-attack weekend. He won't say it publicly, but he needs a win. Everything else is arithmetic that doesn't work in his favor.

There is no sprint at Austin in 2026. Just 68 laps, a Sunday afternoon race start, and a championship that could get significantly closer to being decided. Races start at 3:00 PM local time (8:00 PM GMT) on Sunday, October 25.
