Russell Is 102 Points Down — Austin Is His Last Realistic Hope

By Ryan Calloway | October 13, 2026 | F1 Fanrecap

George Russell arrived in Singapore among the fastest men on the circuit. He was quickest in practice. He was leading the sprint race on Saturday when the rain came and the chaos followed. On Sunday, after a 40-place grid penalty dropped him to the back of the field, he put in one of the more quietly impressive drives of his season — recovering from 21st to ninth in a grand prix won by Max Verstappen.

He earned two championship points. His teammate earned 15. The gap after Singapore: 102 points.

The Brutal Arithmetic

There are approximately six races left in the 2026 Formula 1 season. Each race offers a maximum of 26 points for a winning, fastest-lap-scoring driver. The total available to Russell over the remaining rounds is approximately 156. To beat Antonelli, he needs to outscore him by 102 of those points. That means Russell needs to win essentially every remaining race while Antonelli scores very little.

The combination of those events happening in sequence over six races is historically rare. Antonelli has been consistent — his reliability issues have been minimal, his pace has been strong, his error rate has been low. The 2026 season has not been kind to Russell when mechanical problems arise: a Bahrain engine failure and a Singapore engine penalty speak to the pressures of running at the absolute limit all season long.

What Russell Has Shown

It would be a disservice to Russell's season to focus only on the gap. He won the Azerbaijan Grand Prix in dominant fashion. He has been competitive at nearly every circuit. His Singapore recovery drive — from 21st to ninth through a field that included the fastest drivers on the planet — is the kind of performance that reminds you why he is a Mercedes No. 1 caliber driver.

The problem is not pace. The problem is accumulation. Every DNF, every grid penalty, every point not scored in a session where Antonelli scores 25 has built this gap over months of racing. It did not happen in one weekend. It happened one miscalculation at a time.

What's Next

Austin is next: the United States Grand Prix, October 23-25, at Circuit of the Americas. It is, functionally, Russell's must-win race. If he cannot close the gap in Texas, the championship conversation becomes almost academic. He is competitive at COTA — the circuit suits his driving style and the Mercedes package — but so does it suit Antonelli, and Verstappen won there in 2025. Russell has 10 days to prepare, reset, and approach Austin as the race that defines his season.
