Verstappen Breaks the Singapore Curse: 73rd Career Win on His 250th Race Weekend

By Ryan Calloway | October 13, 2026 | F1 Fanrecap

Every great driver carries a circuit they haven't cracked. For Max Verstappen, it was Marina Bay. The Singapore Grand Prix had eluded him through his dominant championship years, through Red Bull's golden era, through every upgrade cycle and strategy attempt. Until Sunday, October 11, 2026, when Verstappen turned everything on its head.

He won from pole. He led every lap. He crossed the line 7.3 seconds ahead of Charles Leclerc's Ferrari. And he did it on his 250th Formula 1 race weekend, adding his 73rd career victory to a list that already places him in the conversation for the greatest driver the sport has ever seen.

A Perfect Singapore Sweep

The weekend started Saturday with the sprint race — a format that Verstappen turned into a personal showcase. He qualified on pole for the sprint, led from the start, and won after a chaotic race that saw eight retirements, including George Russell crashing out while leading on the second lap. Verstappen stayed clean through the carnage.

Sunday's grand prix was less dramatic, but no less commanding. Leclerc pushed hard in the final laps on fresh soft tyres, attempting to reduce the gap through the Marina Bay night. Verstappen responded — not panicking, not making errors — and held him to 7.3 seconds at the chequered flag. Kimi Antonelli completed a tightly contested top three from third.

The Weight of 73 Wins

Verstappen is in his early-to-mid 20s. He has 73 wins. The pace at which he has accumulated victories — through three consecutive championship seasons and now into this more competitive era under new technical regulations — puts him on a trajectory that few drivers have matched in the sport's history.

The 250th race weekend marker landed with quiet significance. In F1, the accumulated mileage of a career is often the measure no one talks about until the number becomes extraordinary. For Verstappen, who made his debut as a teenager, the milestone arrived at precisely the right moment: the race that had always been missing from his collection.

Where He Stands

In the championship, Verstappen sits fourth on 221 points — 119 behind leader Antonelli. The title race is over for him in 2026. But the wins are not. He arrives at Austin's Circuit of the Americas in ten days as the defending US Grand Prix champion, fresh off what may be the most complete weekend of his season. The championship belongs to Antonelli. The race victories belong to whoever is fastest. Verstappen made his claim clear on Sunday.

What's Next

The United States Grand Prix runs October 23-25 at Circuit of the Americas in Austin, Texas. Verstappen won there in 2025 and arrives in 2026 as one of the frontrunners. His Singapore victory has reset the narrative heading into F1's American swing — and there are still six races to add to a win total that keeps growing.
