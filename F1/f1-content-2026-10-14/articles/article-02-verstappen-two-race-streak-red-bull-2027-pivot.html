Verstappen Has Won Two in a Row — and Red Bull Is Already Building Next Year's Car

By Marcus Cole | October 14, 2026 | F1 Fanrecap

In Malaysia, Max Verstappen won. In Singapore — on his 250th Grand Prix weekend, at a circuit where he had never previously won in nine attempts — he won again. Two consecutive Formula 1 victories for the four-time world champion, closing the gap on championship leader Kimi Antonelli from 145 points to 119.

His team, according to multiple paddock reports, is already directing its primary development resources toward the 2027 car.

This is Formula 1 in the modern cost-cap era: a driver on a winning streak and a team that may have already accepted that winning the championship this year is not the plan.

A Difficult Season Turned Useful

The 2026 season was not kind to Red Bull in the early months. The new regulations — a 50/50 power split between internal combustion and electrical systems, active aerodynamics, and significantly reshaped cars — exposed weaknesses in the RB22 that were not apparent until the racing started. Verstappen suffered unexpected spins in Austria and Silverstone attributed to a controversial rear wing concept that the team eventually removed.

The removal helped. The second half of the season has been better. And Verstappen — whose competitive instinct has never required team authorization — has been extracting everything from a car that was clearly not the fastest in the field for the majority of the year.

His 73rd career win at Singapore came from pole position, leading every lap, winning by more than seven seconds from Leclerc. When the car is working, Verstappen is still the standard against which every other driver is measured.

The 2027 Shadow

The tension in Red Bull's situation is real. Verstappen is signed through 2030. He is 27 years old. He is winning races in October. He has every motivation to keep pushing for a fifth championship, even with 119 points to overcome and six races remaining.

But teams operate on a longer planning horizon than drivers do. The cost cap limits how much can be spent. Redirecting engineering resources to the 2027 car before the 2026 season ends is not a sign of defeat — it is a sign of strategic calculation. If the RB22 cannot close a 119-point gap, the RB23 needs to be the car that starts the next championship campaign from a position of strength.

Verstappen will race in Austin. He will race in Mexico, São Paulo, Las Vegas, Qatar, and Abu Dhabi. He will try to win every one of them. The question is whether his team's strategic energy is fully pointed at the same target he is.

What's Next

The US Grand Prix runs October 23-25 at the Circuit of the Americas. Verstappen won COTA in 2025. He starts the Austin weekend as the form driver in the paddock, with consecutive wins behind him and a championship gap that is impossible but not mathematical impossibility. Red Bull's dual focus — present racing, future planning — will define the rest of the year.
