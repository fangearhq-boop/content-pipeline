McLaren Is Bringing Upgrades to Every Final Race of 2026 — Andrea Stella Confirms the Commitment

By Elena Voss | October 14, 2026 | F1 Fanrecap

McLaren won the 2025 Formula 1 Constructors' Championship. In 2026, they sit third — 239 points behind Mercedes, 105 points behind Ferrari in second. The new regulations arrived in 2026 and redistributed the competitive order in ways the previous champions could not fully predict.

McLaren team principal Andrea Stella confirmed this week that the team has not redirected its development resources toward 2027. Instead, the team will introduce a phased series of upgrades across each of the remaining six race weekends. The commitment is to the present season, not the next one.

What the 2026 Regulations Did to McLaren

The 2026 rules represented F1's biggest regulatory overhaul since 2014: a 50/50 power split between the internal combustion engine and an expanded MGU-K, the elimination of DRS in favor of active aerodynamics, and substantially reshaped cars with shorter wheelbases and lower weights. Mercedes extracted more from these rules early. Ferrari developed faster mid-season. McLaren found themselves defending a championship title with a car that was — for most of 2026 — not the fastest on the grid.

Lando Norris sits sixth in the drivers' standings with 201 points. Oscar Piastri is fifth. Both are talented enough to have challenged for the title with the right car, and both are under long-term contracts that ensure McLaren's core is stable. What they need now is performance.

The Constructors' Battle Below the Surface

While Antonelli and Russell dominate the drivers' championship narrative, a separate constructors' fight is unfolding between Ferrari (444 points) and McLaren (339 points). The 105-point gap is significant but not insurmountable across six races — if McLaren's upgrade program produces meaningful time, and if Ferrari does not continue its own development pace.

Ferrari finished Singapore with Hamilton in sixth and Leclerc in second. McLaren had Norris fourth and Piastri fifth. The results are competitive. The question is whether the gap can be closed from below, or whether Ferrari's trajectory simply continues upward.

What's Next

The US Grand Prix at COTA is October 23-25. It is the first of six remaining rounds. If McLaren's upgrade program begins delivering consistent lap-time gains starting in Austin, the constructors' battle with Ferrari will become one of the more compelling storylines of the season's final act. The 2026 championship is not McLaren's to lose — but the fight for second place is entirely alive.
