Six Races Left, Six Seats Still Open: The State of F1's 2027 Driver Market

By Marcus Cole | October 14, 2026 | F1 Fanrecap

Formula 1's "silly season" — the annual period when drivers and teams announce, negotiate, and leak their future arrangements — has reached its final act alongside the 2026 championship itself. Several major seats remain unconfirmed. At least one veteran is still fighting for his future. And Cadillac F1's historic debut has created new opportunities for drivers who might never have had them otherwise.

What's Confirmed for 2027

The top teams have largely locked in their talent. Max Verstappen signed an extension through 2030 at Red Bull, ending any discussion of a potential move elsewhere. Lando Norris is committed to McLaren through 2030, Piastri through 2028. Charles Leclerc's Ferrari extension keeps the Monaco driver at the Scuderia for the foreseeable future. Pierre Gasly and Franco Colapinto are confirmed at Alpine for 2027.

These confirmations reflect the basic truth of modern Formula 1: the elite teams know who they want, they pay the price to keep them, and the silly season drama concentrates in the middle and lower portions of the grid.

The Open Questions

Haas needs a replacement for Esteban Ocon, with the American team's second seat unconfirmed as of mid-October. Fernando Alonso's future at Aston Martin remains uncertain — the 45-year-old Spaniard has not publicly committed to a 2027 seat, and neither has the team. Racing Bulls has not confirmed its 2027 driver pairing.

The largest uncertainty is Cadillac F1, which makes its debut in Formula 1 as a new constructor in 2026. Their 2027 driver lineup — at least two seats by definition — has not been publicly announced. The Cadillac entry represents an opening for drivers on the outside of the grid who might otherwise have no path into F1, and the team's announcement is one of the more anticipated pieces of news remaining in the paddock's offseason.

Alonso's Last Season?

Fernando Alonso is 45 years old and has been racing in Formula 1 across five different decades. His future has been uncertain before — he retired once, came back, and has been one of the sport's most competitive midfield drivers through his return with Aston Martin. Whether he continues in 2027, moves to a team role, or retires remains genuinely open. He has said he is not ruling anything out. Aston Martin has not said anything publicly beyond that.

What's Next

Six races remain in 2026 before the Abu Dhabi season finale on December 6. The silly season will continue in parallel — team announcements typically cluster around the final races, when the championship picture is clearer and attention turns to next year. The 2027 grid will be complete by the time the checkered flag falls in Yas Marina.
