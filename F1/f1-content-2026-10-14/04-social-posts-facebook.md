- **Posting window:** 5:00 PM ET
- **Type:** Long-form feature

**Long-Form Post:**
Max Verstappen has won back-to-back Formula 1 races. He won in Malaysia and then, on his 250th Grand Prix weekend, finally broke his personal Singapore curse for a first-ever Marina Bay victory.

He is still 119 points behind Kimi Antonelli.

And his team is reportedly already thinking about 2027.

Red Bull entered the 2026 season on the back foot — the new regulations didn't suit the RB22 as well as they suited the Mercedes W17 and the Ferrari SF-26. Early in the year, Verstappen suffered unexpected spins in Austria and Silverstone linked to a controversial rear wing concept that the team eventually removed. But Verstappen kept driving. And now, in the season's final stretch, he has done what he has always done: found speed when most people assumed the title was settled.

The question is whether Red Bull matches his ambition. Reports from multiple paddock sources suggest the team has already shifted its primary development focus to the 2027 car. Verstappen is signed through 2030. The current car may be the best it gets. The next one — built around lessons from this difficult but ultimately instructive year — might be the one that brings him a fifth championship.

### STORY 3: McLaren Upgrades

- **Posting window:** 8:00 AM ET
- **Type:** Technical update

**Long-Form Post:**
McLaren won the 2025 F1 Constructors' Championship. In 2026, they sit third — 239 points behind Mercedes.

That is what a regulation change can do in Formula 1.

The 2026 rules introduced a 50/50 power split between the internal combustion engine and the electrical system, active aerodynamics, and a new generation of cars that reshaped the competitive order almost entirely. Mercedes built the best car under the new rules. Ferrari developed the fastest. McLaren found themselves chasing.

But Andrea Stella, McLaren's team principal, is not conceding. On Tuesday, he confirmed that the team will introduce a phased series of upgrades across each of the remaining six race weekends — a commitment to developing the 2026 car through Abu Dhabi rather than redirecting engineering resources entirely to 2027.

The gap to chase: 105 points behind Ferrari for the P2 position in the constructors'. Lando Norris is P6 in the drivers' standings with 201 points. Oscar Piastri is P5 in the standings and looking for a strong finish to the season.

McLaren hasn't stopped fighting. Six races left to prove it.

### STORY 4: Ferrari Development

- **Posting window:** 10:00 AM ET
- **Type:** Technical analysis

**Long-Form Post:**
Ferrari is developing faster than anyone else in Formula 1 this season.

That sentence would have seemed improbable at the start of the 2026 campaign, when Mercedes's W17 appeared to have a structural advantage that would define the year. But round by round, the Scuderia has closed the gap — and at events like Miami, where they brought the largest upgrade package of any team in the paddock, they have found meaningful pace.

The result: Lewis Hamilton sits third in the drivers' standings with 229 points. Charles Leclerc is fifth with 215. Ferrari is second in the constructors' with 444 points — 134 behind Mercedes, but 105 ahead of McLaren.

McLaren's technical director has publicly stated that he wants to understand how Ferrari is achieving its development pace under the same cost cap that constrains every team on the grid. That is a remarkable admission — one competitive team acknowledging that another has found an efficiency advantage in the rules that should, theoretically, level the playing field.

In six races, Ferrari still has time to close on Mercedes. Hamilton's podium finishes are the proof of concept. The development rate is the engine.
