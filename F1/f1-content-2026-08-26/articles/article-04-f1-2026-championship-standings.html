2026 F1 Championship Standings: Antonelli Leads by 59 With 11 Races Left

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | August 26, 2026

After Lando Norris's commanding victory at the final Dutch Grand Prix in Zandvoort, the 2026 Formula 1 World Drivers' Championship picture sharpened further — and the Italian Grand Prix at Monza, September 4-6, looms as the most consequential round of the season so far.

Kimi Antonelli holds the championship lead with 242 points. The 20-year-old Mercedes driver has been the dominant force in 2026 — six wins, six pole positions, and consistency that has translated a spectacular season into a 59-point lead heading into Monza. George Russell is second on 183 points. Lewis Hamilton is third, also on 183 points, but Russell holds the tiebreaker with two wins to Hamilton's fewer victories. Lando Norris is fourth on 159 points, his Dutch GP win reducing the gap to Antonelli to 83 points.

The Monza Variable

Every championship projection runs through Monza, and every projection this week has to account for the same unusual fact: the championship leader is starting the Italian Grand Prix from the back of the grid. Antonelli will take a grid penalty at Monza due to a power unit change beyond the allowed allocation. Mercedes views the fresh engine as a strategic investment. The rest of the field views it as an opportunity.

Russell is the closest rival in points, but Hamilton — with Ferrari's new ADUO-2 engine upgrade — is the driver best positioned to capitalize on Antonelli's difficult starting position. A strong Italian GP for Hamilton, combined with a recovery race from Antonelli that yields fewer points than expected, could cut the championship gap significantly. The difference between 59 points and, say, 40 points changes how the second half of the season reads.

Norris, meanwhile, is resurgent. His Dutch GP victory was dominant — 11 seconds clear of Antonelli and Russell — and his McLaren is showing the speed that made Oscar Piastri the 2025 champion. If Norris continues this form into Monza, he could become a genuine threat to the Mercedes-Ferrari two-way fight.

What's Next

The Italian Grand Prix is September 4-6 at Autodromo Nazionale Monza. After Monza, the calendar continues with the Spanish GP (September 11-13), then further through October and November. With 11 races remaining, Antonelli's 59-point lead is comfortable but not insuperable — and Monza is the one race where that comfort gets its most serious test. The second half of 2026 starts now.
