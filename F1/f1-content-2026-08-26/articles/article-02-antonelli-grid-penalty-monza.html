Happy Birthday, Kimi: Antonelli Starts His Home Race From the Back at Monza

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | August 26, 2026

Kimi Antonelli turned 20 years old on August 25. His Mercedes team marked the occasion with a decision that would make most drivers wince: the championship leader will start the Italian Grand Prix — his home race at Autodromo Nazionale Monza — from the back of the grid. Mercedes confirmed the grid penalty this week after deciding to install a new power unit that exceeds Antonelli's season allocation.

The penalty is automatic under F1 regulations: taking a new power unit beyond the allowed number triggers a grid drop that typically exceeds 10 places. For a driver who has won six races and earned six pole positions in 2026, starting last at Monza on September 6 is a jarring change of circumstances. But Mercedes has made the calculation deliberately, and the logic holds.

Why Mercedes Chose Monza

Monza is the highest-speed circuit on the Formula 1 calendar. The aerodynamic load is stripped to a minimum, straight-line speed is maximized, and overtaking — particularly into the heavy braking zone at the first chicane — is more achievable here than at almost any other venue. If you're going to absorb a grid penalty somewhere, this is the track for it.

The fresh power unit provides benefits far beyond a single race. A new engine, operating at full specification without the wear accumulated over a season's worth of race distances, delivers performance advantages that compound across the remaining 11 races. Toto Wolff and the Mercedes strategists have decided those advantages outweigh a difficult starting position at one venue.

For Antonelli, who leads the championship by 59 points over George Russell, the cushion exists to absorb the risk. A strong charge through the Monza field — aided by the new power unit, DRS zones, and the low-downforce setup that suits overtaking — can still yield a decent points haul. A difficult day does not threaten his title lead from 59 points away.

What's Next

The Italian Grand Prix weekend runs September 4-6 at Autodromo Nazionale Monza. Qualifying is Saturday, the race is Sunday. Antonelli will begin his birthday weekend on the grid at the back, the tifosi will be rooting for Ferrari and Hamilton, and the 2026 championship will take its most dramatic turn yet. Starting last with a fresh engine in your home race is the kind of storyline that defines a driver's season — one way or another.
