The 2026 Monaco GP Result May Not Be Final — FIA Court Hears FOM's Measurement Error

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | August 26, 2026

The 2026 Monaco Grand Prix result is still in dispute. The FIA's International Court of Appeal held a hearing behind closed doors in Paris on August 25, examining whether time penalties issued during the Monaco race — penalties that altered the final classification — were based on incorrect data.

At the center of the controversy: FOM, Formula One Management itself, has admitted that its pitlane distance measurement at Monaco was wrong. The error caused average speeds in the pitlane to be reported as higher than they actually were. Drivers who were penalized for speeding in the pitlane may have received those penalties based on figures that never reflected reality.

Pierre Gasly of Alpine is the most directly affected driver. He finished third on the road in Monaco — a significant result for the French team. Two time penalties for alleged pitlane speeding dropped him from P3 to P7 in the final classification. If the ICA overturns the penalties using the correct speed data, Gasly's third-place finish could be restored. If it upholds the penalties, seventh stands.

Piastri's Complicated Position

McLaren's Oscar Piastri adds another layer. He too received a pitlane speeding penalty at Monaco — but McLaren could not appeal it after the race because Piastri served the penalty by making an additional pitstop during the race itself. A penalty served before the chequered flag falls is considered final under F1 regulations and removes the right of appeal.

That means even if the ICA rules that the measurement error was fundamental and affected multiple drivers, Piastri's served penalty may remain on the books regardless of what the underlying data shows. Lewis Hamilton issued a statement on "responsibility" as the hearing began, suggesting the championship-level implications of Monaco are still being processed weeks after the race.

The implications extend beyond individual driver standings. Points changes at a street circuit like Monaco, where gaps are tight and finishing positions are earned over 78 laps of controlled precision, can reshape a championship race. The ICA's verdict — expected in the days following the hearing — will be watched closely by every team, driver, and official in the paddock.

What's Next

The ICA verdict is expected imminently. No official timeline has been given, but hearings of this type typically produce rulings within days. If Monaco's results change, the championship standings shift with them — and every team that earned or lost points that day may see their season rewritten. In F1, even the finish line isn't always the end of the race.
