McLaren Points to the Floor: Oscar Piastri's 2026 Struggles Have a Technical Explanation

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | August 26, 2026

Oscar Piastri won the 2025 Formula 1 World Championship. He is now watching his teammate Lando Norris win races in 2026 while he searches for the pace that made him a world champion a year ago. McLaren has now offered a technical explanation for what is happening: it is a floor problem, not a driver problem.

The team revealed that floor-related issues are specifically affecting Piastri's car — issues that are not replicated in Norris's setup. In F1, the floor generates a significant portion of a car's total downforce. When the floor is not working correctly, the entire aerodynamic balance of the car is compromised. Grip suffers. Corner entry is unstable. The car simply does not do what the driver asks of it.

What This Means for Piastri

The implications of McLaren's admission are significant. If the floor issue is confirmed to be a persistent technical problem affecting Piastri's car specifically, then his 2026 results are not a reflection of his ability — they are a reflection of a mechanical deficit that his team failed to prevent or identify quickly enough.

Norris, for his part, has been exceptional. His Dutch GP victory — 11 seconds clear of the Mercedes pair — was among the best drives of the season. McLaren has not explained why the floor issue affects Piastri's car and not Norris's. That question, as much as any technical answer, will define the team's credibility in how they manage this situation.

Piastri is a technically meticulous driver — known in the paddock for his feedback precision and setup work. The floor issue will not have escaped his attention, and the McLaren engineers will have been working on it with the urgency that a defending champion's season deserves. The hope is that Monza — a new circuit, a reset, fresh setup from scratch — provides the clean-slate performance that Piastri and McLaren need.

What's Next

Monza, September 4-6, is the next test. The Italian Grand Prix's low-downforce philosophy strips away some of the nuance of floor performance — raw engine power and straight-line speed matter more here than at a traditional downforce circuit. That could work in Piastri's favor. If the floor issue is largely resolved, or if the Monza setup minimizes its impact, the world might remember once more what the 2025 world champion looks like when his car is working. McLaren needs him there.
