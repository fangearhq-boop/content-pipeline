### STORY 1: Monaco GP FIA Court Hearing

#### Long-Form Post

The 2026 Monaco Grand Prix result may not be final.

The FIA's International Court of Appeal held a hearing yesterday — August 25 in Paris — regarding penalties issued during the Monaco GP that may have been based on inaccurate data. At the heart of the case: FOM (Formula One Management) has admitted there was an error in how pitlane speeds were measured. The distance measurement used to calculate average speeds in the pit lane was wrong, meaning average speeds were reported as higher than they actually were. That led to drivers being penalized for violations that, according to the correct measurements, may not have occurred.

Pierre Gasly of Alpine bore the brunt of this. He finished third on the road in Monaco — a remarkable result. Two time penalties for pitlane speeding dropped him to seventh in the final classification. If the court overturns the penalties, Gasly moves back up. If it upholds them, P7 stands.

McLaren's Oscar Piastri is the other central figure. He made an extra pitstop to serve his own penalty during the race — meaning McLaren couldn't appeal it after the fact. Hamilton issued a public statement on "responsibility" as the hearing began, suggesting the championship picture around Monaco is still being disputed at the highest level.

A verdict is expected imminently. This is not a theoretical dispute — this is a real race result that may be rewritten weeks after the cars stopped running.

### STORY 2: Antonelli Grid Penalty at Monza

#### Long-Form Post

Kimi Antonelli turns 20 years old today. It's his birthday. And Mercedes gave him an interesting present.

The Formula 1 championship leader will start the Italian Grand Prix — his home race, at Monza — from the back of the grid. Mercedes confirmed on Wednesday that Antonelli will take a new power unit for the September 4-6 weekend, exceeding the allowed season allocation and triggering an automatic grid penalty expected to exceed 10 places.

The logic is pure F1 strategic chess. Monza is a high-speed, low-downforce circuit where overtaking is significantly more achievable than at twisty, narrow tracks. If you're going to start last somewhere, this is one of the better venues to do it — and the fresh, powerful engine provides benefits that compound through the rest of the season, not just at Monza.

But this is also Antonelli's home race. He is Italian. The Monza crowd will be cheering for him. Starting last in the temple of speed at your home circuit, with your birthday in the rearview mirror, is an unusual way to enter the weekend — but Antonelli is leading the championship by 59 points. He can absorb a difficult race better than almost anyone else in the field right now.

Whether he charges from last to points, or whether Hamilton capitalizes with Ferrari's new engine, Monza just became a defining weekend.

### STORY 3: Ferrari ADUO-2 at Monza

#### Long-Form Post

Ferrari chose Monza to unleash its biggest engine upgrade of the 2026 season.

The ADUO-2 power unit — estimated to deliver roughly 15 additional horsepower compared to the current specification — debuts at the Italian Grand Prix (September 4-6). And the timing could not be more strategically significant.

Monza is the highest-top-speed circuit on the F1 calendar. Cars reach 350+ km/h down the main straight. The aerodynamic load is minimal by design. In this environment, raw engine power differentiates more than at any other venue. Ferrari's 15hp advantage translates to a meaningful gain in top speed, braking zone entry, and overtaking force.

Lewis Hamilton enters this scenario with the upgrade behind him and Kimi Antonelli — who leads Hamilton by 53 points — confirmed to start from the back of the grid with a grid penalty. Hamilton arriving at Monza with more power, fewer obstacles ahead of him at the start, and Ferrari's iconic home crowd in the grandstands is about as favorable a championship scenario as he's had all season.

This is Ferrari's title shot. If Hamilton wins Monza and Antonelli struggles from the back, the gap closes dramatically. The championship fight that has been one-sided for much of the season gets genuinely interesting.

### STORY 5: Oscar Piastri — McLaren's Floor Explanation

#### Long-Form Post

Oscar Piastri was the 2025 Formula 1 World Champion. He is now the McLaren driver who can't seem to keep pace with his teammate Lando Norris — and the team has offered an explanation.

McLaren has identified floor-related issues affecting Piastri's car that are specific to his setup and are not replicated in Norris's car. The team says this is a technical problem, not a driver form issue. They are investigating and working to resolve it.

That is a significant admission. It means Piastri is not underperforming — he's being held back. The 2025 champion who won a World Title on merit is being outpaced by a mechanical disadvantage that the team created (or failed to prevent) in his car's floor configuration.

Norris, meanwhile, just won the Dutch Grand Prix. He has been one of the fastest drivers in 2026 when the car is right. For McLaren's Constructors' Championship ambitions, having Piastri at full capacity matters enormously. A floor fix at Monza could suddenly turn a two-vs-one Constructors fight back into a full McLaren offensive.

For Piastri, the season's frustration deserves a technical resolution — and soon. He is too good a driver to be a footnote.
