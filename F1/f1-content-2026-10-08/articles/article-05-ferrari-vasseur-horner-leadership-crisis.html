Ferrari CEO Backs Vasseur — But the Horner Speculation Won't Stop

By Elena Voss | October 8, 2026 | F1 Fanrecap

There is a particular grammar to Formula 1 team politics. When a CEO feels the need to publicly reaffirm their faith in the team principal, the subtext rarely goes unnoticed. So when Ferrari CEO Benedetto Vigna issued a statement on October 3 describing his relationship with Frédéric Vasseur as built on "shared ambition, mutual expectations, and clear responsibility," the F1 community noticed — and immediately began reading between the lines.

The official Ferrari position is unambiguous: "We have full confidence in Fred Vasseur and we continue to do so." Vasseur's contract was extended in July 2025 for a multi-year term. He remains in his position at Maranello.

And yet, the same week as Vigna's statement, RacingNews365 reported that Ferrari has a "provisional plan" for managing Vasseur's potential exit at the end of the 2026 season. The names being discussed internally as possible successors: Antonello Coletta, who leads Ferrari's World Endurance Championship program, and Jérôme d'Ambrosio, currently serving as deputy F1 team boss.

The Horner Factor

If internal succession planning were the only angle, this would be a routine motorsport management story. But Christian Horner's name has inserted itself into the narrative in a way that is proving impossible to dismiss.

Horner — former Red Bull Racing team principal and one of the most experienced figures in modern Formula 1 — said publicly that Ferrari is "the dream," describing it as the only team in the paddock where he would accept a senior role without an ownership stake. That statement was not made in passing. It was deliberate, precise, and calculated to land exactly where it did.

Vasseur has responded to the Horner speculation by calling it exactly what he says it is: speculation. But in a sport where information often moves through layered denial before emerging as confirmed fact, the mere existence of the conversation carries weight.

The Performance Context

None of this speculation would have legs without the underlying pressure of Ferrari's 2026 season. The Scuderia assembled what many regarded as the most exciting driver lineup in the paddock: Lewis Hamilton arriving as a seven-time champion, partnered with Charles Leclerc in what Ferrari hoped would be a title-winning combination.

The reality has been more complicated. Ferrari currently sit second in the constructors' championship on 405 points, trailing Mercedes by 151 — a gap that has proved impossible to close. Hamilton is third in the drivers' standings on 214 points, Leclerc fourth on 191. Both are competitive, both are scoring regularly. But neither has been able to challenge the dominant Mercedes-Antonelli combination in the way Ferrari expected when they committed to this era of the sport.

Ferrari have had seven different team principals since 2007. The pattern at Maranello — of official backing followed by transition — is well established in the sport's institutional memory. Vigna's statement and the succession reports are not necessarily contradictory. They may simply be simultaneous truths: Vasseur has Ferrari's confidence, and Ferrari is prudently prepared for any eventuality.

What It Means for Singapore and Beyond

The leadership narrative does not change what happens on track at Marina Bay this weekend. Hamilton and Leclerc will both be pushing hard at Singapore — a circuit that rewards Ferrari's high-downforce setup capabilities — and a strong result for the Scuderia this weekend would ease some of the pressure that has been building since the summer.

But the longer the championship gap to Mercedes extends, the louder the questions about Ferrari's direction will become. If Antonelli clinches the drivers' title before the season ends — which the mathematics currently suggest could happen within the next few rounds — the internal conversation at Maranello may shift from "provisional planning" to active decision-making.

What's Next

Ferrari head to Singapore for Round 17, a sprint weekend at Marina Bay. Hamilton and Leclerc will look to challenge at a high-downforce street circuit that historically suits their car's characteristics. The team's constructors' championship battle against McLaren for second place — trailing by 89 points — provides an immediate competitive target regardless of what happens at the top of the standings.

For Vasseur, Singapore is another opportunity to show the results that make leadership questions irrelevant. The best response to speculation, as ever in Formula 1, is performance on the circuit.
