Verstappen and Norris Tied at 188 — Malaysia Win Reignites the Red Bull Championship Math

By Ryan Calloway | October 8, 2026 | F1 Fanrecap

Max Verstappen's victory at the Malaysian Grand Prix was a masterclass in wet-dry conditions. It was also something much larger for Red Bull Racing: the first win for Red Bull Ford Powertrains under the 2026 engine regulations, arriving at Round 16 of 23 and providing proof that the Ford-badged power unit has genuinely arrived.

The final result at Sepang tells one story — Verstappen first, Antonelli second, Hamilton third — but the championship consequence tells another. By winning in Malaysia, Verstappen pulled level with Lando Norris in the drivers' standings. Both now hold 188 points, tied for fifth and sixth in the championship. Neither is mathematically eliminated from the title, though both trail leader Kimi Antonelli by 132 points.

This is the closest the second group in the championship has been to relevance all season.

A Win That Validates the Ford Partnership

Red Bull's partnership with Ford — the American automotive giant's first F1 engine involvement since 2004 — was the off-season storyline that captured the most global attention heading into 2026. The pressure to deliver results has been substantial, and the early months of the season saw the Red Bull Ford combination searching for competitiveness against Mercedes' dominant power unit.

The Malaysia win, secured in mixed wet-dry conditions that rewarded adaptability and driver skill, validated what Red Bull engineers had been arguing quietly for months: the package was improving, and the right conditions would expose its potential. Verstappen delivered that result in full.

The win marked Verstappen's first of the 2026 season — his second consecutive Malaysian Grand Prix victory — and came with a podium that included both Hamilton (P3, +4.919 seconds) and Leclerc (P4, +7.258 seconds), providing useful data points on Ferrari's relative pace.

The Norris Equation

Lando Norris finished seventh in Malaysia, a result that allowed Verstappen to close the gap between them entirely. The two drivers are now level on 188 points, separated by whichever tiebreaker the championship uses. This creates an intriguing dynamic heading into Singapore: both are in the same points position but with very different cars and very different Singapore prospects.

McLaren has been competitive throughout 2026, and Norris will approach Marina Bay with confidence. Red Bull, reinvigorated by the Malaysia result, will be targeting a strong sprint and race result at Singapore — a circuit where strategy and qualifying position typically matter more than raw power unit output. For Red Bull, that is a favorable equation.

The Title Mathematics

With seven rounds remaining — including Singapore this weekend — Antonelli needs just 84 points more than Russell to clinch the championship regardless of what Verstappen or Norris do. For Verstappen or Norris to win the title, they would need Antonelli to score very few points across multiple remaining rounds while they win virtually everything.

That scenario is unlikely. But both drivers at 188 points demonstrate that the midfield of the championship is genuinely contested. The fight for second and third in the standings between Russell, Hamilton, Leclerc, Norris, and Verstappen has championship pride and constructors' points implications that will run to the final race.

What's Next

Singapore — Round 17 — is a sprint weekend, which means sprint points are available in addition to Grand Prix points. Verstappen enters as the bookmakers' favourite at Marina Bay, a circuit that suits his aggressive, committed driving style. With Russell starting from the back due to a power unit penalty, and Antonelli in full title-charge mode, this weekend could see Verstappen make a significant move up the standings regardless of what happens at the very front.

After Singapore, the 2026 F1 calendar heads to Austin and Mexico City. Red Bull, having broken its 2026 win drought, arrives at the final stretch of the season with renewed energy — and a Ford engine that just proved it can win.
