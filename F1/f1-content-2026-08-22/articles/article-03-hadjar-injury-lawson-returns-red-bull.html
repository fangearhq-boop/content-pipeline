Hadjar's Boxing Injury Brings Lawson Back to Red Bull — and Tsunoda Back to Racing Bulls

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap

August 22, 2026

Isack Hadjar picked up a wrist injury during the summer break — at a boxing gym, of all places — and the ripple effects have reshuffled the driver lineups at both Red Bull junior teams ahead of the Dutch Grand Prix.

Hadjar will not race at Zandvoort. In his place, Liam Lawson has been confirmed as the Red Bull race driver for this weekend, stepping up from reserve duties. And as a consequence of Lawson moving to the senior team, Yuki Tsunoda — who was displaced by Hadjar when the 2026 season started — has returned to Racing Bulls alongside rookie Arvid Lindblad.

It is, by any measure, an unusual way to kick off the post-summer-break portion of the season.

Hadjar's Difficult Summer

Isack Hadjar came into 2026 as one of the most anticipated young driver promotions in Red Bull's recent history. The French-Algerian driver had impressed in junior categories and was given the senior Red Bull seat for the new season — a promotion that came at Yuki Tsunoda's expense despite the Japanese driver's strong 2025 campaign.

His rookie year has had its moments, but a wrist injury suffered during a boxing session in the summer break has cost him this weekend entirely. The nature of the injury and its recovery timeline have not been fully detailed publicly, but Red Bull confirmed he is unable to race at Zandvoort safely.

Lawson's Return

Liam Lawson has 46 career F1 starts and genuine race experience at the top level. The 24-year-old New Zealander impressed in his stints with Red Bull-affiliated teams in previous seasons and has maintained his preparation as reserve driver for both Red Bull outfits.

There is a notable symmetry in his return: Lawson made his F1 debut at the 2023 Dutch Grand Prix, filling in for an injured Daniel Ricciardo. Three years later, he is filling in for an injured Hadjar at the same circuit. The final Dutch GP at Zandvoort completes a loop for Lawson that he likely didn't anticipate.

In sprint qualifying on Friday, Lawson qualified 11th and was eliminated in SQ2. The Dutch GP circuit and its unique characteristics are familiar territory, but the Red Bull car requires adjustment time, and a single session is a steep learning curve.

Tsunoda Back at Racing Bulls

The subplot worth following: Yuki Tsunoda is back at Racing Bulls, the team where he spent much of his career before being displaced when Hadjar was promoted. Tsunoda qualified 12th in sprint qualifying and was also eliminated in SQ2.

The timing is ironic. Tsunoda was considered unlucky to lose the Red Bull seat to Hadjar at the start of 2026. Now, with Hadjar sidelined by injury, the Japanese driver is back in a lower-rung seat for a weekend that he might have expected to drive for Red Bull had the calendar played out differently.

What's Next

Lawson will race for Red Bull in Sunday's Dutch Grand Prix. His result will draw inevitable comparisons to Hadjar's season-to-date performance and may influence conversations about 2027 lineups, depending on how the remainder of 2026 develops.

Hadjar's recovery timeline will determine whether he returns for the Italian Grand Prix at Monza, the next race on the calendar (September 6). For now, Zandvoort belongs to Lawson — and to the farewell weekend of one of F1's great modern atmospheres.
