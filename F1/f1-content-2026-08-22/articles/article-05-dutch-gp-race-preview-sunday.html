Dutch GP Race Preview: Title Fight, Verstappen's Last Home Race, and a Rain Wildcard

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap

August 22, 2026

Sunday, August 23. 15:00 CEST at Circuit Zandvoort. 72 laps. One race. Multiple storylines. And the last Dutch Grand Prix this circuit will ever host.

Here is everything you need to know before the final Zandvoort race goes green.

The Championship Stakes

Kimi Antonelli leads George Russell by 56 points heading into Sunday's race. Both drivers are on Mercedes, both are fast, and both know that the result here could define the trajectory of the 2026 title fight.

Russell won Saturday's sprint. He needs to show that momentum carries into the full race distance, where strategy, tire management, and race craft matter as much as raw pace. Antonelli needs to respond after a sprint weekend where his SQ1 floor damage cost him performance.

A gap of 56 points sounds comfortable but it is not — not with this many races remaining, not with Russell in this form. Antonelli cannot afford another weekend where the gap closes; he needs to re-establish distance. Sunday is that opportunity.

Verstappen: One Last Chance at Home

Max Verstappen has won the Dutch Grand Prix five consecutive times. In what is the final-ever Dutch GP at Zandvoort, the four-time world champion starts from sixth on the sprint grid and will need to qualify strongly on Saturday afternoon to have a realistic shot at the front.

Verstappen in front of the Orange Army at his home circuit is one of the more reliable spectacles in motorsport. Even when his car has not been at its absolute best in 2026, he has found ways to extract performance at Zandvoort specifically — the circuit's characteristics, the crowd energy, and years of familiarity all add up to a performance multiplier.

The question for Sunday: can he reach the front? And if he does, can he hold it against championship-motivated rivals?

The Orange Army needs a reason to celebrate this farewell. Verstappen is their last best hope of getting one.

The Rain Factor

Weather forecasts suggest rain is possible on Sunday at Zandvoort. Wet conditions at this circuit are a genuine wildcard — the banked final sector becomes treacherous, the narrow track offers little room for error, and tire strategy can swing the race outcome dramatically.

If it rains, Verstappen's circuit knowledge and Red Bull's historically strong wet-weather car setup become additional factors. If it's dry, the Mercedes-McLaren-Ferrari battle at the front shapes the race. Either scenario is worth watching.

Other Storylines to Watch

McLaren's Lando Norris finished the sprint in third and has been consistently quick in recent rounds. His race performance on Sundays has been strong — if McLaren's strategy team gets the tire windows right, Norris could be a genuine winner rather than a bridesmaid.

Charles Leclerc finished second in the sprint for Ferrari. Ferrari has shown improvement at high-speed circuits; Zandvoort's banked sector may suit their 2026 car. Whether Ferrari executes a clean two-stop strategy or gambles on an alternative plan will be worth tracking.

And Liam Lawson will drive for Red Bull for the first time since returning as Hadjar's replacement. A strong race performance in the senior car — on his F1 debut anniversary circuit — would not go unnoticed in Red Bull's driver evaluation conversations.

What's Next

The starting grid will be set by Saturday afternoon qualifying, with results released before Sunday's race. The final Dutch Grand Prix goes green at 15:00 CEST (09:00 AM ET) on August 23. After 72 laps, Zandvoort's F1 story ends.

Whatever happens Sunday — whether it's an Antonelli dominant win, a Russell charge, a Verstappen fairytale, or a Norris breakthrough — it will be the last time F1 comes to this remarkable, atmospheric circuit. Watch it like it's the last time. It is.
