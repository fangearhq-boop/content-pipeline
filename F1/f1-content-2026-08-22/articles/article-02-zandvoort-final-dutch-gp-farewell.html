Goodbye, Zandvoort: The Final Dutch Grand Prix and the End of an Orange Era

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap

August 22, 2026

After Saturday's qualifying session concludes at Circuit Zandvoort, there will be only one more F1 session left at this circuit: Sunday's 72-lap race. Then it ends.

The 2026 Dutch Grand Prix is the final Formula 1 race to be held at Zandvoort. When the checkered flag falls on Sunday afternoon, one of the most atmospherically unique venues in modern motorsport will close its F1 chapter for good.

How It Started

The Dutch Grand Prix was absent from the F1 calendar for 35 years before its revival in 2021. The return happened for one reason: Max Verstappen had become a world champion and a phenomenon, and the Netherlands wanted their hero racing at home.

What followed exceeded every expectation. The Orange Army — a sea of 100,000-plus fans wearing orange from head to foot — transformed Zandvoort into an experience unlike anything else in the modern F1 paddock. Even drivers who weren't Max Verstappen spoke of the atmosphere with genuine awe. The sound levels when Verstappen passes the main grandstand in qualifying are, by multiple accounts, the loudest in any sport.

Verstappen won the Dutch Grand Prix in 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, and 2025. Five consecutive victories at his home circuit. The record exists because Verstappen is that good, and because Zandvoort's characteristics — high-speed banking, complex sector two, flowing final sector — suited the Red Bull car during its dominant era.

What Made Zandvoort Special

Circuit Zandvoort is not a modern Tilkedrome. It doesn't have the sterile runoff areas and identical pit lane structures of Abu Dhabi or Bahrain. It's a narrow, old-school racing circuit set among the dunes of the Dutch coast, with genuine challenge: walls close in on drivers, the banked final corner tests the car's limits, and overtaking requires genuine skill rather than DRS-assisted power.

The circuit's revival also included infrastructure upgrades — new pit lane, new grandstands, expanded fan areas — while preserving the character that made historic races here memorable. The result was a circuit that looked old but felt thrillingly alive.

It ends this weekend not because the racing was poor, but because of F1's calendar dynamics: the sport is expanding to new markets, and older European venues sometimes lose their slot. Zandvoort was unable to secure terms that satisfied both F1 management and circuit requirements. And so the farewell is here.

Verstappen's Last Home Race

Max Verstappen will race at Zandvoort for the last time on Sunday. The man whose stardom rebuilt the Dutch GP now presides over its closure. He starts the race from sixth on the sprint grid, with a deep championship year and the weight of a nation's expectations on his shoulders.

The Orange Army won't be quiet. They'll want one more Verstappen win. They'll want a send-off worthy of everything this venue gave them over five extraordinary seasons.

Whether Verstappen delivers it — racing against a Mercedes title fight between Antonelli and Russell, a McLaren challenge from Norris and Piastri, and a Ferrari threat from Leclerc — is tomorrow's question. But this weekend, regardless of the race result, belongs to Zandvoort and to the fans who made it what it became.

What's Next

Sunday's race begins at 15:00 CEST (09:00 ET). When it ends, so does Zandvoort's F1 story. The next Dutch Grand Prix — if it ever happens — will be at a different circuit, in different times. What the Orange Army built at this narrow dune-side track between 2021 and 2026 was something remarkable.

Watch Sunday's broadcast. Stay for the closing laps. Enjoy the orange. It won't look like this again.
