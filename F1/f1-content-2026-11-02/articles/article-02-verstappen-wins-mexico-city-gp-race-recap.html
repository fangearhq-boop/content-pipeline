Verstappen Wins Wild Mexico City GP — Hamilton P2 as Antonelli Takes the Title from Third

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | November 2, 2026

Max Verstappen delivered one of his characteristically dominant performances at the Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez, crossing the finish line 0.799 seconds ahead of Lewis Hamilton's Ferrari to win the 2026 Mexico City Grand Prix. But while Verstappen was celebrating victory in the conventional sense, the real story belonged to the man who finished third: Kimi Antonelli, who secured the 2026 Formula 1 World Drivers' Championship in the most composed way possible.

Antonelli finished 4.006 seconds behind the winner and 3.207 seconds behind Hamilton, but in terms of the season's narrative, his podium was worth infinitely more than his rivals' higher-placed finishes. It was the result that made him champion.

Verstappen Back in Victory Lane

The Dutchman's win was a reminder of what he brings to any given Formula 1 weekend when his Red Bull is properly dialled in. Verstappen has been the sport's most reliably spectacular race-day driver for the better part of a decade, and Sunday's performance at altitude — managing tire wear, managing Hamilton's pressure over the final laps, and executing a nearly flawless strategic call — was textbook Verstappen.

Lewis Hamilton did everything he could. The Ferrari was quick enough to threaten for much of the race, and Hamilton's post-race acknowledgment that "we know where we need to improve" reflected a driver still focused on Ferrari's development trajectory even in a week dominated by a championship celebration happening directly behind him on the podium.

Penalties Reshuffle the Lower Order

After the checkered flag, Formula 1 stewards issued post-race penalties to Oscar Piastri and two Alpine drivers, creating one of the more complicated final classifications of the 2026 season. The penalties affected constructors' points totals for McLaren and Alpine — particularly relevant given how tight the Red Bull-McLaren battle is in the teams' standings.

Charles Leclerc finished fourth, Lando Norris fifth, and George Russell sixth before penalty adjustments. The final official classification from the FIA resolved some of those lower positions, but the broad shape of the top six held.

What's Next

The full focus now shifts to the constructors' championship as the São Paulo Grand Prix approaches on November 8. Verstappen's Mexico win may have been his best of the final stretch, and he will need more of the same if Red Bull is to close the gap on McLaren. For Antonelli, the São Paulo weekend begins a new phase of his Formula 1 career — champion, not contender. That changes everything about how races feel, and how they're raced.
