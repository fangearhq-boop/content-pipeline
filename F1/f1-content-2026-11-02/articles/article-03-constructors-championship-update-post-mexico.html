Constructors' Championship: Four Races to Settle the Team Battle After Mexico City

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | November 2, 2026

With Kimi Antonelli's drivers' title confirmed in Mexico City, Formula 1's second great prize — the Constructors' World Championship — now becomes the defining narrative of the season's final four rounds. The standings heading into Mexico showed Mercedes far ahead on 607 points, with Ferrari chasing on 474. But the more compelling battle was between Red Bull and McLaren, who were tied at 355 points each with no separation whatsoever.

Mercedes Closes In

The Mexico City result — Antonelli third, Russell sixth — adds to Mercedes' total and extends their lead over Ferrari, who scored points through Hamilton's second place and Leclerc's fourth. Mercedes entered Mexico needing to protect their 133-point cushion over Ferrari, and the team's double points haul from Sunday will not have harmed that objective.

The teams' title would be the cherry on top of a season in which Mercedes produced arguably the fastest car in F1, the youngest ever world champion, and one of the most dominant championship campaigns in recent memory. Three consecutive titles in the hybrid era are not unusual for Mercedes; adding another in the new technical regulations would be a statement of excellence.

Red Bull vs McLaren: The Real Fight

Before Mexico, Red Bull and McLaren were equal on points. The Mexico result — Verstappen first, Norris fifth, with Piastri affected by a post-race penalty — may separate the two teams in one direction or another, but the gap was so small heading in that any movement will be marginal.

For McLaren, the priority is understanding what the penalty against Piastri means for their constructors' points and how aggressive their strategy needs to be in São Paulo. For Red Bull, Verstappen's win is a momentum boost that gives the team real confidence about their pace in the final flyaway calendar stretches — Las Vegas and Abu Dhabi in particular have historically suited Red Bull's car.

What's Next

Four races remain: São Paulo (November 8), Las Vegas (November 19-22), Qatar (late November), and Abu Dhabi (early December). Each race distributes up to 43 points per team (if both drivers score in the top 10). The midfield battle will be as important as the front-runners in deciding whether Mercedes' lead is enough to be uncatchable, and whether Red Bull can overtake McLaren for third. The constructors' fight is alive and real. Mexico was the beginning of the end — not the end.
