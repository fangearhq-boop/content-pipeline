Zak Brown Says Verstappen Talks "Didn't Go Anywhere" — But Insiders Still Point to McLaren

By Ryan Calloway | August 17, 2026 | F1 Fanrecap

The most significant driver market story in Formula 1 took a sharp public turn on Monday — though whether that turn reflects reality or negotiating strategy remains deeply unclear.

McLaren Racing boss Zak Brown went on record this week stating that discussions with Max Verstappen "didn't go anywhere" and that he is "very happy" with his current lineup of Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri. By every public measure, McLaren has closed the door on adding the four-time world champion to their roster for 2027.

The problem is that almost everything else points in the opposite direction.

What Insiders Are Saying

PlanetF1 reported that Verstappen is in "advanced talks" to leave Red Bull for McLaren for the 2027 season. GPFans' insider column on August 16 went further: Verstappen's bond with Red Bull is "broken," the team had made Verstappen a fresh contract offer that received no response, and McLaren remains the frontrunner among the destinations being seriously considered.

The contradictions are stark. Brown says talks went nowhere. Insider sources say they're very much alive. The pattern — public denial combined with private progress — is a common feature of F1's most sensitive negotiations.

Verstappen's Situation at Red Bull

Verstappen's exit clause became active during the summer break after he entered it sitting sixth in the championship, approximately 110 points behind championship leader Kimi Antonelli. The clause was tied to his position in the standings — and the 2026 season has been, by his own standards, the most difficult of his career. Red Bull's RB22 has not matched the pace of the Mercedes or Ferrari machinery, leaving the four-time champion without a race win in a way that has fueled persistent speculation about his future.

The exit window runs through October. The Dutch Grand Prix — which takes place August 21-23 at Zandvoort, just four days away — is Verstappen's home race, a sellout farewell event at a circuit where he has won three times. If there was ever a moment engineered for a dramatic announcement, this is it.

The Brown Calculation

Brown's public statement serves at least two possible purposes. If McLaren genuinely has no intention of signing Verstappen, the statement closes the story and allows Norris and Piastri to feel fully secure in their positions. If McLaren IS actively pursuing Verstappen, the statement creates negotiating leverage — allowing McLaren to be seen as disinterested while maintaining private conversations.

The Norris-Piastri factor is genuine: both drivers are under contract with McLaren for 2027 and beyond. Adding Verstappen would require displacing one of them — a complicated proposition that would send its own set of market shockwaves.

What's Next

The Dutch GP is the pressure point. Four days from now, Verstappen will race in front of 100,000 orange-clad Dutch fans who have been waiting all summer for news about their hero's future. The media attention, the questions from the paddock, and the weight of the farewell atmosphere at Zandvoort create a natural environment for a resolution. Watch for any paddock movement, team principal comments, or official statements in the days surrounding the Dutch GP. Brown's "talks didn't go anywhere" may be the opening line of the final chapter — or the most convincing bluff in recent F1 history.
