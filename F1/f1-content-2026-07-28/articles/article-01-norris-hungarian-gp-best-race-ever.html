Norris Calls Hungarian Win "Probably My Best Race" — The Reigning Champion Is Back

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | July 28, 2026

A retirement, a penalty, and a reigning champion who found his very best form at exactly the right moment.

Lando Norris chose his words carefully and then delivered them with complete conviction. Speaking after his Hungarian Grand Prix victory, the reigning Formula 1 World Champion offered an assessment that said everything about where he is right now: "My pace today was probably some of the best pace I've ever had. The car was beautiful to drive and I felt very confident."

That is not the kind of statement a champion makes after a fortunate win. It is the statement of a driver who found something special and knew it.

The context of the race makes the assessment more meaningful, not less. The opening lap was not beautiful — Norris himself said the car felt "horrible" early on, losing the rear repeatedly with what he described as "big snaps." He survived the chaos on a circuit where first-corner contact is routine and came through unharmed. Oscar Piastri, his McLaren teammate, had controlled the race from pole position before his retirement dropped Norris into the lead. From there, Norris drove with the kind of precision and pace that a 15-second winning margin represents.

A Win That Came Through Adversity

The result completes a strange kind of catharsis for McLaren. The team that arrived at the 2026 season as reigning Constructors' Champions had watched Mercedes win 8 of the first 10 races heading into Hungary. Their best results had come from Piastri's podium finishes. Norris — the Drivers' Champion — had been scoring points without the wins that define a title challenge.

Hungary changed that in the most chaotic way possible. Max Verstappen finished second, 15 seconds behind Norris. Andrea Kimi Antonelli took third to extend his championship lead. Lewis Hamilton was fourth across the line but received a five-second penalty for speeding in the pit lane, dropping to fifth. Charles Leclerc inherited the final podium place.

The race also tightened the McLaren internal dynamic heading into the summer break. Piastri's retirement denied the team a potential one-two that would have made a dramatic statement about their second-half intentions. Norris's win underlined that his form, when the car is right, remains at the level of a World Champion.

What Comes Next for McLaren

The summer break gives McLaren time to evaluate where they stand. They trail Mercedes by 72 points in the Constructors' Championship. Norris sits fifth in the Drivers' Championship — not the position he would have envisioned defending from — but with momentum now back in his corner.

Piastri will need a clean race to return to the form he showed in the first stint at the Hungaroring. The question for McLaren's second half is whether the car is genuinely competitive enough to challenge Antonelli and Mercedes at circuits that suit different setups.

What Norris said after Hungary, though, suggests he is not conceding anything. "Probably some of the best pace I've ever had" is a data point. The form is there. When the season resumes at Zandvoort on August 21, the championship might look different — but the reigning champion served notice on Sunday that he intends to make it complicated.

What's Next

Formula 1 enters its summer break until the Dutch Grand Prix at Zandvoort on August 21-23. Norris will arrive there as a race winner with his best-ever pace on record, 91 points behind Antonelli in the standings. If McLaren can close the gap between their car and Mercedes' across the remaining races, the second half could still produce a title fight. If they cannot, Norris's Hungarian win will stand as the bright moment in a season that belonged to someone else.
