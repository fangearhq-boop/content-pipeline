Verstappen's Red Bull Exit Clause Is Active — ESPN Reports McLaren Deal "Imminent"

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | July 28, 2026

The clause window is open. The signals are conflicting. And the most consequential driver market situation in Formula 1 history is now at its tipping point.

The precise condition that needed to occur for Max Verstappen to be free to leave Red Bull has now been met. The four-time World Champion finished outside the championship's top two positions at the summer break — sitting fifth with approximately 76 points — triggering the exit clause in his contract that allows him to negotiate his departure for the 2027 season.

ESPN reported that Verstappen and McLaren CEO Zak Brown have spoken privately, and that a three-year deal is "imminent." The veracity of that framing — and the conflicting signals coming from Red Bull's own camp — make this summer break the most consequential in driver market history.

What Is Actually Known

The exit clause itself is confirmed from multiple sources: f1chronicle.com, f1oversteer.com, and racingnews365.com have all reported its mechanics clearly. The clause activates if Verstappen is outside the championship top two at the summer break. He is. The clause is triggerable.

Verstappen's long-time race engineer Gianpiero Lambiase has already been confirmed for McLaren's 2028 season — a development that has widely been read as a precursor signal for Verstappen's own move.

Red Bull Team Principal Laurent Mekies told media on July 28 that a "logical" 2027 lineup decision is close. That language was immediately interpreted across the F1 press as Red Bull preparing Isack Hadjar as Verstappen's replacement — suggesting the team is planning for a departure, even if no official announcement has been made.

The Conflicting Signal

Against all of this: Red Bull sources have told some outlets that Verstappen intends to stay for 2027. These reports are real. They may reflect genuine negotiating posture, internal uncertainty, or simply the competitive noise that surrounds every major driver market announcement before it is finalized.

What is not disputed is that the clause window exists and is now open. What happens between now and the Dutch Grand Prix on August 21 — when the teams reconvene in the paddock for the first time after the break — will resolve this. These decisions typically move quickly once the conditions are met.

What This Means for McLaren and Red Bull

If Verstappen moves to McLaren, the implications are immediate and significant. He would join a team that, with a fully competitive car in 2026, could provide a genuine championship vehicle in 2027. He would be alongside Oscar Piastri — a teammate dynamic that would be one of the most compelling in the sport's modern era. Lando Norris, the current World Champion, would need to be accommodated somewhere in that picture.

Red Bull, meanwhile, would need to rebuild around Hadjar and a 2027 technical reset. The team has faced structural challenges under the 2026 regulations, and losing Verstappen would strip away the competitive cover that has kept them in conversations about the front of the grid.

What's Next

The summer break is the decision window. Verstappen's announcement — whatever it contains — is expected before or at the Dutch Grand Prix on August 21. That race, at Zandvoort in the Netherlands, would then carry the additional weight of being either Verstappen's home race as a Red Bull driver for the last time, or his home race knowing he had just chosen to stay. Either way, the Dutch crowd will be there, and the answer will come.
