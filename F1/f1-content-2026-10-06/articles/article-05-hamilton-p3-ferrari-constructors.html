Hamilton Third in Malaysia: Ferrari's Season-Ending Push Gets a Boost at Sepang

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | October 6, 2026

Through the chaos of the Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia — the rain delay, the safety cars, the power unit failures, the strategy gambles — Lewis Hamilton quietly delivered one of his best results of 2026. Third place at Sepang. A podium. Valuable points for Ferrari in both championships.

Commentators called it a "remarkable recovery drive." Hamilton started deep in the order after qualifying complications and worked his way through the field with the kind of methodical race craft that has defined a seventeen-year career at the front of Formula 1. At 41, the details of his technique are unchanged. The patience. The tire management. The ability to wait for moments rather than force them.

Where Hamilton Stands

The result moves Hamilton to 214 points in the Drivers' Championship — third overall, 22 points behind George Russell and 23 ahead of Charles Leclerc in fourth. Lando Norris sits fifth with 188 points. Four drivers — Russell, Hamilton, Leclerc, Norris — are separated by just 48 points in the fight for second through fifth place.

For Hamilton, that battle has significant personal stakes beyond the arithmetic. His first season at Ferrari, after thirteen years at Mercedes, has been an adjustment — a new car philosophy, a new team culture, new relationships to build. Third in the championship would be a legitimate result given those circumstances. Second would be extraordinary.

The Malaysian podium won't change the championship order meaningfully by itself, but it confirms that Hamilton and Ferrari are serious threats in the final race meetings.

Ferrari's Constructor Battle

The Constructors' Championship battle behind Mercedes is a separate, closely contested fight. Ferrari's combined points from Hamilton and Charles Leclerc put them in a position to challenge McLaren for the No. 2 constructor slot — a battle that, depending on sponsorship implications and prize money calculations, carries significant financial and reputational weight.

McLaren's Norris finished behind Hamilton in Malaysia, limiting the Papaya team's gains in the constructors' fight. Oscar Piastri's sixth place added points, but Ferrari closed ground across both drivers. The battle between these two constructors is likely to go down to the wire in the remaining races.

The Ferrari Era Under Hamilton

Context matters when evaluating Hamilton's 2026 season. This is a driver who spent thirteen years with Mercedes — a team he helped build into the most dominant constructor in Formula 1 history. His move to Ferrari was one of the most debated decisions in the sport's recent history, with many expecting a difficult transition period.

Difficult it has been, at times. But not unsuccessful. Hamilton has qualified well in multiple events, shown race pace that rivals the best, and contributed to Ferrari's development trajectory in ways that don't always show up in points. A podium in the race following George Russell's crushing retirement is a reminder of what Hamilton's experience and consistency bring to any team.

What's Next

Singapore on October 11 offers Hamilton another opportunity to score heavily on a circuit where experience, car management, and race craft — his three greatest strengths — are rewarded. Ferrari will hope for dry conditions that flatten the playing field between their car and the dominant Mercedes, though in a sprint format weekend, variable weather is possible.

Hamilton's best chance at a landmark result — a race win or a very high points haul — in the 2026 season lies in the final Grands Prix. After Malaysia, the tools are there. The form is there. Singapore is next.
