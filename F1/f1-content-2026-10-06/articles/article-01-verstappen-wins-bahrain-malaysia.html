Verstappen Wins Bahrain GP in Malaysia: Red Bull Breaks 2026 Drought in Chaos

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | October 6, 2026

Max Verstappen has done it again. In conditions that would have defeated lesser drivers, the four-time world champion delivered Red Bull Racing its first Formula 1 victory of the 2026 season at a dramatic, rain-hit Bahrain Grand Prix at the Sepang International Circuit in Malaysia.

Verstappen crossed the line 2.307 seconds ahead of championship leader Kimi Antonelli after 55 laps of attrition, strategy, and nerve, while Lewis Hamilton completed a stunning recovery drive to claim third for Ferrari. George Russell, who needed a strong result to stay alive in the championship, suffered a power unit failure on Lap 50 behind the Safety Car — a heartbreaking end to what had been a solid afternoon's work.

93 Minutes of Waiting

The race's defining character was set before a wheel had turned in anger. Torrential rain at Sepang delayed the scheduled start by 93 minutes. When the formation lap finally attempted to begin, multiple cars lost power — a problem traced to suspected software issues — and the process was red-flagged. It took coordination, patience, and a second attempt to get the field rolling.

By the time the lights went out for real, the track was still unpredictably slippery, and teams were squinting at weather radar rather than lap time data. The decision to run intermediate tyres was near universal at the front of the grid, though the strategy would diverge dramatically as conditions evolved.

How Verstappen Won

Polesitter Verstappen found himself behind both Antonelli and Russell on the run down to Turn 1, with all three on intermediates. He settled into an early rhythm rather than forcing the issue, managing his tyres through the first complex while the Safety Car came out for Valtteri Bottas's spin.

The timing of that Safety Car was crucial. Verstappen used it to close on both Mercedes cars, then picked off Russell and Antonelli in the laps that followed, first under braking at Turn 1 and then with a bold move through the middle sector. Once he was clear in front, he was never seriously threatened, managing the gap to Antonelli as the track continued to evolve.

Norris earned a driving reprimand for a Lap 50 Safety Car near miss that underlined how treacherous conditions remained even in the final stages.

Red Bull's Long Wait

The victory ended a difficult stretch for Red Bull Racing under the 2026 technical regulations. The new rules have heavily favored Mercedes, who won eight of the first eleven Grands Prix and dominated qualifying throughout the season. Red Bull's RB22 has been competitive in patches — Verstappen has often qualified well — but converting that pace into race victories has eluded them.

Malaysia was different. The chaos of the conditions played to Verstappen's greatest strengths: tire management, car control on the limit, and the ability to perform when the script tears itself up. He's won championships in dry conditions, in monsoons, and in everything in between. This was another chapter in a career that keeps producing them.

What's Next

The Singapore Grand Prix at Marina Bay arrives on October 11, and Verstappen will carry genuine momentum into the street circuit for the first time in months. With the championship all but Antonelli's, Singapore becomes a race about pride, secondary positions, and constructors' points. For Verstappen, it's a chance to show the RB22 can win back-to-back. For Red Bull, it's an opportunity to prove Malaysia was a turning point rather than an exception.

The final stretch of the 2026 season starts now. Verstappen is already moving.
