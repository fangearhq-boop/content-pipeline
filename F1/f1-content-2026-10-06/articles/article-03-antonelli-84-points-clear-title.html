Antonelli 84 Points Clear: The 2026 F1 Title Is Kimi's to Lose

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | October 6, 2026

Kimi Antonelli is on the cusp of becoming Formula 1's youngest world champion in modern memory. After his second-place finish at the Bahrain Grand Prix at Sepang, the 19-year-old Mercedes driver leads the 2026 Drivers' Championship with 320 points — 84 clear of teammate George Russell with the season heading into its final races.

The arithmetic is on his side. The momentum is on his side. The machinery is on his side. What's left is simply the doing of it — arriving in Singapore, and in whatever races follow, and collecting enough points to make it official.

A Season-Long Masterclass

Antonelli's 2026 campaign has been one of the most impressive debut championship runs in recent F1 history. Six Grand Prix victories. A lead he has held virtually uninterrupted. The kind of composure under pressure that drivers twice his age struggle to replicate.

He arrived in Formula 1 as Mercedes' most hyped young signing in years — a teenager replacing Lewis Hamilton, who had departed for Ferrari after an extraordinary thirteen-season career at the Silver Arrows. The weight of expectation could have been paralyzing. Instead, Antonelli treated it as fuel.

Under the new 2026 technical regulations, Mercedes built a car that immediately dominated. Eight wins in the first eleven Grands Prix. Ten pole positions. A constructors' championship that, like the drivers' title, looks increasingly like a formality. Antonelli was both the driver of that machine and its best advertisement.

What 84 Points Means

The practical implications of an 84-point lead with limited races remaining are stark. For Russell to win the championship, he would need to outscore Antonelli by 84 points across the remaining Grands Prix. Given that a race win is worth 25 points, Russell would need to win essentially every remaining race while Antonelli consistently finished outside the points — a scenario that has no precedent in recent seasons.

The current top five in the Drivers' Championship tell the full story:

  • Kimi Antonelli (Mercedes) — 320 points
  • George Russell (Mercedes) — 236 points
  • Lewis Hamilton (Ferrari) — 214 points
  • Charles Leclerc (Ferrari) — 191 points
  • Lando Norris (McLaren) — 188 points

The contest below Antonelli — between Russell, Hamilton, Leclerc, and Norris — is the championship within the championship. Those four drivers are separated by just 48 points, and the final race order of that battle remains genuinely open.

The Road to History

If Antonelli wins the championship, he will become one of the youngest world champions in Formula 1 history. He will also be the first Italian driver to win the title since... well, it has been a long time. The significance of that achievement would not be lost on a sport that has deep roots in Italy and a fanbase there that has waited decades for a champion.

Singapore is next. It is a sprint weekend — the final one of the 2026 season. Points come faster in sprint weekends, which theoretically accelerates the timeline for a mathematical title confirmation. Whether Antonelli clinches in Singapore or after will depend on the race results, but the trajectory is clear.

What's Next

Kimi Antonelli heads to Marina Bay on October 11 as the overwhelming championship favorite. His task is simple in theory, complex in execution: finish ahead of Russell by enough that the math closes out. Given his season-long form, there is no reason to expect him to stumble now.

The 2026 F1 championship is not over. But it's close enough that the world is already starting to picture Antonelli with a trophy. After this season, it's hard to argue he hasn't earned it.
