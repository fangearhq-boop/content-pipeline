La Monumental and the Circuit Built to Announce Madrid's Return to Formula 1

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | September 12, 2026

The Madring debuts in Formula 1 this weekend after 45 years away from the Madrid region. Its most distinctive feature — a 550-meter banked turn inspired by the city's historic bullrings — is unlike anything else on the 2026 calendar.

Formula 1 raced in the Madrid region for the last time in 1981. The Circuito del Jarama, a tight, twisting permanent facility north of the city, hosted the Spanish Grand Prix and then watched F1 move its Spanish home south to Barcelona, where it stayed for 45 years. The Madring is what happens when a global sport decides to return to a capital city with something to prove.

The circuit is unlike anything currently on the calendar. It is not a street circuit in the traditional sense — too many sections run on purpose-built private land around the IFEMA Madrid exhibition center to qualify. It is not a permanent racing facility in the traditional sense — too many of its sections follow the lines of public roads. It is a hybrid, and its hybrid nature produces a layout that 5.47 kilometers long with 22 corners, two tunnel sections, and one turn that immediately distinguishes it from every other circuit in modern Formula 1.

La Monumental

Turn 12 at the Madring has a name before it has a race history. La Monumental — the name given to the circuit's signature banked corner — was designed to invoke Madrid's historic bullrings, particularly Las Ventas, the city's most famous arena. The architectural reference is not merely aesthetic. The banked design serves a structural function: it allows cars to carry more speed through a corner that would otherwise require significant braking, generating the highest vertical load of any single corner on the 2026 F1 calendar.

La Monumental spans 550 meters. Its gradient reaches 24% at maximum inclination. The banking reaches 13.5 degrees at the apex — a figure that places it in the same category as the banked oval sections used at certain American motorsport venues, translated into the context of a European road circuit. An F1 car traverses it in approximately six seconds at racing speed, experiencing a load that tests the driver's neck and the car's tire wear in ways no other corner on the calendar replicates.

The Circuit Layout

Beyond La Monumental, the Madring presents drivers with a varied set of challenges. The 22-corner layout transitions between slower technical sections requiring precise braking and trail-throttle balance, and open high-speed arcs where aerodynamic grip and chassis stiffness determine pace. Organizers projected an average lap speed of 218kph — making the Madring one of the faster circuits on the calendar, though still below the pure-speed venues like Monza.

The two tunnel sections add to the circuit's distinctive character. Running through enclosed structures changes the acoustic experience for both drivers and spectators and creates aerodynamic conditions that differ from open-air sections of the same lap. The tunnels are short but perceptually significant — particularly on a circuit where F1 is visiting for the first time.

The race distance is 57 laps, which at 5.47km per lap gives a total race distance of approximately 311.8km — comfortably within the 305km minimum required by FIA regulations.

The Historical Return

The Spanish Grand Prix has been part of the Formula 1 calendar, in one form or another, since the championship's earliest years. Madrid hosted the race at the Circuito del Jarama from 1968 through 1981, with interruptions due to political and financial complications. Barcelona's Circuit de Barcelona-Catalunya became home to the Spanish GP from the early 1990s, building one of the most consistent presences on the European calendar.

The move to Madrid represents a conscious choice by both F1 and the Spanish government to expand the sport's footprint in the country's capital while building a venue around permanent infrastructure that generates value beyond a single weekend per year. The Madring's location adjacent to the IFEMA exhibition center means the facility hosts events throughout the calendar year, with the Grand Prix circuit activated specifically for the F1 weekend.

What's Next

Qualifying today and the race on Sunday write the first chapter of the Madring's Formula 1 history. La Monumental will produce its first pole position sector today and its first race leader through a banked corner tomorrow.
