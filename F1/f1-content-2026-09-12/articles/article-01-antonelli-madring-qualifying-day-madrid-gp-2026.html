Qualifying Day at the Madring: Antonelli's Championship March Reaches a Brand-New Circuit

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | September 12, 2026

The first-ever Formula 1 qualifying session at Madrid's Madring circuit is today. Championship leader Kimi Antonelli topped FP2. Ferrari is 0.1 seconds behind. A circuit nobody has raced at before, a title fight that has never been closer, and a pole position that matters beyond today's grid order.

Qualifying day at the Madring arrives with a question that no historical data can answer: who knows this circuit best after 90 minutes of practice? The Madring has never held a Formula 1 race. No driver has a reference lap stored from a previous qualifying session. No team has a database of tire behavior, brake wear, or wind sensitivity built from years of events. Friday's two practice sessions are the complete record, and that complete record belongs to everyone equally.

Kimi Antonelli arrived at FP2 and made himself slightly more equal than others.

The Mercedes driver, leading the 2026 Drivers' Championship by 66 points, set the fastest lap of Friday at the Madring — a 1:33.662s in the afternoon session that left Ferrari's Charles Leclerc and Lewis Hamilton 0.09 and 0.1 seconds behind respectively. George Russell had topped the morning with a 1:34.077s, but the afternoon's order — Antonelli, Leclerc, Hamilton — tells a more meaningful story about race pace and aerodynamic efficiency over the Madring's 22-corner layout.

The Circuit Nobody Knows

The Madring presents a genuine unknown that no amount of simulator preparation fully resolves. The 5.47km hybrid circuit — part public road, part purpose-built private track around the IFEMA Madrid exhibition center — was designed without the accumulated racing heritage that most Formula 1 venues carry. Teams could model it. They could simulate it. They could not replicate the physical reality of rubber laid down in anger, brake zones verified at speed, and tire degradation understood through laps rather than projections.

What the circuit does offer, for the first driver who learns it fastest, is a genuine competitive advantage. Adapting to a new layout under qualifying pressure separates certain drivers from others. Antonelli's FP2 performance suggested he adapted quickly. Russell's FP1 dominance suggested the same. What qualifying resolves is which adaptation translates to the committed single lap that decides the grid.

The Championship Numbers

Antonelli arrives in Madrid having won seven races from 13 starts in 2026 — a pace that has produced a 66-point advantage over teammate Russell heading into the second half of the season. He won the Italian Grand Prix at Monza one week ago from 19th on the grid after a power unit penalty, overtaking the field and beating Russell to become the first Italian driver to win at Monza since Ludovico Scarfiotti in 1966. The victory extended a championship lead that began the season as a whisper and has grown into a declaration.

258 points remain available. Antonelli does not need to protect a lead so much as continue doing what he has been doing — scoring, adapting, and responding to every pressure point his championship rival creates. A Madrid pole adds another marker to a season-long record of qualifying excellence. A Madrid win extends the most impressive debut championship campaign of his generation.

Qualifying begins at 16:00 local time in Madrid — 10:00 AM ET. The race is Sunday at 15:00 local, 9:00 AM ET. One day separates practice data from race result.

What's Next

The qualifying session at 16:00 local time will determine the grid for Sunday's inaugural Madrid Grand Prix. Antonelli, Leclerc, Hamilton, and Russell are the four drivers most likely to compete for the front rows based on Friday's data. La Monumental, the circuit's signature banked turn, will produce the most spectacular single sector of any qualifying session this season.
