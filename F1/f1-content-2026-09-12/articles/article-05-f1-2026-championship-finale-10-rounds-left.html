Ten Rounds Left. 258 Points Available. The 2026 F1 Title Race Has Not Been Decided.

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | September 12, 2026

Kimi Antonelli leads George Russell by 66 points heading into the Madrid Grand Prix. The math still allows a championship comeback. The question is whether the 10 remaining rounds can produce the swing Russell needs — starting today's qualifying at the Madring.

The numbers are clear. Kimi Antonelli has 267 points after 13 races. George Russell has 201. The gap is 66 points. With 258 points remaining across 10 Grands Prix and one sprint race, Russell needs to outperform Antonelli by an average of 6.6 points per round for the rest of the season — every round, without exception, until the Abu Dhabi finale.

That is difficult. But it is not mathematical elimination. The 2026 title has not been decided. The Madring qualifying session today is the first of the final ten opportunities to change the direction of that calculation.

How Antonelli Built the Lead

Kimi Antonelli entered 2026 as the youngest driver on the Mercedes roster and the most scrutinized newcomer in years. The expectation — from fans, from rivals, from his own team — was that his talent was evident but his consistency under championship pressure was untested. He has tested it, consistently, and produced one of the most dominant first halves of a championship season in the sport's recent history.

Seven wins. Six pole positions. Eleven podiums. A victory at Monza from 19th on the grid — power unit penalty absorbed, field overtaken, teammate beaten to the line. His championship lead of 66 points is not luck or circumstance. It is accumulated precision across circuits where he has been the class of the field.

The most relevant fact for the final ten rounds: Antonelli's deficit conversion at Monza — where he faced the prospect of losing significant ground to Russell and instead gained seven points — demonstrates an ability to respond under championship pressure that goes beyond individual circuit mastery.

The Constructors' Picture

Mercedes leads the Constructors' Championship by 122 points over Ferrari with 446 available. The constructors' battle is more open than the drivers' standing, primarily because Ferrari has shown, particularly in Friday's practice at Madring, that the SF-2026 has circuits where it genuinely challenges Mercedes. A consistent run of podiums from both Leclerc and Hamilton over the final third of the season could close a gap that looked comfortable at Monza.

For Mercedes, managing the constructors' lead alongside supporting Antonelli's drivers' campaign creates strategic complexity that will define their approach at each of the final ten circuits. The team must balance protecting Antonelli's championship advantage with ensuring both cars score — a calculation that, at the Madring, begins today with qualifying.

The Calendar Question

FIA and F1 CEO Stefano Domenicali has a mid-September deadline to confirm the final ordering and details of the season-ending rounds in Qatar and Abu Dhabi. The Qatar Grand Prix and the Abu Dhabi Grand Prix have been confirmed as the season's final two events, but logistical and scheduling specifics remain subject to confirmation. The deadline falls within the Madring race weekend or shortly after — meaning the calendar's back end will be formally settled as the front end begins at Madrid.

The calendar matters for championship strategy: teams building race-specific setup packages need confirmed venues to allocate engineering resources. Uncertainty at the end of a calendar forces generalist preparation rather than specialized optimization. Domenicali's announcement will allow both Mercedes and Ferrari to finalize their development paths for the season's conclusion.

What's Next

Qualifying today at 16:00 local time in Madrid begins the process of answering the championship question. The race is Sunday. Nine more rounds follow.
