São Paulo GP Preview: Schedule, Thunderstorm Warning, and Five Key Storylines

By Marcus Cole | November 3, 2026

The Formula 1 circus moves to South America this week, and for once the storylines heading into a race weekend are almost too rich to catalogue. The São Paulo Grand Prix at Interlagos — November 6–8 — carries championship implications, emotional weight, and now a weather forecast that threatens to reshape the entire grid before a single race lap is turned.

The Schedule

The 2026 São Paulo Grand Prix uses a conventional format with no sprint race, giving drivers and teams the full benefit of three practice sessions before qualifying.

  • Friday, November 6: Practice 1 and Practice 2
  • Saturday, November 7: Practice 3 and Qualifying (6:00 PM UK / ~1:00 PM ET)
  • Sunday, November 8: Race — 71 laps of 4.309 km — 5:00 PM UK / 12:00 PM ET

The circuit runs 4.309 kilometres per lap, and the full race distance covers approximately 305.9 km. Interlagos is renowned for its unpredictable weather, altitude, and the unique anti-clockwise layout that places specific demands on tyres and fuel management.

Thunderstorm Warning

The weather forecast for Saturday is already the most discussed topic in the F1 paddock. Thunderstorms are currently expected during qualifying, which could produce a completely scrambled starting grid. A wet Interlagos qualifying session benefits drivers with strong car control in changeable conditions and punishes those who rely on outright pace in clean air.

Should qualifying run in the rain, the race strategy on Sunday becomes significantly more complex. Teams will need to plan for multiple scenarios, and the margin for error — even for front-runners — is much smaller when the track dries between Saturday night and Sunday afternoon.

Five Storylines to Watch

1. Antonelli's First Race as Champion. The 2026 title holder arrives in Brazil for the first time wearing the champion's crown. He has also confirmed a special Senna tribute helmet, marking the 35th anniversary of the Brazilian legend's 1991 home win at this very circuit.

2. Mercedes Constructors' Title Clinch. One more strong weekend could wrap up the Constructors' Championship for Mercedes, completing a title double with Antonelli's Drivers' crown. The mathematics heavily favour the silver arrows.

3. Ferrari's Internal Tension. Hamilton and Leclerc arrive in Brazil carrying the weight of their Mexico City clash. Team boss Vasseur has promised discussions, but how that translates to on-track behaviour is the great unknown of the weekend.

4. The Brazilian Crowd. Interlagos produces some of the most passionate race-day atmospheres in F1. With Antonelli wearing Senna's colours and Mercedes on the verge of a title, the energy in the grandstands will be exceptional.

5. The Weather Wildcard. Thunderstorms for qualifying could produce genuine surprises. The teams most adaptable to changing conditions — and the drivers most comfortable in the wet — will have an advantage that pure speed alone cannot compensate for.

What's Next

Practice begins Friday. Qualifying could be a spectacle under the storms on Saturday. And the race itself — with title implications, emotional tributes, and team politics all converging — could be one of the most memorable rounds of the 2026 season.

Set your alarms for 12:00 PM ET Sunday. This one is worth the early wake-up.
