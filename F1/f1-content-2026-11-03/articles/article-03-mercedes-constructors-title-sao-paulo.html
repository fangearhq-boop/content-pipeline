Mercedes Can Clinch 2026 F1 Constructors' Title at São Paulo Grand Prix

By Elena Voss | November 3, 2026

The 2026 Formula 1 season has already delivered its Drivers' Champion in Kimi Antonelli. Now, this weekend in São Paulo, Mercedes has the opportunity to complete a title double that would define the team's year as one of the great campaigns in the sport's modern era.

Mercedes enters the São Paulo Grand Prix holding a 126-point advantage over Ferrari in the Constructors' Championship standings, and according to Sky Sports, the silver arrows could clinch the title at Interlagos this Sunday. With only a handful of rounds remaining after Brazil, the mathematics are firmly in Mercedes' favour.

How They Got Here

The 2026 Constructors' race has been a season-long story of Mercedes' ability to extract the most consistent performance from both of its cars. Antonelli's extraordinary individual campaign obviously drove much of the points haul, but a Constructors' title requires both drivers to deliver — and Mercedes' second car has contributed reliably throughout the season.

A 126-point gap with a limited number of races remaining is a margin that does not disappear overnight. Ferrari would need an almost perfect set of results, paired with Mercedes outright failures, for the gap to be bridged at this stage.

The Title Double in Perspective

Winning both the Drivers' and Constructors' Championships in the same season is a rare achievement, and doing so emphatically — by a margin that becomes insurmountable before the season ends — is rarer still. If Mercedes clinches in São Paulo, the season will be remembered as one of the most dominant championship campaigns of the modern Formula 1 era.

For the team and its management, the significance extends beyond trophy cabinets. A title double validates the technical direction Mercedes has taken, the driver lineup decisions, and the strategic calls that have defined this campaign.

Ferrari's Fading Hopes

From Ferrari's perspective, the constructors' picture is bleak. The Mexico City Grand Prix, where an internal dispute between Hamilton and Leclerc cost the team potential points, underlined the challenge Ferrari faces when trying to maximize results from both cars simultaneously. A Mercedes clinch at São Paulo would formally end Ferrari's mathematical challenge.

The red cars will still race hard at Interlagos — pride, sponsorship, and next year's development direction all provide motivation — but the title fight, for all practical purposes, will be over before the final laps are run on Sunday.

What's Next

For Kimi Antonelli and George Russell, Sunday's race carries the twin motivations of a potential team title clinch and the competitive desire to begin the São Paulo weekend strongly. Thunderstorms are forecast for Saturday qualifying, which could scramble grid positions and add unpredictability to the race day strategy.

If Mercedes crosses the line in the right combination of positions, Interlagos will see one of the most celebrated moments in the team's history — a Drivers' and Constructors' double sealed at one of the sport's most storied circuits.
