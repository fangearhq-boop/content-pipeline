Ferrari's Internal Battle: Why Hamilton and Leclerc Must Repair Trust Before Brazil

By Ryan Calloway | November 3, 2026

Ferrari's problem in 2026 has never been talent. Charles Leclerc and Lewis Hamilton are two of the greatest drivers of their generation, each capable of winning on any given weekend. The problem has been making those individual talents function as a cohesive unit.

The Mexico City Grand Prix made that clear in the most public way possible. A Turn Four incident, a five-second penalty, and Hamilton's subsequent demand for "serious talks" within the team have brought the internal friction into full view. With the São Paulo Grand Prix just days away, Ferrari's leadership faces the challenge of resolving — or at least containing — an intra-team dynamic that is now a competitive liability.

A Pattern That Keeps Repeating

The Mexico incident did not emerge from nowhere. Throughout the second half of the 2026 season, both Leclerc and Hamilton have occasionally prioritised their individual race positions over collectively optimal team outcomes. This is understandable — both drivers are world champions, both believe they are the faster car on any given day, and neither is inclined to simply yield to the other without cause.

But Formula 1 is as much a team sport as an individual one, especially in the final weeks of a Constructors' Championship fight. Every unnecessary point surrendered through internal conflict is a gift to Mercedes, which has the luxury of managing a straightforward team dynamic while Ferrari manages a political one.

What Ferrari Needs in Brazil

The mathematical reality at São Paulo is unforgiving. Mercedes can clinch the Constructors' title at Interlagos this Sunday. Ferrari's only response is maximum points from both cars — which requires clean racing, coordinated strategy, and, above all, the mutual restraint to not race each other into disaster.

That means Leclerc and Hamilton arriving at the circuit having resolved — at least temporarily — the tensions from Mexico. Team boss Vasseur has indicated the conversation will happen before practice begins. Whether that produces a clear, agreed framework for racing each other, or simply manages the symptoms without addressing the cause, remains to be seen.

The Ferrari Tradition of Internal Tension

Ferrari is a team with one of the richest histories in motorsport, and that history includes no shortage of intra-team conflicts that have derailed seasons which, on paper, could have ended in championships. The pressure at Maranello is unlike anywhere else in the sport, and that pressure has a tendency to amplify rather than resolve competitive tensions between teammates.

Whether 2026 will be remembered as the year Ferrari's talent finally came together, or another chapter in its long history of self-inflicted complications, may well be determined by what happens at Turn Four in Brazil.

What's Next

Practice at Interlagos begins Friday, November 6. Thunderstorms are forecast for Saturday's qualifying — a wild card that could scramble the grid and demand exactly the kind of cohesive team response that Ferrari has struggled to produce under pressure.

Hamilton and Leclerc will both want to win on Sunday. The question is whether they can agree, even if only tacitly, on the terms under which they compete. Ferrari's season may hinge on the answer.
