### STORY 1: Hamilton FP2 Ferrari 1-2 / McLarens Hit Wall

**FB Long-Form (8:00 AM ET)**
Thursday's second practice session in Baku just rewrote the narrative for the Azerbaijan Grand Prix weekend.

Lewis Hamilton topped FP2 with a 1m41.293s, leading a Ferrari 1-2 with Charles Leclerc 0.074 seconds behind. George Russell slotted into 3rd with championship leader Kimi Antonelli 4th.

But the bigger story wasn't who was fastest — it was who hit the walls.

Lando Norris lost the rear of his McLaren at Turn 4, smacking the barrier and breaking his left-rear suspension. His session ended there in 10th. Oscar Piastri wasn't much better, hitting the barriers at T15 before recovering to 12th. The McLarens were struggling for grip under braking throughout the session.

McLaren brought a Baku-specific upgrade targeting the second sector. Thursday's results suggest it isn't working as intended. Qualifying is Saturday morning. They have one more session to find answers.

Ferrari, on the other hand, looked genuinely strong. Hamilton's pace, Leclerc's speed despite starting from the back with a new power unit, and a Prancing Horse that may be well-suited to Baku's long straight and late-braking zones.

### STORY 2: Antonelli's Nightmare Thursday

**FB Long-Form (12:00 PM ET)**
Championship leaders are supposed to have smooth weekends. Kimi Antonelli has not had a smooth Thursday in Baku.

It started in Free Practice 1 when Antonelli pulled off at Turn 7 after just nine laps with a hydraulic failure. Marshals deployed a fire extinguisher on his car as Race Control called a Virtual Safety Car. A clean lap count of nine — in a one-hour session — is essentially nothing.

He recovered in FP2 to finish 4th. But now he's under investigation for allegedly failing to slow under yellow flags during the session.

Here's the context that makes this matter: Antonelli leads the Drivers' Championship by 81 points over George Russell with nine rounds remaining. He doesn't need a perfect Baku weekend. He needs a clean one. A grid penalty from the yellow flag investigation would complicate qualifying at a circuit where starting position matters.

The investigation outcome is still pending. But for a 19-year-old who has run one of the most dominant championship campaigns in recent memory, Thursday in Baku was a test of composure he didn't ask for.

### STORY 3: Leclerc Grid Penalty — New Engine Working

**FB Long-Form (5:00 PM ET)**
Charles Leclerc is going to start Saturday's Azerbaijan Grand Prix from the back of the grid. He also just went second-fastest in Free Practice 2.

Ferrari confirmed Leclerc will take a power unit penalty, triggering after they introduced a new specification engine from FP2 onwards. The old unit ran in FP1. The new unit — reportedly around 0.2 seconds per lap faster — ran in FP2. The result: P2 behind Hamilton.

The penalty is already baked in. Leclerc will start last regardless of how well he qualifies. But the data from FP2 changes the story around his race.

Baku is the best circuit on the calendar for making up positions. The 2.2-kilometer main straight is the longest in Formula 1. Under 2026's new regulations with lower downforce levels, energy management and straight-line speed become even more decisive. A Ferrari with a faster power unit starting from last place at the world's longest-straight circuit is a very different proposition than the same penalty at Monaco.

### STORY 4: Norris Championship Crisis

**FB Long-Form (2:00 PM ET)**
Lando Norris needed Baku to be a turning point. Thursday suggested it might go the other way.

The defending champion arrived in Azerbaijan 106 points behind Kimi Antonelli with nine rounds remaining. McLaren brought a circuit-specific upgrade package. Then both cars hit the walls.

Norris made contact at Turn 4 in FP2, breaking his left-rear suspension and ending his session in 10th. Oscar Piastri hit the barriers at T15. The root cause, according to multiple reports, was a braking grip issue that both drivers struggled with throughout the session. The upgrade designed for the second sector hasn't addressed the fundamental problem.

233 championship points remain in the season. That means a perfect second half could technically get Norris to Antonelli's level. But perfect weekends don't start with wall damage in free practice.

### STORY 5: Williams FW48 2.0 Debut

**FB Long-Form (10:00 AM ET)**
Williams debuted a car in Baku today that James Vowles himself admitted probably won't score points.

The FW48 2.0 — internally called the B-spec — is the result of a new lighter carbon manufacturing process. The original FW48 was overweight; this version addresses that fundamental flaw. Vowles called it "almost completely new." He also offered an honest caveat: it "won't be enough for points" this season.

So why show it at a race? Because this car is the template for the FW50 — Williams' 2027 chassis. The regulations change dramatically next year and teams are already validating their architectural concepts for that ruleset. Every lap Carlos Sainz and Alex Albon turn in Baku with the B-spec is data that feeds into 2027 development.

Williams is building for a future that they believe will be significantly better than the present. The FW48 2.0 isn't a points car. It's a proof of concept for the next era.
