Williams Debuts "Almost Completely New" FW48 in Baku — and It's Really About 2027

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | September 24, 2026

Williams unveiled what team principal James Vowles called an "almost completely new" car in Free Practice 1 in Baku on Thursday. He also told anyone who asked that it probably won't score points.

The FW48 2.0 — the B-specification of this year's car — debuted in Azerbaijan as the final major upgrade Williams plans to introduce in the 2026 Formula 1 season. Understanding why it exists requires looking past the current season entirely.

What's Different About the FW48 2.0

The original FW48 arrived overweight at the start of the 2026 season. Williams identified a manufacturing solution: a new, lighter carbon fiber production process that reduces the car's mass and allows for more optimal weight distribution. The FW48 2.0 incorporates that process throughout the chassis — hence Vowles' description of it as almost completely new.

Carlos Sainz and Alex Albon drove the updated car in FP1, generating the data Williams needs from live running. Vowles offered no illusions about the immediate competitive implications: the upgrade "won't be enough for points" in Baku or in the near term.

The Real Purpose: FW50 Development

Formula 1 introduces dramatically revised regulations for the 2027 season. Every constructor is already designing their car for that ruleset, and the most meaningful preparation comes not from simulations but from running real structures at real speeds on real circuits.

The FW48 2.0's new carbon manufacturing process is the same process Williams intends to use on the FW50, their 2027 challenger. Every lap Sainz and Albon complete in Baku validates the structural behavior of that architecture under race conditions. If the FW48 2.0 performs as expected, Williams enters 2027 with confidence in a fundamental manufacturing approach that could unlock the weight advantages they've been chasing since the season started.

What's Next

Williams will track performance data from the FW48 2.0 through the remainder of the 2026 season, comparing it to the original specification and incorporating findings into FW50 development. Sainz and Albon continue in the car through Baku qualifying and Saturday's race. It won't win the Azerbaijan Grand Prix — but it might win Williams a season or two from now.
