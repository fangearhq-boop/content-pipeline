Hamilton Leads Ferrari 1-2 in Baku FP2 as Both McLarens Hit the Wall

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | September 24, 2026

Lewis Hamilton topped Free Practice 2 for the Azerbaijan Grand Prix with a time of 1m41.293s, leading a Ferrari 1-2 as both McLarens suffered costly contact with the Baku barriers on a dramatic Thursday afternoon.

Charles Leclerc was 0.074 seconds behind his Ferrari teammate in second, followed by George Russell in third and championship leader Kimi Antonelli in fourth. The session told a very different story than the FP1 top times suggested: Ferrari looks genuinely strong for race pace, and McLaren has a serious problem.

McLaren's Wall-Hitting Afternoon

Lando Norris lost the rear of his MCL41 at Turn 4 and hit the barrier, breaking his left-rear suspension. His session effectively ended there — he was classified 10th. Oscar Piastri then contacted the wall at Turn 15, though he escaped with minimal damage and was able to complete race simulations at the end of the session, finishing 12th.

The contact both drivers experienced appeared symptomatic of a wider issue: McLaren was struggling for grip under braking throughout FP2. The team had brought a Baku-specific upgrade package targeting the second sector, but it wasn't delivering the expected performance gains. When a championship-contending team brings circuit-specific upgrades and both cars end up in the barriers, the debrief becomes very important very quickly.

Ferrari's Quiet Statement

While McLaren was dealing with suspension damage and missed lap time, Ferrari was quietly building a picture that looks strong for Saturday's race. Hamilton's pace was smooth and controlled. Leclerc — running the new power unit that forces him to start from the back of the grid — was equally fast in the second-fastest time, confirming that the new specification engine is delivering the performance Ferrari expected.

George Russell survived a brush with the barriers himself in FP2 but kept his car clean enough to post the third-best time. Antonelli, still in fourth despite a compromised session after his FP1 hydraulic failure, showed composure to recover from his morning troubles.

What's Next

Qualifying takes place on Friday in Baku. McLaren will need to understand overnight why both cars were so unstable under braking and whether the damage to Norris's suspension has any knock-on effects on setup choices. Ferrari heads into qualifying with quiet confidence and the knowledge that even Leclerc — starting from last — represents a threat in a race that historically rewards straight-line speed and overtaking.
