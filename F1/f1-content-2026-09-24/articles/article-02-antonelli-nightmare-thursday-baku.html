Antonelli's Nightmare Thursday: Hydraulic Failure, Extinguisher on Car, Yellow Flag Investigation

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | September 24, 2026

Kimi Antonelli came to Baku as the Formula 1 championship leader, 81 points clear of the field with nine rounds to go. He leaves Thursday with a hydraulic failure on his record, a fire extinguisher incident, and a pending investigation for an alleged yellow flag breach.

For a 19-year-old running one of the most dominant championship campaigns in recent memory, this was not the day he needed.

FP1: Nine Laps Then Silence

The morning session lasted just nine laps for Antonelli before he pulled off at Turn 7 with a hydraulic failure. Marshals deployed a fire extinguisher on the stricken Mercedes — an image that immediately circulated across social media — as Race Control called a Virtual Safety Car to recover the car.

Nine laps out of a one-hour session gives an engineer almost nothing to work with. Setup data, tyre behavior, energy deployment patterns — all of it was compressed into a fraction of the usual preparation time. Antonelli was classified fifth in FP1 despite the limited running.

FP2: Recovery and New Problems

The afternoon session showed Antonelli's resilience. He recovered to fourth in FP2, getting representative lap time and race simulation data. But then came the investigation notice: Antonelli is under scrutiny for allegedly failing to slow under yellow flags during the session.

The outcome is still pending. If a penalty is applied, the most common sanction for a yellow flag breach is a grid drop or a time penalty. At a circuit where starting position can be significant — despite Baku's overtaking opportunities — a qualifying penalty would add another complication to a weekend that has already demanded extra management from the Mercedes garage.

The Championship Context

Antonelli holds 292 championship points after 14 rounds. His nearest rival, George Russell, is 81 points behind. With 233 points remaining in the season, he doesn't need a perfect result in Baku — he needs a clean one. Bad weekends can be absorbed from this position. But they shouldn't be self-inflicted.

What's Next

The investigation result will be known before qualifying on Friday. If a grid penalty is applied, Mercedes will need to factor that into qualifying strategy. Regardless, Antonelli showed in FP2 that he can set competitive lap times despite the disrupted day. The title is not in danger from a single difficult Thursday — but the Baku weekend just became considerably more complicated.
