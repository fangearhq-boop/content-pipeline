Leclerc Starts Last in Baku — But His New Ferrari Engine Just Went P2 in FP2

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | September 24, 2026

Charles Leclerc will start Saturday's Azerbaijan Grand Prix from the back of the grid. He will also go into that race knowing his Ferrari has one of the fastest power units on circuit.

Ferrari introduced a new power unit specification for Leclerc beginning in Free Practice 2 on Thursday, triggering the mandatory grid penalty that drops him to last regardless of his qualifying result. The old unit ran in FP1. The new one ran from FP2 onwards. The result: second-fastest time of the session with a 1m41.367s, just 0.074 seconds behind Lewis Hamilton.

The New Unit Performance

Ferrari's internal data pointed to the new power unit delivering approximately 0.2 seconds per lap over the previous specification. Thursday's FP2 result validated that claim. Leclerc was not just competitive — he was second-fastest in a session where both McLarens were struggling and most of the field was working on race setup rather than single-lap pace.

The confirmation that the engine is performing as expected changes the calculus around Leclerc's Baku race. He will start last. But he will start last in a Ferrari that may be faster in a straight line than almost anything else on the grid.

Why Baku Is the Right Circuit to Take a Penalty

Ferrari's decision to introduce the new unit at the Azerbaijan Grand Prix rather than a tighter street circuit or a high-downforce venue is strategic. The Baku City Circuit features the longest main straight in Formula 1 at 2.2 kilometers. Under the 2026 regulations — which reduced downforce levels significantly compared to previous generations — straight-line speed and energy deployment become even more decisive than in prior years.

The same penalty taken at Monaco or Singapore would almost certainly mean a lonely race at the back. In Baku, it means a recovery drive with every overtaking opportunity the calendar can offer.

What's Next

Leclerc will qualify on Friday, establishing his pace and potentially setting up an interesting tactical scenario if he qualifies near the top before being dropped to the back. The race is on Saturday. Watch for his opening lap aggression on the long straight and how quickly Ferrari can bring him through the field from a last-place start.
