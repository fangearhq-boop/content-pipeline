Norris Hits the Wall in Baku FP2 as McLaren's Championship Crisis Deepens

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | September 24, 2026

Lando Norris needed Baku to be the start of a turnaround. He needed McLaren's circuit-specific upgrade to work, clean laps, and the kind of gap-closing performance that keeps a championship mathematically alive. He got a wall hit and a broken suspension instead.

Norris contacted the barriers at Turn 4 in Free Practice 2, losing the rear of his MCL41 and smashing into the wall hard enough to break his left-rear suspension. His session ended there in 10th place. Oscar Piastri added another McLaren wall moment at Turn 15, finishing 12th.

The Upgrade That Isn't Working

McLaren arrived in Baku with a package specifically targeting the second sector of the circuit. The intention was to gain ground on the competition at a circuit where points are available. The reality was both cars struggling for grip under braking throughout the session, with the instability culminating in two separate barrier contacts.

When a circuit-specific upgrade creates a braking grip problem severe enough that both drivers hit the walls in the same session, the engineering team has a significant overnight puzzle to solve before qualifying.

The Championship Numbers

Norris is fourth in the Drivers' Championship with 186 points, trailing Kimi Antonelli by 106. There are 233 points left to be awarded across the remaining nine rounds of the 2026 season. A perfect second half — winning every race — would put Norris on 419 points. Antonelli, even scoring zero from here, would end on 292. The pure maths still works, technically. But championships aren't won on theoretical perfect runs.

What Norris needed in Baku was a fast car, a clean race weekend, and ground returned. Thursday gave him wall damage and a session he'd rather forget.

What's Next

McLaren's engineers will work overnight to understand the braking instability that affected both cars. Norris's suspension damage needs assessment before qualifying. If the car can be repaired and the setup issue identified, Baku still offers overtaking opportunities — the long straight is one of the best passing zones in the sport. But the Norris title bid can no longer afford more afternoons like this one.
