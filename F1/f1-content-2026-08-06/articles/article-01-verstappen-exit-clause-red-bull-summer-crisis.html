Max Verstappen's Red Bull Exit Clause Is Now Active — The F1 Driver Market's Summer of Chaos Begins

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | August 6, 2026

F1 Driver Market / Red Bull / Max Verstappen

The contract clause that Formula 1 has been watching all season is now officially active. Max Verstappen's performance-based exit clause at Red Bull Racing has been triggered — and the four-time world champion has until October to decide whether to use it.

The clause was negotiated in 2022 by Verstappen's manager, Raymond Vermeulen, as a safety valve against the uncertainties of the major 2026 technical regulation changes. The trigger condition was specific: if Verstappen was outside the top 2 in the Drivers' Championship at the summer break, he would have the contractual right to leave Red Bull before his deal expired.

After a DNF at the British Grand Prix, Verstappen entered the summer break in P6 with 109 points — well outside the top 2, and with no mathematical path to climb there before the break. The clause is active.

The Numbers That Tell the Story

Verstappen's 109 points after 11 races represents a dramatic fall from the trajectory that made him the dominant force in Formula 1 for four consecutive seasons. Championship leader Kimi Antonelli sits at 219 points. Second-placed Lewis Hamilton has approximately 169. Verstappen is 110 points off the lead with 11 races remaining — a deficit that, in the current competitive landscape, would require an extraordinary reversal of Red Bull's form to bridge.

The Hungarian Grand Prix — the final race before the summer break — was particularly symbolic. Norris won for McLaren. Verstappen finished second. Antonelli, his notional teammate's equal in the other championship-leading car, was third. The gap at the front of the grid between where Verstappen is and where the championship is being decided has rarely felt wider.

What the Clause Means in Practice

Verstappen has a window — from now through October 2026 — to formally trigger the exit clause and initiate a departure from Red Bull. This would not be immediate; the mechanics of contract releases in Formula 1 are complex, and any move would need to be structured around seat availability for 2027.

The names already circulating in the paddock as potential destinations: Mercedes and McLaren. Both have the competitive infrastructure, the budget, and the ambition to accommodate a driver of Verstappen's caliber. Both are also running championship-contending machinery in 2026 — a fact that would have been unthinkable when he signed his current Red Bull deal.

Red Bull's internal response has been complicated. Sources describe leadership as "split" on how to handle the situation — caught between wanting to retain their defining star of the past four years and managing the reality that the team's competitive standing makes retention difficult without credible promises about the 2026-27 car development trajectory.

Verstappen's Silence

What Verstappen has not done is publicly commit to staying at Red Bull for 2027. In a paddock where driver announcements routinely precede their formal contractual basis by months, that silence is its own statement. He has remained professionally appropriate in public comments — praising the team, focusing on performance — but the absence of a clear "I'm staying" from either him or the team speaks volumes.

The clause Vermeulen negotiated in 2022 was prescient. At the time, it looked like a theoretical protection against a scenario most observers considered unlikely. Four years later, it is not theoretical at all.

What's Next

The Dutch Grand Prix at Zandvoort on August 21-23 is the next race after the summer break — and it has historically been a home game for Verstappen and his devoted Dutch fan base. The contrast between that atmosphere and the contractual uncertainty swirling around him will be one of the storylines of the season's second half.

Watch for movement before the Dutch GP. In F1 silly season, the summer break is when deals are made — quietly, and then announced loudly. Verstappen's decision will trigger a cascade across the grid that reshapes 2027 before a single lap of the second half has been driven.
