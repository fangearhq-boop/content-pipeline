Ferrari's Final Major Upgrade: New Floor and Sidepods Head to Austin as 2027 Development Takes Over

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | October 16, 2026

Ferrari is heading to the 2026 United States Grand Prix with its most significant aerodynamic upgrade package of the season's second half — and it may be the last meaningful development work the SF-26 sees before the team's full attention shifts to 2027.

The package, confirmed for deployment at Circuit of the Americas, includes a new floor design, revised sidepod bodywork, and some weight reduction. It represents Ferrari's closing argument in the 2026 Constructors' Championship — and the Scuderia needs it to work.

Why This Package Matters

Ferrari sits second in the Constructors' standings with 444 points, 134 behind Mercedes. McLaren is third with 339. The gap to Mercedes is substantial but not insurmountable across six remaining races — if Ferrari can find meaningful performance with this package and convert it into podiums and wins.

Charles Leclerc finished second at the Singapore Grand Prix, finishing 7.3 seconds behind Verstappen and ahead of Antonelli. Lewis Hamilton, Ferrari's other driver, needs consistent results to hold his third place in the Drivers' standings. Both of those stories play into why Austin matters beyond the title narrative at the front.

The SF-26 has been praised internally as the fastest-developing car on the grid in 2026. McLaren's own technical director acknowledged Ferrari's development rate when asked about the midfield battle. The floor and sidepod package at COTA is the culmination of that development arc.

The 2027 Handoff

After Austin, the SF-26's development story is effectively over. The $135 million cost cap that governs Formula 1 teams puts hard limits on how much resource can be deployed simultaneously across two car programs. Ferrari has signaled that its engineering priorities are shifting to the 2027 car, which will run under revised regulations.

That makes the Austin package Ferrari's last swing. If the floor and sidepod revision delivers the expected performance gain, it could allow the team to apply pressure to Mercedes in the Constructors' race during the season's final five rounds. If it underperforms, Ferrari accepts the gap and banks its chips on 2027.

Circuit of the Americas: Why It Suits the SF-26

COTA is a high-downforce circuit — the 20-turn, 5.513-kilometer layout at Austin demands strong mechanical grip, aerodynamic efficiency, and the ability to manage tyre wear over 56 laps. Ferrari's development philosophy in 2026 has leaned into high-downforce performance, making COTA a circuit where the new floor package could generate meaningful laptime improvement.

The track's famous Turn 1 — an uphill hairpin that launches the cars over a crest before the tight left-hand apex — places specific demands on how a car loads into a corner. A revised floor and sidepod profile that improves front-to-rear aero balance could make a real difference across those opening sectors.

What's Next

Ferrari will run the new floor and sidepod package through all three practice sessions at COTA — October 23, 24, and 25 — to collect data ahead of qualifying. Saturday's qualifying (October 24) will give the clearest indication of whether the upgrade has delivered the expected performance step.

The race is October 25. Ferrari has everything to gain — and one last major package to do it with.
