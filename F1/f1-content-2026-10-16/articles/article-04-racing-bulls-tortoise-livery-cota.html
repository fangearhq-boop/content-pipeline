Racing Bulls Go Tortoise at COTA: Team Reveals Second Special Livery of the 2026 Season

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | October 16, 2026

Racing Bulls are arriving at the 2026 United States Grand Prix with a look that stands apart from everything else on the Circuit of the Americas grid.

The Faenza-based team has revealed a special one-weekend livery for Austin — a striking "tortoise" color scheme that replaces their standard design for the duration of the COTA weekend. It marks the second alternate one-off look Racing Bulls have run in 2026, following their Miami special earlier in the season.

The Design

The tortoise livery takes Racing Bulls' visual identity in a distinctive direction. The scheme is a deliberate departure from the team's regular look — one designed to make them one of the most visually recognizable cars in Austin's paddock and on the circuit during race weekend.

Racing Bulls have established a pattern of one-weekend special designs at high-profile North American events. Their Miami alternate livery earlier in 2026 drew significant attention and engagement across social media. The COTA tortoise scheme continues that approach at one of the season's most-attended races.

The Team's Context

Racing Bulls — the Red Bull junior team, also serving as a development pipeline for younger drivers — bring Yuki Tsunoda and Isack Hadjar to Austin. Tsunoda, the experienced lead driver, has accumulated strong points totals through 2026 while Hadjar has developed in his first full F1 season.

COTA's 56-lap layout, with its mix of high-speed sections, tight infield complexes, and elevation changes, suits cars that can manage tyre degradation over a long race. Racing Bulls have shown the ability to score points on mixed-strategy weekends — and a distinctive livery can only help their visibility in a field where championship drama at the front tends to draw all the attention.

Special Liveries in Modern F1

The trend of team-specific one-weekend special liveries has grown across the grid. Several teams now treat North American race weekends — particularly Austin, Miami, and Las Vegas — as opportunities to run alternate designs that attract new audiences and generate social content outside of the performance headlines.

For a midfield team competing with less raw pace than the championship contenders, a memorable visual identity at a major event can be one of the most effective ways to stand out in a busy news cycle.

What's Next

The tortoise livery makes its on-track debut during practice on October 23 at Circuit of the Americas. Qualifying is October 24, with the race on October 25. Racing Bulls will be among the most watched cars in the Austin paddock — not just for their performance, but for the look they bring to the Texas sun.
