Formula 1's 2027 season is going to look very different — and Stefano Domenicali confirmed it today.

For the season opener, Bahrain and Saudi Arabia remain the target in mid-March, though Middle East contingency plans are in place given the ongoing regional conflict.

2026 already saw two rounds cancelled (Bahrain and Saudi Arabia) due to the situation, leaving a 22-race calendar. The plan for 2027 is to restore full strength — and go further.

### STORY 3: Williams Baku Upgrade

**Long-Form Post**
Window: 12:00 PM ET

Williams are banking everything on Baku — and even their own drivers aren't sure it'll pay off.

Team principal James Vowles has been building anticipation for the Azerbaijan Grand Prix upgrade all season, at one point describing the package as "almost an entirely new car." It's Williams' biggest single development push of 2026, and it arrives at a circuit — the long-power-sensitive Baku streets — that at least gives them a fighting chance.

The catch: neither Alex Albon nor Carlos Sainz has been willing to endorse the hype. Albon has admitted the upgrade "won't fix" their 2026 season. Sainz has issued a "stark warning" not to expect a rescue package.

The backstory matters here. Williams missed the January Barcelona shakedown due to production delays — a decision that cost them crucial development time at the start of the new 2026 regulation cycle. The team that won multiple podiums in 2025 now sits ninth in the Constructors' Championship with 11 points, none since Monaco in June.

Baku is their biggest shot. They're just not promising anything.

Ferrari needs a strong Baku. McLaren needs more of the same. Either way, this fight is far from over.
