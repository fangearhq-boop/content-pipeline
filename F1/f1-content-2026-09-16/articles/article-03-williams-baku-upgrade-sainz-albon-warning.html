Williams Bring "Almost an Entirely New Car" to Baku — But Sainz and Albon Aren't Promising Much

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | September 16, 2026

Williams have talked up their Azerbaijan Grand Prix upgrade package all season. Team principal James Vowles even called it "almost an entirely new car." But as Baku approaches on September 26, the team's own drivers have delivered a consistent message: temper your expectations.

Alex Albon has said the upgrade won't be enough to "fix" their 2026 season. Carlos Sainz, in his first year with the team, has issued what reporters described as a "stark warning" — the Baku package isn't the rescue mission it was built up to be. When two experienced drivers align on skepticism before a major upgrade even arrives at the circuit, it's worth paying attention.

What Went Wrong in 2026

Williams' struggles this season trace directly to a single decision: skipping the January Barcelona shakedown due to production delays. The 2026 season brought sweeping new regulations, and every lap run in pre-season testing was development currency. Williams spent theirs late.

The team that won multiple podiums in 2025 has scored just 11 points in 2026, none since the Monaco Grand Prix in June. They sit ninth in the Constructors' Championship. The gap between their early-season pace and the midfield has defined their year.

Why Baku at All?

The choice to time the big upgrade for Baku isn't random. The Azerbaijan circuit is one of the longest-straight tracks on the calendar, which means raw top speed matters more than aerodynamic efficiency. A new aero package that improves downforce without hurting straight-line speed — or a chassis update that changes mechanical balance — could show positive results at Baku even if the car still struggles at conventional circuits.

That logic explains why Vowles chose to hype the package for this specific venue. It also explains why Albon and Sainz aren't getting excited: Baku results don't always translate elsewhere on the calendar.

What's Next

The Azerbaijan Grand Prix weekend runs September 24-26, with the race on Saturday. Watch for Williams' pace in free practice — it will be the first real indicator of whether the upgrade delivers or disappoints. If the car shows a genuine step forward, the conversation around Williams in the final nine rounds of 2026 changes significantly. If it doesn't, the postmortem on their season will begin early.
