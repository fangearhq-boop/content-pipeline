Lewis Hamilton's 2026 Title Is Gone — But Baku Offers Something Else

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | September 16, 2026

Lewis Hamilton is 101 points behind Kimi Antonelli in the 2026 Drivers' Championship with nine races left. The mathematics of catching that gap are essentially impossible. Hamilton's first season at Ferrari — the dream move that was supposed to deliver him a record-breaking eighth world title — has not gone as planned.

What remains is something different: the chance to win, to show the paddock what Ferrari and Hamilton can do together, and to play a meaningful role in keeping McLaren from overtaking Ferrari in the Constructors' Championship. None of those things are nothing. The next race is Baku, and it's a circuit that historically suits Hamilton's style.

Why Baku Could Change the Conversation

The Azerbaijan Grand Prix is built around a six-kilometre layout that features the longest straight in Formula 1 — nearly two kilometres of flat-out acceleration along Baku's Caspian Sea shoreline. Ferrari's power unit has historically been competitive in straight-line speed, and the circuit's combination of high-energy corners and long straights places a premium on the kind of car characteristics Ferrari has been developing throughout the season.

Hamilton, meanwhile, has shown he can drive this track. His history at Baku includes strong qualifying performances and an ability to manage tire life through the demanding old-town section. A race weekend where the Ferrari is genuinely competitive at Baku would give Hamilton something tangible to build on ahead of the final eight rounds of the season.

The Bigger Picture: Ferrari vs. McLaren

Hamilton's individual title ambitions are over, but the team fight isn't. Ferrari holds second place in the Constructors' Championship with a 52-point lead over McLaren — a gap that has been shrinking. Every point Hamilton scores in Baku and beyond is a point that either protects that position or helps recover from further ground lost.

For Ferrari, the constructors' title is now the primary seasonal objective. Hamilton is central to that mission. His motivation to win in Baku is real, even if the reason has shifted.

What's Next

The Azerbaijan Grand Prix weekend runs September 24-26, with the race on Saturday — moved from Sunday to avoid a clash with Azerbaijan Remembrance Day. Free practice sessions begin Thursday. Watch for Hamilton's qualifying performance on Friday as the first indicator of whether Ferrari has the pace at this circuit to challenge for podiums or wins. After a difficult season, Baku is where the narrative could turn.
