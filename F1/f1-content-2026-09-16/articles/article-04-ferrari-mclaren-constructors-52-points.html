52 Points and Closing — McLaren's Constructors' Surge Is Ferrari's New Nightmare

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | September 16, 2026

Ferrari's second-place position in the 2026 Formula 1 Constructors' Championship is no longer comfortable. McLaren have reduced the gap to just 52 points after the Spanish Grand Prix in Madrid — down from 59 points after the Italian Grand Prix — and with nine rounds remaining, the Prancing Horse needs to respond or risk surrendering the runner-up prize to Woking.

Mercedes is long gone in first, holding a 145-point advantage and closing in on a dominant constructor's title. But the battle for second carries its own prestige: prize money, sponsor value, and the narrative heading into the 2027 regulation cycle all hinge on finishing ahead of McLaren.

How McLaren Closed the Gap

The trend line is clear. McLaren have been the better-scoring team across the final stretch of the 2026 season's first half. Their car development arc — a recovery mission after their 2025 constructors' champion year ran out of momentum under the new regulations — has seen them steadily improve their single-lap pace and race pace from the midpoint of the season onward.

Ferrari, by contrast, has had more variance. Lewis Hamilton's title challenge stalled, and Charles Leclerc's results have been inconsistent in the second tier of the championship. The combination hasn't added up to enough points to stave off the orange threat.

What Baku Means for This Battle

The Azerbaijan Grand Prix on September 26 is a critical flashpoint for both teams. The Baku circuit rewards power and straight-line speed — a profile that has historically suited Ferrari's engine characteristics. If Ferrari can outscore McLaren in Azerbaijan, the gap holds or extends. If McLaren have the stronger weekend, the 52-point margin narrows further and the P2 race becomes genuinely tense through the final eight rounds.

What's Next

Nine rounds remain, which means a theoretical maximum swing of around 200 points is still possible. Ferrari cannot afford to give McLaren another positive weekend without a strong response. Watch how both teams deploy strategy and driver orders in Baku — with the constructors' fight this tight, every point matters, and neither team will be managing results conservatively.
