Domenicali Confirms Radical 2027 F1 Calendar — 24 Races, 10 Sprints, Monaco Gets a Sprint for the First Time

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | September 16, 2026

Formula 1 CEO Stefano Domenicali confirmed Tuesday that the 2027 season will feature 24 grand prix and 10 sprint races — the most ambitious deployment of the sprint format in the sport's history. Among the new sprint venues: Monaco, a circuit so tight that it has historically been a near-processional race on Sundays and is now set to host a standalone sprint for the first time ever.

Domenicali cited the desire to bring "intensity" to the schedule as the driving force behind the expansion. The full calendar is expected to be presented formally in the autumn, with Bahrain and Saudi Arabia targeted as the season-opening back-to-back in mid-March.

Monaco: The Unlikely Sprint Venue

Of all the tracks on the F1 calendar, Monaco has historically been the most resistant to exciting racing. Its narrow streets and near-impossibility of overtaking have made qualifying the de facto main event for decades. Adding a sprint race there raises immediate questions about format and fan value — but also about whether the format's structure might unlock something the traditional Sunday race cannot.

Sprint races run on fresh tyres over shorter distances, often producing more position changes than the longer Sunday grand prix. At Monaco, where track position is everything and tire strategies are constrained by geography, a sprint format might actually deliver more action than the race that follows it on Sunday.

Middle East Contingencies Remain in Place

The 2026 season lost two rounds — Bahrain and Saudi Arabia — due to the ongoing conflict in the Middle East, resulting in a 22-race calendar. The 2027 plan aims to restore full strength at 24 rounds, but Domenicali was careful to note that contingency plans are ready "with different options" that will maintain a 24-race target even if regional conditions require adjustments.

The aspiration to return to Bahrain and Saudi in mid-March keeps the traditional season-opening format intact, though nothing is confirmed until the autumn calendar release.

What's Next

The formal 2027 calendar presentation is expected before the end of the year. Teams and fans will be watching closely for which circuits were added or removed to accommodate the expanded sprint schedule, and whether the Monaco Sprint format is confirmed as a standalone weekend element. The 2026 season continues with the Azerbaijan Grand Prix on September 26 — one of the existing sprint weekends on the current calendar.
