Antonelli's P16 to P5 in Baku: The Championship Masterclass Nobody Expected

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | September 30, 2026

When Kimi Antonelli lined up 16th on the Baku grid due to a grid penalty, the question wasn't whether he'd lose ground. The question was how much.

The answer: remarkably little. Antonelli drove from P16 to fifth in one of the most composed damage-limitation performances of his brief but extraordinary Formula 1 career, emerging from Baku with his championship lead reduced only from 81 points to 66.

Reading the Race

While drivers around him crashed, retired, and made mistakes, Antonelli kept his car clean and collected the points available to him. Six other drivers retired from the race, which naturally brought him forward, but Antonelli's pace was also genuine — he didn't simply inherit positions through attrition.

The championship math after Baku: Antonelli 302 points, Russell 236 (-66), Hamilton 199 (-103), Norris 186 (-116), Leclerc 179 (-123). Eight races remain with approximately 208 maximum points available.

The Number That Matters

If Antonelli maintains his 2026 average of roughly 21 points per race, he'll reach a total that is mathematically unreachable by Russell within the next few rounds. He needs approximately 95 more points to make his lead uncatchable by his teammate.

Grid penalties in championship years can unravel a title contender. Antonelli absorbed this one, drove a flawless recovery, and left Baku in a stronger psychological position than most of his rivals.

History in the Making

If Antonelli secures the title — which could happen as early as the Singapore Grand Prix on October 9-11 — he would become the youngest world champion in Formula 1 history, shattering the record held by Sebastian Vettel. The Bolognese driver only turned 19 during the current season.

What's Next

Antonelli heads to Singapore having survived one of the season's trickiest situations. He's eight races from a championship that, barring something extraordinary, appears to be within his grasp. At 19 years old, he's handling it like a veteran.
