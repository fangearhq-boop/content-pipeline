COTA 2026: How the New F1 Rules Change America's Race

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | October 21, 2026

The 2026 United States Grand Prix at the Circuit of the Americas is not the same race it was a year ago. Two significant changes have altered the technical picture heading into Sunday's event, and understanding them is essential to understanding the race.

The first is structural: Formula 1's 2026 technical regulations eliminated the Drag Reduction System and replaced it with a new active aerodynamics framework. The second is circuit-specific: COTA's Turns 9-11 complex has been entirely resurfaced for this season. Together, they make COTA 2026 a genuinely novel challenge for every team and driver in the paddock.

No More DRS — Welcome to Overtake Mode

For more than a decade, DRS defined overtaking battles in Formula 1. Drivers entering a detection zone within one second of the car ahead could open their rear wing flap on the following straight, generating a speed advantage large enough to make a pass.

In 2026, that system is gone. The new active aerodynamic framework operates continuously throughout a lap, with an "Overtake Mode" — a driver-activated reduction in drag equivalent to, and in some estimates more powerful than, the old DRS boost. The key difference is deployment flexibility. Unlike DRS, which was restricted to designated zones, Overtake Mode can be engaged anywhere on the circuit — at the driver's discretion.

At COTA, this changes the calculus for every overtaking attempt. The long back straight between Turn 11 and Turn 12 — historically the primary overtaking zone — remains important, but the hairpin at Turn 12 now sees attacks from different lines and angles as drivers manage their Overtake Mode windows. The tighter sections of the circuit, where DRS never operated, now become potential late-braking ambush points.

Turns 9-11: New Tarmac, New Questions

The COTA corner sequence at Turns 9-11 is one of the circuit's most distinctive features — a flowing, undulating series of medium-speed curves that requires balance, mechanical grip, and confidence. For 2026, COTA has completely resurfaced this section.

New tarmac means different grip characteristics. In the first few sessions of the race weekend, drivers will need to find the new limits of the surface, identifying where grip is gained and where it falls away compared to what they knew from past visits. Lap records from prior years are unreliable guides; the turn entry, apex, and exit behaviors will all have changed to some degree.

For tire management, the implications run deeper. New asphalt tends to be more abrasive initially, generating higher degradation rates as rubber is laid in and the surface settles. For strategists, this introduces a variable that won't be fully understood until long run simulations during Friday practice. The Pirelli compounds selected for Austin — C2 (Hard), C3 (Medium), and C4 (Soft) — were chosen before the extent of the resurfacing work was known to the teams.

What Teams Are Looking For

The most valuable data from Thursday's preview day and Friday's practice sessions won't be single-lap pace. It will be the tire wear numbers from the long runs — how quickly the new tarmac at Turns 9-11 degrades each compound, and whether the resurfaced section favors a one-stop or two-stop race strategy on Sunday.

For teams like Mercedes and Red Bull — who have large simulation and aerodynamic departments — adapting their setup baseline to new tarmac is a routine process. For smaller teams, the uncertainty creates both risk and opportunity. If the resurfaced section changes the car characteristics that COTA historically required, the relative order in practice may look different from past years.

What's Next

Free practice at COTA is more important than usual in 2026. Teams that arrive with last year's setups will need to update them quickly. The resurfaced Turns 9-11 will generate early questions, and the Overtake Mode optimization work will run in every corner analysis session.

By Saturday evening, after qualifying, the full picture will be clearer. But for now — on the eve of the first COTA practice day of the 2026 season — this is a track that Formula 1 is meeting again for the first time.
