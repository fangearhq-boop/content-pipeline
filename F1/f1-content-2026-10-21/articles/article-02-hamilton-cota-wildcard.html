Hamilton at COTA: The Wildcard With a History at America's Circuit

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | October 21, 2026

Lewis Hamilton needs a miracle. He also has a history of creating them at the Circuit of the Americas.

Third in the 2026 F1 standings with 229 points — 111 behind championship leader Kimi Antonelli — Hamilton's title mathematics require a level of chaos that borders on the extraordinary. Antonelli and Russell would both need to stumble repeatedly, and Hamilton would need to convert every opportunity that materialized.

The rational assessment says Hamilton's title is gone. The history at COTA says don't count Lewis out in Austin.

A Circuit That Suits His Style

Hamilton built one of the most dominant records in the history of the United States Grand Prix during his Mercedes career. The Circuit of the Americas, with its flowing first sector and technical third sector, rewards the combination of car balance and tire management that has always been Hamilton's strongest suit.

Now in his first season at Ferrari, Hamilton brings that accumulated knowledge of COTA to a new team, a new car, and a different competitive context. Ferrari has been strong in 2026 — constructors' title challenger, Leclerc on the podium at Singapore — and if the SF-26 suits COTA's demands, Hamilton has the experience to exploit it.

The Chaos Agent

Even setting aside his personal title ambitions, Hamilton's presence at COTA complicates Antonelli's clinch scenario. For Antonelli to secure the championship in Austin, Hamilton must finish fourth or lower. If Hamilton is in a strong position — running in the top three, threatening for the win — the Antonelli clinch cannot happen on Sunday regardless of what Russell does.

In that sense, Hamilton's best race is also the scenario that most effectively delays Antonelli's celebration. The interests of "Hamilton the championship contender" and "Hamilton the chaos agent" align perfectly in Austin.

The Ferrari Dimension

This is Hamilton's first COTA with a Ferrari. The SF-26 has shown genuine top-end pace at multiple circuits this season, and Leclerc's performances suggest the car can qualify and race near the front. If Ferrari's setup work through free practice unlocks the circuit's technical demands, Hamilton could arrive at qualifying Saturday as a genuine front-row contender.

A Hamilton on pole at COTA in a Ferrari would be one of the most compelling qualifying stories of the season.

What's Next

The 2026 United States Grand Prix starts for real in practice this week. Hamilton will spend those sessions learning the resurfaced Turns 9-11, calibrating his feel for Overtake Mode at COTA's overtaking zones, and looking for the setup window that makes the Ferrari competitive against Mercedes and Red Bull at altitude in Texas heat.

His title is probably gone. His moment in Austin is still very much possible.

That's the thing about Lewis Hamilton. You don't put him in a box until the car stops.
