Max Verstappen and Red Bull: The Contradiction Nobody in F1 Can Explain

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | August 3, 2026

On the same week in July, two credible Formula 1 outlets published directly opposite conclusions about Max Verstappen's future.

SportBible reported that Verstappen was in the "closing stages" of a major move away from Red Bull. GrandPrix.com ran the headline: "Exit clause closes as Verstappen commits to Red Bull." Both outlets cited sources. Both presented their findings with confidence.

Both cannot be true. And yet here we are — in August, 18 days before the Dutch Grand Prix, with no resolution and no clarity on the single biggest driver question in Formula 1.

What We Know for Certain

Verstappen's exit clause is active. He wrote the clause into his Red Bull contract at his own insistence — specifically because of uncertainty about how the 2026 regulation changes would affect the team's competitiveness. The clause triggers if he is outside the top two in the championship after the summer break. He is currently seventh. The clause is active from now until October.

Red Bull has zero wins in eleven races in 2026. They have one of the most successful organizations in the sport's history and none of their engineering advantages of the 2022-2025 era have transferred to the ground-effect era. Verstappen called them "the fourth team" — behind Mercedes, Ferrari, and McLaren. That was not hyperbole. The data supports it.

He is not happy at Red Bull. That much is not in dispute.

The Competing Narratives

Red Bull adviser Helmut Marko has publicly stated that Verstappen is staying. Marko has also been Red Bull's most visible advocate for the relationship at every moment of tension over the past four seasons, which means his public position and the internal reality may not align.

McLaren CEO Zak Brown publicly denied any talks with Verstappen. McLaren already has Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri locked in — Piastri through 2028. Adding Verstappen would require removing one of them, which Brown has indicated is not the plan. The public denials from Brown carry more weight than a typical "no comment" because they are specific and repeated.

A single-source report linked Verstappen's race engineer to early discussions with McLaren. Single-source reports during the silly season carry inherent risk. They are often accurate and often wrong in equal measure.

Red Bull reportedly offered Verstappen approximately €65 million per year through 2029, on the condition that the exit clause be removed. That offer would make him the highest-paid driver in the sport. Verstappen has not publicly responded. No deadline on the offer has been announced. Whether he is considering it, dismissing it, or using it as leverage for a third option is unknown.

The Dutch GP as Possible Inflection Point

The Dutch Grand Prix on August 21 is the first race back from the summer break and one of the most emotionally charged events of the 2026 calendar. Zandvoort is Verstappen's home circuit. He has won the Dutch GP three times since its return in 2021. This is the final-ever F1 race at Zandvoort — the circuit leaves the calendar after 2026.

The combination of factors — his home crowd, the farewell weekend, his unresolved future, and the first race back after weeks of silence — makes Zandvoort a natural moment for an announcement. Whether Verstappen uses it that way is entirely his call. He has demonstrated throughout his career that he moves on his own schedule and announces on his own terms.

What the Dutch GP cannot avoid: every microphone in the paddock will ask the same question. Every press conference will contain it. Every post-race interview will circle it. Verstappen will be forced to respond, even if his response is to say nothing meaningful.

What's Next

The October deadline is the hard boundary. Before it, Verstappen can trigger the clause and leave with no compensation to Red Bull. After it, he is contractually obligated through the end of his existing deal. The Dutch GP on August 21, followed by the Italian Grand Prix at Monza in early September, are the two races between now and when the paddock pressure to have an answer becomes genuinely difficult to resist. Whether Verstappen chooses clarity before then — or forces the soap opera deeper into the season — is the question that will define F1's silly season. Whatever he decides will shift the driver market for every team below the McLaren front row.
