Goodbye Zandvoort: The Last Dutch Grand Prix Is a Sprint — and the Perfect Sendoff

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | August 3, 2026

When the checkered flag falls in Zandvoort on Sunday, August 23, it will fall for the last time in Formula 1 history at that circuit.

The Dutch Grand Prix is back from the summer break as the season's first post-break event. It is also, officially and finally, the end of Zandvoort's run on the calendar. The track was brought back to F1 in 2021 after decades away — largely because Max Verstappen had made the Netherlands one of the most passionate F1 nations on earth — and five seasons later, it leaves.

To mark the occasion, Formula 1 is staging the first-ever Sprint weekend at Zandvoort. The circuit has never hosted a Sprint before. It ends with one.

The Format for the Final Weekend

The Dutch Grand Prix weekend runs from August 21 to 23. The schedule:

Friday, August 21: Free Practice 1 and Sprint Qualifying. Saturday, August 22: The Sprint race at noon CET, followed by Grand Prix Qualifying. Sunday, August 23: The main Dutch Grand Prix.

The Sprint format adds competitive running on Saturday that Zandvoort has never had before — a short race over roughly one-third of the Grand Prix distance, with points awarded to the top eight finishers. It gives the farewell weekend an intensity that a normal race format would not provide.

It also gives championship implications earlier in the weekend. Antonelli leads Hamilton by 50 points. The Sprint is a live championship event.

What Zandvoort Became

When Zandvoort returned to the calendar in 2021, it was an experiment in atmosphere. Dutch fans had waited decades for a home race for their national hero. They filled the grandstands in orange, stood in tifo-style sections, generated noise more commonly associated with football than motorsport.

What they produced was one of the most electric environments in Formula 1. Zandvoort became a destination. Not for the track layout alone — it is a technically demanding circuit but a relatively short one — but for what the crowd created around it. F1 discovered that the Dutch fans' intensity was a feature, not a novelty.

Verstappen won the Dutch Grand Prix in 2021, 2022, and 2023. Three consecutive home victories before the calendar and competitive dynamics shifted. The circuit was his stage and he delivered on it.

The Exit Clause Backdrop

The final Dutch Grand Prix arrives at the most uncertain moment of Verstappen's career since he joined Red Bull's senior team. His exit clause is active. He called Red Bull "the fourth team." There are contradictory reports about whether he is leaving or staying. The October deadline is eight weeks away.

This is the circuit where he was treated as a deity. These are the fans who wore his orange and chanted his name. And the home crowd will be watching a driver whose future is, for the first time in years, genuinely unclear.

If this is Verstappen's last Dutch Grand Prix in a Red Bull car, the circuit could not have written a more fitting dramatic frame. If he announces his future at Zandvoort — in either direction — it will happen in front of the most emotionally invested crowd in the sport.

What's Next

The Dutch Grand Prix, August 21-23, is the circuit's finale — and F1's first competitive weekend after the summer break. The championship picture has Antonelli leading Hamilton by 50 points, with 291 available from the remaining races. A Sprint on Saturday gives the standings an early jolt. Whatever happens with Verstappen, the racing and the atmosphere will be genuine. Zandvoort's farewell deserves everything it is about to receive.
