Alonso's Summer Break Decision: The B-Spec, the Adrenaline, and the Question Nobody Can Answer

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | August 3, 2026

Ted Kravitz says Fernando Alonso will retire. Spanish insiders say the decision has already been made. And then Alonso got out of a competitive car for the first time in 2026 and told the world he had missed the adrenaline.

Those three words may have changed everything. Or they may have changed nothing. That is, essentially, where Fernando Alonso's Formula 1 future stands as the summer break begins.

Alonso is 44 years old. His Aston Martin contract expires at the end of the 2026 season. He is not expected to announce any decision until after the break. The Dutch Grand Prix on August 21 is when the paddock reconvenes — and with it, the pressure to have an answer.

What the B-Spec Changed

For the first fifteen races of the 2026 season, Alonso spent most of his weekends in Q1. The AMR26 was, by the team's own admission, a car that simply did not suit the demands of the new regulations. It was unpredictable, difficult to position, and unable to generate the front-end confidence Alonso has spent his career demanding from a race car.

Then the B-spec arrived at Hungary. Sixteen new components. Only the front suspension, engine, and gearbox carried over from the previous specification. The team stayed through the curfew to install it. When Alonso drove it in practice, the feedback was immediate.

"More glued to the ground, more predictable, easier to drive," he said. He reached Q2 for the first time all season. He finished 14th in the race, which is not a spectacular result — but the qualifying performance was the data point that mattered.

And then: "That adrenaline was missed."

That sentence did not come from a team press release. It came from a driver who has spent most of 2026 hiding his frustration behind diplomacy. Hearing him say he missed the feeling of competing — the actual feeling of the car responding to his inputs the way a competitive car responds — was a signal that the B-spec had reached something in him that the calendar year had not.

What Kravitz and the Spanish Insiders Say

Sky Sports' Ted Kravitz, one of the paddock's most connected observers, stated his belief plainly: Alonso will retire. Not because of the car — the B-spec changed the car calculus. But because, in Kravitz's assessment, Alonso "wants to race in other stuff" and "isn't really enjoying it anymore" at the Formula 1 level.

That framing — fatigue not with speed but with the F1 ecosystem — is a characterization of a man who has competed at the absolute apex of motorsport for over two decades. The politics, the scrutiny, the constant travel, the weight of expectation: Kravitz's read is that Alonso's appetite for all of that has run out even if his appetite for the driving itself has not.

Spanish insiders add a layer of specificity: according to multiple reports, the decision has already been made internally. Alonso knows what he is doing next. The announcement is simply a matter of timing.

But Alonso himself has said only that he will assess Aston Martin's competitiveness and his own desire. Both of those assessments take on a different shape after the B-spec.

What Comes Next — For Alonso and for Aston Martin

Aston Martin is bringing the B-spec's second development step to Zandvoort for the Dutch Grand Prix. Honda is also delivering a power unit upgrade to the same race. Two simultaneous upgrades at a circuit Alonso has historically enjoyed. If that combination delivers Q3 — or even a points finish in Q2 — the retirement calculus changes again.

Aston Martin, for their part, need to know what Alonso is doing. They cannot build their 2027 season with a vacant seat and no plan. If he goes, they need to move quickly in a market where every available seat has three credible candidates. If he stays, they need to build the car around a driver who has now said publicly he rediscovered his drive.

The summer break is when the answer forms. The Dutch GP is when the paddock reconvenes and everyone starts asking the question again. Whether Alonso announces in Zandvoort, at Monza, or at the end of the season is less important than the fact that the B-spec may have delayed the inevitable by weeks — or changed it entirely.

What's Next

Alonso's next on-track moment will be at Zandvoort on August 21, where both the B-spec step 2 and a Honda PU upgrade will give him a more complete data set to evaluate. If the upgrades deliver genuine midfield competitiveness, expect the retirement story to quiet down. If they disappoint, the narrative accelerates. Alonso has said he'll speak when he's ready. The sport, as it always does, will wait — and watch.
