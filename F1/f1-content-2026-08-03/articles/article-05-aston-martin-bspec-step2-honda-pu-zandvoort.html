Aston Martin's Double Upgrade at Zandvoort: B-Spec Step 2 and Honda PU Together

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | August 3, 2026

At the Hungarian Grand Prix, Aston Martin debuted a B-spec upgrade that was, by their own description, a near-complete redesign of the aerodynamic components on the AMR26. Sixteen new parts. The result: Fernando Alonso in Q2 for the first time in the entire 2026 season.

The team said from the beginning that Hungary was the first step. Zandvoort, on August 21, is step two.

And Honda — which supplies Aston Martin's power unit — is also bringing a PU upgrade to the Dutch Grand Prix.

Two separate development upgrades, arriving at the same race. That is an unusual convergence. It is also, for a team that spent the first half of 2026 struggling to find competitive form, a potentially transformative moment.

What the B-Spec Actually Delivered

The B-spec's first appearance at Hungary told a specific story. Alonso went from a car that had failed to reach Q2 in any of the preceding eleven races to one that delivered a genuine Q2 performance in its first weekend. The descriptors he used afterward were precise: more predictable, more glued to the ground, more responsive to input.

These are not aesthetic preferences. They are the qualities a driver needs to push a car to its absolute limit in qualifying — to find the last tenth, the last hundredth, that separates Q2 from Q3. For Alonso, a driver whose mechanical sensitivity is one of the most refined in the sport's history, the change in character was material.

The race result (14th) reflected remaining limitations in race pace and tire management. But qualifying pace is where the development direction shows first. Hungary said: the direction is correct.

What Step 2 Could Add

The B-spec's second development iteration is not a wholesale redesign of a redesign. Teams typically use "step 2" language to describe targeted refinements — specific aerodynamic areas where wind tunnel or CFD work identified additional gain from the foundation laid by the initial package.

If step 1 moved Aston Martin from Q1 to Q2 at Hungary, step 2 is aimed at pushing further into Q2 and potentially reaching the Q3 barrier. That barrier — the top ten in qualifying — represents the difference between scoring mid-table points reliably and competing for podium positions when circumstances align.

The Honda PU upgrade adds a separate dimension. Aston Martin's relationship with Honda is the energy source for their long-term ambitions. Honda committed to F1 through 2030 and has invested heavily in developing a power unit that can challenge the front runners. A mid-season update brings improved combustion efficiency, energy deployment, or both — the specific parameters are not publicly announced ahead of deployment.

Combined, the two upgrades address both ends of the car's performance equation simultaneously. Chassis aerodynamics and power output improving together at the same race is an opportunity Aston Martin's engineers have been targeting since the B-spec's Hungary debut.

Why This Matters Beyond the Track

The upgrade at Zandvoort also feeds directly into Fernando Alonso's summer break decision. He said the B-spec brought back adrenaline he had missed. If step 2 and the Honda upgrade produce a car capable of Q3, Alonso has different data to weigh when he considers his 2027 options.

A driver who is fighting at the front of the midfield with legitimate Q3 potential is engaged in a way that a driver marooned in Q1 cannot be. Alonso said himself that the competitive feeling returned at Hungary. Whether Zandvoort confirms or deepens that feeling could be the moment his retirement calculation shifts.

What's Next

Zandvoort's race weekend begins August 21 with FP1 and Sprint Qualifying. The Sprint on Saturday morning will give the team an early read on the combined upgrade package's performance relative to rivals. The Grand Prix Qualifying on Saturday afternoon — where Alonso's Q2 result at Hungary benchmarked the B-spec step 1 — is where the clearest measurement of step 2 will come. If Aston Martin qualifies in Q3, the team's second-half season changes fundamentally. If they're consistently in Q2, they've still taken a meaningful stride from where they began. Either way, Zandvoort is the first real test of whether Aston Martin's technical reset can hold through the second half of a championship season.
