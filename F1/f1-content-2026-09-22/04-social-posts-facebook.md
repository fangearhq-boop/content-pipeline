### STORY 1: Leclerc Engine Penalty

**FB Long-Form (8:00 AM ET)**
Charles Leclerc is heading for a grid penalty at the 2026 Azerbaijan Grand Prix — and it may be the best possible scenario for Ferrari given the circumstances.

The 2026 Italian Grand Prix at Monza ended badly for Leclerc. He crashed heavily at the high-speed Parabolica corner, and the brand-new power unit in his SF-26 — part of Ferrari's second ADUO allocation — sustained enough damage that it is now deemed unsuitable for qualifying and race use. The engine can run in practice sessions, but for anything high-stakes, it is a liability.

Ferrari team principal Fred Vasseur has been clear about the calculus: taking the penalty at Baku, where the 2.2-kilometre main straight and multiple overtaking zones give a driver the best chance of recovering from a grid drop, is preferable to carrying the problem into Singapore or Japan, where street circuits are far less forgiving of rearward grid positions.

Vasseur himself called Baku "quite suitable" for absorbing the penalty. Leclerc has been even more direct — he says a penalty is "probably" coming "at some point." With Baku's race scheduled for Saturday, September 26, and the details expected to be confirmed before then, this week is when Ferrari makes its decision.

### STORY 2: Williams FW48 2.0

**FB Long-Form (10:00 AM ET)**
Williams Racing is arriving in Baku with what team principal James Vowles has called an "almost completely new" car — and it could be the most important weekend in the team's recovery arc under his leadership.

The FW48 2.0, which debuts at the 2026 Azerbaijan Grand Prix, features a revolutionary new carbon manufacturing process developed over several months of intensive work at the Williams factory in Grove. The original FW48 had significant weight problems when it first appeared on track; this new chassis is designed to resolve those structural issues while also forming the technical foundation for the 2027 Williams.

Vowles has been careful to manage expectations. He has publicly stated that the upgrade "won't be enough for points" in Baku — the midfield is too competitive for a debut performance to immediately translate into Q3 appearances or points finishes. But that is not the point of bringing it here. The point is that from this moment forward, Williams has a proper car to develop. From Baku through the end of the season and into 2027, everything improves from here.

Drivers Carlos Sainz and Alex Albon finally have the foundation Vowles promised them when they signed.

### STORY 3: Norris Baku Favorite

**FB Long-Form (12:00 PM ET)**
Lando Norris is the man to beat at the 2026 Azerbaijan Grand Prix — and he knows it.

McLaren brought circuit-specific upgrades to Baku, targeting the challenging second sector of the Baku City Circuit where mechanical grip and aerodynamic precision are at a premium. The result is a car that, on pace simulations, looks like the fastest package on the grid for this specific venue. Norris is the market favourite for both pole position and race victory on Saturday.

He also has history here. Norris won the Azerbaijan Grand Prix in both 2024 and 2025, making him the back-to-back Baku champion. He knows the walls, the timing of the safety car windows, and the strategic games that get played on a circuit where one mistake can undo an entire weekend.

None of that changes the mathematics. Norris is 106 points behind Kimi Antonelli in the championship with nine races remaining. The maximum he can score from here, assuming Antonelli finishes second in every race, would barely be enough to close the gap. For Norris, a Baku win is not optional. It is required — and it needs to come alongside a Antonelli result that costs the young Mercedes driver points.

Saturday at Baku is the moment McLaren has been building toward all season.

### STORY 4: Antonelli Title Math

**FB Long-Form (5:00 PM ET)**
Kimi Antonelli stands 81 points clear of George Russell, 101 ahead of Lewis Hamilton, and 106 ahead of Lando Norris heading into the 2026 Azerbaijan Grand Prix. With nine races remaining — including Saturday's race at Baku and the sprint weekend in Singapore from October 9-11 — the youngest potential world champion in F1 history is reading the board carefully.

The math, for now, is on his side. If Antonelli wins Baku and Russell and Norris do not score well, the gap approaches the territory where the Singapore sprint weekend becomes the backdrop for a title celebration. Nico Rosberg, who knows something about the weight of leading a title race, said this week that "Kimi Antonelli versus his mind will decide the 2026 Formula 1 title race" — an acknowledgement that the 20-year-old's psychological composure, not his driving speed, is now the decisive variable.

Antonelli's response to that challenge has been consistent. He won the Italian Grand Prix from 19th on the grid. He has averaged 20.9 points per race across 14 races. He has not cracked under pressure in any meaningful way this season.

What Baku provides is a test: can he perform under the combined weight of a looming title, a pressuring McLaren in race-winning form, and the scrutiny of the entire motorsport world?

### STORY 5: Baku Weekend Preview

**FB Long-Form (8:00 PM ET)**
The 2026 Azerbaijan Grand Prix weekend begins in 48 hours — and few F1 weekends this season have carried as many storylines into the opening practice session.

Here is everything you need to know:

The schedule: FP1 and FP2 run on Wednesday, September 24. FP3 and qualifying follow on Thursday, September 25. The race itself is Saturday, September 26, making it the first Saturday race in Baku history. The shift happened because September 27 is Azerbaijan's national Remembrance Day.

The circuit: The Baku City Circuit is 6.003 kilometres long and features the longest straight in Formula 1 — 2.2 kilometres — where cars approach 340 kilometres per hour. It is a street circuit in design but a power-circuit in character; the combination of low-speed technical sections and ultra-high-speed blasts makes setup one of the most difficult balancing acts on the calendar.

The storylines: Charles Leclerc is expected to start from the back with a fresh power unit after his Monza crash. Williams debuts its revolutionary FW48 2.0 chassis. Lando Norris is the race favourite with McLaren's Baku-specific upgrade. And Kimi Antonelli arrives leading by 81 points, knowing that the right weekend could accelerate his path to the title.

This is F1 at its most chaotic. The walls are close, the overtaking is real, and anything can happen. That is what Baku has always delivered.
