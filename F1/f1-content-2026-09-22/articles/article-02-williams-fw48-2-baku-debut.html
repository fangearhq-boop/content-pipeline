Williams Arrives at Baku With an Almost Completely New Car — The FW48 2.0 Debuts

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | September 22, 2026

James Vowles has been talking about this moment since March. The Williams team principal stood in front of the media at the season opener and promised a car that would evolve through the year, get progressively better through a series of upgrade packages, and ultimately arrive at a point where it was worthy of its drivers and its targets. That moment arrives at Baku.

Williams will debut the FW48 2.0 — a B-spec chassis that represents the most significant single-race upgrade package the team has brought to a Grand Prix in years — at the 2026 Azerbaijan Grand Prix. It is, in Vowles's own words, "almost completely new." And that description is not a marketing exaggeration.

What Changed

The original FW48 arrived at the 2026 season opener with a significant problem: it was overweight. When a Formula 1 car is above the minimum weight limit, the regulations require ballast to be carried at fixed positions rather than in optimized locations, and the aerodynamic and handling consequences compound from there. Williams spent months working around a structural problem that they had not been able to solve in the wind tunnel before the season began.

The FW48 2.0 addresses that directly. The new chassis uses a revolutionary carbon manufacturing process developed internally at Williams's Grove factory — a process that produces a lighter, stiffer structure while maintaining the safety requirements mandated by the FIA. The chassis is not simply an updated version of the original. According to Vowles, it is a foundational rebuild, and it will also serve as the technical basis for the 2027 Williams chassis.

That last point matters. The decisions Williams makes in manufacturing the FW48 2.0 are not just about the final nine races of 2026. They are about the next chapter of the team's rebuild. Baku is where that chapter begins.

Expectations Management

Vowles has been careful — almost uncomfortably direct — about what to expect in Azerbaijan. He has publicly stated that the upgrade "won't be enough for points" in Baku. The Q2 barrier, consistently cleared by the midfield's top teams, remains out of reach for this iteration of the FW48 regardless of how much better the 2.0 is relative to the original.

That kind of honesty from a team principal, delivered publicly, is not a sign of pessimism. It is a sign of a leader who has learned from previous environments where overpromising eroded trust. Carlos Sainz and Alex Albon signed with Williams in part because of Vowles's transparency about timelines. Telling them the new car will not immediately transform results is the same credibility that got them to sign in the first place.

What the FW48 2.0 should provide is data. Lap time data that reflects the car's actual potential rather than the distorted picture created by the weight penalty. Setup data that can be extrapolated into genuine development directions. A reference baseline for everything that follows through the end of the season and into 2027 testing.

What It Means for Sainz and Albon

Carlos Sainz left Ferrari for Williams after his contract was not renewed. Alex Albon, who has been the de facto team leader since the day he arrived, has driven with extraordinary commitment through a season where the car has rarely given him the tools to show what he can do. Both drivers will feel the difference of an FW48 2.0 in the cockpit the moment they leave the garage for FP1 on Wednesday, September 24.

How much of that difference translates to lap time is the question Williams will answer through free practice and qualifying. But the foundation is finally there. The car that Vowles promised — the real Williams, not the overweight compromise — rolls out at Baku for the first time. From here, the trajectory should only go up.

What's Next

Free practice at Baku begins Wednesday, September 24. Williams will use both FP1 and FP2 to gather data from the new chassis and establish the setup baseline that Carlos Sainz and Alex Albon need for qualifying on Thursday evening. The race is Saturday, September 26. Whatever the result at Baku, the data Williams collects this weekend will shape their development program through the final eight races of 2026 and into the 2027 season.
