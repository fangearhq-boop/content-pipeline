Ferrari Sets Sights on Baku for Leclerc's Engine Penalty — Why the Timing Makes Sense

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | September 22, 2026

A grid penalty is never good news. But if you are Charles Leclerc and you have to take one somewhere, Baku is the least damaging venue on the calendar — and Ferrari knows it.

The backdrop: Leclerc crashed heavily at the Italian Grand Prix at Monza, spinning off at the high-speed Parabolica corner and striking the tyre barrier with the rear of his SF-26. The power unit installed in the car was brand new — part of Ferrari's second ADUO allocation, meaning its value to the team for the remainder of the season was significant. The damage it sustained in the crash has left it unsuitable for competitive use, capable of running in practice but not in qualifying or race conditions where the engine is under full load.

Ferrari's options are limited: use the damaged unit and risk a failure, or take a fresh unit and accept the grid penalty that comes with exceeding the allocation limit. They are almost certainly going to choose the latter. The question has been where.

Why Baku

Fred Vasseur, Ferrari's team principal, was direct about the reasoning when asked: Baku is "quite suitable" for absorbing the grid drop. That is not vague PR language — it is a specific engineering and strategic assessment. The Baku City Circuit's 2.2-kilometre main straight is the longest in Formula 1, and cars approach 340 kilometres per hour before braking for Turn 1. The multiple overtaking zones throughout the lap mean that a driver starting from the back of the grid has a realistic chance of recovering positions over the course of the 51-lap race.

Compare that to the Singapore Marina Bay Street Circuit — the next race on the calendar after Azerbaijan — or the Japanese Grand Prix at Suzuka. Both venues are notoriously difficult for overtaking. A grid penalty at either location would be far more damaging in terms of race results. Leclerc has historically been strong at Baku in qualifying — he took pole position there four years running before the 2026 season — which suggests he could manage from wherever he starts.

Leclerc's Perspective

Leclerc himself has been characteristically straightforward on the matter. He acknowledged that a penalty is "probably" coming "at some point" — an acceptance of reality that reflects both his confidence in Baku as a recovery venue and his awareness that carrying the damaged unit any further creates more risk than taking the hit now.

The Monegasque driver has four consecutive pole positions at Baku in his recent history. His setup knowledge and feel for the circuit's technical demands — particularly the low-speed castle section and the snap of Turn 8 — give him a baseline advantage regardless of grid position. Ferrari's strategists will construct a race strategy around the idea of clearing traffic on the long straight and building position through the first half of the race.

What It Means for Ferrari's Championship

Ferrari sits in second place in the Constructors' Championship with 358 points, 145 behind Mercedes and 52 ahead of McLaren. A Leclerc penalty at Baku represents a strategic concession, not a catastrophe. If he recovers to a points-scoring position — even seventh or eighth — Ferrari limits the damage while ensuring the new power unit is available for the final eight races of the season.

The constructors' battle with McLaren is the more pressing concern. McLaren arrives in Baku with Lando Norris as the race favourite and circuit-specific upgrades designed to maximize performance here. If Norris wins and Leclerc starts from the back, the gap between Ferrari and McLaren in the constructors' table could shrink. That arithmetic is not lost on Vasseur.

What's Next

Ferrari is expected to confirm the power unit decision before practice begins at Baku on Wednesday, September 24. Once the penalty is official, the team will begin constructing a strategy around Leclerc's likely back-of-grid starting position for Saturday's race. Watch for any change in Ferrari's tyre choice or fuel-load planning during the free practice sessions — those will signal how ambitious their recovery plan is going to be.
