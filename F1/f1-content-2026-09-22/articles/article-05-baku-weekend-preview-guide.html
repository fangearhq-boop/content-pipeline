Baku Grand Prix Weekend Preview — Everything You Need to Know Before Wednesday

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | September 22, 2026

In 48 hours, Formula 1 arrives in Azerbaijan for what may be the most story-laden street circuit weekend of the 2026 season. Here is the schedule, the circuit, and the five reasons why this race belongs at the top of your watching priority list.

The Schedule

Free Practice 1 and Free Practice 2 are on Wednesday, September 24 — an early start to the weekend that gives all teams two full sessions to gather data before FP3 and qualifying begin. Free Practice 3 and qualifying follow on Thursday, September 25, with qualifying deciding the grid for Saturday's race. The 2026 Azerbaijan Grand Prix itself runs on Saturday, September 26, in the first Saturday race in Baku's history.

The schedule shift was requested by Azerbaijani authorities because September 27 is the country's annual Day of Remembrance — a national holiday that makes hosting a major motor racing event impractical. The Saturday race is an unusual format, but it preserves the entire weekend's structure for fans and teams while respecting the local context.

The Circuit

The Baku City Circuit is 6.003 kilometres long and winds through the centre of Azerbaijan's capital, incorporating medieval walls, modern government buildings, and the boulevards along the Caspian Sea waterfront. The circuit's signature feature is its main straight — 2.2 kilometres of flat-out acceleration that produces top speeds approaching 340 kilometres per hour, making it the fastest section on any street circuit in Formula 1.

The circuit is a study in contrasts: ultra-high-speed blasts down the straight followed by the demanding castle district, where walls are close and the rhythm of the track changes completely. Setup is one of the hardest balancing acts in F1 — too much downforce and you lose straight-line speed; too little and the castle section becomes a danger zone.

The race distance is 51 laps. Safety cars appear at nearly every Baku Grand Prix. Chaos is built into the venue's DNA.

Five Storylines to Watch

1. Leclerc's grid penalty. Ferrari is expected to confirm before Wednesday whether Charles Leclerc will start from the back with a fresh power unit following his Monza engine damage. Fred Vasseur called Baku "quite suitable" for the drop. If the penalty is confirmed, watch Leclerc's recovery drive as a sub-plot that runs through the entire race.

2. Williams FW48 2.0. James Vowles's rebuilt, almost completely new car debuts in FP1. The new carbon manufacturing process addresses the weight problems that have hampered the team all season. How fast it is relative to the original will be visible from the first flying lap.

3. Norris versus Antonelli. Lando Norris is the race favourite. Kimi Antonelli has an 81-point lead. This is the direct conflict that defines the championship's closing phase. If Norris wins and Antonelli struggles, the gap shrinks. If Antonelli matches or beats Norris, the title race may be functionally over before Singapore.

4. The constructors' battle. Ferrari leads McLaren by 52 points. A Norris win with Leclerc's grid penalty recovery falling short of the points could change that number significantly. The constructors' title is a separate competition with significant financial implications for both teams.

5. Safety car wildcards. Baku produces safety cars. Every team knows it. The driver who manages the virtual safety cars and real safety car periods best — choosing the right lap to pit, the right tyre compound to gamble on — will have an outcome their qualifying performance never predicted. This is a race where preparation meets fortune, and Baku has no obligation to be fair.

What's Next

Free practice begins Wednesday, September 24. Follow both FP1 and FP2 for pace data on the Williams FW48 2.0, McLaren's Baku upgrade performance, and any setup signals from Mercedes that suggest how Antonelli is approaching the weekend. Qualifying Thursday evening determines the grid. The race is Saturday, September 26 — the 15th race of the 2026 Formula 1 season, and one of the most consequential the championship has seen in years.
