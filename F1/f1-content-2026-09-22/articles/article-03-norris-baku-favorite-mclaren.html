Norris Is the Baku Favourite — and He Needs Every Point He Can Get

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | September 22, 2026

The Baku City Circuit has a way of producing moments that reshape narratives. Safety cars appear at exactly the wrong time. Walls reach out and collect cars that have been clean all weekend. Tyres fail on the final straight. In the chaos, drivers who prepare properly and execute without error take victories that look inevitable only in retrospect.

Lando Norris has twice been that driver. He won the Azerbaijan Grand Prix in 2024 and again in 2025, both times by reading the chaos correctly and converting his pace into points when it mattered. He arrives in Baku for the 2026 edition as the odds-on favourite for pole position and race victory — and with the most urgent reason of anyone on the grid to make it happen.

McLaren's Upgrade Advantage

McLaren has brought Baku-specific upgrades to the 2026 Azerbaijan Grand Prix. The development targets the second sector of the Baku City Circuit — the section where mechanical grip and aerodynamic precision are most critical, encompassing the tight castle district and the approach to the long straight. The upgrades improve both downforce generation and stability through that section, translating into better sector times that compound over a 51-lap race distance.

The result, according to pre-race pace simulations and betting market movement, is a McLaren that looks like the fastest car in Baku this weekend. Norris, who already knows this circuit as well as any driver on the grid, is the beneficiary of a team that has been building toward this specific race's conditions for weeks.

The Championship Math

Lando Norris is 106 points behind Kimi Antonelli in the 2026 Drivers' Championship with nine races remaining, including this one. The mathematics are not on his side. To overtake Antonelli, he would need his Mercedes rival to effectively stop scoring points — an outcome that the consistent 20-year-old has given no indication of producing.

But "the championship is over" and "the championship is alive" are two different statements. Norris cannot win the title in a straightforward mathematical sense. What he can do is win at Baku, have Antonelli finish fifth or lower, and reduce the gap to 75 or below. That keeps the conversation going through Singapore and Japan. A win here does not give Norris the championship. But it ensures the story is not finished before October.

George Russell, who trails Antonelli by 81 points, faces similar pressure. Neither he nor Norris can afford another race where Antonelli takes a win or a podium and they fail to match it.

The Leclerc Wildcard

Charles Leclerc's expected grid penalty introduces an element that McLaren's strategists are tracking carefully. Leclerc starting from the back means one fewer car that will be fighting at the front in the early laps — a factor that reduces congestion in the midfield and potentially gives Norris cleaner air in the opening phase of the race. It also means one fewer car competing with McLaren for the constructors' points that remain crucial in the battle with Ferrari for second place.

The constructors' table has Ferrari at 358 points and McLaren at 306 — a 52-point gap. A Norris win with Leclerc finishing tenth or lower could reduce that margin by more than 30 points in a single afternoon. That is the secondary prize at Baku: not just the driver championship pressure, but the constructors' gap that has been stubbornly wide all season.

What's Next

Qualifying for the 2026 Azerbaijan Grand Prix takes place on Thursday, September 25. Norris will enter the session as the driver most capable of taking pole position, and a front-row start on the 2.2-kilometre straight makes the race considerably easier to control. The race itself is Saturday, September 26. Watch Norris's performance in Sector 2 during free practice on Wednesday — if the McLaren upgrades are delivering what the team expects, the pace gap to the field will be visible from the opening session.
