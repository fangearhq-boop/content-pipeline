The F1 Class of 2026 Is Overachieving — Audi Jumps Williams, Cadillac's Perez Turns Heads

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | August 5, 2026

The consensus before the 2026 Formula 1 season began was fairly consistent: be patient with the new teams. Audi and Cadillac were entering a sport that demands years of institutional knowledge, development continuity, and supply chain sophistication. The idea that either team would be meaningful in the Constructors' Championship before 2028 was considered generous.

Eleven rounds in, the consensus looks wrong.

Audi has scored 12 points in its debut season, jumped Williams in the Constructors' Championship to eighth place, and is generating genuine midfield battles in the second half of races. Cadillac, the American entry featuring Sergio Perez alongside Valtteri Bottas, has impressed enough in the midfield that Perez's individual performances are reportedly drawing attention from rival teams. Both programs are operating well ahead of where first-year teams typically operate.

Audi's Turning Point

Audi entered 2026 with Nico Hulkenberg and Gabriel Bortoleto — an experienced midfield presence paired with one of the most promising young drivers in recent junior formula history. Their first seven rounds were quiet: points came rarely, reliability was the priority, and the power unit was a work in progress. Then they introduced a driveability-focused upgrade at the Barcelona Grand Prix.

The results since: ten of their 12 championship points came in the three rounds following that upgrade. Hulkenberg, averaging twelfth in qualifying across the season, has started to push into Q3. Bortoleto, averaging thirteenth, is developing the race management skills that made him so compelling in Formula 2. Racing director Allan McNish, the former Le Mans champion who joined to guide the program's technical direction, told Motorsport.com he was "positively surprised" by the team's start. He is not alone.

Notably, Audi will not upgrade the power unit again until 2027. The team is managing a development runway and betting that the Barcelona-era gains will be sufficient to stay competitive through the year's end. Jumping Williams — a constructor with a full decade of recent F1 experience in their current ownership era — in the Constructors' Championship by midseason is a meaningful statement of intent.

Cadillac and Perez

Cadillac, the American entry that caused significant controversy in the years it spent trying to gain FIA and F1 Commission approval before finally securing its spot on the grid, has arrived with a lineup that carries genuine credentials. Sergio Perez brings four consecutive Red Bull seasons and significant experience in machinery that was fighting for championships. Valtteri Bottas brings institutional knowledge of what working at a serious F1 program looks like.

The team's early season has focused on reliability and learning the sport's operational rhythms. But Perez's individual performances have been consistently stronger than the midfield position suggests. He told Motorsport.com the team is "in a massive hurry to find performance" — which is not an unusual statement for a first-year team, but one that carries more weight when the performances already indicate genuine pace is there to unlock.

The reports of rival team interest in Perez are the most significant external signal. In Formula 1, reputation travels through paddock conversations before it shows up in points. The fact that teams with established programs are paying attention to what Cadillac's lead driver is doing speaks to results that have clearly made an impression.

What This Means for the Sport

F1's expansion to a larger grid was not without controversy. Existing teams argued that the grid was already sufficiently competitive and that new entries would strain resources, dilute broadcasting focus, and struggle for years before they became meaningful. The class of 2026 is beginning to challenge that argument.

If Audi continues its development trajectory and Cadillac finds performance in the second half of the year, the 2026 season will be remembered not just for the championship battle between Antonelli and Hamilton, but for the moment when two new teams arrived and immediately began pulling their weight. That is a story Formula 1 needed — and one both programs are clearly motivated to continue writing.

What's Next

The Dutch Grand Prix at Zandvoort on August 21-23 is the first race back from the summer break. Eleven rounds remain. For Audi and Cadillac, each race is another learning experience and another opportunity to prove that the consensus on new teams was underestimating what these programs could achieve. Their rookie seasons are not over. The best chapters may still be ahead.
