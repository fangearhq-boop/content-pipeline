George Russell Says He's 'Never Had a Season Like This' — 59 Points Behind His Own Team-Mate With the Fastest Car

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | August 5, 2026

George Russell won the Australian Grand Prix. He drove for championship-leading Mercedes all season. He has been in one of the two or three fastest cars on the grid since the opening round. And heading into the F1 summer break, he is 59 points behind his 20-year-old team-mate.

After suffering an anti-stall failure before Turn 1 at the Hungarian Grand Prix — the latest in a sequence of mechanical disasters that has defined his 2026 campaign — Russell told Sky Sports: "I've never had a season like this in my career."

David Coulthard, assessing the situation in a separate Motorsport.com interview, went further. Russell's run of bad luck, Coulthard said, is "beyond a Rocky story." The implication being that even the sport's most famous redemption narrative does not capture the absurdity of what has happened to Russell this year.

The List

Russell's 2026 season of disasters reads like a particularly cruel screenplay. In China, technical failure eliminated him from contention. In Japan, safety car timing denied him a result he had earned. In Monaco, a penalty cost him positions. In Canada, he was leading the race when the engine failed. In Belgium, power unit issues again. In Hungary, an anti-stall glitch meant the race ended before it began — before he had completed a single lap in earnest competition.

Set against this backdrop is the championship reality. Kimi Antonelli — his team-mate at Mercedes, driving the same car, subject to the same setup decisions and engineering support — has 219 points, six wins, six poles, and nine podiums from 11 starts. Russell trails by 59. The gap between them is not explained by talent. It is not explained by pace. It is explained, almost entirely, by a sequence of mechanical and timing disasters that would be remarkable for any driver in any season.

The Cruel Mathematics

The mathematics are worth sitting with. If Russell had finished second in every race Antonelli won — not beating him, just following him home — he would currently be leading the championship. If the engine had not failed in Canada, where he was leading, he would almost certainly be within 15 to 20 points of the summit. Instead, he is 59 behind and the conversation around Mercedes is entirely about when Antonelli clinches his first title, not whether Russell can mount a comeback.

Russell won in Australia. He proved, in that race, that he is capable of winning in this car and in these regulations. That the season has produced only that one victory — and a handful of strong results that statistical bad luck has converted into nothing — is a genuine injustice that the points table does not convey.

A racefans.net head-to-head breakdown published August 5 documents the gap between the two drivers in terms of qualifying pace (minimal), race pace (minimal), and points scored (enormous). The conclusion is the same one Russell himself has reached: the results do not reflect the performance.

What the Summer Break Means for Russell

The summer break does not fix mechanical reliability. It does not retroactively convert DNFs into points. What it does is give Russell two weeks without another mechanical catastrophe, which at this point may be the most optimistic possible framing of his situation.

The championship arithmetic is not quite dead. 291 points remain available across 11 rounds. A 59-point gap is not technically insurmountable. But for Russell to win this championship, Antonelli would need to have a significant reversal of fortune — and Russell would need to produce results that his machinery has so far refused to deliver.

The more realistic framing: Russell is driving for a contract that earns him a better fortune in 2027, and for the dignity of finishing a season with results that reflect how well he has actually driven. In that sense, the second half of 2026 still matters enormously — even if the trophy is no longer the target.

What's Next

The summer break runs until the Dutch Grand Prix at Zandvoort on August 21-23. It is the first race back and the beginning of what Russell needs to be an entirely different chapter. Eleven rounds. 291 points. A team-mate he cannot catch without help from fate reversing itself. George Russell has never had a season like this. He would very much like the next eleven rounds to be nothing like the last eleven.
