Qatar GP Preview: Perez's Penalty, Mandatory 2-Stop Strategy, and the Final Push

By Elena Voss | November 26, 2026

The 2026 Formula 1 season enters its penultimate chapter on Thursday, when FP1 and FP2 at Lusail International Circuit open the Qatar Grand Prix weekend. Qualifying follows on Saturday and the race runs on Sunday — 57 laps on a circuit that has historically been among the most demanding on tires in the entire calendar.

Several key factors will shape how the weekend unfolds, beginning before the first qualifying lap has even been set.

Perez Starts With a Handicap

Cadillac's Sergio Perez arrives in Qatar carrying a 5-place grid penalty, handed down by the stewards following his crash with Rafael Camara during the Las Vegas Grand Prix. Whatever position Perez qualifies in, he starts five places further back — a significant burden on a circuit where track position matters and overtaking, while possible, requires careful tire and energy management.

For Cadillac, it complicates an already challenging weekend. The team is in the final stages of building its F1 infrastructure, and managing a compromised starting position adds pressure to both strategy and pit-lane execution.

Mandatory Two-Stop Strategy

Pirelli has set a 27-lap maximum stint at Lusail, which means every driver will be required to make at least two pit stops during Sunday's 57-lap race. No single-stop strategy is viable, regardless of tire management or pace differential.

The mandatory two-stop format puts a premium on pit-lane execution, strategy timing, and the ability to maintain tire performance across stints. Teams that run clean pit stops and time their undercuts or overcuts correctly will gain positions that are difficult to recover once lost. For teams whose pit crews have been inconsistent in 2026, Qatar is a significant test.

The Championship at Stake

Kimi Antonelli arrives in Qatar as the 2026 champion with the title already secured. For the drivers around him, Qatar is one of two remaining opportunities to define their final standings.

Max Verstappen leads Lewis Hamilton by 37 points for second place in the Drivers' Championship. With 50 points available across Qatar and Abu Dhabi, Hamilton needs to outscore Verstappen significantly in both races. The math is tight but achievable if Verstappen encounters trouble and Hamilton takes maximum points from Qatar.

Further back, Russell and Norris are both on 264 points — 3 points behind Hamilton — making the battle for fourth place another active subplot heading into the weekend.

What's Next

Practice at Lusail begins Thursday. Teams will use FP1 and FP2 to gather tire degradation data, establish setup balance, and evaluate pace across the compound options. With the mandatory two-stop rule defining the strategic envelope, early data on how the medium and hard compounds perform over long stints will be particularly valuable. The race on Sunday will be decided as much in the pits as on the track.
