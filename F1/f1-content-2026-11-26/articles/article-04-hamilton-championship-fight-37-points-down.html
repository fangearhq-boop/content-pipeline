Hamilton's Last Stand: 37 Points Down, Two Races Left, the Math Is Punishing but Possible

By Ryan Calloway | November 26, 2026

Lewis Hamilton's 2026 Formula 1 season has been defined by consistency, resilience, and the kind of intelligent racing that has characterized his career since he first arrived in the sport. But with two races remaining and 37 points separating him from Max Verstappen in the battle for second place in the Drivers' Championship, the final chapter requires something more: near perfection from him, and imperfection from Verstappen.

Hamilton sits on 267 points. Verstappen has 304. Qatar and Abu Dhabi offer 25 points each for a race win — a maximum of 50 points remaining across the two rounds.

The Math

For Hamilton to overtake Verstappen in the final standings, he needs to outscore him by 38 points across Qatar and Abu Dhabi. The most direct path requires Hamilton to win both races while Verstappen finishes outside the points in at least one of them.

More realistically, Hamilton needs Verstappen to have a mechanical failure or a collision that keeps him off the leaderboard, combined with Hamilton maximizing points in both rounds. That combination of circumstances is not impossible — it has happened before, in this sport and in this era — but it requires forces outside Hamilton's control to align.

What is in Hamilton's control: racing at the highest level across both weekends. He has done that throughout 2026 at Ferrari. The car is capable of winning. Hamilton has shown repeatedly that when the pressure is at its highest, he finds performance that others cannot match.

Ferrari's Position

Hamilton joined Ferrari ahead of 2026 and the partnership has been one of the season's most compelling storylines. The Maranello team gave him a genuinely competitive car, and Hamilton has extracted results from it throughout the year — with enough wins and podiums to keep him in the top three in the standings heading into the final two rounds.

Qatar suits Ferrari's strengths. Lusail International Circuit demands high downforce, good tire management, and mechanical grip through its long, sweeping corners — an envelope that Ferrari has operated in competently throughout the 2026 season. If there is a track in the remaining calendar where Hamilton has the best chance of converting pace into a win, Lusail may be it.

Verstappen's Record at the Final Two Circuits

The complicating factor is Verstappen's own record. His Las Vegas victory — third consecutive at the Strip Circuit — is a reminder that he does not give up victories easily on demanding circuits. His pace at Red Bull-designed circuits and high-speed tracks has been exceptional across his career, and he arrives in Qatar having won three of the last five races.

To close a 37-point gap, Hamilton needs Verstappen to either have bad luck or make mistakes. Neither is routine for the four-time world champion.

What's Next

The Qatar Grand Prix weekend begins Thursday at Lusail International Circuit. For Lewis Hamilton, every session matters. Practice will establish whether the Ferrari has the race pace to challenge Verstappen from the front. Qualifying will determine whether Hamilton can put himself in position to win. And Sunday's race, over 57 laps with a mandatory two-stop strategy, will begin to answer whether the 2026 runner-up story has one more twist to deliver.
