FIA Says Herta Still One Point Short After Mexico FP1 — Cadillac Dispute Complicates F1 Future

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap

October 31, 2026

The most dramatic moment of Friday's Mexico City Grand Prix practice sessions did not involve a fast lap or a collision. It involved a counting error that may have cost Colton Herta his path to Formula 1.

The American driver completed his planned FP1 run in Valtteri Bottas's Cadillac, finishing 24 laps — comfortably exceeding the 100-kilometer threshold required to earn a Super License point. Cadillac celebrated. Multiple outlets reported that Herta had earned his FIA Super License. The 2027 F1 grid, many assumed, was about to have an American on it.

Then the FIA issued a correction. Herta, the governing body confirmed, remains at 39 of the 40 points required to hold an FIA Super License. One point short.

The Dispute Explained

The gap between Cadillac's calculation and the FIA's official count appears to center on how Super License points are accumulated through FP1 appearances. A driver earns a point for completing a minimum distance in a Formula 1 free practice session, but the exact qualifying conditions — including whether the session itself is incident-free, whether the distance was completed under specific conditions, and how the FIA records its count — are not always transparent to teams in real time.

Cadillac believed that Herta's five FP1 appearances in 2026 had taken him to the 40-point threshold. The FIA's count places him at 39 — one point behind. The cause of the discrepancy had not been officially clarified by Friday evening.

The Stakes

This is not an administrative detail. Herta has been one of IndyCar's most decorated drivers, and the Super License barrier has been the central obstacle between him and a Formula 1 career for years. The 2027 Cadillac F1 seat — the team's second season on the grid — was identified as a realistic destination if he earned his license in 2026.

Mexico City was his final planned FP1 appearance of the 2026 season. There are no more designated sessions on the calendar where he could earn the remaining point through an FP1 run. The question now is whether alternative pathways exist — a formal appeal of the FIA's calculation, an approved race substitute event, or another qualifying mechanism — that could close the gap before the 2027 driver registration deadline.

The ambiguity is agonizing. One point is as close as it gets, and the difference between 39 and 40 represents the difference between competing in Formula 1 and watching from the sidelines.

What's Next

Herta's camp is expected to seek clarification from the FIA on the exact calculation and whether any formal challenge to the point total is permissible. Cadillac, as the employing team, has a direct stake in the outcome — their American driver lineup plans for 2027 could depend on the resolution.

The FIA will need to provide a transparent accounting of how the 39-point total was arrived at, and whether any path to the 40th point remains open outside of a standard FP1 appearance. Until that clarity arrives, Herta's F1 future remains in an uncomfortable and public limbo.
