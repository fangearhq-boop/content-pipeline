Russell and Hamilton Separated by One Point With Three Races to Go

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap

October 31, 2026

While the world watches Kimi Antonelli approach the World Drivers' Championship, the second title story at the Mexico City Grand Prix is playing out between two former teammates sharing a moment of competitive reckoning: George Russell and Lewis Hamilton, separated by just one championship point heading into the final three races.

Russell stands at 242 points. Hamilton holds 241. The closest intra-championship gap of the season, between two drivers who know each other better than almost any pairing on the current grid, arrives at the worst possible moment for Hamilton — on a weekend where his Ferrari developed a power-unit issue during Friday's practice.

Hamilton's FP2 Problem

Hamilton was forced to park his Ferrari in FP2 after the car lost power on track. The exact nature of the failure was not disclosed, and Ferrari's engineers were working to diagnose the issue through the night. In normal circumstances, a practice-session retirement is an inconvenience. At this point in the season, with one championship point separating him from a teammate competing on a different team, it is more than that.

If Ferrari cannot fully resolve the issue before qualifying, Hamilton risks starting from a compromised grid position. A mid-field start in Mexico City — a circuit where track position matters and overtaking is possible but not always straightforward — could cost him points he desperately needs against Russell.

Three Races, Maximum 75 Points

With Mexico, São Paulo, and Abu Dhabi remaining, the maximum points available to any driver is 75 — 25 per race if they win with fastest lap each time. Given the single-point gap and three races to go, the fight for second place will almost certainly be resolved by the final race of the season in Abu Dhabi.

Russell's position on the front foot is marginally more comfortable only because the lead is his to defend rather than close. But one point is not a margin anyone can afford to take lightly, especially with an opponent of Hamilton's experience and motivation.

What's Next

Qualifying on Saturday is the first opportunity for either driver to build an advantage. Russell finished third in FP2 and is well placed for a strong qualifying run. Hamilton's starting point depends on what Ferrari finds overnight.

The secondary championship battle at the Mexico City GP has the ingredients to be its own compelling story: one point, two experienced competitors, three races to sort it out, and a power-unit scare that could shift everything. Sunday will add its chapter to the ledger.
