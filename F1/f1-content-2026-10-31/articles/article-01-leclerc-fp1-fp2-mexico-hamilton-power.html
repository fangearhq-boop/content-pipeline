Leclerc Fastest in Both Mexico Practice Sessions; Hamilton Forced to Park Ferrari

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap

October 31, 2026

Charles Leclerc put Ferrari on notice Friday at the Mexico City Grand Prix, topping both Free Practice sessions at the Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez. In a weekend layered with championship drama, Leclerc's FP1 and FP2 pace was the most straightforward story of the day — and perhaps the most significant.

FP1 ended with Leclerc fastest, Kimi Antonelli second and Mercedes substitute Frederik Vesti third as part of a mandatory rookie substitution wave that saw ten regular drivers sit out the opening session. FP2 brought even sharper times: Leclerc clocked a 1:17.827, 0.103 seconds ahead of Lando Norris, with George Russell third and Antonelli fourth.

Ferrari Thriving at Altitude

The Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez sits at 2,240 meters above sea level — one of the highest circuits on the F1 calendar. The thin air affects aerodynamic downforce and engine breathing in ways that create a unique performance hierarchy compared to sea-level tracks. On Friday, Ferrari and Leclerc rose to the top of that hierarchy.

Leclerc's consistent pace across two very different sessions — one populated by rookies, one more representative of the full grid — suggests this is not a fluke. If that form translates to qualifying, Leclerc will be in pole contention when the session gets underway at 5:00 PM ET on Saturday.

Hamilton's FP2 Ends Early

The significant subplot of Friday's running was Lewis Hamilton being forced to park his Ferrari with a power-unit issue in FP2. The exact nature of the problem had not been disclosed by Friday evening, but the timing is poor for a driver in the middle of a championship battle.

Hamilton sits just one point behind teammate Russell in the fight for second place in the standings. Three races remain to settle that internal battle. An engine concern heading into a pivotal qualifying session and race is exactly the kind of complication Hamilton cannot afford in a season where every point between now and Abu Dhabi will matter.

Ferrari's engineers will work overnight to diagnose and resolve the issue before qualifying. Whether Hamilton takes the grid in full confidence or with an asterisk over his power unit is a question that will define his weekend.

Verstappen and Hadjar Clash

Max Verstappen and his Red Bull teammate Isack Hadjar made contact during FP2 — another incident for stewards to review. The collision did not appear to cause serious damage, but any penalties or equipment concerns coming out of the session could reshape the starting grid for Sunday.

Verstappen, who has five Mexico City race wins to his name, will be looking to put the session behind him and focus on delivering a front-row qualifying performance. He remains one of the chief threats to Leclerc's practice-session momentum.

What's Next

Qualifying begins at 5:00 PM ET on Saturday at the Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez, with Q1, Q2, and Q3 sessions deciding the grid for Sunday's race. Leclerc's practice dominance makes him the early favorite, but qualifying at altitude has a way of scrambling expectations — and Verstappen's track record here demands respect.

For Antonelli, who leads the championship by 123 points and needs only a ninth-place finish Sunday to clinch the title, qualifying is about securing a clean position from which he can safely manage the race. The fastest lap can wait; the championship cannot.
