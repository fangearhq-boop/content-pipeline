Mexico City GP Qualifying Preview: Leclerc's Practice Pace vs. Verstappen's Track Record

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap

October 31, 2026

Saturday's Mexico City Grand Prix qualifying session, starting at 5:00 PM ET, arrives with a clear practice leader and an open question: can Charles Leclerc's dominant Friday form translate to a Q3 pole, or does the altitude of the Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez have more to say once every tenth of a second is on the table?

Leclerc topped both FP1 and FP2 on Friday. In FP2 — the most representative session — he clocked a 1:17.827, 0.103 seconds ahead of Lando Norris. It was Ferrari's strongest practice showing of the recent season, and it sets up a qualifying session with genuine contenders on multiple teams.

Leclerc: Converting Practice Into Pole

The Ferrari driver has been here before — leading practice, only to face a different story when the times truly matter. But the margins in FP2 were compelling, and Leclerc's feedback about the car's behavior at altitude has been positive. If Ferrari maximizes their setup overnight, there is no reason to believe the pace advantage disappears when Q3 begins.

A pole at Mexico would be Leclerc's clearest statement of the weekend: not just that Ferrari is quick, but that the Scuderia has a genuine package capable of challenging for Sunday's race win.

Verstappen: Five Mexico Wins, and a History of Delivering Under Pressure

Max Verstappen has won the Mexico City Grand Prix five times. No active driver has won here more. His knowledge of the Autódromo's characteristics — the long, flat-out first sector, the tight stadium section, the tricky final chicane — is encyclopedic, and his qualifying record at this circuit reflects that mastery.

The contact with teammate Isack Hadjar in FP2 introduces a minor distraction, but Verstappen's ability to compartmentalize and deliver in qualifying is well established. Betting markets favor him for pole, and those markets have access to team performance data that practice sessions alone do not always reveal.

Antonelli: Eighth Title, Managed Weekend

Kimi Antonelli needs ninth place Sunday to clinch the World Championship. Qualifying matters — starting from the front reduces risk — but his weekend priority is clean execution rather than the absolute fastest lap. Antonelli was fourth in FP2, close enough to the top to find the front row if everything clicks in qualifying, but not at a level that suggests he is pushing at the limit of his car's potential.

The championship leader's qualifying performance should be read in that context: he is playing percentage-point F1, not heroics. If he gets P3 or P4 on the grid, that is an excellent starting point for a title-winning Sunday.

What's Next

Qualifying at the Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez begins at 5:00 PM ET on Saturday. Q1 eliminates the five slowest drivers; Q2 eliminates five more; Q3 delivers the top ten, with pole to the fastest. The grid set today determines Sunday's race — and if Antonelli starts from a strong position, the next 24 hours could end with F1 celebrating its youngest World Champion in the shadow of the Mexican capital.
