### STORY 2: Colton Herta Super License Dispute

**FB Long-Form (10:00 AM ET)**
The dream and the controversy arrived at the same time for Colton Herta on Friday.

The American driver completed 24 laps — more than the required 100 kilometers — in Valtteri Bottas's Cadillac during FP1 at Mexico City. The Cadillac team celebrated. Multiple outlets reported that Herta had earned his FIA Super License, the final step toward a confirmed 2027 F1 seat.

Then the FIA issued a correction.

According to the governing body, Herta remains at 39 of the 40 super license points required to race in Formula 1. One point short. The confusion stems from a discrepancy between how Cadillac calculated the points earned from his FP1 appearances and how the FIA's official count records them.

This was Herta's final planned FP1 session of the 2026 season. With no more appearances on the schedule, he has no obvious path to earning that final point before the 2027 season begins. The resolution — whether through an appeal, a recalculation, or an alternative approved qualifying event — is now the most pressing question around his F1 future.

One point. This close to history, and the door may not open.

Antonelli leads the standings with 365 points, 123 clear of Mercedes teammate George Russell. He has won nine races this season and claimed seven poles. The numbers are historic already — and if he takes the title Sunday, he becomes the youngest World Champion in Formula 1 history.

Friday practice didn't show him at the very top, but fourth-fastest in FP2 means he's comfortably in contention. His weekend strategy is almost certainly about consistency and a clean race rather than chasing a fastest lap or a risky overtaking move that could compromise his result.
