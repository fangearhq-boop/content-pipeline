Lewis Hamilton Is 50 Points Back — and the Second Half of 2026 Hasn't Started Yet

By Ryan Calloway | F1 Fanrecap | August 8, 2026

F1 Championship / Lewis Hamilton / Ferrari / Kimi Antonelli

After 11 rounds of the 2026 Formula 1 season, the standings are clear: Kimi Antonelli leads with 219 points, Lewis Hamilton is second with 169, and George Russell sits third at 160. Mercedes leads the Constructors' Championship with 379 points, Ferrari roughly 72 back.

The gap between Antonelli and Hamilton is 50 points. The number of points still available in the remaining 11 rounds — 11 Grands Prix plus two Sprint races — is 291. By any reasonable mathematical definition, this title fight is live.

ESPN said it this week: "Lewis Hamilton eyes stronger second half of F1 2026." That is not wishful thinking. It reflects a genuine shift in Hamilton's trajectory from the first half of the season.

What Changed at Ferrari

The narrative around Hamilton's Ferrari move in 2025 was complicated — early adaptation difficulties, Leclerc outqualifying him by 2.5 tenths of a second on average. In 2026, the picture has reversed.

Hamilton won the Spanish Grand Prix in Barcelona — an emotional, significant victory. He has five podiums on the season and has not finished outside the top six in any race. In qualifying, he is now 0.017 seconds per lap faster than Leclerc on average. The man who arrived at Ferrari as a question mark entering his age-40 season has answered at least some of those questions.

Sky Sports and The Race have both published pieces on "what Ferrari thinks Hamilton has unlocked" in the 2026 season. The convergence of reporting around his improvement is real. He is not the story of a seven-time champion fading. He is the story of a driver who found something at Ferrari.

The Engine Gap and What Comes Next

Ferrari's primary deficit to Mercedes in 2026 is a power unit gap. ESPN reported this week that Ferrari is specifically "chasing power" in the second half. Toto Wolff issued what RacingNews365 called a "striking engine claim" countering Ferrari's narrative about closing the gap — the details of Wolff's statement were not fully elaborated in available reports, but the competitive tension between the two teams is clear.

If Ferrari can close the PU gap in the second half — through development, upgrade packages, or deployment tuning — Hamilton's on-track performance suggests he would immediately convert that extra performance into results. The question is whether Ferrari can deliver the tools quickly enough.

Antonelli, for his part, has been extraordinary. Six wins, six poles, nine podiums in 11 starts — numbers that would make any season historically significant. The 20-year-old Italian is on track to become the youngest F1 champion since Sebastian Vettel in 2010. The pressure of the title fight, which Antonelli has not experienced before at this level, is one of the variables Hamilton is counting on.

What's Next

The second half of the 2026 F1 season begins at the Dutch Grand Prix, August 21-23 at Circuit Zandvoort. It is the circuit's final F1 race in history. Hamilton's performance in the opening rounds of the second half — Zandvoort, Monza, and beyond — will determine whether the 50-point gap to Antonelli begins to close, or whether the championship becomes a formality. With 291 points available and a driver who is clearly improving, the title fight is not over.
