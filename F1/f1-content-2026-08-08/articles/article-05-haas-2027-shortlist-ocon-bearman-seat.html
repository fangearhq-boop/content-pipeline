Haas Has Narrowed Its 2027 Driver Shortlist — Ocon's Seat Is in Serious Jeopardy

By Elena Voss | F1 Fanrecap | August 8, 2026

F1 Team News / Haas / Esteban Ocon / Oliver Bearman

Haas has reportedly narrowed its 2027 driver shortlist to two names, according to Yahoo Sports. The team has not confirmed identities. But anyone looking at the 2026 scoreboard can draw their own conclusions about what that list probably reflects.

Through the first seven races of the 2026 season, Oliver Bearman has scored 18 points. Esteban Ocon has scored three. In Formula 1, where a driver's seat depends entirely on their ability to deliver results for the team, a gap of that magnitude over seven rounds does not go unaddressed.

The Story the Numbers Tell

Haas entered 2026 with what appeared to be a stable lineup: Ocon in his second season with the team, and Bearman continuing his development following an impressive debut campaign. The expectation was a competitive pairing as Haas developed through the new 2026 regulations.

The reality has been one-sided. Bearman scored a seventh-place finish at the Australian GP opener and has accumulated points consistently across multiple rounds. Ocon's three points represent a single scoring result. The gap is not marginal — it is the difference between a driver contributing meaningfully to the team's Constructors' Championship position and one who is not.

Neither driver controls the other's car, and Formula 1 results are never purely a function of driver performance — reliability, strategy, and race incidents all play roles. But seven rounds is enough data to establish a meaningful pattern.

The Hirakawa Signal

The strongest indicator of Haas's thinking came in two specific decisions. At the Hungarian Grand Prix weekend, Ryo Hirakawa — the reigning Le Mans champion and Toyota WEC star — was given FP1 driving time in the Haas. Earlier in the season, at the Austrian Grand Prix, Ocon was replaced for a free practice session.

Teams do not give FP1 time to drivers without purpose. The standard reasons are contractual obligations to give young drivers experience — or genuine evaluation of a candidate for a future role. Hirakawa is not a junior driver who needs mandated F1 running. He is an established, decorated motorsport champion who would represent a genuine and ready option for a race seat.

GPFans reported a headline: "Haas to make F1 driver lineup change at Hungarian Grand Prix" — mentioning Bearman and Hirakawa. Whether the reported change was for a free practice session or a different arrangement, the presence of Hirakawa in the Haas ecosystem during a race weekend is a clear signal.

What This Means for Ocon

Esteban Ocon joined Haas after a difficult final season at Alpine — a departure that was not entirely on his terms. He arrived in 2026 as an experienced, ten-year F1 veteran with genuine race victories on his record. The expectation was that he would be a stabilizing, points-scoring presence for the team.

The results have not delivered on that expectation. With the summer break providing natural pause for team leadership to assess their plans, and the 2027 driver market beginning to move rapidly across the paddock, Ocon's seat is more exposed than it has been at any point in his career.

Bearman, for his part, appears to be driving as though the seat is already his — which is exactly the approach that wins these internal battles.

What's Next

The summer break is when driver market decisions crystallize in F1. Watch for any Haas announcement regarding their 2027 lineup before the Dutch Grand Prix on August 23 — or in the weeks that follow. If Hirakawa is one of the two names on their shortlist, a contract announcement would be significant news for both Haas and the broader F1 driver market. Ocon, meanwhile, will be focused on delivering results in the second half of 2026 — the only currency that matters in driver market negotiations.
