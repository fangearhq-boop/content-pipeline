Fernando Alonso Is Taking His Retirement Decision "Day By Day" — the Summer Break Is His Deadline

By Marcus Cole | F1 Fanrecap | August 8, 2026

F1 Driver News / Fernando Alonso / Aston Martin

ESPN published the headline plainly this week: "Fernando Alonso says 2026 is his last year in F1."

The evidence has been accumulating all season. His Aston Martin contract expires at the end of 2026. Sky Sports insider Ted Kravitz predicted retirement when the season ends. Newsweek described a "bombshell" retirement statement from the Spaniard. When Alonso described his race in Barcelona as "probably my last time" at that circuit — and Barcelona is not on the 2027 Formula 1 calendar — it landed as a farewell that dared not use the word.

And yet Fernando Alonso, who has spent his entire career refusing to make anything simple, told Motorsport.com this week that he is taking 2026 "day by day."

The Condition He Attached to Retirement

Alonso's retirement logic has a characteristic twist. He has said publicly that "it is hard to retire if the car is not competitive." The implication is almost the inverse of what you might expect: if Aston Martin is finally fast — thanks to Adrian Newey's technical leadership — then Alonso would want to keep going and take advantage of the car. If the car remains uncompetitive, walking away becomes easier.

This is not circular reasoning. It reflects what Alonso actually wants from racing: to win. He has been patient with Aston Martin's development for two seasons, waiting for Newey's transformation of the team's technical approach to bear fruit. The 2027 car, designed under Newey's guidance from first principles, is expected to be a significant step forward. The question is whether Alonso is around to drive it.

The full fruits of Newey's work are not expected until 2027. Alonso's contract expires before then. He is 44 years old, with 32 race wins and two world championships. The career argument for walking away at the end of 2026 is obvious. The emotional argument — one last season in a competitive car — is equally real for him.

The Summer Break as Decision Window

Alonso himself identified the summer break as the period for his decision. That window is here. The Dutch Grand Prix on August 23 will be the first race after the break, and it is possible — perhaps likely — that a decision of some kind arrives before then.

F1 Oversteer described his retirement as "very likely" given Aston Martin's current performance. Newsweek reported the statement that started the avalanche of coverage. GPFans traced Ted Kravitz's prediction back to reporting from inside the paddock. The consensus from media who cover this sport closely is that Alonso's time in Formula 1 is ending.

Alonso, of course, has confounded consensus before. He came out of retirement to race for Renault in 2021 and extended his career through three additional seasons. The "day by day" framing is either genuine openness — or the most Alonso response possible to a question everyone already thinks they know the answer to.

What's Next

Watch for an announcement from Alonso before or during the Dutch GP weekend (August 21-23). If he has confirmed retirement, Aston Martin will need to announce their 2027 driver lineup — an announcement that would carry its own significant news value. If he decides to continue, the story becomes the 2027 car under Newey and whether Alonso gets the competitive season he has been waiting for. Either way, the summer break is ending, and a decision is due.
