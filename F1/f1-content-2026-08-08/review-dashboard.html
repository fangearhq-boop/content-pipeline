Following his DNF at the British Grand Prix, it became mathematically impossible for Verstappen to be within the top two in the Drivers' Championship by the end of July 2026 — the threshold written into his contract. His exit clause activated. He can act on it between now and October 2026. If triggered, Red Bull receives no financial compensation. Here is everything we know: **Why the clause exists.** The exit clause was specifically written into Verstappen's Red Bull contract due to his "longstanding apprehension" about the new 2026 Formula 1 regulations. He was never fully confident the new power unit architecture — built around a 50/50 ICE/electric split — would suit Red Bull's approach. His concerns proved prescient: Red Bull has not won a race in the 2026 season. **What Red Bull says.** The team is maintaining a confident public stance. Red Bull insists they have "a key advantage" in their hunt for their first 2026 win. Whether that claim holds up on track remains to be seen — but it is the posture they are taking heading into the second half of the season. **What Verstappen says.** Very little. Planet F1 reported this week that he has "refused to discuss" his future. That silence is notable — a driver comfortable with his situation typically doesn't decline comment. **The Dutch GP.** Verstappen's home race is August 21-23, two weeks away. It is also Zandvoort's final Formula 1 race ever. If there is a moment for a dramatic announcement — in either direction — the week before that race, or the race itself, would be it. The clock is running. The window is open. The most consequential driver decision in years is coming. 💬 Does Verstappen stay at Red Bull and try to fix it from within — or does he walk away? ### Long-Form Post 🔴 Lewis Hamilton is 50 points behind Kimi Antonelli heading into the second half of the 2026 Formula 1 season. With 291 points still available across the remaining 11 rounds, that gap is completely closeable. And for the first time since arriving at Ferrari, there are real reasons to believe Hamilton is hitting his stride. Here is the state of play: **The championship math.** Antonelli leads with 219 points. Hamilton has 169. George Russell sits third at 160. Mercedes leads the Constructors' Championship at 379 points, with Ferrari roughly 72 back. The Dutch GP at Zandvoort on August 23 is the first race back — and the first race of the second half. **Hamilton's 2026 form.** He won the Spanish Grand Prix in Barcelona, an emotional victory that signaled something had clicked. He has five podiums in 2026, and has not finished outside the top six all season. Perhaps most striking: in qualifying, Hamilton is now 0.017 seconds per lap faster than Charles Leclerc on average — a complete reversal from 2025, when Leclerc outqualified him by 2.5 tenths. **The engine chase.** Ferrari's current deficit to Mercedes is primarily a power unit gap. ESPN reported this week that Ferrari is specifically "chasing power" in the second half. Toto Wolff, meanwhile, issued what RacingNews365 called a "striking engine claim" countering Ferrari's narrative. The team that closes the PU gap first will likely determine whether Hamilton can threaten Antonelli. The second half of 2026 starts in two weeks. The title is alive. Hamilton knows it. 💬 Can Hamilton overcome 50 points in 11 rounds? Who's your bet for the 2026 F1 champion? ---
