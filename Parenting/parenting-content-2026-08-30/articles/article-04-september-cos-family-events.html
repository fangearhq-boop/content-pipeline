September doesn't have to mean a full calendar of paid activities to keep kids engaged after school. Two free family events are coming up in Colorado Springs this month — one a music evening with food trucks, the other a stroller-friendly morning hike — and both are worth adding to the calendar now.

Music in the Park — September 10 at Piñon Valley Park

The city's Music in the Park series returns on September 10, this time at Piñon Valley Park. The format is straightforward: bring blankets and lawn chairs, find a spot on the grass, and settle in for an evening with a local live band. Food trucks will be on-site for dinner. There's also a youth kickball game and family-friendly giveaways, which tends to make the event feel more like a neighborhood block party than a city concert.

Admission is free. The event is organized through the City of Colorado Springs Parks, Recreation, and Cultural Services department, so weather-related updates and any time changes would be posted to coloradosprings.gov/PRCS. This is one of those low-effort, high-enjoyment evenings that costs nothing but tends to linger in a kid's memory longer than you'd expect.

UpaDowna Family Hike — September 12 at Boulder Park

UpaDowna is a Colorado Springs nonprofit that runs free guided hikes through the city's parks and open spaces. The September 12 family hike is at Boulder Park, running from 10 to 11 AM — a one-hour commitment on a route specifically designed to work with strollers.

After the hike, there's a kids' craft activity, which makes it a complete morning outing rather than just a walk. The route is built for all ages, and the stroller-friendly design means families with infants or toddlers who aren't yet walking long distances can participate fully. A few things to know before you go: no pets are allowed on the hike, and all children must have adult supervision throughout.

Registration details are available through the City of Colorado Springs PRCS portal at coloradosprings.gov/PRCS. UpaDowna runs these hikes regularly throughout the year, so if the September 12 date doesn't work, it's worth checking their schedule for upcoming options.

Why These Two Events Stand Out

September can be a hectic month for Colorado Springs families — school routines are still getting locked in, fall sports schedules are ramping up, and the pull toward screens after a long school week is real. Both of these events offer a structured reason to get outside without adding cost or logistical complexity. One evening of live music and food trucks, one morning hike with a craft at the end. Neither requires a lot of planning.

These are the kinds of events that make it easier to say yes. The fact that they're free removes the usual hesitation.

What's Next

Check coloradosprings.gov/PRCS for event times, any registration requirements, and updates closer to the dates. Music in the Park events sometimes adjust start times based on sunset, and UpaDowna hikes can reach capacity, so early registration is worth doing if the September 12 date is on your radar. For the full city events calendar — including events further into fall — the PRCS site is the most current source for Colorado Springs parks programming.
