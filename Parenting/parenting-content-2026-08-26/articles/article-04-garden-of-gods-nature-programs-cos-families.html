Garden of the Gods Free Nature Programs: An Outdoor Learning Day for Colorado Springs Families

By Jamie Rivera | COS Parenting | August 26, 2026

One of Colorado Springs' most remarkable assets for families costs nothing and is open year-round. Garden of the Gods — the 1,341-acre natural park of red sandstone formations at 1805 N. 30th Street — is free to enter, easy to access, and home to programming that turns a trail walk into an educational experience for children.

Among the available experiences is a hands-on nature detective program for children, in which participants learn to identify animal tracks, scat, and other wildlife signs found throughout the park. It is the kind of outdoor science that engages children's natural curiosity in a way that structured classroom time often cannot replicate.

Quick Reference

  • Location: Garden of the Gods, 1805 N. 30th Street, Colorado Springs
  • Admission: Free (year-round)
  • Parking: Free
  • Nature programs: Nature detective program — animal tracks, scat, wildlife signs
  • Starting point: Visitor and Nature Center
  • Best time to visit: Early morning (cooler, fewer crowds)
  • More info: gardenofgods.com

What the Nature Detective Program Offers

The nature detective program is a hands-on activity designed for children that focuses on reading the signs animals leave behind. Participants learn to identify different types of animal tracks, understand what scat can tell you about a local animal's diet and habits, and spot other signs of wildlife that are easy to miss on a casual walk.

These skills turn the park into an active puzzle rather than a scenic backdrop. Children who might disengage on a straightforward hike often find a purpose when they have something specific to look for. The approach is rooted in standard nature education practices and requires no supplies beyond attention and curiosity.

The Visitor and Nature Center is the recommended starting point for families unfamiliar with the programming. Staff there can provide trail maps, point families toward the routes most likely to have recent wildlife activity, and explain what to look for at different times of year.

Practical Information for Colorado Springs Families

Garden of the Gods is open year-round, and entry is free for everyone. Parking is also free. The park includes both paved and natural surface trails at different difficulty levels, making it accessible to families with younger children as well as older ones ready for longer hikes.

In late August, afternoon temperatures in Colorado Springs can still reach the high 70s or low 80s. Early morning visits — before 10 AM — are typically cooler and quieter. The park's elevation and exposure also mean sun protection is important regardless of temperature. Water, sunscreen, and hats are worth bringing.

Dogs are welcome on leash in most areas of the park, which makes it a viable destination for families who want to combine a family outing with a dog walk. Check park signage for specific trail restrictions.

Why This Week Is a Good Time to Go

Colorado Springs families are at a point in the school year — weeks two through four depending on district — where routines are forming but weekends still have some flexibility before fall activities fully take over. The Garden of the Gods represents exactly the kind of low-effort, high-reward family outing that works before the season fills up with school obligations, sports schedules, and homework commitments.

Late August conditions at the park are typically good for wildlife observation. Temperatures are moderating, and the park's varied terrain — from open meadows to rocky outcroppings — supports a range of animal activity including birds, squirrels, deer, and occasionally larger wildlife passing through.

What's Next

For families looking to extend their outdoor learning, Cheyenne Mountain State Park (410 JL Ranch Heights Rd) is a nearby option with additional trail and camping options. The Colorado Farm and Art Market at Alamo Square Park runs every Wednesday through October 9, making it easy to combine a morning market visit with an afternoon at Garden of the Gods. Both are low-cost options for Colorado Springs families looking to close out August with outdoor time before fall schedules tighten.
