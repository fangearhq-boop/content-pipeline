### STORY 1: 2026 National Parent Survey — Quality Time

**Long-Form Post:**

A new national survey of more than 5,000 parents makes one thing clear: what families want most is more time.

The 2026 National Parent Survey, conducted by NORC at the University of Chicago for New America, found that 72 percent of parents wish for more quality time with their children. That number cuts across income levels, geography, and family structure. The desire is nearly universal.

But for lower-income families, the wish runs into a wall. Nearly half — 47 percent — of parents with household income below 100 percent of the federal poverty level say they simply cannot spend their time the way they want because they cannot afford to. One in four of those lower-income working parents has shifted to gig or platform work specifically because the schedule flexibility it offers is worth the trade-offs.

The survey also found that more than half of parents name child care cost as the primary barrier to getting the care arrangement they would ideally want.

For Colorado Springs families navigating these pressures: the Colorado Child Care Assistance Program (CCAP) provides financial help for families who qualify. El Paso County DHS administers the program locally at 719-444-5000, and information is also available at cdhs.colorado.gov/ccap.

### STORY 2: Goody King Magnetic Building Cubes Recall

**Long-Form Post:**

If you have Goody King magnetic building cube sets in your home, stop using them now.

The Consumer Product Safety Commission has recalled approximately 213,500 Goody King Magnetic Building Cubes and Blocks, imported by Yi Suen Commerce. These magnetic cube sets were sold on Amazon between January 2024 and July 2026 in sets of 45, 56, 100, 120, 150, and 300 cubes across multiple themes — forests, dinosaurs, unicorns.

The problem: the cubes can break or open, releasing high-powered magnets. When multiple high-powered magnets are ingested, they can attract each other inside the body, causing intestinal perforations, blockages, and twisting — conditions that can lead to blood poisoning and death. Two children have already required surgery after ingesting magnets from these sets.

What to do: Stop using immediately and contact Yi Suen Commerce for a full refund. The company will send a prepaid shipping label once you register the recall.

Check your home. This recall covers a large number of units — more than 200,000 sets sold over two and a half years. These are a popular product category. If you have any magnetic building cube sets (Goody King brand or similar products with small powerful magnets), verify the brand now.

### STORY 4: Garden of the Gods Nature Programs

**Long-Form Post:**

Before fall schedules fill up, one of the best things Colorado Springs families can do with their children is completely free.

Garden of the Gods — the 1,341-acre sandstone formation park at 1805 N. 30th Street — is open year-round with no admission charge. Beyond just walking the trails, the park offers a hands-on nature detective program for children: participants learn to identify animal tracks, scat, and other wildlife signs found throughout the park. It is the kind of outdoor science that children often find more engaging than a classroom lesson.

The park is accessible to most families, with paved and unpaved trails at different difficulty levels. Early morning visits are typically quieter and cooler, especially as Colorado Springs temperatures in late August can still reach the high 70s or 80s.

A few practical notes: the park's Visitor and Nature Center is a good starting point for programs and trail maps. Parking is free. The nature detective activities require nothing more than attention and curiosity.

If you are looking for a low-cost way to spend time outdoors before the school week fully consumes your schedule, Garden of the Gods is one of Colorado Springs' best family resources.

### STORY 5: Back-to-School Sleep Tips

**Long-Form Post:**

The first few weeks of school are exciting — and exhausting. Most children in Colorado Springs are now in week two, three, or four of the new school year, but many families are still working through the summer-to-school sleep transition.

The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends that school-age children (ages 6–12) get 9 to 12 hours of sleep per night. Teenagers (ages 13–18) need 8 to 10 hours. These are not aspirational targets — insufficient sleep in school-age children is linked to attention problems, mood changes, and lower academic performance.

A few strategies that help with the back-to-school sleep adjustment:

Set a consistent bedtime and stick to it even on weekends. The body's circadian rhythm responds to routine, and inconsistent weekend schedules are one of the most common causes of ongoing adjustment problems.

Limit screens 30 to 60 minutes before bed. The AAP specifically recommends this as part of healthy sleep habits for children. This applies to both young children and teenagers.

Get morning light exposure. Opening curtains or going outside shortly after waking helps signal to the body that the day has started, which makes falling asleep at the right time easier.

One factor specific to Colorado Springs families: the city sits at approximately 6,035 feet in elevation. Altitude can affect sleep quality for some people, particularly those who have recently arrived or whose bodies are still adapting. If sleep disturbances are persistent and seem unrelated to schedule, elevation may be a contributing factor worth discussing with a pediatrician.
