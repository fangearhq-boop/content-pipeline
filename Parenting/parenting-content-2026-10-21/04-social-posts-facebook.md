### STORY 1: Colorado Flu — First Child Death

**Long-Form Post (8:00 AM MT):**

Colorado has confirmed its first child flu death of the 2026-27 season — and the season is only getting started.

The child was elementary school age, from outside the Denver metro, and had not received the current flu vaccine. State health officials are reporting 46 flu hospitalizations in the most recent weekly count, up from just 8 in early September. That's a steep climb, and CDPHE is urging all Coloradans 6 months and older to get vaccinated now — "ideally before Halloween gatherings," the agency said.

For context: last season (2025-26) was the worst on record for Colorado, with nearly 6,000 flu hospitalizations and 8 pediatric deaths. The flu vaccine has been updated for the 2026-27 season to target all three circulating strains, including influenza A H3N2. The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends the flu shot for all children 6 months and older. Colorado's child flu vaccination rate currently sits at about 32% — meaning nearly seven in 10 kids in the state are heading into Halloween season unprotected.

If your child hasn't been vaccinated yet, your pediatrician, Walgreens, King Soopers, and most pharmacies in Colorado Springs are offering the shot now. Don't wait for a sick child to be the reminder. 💉

### STORY 2: AAP COVID Vaccine Guidance

**Long-Form Post (10:00 AM MT):**

The American Academy of Pediatrics just updated its COVID-19 vaccine guidance for kids — and it's more definitive than what the federal government is recommending.

On October 19, the AAP released its 2026-27 policy. Here's the breakdown: all infants between 6 and 23 months should be vaccinated. Kids 2-18 who are at high risk — including those who are immunocompromised, unvaccinated, or living with high-risk household members — should receive a single dose. For other kids 2-18, the vaccine should be offered if a parent or guardian wants it.

The reason this matters: the federal government has moved COVID-19 vaccines into a "shared decision-making" category, meaning your doctor isn't automatically recommending it for every child. The AAP disagrees and is taking a stronger stance. Context that explains why: fewer than 4% of recently hospitalized eligible children were up to date on COVID vaccines.

If you have questions about your child's COVID vaccine status, the best step is a quick call to your Colorado Springs pediatrician before the fall and winter respiratory season hits.

### STORY 3: Halloween Weekend Events

**Long-Form Post (12:00 PM MT):**

The Halloween weekend before Oct 31 is finally here — and Colorado Springs is bringing the fun.

This Saturday, October 24, is when things really get wild in Manitou Springs. The 32nd Annual Emma Crawford Coffin Races draw an estimated 10,000 people to watch (and cheer for) teams of five racing decorated coffins uphill. It's completely free and genuinely unlike anything else in Colorado. Road closures begin at 9 AM. The whole spectacle runs 10 AM–5 PM — bring the kiddos, a lawn chair, and a sense of humor.

Also Saturday: the Downtown Colorado Springs Candy Crawl (2–5 PM, 20+ businesses, free) is a great option for younger kids who aren't quite ready for the coffin race vibe. Sunday brings the World Ballet Company's Swan Lake at Pikes Peak Center (6 PM, ages 5+, live orchestra) for families wanting something a little more elegant with their Halloween weekend. And Monster Jam runs all three nights at Broadmoor World Arena.

Don't forget: Boo at the Zoo runs through Sunday (4–8:30 PM each night), Pumpkins in the Park is free at Bancroft Park on Saturday (noon–6 PM), and Venetucci Farm is open both days for pumpkin picking ($8 per person, $10/pumpkin, hayrides $5). 🎃

### STORY 4: D49 Student Success Center

**Long-Form Post (3:00 PM MT):**

If you have a D49 child in PEAK, Elevates, preschool, or the Base49 before/after care program, here's an update worth knowing: District 49 opened its new Student Success Center at the former Falcon Elementary site when school started in August.

The center brings several specialized programs together under one roof — in a permanent building with a full gymnasium, cafeteria, and life-skills equipment. Before the SSC opened, many of these programs operated out of aging modular classrooms. PEAK (the district's gifted and advanced program) and Elevates (specialized instruction and life-skills classes) now have a real home. Two full-time preschool classrooms and Base49 care are also at the site.

Falcon Elementary School of Technology, which was previously at that location, has relocated to the Bennett Ranch Elementary campus. The D49 school board holds its regular meeting today, October 21.

If you're a D49 family trying to figure out where a program is now located, d49.org has the updated campus information.

### STORY 5: Rock Ledge Ranch Homeschool Day

**Long-Form Post (5:00 PM MT):**

One week from today, Rock Ledge Ranch Historic Site is hosting its Fall Homeschool Day — and it's a genuinely wonderful outing for curious kids of any age.

On Wednesday, October 28, from 10 AM to 2 PM, families can take a self-paced tour of the historic site near Garden of the Gods and dig into a variety of hands-on activities. One of the highlights: making a cornhusk doll at the Homestead Cabin while learning about 1860s pioneer life in Colorado. There's also farming and ranching history, railroad lore, and — a perennial crowd-pleaser — a blacksmithing demonstration.

Admission is approximately $4 per person (children 2 and under are free), and parking is free at the Garden of the Gods Visitor Center. The activities are mostly outdoors, so dress in layers — late October mornings in Colorado can be brisk. D11 kids are back in school by Oct 28, but it's a great mid-week break for homeschool families, and a Saturday-alternative outing for anyone who wants a quieter, hands-on experience.

Check rockledgeranch.com to confirm current pricing and registration before you go.
