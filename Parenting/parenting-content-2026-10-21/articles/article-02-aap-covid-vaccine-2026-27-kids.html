AAP's New COVID Vaccine Guidance: What It Means for Your Kids This Fall

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting | October 21, 2026

The American Academy of Pediatrics just released its 2026-27 COVID vaccine recommendations — and it takes a clearer stance than what federal agencies are currently advising.

Two days ago, the American Academy of Pediatrics published its 2026-27 COVID-19 vaccine policy for children and adolescents. If your pediatrician brings it up at your next appointment, here's what the guidance actually says — and why it differs from what you might have heard from federal health agencies.

The AAP recommends that all infants between 6 and 23 months receive the updated COVID vaccine. Infants who have already completed an earlier vaccination series should receive one dose of the current formulation. Infants who have not yet been vaccinated should begin the series. For children between 2 and 18 who are at high risk — including those who are immunocompromised, living in congregate care settings, unvaccinated, or living with high-risk household members — the AAP recommends a single dose. For other children 2 to 18, the vaccine is offered if a parent or guardian requests it.

Why the AAP and Federal Agencies Are Not Aligned

The difference between what your pediatrician may recommend and what the federal government is currently encouraging has a direct explanation. HHS and the CDC have moved COVID-19 vaccines into a "shared decision-making" framework, meaning they no longer universally recommend the vaccine for all children the way they do for flu. The AAP has declined to follow that approach.

The AAP's reasoning is grounded in a stark statistic: fewer than 4% of recently hospitalized eligible children were up to date on COVID vaccines. That figure reflects how far vaccination rates have dropped, and it informs the pediatric organization's judgment that a stronger recommendation — not just an offer — is warranted for the youngest and most vulnerable patients.

What This Means for Colorado Springs Families

In practice, the new guidance means your child's doctor may proactively recommend the COVID vaccine at upcoming well visits, particularly for infants and for children with chronic health conditions. Families with children 2 to 18 who are otherwise healthy should expect to hear about the option and make a decision together with their pediatrician.

This is not a requirement — there are no school mandates for COVID vaccination in Colorado. But for families trying to understand the current medical consensus, the AAP's position is now clear: for infants and high-risk older children, the recommendation is to vaccinate. For other children, the vaccine is available and the conversation is worth having.

Colorado's respiratory virus season is already beginning in earnest. Flu hospitalizations are up sharply, and COVID-19 continues to circulate alongside RSV and other respiratory viruses through fall and winter. The AAP's guidance is issued with that seasonal context in mind.

What's Next

The updated COVID vaccine is currently available at most pediatric offices and pharmacies in Colorado Springs. If you have questions about your child's vaccination history or eligibility, contact your pediatrician directly. For families navigating insurance coverage, the AAP's website at healthychildren.org has updated guidance on which vaccines are covered under the Vaccines for Children program and private insurance. The AAP's full 2026-27 policy statement is available at publications.aap.org.
