Colorado Confirms First Child Flu Death of 2026-27 Season: What COS Families Need to Do Before Halloween

By Jamie Rivera | COS Parenting | October 21, 2026

The 2026-27 flu season has claimed its first young life in Colorado — and health officials say the window to protect your family is closing fast.

Colorado health officials have confirmed the first pediatric flu death of the 2026-27 season. The child was elementary school age, lived outside the Denver metro area, and had not received the current flu vaccine. The Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment is urging all Coloradans 6 months and older to get vaccinated now — specifically before Halloween gatherings.

The timing is not reassuring. State flu hospitalization numbers have jumped from 8 in early September to 46 in the most recent weekly count. That's a nearly sixfold increase in about six weeks, and the season is still early. CDPHE's guidance is direct: vaccinate before end of October, ideally before trick-or-treating and fall parties bring extended family and community contacts together.

Last Season Was the Worst on Record

Context matters here. The 2025-26 flu season was the most severe Colorado has recorded since the state began tracking hospitalizations in 2004. Nearly 5,931 people were hospitalized with flu-related illness, and 1,169 of those were children. Eight children died. That season also saw Colorado's child flu vaccination rates lag — and this year, the state's vaccination rate for kids sits at approximately 32.3%, meaning roughly seven out of 10 children are heading into peak flu season without protection.

The 2026-27 vaccine has been updated to target the strains currently circulating, including influenza A H3N2. The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends the flu shot annually for all children 6 months and older, and that recommendation was reaffirmed this August.

Why Halloween Matters for Flu Season

CDPHE's specific mention of Halloween gatherings reflects a straightforward epidemiological reality: trick-or-treating, fall parties, and school events in late October accelerate community spread. Respiratory viruses move faster when children spend time in close contact, indoors or in crowds. Last October, flu activity in Colorado was elevated heading into November — and this year's early case spike suggests a similar or steeper trajectory.

Flu vaccines take about two weeks to build full protection. That means families vaccinating on Halloween are not fully protected until mid-November, when community spread typically reaches its seasonal peak.

What to Watch For

Flu symptoms in children include sudden fever (typically above 100 degrees Fahrenheit), body aches, fatigue, cough, and sore throat. In young children, vomiting and diarrhea are more common than in adults. The key warning signs that require immediate medical attention: difficulty breathing, persistent chest pain, severe or persistent vomiting, confusion, or seizures.

El Paso County families can reach the CDPHE flu information line or visit their pediatrician if symptoms develop. Children in high-risk groups — including those with asthma, diabetes, or weakened immune systems — face higher risk for serious illness and should contact their doctor early if flu symptoms appear.

What's Next

CDPHE will continue updating weekly flu surveillance data through the season. The agency's target is to see vaccination rates climb before November, when community transmission typically peaks. Colorado Springs pediatric offices and most area pharmacies are currently well-stocked with the updated 2026-27 vaccine. If your family hasn't been vaccinated yet, the next few days — before this weekend's Halloween gatherings begin — are the window officials are pointing to.
