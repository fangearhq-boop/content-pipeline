Hands-On History for Kids: Rock Ledge Ranch Fall Homeschool Day Is October 28

By Jamie Rivera | COS Parenting | October 21, 2026

One week from today, Rock Ledge Ranch Historic Site is hosting a self-paced, hands-on learning day open to all families — for about $4 a person.

Rock Ledge Ranch Historic Site, tucked alongside Garden of the Gods in Colorado Springs, is one of those local gems that families often drive past without stopping. Next Wednesday, October 28, is a good reason to actually go.

The Fall Homeschool Day runs from 10 AM to 2 PM and is designed as a self-paced experience — families move through the historic site at their own speed, engaging with hands-on activities along the way. Admission is approximately $4 per person. Children two and under are free. Parking is free at the Garden of the Gods Visitor Center.

What Families Will Do

The day centers on Colorado frontier life in the 1860s. At the Homestead Cabin, one of the featured activities is making a cornhusk doll — a traditional craft that connects kids to the materials and resourcefulness of pioneer households. The activity sounds simple, but it tends to hold attention longer than expected: it requires patience, fine motor skill, and a bit of creativity.

Beyond the homestead, the site covers several chapters of Colorado history through demonstrations and interactive stations. Families can explore:

  • Farming and ranching — the agricultural practices that defined early Colorado settlement
  • Railroad history — the role of the railroad in connecting the region and shaping communities
  • Blacksmithing — a live or demonstration station showing traditional metalwork

Activities are spread across the outdoor site, so dress in layers. Late October mornings in Colorado can be cold, particularly near the foothills, and the day's programming runs through early afternoon when temperatures are more comfortable.

Who This Is For

The event is called a Homeschool Day, but it's genuinely appropriate for any family with curious kids. It's not a classroom experience — it's a self-paced walkthrough of a working historic site with hands-on components. Elementary and middle school-age kids tend to get the most out of it, though younger children enjoy the outdoor setting and craft activities, and the pricing makes it accessible for larger families.

D11's fall break ends October 23, which means most Colorado Springs school-district families will be back in class by October 28. This makes the weekday timing most convenient for homeschool families — but it's also a viable option for families who want to pull a child for an educational day outing.

What's Next

Confirm current admission pricing and any registration requirements at rockledgeranch.com before you go — the approximately $4 per person figure is based on the site's standard historical pricing, and it's worth a quick check before the visit. The site is open seasonally and runs multiple programming events through October, so the website also has information on any upcoming events after the homeschool day. Rock Ledge Ranch is part of the City of Colorado Springs Parks system; more information is also available at coloradosprings.gov.
