## Story 5: Finnish Screen Time / Cognition Study

**Summary:** 8-year study of 260 children finds more cumulative screen time associated with faster cognitive processing in teens, but with caveats.

**Key Facts:**
- Published: ScienceDaily release November 17, 2026 — HIGH
- Study: 8-year follow-up — HIGH
- Sample size: 260 children (124 girls, 136 boys) — HIGH
- Setting: Finland, PANIC project, University of Jyväskylä + University of Eastern Finland — HIGH
- Average age at follow-up: approximately 15.8 years — HIGH
- Finding: more cumulative childhood screen time → better cognitive processing speed scores at follow-up — HIGH
- Caveat: faster processing paired with lower accuracy in some tasks — HIGH
- Study design: observational only; did not distinguish type of screen activity — HIGH
- Researcher quote: "It depends on what kind of activities children are doing on screen" — Petri Jalanko — HIGH
- Practical implication: content and context of screen use matters more than raw screen time — MEDIUM (researcher inference)

**Sources:**
- https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261116014725.htm
- https://www.jyu.fi/en/news/more-screen-time-since-childhood-associated-with-better-cognitive-processing-in-adolescence

**Confidence:** HIGH — peer-reviewed, university sources, ScienceDaily.
