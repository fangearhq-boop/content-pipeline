D11 Flags 11 Underused Schools — But the District Says No Closures Are Coming

By Jamie Rivera November 23, 2026

At a board work session on November 18, Colorado Springs School District 11 presented enrollment and building capacity data that identified 11 district campuses meeting the state's criteria for being underused. For families who've seen school-closure headlines in other Colorado districts, the question is a reasonable one: is D11 heading that direction?

District leaders were clear: closures are not being pursued. The strategy on the table is co-location — bringing charter schools or other educational programs into buildings that have excess capacity. The board session was a data review, not a vote on any specific action.

What the Enrollment Numbers Show

D11's current enrollment picture is a study in contrasts. The district had 23,458 students in the 2025-26 school year — up from 22,265 the prior year, a healthy year-over-year increase. But that number is still more than 3,000 students below the district's peak enrollment in 2001-02.

Elementary schools are currently operating at 81% of capacity. D11's projections show that number falling to 73% over the next ten years if current trends continue. That gap — between buildings sized for a larger student population and the number of kids actually enrolled — is what creates the "underused" designation.

Under state criteria, a campus is considered underused when its enrollment falls below a certain percentage of its designed capacity. Meeting that criteria doesn't trigger automatic action from the state, but it does give districts a data point for planning conversations.

Co-Location: What It Means for D11 Families

Co-location is a strategy several large urban districts have used when they face the same math D11 is navigating: buildings built for more students than currently attend. Under a co-location model, a charter school, district program, or community organization shares physical space in an underutilized building. The neighborhood school typically continues operating; it simply shares hallways, common areas, or unused wings with another program.

The practical implications for families vary depending on how co-location is structured. Shared cafeteria and gym time, staggered schedules, and separate entrances are common accommodations. D11 has not announced specific co-location pairings at this point — the November 18 session was a data presentation, with implementation details still to be developed.

What This Does and Doesn't Mean

For the vast majority of D11 families, the November 18 presentation changes nothing about their child's school day or assignment. No closures were announced, no specific schools were named for action, and the co-location planning is in an early stage.

What the data does signal is that D11 is managing a long-term capacity challenge that will shape district decisions over the next decade. The enrollment trajectory — up year-over-year but still well below the district's peak — reflects broader demographic patterns in Colorado Springs that are playing out across all three major districts.

Parents who have questions about their specific school's capacity status or the co-location planning process can contact D11's community engagement office or attend an upcoming board meeting. D11's community calendar and board meeting schedule are available at d11.org.
