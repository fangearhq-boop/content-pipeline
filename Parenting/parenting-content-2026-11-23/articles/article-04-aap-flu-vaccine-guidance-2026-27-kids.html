AAP 2026-27 Flu Guidance: Annual Vaccine Still Recommended for All Kids 6 Months+

By Sarah Morales November 23, 2026

With Thanksgiving travel coming up and flu season arriving in Colorado, the American Academy of Pediatrics has published its 2026-27 influenza guidance — and the core recommendation hasn't changed: every child 6 months and older should receive an annual flu vaccine, with no medical contraindication.

The recommendation carries more urgency than usual this year. The 2025-26 flu season was classified as high severity, resulting in 190 pediatric deaths nationally. That number is a reminder that influenza isn't a minor inconvenience for all children — and that vaccination is the most reliable protection available.

What Changed This Season

Several updates are relevant for Colorado Springs families scheduling flu vaccines this fall:

Trivalent vaccines only. All US-licensed flu vaccines for the 2026-27 season are now trivalent formulations. The Afluria vaccine has been discontinued. Families who have a vaccine preference based on past experience should ask their pediatrician or pharmacist what formulations are available.

Updated antiviral guidance. The AAP has revised its recommendations for antiviral treatment of children with confirmed or suspected flu. Parents should ask their pediatrician about antiviral options if a child develops flu symptoms — early treatment can reduce the severity and duration of illness.

AAP and CDC guidance now differ. Following changes in federal vaccine scheduling guidance in 2026, the AAP and the CDC now have differing recommendations in some areas. Pediatricians across Colorado Springs have been advised to follow AAP guidance while the federal guidance is unresolved. If you have questions about which vaccines your child should receive, your pediatrician is the right source — not the CDC's public website, which may not reflect the most current clinical guidance for children.

Why Timing Matters Right Now

Thanksgiving is one of the highest-travel periods of the year. Airports, family gatherings, and long car trips create exactly the conditions in which respiratory viruses spread. Children who haven't yet been vaccinated this season are going into those environments without the protection that's available.

Flu vaccination typically takes about two weeks to reach full effectiveness. That means families who schedule a vaccine this week are covered before holiday gatherings hit full swing.

Colorado Springs has multiple options for flu vaccines for children, including pediatric offices across D11, D20, and D49 areas, local pharmacies, and the El Paso County Public Health vaccine clinic. Children under age 8 who are receiving a flu vaccine for the first time, or who received only one dose last season, need two doses spaced at least four weeks apart — confirm timing with your provider.

What's Next

If your children haven't received flu vaccines yet this season, now is a good time to schedule. Contact your pediatrician or visit a local pharmacy — most offer flu vaccines without an appointment. The AAP's full 2026-27 influenza guidance, including specific recommendations for children with high-risk conditions, is available at HealthyChildren.org.
