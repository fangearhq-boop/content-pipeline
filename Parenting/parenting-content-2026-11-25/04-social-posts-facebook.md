### STORY 1: ADHD Classroom Accommodations Study

#### Long-Form Post — 2:00 PM MT

**Long-Form Post:**

A new study published in JAMA Pediatrics is making waves in parenting and education circles, and it's something worth knowing before your next IEP or 504 meeting.

Researchers tested four of the most common classroom accommodations for kids with ADHD: extended time on tests, frequent breaks, standing desks, and low-distraction workspaces. They studied 255 children across K–6 in a summer treatment program setting. The finding: none of the four accommodations produced measurable improvement in outcomes. Some actually reduced academic productivity.

What did work? Behavioral interventions cut rule violations by 37%. Stimulant medication cut them by 61% and increased correct completed assignments by 22%.

The researchers are careful to note that this study took place in a summer treatment program, not a traditional school year classroom. That context matters. But the findings are striking enough that they're worth raising with your child's school team.

Have you found classroom accommodations helpful for your kids? Or have you noticed a gap between what's on the IEP and what's actually working?

### STORY 2: Cipton Bike Helmet Recall + Bambora Sling Warning

#### Long-Form Post — 2:30 PM MT

**Long-Form Post:**

Two safety alerts this week that Colorado Springs parents should know about before the holiday shopping season kicks off.

First: a CPSC recall for approximately 16,730 Cipton youth bicycle helmets. The model is CPSK300CC — small size, white with a built-in LED strip. These were sold at Dick's Sporting Goods and JCPenney between April 2024 and May 2026 for about $35. The helmets fail federal safety standards for impact protection and certification. If you have one: stop using it immediately, cut the straps, write "Recalled" on the helmet, and email a photo for a full refund.

Second: a CPSC warning (not a formal recall, because the company did not cooperate) for Bambora sling carriers sold on Bamboraco.com for $59–$118. The restraint strap can separate, creating a fall hazard for infants. Because Bambora didn't respond to the CPSC, there's no refund program. The guidance is to stop use immediately, cut the fabric in half, and dispose of it.

If you're doing holiday shopping for baby gear or kids' bikes, it's worth a quick check at cpsc.gov/recalls before you finalize your cart.

Which product do you usually check for recalls before buying — car seats, strollers, something else?

### STORY 3: Flu Surge

#### Long-Form Post — 4:00 PM MT

**Long-Form Post:**

With Thanksgiving travel starting tomorrow, there's one thing worth talking about before your family hits the road or the gathering hall: flu.

Colorado is seeing an unusually early flu season this year. CDPHE has documented rising cases across the state, and Colorado has already reported the first pediatric flu death of the 2026-27 season. It's not a cause for panic — but it is a reason to be thoughtful about Thanksgiving gatherings, especially if you're around grandparents, infants, or anyone immunocompromised.

The AAP recommends flu shots for everyone six months and older. One thing to know this season: the Afluria vaccine has been discontinued, so the available options are trivalent vaccines. If you're not sure what your family got or whether it covered this season's strains, your pediatrician or local pharmacy can help you sort it out.

Locally, El Paso County Public Health investigated a flu and COVID outbreak at a COS elementary school in early November. It's a reminder that flu travels fast in school communities and that Thanksgiving break's timing isn't coincidental — it falls right as transmission usually spikes.

How is your family handling the holiday health question this year — are you asking guests to test, skipping gatherings, or going ahead as normal?

### STORY 4: COS Holiday Season Kickoff

#### Long-Form Post — 5:30 PM MT

**Long-Form Post:**

December is almost here, and Colorado Springs has a solid holiday season lineup for families. Here's what's coming up and when.

The Cheyenne Mountain Zoo Electric Safari opens December 2 and runs through January 1, 2027 — night-time zoo experience, lights throughout the grounds, worth booking early for popular dates. Important heads-up: the zoo is closing at 3 PM starting tomorrow, November 26, through the end of the season to prepare for setup. Check cmzoo.org to plan your visit.

The Festival of Lights Parade in downtown Colorado Springs returns for its 36th year on December 5 at 5:50 PM on Tejon Street. It's free, family-friendly, and one of those COS traditions that gets better when you bring the kids.

Christkindlmarket at Bancroft Park (2408 W Colorado Ave) runs December 4–6, 9–13, and 16–23, noon to 8 PM daily. Free admission. Good for grabbing a hot drink and letting the kids browse the market stalls without it costing you at the door.

And the Manitou Springs Elf Hunt returns this holiday season — a free scavenger hunt through Manitou shops that the little ones genuinely enjoy. Check manitousprings.org for the 2026 dates.

### STORY 5: Thanksgiving Weekend in COS

#### Long-Form Post — 6:00 PM MT

**Long-Form Post:**

The Turkey Trot is tomorrow morning — if you're running it, good luck (we'll be there in spirit at the starting line). But Thanksgiving weekend in Colorado Springs runs through Sunday, and there's more to do after the food settles.

Skate in the Park at Acacia Park is open daily this season. It's $15 per person with skates included, and kids four and under get in free with a paid adult. Tickets are sold on site only, so no advance booking required. It's a solid midday activity when you need something to do with restless kids post-Turkey Day.

Garden of the Gods is always free and open to the public, but Thanksgiving weekend is genuinely one of the nicer times to visit — crisp air, manageable crowds, and the red rock formations don't get old. Hours are 5 AM to 9 PM through April. The Visitor Center is closed on Thanksgiving and Christmas, but the park itself stays open.

Locally, the Old Colorado City Christmas Stroll traditionally falls on the Saturday after Thanksgiving — meaning this year, that's November 28. It's a neighborhood tradition, historically free and starting around 4 PM. Check shopoldcoloradocity.com for confirmed 2026 details.

Miramont Castle Victorian Christmas also typically returns for its holiday season around this time. Worth checking miramontcastle.org for 2026 dates and pricing.
