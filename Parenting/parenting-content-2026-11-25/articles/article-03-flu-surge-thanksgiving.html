Flu Is Surging Early in Colorado — What COS Parents Need to Know Before Thanksgiving

By Jamie Rivera | November 25, 2026 | COS Parenting

Colorado's flu season is running ahead of schedule this year. The Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment has documented a rise in cases and flu-related hospitalizations over the past several weeks — an unusually early surge for a state that typically sees flu peak in January and February. The state has also reported the first pediatric flu death of the 2026-27 season.

With Thanksgiving travel starting tomorrow and family gatherings over the next four days, it's worth taking a moment to understand where we are in the season and what families in Colorado Springs can do about it.

Why the Timing Matters

Thanksgiving is one of the highest-travel weekends of the year. That means airports, rest stops, and relatives' homes filled with people who've been in contact with different communities across the country. Flu spreads easily in those conditions — and it spreads before most people know they're sick.

Children are particularly efficient transmitters of flu. They shed the virus in higher quantities than adults, for longer periods, and they're often in contact with extended family members — grandparents, elderly relatives — who face higher risk of serious complications. If your kids were in school last week, they may have been exposed.

Earlier this month, El Paso County Public Health investigated a flu and COVID outbreak at a local COS elementary school. That serves as a reminder that flu is already circulating in our community, not just in distant cities.

What to Know About This Season's Flu Vaccine

The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends flu shots for everyone six months and older, every season. That recommendation hasn't changed.

What has changed this season is vaccine availability. The Afluria flu vaccine has been discontinued and is no longer available. The vaccines your pediatrician or pharmacy will have this year are trivalent flu vaccines — meaning they protect against three strains. If you haven't gotten flu shots for your family yet, you can still get them; they're worth getting even partway through the season.

If you're unsure whether your family's flu shots cover this season's circulating strains, your pediatrician or any major pharmacy in Colorado Springs can help you check.

Practical Steps for Thanksgiving Weekend

None of this means you need to cancel your Thanksgiving plans. It does mean it's worth being thoughtful about a few things. If your child has a fever, respiratory symptoms, or just seems off — even mildly — keeping them home from gatherings protects the people around them, especially older relatives. Flu symptoms can appear quickly and escalate fast in young children and the elderly.

Encourage handwashing before and after eating, before greeting relatives, and after using public restrooms during travel. It's basic, but it still helps. And if anyone in your household does develop flu symptoms over the holiday weekend, your pediatrician's after-hours line or an urgent care clinic in COS can help you assess whether a flu test or treatment is needed.

For families traveling home after Thanksgiving, keep in mind that the return trip is often when exposure is highest. What your family brings back from out of state tends to show up as a school absence the following week.

What's Next

For the latest CDPHE flu activity data for Colorado, visit cdphe.colorado.gov/flu. The AAP's up-to-date flu guidance for children and families is available at healthychildren.org. If you need a flu shot in Colorado Springs, your pediatrician's office, Walgreens, CVS, King Soopers pharmacy, and most urgent care clinics carry flu vaccine.
