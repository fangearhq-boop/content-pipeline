Thanksgiving Weekend in Colorado Springs: What to Do with Kids Nov 27–30

By Jamie Rivera | November 25, 2026 | COS Parenting

The Turkey Trot is tomorrow morning and the Nutcracker opens Thursday — if you need details on those, we covered them in yesterday's guide. But Thanksgiving weekend runs through Sunday, and there are four days of Colorado Springs to work with. Here's what else is on the table for families with kids.

Skate in the Park — Acacia Park

Skate in the Park returned to Acacia Park this November, and it's one of the more accessible holiday activities COS has to offer. Admission is $15 per person, skates included. Kids four and under get in free when accompanied by a paying adult. Tickets are sold on site — no advance booking needed, just show up.

Acacia Park is in the heart of downtown Colorado Springs, which makes it easy to pair with lunch or a coffee stop before or after. The rink is open daily this season, so there's no specific window to worry about. Thanksgiving afternoon and Friday are both good days if the morning is taken up with the Turkey Trot or other plans.

It's worth having a backup plan for parking — the downtown area can get busy over the holiday weekend. The parking garages off Tejon and Cascade are usually your best options.

Garden of the Gods — Always Free

Garden of the Gods is always free and open to the public year-round. During November through April, the park is open from 5 AM to 9 PM. That means Thanksgiving morning, Thanksgiving afternoon, Saturday, Sunday — any of those work. The park is open on Thanksgiving Day itself; the Visitor and Nature Center is closed, but the park grounds and trails are fully accessible.

Thanksgiving weekend is actually a quieter time to visit than the summer peak, though it's not empty. The cold keeps some visitors away, which means less competition for the best viewpoints. If you've got family in town who haven't seen the red rocks before, this is an easy way to give them a genuine Colorado moment for free.

Dress for it — late November mornings in Colorado Springs can be in the 20s, and the wind picks up at the exposed ridge trails. Kids who are bundled up tend to stay out longer and complain less.

Old Colorado City Christmas Stroll — Saturday, Nov 28

The Old Colorado City Christmas Stroll is an annual neighborhood tradition that falls on the Saturday after Thanksgiving. If the pattern holds for 2026, that's November 28. It's historically a free event starting around 4 PM, with shops open late, lights throughout the neighborhood, carolers, and a general festive energy that works well for families.

Old Colorado City is on the west side of COS along Colorado Avenue — a walkable stretch of independent shops and restaurants that takes on a different character during the holiday season. The stroll has been a neighborhood tradition for decades, and it's the kind of event that's genuinely pleasant rather than crowded and commercial.

Before you plan around it, confirm the 2026 details at shopoldcoloradocity.com — dates and start times can shift year to year.

Miramont Castle Victorian Christmas

Miramont Castle in Manitou Springs typically opens for its Victorian Christmas experience in late November and runs through the holiday season. Tours move through the castle's ornate Victorian rooms decorated for a 19th-century Christmas — it's a genuinely different kind of holiday experience compared to a standard holiday event, and older kids and adults tend to find it memorable.

In 2025, admission was $17 for adults, $15 for seniors (60 and older), and $12 for children 12 and under. The 2026 season dates and pricing haven't been officially confirmed as of publication. Check miramontcastle.org before making plans.

If you're already heading to the Manitou Springs area for the Elf Hunt or other activities, Miramont is at 9 Capitol Hill Ave and makes for a natural add-on.
