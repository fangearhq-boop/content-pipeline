Two Safety Alerts for Colorado Springs Parents: Cipton Helmets Recalled, Bambora Slings Flagged

By Sarah Morales | November 25, 2026 | COS Parenting

Two new safety alerts landed this week from the Consumer Product Safety Commission — one a formal recall, one a warning — and both involve products that may already be in Colorado Springs homes. Here's what you need to know and what to do.

Cipton Bike Helmets Recalled — Full Refund Available

The CPSC announced a recall of approximately 16,730 Cipton youth bicycle helmets on November 19, 2026. The specific model affected is the CPSK300CC — a small-size helmet in white with a built-in LED light strip. These helmets were sold at Dick's Sporting Goods and JCPenney stores between April 2024 and May 2026, priced at approximately $35.

The reason for the recall is that the helmets fail three federal safety standards: impact attenuation, positional stability, and certification requirements. Those are the core functions a bike helmet is supposed to perform — protecting a child's head in a fall and staying in place when it matters. A helmet that doesn't meet these standards shouldn't be on a child's head.

If you have one of these helmets, the remedy is straightforward. Stop using it immediately. Cut the straps so the helmet can't be used again accidentally. Write "Recalled" on the helmet. Then email a photo to the manufacturer for a full refund. For complete instructions and contact information, visit cpsc.gov.

With the holiday shopping season starting, it's worth noting that this model was available as recently as May 2026. If a Cipton helmet was given as a gift in the past couple of years, check the model number on the inside of the helmet before the kids ride again.

Bambora Sling Carriers — CPSC Warning, No Refund

The November 12 Bambora situation is different from a standard recall, and the difference matters for families who may own one.

The CPSC issued a warning — not a formal recall — for Bambora sling carriers sold on Bamboraco.com for $59 to $118. The hazard is a restraint strap that can separate from the carrier, creating a fall hazard for the infant inside. The CPSC's guidance is unambiguous: stop use immediately.

Here's what makes this case different from the Cipton recall: Bambora, the company, did not cooperate with the CPSC. When a company doesn't cooperate, the CPSC can issue a warning but can't compel a formal remedy process. That means there's no refund program, no exchange, and no manufacturer contact to reach out to. The only action available to consumers is to stop using the product and dispose of it.

The CPSC's recommended disposal method is specific: cut the fabric of the sling in half before throwing it away. This prevents the carrier from being reused or passed along to someone else who doesn't know about the warning.

If you purchased a Bambora sling through the company's website or as a gift recently, the safest step is to remove it from use today.

What's Next

For the Cipton helmet recall, visit cpsc.gov for step-by-step refund instructions. For the Bambora warning, visit cpsc.gov/warnings. To check whether any other products in your home have an open recall or warning, search the CPSC database at cpsc.gov/recalls — it's searchable by product type, brand, and date.
