## STORY 1: Babies' Magic Tea Gripe Water Recall

**Long-Form Post (8:00 AM MT)**

⚠️ Heads up for parents using Babies' Magic Tea Gripe Water.

The FDA issued a recall alert on October 7 for lot number 25122bw of this product. Testing found it contains undeclared ethanol — 8.3% — plus methanol and isopropanol. Those are substances that should not be in an infant supplement, full stop.

The product is sold on Amazon and through the Secrets of Tea brand. If you have it at home, the guidance is to stop using it now and either dispose of it or contact the seller. Do not return it to a retail store.

No injuries have been reported, but this is the kind of thing worth checking tonight.

## STORY 3: Halloween Candy Safety / Teal Pumpkin

**Long-Form Post (5:00 PM MT)**

🍬 Halloween is almost here — and if you have a child with a food allergy, the next 11 days are worth using to plan.

A few things worth knowing:

The Teal Pumpkin Project, run by FARE (the Food Allergy Research & Education organization), lets participating households signal that they have non-food treats available alongside candy. If you're handing out candy, putting out a teal pumpkin means allergy families know your door is safe for their kids.

If your child carries an EpiPen, make sure it's accessible on trick-or-treat night — not in a bag at home. For kids old enough, they can carry it themselves in a fanny pack. For younger ones, a parent carries it.

Common allergens to watch for in Halloween candy: peanuts, tree nuts, milk, egg, wheat. A lot of the classic candy-bowl staples have hidden allergens.

FARE's website (foodallergy.org) has a Teal Pumpkin map where you can register your address or find participating houses in your area.

## STORY 5: D11 Bond Vote Nov 3

**Long-Form Post (6:30 PM MT)**

📋 D11 voters: November 3 is two weeks out, and the $775 million bond measure is on the ballot.

We covered what it would cost property owners a couple of weeks ago. Today we want to focus on a different question: what actually gets built if it passes?

The bond covers five main categories. Safety upgrades — security vestibules and updated camera systems across all schools. HVAC replacement — many D11 buildings are running on systems that are 30 or more years old, and air quality matters especially now. Asbestos abatement in older buildings. Career and Technical Education (CTE) lab expansions — D11 has been expanding trade programs, and this would build out more dedicated spaces. And general classroom renovations across the district.

None of those projects are controversial on their own. The debate is whether $775 million is the right number and the right way to fund it.

If you're a D11 family voting in November — what's the part of this that matters most to you?
