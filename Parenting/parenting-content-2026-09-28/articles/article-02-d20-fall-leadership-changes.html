D20 Begins 2026-27 School Year With New Board President After Amy Shandy's Resignation

By Sarah Morales | September 28, 2026

Academy School District 20 families are heading into a new school year with different leadership at the top of the board of education. Board President Amy Shandy resigned in August 2026 after her family relocated out of Colorado for her husband's job, and Vice President Susan Payne has assumed the president role.

The transition comes at an already consequential moment for D20. The board voted in May 2026 to part ways with the district's superintendent, leaving the top administrative position in an interim structure going into the fall. Two major leadership roles have shifted in roughly the same four-month stretch.

The Board President Transition

Shandy's resignation was announced in August, and her final board meeting was September 3, 2026. Her departure was driven by personal circumstances — her family's move out of state — rather than any dispute with the board or district. Board members must reside within the district boundaries to serve, so a move out of Colorado required her to step down.

Susan Payne, who was serving as vice president, has stepped into the board president role. Payne's assumption of the presidency follows standard board succession protocol; the position doesn't require a new election when filled from within existing board leadership. She takes on the role at a time when the board is also managing the superintendent transition.

Superintendent Departure Context

In May 2026, the D20 board voted to part ways with the district superintendent. That vote was reported by multiple Colorado Springs news outlets and came during the final months of the school year. The superintendent's departure put the district's administrative leadership in an interim arrangement heading into summer and fall.

The combination of a board president transition and an interim structure in the superintendent's office means D20 is navigating school year startup with more leadership uncertainty than usual. That said, district officials have consistently noted that daily school operations and instruction aren't being affected by the administrative changes.

What This Means for D20 Families

For most D20 families, the day-to-day experience at their child's school isn't likely to change as a result of these leadership shifts. The district's 26 schools opened on schedule, and there are no reported impacts on instruction, extracurriculars, or services.

Where D20 families may notice a difference is at the board level. The board of education makes decisions about district policy, budget, and leadership — including the superintendent search that's presumably ongoing. Parents who attend board meetings, participate in public comment, or track district decision-making will be engaging with a board that's working through these transitions alongside its normal responsibilities.

The board's next major task on the administrative side is presumably filling the superintendent role on a permanent basis. That process, and its timeline, is one to watch for families who want to understand where D20 is headed.

About Academy School District 20

D20 serves the northern Colorado Springs area and operates 26 schools across elementary, middle, and high school levels. It's one of three major school districts serving Colorado Springs families, alongside D11 and D49.

What's Next

The immediate question for D20 families following these changes is the superintendent search. The district is operating with interim administrative leadership in that role, and a permanent hire will be a significant decision for the board under Payne's leadership. Board meeting schedules and updates on the superintendent search are typically posted on D20's district website. Parents who want to stay informed on the board's direction can follow meeting agendas and attend public sessions.
