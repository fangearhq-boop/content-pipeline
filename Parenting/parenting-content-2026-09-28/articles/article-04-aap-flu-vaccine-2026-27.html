AAP 2026-27 Flu Vaccine Guide: Which Kids Need Two Doses This Season

By Sarah Morales | September 28, 2026

The American Academy of Pediatrics has released its influenza prevention and treatment recommendations for the 2026-27 flu season, and there's a key update that affects a significant share of families with young children. Some kids between six months and eight years old need two flu shots this season instead of one. Here's how to know if your child is in that group and how to get them both doses in time.

Quick Reference: Two-Dose Requirements

Condition Two Doses Required?
First time ever receiving flu vaccine Yes (ages 6 months–8 years)
Received only one lifetime dose before July 1, 2026 Yes (ages 6 months–8 years)
Vaccine history unknown Yes (ages 6 months–8 years)
Received two or more doses before July 1, 2026 No — one dose is sufficient
Timing between doses At least four weeks apart
Target completion Both doses by end of October 2026

Who Needs Two Doses This Season

The two-dose requirement is specifically for children between the ages of six months and eight years. Not every child in that age range needs two doses — the distinction depends on their flu vaccine history.

Children in this age group need two doses if they're getting the flu vaccine for the first time ever, if they've received only one dose total in their lifetime before July 1, 2026, or if their vaccination history is unknown. This last category matters more than it might seem. If a child was vaccinated at a clinic that didn't integrate with a family's current pediatrician, or if records have gaps, the AAP's guidance defaults to treating that history as unknown — which means two doses.

Children nine and older follow the standard single-dose recommendation regardless of their history. Children under six months aren't eligible for flu vaccination at all.

Scheduling the Doses

If your child needs two doses, the doses must be at least four weeks apart. The AAP recommends completing both doses by the end of October. That's a practical deadline because flu season typically starts gaining steam in late fall, and it takes about two weeks after the final dose for full immunity to develop. Getting the second dose in November or December isn't useless, but the protection window is tighter.

If you need to schedule two appointments starting now, that's roughly a four-to-six-week window from today (late September) to get both doses in before the end of October. It's tight but doable if you book quickly. Call your pediatrician or check a local pharmacy to get the first dose on the calendar now.

The two doses don't need to be the same brand or formulation. The AAP explicitly notes that this doesn't matter — whatever your pediatrician or pharmacy has available for the first appointment is fine for the second appointment too.

What Vaccines Are Available This Season

All 2026-27 flu vaccines in the United States are trivalent, meaning they protect against three strains of influenza, with updated compositions for this season's circulating strains. The AAP doesn't express a preference between the available formulations for this age group — egg-based vaccines, cell culture-based vaccines, recombinant vaccines, and the live attenuated intranasal vaccine (LAIV, the nasal spray version) are all acceptable options.

Some families prefer the nasal spray for younger children who are afraid of needles. If that's your situation, confirm with your pediatrician that LAIV is appropriate for your child — it's not recommended for some children with specific health conditions.

Separate From COVID Vaccine Guidance

If you've been following the separate AAP COVID-19 vaccine updates from earlier this week, it's worth noting that this is an entirely different recommendation. The flu guidance and the COVID guidance are independent — they have separate schedules, separate dose structures, and separate considerations. The timing of the announcements was coincidental.

What's Next

If you have a child between six months and eight years old and you're not certain they've had at least two lifetime flu vaccine doses before July 1, 2026, it's worth calling your pediatrician this week to check their records and schedule the first dose. Getting the first appointment on the calendar now is the key step — the four-week gap between doses means there's no time to wait if you want to hit the end-of-October target. The AAP's full 2026-27 influenza prevention and treatment recommendations are available at healthychildren.org.
