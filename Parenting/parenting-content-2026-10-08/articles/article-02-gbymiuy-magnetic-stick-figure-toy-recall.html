CPSC Recalls GBYMIUY Magnetic Stick Figure Toys — Loose Magnets Can Be Fatal If Swallowed

By Jamie Rivera | October 8, 2026

The Consumer Product Safety Commission recalled approximately 10,750 GBYMIUY Magnetic Stick Figure Toy Sets on October 1, 2026. If you have these toys at home, remove them from children immediately.

The sets were sold exclusively on Amazon between April and July 2026 by Ningbo Jiboyou Trading Co., operating under the name GBYMIUY. Each pack contains 30 colorful stick figures with small high-powered magnets embedded in the hands and feet.

The Hazard

The recall was issued because the sets can shed loose magnets. Swallowing a single small magnet may pass without harm, but if a child swallows more than one — or swallows one while another is already in the digestive system — the magnets attract to each other through the intestinal walls. This can cause intestinal perforations, twisting, and blockage, as well as blood poisoning. The injuries can be serious and fast-moving.

No injuries or incidents had been reported at the time of the recall, but high-powered magnet ingestion is well-documented as a cause of emergency surgery in young children. The CPSC classifies it as a potentially life-threatening hazard.

What Families Should Do

Stop using the product immediately and keep it away from children. The CPSC asks families to dispose of the toy set and submit a photo of the disposed product when requesting a refund. To request a refund, email zhangfa0426@outlook.com. Do not attempt to repair, resell, or donate the product.

If you purchased the set on Amazon, you may also be able to initiate a return or refund through Amazon's platform depending on the timing of your purchase.

What's Next

For the most current information on this recall, including any updates, visit cpsc.gov/Recalls and search for "GBYMIUY." Parents with questions about magnet toy safety more broadly can review CPSC's magnet safety guidance at cpsc.gov. If a child may have swallowed a magnet, contact Poison Control (1-800-222-1222) or go to the nearest emergency room immediately.
