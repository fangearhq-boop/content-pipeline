CPSC Warns Parents to Stop Using Mbabyjon Crib Bumpers — Suffocation Risk, Seller Refused Recall

By Sarah Morales | October 8, 2026

Colorado Springs, CO

The Consumer Product Safety Commission issued a consumer safety warning on October 1, 2026 about Mbabyjon padded crib bumpers. If you have this product in your home, the CPSC's guidance is to stop using it immediately and dispose of it. The frustrating part: the seller has refused to cooperate with a recall, which means there's no refund program and no formal remedy offered to families who bought it.

Why Padded Crib Bumpers Are Banned

Padded crib bumpers have been prohibited under federal law since the Safe Sleep for Babies Act was enacted. The ban exists because padded bumpers can obstruct a sleeping infant's airway — an infant who rolls face-first into a padded bumper does not have the muscle control to move away from it. The hazard is suffocation.

The Mbabyjon products are padded crib bumpers that violate this federal prohibition. The CPSC's October 1 consumer warning confirms that these products are illegal to sell in the United States and pose a suffocation risk to infants.

What Makes This Warning Unusual

A typical product safety action results in a recall — the manufacturer or seller agrees to stop selling the product, and a refund or replacement program is set up for consumers who already bought it. That didn't happen here. Mbabyjon, the seller identified by the CPSC as being based in Newark, Delaware, declined to do a voluntary recall.

Without a recall, there's no formal refund program. Families who purchased Mbabyjon crib bumpers are left to dispose of them at their own cost, with no remedy from the seller. The CPSC's consumer warning is the mechanism available when a seller refuses to cooperate — it puts the information in front of families, but it doesn't force a remedy.

If you purchased Mbabyjon crib bumpers and want to pursue a refund, you may be able to initiate a dispute through your credit card company or through the platform where you purchased the product (Amazon, for example, has processes for safety-related returns). The CPSC does not currently have an active refund channel for this product.

What to Do if You Have This Product

The CPSC's guidance is specific: stop using the product immediately, dispose of it, and do not resell it or give it away to anyone else. Reselling or passing along a prohibited product — even secondhand, even for free — is not a safe option. The hazard doesn't go away with a change of ownership.

If you're unsure whether a crib bumper in your home is the Mbabyjon product specifically, the CPSC's warning applies to any padded crib bumper. The ban covers the category, not just this brand. If you have a padded bumper in a crib currently used for an infant, remove it.

Safe Sleep — The Short Version

The AAP's safe sleep guidance is intentionally simple. A safe sleep environment for an infant is a firm, flat mattress with a fitted sheet, and nothing else. No bumpers — padded or mesh. No pillows. No blankets. No positioners or wedges. No stuffed animals.

The simplicity is the point. Every item added to a crib is another potential hazard. Crib accessories show up regularly at secondhand sales, baby showers, and hand-me-down lots — if someone offers you a crib accessory or you're buying one used, check cpsc.gov/Recalls and cpsc.gov for any warnings before putting it near an infant.

What's Next

The CPSC's consumer warning about Mbabyjon padded crib bumpers is available at cpsc.gov. For current recall and warning information on any product, search at cpsc.gov/Recalls. The AAP's full safe sleep guidance — including recommendations on cribs, sleep positions, room sharing, and sleep environment — is at healthychildren.org. If you have questions about your infant's sleep setup, your pediatrician can review it with you.
