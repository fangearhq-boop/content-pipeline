### STORY 2: Magnetic Stick Figure Toy Recall

**Long-Form (8:00 AM MT)**
If you have GBYMIUY Magnetic Stick Figure Toy Sets at home — the ones that come as a pack of 30 figures with small magnets in the hands and feet — the CPSC has recalled them and we'd suggest checking your toy bins today.

The recall covers approximately 10,750 units sold on Amazon between April and July 2026. The problem: the sets can shed loose high-powered magnets. If a child swallows more than one, they attract to each other through intestinal walls. That can cause perforations, twisting of the intestines, blood poisoning, and in serious cases, death. The CPSC confirmed no incidents at the time of the recall, but this is one of those hazards where you really don't want to wait.

The packaging shows "GBYMIUY" and the product ID "X004TVHD2Z." If you find a match, remove it from your kids immediately and email zhangfa0426@outlook.com for a full refund. The CPSC asks that you submit a photo of the disposed product.

This is the kind of hazard that doesn't look dangerous at first glance — small, colorful figures, powerful magnets 🧲. That's exactly why it made the recall list.

### STORY 4: Flu Vaccine October Deadline

**Long-Form (1:00 PM MT)**
We're at the point in October where flu vaccine timing actually matters for the youngest kids — and it's worth a quick check before the month gets away.

If you have a child between 6 months and 8 years old who's getting the flu vaccine for the first time, or who only received one dose last season, they need two doses this year. Those two doses need to be at least four weeks apart. To have both doses on board before flu season typically ramps up (late October through February), families need to start this week.

Children 9 and older, and kids who already got two doses last season, just need one dose. The AAP recommends the flu vaccine annually for all children 6 months and up.

The easiest thing to do is call your pediatrician's office or check with a pharmacy about scheduling. Most do same-week or same-day appointments for vaccines at this time of year 💉.

### STORY 5: Mbabyjon Crib Bumper Warning

**Long-Form (5:00 PM MT)**
This one comes with a frustrating twist: the CPSC issued a safety warning about Mbabyjon padded crib bumpers — but the seller has refused to do a voluntary recall. That means there's no formal remedy, no refund program, no replacement offered.

What there is: a federal ban. Padded crib bumpers have been prohibited under the Safe Sleep for Babies Act because they can obstruct an infant's airway and pose a suffocation risk. Mbabyjon's products violate that ban. The CPSC's October 1 consumer warning says to stop using them immediately, dispose of them, and not sell or give them away to anyone else.

If you're unsure whether your crib setup is safe, the guidance is simple: firm, flat mattress with a fitted sheet. No bumpers. No pillows. No positioners. No blankets 🛏️. This applies to all infants, not just those with Mbabyjon products.

Given how many crib accessories show up at secondhand sales and baby showers, it's worth a reminder: always verify that any item is not subject to a CPSC warning before using it.
