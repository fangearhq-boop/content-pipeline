### STORY 2: Flu Season Starting Early

#### Long-Form Post — 10:00 AM MT
```
Colorado's flu season is arriving earlier than usual this year, and pediatricians are urging parents to get kids vaccinated before Thanksgiving.

Here's the context: last season, 8 Colorado children died from the flu and 1,169 were hospitalized — the highest numbers the state has recorded. UCHealth is already seeing six times more positive flu tests compared to the same time last year.

The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends annual flu shots for all children 6 months and older. The updated vaccine this season targets the dominant variant from last year. Most COS pharmacies and pediatric clinics have doses now.

The timing matters. It takes about two weeks for protection to kick in, so vaccinating in early November means your kids are covered before holiday gatherings.

Has your family gotten flu shots yet this season?
```

### STORY 3: DST First School Day

#### Long-Form Post — 8:00 AM MT
```
If your kiddos were harder than usual to get out the door this morning, you're not imagining it.

Today is the first school day after clocks fell back. That hour "gained" over the weekend didn't actually translate into more rest — it shifted when the body expects to wake up. Your kid's body thinks it's still an hour earlier than the clock says. School starts at the same time it always does, but their internal clock is lagging behind.

The good news: school-age kids typically adjust in about 5-7 days. Here's what actually helps this week:

→ Keep bedtime the same. Don't put kids to bed earlier just because it's dark out — that can backfire and make the adjustment harder.
→ Get outside in the morning. Even 10 minutes of natural light helps reset the body clock.
→ Push screens back after dinner. That hour before bed matters more this week than usual.

Expect grumpy mornings through about Thursday or Friday. It does get better.

How did school drop-off go this morning?
```
