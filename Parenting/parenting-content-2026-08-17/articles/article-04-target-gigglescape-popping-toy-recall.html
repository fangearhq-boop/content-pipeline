Target Recalls 49,000 Gigglescape Under the Sea Popping Toys — Choking Hazard, Full Refund

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting | August 17, 2026

Target has recalled approximately 49,000 Gigglescape Under the Sea Popping Toys after nine reports of the clear plastic dome detaching from the base. In one of those reports, a child began to choke on the small plastic balls inside. If your family owns one, stop using it immediately and return it to any Target store for a full refund, or contact Target for a prepaid return label.

How to Identify the Recalled Toy

The recalled product is a blue plastic toy shaped like a whale. It has a clear dome on top filled with small, brightly colored plastic balls, and a plunger that makes the balls pop and bounce inside when pressed. Parents can identify the recalled toy by looking for the "Gigglescape" name on the front of the packaging and imprinted on the bottom of the blue plastic base. The recall number is 26-598.

The toys were sold exclusively at Target stores across the United States from August 2025 through January 2026, at approximately $10 per unit. This puts them in the price range of impulse buys, stocking stuffers, and party favor bags — which means they may have been passed on to households that didn't originally purchase them.

The Hazard

The clear plastic dome can detach from the blue plastic base. When it does, the small plastic balls inside become accessible to children — a choking hazard for younger kids and toddlers. Nine incident reports of the dome coming loose have been logged; one involved a child who began to choke.

What's Next

This is the second Target recall of 2026 involving children's products sold in the last 12 months — Cat & Jack Toddler Girls' Sequerah Sandals (approximately 211,000 pairs) were recalled earlier this year for decorative pearl detachment. Families with young kids should build a habit of checking the CPSC's recall page monthly, or subscribing to email alerts. Toys under $15 tend to have the widest distribution and the highest chance of ending up in daycare toy bins, grandparent houses, and friends' hand-me-down bags — check outside your own toy box, too.
