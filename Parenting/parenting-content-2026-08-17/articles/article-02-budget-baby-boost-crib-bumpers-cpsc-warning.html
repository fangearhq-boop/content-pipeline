CPSC Warning: Stop Using Budget Baby Boost Crib Bumpers Immediately (Suffocation Hazard)

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting | August 17, 2026

On August 6, 2026, the Consumer Product Safety Commission issued a warning to consumers to immediately stop using Budget Baby Boost Crib Bumpers. The product poses a suffocation risk and violates the federal ban on crib bumpers established under the Safe Sleep for Babies Act. About 141 units were sold. The CPSC's guidance to consumers is direct: stop use immediately, and dispose of them. Do not resell. Do not give away.

The Broader Issue: All Crib Bumpers Have Been Federally Banned Since 2022

The Safe Sleep for Babies Act took effect in June 2022. Since then, the manufacture and sale of crib bumpers of any kind has been federally prohibited in the United States. But older products still turn up. They're passed down from friends and family. They appear in decorative nursery sets purchased from resellers. They arrive in gift boxes from well-meaning relatives who purchased them years ago and never used them.

The August 6 warning is one of several the CPSC has issued this year targeting individual brands that violated the ban — CPLRECR and LDLXLHTE crib bumpers were the subject of similar warnings the same week. The pattern reflects an enforcement posture the CPSC has maintained since the ban took effect: identify the offending sellers, warn the public, and pull the products off marketplaces.

What the CPSC Is Warning About

Crib bumpers — padded liners that wrap the interior sides of a crib — pose a suffocation risk to infants who cannot roll away from soft objects that press against their face. The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends bare cribs only: a firm mattress, a fitted sheet, and nothing else. No bumpers, no blankets, no pillows, no stuffed animals, no positioners.

The Budget Baby Boost warning names one specific product, but the underlying safety guidance covers every crib bumper on the market — including anything that predates the 2022 ban.

What's Next

The CPSC's ongoing enforcement of the crib bumper ban is likely to produce additional warnings in the coming months. For COS families setting up a nursery for a new baby or preparing gear for a second or third child, this is a good moment to do a nursery audit. Remove anything soft from the crib. Confirm the mattress is firm and the sheet fits. Check any product with a manufacture date before mid-2022 against the CPSC's active recalls page. The safe sleep environment is bare, and the guidance is boring on purpose.
