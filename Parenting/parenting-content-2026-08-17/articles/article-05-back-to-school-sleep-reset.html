Back-to-School Sleep Reset: Pediatric Guidance for COS Families One Week In

By Jamie Rivera | COS Parenting | August 17, 2026

D49 is 13 days into the new school year. D11 is 5 days in. D20 opens today. That means every COS family is somewhere in the same window: bedtime slipped over the summer, the morning alarm hurts, and the first-week routines are still finding their footing. The good news is that pediatric sleep guidance is straightforward and it works within about a week.

How Much Sleep Kids Actually Need

The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends 9 to 12 hours per night for children ages 6 to 12, and 8 to 10 hours per night for teens ages 13 to 18. Those totals include the whole night — the count doesn't reset if a kid is up for a bathroom trip or an early wake, but it also doesn't count time spent scrolling in bed. Actual sleep, from lights-out to alarm.

Preschoolers (ages 3-5) need 10 to 13 hours including naps. Toddlers (ages 1-2) need 11 to 14 hours including naps. The younger the kid, the more forgiving the numbers — and the more brutal the fallout if the totals slip.

The 15-Minute Rule

To reset a summer-shifted schedule, move bedtime 15 minutes earlier each night for seven nights. That gradual shift is easier for the body to absorb than a hard reset — a sudden hour-earlier bedtime tends to produce a kid who lies in bed staring at the ceiling and a parent who's annoyed. The 15-minute approach lets the internal clock adjust with less resistance.

Pair the earlier bedtime with an earlier wake time on the same shift, including on weekends for the first two weeks. Weekend sleep-ins undo weekday progress and stretch the reset from one week to three.

What Actually Moves the Needle

Screens off 60 minutes before bed helps more than any other single change. Blue light suppresses melatonin, and social media in particular tends to spike the alertness that bedtime is supposed to lower. Read a book, do a puzzle, take a bath — anything that isn't a screen.

Blackout curtains earn their keep in COS in August. Sunset is after 7:45 PM through the first weeks of school, and bright bedrooms delay the body's natural sleep signal. A dark room at bedtime tells the brain what time it actually is.

Cool the room to 65-68°F. Cooler bedrooms produce deeper sleep. Colorado nights help — open a window early in the evening if the temperature is dropping.

What's Next

Most families are through the reset in seven to ten days. If bedtime is still a fight after day ten, talk to your pediatrician — persistent sleep resistance can be an early signal of allergies, anxiety, or an underlying sleep issue that has nothing to do with routine. For everyone else: coffee for you, dim lights for them, and a quiet week of gradual bedtimes. School will settle by Labor Day.
