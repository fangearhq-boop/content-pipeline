### STORY 2: Budget Baby Boost Crib Bumpers CPSC Warning

**Long-Form Post** (Posting window: 10:00 AM MT)

Federal safety warning that COS families with infants should read now. ⚠️

On August 6, the Consumer Product Safety Commission warned consumers to immediately stop using Budget Baby Boost Crib Bumpers. The product poses a suffocation risk and violates the federal ban on crib bumpers established under the Safe Sleep for Babies Act. About 141 units were sold.

The bigger issue: any crib bumper — from any brand — has been federally illegal to sell since 2022. But they still turn up. Passed down from older siblings. Gifted at baby showers. Bought on secondary marketplaces. If you're setting up a nursery right now, or unpacking gear that was in storage, check.

The CPSC's guidance is direct: stop using immediately, and dispose of them. Do not resell. Do not give away. AAP recommends bare cribs only — a fitted sheet and nothing else.

### STORY 4: Target Gigglescape Popping Toy Recall

**Long-Form Post** (Posting window: 12:30 PM MT)

Toy box check tonight, families. 🐳

Target recalled about 49,000 Gigglescape Under the Sea Popping Toys after nine reports of the clear plastic dome detaching. In one of those reports, a child began to choke on the small plastic balls inside. The toy is a blue plastic whale with a clear dome on top and a plunger — the balls pop and bounce inside when pressed.

It was sold at Target stores nationwide from August 2025 through January 2026 for about $10. The recall number is 26-598. You can identify it by the "Gigglescape" name on the packaging and imprinted on the bottom of the blue plastic base.

If you have one: stop using it immediately. Return it to any Target store for a full refund, or contact Target for a prepaid return label. Toys at this price point tend to end up in stockings, party favor bags, and hand-me-down toy chests, so check with grandparents and daycare too.

### STORY 5: Back-to-School Sleep Reset

**Long-Form Post** (Posting window: 8:00 PM MT)

If bedtime is a battle in your house right now, you're not alone. 😴

D49 is 13 days in. D11 is 5 days in. D20 starts today. That means every COS family is somewhere in the "we let bedtime slip all summer and now the alarm hurts" window. The good news: pediatric sleep guidance is straightforward and works.

Kids ages 6-12 need 9-12 hours of sleep per night. Kids ages 13-18 need 8-10 hours. To reset a summer-shifted schedule, move bedtime 15 minutes earlier each night for a week — it's gradual enough that the body adjusts without a fight. Screens off 60 minutes before bed helps more than any other single change. And blackout curtains are worth it in COS in August — sunset is still after 7:45 PM through the first weeks of school, and bright bedrooms delay melatonin.

The transition is usually about a week. If you're not through it by day 10, talk to your pediatrician — sometimes an underlying issue like allergies or anxiety is the actual driver.
