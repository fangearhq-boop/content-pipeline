CPSC Orders Stop Use of Tuyedoqe Travel Bassinets: Company Refused Recall, No Refund Program Exists

By Sarah Morales | September 24, 2026

National Parenting | Product Safety

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission is warning parents to immediately stop using Tuyedoqe brand travel bassinets. This situation is different from a standard product recall, and that difference matters for families trying to figure out their options.

The manufacturer, Tuyedoqe — based in China — refused to issue a voluntary recall after the CPSC identified multiple safety hazards with the product. Because the company declined to cooperate, the CPSC issued a Notice of Violation instead of a standard recall announcement. That also means there is no refund or replacement program for consumers who purchased the product.

The Hazards Identified

The CPSC identified three distinct hazards with the Tuyedoqe travel bassinet. First, the restraint straps pose a strangulation risk for infants. Second, the sides of the bassinet are too low to meet the federal standard for infant sleep products, creating a fall hazard. Third, the bassinet is not designed to be used on any elevated surface — it lacks a stand — which adds to the fall risk if placed on a bed, table, or other raised area.

These types of hazards — in a product intended for infant sleep — are among the most serious categories the CPSC addresses. The agency's standards for infant sleep products exist specifically to prevent entrapment, suffocation, and fall incidents.

Why There Is No Refund

When a manufacturer refuses to issue a voluntary recall, the CPSC's enforcement tools are limited but include the ability to issue a public Notice of Violation and to pursue legal action. The agency cannot force a company to fund a refund or replacement program on its own authority — that requires either company cooperation or a court order.

The result for consumers is a frustrating situation: the product is unsafe, the government has told you to stop using it, but you cannot get your money back through any official program. Your options are to contact the CPSC directly at cpsc.gov to report your purchase and ask about any ongoing enforcement action, or to seek a refund through the platform where you purchased it — in this case, Amazon. Some platforms have buyer protection programs that may apply, particularly for products that have been flagged by a federal safety agency.

What's Next

If you have a Tuyedoqe travel bassinet, stop using it immediately. Do not donate it or pass it on — that puts another family's infant at risk. Safely dispose of the product.

The CPSC Notice of Violation for this product is available at cpsc.gov. The agency also maintains a full list of current recalls and warnings at cpsc.gov/Recalls. Parents concerned about other infant sleep products they may own can use the recall search tool on that site to check any item by product name or manufacturer.
