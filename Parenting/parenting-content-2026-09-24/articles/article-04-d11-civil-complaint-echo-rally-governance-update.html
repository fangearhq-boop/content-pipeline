D11 Governance Update: Civil Complaint Filed, ECHO Rally Calls for Transparency Ahead of Board Meeting

By Jamie Rivera | September 24, 2026

Local News | School Districts

Colorado Springs School District 11 is navigating an internal dispute that has moved into public view. On September 8, a district employee filed a civil complaint against the D11 superintendent and deputy superintendent, alleging the theft of confidential files. The specific nature of the files has not been disclosed publicly.

The complaint has drawn attention from an organized advocacy group. On September 23, ECHO — Education and Community Help Organization — held a rally calling for transparency and accountability from district leadership. The rally preceded a special board meeting that D11 administration has announced, though a confirmed date and time for that meeting had not been publicly released as of this writing.

What This Means for D11 Families

School operations across D11 are continuing without disruption. The dispute is a matter of district administration, not instruction or school-level programming. Parents whose children attend D11 schools do not need to take any action at this time.

That said, board governance situations like this one — where an employee complaint goes public and advocacy groups mobilize — can sometimes affect long-term district decisions about leadership, policy, or budgeting. Families who want to stay informed are encouraged to monitor d11.org for official announcements and KRDO for news coverage as the situation develops.

The special board meeting, when publicly scheduled, is open to community members. D11 board meetings are typically listed at d11.org.

D11's Strong Academic Year

The governance dispute is one thread in what has otherwise been a positive year for D11. All of the district's high schools entered the 2026–27 school year at the state's top performance tier — a significant result that reflects years of academic improvement work. The district also partnered with Edukit to provide free, grade-specific school supply kits to more than 14,000 K–8 students on the first day of school, August 12.

Those outcomes matter to families and are worth keeping in perspective as the governance situation plays out. The day-to-day academic work happening in D11 classrooms is not what's in dispute here.

What's Next

The civil complaint is an active matter. D11 has not issued a detailed public response to the specific allegations as of this writing. The special board meeting will likely be the next major public milestone for this situation — that's where community members will have the opportunity to hear from district leadership and potentially ask questions.

Monitor d11.org for official meeting schedules and KRDO for ongoing news coverage. This story is developing.
