### STORY 2: HALO Dream Magic Sleepsuits Recall

**Long-Form Post [10:00 AM MT]**

Product safety check for parents — the HALO Dream Magic Sleepsuit has been recalled.

The CPSC announced a recall of approximately 45,000 HALO Dream Magic Sleepsuits after reports that the zipper heads can detach and create a choking hazard for babies. These were sold at Amazon, Walmart, and Target between September 2025 and February 2026 for approximately $50.

The recall covers three specific batch codes printed on the tag inside the garment: PO30592, PO30641, and PO30685. If your sleepsuit matches any of those, the steps are straightforward — stop using it, register at sleepsuitrecall.com, write "RECALL" in permanent marker on the front, then mail it back for a refund or replacement.

The CPSC received 15 reports of zipper detachment before the recall was issued, with no injuries reported. That's a good outcome, but it means tens of thousands of these are still in homes.

If you're not sure whether you have an affected version, checking the tag takes about 10 seconds. The recall registration process is quick.

### STORY 3: Tuyedoqe Travel Bassinets — CPSC Warning

**Long-Form Post [12:00 PM MT]**

A heads-up for anyone who bought a travel bassinet on Amazon in the past year — specifically the Tuyedoqe brand.

The CPSC issued a stop-use warning for Tuyedoqe travel bassinets after identifying multiple hazards: the restraint straps pose a strangulation risk, the sides are too low (creating a fall hazard), and the unit is not safe on any elevated surface because it lacks a stand.

What makes this one a bit different from a typical recall: the manufacturer — based in China — refused to cooperate with a voluntary recall. So instead of a standard recall announcement, the CPSC issued a Notice of Violation. That also means there's no refund or replacement program. The units were sold on Amazon between April and October 2025, approximately $40 each, with an estimated 250 units sold.

If you have one, stop using it immediately. You won't get your money back through the company, but you can contact the CPSC directly at cpsc.gov for guidance on safe disposal.

### STORY 4: D11 Governance Update

**Long-Form Post [3:00 PM MT]**

Something to keep an eye on if you have kids in D11 schools this year.

In September, a district employee filed a civil complaint against the superintendent and deputy superintendent, alleging that confidential files were stolen. The details are still playing out publicly — an advocacy group called ECHO held a transparency rally on September 23, calling for accountability, and a special board meeting is pending.

What we know: school operations and classrooms are not disrupted by this. The dispute is at the district administration level. That said, board governance situations like this can sometimes affect long-term district decisions, so it's worth staying aware.

On a positive note, D11 has had a strong academic year. All of the district's high schools entered 2026–27 at the state's top performance tier, and the district partnered with Edukit to provide more than 14,000 K–8 students with free school supply kits on the first day of school. Those are meaningful wins for COS families.

For updates, monitor d11.org and KRDO. The special board meeting date hasn't been publicly confirmed yet.
