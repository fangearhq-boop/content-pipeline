### STORY 1: Colorado CCCAP Childcare Waitlist

**Long-Form Post:**
If you're one of the families in Colorado Springs trying to access subsidized childcare — and getting nowhere — here's why, and what's going on.

Colorado's Child Care Assistance Program, known as CCCAP, has seen its waitlist explode in the past three years. In 2023, just four children across the entire state were waiting for assistance. As of September 2026, that number has surpassed 14,000. That's not a typo.

El Paso County — our county — moved to a waitlist model in April 2026 after the demand for help outpaced the program's capacity. The root cause: COVID-era federal stimulus money that helped keep CCCAP functioning has wound down, while the cost of providing childcare subsidies has gone up. Individual counties run CCCAP locally, and 28 of Colorado's counties now have either enrollment freezes or waitlists. A bill that would have restored $105 million in program funding was killed by the state legislature in May.

What families can do right now: contact El Paso County Human Services to add your name to the waitlist. The program is not closed — it's just overwhelmed. Families who qualify will move up as funding becomes available.

Have you run into the CCCAP waitlist? We'd love to hear what the experience has been like for your family.

### STORY 2: Weekend Roundup Sep 11-13

**Long-Form Post:**
Your weekend plan for Colorado Springs families is here, and there's something good on both days.

**Saturday, September 12 — Dahlia Days at Gather Mountain Blooms**
One of the most underrated fall experiences in the Pikes Peak region, the late-September dahlia fields at Gather Mountain Blooms are at or near peak bloom this weekend. Admission is $10 per person, and you'll find yourself wandering through color that does not look real. The farm is located at 5210 S. US-85/87. It's a great stop for photos and a low-key morning or afternoon.

**Sunday, September 13 — Pikes Peak Youth Fishing Derby at Crystal Creek Reservoir**
Free and open to kids ages 6 through 15. Crystal Creek Reservoir is one of those places COS families sometimes forget about — it's a beautiful spot and this derby is exactly the kind of low-pressure, get-outside morning that works well on a September Sunday. Bring your gear and get there early.

There's also the usual lineup of free outdoor options — Monument Valley Park, Garden of the Gods, and the Pikes Peak Greenway Trail are all great this time of year before the crowds completely wind down.

For the full list of things to do with kids in Colorado Springs this weekend, head to Springs Daily's weekend guide — they've got the complete picture.

### STORY 5: October Family Events Preview

**Long-Form Post:**
We're barely two weeks into September, but October sneaks up fast. Here's what Colorado Springs families should have on their radar for next month.

**Monster Jam — October 23-25, Broadmoor World Arena**
The 12,000-pound trucks are back, and the Broadmoor is one of the better venues in the state for it. Kids who are into massive machines and loud engines will be obsessed. Plan ahead — this one sells tickets fast.

**Oktoberfest at Gather Mountain Blooms — October 3-4**
Same farm that's doing Dahlia Days this weekend. In early October they shift to Oktoberfest mode: local craft beers, German food, pumpkin and flower picking, and family-friendly activities. Gather Mountain Blooms does fall well.

**Emma Crawford Coffin Races — October 24, Manitou Springs**
One of the stranger and more beloved traditions in the region. Teams dress up, race coffins (with a runner inside playing "Emma") down Ruxton Avenue. It runs from 10 AM to 5 PM and it's a genuinely fun family outing, especially with older kids who appreciate that it's completely absurd.

**Western Museum of Mining & Industry — Saturdays in October**
Pumpkin picking, cider presses, vintage steam engine demonstrations, and hay rides with views of the Rampart Range. Low-key, uncrowded, and one of those hidden-gem fall spots that COS families who've been once tend to return to every year.

What's on your October family calendar?
