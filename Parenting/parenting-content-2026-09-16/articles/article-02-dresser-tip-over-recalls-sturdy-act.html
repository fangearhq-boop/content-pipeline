Three Dresser Brands Recalled for Tip-Over Hazard — More Than 16,000 Units Affected

By Jamie Rivera

September 16, 2026

If you've purchased a six-drawer dresser from Amazon or Wayfair in the past two years, it's worth checking whether your specific model is among the three brands recalled this month by the Consumer Product Safety Commission. Together, the three recalls cover more than 16,000 dressers that can tip over and trap children if they're not anchored to a wall — a hazard the CPSC classifies as a risk of serious injury or death.

All three recalls cite violations of the mandatory federal safety standard required under the STURDY Act, which sets specific tip-over resistance requirements for clothing storage units. When dressers fail this standard, a child who climbs or pulls on a drawer can cause the entire unit to fall forward, trapping them underneath.

What to Do Right Now

The CPSC's instruction is clear: if you have one of these dressers and it is not currently anchored to a wall stud with a furniture strap, stop using it immediately. Move it to an area where children cannot access it. Do not allow children to use it or be near it unsecured.

For the FJTJBSI dresser specifically, the remedy process is as follows: write the word "RECALLED" in permanent marker on the dresser, then email a photo showing the marking to FJTJBSIrecall@outlook.com to receive your refund. No return shipment is required.

For Gizoon and Alanca, full remedy instructions — including refund or replacement details — are available on CPSC.gov. Search the brand name alongside "dresser recall" to locate the specific recall page and follow the remedy steps provided.

What the FJTJBSI Dresser Looks Like

The FJTJBSI unit is a black or white six-drawer dresser measuring approximately 15.1 inches long by 27.6 inches wide by 42.2 inches high and weighing about 68 pounds. It has six drawers with silver or black handles and was sold in both color options on Amazon. If you ordered a dresser matching this description between November 2024 and July 2026, check CPSC.gov to confirm whether your specific model is included in the recall.

The Broader Context

Dresser tip-overs are among the more common serious injury scenarios in homes with young children, and the STURDY Act — which took effect for new products — was specifically designed to address the hazard after years of child deaths and injuries. These three September recalls are a reminder that some products that passed through retail channels before full STURDY Act enforcement may not meet the standard. For families who purchase furniture online without the ability to inspect the anchoring hardware in advance, the risk is real and worth acting on.

If you're unsure whether your dressers are properly anchored, anti-tip furniture straps are widely available at hardware stores and install in a few minutes. It's one of the lower-effort safety upgrades a family can make to a child's room.
