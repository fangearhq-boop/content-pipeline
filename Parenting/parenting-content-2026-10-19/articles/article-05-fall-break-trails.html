Fall Break Is Here — Three Free COS Trails That Work for Every Age

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting | October 19, 2026

COLORADO SPRINGS — LOCAL EVENTS

D11 fall break started today and runs through Friday, October 23. D49's two-week break also ends this Friday. With kids home all week and October weather in Colorado Springs still cooperating, it's a good week to get outside before the cold settles in.

All three options below are free, accessible across a range of ages, and close enough to serve as a half-day activity without major planning.

Palmer Park

Palmer Park at 3650 Maizeland Rd is one of the most versatile family outdoor spaces in Colorado Springs. The park covers 730 acres with a mix of flat, paved loops at lower elevations and more rugged natural trails on the upper mesa. The red rock formations and views of Pikes Peak make it feel like a genuine outdoor experience even for younger kids.

The lower paved areas work for strollers and early walkers. Older kids can handle the rougher trails and rocky outcrops. The park has restrooms and ample parking. It's free and open daily from dawn to dusk.

Monument Valley Park

Monument Valley Park runs through the center of the city along Fountain Creek, with paved paths accessible from several entry points. It's the right pick for families with very young children or anyone using a stroller or balance bike — the paths are flat, smooth, and wide.

The park connects to the city's trail system and can be extended into a longer walk depending on where you park. Entry is free. The Fontanero Street and Boulder Street areas are popular starting points.

Lower Cheyenne Canyon

For fall color, lower Cheyenne Canyon (2120 S Cheyenne Canyon Rd) is hard to beat this time of year. The lower canyon road follows the creek into the canyon, with the sound of the water and canyon walls on either side. The canyon road is paved and accessible for most families, with the steeper hikes beginning further in.

There's no admission fee for the lower canyon. The canyon road is open to pedestrians and cyclists. Parking fills up on weekends during fall color peak, so a weekday visit during fall break has some advantages.

What's Next

Fall break ends Friday, October 23. El Paso County maintains a full list of county open spaces and trails at communityservices.elpasoco.com — many are free and include restrooms and parking areas suitable for families with young children.

The City of Colorado Springs parks and recreation page (coloradosprings.gov/parks) has current status for city parks including trail closures and seasonal programming. Weather in the mid-October window tends toward mild afternoons, but temperatures can drop quickly after dark, so plan on finishing before sunset.
