### STORY 2: CPSC Recalls 37,000 ABC Trading Squishy Toys for Water Bead Hazard

**Long-Form Post (8:00 AM MT)**

If you've got squishy dumpling toys or "crazy squib ball" toys at home, check them now. 🚨

The CPSC recalled approximately 37,000 ABC Trading squishy toys on October 1, 2026. The two products affected are the Squishy Dumpling (model number 3655) and the Crazy Squib Ball (model number 3984). They were sold from April through June 2026 at ABC Trading stores and retailers nationwide for between 41 cents and $1.25.

The hazard: the water beads inside these toys expand past the permitted size. If a child swallows one, it can expand inside their body and cause a life-threatening blockage. Do not induce vomiting. If you think your child swallowed one, go to the emergency room right away and tell the staff what happened.

What to do: stop use immediately, mark the toy "RECALLED," throw it away, and email a photo of the discarded toy to recallabc@gmail.com to request a refund.

These are very inexpensive toys, so they may not be on your radar — but they're in enough homes that 37,000 are being recalled. Worth a quick scan of the toy bin today.

### STORY 3: AAP and CDC Now Have Different Vaccine Schedules — What COS Families Should Know

**Long-Form Post (12:00 PM MT)**

If you've been confused about vaccines lately, you're not alone.

In January 2026, the CDC revised its routine childhood immunization schedule, removing or narrowing recommendations for several vaccines. The American Academy of Pediatrics reviewed those changes and chose not to follow them. The AAP still recommends protection against 18 diseases for all children, a position endorsed by 12 medical organizations including the American Medical Association and the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists.

The differences that matter most for young children: the AAP continues to recommend routine COVID-19 vaccination for kids ages 6–23 months, while the CDC now limits it to high-risk patients. There are also differences on RSV and HPV timing. The AAP argues the CDC's narrowed approach leaves some kids with gaps in protection.

Most pediatricians in Colorado follow the AAP schedule — but it's a good question to bring up at your next well visit. If your doctor follows the AAP guidelines, nothing changes for your family. If you have questions about any specific vaccine, your child's pediatrician is the right person to ask.

### STORY 4: AAP Updates Iron Deficiency Screening Guidelines for Infants and Toddlers

**Long-Form Post (3:00 PM MT)**

The AAP just updated its iron deficiency guidelines for the first time since 2010, and there are a few key changes worth knowing before your baby's next well visit. 🩸

The new guidance, published in the July 2026 issue of Pediatrics, calls for screening with a complete blood count and serum ferritin test — not just a simple hemoglobin check. The timing depends on how your baby is fed: breastfed babies should be screened between 9 and 12 months, while formula-fed babies who've switched to cow's milk should be screened between 15 and 18 months.

On supplementation: if your baby is exclusively breastfed, the AAP now recommends starting iron drops at 1 mg/kg/day by 4 months. (You can wait until 6 months if you're introducing iron-rich foods at that point.) For premature babies on full feeds, iron should start by 2 weeks of age.

One thing that hasn't changed: cow's milk before 12 months is still a no. And after 12 months, too much cow's milk is actually a risk factor for iron deficiency — it can crowd out iron-rich foods.

If your child has been having a lot of milk lately or you're unsure about their iron intake, this well visit is a good time to bring it up.

### STORY 5: Fall Break Hikes for COS Families — Trails to Know This Week

**Long-Form Post (5:00 PM MT)**

Fall break week is finally here, and the COS trails are calling. 🍂

With D11 and D49 kids home through at least October 23, this is a solid week for getting outside before temperatures really drop. Here are three spots worth putting on the list:

Palmer Park (3650 Maizeland Rd) is one of the best all-ages outdoor spots in Colorado Springs. It's 730 acres with a mix of flat, paved loops and more rugged natural trails. The red rock formations and mountain views make it feel like a real outdoor adventure even for younger kids.

Monument Valley Park runs along Fountain Creek through the middle of the city. The paved paths are great for strollers, scooters, and littles who aren't quite up for trail hiking yet. It's free and accessible from multiple parking areas.

Lower Cheyenne Canyon (2120 S Cheyenne Canyon Rd) has some of the most beautiful fall color in the city. The lower canyon road is paved and open to walkers, with the creek running alongside. Worth the short drive to the west side.

All three are free. All three work for a wide range of ages. And after a week inside, everyone — kids and parents alike — will feel better for it.
