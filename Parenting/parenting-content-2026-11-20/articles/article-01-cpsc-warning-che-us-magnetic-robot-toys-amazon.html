CPSC Warns Parents to Throw Away CHE-US Magnetic Robot Toys — Over 100,000 Units Sold on Amazon

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting | November 20, 2026

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission has issued a formal warning for a set of magnetic stick figure robot toys sold under the CHE-US and CHESLICR brand names on Amazon. The CPSC is telling parents to stop using the toys and throw them away immediately. About 101,282 units were sold before the warning was issued, and there is no recall, no refund, and no exchange available.

The toys are made by Guangzhou Shian Trading Co., Ltd. When the CPSC found that the magnets in the toys violated federal safety standards, they asked the company to cooperate with a voluntary recall. The company refused. That refusal means the CPSC can warn parents but cannot force a standard recall process — leaving families who bought the toys without any recourse except disposal.

Why the Magnets Are Dangerous

The toys are designed as small humanoid stick figures with articulated arms and legs. The problem is that those limbs can be pulled apart by a child — and when they are, they release high-powered magnets that are small enough to pass the CPSC's small parts cylinder test. That means they're a choking hazard for children under three. But even for older kids, the magnetic risk is the more serious concern.

High-powered magnets become dangerous when more than one is swallowed. If two or more magnets are ingested separately and reach the digestive system, they can attract each other through the walls of the intestines. That pull is strong enough to cause intestinal perforations, blockages, or sepsis. All of these outcomes can be life-threatening without surgical intervention. The CPSC has been warning about high-powered magnet ingestion risks for years — this is not a theoretical hazard.

The toys were also found to violate ASTM F963, the federal toy safety standard for children's products. The magnets exceed the allowable flux index level for toys.

No Refund, No Exchange

Normally, a product recall comes with a remedy — a refund, a replacement part, or a free repair. This situation is different because the seller chose not to cooperate. Guangzhou Shian Trading Co. declined to work with the CPSC on a voluntary recall, which means there is no mechanism for consumers to get money back.

The CPSC has limited legal options when an overseas manufacturer refuses to engage. The agency can list the warning, work with Amazon to restrict sales, and issue the public guidance it's issuing now — but it cannot create a refund process on its own. If you bought this product and want to pursue a complaint, the CPSC accepts reports at SaferProducts.gov.

What to Do Right Now

Throw the toys away. Put them in a bag before placing them in the trash so loose magnets don't get handled by others. Do not donate them, resell them, or give them to other families. If you gave sets as gifts, let the recipients know and share the model code so they can identify the product.

If a child has swallowed a magnet — or if you suspect they may have — call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 or go to the nearest emergency room. Time matters with magnet ingestion injuries.

What's Next

The CPSC warning remains active. The agency may pursue additional legal action against the seller, and Amazon has been notified. Check your Amazon order history if you're unsure whether you purchased this product — search for "CHE-US robot" or "magnetic stick figure." The model code X004N3MGCB should appear on the original packaging if you still have it. If you have additional questions, the CPSC can be reached at 1-800-638-2772 or at CPSC.gov.
