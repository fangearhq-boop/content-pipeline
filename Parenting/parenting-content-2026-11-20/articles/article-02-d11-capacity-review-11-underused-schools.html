D11 Has 11 Underused Schools — What the Capacity Review Means for COS Families

By Jamie Rivera | COS Parenting | November 20, 2026

Colorado Springs School District 11 reviewed enrollment and building capacity at a November 18 work session. The finding that emerged: 11 D11 schools now meet the state's threshold for underused campuses. The board is not moving toward closures, but the review makes clear that D11 is managing a district that looks significantly different than it did a decade ago.

District enrollment has fallen 21.6% to approximately 22,265 students. At the same time, D49 has grown 65.5% over the same period. The enrollment shift reflects what a lot of Colorado Springs families have experienced firsthand — many households have moved into the D49 zone or chosen it deliberately, changing the balance between districts in a way that now shows up in D11's building capacity numbers.

What "Underused" Means in Colorado

Colorado uses a utilization threshold to identify schools that are significantly below their designed capacity. When a school's enrollment drops below roughly 65% of what the building was designed to hold, it typically meets the state's criteria for an underused campus. That threshold is meant to flag schools for planning conversations — it's not an automatic trigger for closure.

D11 currently has 11 schools meeting that description. The district presented this as a planning data point, not a list of schools slated for any particular action. The board emphasized at the work session that it has not directed staff to plan closures.

Co-Location, Not Closure

The option the board wants to explore is co-location: placing more than one school or program in the same building. That could mean a charter school sharing a D11 building, two smaller programs combining under one roof, or district support services moving into a school with available space.

Co-location is a way to keep buildings active and reduce per-pupil facility costs without the community disruption of a school closure. It's also not a simple fix — co-location arrangements require negotiations, transportation adjustments, and sometimes construction work to separate spaces within a building. The board acknowledged it's an exploratory conversation, not a plan.

What This Means for Families Right Now

Question Answer
Is my D11 school closing? No decisions announced; board ruled out pursuing closures at this stage
What is the district actually doing? Exploring co-location options for underused buildings
When do decisions happen? Planning for 2026-27 school year; no timeline confirmed
How did we get here? D11 enrollment down 21.6%; D49 up 65.5% over the same period
What is "underused"? Below Colorado's threshold, approximately 65% of designed building capacity

For most D11 families, nothing changes immediately. The November 18 session was a data presentation, not a decision meeting. The district will continue working through capacity planning over the coming months, and further work sessions or public input opportunities are likely before anything material happens.

The D49 Factor

D49's 65.5% growth over the same period that D11 declined 21.6% isn't background noise — it's the central dynamic. Much of D49's growth has come from new residential development on the east side of Colorado Springs, areas that D11 doesn't serve. Families who moved there in the last several years were always going to end up in D49, not D11.

But some of the shift also reflects family choices about schools. The growth of charter options, boundary decisions, and housing affordability in different parts of Colorado Springs have all played a role. D11 has been aware of this trend; the capacity review makes the building-level impact of it visible in a formal way.

What's Next

The district is in an exploratory phase heading into spring planning for the 2026-27 school year. D11 hasn't announced a timeline for follow-up meetings on the capacity review, but the work session typically precedes a more public engagement process. If you have kids in D11, it's worth watching for any community input opportunities the district announces in the coming weeks. We'll continue following this story as the planning process advances.
