D11's $775M School Bond Is Official — Here's What Colorado Springs Families Can Expect

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting | November 20, 2026

Colorado Springs School District 11 voters approved Ballot Issue 4A, a $775 million bond measure that will fund building improvements at every school in the district. The measure had nearly 50,000 yes votes in early results and has since been confirmed. It's the first D11 bond to pass in 20 years and the largest school bond in El Paso County history.

For families with kids in D11 schools, the bond represents a significant commitment to the buildings your children use every day. It doesn't change what happens in classrooms immediately, and specific campus timelines haven't been announced. But the investment is now locked in, and the planning work that determines what gets done where and when is next.

What the Bond Covers — and What It Doesn't

The $775 million is capital funding. That means it's specifically for physical improvements to buildings — aging infrastructure, deferred maintenance, facility upgrades, and accessibility improvements across D11 campuses. Bond funding in Colorado cannot be used for teacher salaries, staff, curriculum, or day-to-day operating costs. Those are separate budget lines funded through mill levies and state per-pupil allocations.

D11 has been open about the fact that many of its buildings are old and have needed work for a long time. Deferred maintenance accumulates when a district is managing enrollment declines and operating on a constrained budget. The bond is the mechanism to address the backlog across all campuses rather than trying to piece together repairs one at a time.

How the Bond Fits Alongside the Capacity Story

This week also brought news that D11 has 11 schools meeting Colorado's threshold for underused campuses, with district enrollment down 21.6%. It might seem contradictory to approve $775 million in building improvements for a district with declining enrollment and underused buildings. The two stories are actually consistent when you look at how the funding works.

The bond money goes to capital improvements — it fixes and upgrades the buildings the district is using and will continue to use. Even underused buildings need maintained systems, working plumbing, updated electrical, and accessible entries. Letting buildings deteriorate while enrollment decisions play out would leave the district with buildings that are both underused and in worse shape. The bond addresses the physical condition of buildings, while the capacity review addresses how many buildings the district needs and how they're configured. Those are separate conversations with separate funding mechanisms.

What Families Should Watch For

The district will develop a capital improvement plan that determines which campuses are prioritized and in what sequence. That plan will likely involve community input, particularly for schools with specific needs. D11 hasn't announced a public engagement process for the capital plan, but one typically follows a bond approval of this scale.

Families won't see visible changes immediately. Bond-funded construction projects involve planning, design, contracting, and construction phases that unfold over months or years. The schools that need the most urgent work — where systems have failed or safety improvements are most pressing — will typically move first.

What's Next

D11 will begin the planning process for how to allocate and sequence the $775 million across campuses. Watch for announcements about community engagement sessions from the district at d11.org. If your child's school has known facility needs, those work sessions will be the opportunity to make sure they're part of the conversation. We'll continue following D11 capital planning as the district moves from bond approval into the implementation phase.
