CPSC Warns Parents to Ditch CHE-US Magnetic Robot Figures — 101K+ Units, Amazon
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post If you've bought a set of those CHE-US stick figure robot toys on Amazon — the ones that come in sets of five or ten for about $9 — the CPSC wants you to throw them away today. The Consumer Product Safety Commission issued a formal warning this week for about 101,000 of these toys. The brand name is CHE-US or CHESLICR, and the model code on the packaging is X004N3MGCB. The hazard isn't obvious at first glance: the arms and legs on the figures can be pulled apart, and when they are, they release high-powered magnets that are small enough to fit in the small parts cylinder (the standard choking hazard test). Here's the serious part. If a child swallows two or more of these magnets, they can attract each other through the walls of the intestines. That can cause perforations, blockages, or sepsis — any of which can be fatal without surgery. This isn't a theoretical risk; high-powered magnet injuries in children are well-documented. The seller, Guangzhou Shian Trading Co. (the company behind the CHE-US brand), refused to work with the CPSC on a voluntary recall. That means there's no refund and no exchange — the CPSC's only option was to issue a warning and tell parents to dispose of the toys immediately. There's no compensation available here, which is frustrating. But the disposal part is straightforward: if you have them, they go in the trash now. If you gave them as a gift, it's worth a heads-up call. Have you seen these toys at your house or a family member's? ---

D11 Reviews School Capacity — 11 Campuses Now Underused
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post D11 held a work session on November 18 to review enrollment and building capacity, and the numbers tell a clear story: 11 schools in the district now meet Colorado's threshold for underused campuses. District enrollment has dropped 21.6% to about 22,265 students. That's a significant decline — and it comes alongside D49 growing 65.5% over the same period, which reflects what many local families already know: a lot of people have been choosing to move into the D49 area or enroll their kids there. The board is not talking about closures. They've been clear about that. The focus right now is on co-location, which means putting multiple programs or schools in the same building rather than shutting a building down. That's a planning conversation, not a decision announcement. The capacity review is a step ahead of the 2026-27 school year. No decisions came out of the November 18 meeting, and the district said it's still in an exploratory phase. If your kids are in D11, nothing is changing immediately. But this is a conversation worth following as the district works through what to do with buildings that aren't at capacity. We'll keep tracking it as the planning process moves forward. What do you think D11 should do with underused buildings? ---

AAP and Understood.org Release First ADHD-Specific Screen Time Guidance for Ages 5–10
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post If you have a kid with ADHD and you've ever felt guilty about how much screen time they're getting — this one's for you. The American Academy of Pediatrics partnered with Understood.org to release the first expert-backed screen time guidance written specifically for parents of kids ages five to ten with ADHD. It came out November 16, and it's worth bookmarking. The core message is a shift away from minute-counting. For kids with ADHD, context and content matter more than how long they're on a screen. A child with ADHD using a device to hyperfocus on something genuinely educational or creative is a different situation from one passively scrolling — and the guidance acknowledges that nuance, which the general AAP screen time advice doesn't really address. It also specifically calls out one of the most persistent myths: screens don't cause ADHD. And it's direct about parental guilt. A lot of families feel like they're doing something wrong when their kid needs more screen time than their neurotypical peers. This paper pushes back on that. You can find it at understood.org. It's free, it's readable, and it's a genuinely useful reference for the conversations we all have at home about devices. What's been the hardest screen time call to make with your ADHD kid? ---

NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Just a quick heads up for families who regularly take the dogs and kids to Bear Creek Regional Dog Park: construction started November 19 and runs through December 18. 🐾 El Paso County is doing ADA accessibility upgrades — specifically upgrading the parking areas and the entrance path to meet ADA standards. That's genuinely good news for families who bring strollers or who have family members with mobility needs. The work just means limited on-site parking for the next month. If you're planning a holiday-season visit, it's worth knowing before you arrive. The timing isn't ideal with December filling up fast, but the construction wraps before the week of Christmas. Plan your trips with a few extra minutes for parking, and you're good. Do you take your kids and dogs to Bear Creek regularly? ---

D11's $775M School Bond Is Official — First in 20 Years
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post The vote is confirmed: D11's Ballot Issue 4A passed. That's $775 million for school improvements across every campus in Colorado Springs School District 11. It's the first D11 bond to pass in 20 years — and the largest school bond in El Paso County history. Nearly 50,000 yes votes came in with the early results, and the final count held. The money is capital-only, which means it covers building upgrades, deferred maintenance, and facility improvements. It can't go toward salaries or operating costs. The district has been open about the fact that many buildings are aging and have needed work for a long time — this bond is what makes that work possible. What it doesn't come with right now is a school-by-school timeline. The district will need to go through a process to prioritize which campuses get what and when. That work will happen over the coming months, and we'll keep tracking it as decisions get made. For families with kids in D11 schools today, the bond passage means renewed investment in the buildings your kids walk into every morning. The improvements aren't immediate, but they're coming. What would you most like to see improved at your D11 campus? ---
