### STORY 1: CPSC Warns Parents to Ditch CHE-US Magnetic Robot Figures — 101K+ Units, Amazon
**Tier:** 1
**Type:** NEW STORY
**Pillar:** National Parenting
**Byline:** Sarah Morales
**Posting Window:** 8:00 AM MT

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission has issued a formal warning for CHE-US magnetic stick figure robot toys sold on Amazon — about 101,282 units total. The toys' arms and legs can be pulled apart to release high-powered magnets that violate the federal toy safety standard and that fit within the small parts cylinder. If swallowed, those magnets can attract each other through intestinal walls, causing perforations, blockages, or sepsis — potentially fatal without surgery. The seller, Guangzhou Shian Trading Co. (branded CHE-US/CHESLICR), refused to cooperate with a voluntary recall, so there's no refund. The CPSC is telling parents to stop using the toys immediately and throw them away. The figures were sold in sets of five or 10, with model code X004N3MGCB on the packaging, for about $9.

### STORY 3: AAP and Understood.org Release First ADHD-Specific Screen Time Guidance for Ages 5–10
**Tier:** 2
**Type:** NEW STORY
**Pillar:** National Parenting
**Byline:** Sarah Morales
**Posting Window:** 12:00 PM MT

A new paper released November 16 by Understood.org, created in partnership with the American Academy of Pediatrics, offers the first expert-backed screen time guidance written specifically for parents of kids ages 5 to 10 with ADHD. The paper addresses common myths — including the belief that screens cause ADHD — and aims to relieve the parental guilt that many families feel about their child's screen use. It emphasizes that for kids with ADHD, context and content matter more than counting minutes. The AAP's existing general screen time guidance focuses on the broader pediatric population; this paper fills a gap for families navigating ADHD alongside typical childhood screen exposure.

### STORY 4: Bear Creek Regional Dog Park ADA Work Starts — Limited Parking Through December 18
**Tier:** 2
**Type:** NEW STORY
**Pillar:** Local News
**Byline:** Jamie Rivera
**Posting Window:** 3:00 PM MT

El Paso County started ADA accessibility work at Bear Creek Regional Dog Park on November 19 — the same day construction began. Work includes upgrading parking areas and the entrance path to meet ADA standards. The project runs through December 18, 2026. On-site parking is limited during construction; the county has designated overflow parking at the Parks Headquarters, Bear Creek Regional Park main lot, and the north side of the dog park. For families who regularly visit with kids and dogs, planning an alternate route or arriving via overflow parking is the workaround until mid-December.

**Tier:** 2
**Type:** NEW STORY
**Pillar:** Local News
**Byline:** Sarah Morales

Colorado Springs School District 11 voters approved Ballot Issue 4A, a $775 million bond measure to upgrade every school in the district — the first D11 bond to pass in 20 years, and the largest in El Paso County history. The measure had nearly 50,000 yes votes in early results and has since been confirmed. The bond money is earmarked for capital improvements across all D11 campuses, addressing aging buildings, deferred maintenance, and facility upgrades. For families with kids in D11 schools, the bond means renewed investment in the buildings they attend every day. No specific campus timelines have been announced yet.
