### STORY 2: Two Teething Toy Recalls

**LONG-FORM POST 2** — 10:00 AM MT

If you have a pull-string teether from Amazon, please take two minutes to read this.

The CPSC recalled two teething toy sets on July 23, 2026, both of which pose a choking and respiratory hazard to infants. The recalled toys are the CuddleCubs Creations highchair teething toy set (BlueOrange/YellowOrange) and the Aojieni Sili Factory pull-string teething toy. Both were sold on Amazon — the CuddleCubs set from December 2024 through April 2026 for $15–$19, and the Aojieni set from May 2025 through April 2026 for $11–$15.

The problem with both: the silicone pull strings are smaller and longer than what's permitted under mandatory toy safety standards. The strings can reach the back of an infant's throat and become lodged, creating a serious choking and respiratory-distress hazard. One report confirmed that the Aojieni toy's strings reached a child's throat and caused gagging.

If you have either toy, stop using it immediately. To get your refund: cut all silicone strings, write "DESTROYED" (CuddleCubs) or "RECALLED" (Aojieni) in permanent marker on the body of the toy, and send a photo by email to get a full refund. CuddleCubs: cuddlecubscreation@126.com. Aojieni: info@silifactory.com. Details on both recalls are at cpsc.gov/Recalls.

Have you seen these toys? Please share this so other parents can check.

### STORY 4: Infant Heat Safety

**LONG-FORM POST 4** — 3:00 PM MT

There's a National Weather Service Heat Advisory in effect for Colorado Springs right now through 9 p.m. tonight — 97 degrees F, with an extreme UV index of 12. If you're out with kids today, a few things from pediatricians are worth knowing.

The one that surprises people most: do not cover a stroller with a blanket to block the sun. Even thin, lightweight cloth prevents air from circulating inside the stroller and traps heat around the child. Use the stroller's built-in canopy for shade instead.

Pediatricians also flagged: check metal parts on strollers, car seat buckles, and playground equipment before touching them to a child's skin — metal absorbs heat and can cause burns even when it looks cool. Before putting a child in the car, run the AC or open all the doors for 10 to 20 seconds first, even in shade.

The best outdoor window for COS families today was 9 a.m. to 11 a.m. The heat advisory continues until 9 p.m., with a 10% chance of afternoon storms after 2 p.m. Drink water, stay in the shade, and take it easy the rest of the day.
