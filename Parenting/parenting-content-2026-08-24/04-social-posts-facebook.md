### STORY 1: CMZoo Little Paws Nature Preschool Opens TODAY

**Long-Form Post:**

Opening day is here for the Cheyenne Mountain Zoo's Little Paws Nature Preschool — Colorado Springs' first state-accredited outdoor preschool for young children.

Starting today, Monday August 24, children as young as 3 will be spending their mornings exploring CMZoo's trails and animal habitats as part of a state-licensed preschool curriculum. It's not your typical classroom. The zoo grounds are the classroom.

Here's what families need to know:

Three-year-olds can enroll in Tuesday/Thursday classes (9am–noon) at $220.75 per month. The program for 4- and 5-year-olds runs Monday/Wednesday/Friday (9am–1pm) at $460.75 per month. Class sizes are kept to approximately 15 students. The program is accredited by the Colorado Department of Early Childhood and follows the Cheyenne Mountain School District 12 calendar.

Teachers are state-licensed early childhood professionals who meet Colorado's director-qualified requirements through the Department of Early Childhood.

If you're interested, email LittlePawsPreschool@cmzoo.org to find out about enrollment availability.

### STORY 2: CPSC Recalls — Magnet Toys and Battery Toys

**Long-Form Post:**

Two CPSC recalls that every Colorado Springs family needs to know about today.

First: Gagaku Children's Ice Cream Toy Playsets, sold on Amazon by Brandworl.USA, have been recalled for a dangerous magnet hazard. High-powered magnets that detach from the playsets can be swallowed by young children, becoming lodged in the digestive system. When two or more magnets are swallowed separately, they can attract through the intestinal wall, causing perforations, twisting, blockage, and blood poisoning — injuries that are life-threatening and require surgery. If you have this product at home, stop use immediately and return it for a refund.

Second: ABC Trading has recalled three children's toy products due to button cell battery compartments that children can easily open. The recalled products are the Toy Headband (model 6300RP, pink with white polka dots), the Electronic Pet Cage-Dinosaur Tribes (model 8266), and the My Pet Bird Cute Bird Tribes (model ZH998-23). These were sold at TOYZ and Joissu Product stores nationwide from November 2022 through October 2025 for $5–$9 each. Button cell batteries — if swallowed — cause internal chemical burns and can be fatal. Stop use immediately and contact ABC Trading for a refund.

If you're ever unsure whether a product has been recalled, check cpsc.gov for the full, searchable database.

Have you seen either of these products at home or at a family member's house? This is a good one to share.

### STORY 3: Paid Paternity Leave and Baby Health

**Long-Form Post:**

Most conversations about parental leave focus on mothers. But a growing body of research is making the case that paid paternity leave isn't just good for fathers — it matters for babies, too.

A report from STAT News this month (August 2026) pulled together the latest pediatric evidence: paid paternity leave is linked to better health outcomes for newborns, not just for the mental health of new fathers. A June 2026 study from Northwestern University and the Ann and Robert H. Lurie Children's Hospital of Chicago found that fathers who don't have access to paid leave face significantly higher rates of anxiety and depression — and those mental health outcomes have direct effects on how present and engaged those fathers are in the critical early weeks.

An AAP Pediatrics study that analyzed 11.4 million births found paid family leave policies improve perinatal outcomes at scale.

And yet: the 2026 National Parent Survey found that fathers take an average of just three days of leave after the birth of a child, compared to mothers' average of 7.2 weeks.

For Colorado Springs families — including the many military families at Peterson Space Force Base, Schriever Space Force Base, and Cheyenne Mountain — leave policies vary widely. Active-duty members have federally mandated parental leave, but civilian contractors, spouses, and community members face a patchwork of state and employer policies. Colorado doesn't currently mandate paid paternity leave beyond what federal law requires.

### STORY 4: 2026 National Parent Survey

**Long-Form Post:**

A new national survey out this year puts hard numbers behind what many Colorado Springs families already know: having children is financially hard, and the burden isn't distributed equally.

The 2026 National Parent Survey from New America surveyed 5,472 parents across the country — including 2,915 with lower incomes. Here's what they found:

Two-parent households experience an average 14% drop in income after having children. Single parents — especially women — may see up to a 36% decrease. Families reported child care as a central financial pressure. The survey also found that most parents want more flexibility in how and when they work, but that flexibility isn't available to everyone equally.

For Colorado Springs, those numbers have real meaning. Child care costs here — like in most mid-size cities — consume a significant portion of take-home pay for families with young children. Colorado has a Child Care Assistance Program (CCAP) that subsidizes costs for income-eligible families, but waitlists can be long and eligibility thresholds vary by county.

The survey also found that parents are making major employment changes — reducing hours, switching jobs, or leaving the workforce entirely — in response to child care costs and caregiving demands. Those decisions have compounding effects on long-term financial health.

What's been your experience with child care costs in Colorado Springs? Have they changed your work situation?
