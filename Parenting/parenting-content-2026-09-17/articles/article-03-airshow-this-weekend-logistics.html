Pikes Peak Regional Airshow Is This Weekend — Day-of Guide for Families

By Jamie Rivera | September 17, 2026

Local Events

The Pikes Peak Regional Airshow arrives this Saturday and Sunday, September 19–20, at Colorado Springs Airport — and with a full lineup of military jets, WWII warbirds, and aerobatic performers, it's one of the biggest family events COS hosts all year. If you've already got tickets (or you're making a last-minute decision), here's everything you need to know before heading out.

The Basics

Dates: Saturday, September 19 and Sunday, September 20, 2026
Location: Colorado Springs Airport, 7330 Embraer Heights, Colorado Springs, CO 80916
Gates open: 8:30 AM
Flying demonstrations: 10:50 AM – 2:25 PM both days
Parking: Free
Tickets: Online only at pprairshow.org (no gate sales)

Ticket prices:

  • Adults: $53.20
  • Kids ages 4–12: $42.18
  • Children under 3: Free
  • Military: $5 off any ticket

What You'll See

The 2026 airshow lineup mixes modern military hardware with historic aircraft. On the contemporary side, F-35 demo pilot Kristin "Beo" Wolfe is headlining — her performances in the F-35A are consistently a crowd highlight, combining speed, precision, and the kind of noise and power that makes the tarmac vibrate.

The warbird program is equally strong. WWII aircraft flying include the B-17 Flying Fortress, B-25 Mitchell, P-47 Thunderbolt, F4U Corsair, SBD Dauntless, F7F Tigercat, and A-1 Skyraider. Aerobatic performer Kyle Franklin rounds out the show in his signature "Dracula" aircraft.

Flying demonstrations run from 10:50 AM to 2:25 PM — plan your day around that window if you're coming specifically for the flying. Arriving earlier lets you explore the static aircraft displays and vendors at your own pace.

Parking and Getting There

Parking is free, which is one of the things that makes this airshow accessible — but it comes with a catch. Traffic into Colorado Springs Airport gets heavy once the airshow crowd arrives, and the lot fills quickly. If you're heading out Saturday morning, plan to leave home earlier than you think you need to and build in at least 30 to 45 minutes for parking and the walk to the gate.

The airport is on the east side of Colorado Springs, accessible via Powers Boulevard. Embraer Heights is the frontage road — the airshow entrance is well-marked. There's no shuttle service; you walk from your parking spot to the gate.

What to Bring with Kids

Tarmac airshows have a few quirks that are worth knowing about before you go, especially if you're bringing young children:

  • Ear protection: Military jet demonstrations — especially the F-35 — are extremely loud. Bring foam earplugs for adults or child-sized headphones for kids who are sensitive to sound. This is the single most useful thing you can pack.
  • Sunscreen and hats: The tarmac offers very little shade, and September afternoons in Colorado still come with real UV exposure. Apply sunscreen before you arrive and bring enough to reapply.
  • Water: Vendors are typically on site, but having your own water — especially for young children — makes the day much more comfortable, especially if you arrive early and stay through the flying.
  • Stroller or carrier: The tarmac is flat and paved, so strollers work fine. For toddlers, a carrier keeps your hands free while you look up at the aircraft.
  • Snacks: Check the airshow's website for the current policy on outside food. Even if outside snacks are allowed, having familiar food on hand can be helpful for picky eaters or young children who need refueling mid-day.

Tips for Families with Sensory-Sensitive Kids

The Pikes Peak Regional Airshow is a genuinely loud event, and for children who are sensitive to sudden loud noises, it can be overwhelming without preparation. Ear protection is essential, not optional. Talk to your child before the event about what the jets are going to sound like — the visual of an aircraft approaching combined with preparation for the noise goes a long way.

You might also consider positioning further back from the flight line during the loudest demonstrations. The airshow grounds are large enough that distance provides some volume reduction, and you can still see the aircraft clearly from further back.

What's Next

If you don't have tickets yet, they're available online only at pprairshow.org. There are no gate sales — if you arrive without a ticket, you won't be able to get in. Tickets are the same price for Saturday and Sunday, so either day works if you need flexibility.

Weather in Colorado Springs this time of year is generally favorable for an outdoor event, but it's worth checking the forecast Friday evening and Saturday morning. The show runs rain or shine in most conditions, but knowing what to expect helps with packing decisions.
