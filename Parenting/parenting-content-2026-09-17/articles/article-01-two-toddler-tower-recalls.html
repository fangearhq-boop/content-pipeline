Two Toddler Kitchen Towers Recalled — More Than 207,000 Units Combined

By Jamie Rivera | September 17, 2026

National Parenting · Product Safety

If you have a toddler kitchen tower at home, you'll want to check the brand name before your child uses it again. The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission has announced recalls on two separate products — the Boon PIVOT Collapsible Toddler Tower and the Woodure Toddler Kitchen Step Stool — covering a combined total of more than 207,000 units sold across Amazon, Target, and other major retailers over the past few years.

The two recalls are unrelated, issued by different manufacturers, and involve different hazards — but both share the same bottom line: stop use immediately and contact the company for a free repair kit.

Boon PIVOT (TOMY): Tip-Over and Entrapment Risk

The Boon PIVOT Collapsible Toddler Tower, sold by TOMY, is one of the more popular kitchen step stools on the market — it retails for around $150 and was sold at Target.com, Amazon.com, and Babylist.com between January 2023 and June 2026. This recall covers 116,908 units.

According to the CPSC, the tower poses two separate hazards. First, it can tip over while a toddler is standing in it. Second, the openings on the front and back of the product are large enough for a child's torso to fit through, creating an entrapment risk that could result in serious injury or death. TOMY has received 11 reports of instability, though no injuries have been reported yet.

To get a free repair kit, visit recall.tomy.com/pivot. In the meantime, the CPSC advises removing the product from use and keeping it out of reach of children until it's been repaired.

The Boon PIVOT has also been recalled in Canada through the Canada.ca recall database.

Woodure: 15 Injuries Reported

The Woodure Toddler Kitchen Step Stool, imported by Shenzhen Muqiqu Creative Development, has a more urgent injury record. The recall covers approximately 91,000 units in three models — WD1764, WD1357, and WD1720 — sold exclusively on Amazon from July 2024 through June 2026 for between $60 and $100.

The CPSC reports that the stools can collapse or tip over, and that a child's torso can fit through openings in the product structure. The company has received 22 reports of instability or tip-overs and 15 reports of injuries, including scrapes, cuts, and bruises to children.

If you own a Woodure step stool, stop use immediately and visit wwoodure.com/recall to register for the free repair kit. The remedy site walks you through which model you have and how to request the replacement parts.

How to Check If Your Product Is Affected

Kitchen step stools and toddler towers are a common household item, and many similar products exist on the market. For both of these recalls, the simplest check is brand name and retailer. If you purchased a step stool or tower for your toddler from Amazon (especially within the past two years) or from Target.com, it's worth confirming the brand before using it again.

For the Woodure models specifically, check the underside of the product for the model numbers WD1764, WD1357, or WD1720. The CPSC recall notices include photographs to help with identification.

What's Next

Both companies are mailing free repair kits to registered owners. Register now — before you need to use the product again — to make sure you're in the queue. The CPSC's full recall notices, including product photos and additional identification details, are available at cpsc.gov.

If you have questions about either recall or believe you've experienced an injury related to these products, you can report it directly to the CPSC at SaferProducts.gov.
