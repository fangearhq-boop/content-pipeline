Two Toddler Kitchen Towers Recalled — More Than 207,000 Units Combined
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post 🚨 Two kitchen step stool recalls worth knowing about if you have toddlers at home. The CPSC has announced recalls on two separate toddler kitchen tower products — the Boon PIVOT Collapsible Toddler Tower (sold at Target, Amazon, and Babylist) and the Woodure Toddler Kitchen Step Stool (sold on Amazon). Together, the recalls cover more than 207,000 units. The Boon PIVOT (TOMY) involves 116,908 units sold between January 2023 and June 2026 for around $150. The concern: the tower can tip over, and a child's torso can fit through openings on the front and back. No injuries have been reported, but TOMY is offering a free repair kit at recall.tomy.com/pivot. The Woodure recall covers approximately 91,000 units (models WD1764, WD1357, and WD1720) sold on Amazon from July 2024 through June 2026 for $60–$100. This one has seen 22 instability reports and 15 injuries, including scrapes, cuts, and bruises. A free repair kit is available at wwoodure.com/recall. The advice from CPSC for both: stop use immediately and store the product out of reach of children until you've received and applied the repair kit. ⚠️ Do you have either of these products at home? Share this post so other parents can check — these recalls don't always make the local news. ### Image Post Caption 🚨 RECALL ALERT: Two toddler kitchen towers pulled — 207K+ units combined. If you have a Boon PIVOT or a Woodure step stool, stop use now and register for the free repair kit. Links in the post above.

AAP Issues 2026–2027 COVID Vaccine Guidance for Kids — What COS Families Need to Know
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post 💉 The American Academy of Pediatrics just released its 2026–2027 COVID vaccine guidance for kids, and it breaks down by age. If you have a baby or toddler between 6 and 23 months, the AAP recommends the new seasonal COVID vaccine — full stop. That recommendation is firm for the youngest age group. For children ages 2 through 18 who are at increased risk, the AAP recommends a single dose of the 2026–27 vaccine. High-risk is defined as being moderately or severely immunocompromised, living in a congregate setting, never having been vaccinated, or having a household member who is at high risk themselves. For healthy children ages 2 through 18 who don't fall into those risk categories, the AAP says the vaccine should be offered to any family who wants it. The decision is yours. The full guidance was published in the journal Pediatrics (doi: 10.1542/peds.2026-079045). If you want to talk through the options for your child, your pediatrician is the right call — or reach out to your school health office in D11, D20, or D49. Where does your family stand on the COVID vaccine this fall — will you be getting it, skipping it, or still deciding? ### Image Post Caption 💉 New AAP guidance is out for 2026–27. Infants 6–23 months: recommended. High-risk kids 2–18: recommended. Healthy kids 2–18: talk to your pediatrician. What are your thoughts?

Pikes Peak Regional Airshow Is This Weekend — What COS Families Need to Know Before Saturday
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post ✈️ The Pikes Peak Regional Airshow is this weekend — Saturday and Sunday, September 19–20 — and if you're heading out with kids, a little prep goes a long way. Gates open at 8:30 AM at Colorado Springs Airport (7330 Embraer Heights). Flying demonstrations run from 10:50 AM to 2:25 PM both days, with an impressive lineup: F-35 demo pilot Kristin "Beo" Wolfe leads the modern military segment, while WWII warbirds including the B-17, P-47, Corsair, B-25, SBD Dauntless, F7F Tigercat, and Skyraider fill out the historic program. Practical tips for families heading out: - Plan to arrive early — parking is free, but it fills up quickly and traffic backs up fast - Build in 30–45 extra minutes for parking and the walk to the gate - Bring earplugs or kid-sized headphones for any children who are sensitive to loud sounds; jet demos can be intense - Shade is limited on the tarmac — bring sunscreen, hats, and plenty of water - Strollers are manageable on the flat paved surface Tickets are online-only at pprairshow.org. Children under 3 are free; kids 4–12 are $42.18; adults are $53.20. Military families get $5 off. There are no gate sales. Are you heading to the airshow this weekend? Cool Science Festival Is 2 Weeks Away — What COS Kids Can Do Oct 3–18
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post 🔬 Two weeks from today, the Cool Science Festival kicks off across Colorado Springs — and it's one of the best annual family events the city has. Running October 3–18, the festival is organized by Cool Science, a local nonprofit, and brings free and low-cost science, technology, and hands-on learning events to multiple venues around COS. It's designed for families, school groups, and curious minds of all ages. The biggest single day is UCCS Carnival Day on Saturday, October 3 — that's where you'll find the most family-friendly programming, interactive demos, and the highest energy of the whole festival run. If you can only make one date work, that's the one to prioritize. Events continue at various COS locations through October 18. Some events require pre-registration, so it's worth checking coolscience.org now to see what's coming up and grab spots before they fill.
