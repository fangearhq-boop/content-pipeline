Colorado Springs' 10-Year Park Plan Is Open for Comment — But Only Until Tomorrow

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting | September 21, 2026

Local News

COS families have a rare chance to shape the city's parks, playgrounds, and trails for the next decade — but the window closes tomorrow. Public comment on Colorado Springs' 2026 Park System Master Plan is open through September 22, giving residents one final day to weigh in on a blueprint that will guide how the city invests in outdoor spaces for the next 10 to 15 years.

The plan, developed by Colorado Springs Parks, Recreation and Cultural Services, covers everything from neighborhood playground upgrades to trail connections to open space preservation. City Council is expected to vote on the final plan in October 2026. Whatever the public emphasizes now is what planners will carry into that approval process.

What the Plan Covers

The 2026 Park System Master Plan is a long-range planning document that addresses how Colorado Springs will develop and manage its parks and recreational infrastructure over the coming decade and beyond. That includes neighborhood parks and playgrounds, regional trails and trail connections, open space management, and cultural facilities and services run by the city.

For families, the plan is directly relevant to everyday decisions: where kids play after school, which trails connect neighborhoods to amenities, whether new developments come with green space, and how the city prioritizes maintenance and accessibility in outdoor areas.

What's Already on the Table

One of the projects highlighted in the plan's draft is a 210-foot pedestrian bridge over Sand Creek that would connect the east and west sides of the Rock Island Trail. That connection has long been a gap for cyclists and walkers who use the trail corridor. The plan also includes a pedestrian-activated flashing crossing at Peterson Road.

These are the kinds of investments that shape how walkable and bikeable Colorado Springs neighborhoods feel for families with young children. Trail access, safe crossings, and maintained open space aren't just amenities — they're infrastructure that affects daily life.

How to Submit Your Comment

To review the draft plan and submit public comment, visit coloradosprings.gov/ParkSystemMasterPlan. The city held two public open houses — on September 10 and September 16 — for in-person input. The online comment portal is the last remaining channel before the September 22 deadline.

There's no required format for comments. Families can be as specific or as broad as they'd like: requesting better playground equipment in a specific neighborhood, noting trail gaps that affect school-age children, or simply expressing support for green space investment in COS.

What's Next

After the comment period closes September 22, Parks, Recreation and Cultural Services will incorporate public input into the final plan. City Council is expected to vote on approval in October 2026. Once approved, the plan serves as the guiding document for parks funding and project prioritization for the next 10 to 15 years.

If you want your family's priorities reflected in how Colorado Springs invests in outdoor spaces, today and tomorrow are your last opportunities. Head to coloradosprings.gov/ParkSystemMasterPlan to read the draft and submit your comment before the deadline.
