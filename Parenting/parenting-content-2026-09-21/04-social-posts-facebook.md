### STORY 1: HARPPA Recall — FB Long-Form (8:00 AM MT)

⚠️ **RECALL ALERT for COS parents:** If you have a HARPPA Nordi Toddler Tower Stool at home, stop using it immediately.

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission has recalled approximately 47,166 HARPPA Nordi Foldable Toddler Tower Stools (model HANS0002). The recall affects white, black, and blue stools with a natural wood-colored step, platform, and safety bars — about 18 inches deep, 18 inches wide, and 38.6 inches tall. These were sold on Amazon.com and harppababy.com between September 2023 and June 2026 for around $130.

The hazards are serious: the stool can collapse or tip over during use, and a child's torso can fit through the front or back openings, creating an entrapment risk. There have been three reported incidents and two injuries, including scrapes, cuts, and bruises.

The good news: HARPPA is offering a free repair kit — protective nets, stabilizing feet, and installation instructions — with free shipping. To request yours, visit harppababy.com. To confirm your model, check the label on the underside of the stool platform for HANS0002.

### STORY 3: D11 Orton Academy — FB Long-Form (12:00 PM MT)

📚 COS families with kids who have dyslexia or reading differences — here's a school update worth knowing about.

Orton Academy, a K-8 charter school in Colorado Springs School District 11 (D11) that specializes in dyslexia education, is moving into Trailblazer Elementary for the 2026-27 school year. The school opened in 2020 and has been chartered within D11 since 2024.

Here's what that means for families: Orton Academy will operate with its own staff, students, and programming in its own classrooms within the Trailblazer building. The two schools share a building, but they remain entirely separate in how they run day-to-day. So if your child is enrolled at Orton, their school experience stays the same — different address, same program.

If you haven't heard of Orton Academy before, it's worth knowing it's one of the few schools in Colorado Springs specifically designed to support students who learn differently due to dyslexia. It serves kids in kindergarten through eighth grade.

### STORY 5: AAP Screen Time — FB Long-Form (5:00 PM MT)

📱 As fall sets in and indoor time picks up, here's a timely update from the American Academy of Pediatrics — and it might be more reassuring than you expect.

The AAP published updated screen time guidelines in 2026, and the big shift is this: they're moving away from strict hour-counting toward a focus on quality, context, and conversation. What your child watches, who they watch it with, and whether you talk about it matters more than hitting a precise daily limit.

Here's the age-by-age breakdown:
- **Under 18 months:** No screens, except video calls (like FaceTime with grandparents)
- **Ages 2 to 5:** Up to 1 hour per day of high-quality programming
- **Ages 6 and up:** Consistent limits that protect sleep, physical activity, homework time, and family connection

The AAP is especially encouraging parents to co-view with younger children — watching alongside them and talking about what they're seeing. That shared experience makes screen time more developmental and less passive.

With more indoor afternoons ahead for COS families, it's a good reminder that the goal isn't a perfect score on the timer — it's being intentional about what goes on the screen.
