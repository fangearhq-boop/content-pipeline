### STORY 1: CPSC Recalls — Baby Rattle Sets and Learning Tower

**Long-Form Post (8:00 AM MT)**

Two new CPSC recalls this week — and both affect families with young children.

The first: SHEIN has recalled approximately 2,657 Baby Rattle and Teether Toy Sets sold on US.Shein.com between November 2025 and July 2026. The purple and orange knobs on these sets can detach, posing a serious choking risk for children under 3. Recall number 27-083. If you have one, stop use immediately — SHEIN is offering a full refund, but you'll need to confirm you've disposed of the product to claim it.

The second: Little Partners has recalled its "Grow N Stow" Folding Learning Tower due to a fall hazard. These towers are popular with parents who want kids involved in kitchen time, so check if yours is affected at cpsc.gov/Recalls.

No injuries have been reported for the SHEIN recall, but the choking risk for under-3s makes stopping use the right call right now. We always try to surface these quickly so COS families have time to act.

Do you do regular recall checks for your kids' toys? Here's the fastest way: bookmark cpsc.gov/Recalls and check it monthly — it only takes about two minutes.

### STORY 2: D11 Boundary Redrawing — Over 100 Families Show Up

**Long-Form Post (10:00 AM MT)**

If your family is in District 11, here's something worth knowing about right now.

D11 is in the middle of a boundary redrawing process. Administrators are reviewing enrollment boundaries and student transfer policies, and more than 100 northwest-area residents recently turned out to a community meeting at Trailblazer Elementary to ask questions and push back on what they're hearing. That kind of turnout in an ongoing process signals that families want a voice in how this plays out.

The numbers behind the review: D11 has shrunk 21.6% over the last decade, dropping to approximately 22,265 students. During the same period, D49 has grown 65.5%. The regional enrollment shift is real, and the district is responding by looking at where its boundaries make sense in a changed landscape.

No vote has been scheduled. Community engagement meetings are still ongoing. That means now is the window for families in the northwest D11 zone — and other parts of the district — to attend, ask questions, and make their voices heard before any changes are finalized.

### STORY 3: Electric Safari at Cheyenne Mountain Zoo Opens Dec. 2

**Long-Form Post (12:00 PM MT)**

We've got just two weeks until one of Colorado Springs' favorite family holiday traditions opens its gates.

The Cheyenne Mountain Zoo's Electric Safari kicks off December 2 and runs through January 1, 2027. This is the one with more than 85 light sculptures spread across 50-plus acres, the illuminated animal inflatables, the holiday-safari drone show, and Santa at Safari Lodge. It's a big, immersive experience that works for a wide range of ages — toddlers react to the lights, older kids love the drone show, and it's genuinely one of those COS events that feels special for the whole family.

USA TODAY has ranked it as the second-best zoo lights experience in North America. That's the seventh straight year it's landed in the top three.

The main thing families need to know: advance tickets are required, and they sell out. If you're planning a visit in December, don't wait until the week you want to go — head to cmzoo.org now and pick your night. Some dates go weeks in advance, especially around the Christmas holiday stretch.

### STORY 4: AAP Report — 1 in 4 Kids Lives With a Parent With Substance Use Disorder

**Long-Form Post (3:00 PM MT)**

A new report from the American Academy of Pediatrics carries a number worth sitting with: about one in four U.S. children lives in a home with an adult who has a substance use disorder.

The report, published in the December 2026 issue of *Pediatrics*, is aimed at pediatricians — it gives providers guidance on how to have these conversations with families in a way that's respectful and nonjudgmental, and how to build in anticipatory guidance during routine well-child visits to promote child safety in these situations.

That's not something a lot of us think about when we schedule a well-child appointment. But it means that if your family has been touched by this in some way — whether it's a parent, a grandparent, another household member — the well-child visit is now a formally supported space to bring that up.

For COS families, the practical takeaway is simple: if this report made you think of someone, it might be worth bringing it to your child's next pediatrician appointment and asking what resources they have. The report is designed to help providers respond.

### STORY 5: Thanksgiving Break in COS — A Family Activity Guide

**Long-Form Post (5:00 PM MT)**

Thanksgiving break is next week, and it's one of those stretches where having a plan makes the week so much smoother.

Here are a couple of COS area events to build around:

The Turkey Trot 5K and Kids' Tiny Trot return to the Briargate YMCA (4025 Family Place) on Thanksgiving morning. Tiny Trot starts at 8:15 a.m. and the 5K follows at 9:00 a.m. Pricing: $43.65 for ages 6–19 and $54.25 for ages 20 and up. Strollers and dogs are welcome, and it's a great way to start the holiday with the kids before the big meal.

If you're up for a 40-minute drive toward Divide, the Colorado Wolf and Wildlife Center hosts a Turkey Toss on Thanksgiving morning from 9 to 11 a.m. — a chance to watch the wolves and other animals receive a Thanksgiving-themed enrichment meal. Ages 6–11 are $20, ages 12 and older are $40.

For the rest of the week, local options include family climbing sessions at CityRock (reserve ahead during holidays) and, for families with babies and toddlers, the PPLD library branches keep regular story time schedules during the break week — check ppld.org to confirm.
