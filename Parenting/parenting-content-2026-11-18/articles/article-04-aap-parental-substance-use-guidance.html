AAP: 1 in 4 Kids Lives With a Parent With a Substance Use Disorder — New Clinical Guidance

By Jamie Rivera | COS Parenting | November 18, 2026

A new clinical report from the American Academy of Pediatrics opens with a figure that stops you in place: approximately one in four U.S. children lives in a home with an adult who has a substance use disorder. The report, published in the December 2026 issue of Pediatrics, isn't aimed at parents directly — it's guidance for pediatricians. But what it means for COS families is worth understanding.

What the Report Says

The AAP report provides guidance for pediatricians on how to work with families affected by parental substance use. The core of the guidance: approach these conversations with respect and without judgment, and use routine well-child visits as an opportunity to build in anticipatory guidance — meaning proactive support for child safety and wellbeing, before a crisis rather than after one.

The report recognizes that substance use disorders affect families across income levels, neighborhoods, and demographics. The well-child visit, which many families attend consistently through early childhood, is one of the few regular touchpoints where a provider can build trust and offer support in a stable, non-crisis setting.

What This Means for Families at a Well-Child Visit

For COS families, the practical implication is that if parental substance use — whether your own, a partner's, or another household member's — is affecting your child or your household, the well-child visit is now a formally supported space to bring that up with your pediatrician. The report is designed to train providers to respond with resources and guidance rather than judgment.

You don't need to wait for a problem to surface. The anticipatory guidance framework means pediatricians are encouraged to have these conversations proactively as part of the visit — asking about household stressors, safety factors, and family support systems, with substance use as one part of a broader picture.

The Numbers in Colorado Springs Context

Colorado has consistently tracked above the national average for substance use disorder rates in recent years. That means the one-in-four national figure likely applies in Colorado Springs — and possibly at a higher rate. The AAP report doesn't provide state-by-state breakdowns, but COS families should understand the statistic as reflecting a reality that exists across the community, not at its margins.

What's Next

If this report resonates for your family, consider raising it at your child's next well-child visit. You can find the AAP's summary and additional resources at healthychildren.org, the AAP's family-facing site. For Colorado Springs-specific family support resources, El Paso County Public Health (elpasocountyhealth.org) maintains referral lists for local services including substance use treatment and family counseling.
