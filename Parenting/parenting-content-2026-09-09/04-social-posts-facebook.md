### STORY 1: Skip Hop Elmo Teether Recall

**Long-Form Post (8:00 AM MT)**

If you have a Skip Hop Elmo teether at home, it's time to set it aside.

The Consumer Product Safety Commission issued a recall on September 3, 2026 for the Skip Hop Baby Sesame Street Elmo Silicone Teether (model 9R263210). About 22,660 units were sold in the U.S. — and if yours is one of them, here's the issue: the black eyes on the teether can detach when the product is frozen, creating a serious choking hazard for babies.

Four consumers have already reported the eyes detaching. No injuries have been reported yet — but the CPSC and Skip Hop aren't waiting to find out. They're asking all families to stop using the product immediately.

The teether was sold at Carter's stores and online, as well as at skiphop.com, from January 2025 through March 2026. If you purchased one for about $12, that's the product. To get a refund (in the form of a gift card valid at Carter's, OshKosh B'gosh, Little Planet, and Skip Hop), visit skiphoprecall.com or call 800-692-4674, Monday through Friday, 9 AM–5:30 PM ET.

Please share this with anyone in your network who might have one. Do you still have yours?

### STORY 2: Pikes Peak Youth Fishing Derby

**Long-Form Post (10:00 AM MT)**

Colorado Springs families — this weekend's outdoor lineup just got a whole lot better.

The third annual Pikes Peak Youth Fishing Derby is happening September 13 at Crystal Creek Reservoir, six miles up the Pikes Peak Highway. It's free for registered kids ages 6–15 and their families, runs from 8:30 AM to 11 AM, and wraps up with an awards ceremony and complimentary lunch at 11:30 AM.

No fishing license needed. The first 100 registered young anglers get free fishing gear to take home. Volunteers from Colorado Parks and Wildlife will run a quick skills session before fishing begins — casting, knot-tying, baiting — so first-timers are absolutely welcome.

This is a great excuse to get up to Pikes Peak for the morning. Crystal Creek Reservoir is one of those spots that's a short drive but feels genuinely off the beaten path, especially as the fall colors start coming in.

Have your kids ever fished before? Drop it below — we're curious how many COS families have a little angler in training.

### STORY 3: Dahlia Days at Gather Mountain Blooms

**Long-Form Post (12:00 PM MT)**

The dahlias are ready, and the clock is ticking.

Gather Mountain Blooms (5210 S. U.S. 85/87, Colorado Springs) is hosting Dahlia Days on September 12 and September 19, from 8 AM to noon. These are the last two dates of the season — the original September 5 event was postponed after a late dahlia arrival this year, so this is your window.

Admission is $10 per person to get into the fields. A cup of fresh-cut dahlias is $30. There's brunch on-site from 4th Avenue Crepes, coffee from Good Gracious Coffee, floral cocktail options, and a mini makers market to browse while you're there.

Dahlia picking is one of those quintessential fall things to do in Colorado Springs that always ends up looking better than you planned. The kids tend to love it — it's a slow, sensory morning that doesn't feel like a "family activity" but kind of is one anyway.

September 12 is this Saturday. Spots go fast. Will you be going? 🌸

### STORY 4: Cool Science Festival — October 3-18

**Long-Form Post (3:00 PM MT)**

Start planning now: the Colorado Springs Cool Science Festival runs October 3–18, 2026, and it's one of the better free family events on the fall calendar.

The big kickoff is Cool Science Carnival Day on October 3 at the University of Colorado Colorado Springs. It's completely free (free parking too), and it's designed for kids in kindergarten through eighth grade. What's there? Science magic shows, lab tours, rockets, slime, drones, live animals, virtual reality, robots, and liquid nitrogen treats — which, if you've never seen kids react to liquid nitrogen frozen things, is worth the trip on its own.

Across all 16 days, most events in the festival are free, though a few may have a small fee. Events cover all ages, including adults and teens. The full schedule is at coolscience.org.

Three weeks out, so you have time to look at the calendar and figure out which days work best. What kinds of science activities get your kids most excited?

### STORY 5: Mid-September School Year Check-In

**Long-Form Post (8:00 PM MT)**

D49 families are around week six. D11 families are around week four or five. D20 families are around week three or four. That puts everyone past the novelty phase and into the grind — which is where it starts to matter whether the routines you set up in August are actually working.
