Skip Hop Elmo Teether Recall — Choking Hazard Warning (TIER 1)
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post If you have a Skip Hop Elmo teether at home, it's time to set it aside. The Consumer Product Safety Commission issued a recall on September 3, 2026 for the Skip Hop Baby Sesame Street Elmo Silicone Teether (model 9R263210). About 22,660 units were sold in the U.S. — and if yours is one of them, here's the issue: the black eyes on the teether can detach when the product is frozen, creating a serious choking hazard for babies. Four consumers have already reported the eyes detaching. No injuries have been reported yet — but the CPSC and Skip Hop aren't waiting to find out. They're asking all families to stop using the product immediately. The teether was sold at Carter's stores and online, as well as at skiphop.com, from January 2025 through March 2026. If you purchased one for about $12, that's the product. To get a refund (in the form of a gift card valid at Carter's, OshKosh B'gosh, Little Planet, and Skip Hop), visit skiphoprecall.com or call 800-692-4674, Monday through Friday, 9 AM–5:30 PM ET. Please share this with anyone in your network who might have one. Do you still have yours? ### Image Post Caption Active CPSC recall: Skip Hop Elmo teethers can lose their black eyes if frozen — choking hazard. 22,660 units affected. Stop use, get a refund at skiphoprecall.com. Share to spread the word.

Pikes Peak Youth Fishing Derby — This Weekend (TIER 2)
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Colorado Springs families — this weekend's outdoor lineup just got a whole lot better. The third annual Pikes Peak Youth Fishing Derby is happening September 13 at Crystal Creek Reservoir, six miles up the Pikes Peak Highway. It's free for registered kids ages 6–15 and their families, runs from 8:30 AM to 11 AM, and wraps up with an awards ceremony and complimentary lunch at 11:30 AM. No fishing license needed. The first 100 registered young anglers get free fishing gear to take home. Volunteers from Colorado Parks and Wildlife will run a quick skills session before fishing begins — casting, knot-tying, baiting — so first-timers are absolutely welcome. This is a great excuse to get up to Pikes Peak for the morning. Crystal Creek Reservoir is one of those spots that's a short drive but feels genuinely off the beaten path, especially as the fall colors start coming in. Have your kids ever fished before? Cool Science Festival — 16 Days of Free STEAM Fun Starting Oct 3 (TIER 2)
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Start planning now: the Colorado Springs Cool Science Festival runs October 3–18, 2026, and it's one of the better free family events on the fall calendar. The big kickoff is Cool Science Carnival Day on October 3 at the University of Colorado Colorado Springs. It's completely free (free parking too), and it's designed for kids in kindergarten through eighth grade. What's there? Science magic shows, lab tours, rockets, slime, drones, live animals, virtual reality, robots, and liquid nitrogen treats — which, if you've never seen kids react to liquid nitrogen frozen things, is worth the trip on its own. Across all 16 days, most events in the festival are free, though a few may have a small fee. Events cover all ages, including adults and teens. The full schedule is at coolscience.org. Three weeks out, so you have time to look at the calendar and figure out which days work best. What kinds of science activities get your kids most excited? ### Image Post Caption Colorado Springs Cool Science Festival: October 3–18. ### Long-Form Post We're well into September now, and if your family is starting to feel that first real wave of school-year exhaustion, that's completely normal. D49 families are around week six. D11 families are around week four or five. D20 families are around week three or four. That puts everyone past the novelty phase and into the grind — which is where it starts to matter whether the routines you set up in August are actually working. A few things worth checking in on right now: Is the homework flow actually sustainable, or is it becoming a nightly standoff? Are after-school activities energizing your kids or depleting them?
