### Long-Form Post Tonight at 5:30 PM, the D11 Board of Education votes on whether to put a $775 million school bond on the November ballot — and it could be the most consequential school decision for Colorado Springs families in more than 20 years. Here's what you need to know. The average D11 building is 58 years old. The district has identified more than $422 million in repairs and improvements — HVAC systems, safety upgrades, modernization, and in some cases entirely new buildings. Its annual capital budget is about $17 million. Do the math: the district can't keep up. If the D11 Board votes tonight to refer the bond to voters, Colorado Springs homeowners will see the question on the November general election ballot. The estimated cost to taxpayers is approximately $6.50 per month for every $100,000 of a home's assessed value. A "yes" vote tonight from the board means you get to decide in November. A "no" vote tonight means the bond doesn't go forward at all. D11's first day of school is August 12. Whatever happens tonight shapes the buildings those kids walk into for decades. The AAP Just Changed How We Think About Screen Time — 2026 Update
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post If you've been quietly ignoring the old "no more than one hour of screen time" rule for your school-age kids, the American Academy of Pediatrics has a message for you: that rule doesn't apply to them anymore. In January 2026, the AAP officially updated its screen time guidance for children ages 6 and up. The change is significant: rigid hour-based limits are out. The new approach centers on quality, context, and whether screen use is getting in the way of the things that matter — sleep, physical play, and face-to-face connection. The framework the AAP introduced is built around what they call the "5 Cs": whether the content is age-appropriate and high quality, whether it's contextually helpful (educational, co-viewed, or developmental), and whether it's displacing other important activities. For kids under 18 months, the guidance hasn't changed: no screens. For ages 2 to 5, 1 hour of high-quality content per day is still the benchmark. But for your 7-year-old who watches nature documentaries after homework, or your 10-year-old who codes on a tablet? The conversation is now about what they're watching, why, and whether the habit is healthy — not counting minutes. As back-to-school season starts, it's a good time to revisit screen habits. The 2026 guidance is more practical and more honest than what came before. What's your family's approach to screens this school year? --- ### Image Post Caption The AAP updated screen time guidance in 2026. CPSC Summer Baby Safety Alert: Vevor Swings and Baby Lounger Recalls
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post If you have a baby at home — or know someone who does — there are active CPSC recalls on infant sleep products that every family should be aware of right now. The Consumer Product Safety Commission has multiple recalls in effect this summer targeting products marketed for infant sleep and support: Vevor baby swings (manufactured by Sanven Technology) — recalled for violating the inclined sleeper ban under the Safe Sleep for Babies Act. The swings have an incline greater than 10 degrees, which can cause infants to roll into a face-down position and block their airway. Stop using immediately. Cpzzkq brand baby loungers (sold on Amazon by CetoPMax) — expanded recall for suffocation hazard, failing the mandatory standard for infant support cushions. Vevor brand baby loungers — recalled for entrapment and fall hazards, failing mandatory infant sleep product standards. These are all separate from the TOMY toddler tower recall covered yesterday. These products are designed for infants under 12 months. If you have any of these items in your home, stop using them and contact the manufacturer for refund or remedy information. You can verify all active recalls at cpsc.gov/Recalls. Is there a recall you've run across that other COS families should know about? --- ### Image Post Caption CPSC alerts this summer: Vevor baby swings recalled for suffocation hazard (inclined sleeper ban).
