CPSC Summer 2026 Alert: Multiple Baby Swings and Loungers Recalled for Suffocation Hazard

By Sarah Morales | August 5, 2026 | National Parenting

If you have a baby at home — or if you know someone who does — there are active Consumer Product Safety Commission recalls on infant sleep and support products that every family should check right now. The CPSC has issued or expanded recalls on three separate product lines this summer, all targeting products marketed for infant use. These are separate from the TOMY toddler tower recall covered yesterday.

Vevor Baby Swings (Sanven Technology) — Recalled

Sanven Technology has recalled its Vevor brand baby swings due to a suffocation hazard. The recall was triggered by a violation of the Safe Sleep for Babies Act — specifically, the swings have an incline greater than 10 degrees, which can cause infants to roll into a face-down position and block their airway. The recalled models include BB501K (white and gray with white base), BB702A (white and gray with gray base), and BB005 (black and gray with black base). Approximately 1,020 units are affected.

If you have any of these Vevor swing models in your home, stop using them immediately. Do not attempt to modify the product to reduce the incline. Contact Sanven Technology for refund information.

Cpzzkq Baby Loungers (CetoPMax on Amazon) — Expanded Recall

The CPSC has expanded the recall on Cpzzkq brand baby loungers sold on Amazon by CetoPMax. The expansion covers a suffocation hazard — the products fail the mandatory standard for infant support cushions. If you purchased one of these loungers on Amazon, it falls under this recall regardless of when you bought it. Stop using it and check the CPSC recall page for remedy information.

Vevor Baby Loungers — Recalled

Separately, Vevor brand baby loungers (distinct from the Vevor swing recall above) have been recalled for entrapment and fall hazards. These loungers fail to meet the mandatory standard for infant sleep products, creating a risk of entrapment or fall injury. Stop using any Vevor-branded baby loungers immediately and contact the manufacturer for remedy options.

What These Products Have in Common

All three recalls involve products designed for infants under 12 months. All three have been found to violate federal mandatory safety standards — in two cases related to suffocation risk, in the third related to entrapment and falls. These are not precautionary recalls based on potential concerns; they're enforcement actions based on confirmed violations of specific safety requirements.

What's Next

If you have any of these products in your home, stop using them now and check cpsc.gov/Recalls for the specific remedy options for each recall. The CPSC recall pages include manufacturer contact information for requesting refunds or remedies. Sharing this information with other parents — especially those with newborns or infants under one year — is worth doing today.
