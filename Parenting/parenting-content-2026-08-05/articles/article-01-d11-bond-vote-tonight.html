D11 Bond Vote Is Tonight: What COS Parents Need to Know Before 5:30 PM

By Sarah Morales | August 5, 2026 | Local News

Tonight, at 5:30 PM, the D11 Board of Education will vote on whether to place a $775 million bond measure on the November general election ballot. If that number feels significant, it is — this is the most consequential vote for District 11 families in more than 20 years. Here's everything you need to know before the vote happens.

Why D11 Is Asking for $775 Million

The math is straightforward, even if the solution isn't. The average D11 building is 58 years old. The district has identified more than $422 million in needed repairs and improvements — HVAC systems, safety upgrades, building modernization, and in at least one case, full replacement. Against that backlog, D11's annual capital budget is approximately $17 million. At that rate, the district would need nearly 25 years just to fund what's already broken. New classrooms, new safety systems, and aging facilities that are still decades behind would fall even further back.

The bond, if it reaches voters and passes in November, would fund a range of projects across the district: upgraded heating and air systems, improved safety infrastructure, building modernization, and one new construction. It would be D11's first major bond in more than two decades.

What the Vote Tonight Actually Decides

Tonight's vote is not whether the bond passes or fails. It's whether you — COS voters — get to make that call in November.

The D11 Board votes on a "ballot referral," which is the formal step of placing the bond question on the November general election ballot. A yes vote from the board means the bond goes to voters. A no vote tonight means it doesn't go forward at all, at least not on this timeline.

If the board approves it tonight, COS homeowners can expect to see the question on the November ballot. The estimated taxpayer cost is approximately $6.50 per month for every $100,000 of a home's assessed value. For a home assessed at $400,000, that's roughly $26 per month — or about $312 per year.

Context: D11's First Day Is August 12

D11 students return to class on August 12 — exactly one week from today. Whatever the board decides tonight, those students will walk into buildings that reflect decades of deferred maintenance. The bond vote, if it reaches November and passes, would be the beginning of a multi-year construction and improvement process. It won't fix anything before this school year, but it would set the direction for the decade ahead.

What's Next

If the board votes yes tonight, the bond measure goes to D11 voters in November. Between now and then, expect community meetings, informational campaigns from both supporters and opponents, and coverage from local news outlets. If the board votes no, this specific measure is off the table — and the district's capital backlog stays where it is.

For the latest on tonight's vote outcome and what comes next, follow D11's official communications at d11.org.
