### STORY 1: Build-A-Bear Heartwarming Hugs Bears Recall

**Long-Form Post (8:00 AM MT):**
If your family purchased a Build-A-Bear Heartwarming Hugs Bear this past winter or spring, this is important to see right away.

Build-A-Bear Workshop has issued a recall of approximately 36,000 Heartwarming Hugs Bears. The issue is with the zipper slider on the side pouch — the pouch holds around 2.5 pounds of ceramic heating and cooling beads. That zipper slider can detach from the pouch, exposing the bead contents and creating a choking hazard for young children.

The bears were sold at Build-A-Bear Workshop stores between January and March 2026, priced at approximately $48. One zipper detachment has been reported in the UK. No injuries have been reported in the United States.

What to do: Stop using the bear right now. Bring it to any Build-A-Bear Workshop store — you do not need a receipt. You will receive either a full refund or a gift card.

For more information, visit cpsc.gov or contact Build-A-Bear Workshop customer service.

Do you have one of these at home? It is worth checking before letting young children near it.

### STORY 2: PIXLABBY Magnetic Fidget Sliders Recall

**Long-Form Post (10:00 AM MT):**
This recall is small in product count but serious in hazard level — please share it with anyone who may have bought a fidget toy on Amazon in the past year.

The CPSC has recalled approximately 860 PIXLABBY Magnetic Fidget Sliders, sold on Amazon between October 2025 and May 2026 for about $10.

The problem: the silicone casing on the sliders can be removed by children, and once removed, it exposes high-powered magnets. High-powered magnets are one of the most dangerous things a child can ingest. When two or more are swallowed, they can attract each other through intestinal walls — causing perforation, twisting, blockages, blood poisoning, and death.

One child has already been hospitalized after ingesting two of the small magnets from this product.

What to do: If you have this product, stop using it immediately. Photograph the product being disposed of and contact PIXLABBY to request a full refund.

Fidget toys and sensory sliders are popular with children and teens. If you have any fidget product at home that contains small internal components, it is worth checking whether it has a known recall — search cpsc.gov by product name or category.

### STORY 4: 81% of Toddler Foods Are Ultra-Processed

**Long-Form Post (12:00 PM MT):**
If you have a toddler and you buy those little pouches, puffs, and snack cups from the grocery store — this study from the University of Texas at Austin is worth a few minutes of your time.

Researchers audited 2,783 toddler food products sold in 21 grocery stores, covering products marketed for children between the ages of six months and three years. Their findings: 81% of those products were ultra-processed. Almost half (49%) failed at least one World Health Organization nutritional recommendation for that age group.

The specific numbers are telling: 25% of the products had too much sodium, 17% had too much sugar, 12% were too calorie-dense, and 11% exceeded recommended fat levels.

Researchers presented the findings at the American Society of Nutrition's NUTRITION 2026 annual conference and noted that ultra-processed foods in early childhood displace the vital nutrients children need for brain development, and are linked over time to childhood obesity, type 2 diabetes, and heart disease.

What does this mean practically? It is not a reason to panic about everything in your shopping cart. The key message from the researchers is that product labels — not package fronts — tell the real story. A pouch or snack that says "organic" or has a fruit on the label can still be classified as ultra-processed and fall short on nutrition.

For Colorado Springs parents shopping at King Soopers, Safeway, Natural Grocers, or any local store: the ingredients list and the nutrition facts panel are your tools. The shorter the ingredients list and the lower the added sugars and sodium, the better.

### STORY 5: CMZoo Giraffe Center Opening Soon

**Long-Form Post (3:00 PM MT):**
Cheyenne Mountain Zoo is about to open something families in Colorado Springs are going to love — and it is coming very soon.

The zoo announced its $40 million International Center for the Care and Conservation of Giraffe is opening shortly. KKTV reported the update on August 6. An exact date has not been announced, but the facility is described as ready.

Here is what the new giraffe center features:

The giraffe barn spans 12,000 square feet and is covered by an ETFE roof — a transparent, lightweight material. The CMZoo team notes it is the largest structure of this type covering an animal exhibit anywhere in North America.

Inside, the feeding zone count goes from 3 to 11. That means more opportunities for families to get close and interact with the animals during a visit. The expanded outdoor giraffe yard, a new gift shop, and a new café round out the experience.

The new center is part of CMZoo's centennial year — the zoo is celebrating 100 years of operation in 2026, and this is one of several major milestones in that anniversary year.

No exact opening date has been announced. Families should check cmzoo.org for the official announcement — it could come any day.

What is your family's favorite CMZoo exhibit? The giraffe center might be about to become a new favorite.
