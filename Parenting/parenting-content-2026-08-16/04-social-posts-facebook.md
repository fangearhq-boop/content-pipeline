### STORY 1: CMZoo Little Paws Nature Preschool

**Long-Form Post**
Colorado Springs parents, this one is worth bookmarking.

The Cheyenne Mountain Zoo is opening its first-ever state-accredited outdoor preschool next Monday, August 24 — and registration is still open.

Little Paws Preschool is not a summer camp or a zoo class. It is a full preschool program, accredited by the Colorado Department of Early Childhood, with state-licensed teachers and a calendar that follows Cheyenne Mountain School District 12. The difference is the classroom: zoo trails, animal habitats, and outdoor learning environments replace four walls and fluorescent lights.

There are two age tracks:

3-year-olds: Tuesdays and Thursdays, 9 AM to noon, $220.75 per month
4-5-year-olds: Mondays, Wednesdays, and Fridays, 9 AM to 1 PM, $460.75 per month

Class sizes are approximately 15 students. Because the program is accredited and calendar-synced with a local school district, it qualifies as a formal early childhood education program — not just enrichment.

If you have a preschool-age child and have been looking for something genuinely different in COS, this is worth a call. Email LittlePawsPreschool@cmzoo.org to request information and an application.

### STORY 2: AAP 2026-27 Flu Vaccine Guidelines

**Long-Form Post**
The American Academy of Pediatrics just released its flu vaccine guidelines for the 2026-27 season. Here is what Colorado Springs families need to know before fall arrives.

First, last season's numbers: 190 children died of influenza in the United States. Of those deaths, 85% occurred in unvaccinated children. Only 49.4% of children ages 6 months through 17 years were vaccinated through May 2026 — well below what pediatricians say is needed for meaningful community protection.

The AAP's recommendation is unchanged in principle but now backed by sharper data: every child 6 months and older without a medical contraindication should receive an annual flu vaccine as soon as it becomes available this fall.

There is one critical detail about dosing that many parents do not know:

If your child is between 6 months and 8 years old AND any of the following applies to them, they need TWO doses this season, at least 4 weeks apart:
- They have never received a flu vaccine before
- They have received only 1 dose total in their entire history (before July 1, 2026)
- You are unsure of their vaccination history

If they do not meet any of those criteria, a single dose is sufficient.

The AAP says there is no preference for a specific brand or formulation — any licensed, age-appropriate vaccine is appropriate. Do not delay if your preferred type is unavailable.

In Colorado Springs, flu vaccines typically become available at pediatricians, Walgreens, King Soopers pharmacies, and through El Paso County Public Health starting in September. The AAP recommends completing your child's full course by the end of October.

With D20 starting tomorrow and the full school year underway, scheduling flu vaccines in the next few weeks is a practical step most families can make time for.

### STORY 4: Vevor Swings and Finger Lights Recalls

**Long-Form Post**
Two more baby and children's product recalls COS parents should know about — both from August 2026 CPSC announcements and neither covered in our recent recall roundups.

VEVOR BABY SWINGS (Sanven Technology) — SUFFOCATION HAZARD
This recall is serious because the product was never safe to begin with: Vevor baby swings violate the mandatory federal standard for infant sleep products AND the inclined sleepers ban. Both of those standards exist because angled, padded products have been linked to infant suffocation.

Details:
- Sold at Vevor.com and Amazon.com between January and August 2025
- Price range: $65-$80
- No injuries reported in the US — but the product is illegal under federal safety law
- Action: Stop using immediately. Contact Vevor for instructions.

FINGER LIGHTS — BUTTON BATTERY HAZARD
Two brands are affected: POPOOO Jungle Safari LED Finger Lights (~62,490 units) and Delightmi Oitnlaughter LED Finger Lights (~1,012 units). Children can access the internal button cell batteries too easily.

Button batteries are a hazard that pediatric ER staff take extremely seriously. When swallowed, they can cause severe internal chemical burns and death within hours — faster than most parents realize. If your child swallows one, go to the emergency room immediately. Do not wait to see if symptoms develop.

Both products were sold on Amazon.com. Check your home for both.
