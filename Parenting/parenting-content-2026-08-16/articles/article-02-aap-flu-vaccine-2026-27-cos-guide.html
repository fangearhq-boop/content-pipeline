AAP Releases 2026-27 Flu Vaccine Guidelines: What Colorado Springs Parents Need to Know Before Fall

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting | August 16, 2026

Quick Reference

  • Who should be vaccinated: Every child 6 months and older without a medical contraindication
  • When to vaccinate: As soon as vaccines are available; complete by end of October
  • Two doses required if: First-time recipient, or only 1 lifetime dose before July 1, 2026, or unknown history — ages 6 months through 8 years only
  • Time between doses: At least 4 weeks
  • Formulation preference: None — any licensed, age-appropriate vaccine is appropriate
  • Where in COS: Pediatricians, Walgreens, King Soopers pharmacies, El Paso County Public Health (vaccines typically available September)
  • Source: American Academy of Pediatrics, 2026-27 Influenza Policy Statement (published August 2026)

The American Academy of Pediatrics released its 2026-27 influenza season policy statement this month, and the data behind it is sobering. In the 2025-2026 flu season, 190 children died from influenza in the United States. Of those 190, 85% were unvaccinated.

Despite those numbers, only 49.4% of children ages 6 months through 17 years received a flu vaccine through May 2026. That is less than half of all children in the recommended age range — and well below the coverage rates that public health experts say are needed to provide meaningful community protection, especially in school settings like those that will begin filling in Colorado Springs this week.

The Core Recommendation

The AAP recommendation is clear: every child 6 months of age and older without a medical contraindication should receive an annual influenza vaccine, beginning as soon as vaccines are available this fall.

There is no preference for a specific brand or formulation. The AAP states that any licensed, age-appropriate vaccine is appropriate, and families should not delay vaccination if their preferred type is temporarily unavailable. Getting vaccinated — with whatever is available — is more important than waiting for a specific option.

The Two-Dose Rule: Who It Applies To

This is the detail most families miss. Children between 6 months and 8 years of age need two doses this season if any of the following is true:

  • This is their first time ever receiving an influenza vaccine
  • They have received only one flu vaccine dose total in their lifetime — received before July 1, 2026
  • Their influenza vaccination history is unknown

The two doses must be given at least four weeks apart. The AAP recommends starting as early as possible so that both doses can be completed well before peak flu season.

Children who are 9 years or older, or children under 9 who have received two or more lifetime doses of flu vaccine (including at least one dose on or after July 1, 2026), need only a single dose.

If you are not sure whether your child falls into the two-dose category, your pediatrician's office can check their vaccination record and advise you accordingly.

Why the Timing Matters for Colorado Springs Families

With D20 starting tomorrow, all three of Colorado Springs' major school districts are now in session for the 2026-27 school year. D49 opened on August 3, D11 on August 12, and D20 begins August 17-18. Influenza spreads efficiently in school settings, and the best window to vaccinate is before community transmission begins — not after.

In Colorado Springs, seasonal flu vaccines typically become available at pediatric practices, Walgreens and King Soopers pharmacies, and through El Paso County Public Health beginning in early to mid-September. The AAP recommends completing the full vaccination course by the end of October, which means families who need the two-dose series should schedule the first appointment as soon as vaccines become available.

What's Next

The practical next step for Colorado Springs parents is straightforward: contact your child's pediatrician now to confirm their vaccination history and schedule their appointment for when fall vaccines arrive. For families without a primary care provider, El Paso County Public Health and local pharmacies offer flu vaccines without a prior appointment — check coloradosprings.gov or epcdph.org for availability updates as September approaches. The 2025-2026 season's 190 pediatric deaths make the case more clearly than any recommendation can: flu vaccination in children is not a minor precaution. It is one of the highest-impact things Colorado Springs parents can do this fall.
