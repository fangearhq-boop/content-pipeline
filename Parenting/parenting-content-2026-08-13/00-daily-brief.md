### STORY 2: Two Safety Recalls Parents Need to Know: GOPO Teething Toys & HARPPA Toddler Tower Stools [TIER 1]
**Headline:** Two Product Recalls: Popular Teething Toy Poses Choking Risk, Toddler Stool Can Collapse
**Pillar:** National Parenting
**Who:** Parents of infants and toddlers
**What:** CPSC recalled ~70,410 GOPO Toys Pull String Teething Toys — silicone strings exceed permitted length and fall below minimum size, reaching the back of a child's throat (3 reported choking/respiratory distress incidents). Stop use immediately; get refund by cutting strings and emailing recalls@gopotoys.com. Also recalled: ~41,166 HARPPA Nordi Toddler Tower Stools (Model HANS0002) — can collapse or tip; child's torso can fit through openings. HARPPA offers free repair kit including nets and stabilizing feet.
**Why it matters:** Combined 111,576 units in circulation. Both products were sold on Amazon. GOPO recall involves a risk of death from choking — highest urgency.
**Angle:** Pair both for a "two-recall alert" article. For social: lead with GOPO (higher urgency, higher unit count).

### STORY 4: Vevor Baby Swings Recalled for Suffocation Risk [TIER 2]
**Headline:** Vevor Baby Swings Recalled: Inclined Design Violates Safe Sleep Law
**Pillar:** National Parenting
**Who:** Parents of infants
**What:** CPSC issued recall #26-591 for ~1,020 Vevor Baby Swings (by Sanven Technology). Models BB501K, BB702A, BB005K. Swings have an incline angle greater than 10 degrees, violating the federal Safe Sleep for Babies Act ban on inclined sleepers. Risk: suffocation. Sold on VEVOR.com, Amazon.com, and Wayfair.com, Nov 2024–Jan 2026, ~$30. Stop use immediately. Full refund available — call 855-599-6320 (Mon–Fri, 9 AM–5 PM PT) or visit vevor.com/pages/vevor-baby-swing-recall.
**Why it matters:** Inclined sleepers have been banned since the Safe Sleep for Babies Act (2022). This is the third baby swing/sleep product recall this month; pattern is relevant for COS families.
**Angle:** Contextualize within the Safe Sleep for Babies Act and the pattern of inclined-sleeper recalls. Distinct from Aug 12's Bimuva and Wnttmt/WURUI.BABY recalls.

### STORY 5: New Study: 4+ Hours of Screen Time Linked to 45% Higher Anxiety in Kids [TIER 2]
**Headline:** Study of 50,000 Kids Links 4+ Daily Screen Hours to 45% Higher Anxiety, 61% Higher Depression Risk
**Pillar:** National Parenting
**Who:** Parents of school-age children (6–17)
**What:** A 2026 study published in *Humanities and Social Sciences Communications* (Nature portfolio) analyzed data from 50,231 U.S. children ages 6–17. Key findings: 4+ hours of daily entertainment screen time linked to 45% higher anxiety risk and 61% higher depression risk. Sleep displacement is the key mechanism — bedroom screens after 9 PM identified as the single highest-risk behavior. HHS and the Surgeon General are calling for urgent action. Takeaways: device-free bedrooms; 1-hour cap ages 2–5; no screens before 18 months (per AAP).
**Why it matters:** Largest recent study on this topic; specific, actionable risk percentages that parents can use. Distinct from Aug 11's AAP screen time framework story (which focused on type/context over hour limits — different angle and different source).
