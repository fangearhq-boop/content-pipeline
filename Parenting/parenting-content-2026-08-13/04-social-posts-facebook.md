### STORY 2: CPSC Recalls — GOPO Teething Toys & HARPPA Toddler Tower Stools

#### Long-Form Post

Two new CPSC recalls worth knowing about if you have young kids at home.

The first — and more urgent — is the GOPO Toys Pull String Teething Toy recall. About 70,410 units were sold on Amazon between August 2023 and March 2026, priced at $11–$15. The hazard is serious: the silicone strings exceed permitted length guidelines and can reach the back of a child's throat, posing a choking and respiratory distress risk. Three incidents have already been reported. If you have one, stop using it immediately. To get your refund, cut all the silicone strings, write "DESTROYED" across the toy body in permanent marker, photograph it, and email the photo to recalls@gopotoys.com.

The second recall is the HARPPA Nordi Toddler Tower Stool (Model HANS0002). About 41,166 units were sold on Harppababy.com and Amazon between September 2023 and June 2026 for around $130 each. These stools can collapse or tip over during use, and the openings on the front and back are large enough for a child's torso to become entrapped — a serious fall and entrapment hazard. HARPPA is offering a free repair kit (protective nets and stabilizing feet) with free shipping. Contact HARPPA to request it.

Both products were widely available on Amazon. If you're not sure whether you have either, check the HARPPA label on the underside of the stool — it'll say "MODEL No.: HANS0002" if it's the recalled unit.

### STORY 4: Vevor Baby Swings Recall

#### Long-Form Post

Another baby product recall to flag, especially if you have an infant at home.

The CPSC has recalled approximately 1,020 Vevor Baby Swings made by Sanven Technology. The affected models are BB501K (white and gray with a white base), BB702A (white and gray with a gray base), and BB005K (black and gray with a black base). These swings were sold on VEVOR.com, Amazon.com, and Wayfair.com between November 2024 and January 2026 for around $30.

The problem: these swings have an incline angle of more than 10 degrees, which violates the federal Safe Sleep for Babies Act. That law prohibits inclined sleepers specifically because inclined sleep positions can allow an infant's head to fall forward and block the airway — a suffocation risk. If you have one, stop using it immediately.

The good news is that Sanven Technology is offering a full refund. You can reach them at 855-599-6320 (Monday through Friday, 9 AM to 5 PM Pacific Time), by email at recalling@vevor.com, or through their recall page at vevor.com/pages/vevor-baby-swing-recall.

This is the third infant sleep product recall in the COS Parenting coverage area this month. The Safe Sleep for Babies Act has been in effect since 2022 — it's worth doing a quick audit of any baby sleep gear you have at home, especially items purchased online.

### STORY 5: Screen Time & Child Mental Health Study 2026

#### Long-Form Post

A new study of more than 50,000 U.S. children has quantified something many parents have suspected: heavy daily screen time is linked to significantly higher rates of anxiety and depression in kids.

The research, published in 2026 in the journal *Humanities and Social Sciences Communications* (part of the Nature portfolio), analyzed data from 50,231 children and adolescents ages 6 to 17. The findings: kids who spent four or more hours per day on entertainment screens had a 45% higher risk of anxiety and a 61% higher risk of depression compared to kids with lower daily screen use.

Here's what made the study particularly useful for parents: it identified the mechanism. It's not primarily about the content kids are consuming — it's about sleep displacement. Screens push out sleep and physical activity. And the single highest-risk behavior wasn't screens in general — it was specifically bedroom devices used after 9 PM.

The researchers noted that adolescents in the U.S. currently average seven to nine hours of entertainment screen time per day. HHS and the Surgeon General have called for urgent policy action in response to findings like these.

For parents, the actionable guidance from this research aligns with AAP recommendations:
- Remove devices from bedrooms, especially after 9 PM
- Limit entertainment screen time to one hour per day for children ages 2–5
- Avoid screens before 18 months (other than video calls)

Back-to-school is the most natural time to reset routines — and screen rules are a routine. If you've been meaning to revisit your family's screen setup, this weekend is a good starting point.
