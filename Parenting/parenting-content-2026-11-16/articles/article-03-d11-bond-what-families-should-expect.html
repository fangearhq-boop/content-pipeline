D11 Bond Is Certified. Here's What Families at Holmes and MacLaren Should Expect Next

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting | November 16, 2026

Colorado Springs School District 11's $775 million bond measure has been certified — roughly 51.68% of voters said yes, making it the first D11 bond since 2006. The result means significant construction is coming to schools across the district. For families at Holmes Middle School and MacLaren Elementary, those changes are the most significant in the bond package. Here's what the road ahead actually looks like.

The Two Biggest Projects

Holmes Middle School is slated for an $80 million rebuild — not a renovation, but a full replacement of the building. MacLaren Elementary is set for $24.18 million in improvements. These are the two largest individual school-level allocations in the bond, which covers work at multiple D11 schools.

Both schools have been dealing with aging infrastructure. The bond measure placed them at the top of the priority list because of the scale of deferred maintenance and the need for updated facilities to serve students effectively.

The Typical Bond Timeline

A certified bond doesn't mean construction starts next month. For a project the size of the Holmes rebuild, the realistic sequence looks like this:

First, D11 enters a planning and procurement phase — hiring architects and construction managers, scoping the work, and aligning the project with district educational priorities. This phase typically takes 6 to 12 months after certification.

Then come community design meetings. D11 will invite families, teachers, and staff at affected schools to weigh in on how the new buildings and spaces should work. These sessions are worth attending. They're where decisions get made about classroom configurations, common spaces, and the features that will serve families for the next 30 years.

After that, design is finalized and construction contracts are issued. Ground typically breaks 12 to 18 months after the planning phase begins. For a full rebuild like Holmes, construction could take two to three years from start to completion.

What Happens to Students During Construction

For a full rebuild, D11 typically keeps students in the existing building as long as safely possible, then arranges temporary placement or phased construction so the school can continue operating. D11 has experience managing active-site construction at schools — the process is disruptive, but it's manageable. Families at Holmes will receive detailed transition plans well before any displacement occurs.

For MacLaren's $24.18 million improvement project, the scope will determine how much, if any, disruption students experience.

The Oversight Committee

Colorado bond measures of this scale typically require the formation of a citizen oversight committee to track how funds are spent. This committee reviews invoices, monitors project timelines, and reports publicly to the school board. Families who want to be involved in holding the district accountable for how bond money is used can watch for the formation of that committee and apply to serve.

What's Next

The best place to follow the bond project timeline is d11.org. D11 board meetings are open to the public and are where timeline decisions will be made in the months ahead. If you're a Holmes or MacLaren family, now is the time to get on the district's email list and plan to attend community design meetings when they're scheduled.

The bond passed. The work starts now.
