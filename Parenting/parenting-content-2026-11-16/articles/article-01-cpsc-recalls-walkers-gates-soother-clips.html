CPSC Recalls Infant Walkers, Safety Gates, and Soother Clips: What COS Families Need to Know

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting | November 16, 2026

Three new CPSC recalls came through this week affecting products many Colorado Springs families keep in daily rotation: an infant walker, a retractable safety gate, and a soother clip. If you have any of these at home, it's worth five minutes right now to check.

Uuoeebb Infant Walkers

The Consumer Product Safety Commission has formally recalled Uuoeebb infant walkers sold on Amazon by BaoD. The walkers pose entrapment and fall hazards and violate the mandatory federal safety standard for infant walkers. If your child uses one of these, stop use immediately.

To find out whether your walker is affected and what the remedy is — typically a refund or replacement — visit the official recall page at cpsc.gov/Recalls.

BabyBond Retractable Safety Gates

Infant Innovations has recalled its BabyBond retractable safety gates for entrapment hazards. The gates violate the mandatory federal standard for infant gates and enclosures — the same category of product families use at the top and bottom of stairs. If you have a BabyBond retractable gate in your home, stop using it now.

Details on the specific models affected and how to get a remedy are on the official recall page at cpsc.gov/Recalls.

Primark Little Bear 2-Pack Soother Clips

Primark has recalled its Little Bear 2-Pack Soother Clips for choking and laceration hazards. These are small accessories that attach a pacifier to a baby's clothing — a product that sees daily use in many families with infants. If you have them, stop using them and check the recall page for the remedy.

The official recall page is at cpsc.gov/Recalls.

How to Check Any Product on the CPSC Database

The CPSC maintains a searchable recall database at cpsc.gov/Recalls. You can search by product category, brand name, or recall number. The database is updated as new recalls are issued — bookmarking it and checking monthly is one of the simplest things COS families can do to stay on top of product safety.

What's Next

If you own any of these products, the first step is to stop use today and visit the CPSC recall page for your item. Most recalls offer a full refund or a free remedy kit. If you're unsure whether your specific product is covered, the CPSC helpline is 1-800-638-2772.

For Colorado Springs families who want to stay ahead of product safety news, the CPSC also offers email and text alerts — subscribe at cpsc.gov/Recalls to get notified when new recalls are issued in product categories relevant to your household.
