### STORY 1: SHEIN Pull & Chew Montessori Teething Toy Recall

### Long-Form (8:00 AM MT)

Heads up if you've ever ordered from SHEIN — there's a product recall parents need to know about right now. 🚨

SHEIN is recalling about 644 of its Pull & Chew Montessori Teething Toys. The silicone strings on these toys are too long and too thin, and the CPSC says they can reach the back of a baby's throat and become lodged — posing a serious, even deadly, choking risk. No injuries have been reported yet, which means acting fast now could prevent one.

If you have these toys, stop using them immediately. The recalled toys are silicone in green, pink, yellow, or blue, sold on SHEIN.com from December 2025 through April 2026 for about $10 each. To get a full refund, cut the silicone strings and send a photo of the destroyed toy to productrecall@shein.com. You don't need to mail anything back — just the photo.

The Consumer Product Safety Commission issued this recall, and it also applies in Canada. If you're not sure whether your toy is included, err on the side of caution.

### STORY 2: VFC COVID Vaccines 2026-27 — Delays

### Long-Form (10:00 AM MT)

If your family relies on the Vaccines for Children (VFC) program, here's what you need to know about the 2026-27 COVID vaccines.

The good news: COVID-19 vaccines have officially been added to the VFC program for the 2026-27 season. That means doctors who participate in the program can now request doses for eligible patients. The VFC program provides vaccines at no cost to children on Medicaid, uninsured children, children who are underinsured (through a Federally Qualified Health Center), and American Indian and Alaska Native children — roughly half of all U.S. kids.

The frustrating news: the CDC hasn't confirmed when those doses will actually be shipped. While families with private insurance may be able to get their kids vaccinated relatively soon, VFC-eligible children could face a wait of weeks or months. A report from CIDRAP described the delay as "CDC inaction delaying COVID shots for low-income children."

If your family is VFC-eligible, the best step right now is to call your pediatrician's office, ask whether they participate in VFC, and get on a waiting list for when doses arrive. Don't wait for the announcement — proactive is better.

### STORY 4: Rock Ledge Ranch Harvest Festival — Oct 3

### Long-Form (3:00 PM MT)

One week from today, one of Colorado Springs' best family fall traditions returns. 🍂

The Rock Ledge Ranch Harvest Festival is happening Saturday, October 3, from 10am to 4pm at 3105 Gateway Road. This is a genuinely fun, hands-on day — not just a pumpkin patch, but a full historic ranch experience with activities all afternoon.

Here's what to expect: wagon rides, old-time music, a chance to grab a pumpkin to take home ($5 each), a candy scramble, festival games, and cider-making demonstrations that dig into the history of apples in the Pikes Peak Region. There are also food trucks, pie, warm cider, tours of the historic homes on site, and a working Blacksmith demonstration. The General Store is open for ranch-made souvenirs.

Tickets are $4–$8 per person (exact pricing by age at the gate). Kids two and under get in free. It's a full afternoon without breaking the budget.

If you're trying to pick between this and the Cool Science Festival Carnival Day (also October 3), good news: both are worth checking out — they're at completely different venues.

### STORY 5: COS 2026 Park System Master Plan

### Long-Form (5:00 PM MT)

Colorado Springs' parks are getting a long-term plan — and it's one Colorado Springs families should know about.

The city's 2026 Park System Master Plan just closed its public comment period on September 22. The plan is designed to guide the future of Colorado Springs parks, trail systems, open spaces, community centers, and recreational programming for years to come. If you missed the comment window, the plan is still worth understanding — it will shape the outdoor spaces your family uses.

One recent example of this kind of investment: the Mary Starsmore Discovery Plaza, which opened in June 2026 at the Starsmore Visitor and Nature Center in North Cheyenne Cañon Park. The project started as a fix for a non-compliant parking lot and grew into a full outdoor plaza with ADA-accessible paths, an outdoor classroom, renovated restrooms, and parking improvements. It's a great reminder that advocacy and planning actually produce real improvements.

Keep an eye on the city's parks website (coloradosprings.gov) as the Master Plan moves toward adoption. There may still be opportunities to weigh in through city council channels.
