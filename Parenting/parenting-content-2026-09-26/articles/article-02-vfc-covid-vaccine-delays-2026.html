COVID Vaccines Added to VFC Program for 2026-27, but Shipment Delays May Leave Low-Income Kids Behind

By Jamie Rivera | COS Parenting | September 26, 2026

The federal Vaccines for Children (VFC) program has officially added COVID-19 vaccines to its 2026-27 contract, meaning providers enrolled in the program can now request doses for eligible patients. For the roughly half of U.S. children who qualify for free VFC vaccines, that's an important milestone. The problem: the CDC has not announced when those doses will actually ship.

The gap matters. Children covered by private insurance may be able to get their 2026-27 COVID vaccine within weeks of it becoming available. VFC-eligible children, whose families depend on the program specifically because they can't afford out-of-pocket vaccination costs, could be waiting weeks or months longer.

Who the VFC Program Serves

The Vaccines for Children program was created to make sure cost never stands between a child and a recommended vaccine. It's run by the CDC, which purchases vaccines at a discount and distributes them to enrolled providers — pediatricians, family doctors, federally qualified health centers, and some public health clinics. The vaccines are then provided at no cost to eligible patients.

A child qualifies for VFC vaccines if they are:

  • Enrolled in Medicaid
  • Uninsured
  • Underinsured (insurance doesn't cover vaccines) — through a Federally Qualified Health Center only
  • American Indian or Alaska Native

Estimates put the share of U.S. children who qualify at roughly half — a substantial portion of the pediatric population. In communities like Colorado Springs with significant Medicaid enrollment, the VFC program is a critical part of routine childhood vaccination.

What's Causing the Delay

According to reporting from the Center for Infectious Disease Research and Policy (CIDRAP), the delay is described as "CDC inaction" that's holding up COVID shots for low-income children. The MnVFC September 2026 announcement confirms that while vaccines have been added to the VFC contract and providers can now request them, "the CDC has not provided a shipment timeline yet. Vaccine will be shipped as soon as it is available."

This language — available but unshipped, no timeline confirmed — is what distinguishes the current situation from a routine rollout. Providers who want to serve VFC-eligible patients are in a holding pattern.

What Colorado Springs Families Can Do Now

If your family is VFC-eligible, the most useful step right now is a phone call. Contact your child's pediatrician or family doctor and ask:

  • Does this practice participate in the VFC program?
  • Have you received your 2026-27 COVID vaccine supply for VFC patients yet?
  • Can we be added to a waiting list when doses arrive?

If your current provider isn't enrolled in VFC, El Paso County Public Health (elpasocountyhealth.org) maintains a list of local VFC providers. The Colorado Immunization Information System (CIIS) can also help families find where vaccines are available.

For families who can access vaccines through private insurance in the meantime, the AAP's 2026-27 COVID vaccine recommendations — covered here on September 25 — call for one dose for all children ages six months through 18 years, regardless of prior vaccination history.

What's Next

Watch for an update from the CDC or Colorado's immunization program when shipment dates are confirmed. When doses do arrive at VFC providers, families who've already called ahead will be first in line. If your child's doctor is not a VFC provider, this is also a good moment to research alternatives — public health clinics and FQHCs often have the fastest turnaround on VFC vaccine availability because they have direct pipeline agreements.

The Colorado Department of Public Health & Environment tracks vaccine availability statewide. Their immunization program page at cdphe.colorado.gov is the most reliable source for updates specific to Colorado families.
