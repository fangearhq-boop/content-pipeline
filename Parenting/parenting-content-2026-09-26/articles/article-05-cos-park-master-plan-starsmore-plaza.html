Colorado Springs' 2026 Park System Master Plan: What Families Should Know as Comments Close

By Jamie Rivera | COS Parenting | September 26, 2026

The Colorado Springs 2026 Park System Master Plan just completed its public comment period on September 22. The plan is the city's long-range roadmap for parks, trail systems, open spaces, community centers, and recreational programming — the infrastructure that Colorado Springs families use every weekend and after school. The comment window may be closed, but the plan's adoption process is still underway, and there are still ways to stay engaged.

Here's a look at what the plan covers, why it matters for families, and what it means in practice — using the newly opened Mary Starsmore Discovery Plaza as a concrete example of what these plans can produce.

What the Plan Covers

The 2026 Park System Master Plan is a comprehensive document guiding how Colorado Springs invests in and manages its parks system. According to the city's page at coloradosprings.gov/ParkSystemMasterPlan, the plan addresses:

  • Parks and neighborhood green spaces
  • Trail systems and connectivity between parks and neighborhoods
  • Open spaces and natural areas
  • Community recreation centers
  • Programming and recreational opportunities
  • Long-term sustainability and maintenance

The city also maintains a TOPS (Trails, Open Space and Parks) program that funds specific improvements. The 2026 TOPS priorities page at coloradosprings.gov/TOPS2026 lists projects currently in the pipeline, which gives families a more immediate look at what's coming in their neighborhoods.

Mary Starsmore Discovery Plaza: A Before-and-After Story

Abstract plans become real when you can point to an example. The Mary Starsmore Discovery Plaza, which opened in June 2026 at the Starsmore Visitor and Nature Center in North Cheyenne Canon Park, is exactly that.

The project started about three years ago with an unglamorous problem: the parking lot at Starsmore didn't meet current ADA requirements. What grew out of that compliance project became a full site improvement: an outdoor plaza and open-air classroom, new ADA-accessible pedestrian pathways throughout the entrance area, renovated public restrooms, and enhanced parking. The outdoor classroom space is designed for both organized programs and informal learning, and the improved access makes the site genuinely usable for families with strollers, wheelchairs, and mixed-mobility groups.

It's a useful reminder that parks investment doesn't always start with a grand vision — sometimes it starts with fixing what's broken, and the result is something families can actually use better.

Why It Matters for Colorado Springs Families

Colorado Springs has a large park system relative to its population, but maintenance and improvement funding has historically been uneven across neighborhoods. Master plans like this one provide the framework for prioritizing where investment goes — ideally based on community input, equity considerations, and condition assessments rather than political pressure or whoever happens to show up at a meeting.

The fact that the public comment period just closed is actually a good sign: it means community input was collected and will inform the final plan. Parks plans that skip public input tend to produce results that feel disconnected from how people actually use the space.

How to Stay Engaged

Even though the September 2–22 public comment period has ended, the adoption process continues. The plan still goes through city staff review, planning commission consideration, and ultimately City Council adoption — each of which involves opportunities for public testimony.

To track progress:

  • Bookmark coloradosprings.gov/ParkSystemMasterPlan for updates
  • Sign up for Parks, Recreation and Cultural Services newsletters at coloradosprings.gov
  • Attend City Council meetings when the plan comes to a vote — the city posts agendas at least 72 hours in advance

What's Next

The 2026 Park System Master Plan is expected to move through staff review and into public hearings over the coming months. Once adopted, it will guide parks spending for several years, making it one of the more consequential local decisions for families who rely on COS parks, trails, and recreation centers.

For families who missed the comment window, it's worth checking whether your neighborhood's parks showed up in the draft plan — and if they didn't, noting that when the adoption hearings open. City council members' offices are also an accessible channel for families who want to advocate for specific improvements.

The full plan and supporting documents are available at coloradosprings.gov/ParkSystemMasterPlan.
