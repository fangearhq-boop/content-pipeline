Seven Baby Product Lines Flagged by CPSC for Suffocation and Safety Risks

By Jamie Rivera October 3, 2026

The CPSC Warnings

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission has identified multiple baby products currently being sold that violate federal safe sleep law or pose documented safety risks to infants. The flagged products span three major retail platforms — Amazon, TikTok Shop, and SHEIN — and include padded crib bumpers, infant bouncers, and baby loungers.

Seven product lines are cited for violating the Safe Sleep for Babies Act, a federal law enacted in 2022 that bans the manufacture and sale of padded crib bumpers in the United States. The law exists because padded bumpers placed inside cribs have been linked to infant suffocation deaths. The ban applies to all padded bumpers regardless of how they're marketed or labeled.

Additional Flagged Products: Bouncers and Loungers

Beyond crib bumpers, the CPSC also flagged two additional product categories for separate safety concerns.

Flyboss Infant Bouncers were flagged for collapse and impact risks. The concern is structural — the bouncer's frame can give way while a baby is in it, potentially causing the infant to fall or be struck by collapsing parts. Parents who own Flyboss bouncers should stop using them and check cpsc.gov for the specific model numbers involved.

CooCooBaby Baby Loungers were flagged for suffocation and fall risks. Inclined or cushioned infant loungers have been under regulatory scrutiny for several years because babies can roll or shift into positions that compromise their airway, and the soft, padded surfaces can increase suffocation risk during unsupervised use.

Safe Sleep Basics

The American Academy of Pediatrics' safe sleep guidance is straightforward: infants should sleep on a firm, flat surface with a fitted sheet and nothing else in the sleep space. No bumpers, no pillows, no positioners, no soft toys, no blankets for young infants.

The rationale for each rule is the same — soft objects and loose items in the sleep environment increase the risk of suffocation, entrapment, and sudden infant death syndrome. Padded crib bumpers were originally marketed as protection against babies bumping their heads on crib rails, but research found they created a suffocation hazard that outweighed any benefit. That finding led directly to the federal ban.

Many parents receive baby products as gifts or purchase them secondhand without realizing that some products have been recalled or banned since they were manufactured. It's worth periodically checking the CPSC recall database at cpsc.gov/Recalls to verify that the products in your home are current and safe.

What's Next

If you have any of the products listed above, the immediate step is to remove them from use and check cpsc.gov for guidance on refunds, returns, or disposal. Retailers are required to cooperate with CPSC enforcement actions, so contacting the seller directly is a reasonable starting point.

For safe sleep questions, your pediatrician or the AAP's HealthyChildren.org resource are the best sources of current guidance. The CPSC also maintains a subscription service at cpsc.gov that sends recall and safety warning notifications directly to your inbox — worth setting up if you haven't already.
