Colorado Springs Parks Master Plan Heads to City Council; Seven New Blodgett Miles Open

By Jamie Rivera October 3, 2026

New Trails Open at Blodgett Open Space

Families looking for fall hiking options in Colorado Springs now have more ground to cover. Blodgett Open Space, located on the north side of the city, opened seven new miles of trails in 2026, bringing its total trail network to 14 miles.

The expansion also includes a new Quarry/North trailhead with 90 parking stalls — a meaningful addition for anyone who's arrived at a popular Colorado Springs trailhead on a weekend morning to find the lot full. The new parking area expands access to the northern sections of the open space and should help distribute visitor volume more evenly across the trail system.

Additionally, a portion of the new trail miles is designated mountain bike-only, creating a dedicated experience for cycling families who've been working with limited options at Blodgett. The combined effect of new trail miles, better parking infrastructure, and dedicated bike trails makes this the most significant access improvement at Blodgett in several years.

The Park System Master Plan

The Blodgett improvements are happening alongside a larger planning process. The City of Colorado Springs' updated Park System Master Plan is advancing to City Council for review this October. The plan covers 2026 through 2036 and will guide where the city invests in parks, trails, and open spaces over the next decade.

Key themes in the draft plan include trail connectivity between neighborhoods and existing open spaces, equitable access to parks across all parts of the city, and a funding approach that addresses deferred maintenance across the existing park system. The plan also considers how Colorado Springs' population growth affects demand for recreational space and how the city can keep up.

The Blodgett expansion reflects exactly the kind of capital project the master plan is designed to prioritize — adding trail miles, improving trailhead infrastructure, and making open spaces more accessible to more residents. As City Council reviews the plan this month, families have an opportunity to weigh in on what recreational investments matter most for the next decade.

Planning a Blodgett Visit

Blodgett Open Space is free to access and open year-round, weather permitting. The new Quarry/North Trailhead is the best starting point for accessing the recently opened miles and the mountain bike-only sections. Trail difficulty ranges from easy to moderate, with some areas more challenging for younger children — checking the current trail map on the Colorado Springs parks website before heading out is a good idea.

October is one of the best months to hike in Colorado Springs. The fall color on the scrub oak and the cooler temperatures make for comfortable conditions, and views of Pikes Peak from the Blodgett trail system are a strong reward for the effort. Dogs are allowed on leash on most Blodgett trails.

What's Next

The Park System Master Plan is expected to come before City Council for review at some point this October. If you want to follow the process or provide public input, check coloradosprings.gov/parks for meeting dates and comment opportunities. The final plan, once adopted, will shape where the city builds new trails and parks through 2036.

For current Blodgett trail maps, trailhead locations, and conditions, visit coloradosprings.gov/parks or trails2000.org, the local nonprofit that advocates for trail access in the Pikes Peak region.
