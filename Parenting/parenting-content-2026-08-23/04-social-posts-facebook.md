### STORY 1: Screen Time & Mental Health Study

#### Long-Form Post

A new 2026 study of more than 50,000 American children and adolescents has some findings worth sitting with this back-to-school season. 📱

Researchers found that kids who spend 4 or more hours a day on screens face a 45% higher risk of anxiety and a 61% higher risk of depression compared to those with lower screen time. The most harmful pattern? Passive consumption — endless scrolling, watching without interacting — paired with social comparison on social media platforms.

Here's the part that caught our attention: the study also found that kids with under two hours of daily screen time AND strong social support had the lowest rates of depression and the highest life satisfaction. It's not just about cutting back — it's about what fills that space instead.

With all three COS school districts back in session and the "wind-down on screens after school" routine in full swing at many households, this is a good week to take stock of how screen time is actually landing for your kids. Not in a guilt-spiral way — just a "let's see what's working" conversation.

If your child is struggling with mood or anxiety and you'd like support, Pikes Peak Mental Health Center is a local resource: 719-572-6100.

What does screen time look like in your house right now — and has it changed since school started? 👇

### STORY 3: Back-to-School Sleep Tips

#### Long-Form Post

Here's something most COS parents are quietly experiencing right now: the 6 AM alarm that nobody asked for. 😴

D49 is into week 4. D11 is wrapping week 2. D20 just finished its first full week. And sleep schedules are still catching up to reality.

After a summer of later nights and lazy mornings, sleep debt builds fast — and the effects show up as crankiness, trouble focusing, and battles at bedtime that seem to get worse, not better. Here's what the sleep experts say:

Kids ages 6-12 need 9 to 12 hours per night. Teens ages 13-18 need 8 to 10. If your kid is getting less than that, they're running a deficit every single day.

A few things that actually help: shift bedtime 10 to 15 minutes earlier each night until you hit the target (going cold turkey to a much earlier bedtime rarely works). Build in a screen-free hour before bed — phones, tablets, and TV stimulate the brain in ways that fight sleep. Keep the weekend schedule within an hour of the weekday schedule, because sleeping in until noon on Saturday basically gives them jet lag by Monday morning.

The bed-is-for-sleep-only rule also matters more than it sounds: homework, reading, and especially screens in bed train the brain to associate the bedroom with being awake. A designated homework spot outside the bedroom makes a real difference.

What's the biggest sleep challenge in your house right now? We're all in it together. 👇

### STORY 4: Cheyenne Mountain Zoo Dog Days

#### Long-Form Post

Attention dog families: this Wednesday, August 26 is Dog Days at Cheyenne Mountain Zoo — and it's one of the very few days a year your pup gets to come along. 🐶

The Cheyenne Mountain Zoo sits at 6,800 feet above Colorado Springs (it's the only mountainside zoo in America), and most days it's a dog-free experience. But Dog Days is an annual exception — a special Wednesday when well-behaved, leashed dogs are welcome on the grounds alongside families.

A few things to know before you go: dogs must be current on vaccinations, kept on a leash at all times, and genuinely well-behaved around crowds, animals, and noise. It's worth being honest with yourself about whether your dog is a good fit for an environment like this — not every pup will enjoy it, and that's okay.

We'd strongly encourage checking cmzoo.org directly before Wednesday for the 2026 hours and ticket prices — we want to make sure you have confirmed details before making the trip.

If your dog is up for the adventure, this is a genuinely fun mid-week outing for the whole family. It's the kind of outing kids talk about for weeks.
