Back-to-School Sleep Tips for COS Families: D49, D11, and D20 Are All In Now

By Jamie Rivera | COS Parenting | August 23, 2026

As of this weekend, all three Colorado Springs school districts are fully in session. D49 started August 3-4 and is now into week four. D11 started August 12 and is wrapping week two. D20 started August 17-18 and just finished its first full week.

For many families, the early alarm is real but the sleep schedule hasn't fully caught up. Sleep debt builds quickly after a summer of later nights, and the effects compound across the school week in ways that show up as crankiness, difficulty concentrating, and bedtime resistance that somehow gets worse instead of better. Here's what pediatric and sleep medicine guidance says about addressing it.

How Much Sleep Do Kids Actually Need

The American Academy of Pediatrics and the Sleep Foundation align on the following recommended ranges:

Age Group Recommended Nightly Sleep
Ages 6–12 9–12 hours
Ages 13–18 8–10 hours

If your child needs to be up at 6:30 AM and they're not asleep until 11 PM, they're running a 1.5-to-3-hour deficit every single day — which compounds across a five-day school week in ways that are hard to shake with a single sleep-in on Saturday.

The Gradual Shift Approach

Pediatric sleep specialists consistently recommend against trying to correct a late summer schedule in one jump. Suddenly enforcing a 9 PM bedtime on a kid whose body is calibrated for midnight rarely works and often leads to conflict without the sleep benefit.

A more effective strategy: move bedtime and wake time 10 to 15 minutes earlier each day until you reach the target. This gives the body's circadian rhythm time to adjust without the shock of a large single shift. If your child was going to bed at 11 PM over the summer and needs to be asleep by 9 PM, a gradual shift takes roughly five to eight days to complete — which means starting now still gets you to a stable schedule within the next week to ten days.

The Pre-Bedtime Window

Screen-free time in the hour before bed is one of the most consistent recommendations across pediatric sleep research. Phones, tablets, gaming consoles, and television all emit light that signals the brain to stay alert, while the content itself — social feeds, games, even benign shows — keeps mental arousal elevated at exactly the wrong time.

For younger children in D49 or D11, the pre-bedtime routine might include a warm bath followed by quiet time and reading. For older students, even a 30-minute reading window before sleep has meaningful effects on sleep onset.

The bed-is-for-sleep-only principle is also worth enforcing where possible: homework, reading, and screens done in bed train the brain to associate the bedroom with wakefulness. A dedicated homework spot outside the bedroom — even just a kitchen table — makes it easier for the brain to switch into sleep mode when it's actually time.

Weekend Consistency Matters More Than It Sounds

One of the most common sleep sabotages for school-age children is the weekend schedule drift. Allowing kids to sleep two or three hours later on weekends essentially gives them weekly jet lag — by Monday morning, their internal clock is shifted again, and the week starts with a deficit before it even begins.

Pediatric sleep specialists recommend keeping the weekend schedule within an hour of the weekday schedule during the school year. That doesn't mean the schedule has to be rigid, but the drift should be small. A 7 AM weekday wake and an 8 AM weekend wake is manageable. A 7 AM weekday wake and a 10 AM weekend wake creates a consistent reset problem.

Signs Your Child Is Running a Sleep Deficit

Beyond the obvious tired-and-cranky signs, pediatric sleep specialists point to a few patterns worth watching: falling asleep in the car on short trips, difficulty waking in the morning despite adequate time in bed, mood swings that seem disproportionate to the situation, and trouble focusing on homework that would normally be easy. These aren't always behavioral issues — they're often sleep issues that look like behavioral issues.

What's Next

The first month of school is the most important window for establishing sleep patterns that hold through the year. For D20 families finishing week one, the timing is ideal — the structure is in place, the adjustment is underway, and small investments in sleep routine this week pay dividends in mood, focus, and overall health through October and beyond.

For families with concerns about persistent sleep difficulties or significant mood changes related to the school transition, El Paso County pediatricians and school counselors are available as resources. Pikes Peak Mental Health Center can also connect families with support at 719-572-6100.
