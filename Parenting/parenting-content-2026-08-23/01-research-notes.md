### STORY 1: Screen Time & Mental Health 2026 Study

**Source: medicaldaily.com, nature.com (Humanities and Social Sciences Communications), favormentalhealthservices.com**

- Study population: 50,231 American children and adolescents ages 6-17 (from 2026 National Survey of Children's Health)
- Published 2026 in Humanities and Social Sciences Communications
- Key finding: 4+ hours of daily screen time linked to:
  - 45% higher anxiety risk
  - 61% higher depression risk
  - Elevated conduct problems
- Passive consumption (scrolling, viewing without interaction) most harmful type
- Social comparison behaviors on social media → anxiety and low self-esteem
- Protective combination: <2 hours screen time + strong social support = lowest depression rates, highest life satisfaction
- Yale School of Medicine findings (March 2026): relationship is complex; varies by gender, social media type, anxiety levels, social support available
- Physical activity and sleep mediate effects (Nature study)
- Source confidence: **MEDIUM** (study confirmed from multiple sources; specific percentage figures from secondary reporting, not direct access to journal)

### STORY 3: Back-to-School Sleep Tips

**Source: sleepfoundation.org, thensf.org, akronchildrens.org/inside/2026/08, pediatricpartnerskc.com**

- Sleep needs by age (AAP/Sleep Foundation):
  - Ages 6-12: 9-12 hours per night
  - Ages 13-18: 8-10 hours per night
- Strategy: Shift bedtime/wake time 10-15 minutes per day over a week until school schedule reached
- Screen-free window: 1+ hour before bedtime (no TV, video games, phones)
- Consistency: Same schedule on weekends and weekdays
- Pre-bedtime routine: quiet time, warm bath, reading (younger children); reading (older children)
- Bed is for sleep only: homework/reading/screens should happen in designated other locations
- Source confidence: **HIGH** (multiple pediatric/sleep medicine sources; established clinical guidance)
