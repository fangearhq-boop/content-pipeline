### STORY 1: Screen Time & Mental Health 2026 Study — TIER 1

**Core Angle:** A large-scale 2026 study (50,231 American children and adolescents) published in Humanities and Social Sciences Communications found 4+ hours of daily screen time correlates with 45% higher anxiety risk and 61% higher depression risk. With all three COS school districts back in session, many families default to screen time as an after-school wind-down — this study challenges that habit with actionable data.

**Audience Hook:** COS parents who suspect their child's screen habits are affecting mood but haven't seen the numbers. The "protective combination" finding (<2 hours + strong social support) gives them something actionable rather than just alarming them.

**Why Today:** Summer screen habits don't auto-correct when school starts. First weeks back are exactly when families need to revisit rules. Timely and practical.

**Key Tension:** Study confirms harm from passive consumption/social comparison; but also shows relationship is complex (varies by gender, app type, social support). Don't catastrophize — frame as a prompt to audit habits.

**CTA/Resource:** Pikes Peak Mental Health Center 719-572-6100 for families who need support.

**Tier Rationale:** Tier 1 — national study with direct local relevance; strong data points; actionable; emotional resonance for parents.

### STORY 3: Back-to-School Sleep Tips — TIER 2

**Core Angle:** All three COS districts are now fully in session, but sleep debt accumulates fast after a summer of later bedtimes. Evidence-based tips: gradual 10-15 min/day schedule shift, screen-free hour before bed, weekend consistency. Framed around where each district is in its transition arc.

**Audience Hook:** Parents noticing cranky mornings, homework struggles, or kids who can't wind down. This offers structure without judgment.

**Why Today:** D20 just finished its first full week. Parents in that district especially are in the thick of adjustment. D11 week 2 wrapping; D49 fully settled but kids can regress.

**Tier Rationale:** Tier 2 — practical and timely but less breaking than S1/S2; strong service content.
