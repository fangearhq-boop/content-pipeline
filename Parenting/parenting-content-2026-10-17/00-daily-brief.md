### STORY 3: Recall Alert: Papablic Bedside Sleepers Pose Suffocation and Entrapment Risk
**Tier:** 2
**Type:** NEW (CPSC recall — different product from Oct 15 ONTHEWEI bath seat/Jocoevol dresser)
**Pillar:** National Parenting
**Byline:** Sarah Morales
**Status:** NEW — CPSC recall posted Oct 15, 2026

The CPSC recalled Papablic convertible bedside sleeper/play yards on October 15, 2026 — about 248 units sold on Amazon and through baby retailers. The mattress can shift and trap an infant, creating a suffocation hazard. The latch mechanism can also fail, causing the product to collapse. Remedy: stop use immediately, disassemble, cut mattress and fabric in half, email photos to support@papablic.com for a refund.

Sources: CPSC.gov (Papablic recall 2027-URL), consumeraffairs.com Oct 16 recall roundup

### STORY 4: Recall Alert: Bellabu Bear Children's Robes Fail Flammability Standards
**Tier:** 2
**Type:** NEW (CPSC recall — burn hazard, children's sleepwear)
**Pillar:** National Parenting
**Byline:** Jamie Rivera
**Status:** NEW — CPSC recall #26-013

About 1,300 Bellabu Bear bamboo sherpa robes for children were recalled because they don't meet the mandatory flammability standard for children's sleepwear. The robes (sizes S/M and M/L, sold at Saks Fifth Avenue and boutiques Jan 2024–Jul 2025, about $60) should be returned for a full refund. No injuries reported.

Sources: CPSC.gov (Bellabu Bear recall), DHSES NY, recalls.justia.com, bellabubear.com/pages/recall

### STORY 5: The AAP Changed Its Screen Time Advice — Here's What That Means for COS Families
**Tier:** 2
**Type:** NEW (National Parenting — AAP screen time guidelines 2026 update)
**Pillar:** National Parenting
**Byline:** Sarah Morales
**Status:** NEW — AAP 2026 update (Feb 2026)

In early 2026, the American Academy of Pediatrics shifted how it thinks about kids and screens — away from tracking minutes toward quality, context, and what families do together. The new 5 C's framework (Child, Content, Calm, Crowding Out, Communication) is designed to help parents think more practically. For ages 6+, the AAP recommends building limits together as a family using a Family Media Plan rather than setting arbitrary time caps.

Sources: EdSurge (Feb 2026 AAP update), Jellie's App screen time guidelines post (Feb 2026), AAP healthychildren.org
