### STORY 2: CPSC Slide Playset Warning — Toddler Death

**Long-Form Post** (Posting window: 9:00 AM MT)

This is the kind of safety warning that needs to reach every parent who has a colorful plastic playset in the backyard or living room. ⚠️

On August 13, the Consumer Product Safety Commission issued an urgent warning — not a standard recall, because the manufacturer refused to cooperate — about the Wenzhou Yidian Model WPX-4 indoor/outdoor slide playset. A 17-month-old child died after becoming entrapped in the unit's head and neck openings. The design flaw is serious: a toddler's body can fit through certain openings that the head cannot, creating an entrapment hazard.

The playset includes a tunnel, slide, and basketball hoop. Colors: red, navy, coffee, gray, green, and yellow. If you have one of these — and many were sold on Amazon and similar platforms — the CPSC's instruction is unambiguous: stop using it immediately and throw it away. Do not sell it. Do not give it away to another family.

Because the manufacturer hasn't agreed to a formal recall, there is no official return program. The CPSC cannot mandate a refund here. The only action available to families right now is disposal.

If you're not sure whether the playset you have matches, check the CPSC.gov safety warning for photos and confirm the model number before using it again.

Have you seen this playset in your neighborhood or a family member's home?

### STORY 3: Goody King Magnetic Cubes Recall

**Long-Form Post** (Posting window: 10:30 AM MT)

Amazon order check time, especially if you've bought building sets for kids in the last two and a half years. 🧲

The CPSC recalled approximately 213,500 Goody King Magnetic Building Cubes and Blocks — imported by Yi Suen Commerce — on August 13, 2026. Two children swallowed magnets and required surgery to remove them. There were 27 additional reports of blocks breaking apart and magnets becoming loose.

The sets were sold on Amazon from January 2024 through July 2026, in sizes ranging from 45 to 300 pieces, priced between $17 and $50. If you have one — or gifted one to a grandkid, cousin, or friend — this is worth a quick check.

Swallowed magnets are a particularly dangerous category of toy hazard. When two magnets are swallowed separately and attract to each other through the intestinal wall, they can cause perforations, blockages, twisting of the intestine, and blood poisoning. That's why these sets need to go back even if you haven't had an incident.

Contact Yi Suen Commerce for a prepaid return label and full refund. Don't toss them in the trash if you can return them — the refund is there.

### STORY 4: First-Week Survival Guide

**Long-Form Post** (Posting window: 3:30 PM MT)

Now that all three COS districts are in session, the first week officially starts. And if you've survived a few of these, you already know: the first week is its own category of exhausting. 😅

Here's what tends to work, especially when kids are adjusting to a new building or schedule:

Give them 20 minutes to decompress after school before jumping into homework or activities. It sounds small but it matters — kids process their day on the drive home or the first half hour back, and asking them to switch into homework mode immediately usually just leads to friction. Let them snack, stare at the ceiling, or tell you the one thing that happened today.

Keep dinner and bedtime predictable this week. Even if homework runs long, even if the commute was rough — the evening rhythm is what signals the nervous system that tomorrow is manageable.

Watch for the kids who are transitioning buildings for the first time: kindergartners, 6th graders, and 9th graders are navigating entirely new social maps, not just new classrooms. Low-key check-ins ("What was the best part? What was the worst part?") are usually more effective than "How was school?"

First weeks set the tone for the whole year. A calm, predictable first week does more for school-year success than almost anything else.

### STORY 5: RSV Season Prep

**Long-Form Post** (Posting window: 5:30 PM MT)

RSV season is about 10 weeks away — and now that Colorado Springs kids are back in shared classrooms, it's worth thinking about now rather than in late October. 🏥

RSV (respiratory syncytial virus) season in Colorado typically runs from late October through January, with peak hospitalizations in December and January. It's one of the leading causes of hospitalization for children under 5, accounting for an estimated 58,000 to 80,000 pediatric hospitalizations in the U.S. each year.

The good news: there's now meaningful protection available for the most vulnerable kids. The AAP recommends nirsevimab (Beyfortus) or clesrovimab for infants under 8 months during RSV season. For mothers who prefer to protect their baby through maternal immunity, the Abrysvo vaccine during pregnancy is an alternative. Children 8–19 months who are at high risk — premature infants, kids with certain heart or lung conditions — should also talk to their pediatrician about RSV immunization.

The timing matters. You want protection in place before RSV season starts, not after the first wave of daycare or classroom exposure. Now that school is back in session, those exposure windows open faster than they did over summer.

Call your pediatrician this month. Ask specifically about nirsevimab eligibility. The earlier you have the conversation, the easier it is to get the timing right.
