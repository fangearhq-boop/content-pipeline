First-Week Survival Guide for COS Families: All Three Districts Are Now in Session

By Jamie Rivera | COS Parenting | August 18, 2026

As of today, all three Colorado Springs school districts are fully in session. D49 opened August 4, D11 opened August 12, and D20 completes its two-day staggered start this morning with grades 1–5, 7–8, and 10–12. The first week is a different kind of hard — not just for kids, but for the adults running the logistics around them. Here's what tends to make it more manageable.

Give Kids Time to Decompress Before Homework

The after-school window matters more than most families realize. Kids who go directly from the bus or carpool line into homework tend to produce lower-quality work and absorb more stress from the transition. A 20-to-30-minute buffer — a snack, some outdoor time, or just quiet — lets the nervous system downshift from school mode. The homework still gets done. It just goes better.

This is especially true for kids who are new to their building this year: kindergartners, 6th graders, and 9th graders who spent the whole day navigating a completely unfamiliar social map. They need more decompression time, not less.

Watch for First-Week Anxiety in Transition Kids

Not every kid will tell you directly that they're struggling with the transition. Some show it through appetite changes at dinner, unusual quiet during the commute home, or more irritability than usual in the evening. None of that is necessarily alarming in the first week — it's often just the cost of processing a new environment.

Low-key check-ins work better than open-ended "How was school?" questions, which many kids deflect with "fine." Try more specific prompts: "What was the best part?" "What was the worst part?" "Did anything surprise you today?" Those questions give kids a narrower target to aim at and tend to produce more actual conversation.

Lock In the Evening Routine Now

The routines you establish in week one tend to stick. That's both a caution and an opportunity. If dinner, homework, and bedtime are predictable and relatively calm this week, they're more likely to stay that way when things get hectic in October and November. If they're chaotic from the start, chaos becomes the baseline.

The AAP recommends consistent sleep schedules for school-age children — meaning bedtime and wake time should stay roughly the same, including weekends, to support the sleep quality that affects everything from attention span to emotional regulation. Getting that right in the first week sets the foundation for the whole year.

Morning Prep the Night Before

The single most effective morning-stress reducer is packing lunches, signing folders, and laying out clothes the night before. It sounds obvious until you're standing in the kitchen at 7:18 AM trying to find the permission slip that was due last Friday. A 10-minute prep routine at 9 PM saves 30 minutes of chaos the next morning.

For families with multiple kids in multiple districts — which is common in Colorado Springs, where D11, D20, and D49 boundaries overlap in many neighborhoods — different start times and different school calendars make this prep even more critical. Put everyone's schedule somewhere visible and keep it current.

What's Next

The first week is the hardest week. By week two, most families have found a rhythm. By week three, it mostly runs on autopilot. The goal right now isn't to have everything perfect — it's to survive with reasonable calm and build habits that will carry you through the year. Take the picture. Drink the coffee. You've done this before.
