Gov. Polis Among Six Governors Urging AAP to Make Early Math Part of Well-Child Visits

By Sarah Morales | July 28, 2026

Colorado Springs, CO

If you've been to a pediatric well-child visit in the past decade, there's a good chance your child's doctor asked about reading — whether you're reading aloud at home, what books are in the house, and whether your child is showing early literacy signs. That's not coincidence. The American Academy of Pediatrics has a program called Reach Out and Read that effectively made early literacy a standard part of routine pediatric care.

Now six governors, including Colorado's Jared Polis, want the AAP to do the same thing for math.

What the Governors Are Asking

On July 23, 2026, the six governors sent a formal letter to the AAP urging the organization to develop clinical guidance on early numeracy — guidance that would sit alongside the existing literacy recommendations and give pediatricians a framework for promoting math skills the same way they now promote reading.

The governors aren't asking doctors to become math tutors. Their specific proposal is that the AAP recommend math-based board games and card games as tools parents can use at home with young children to build early number sense. It's the same approach the literacy program uses: translate research into a concrete, practical recommendation that a pediatrician can deliver in a few minutes during a routine visit.

The other five governors who signed the letter are Daniel McKee of Rhode Island, Wes Moore of Maryland, Ned Lamont of Connecticut, Matt Meyer of Delaware, and Josh Stein of North Carolina. The effort is organized by the Collaborative for Student Success, a nonprofit focused on K–12 academic outcomes.

Why Math Skills at Kindergarten Entry Matter

The research behind this push is worth understanding, because it's more compelling than many parents realize. Studies on early childhood development consistently show that a child's math skills at kindergarten entry are among the strongest predictors of their long-term academic trajectory — in some studies, stronger predictors than early reading skills.

The concern is that early math often gets less intentional attention at home than early reading. Most parents know to read aloud to their children, to visit the library, to point out words in the environment. The parallel practices for math — counting objects during play, comparing quantities, using numbers in conversation — are just as valuable but less commonly named as a parenting priority.

Closing the gap requires reaching parents when they're paying closest attention to their child's development. A pediatrician's office is one of the most reliable touch points for families across all income levels and educational backgrounds.

What COS Families Can Do Now

No AAP guidance change is imminent — the letter is a request, and any formal recommendation would go through the AAP's policy development process before becoming clinical guidance. But the research that underlies the governors' request is already well-established, which means families don't need to wait for a policy change to start.

The types of games and activities that build early numeracy are ordinary household items: card games that involve number recognition (War, Go Fish with number cards), board games with dice and counting (Chutes and Ladders, Trouble), and simple comparison activities during everyday routines — which pile has more grapes, how many steps to the door, which container holds more water. These activities build the same number sense that formal math education later depends on.

At your child's next well-child visit, it's worth asking the pediatrician what early math resources they recommend. Even before any new AAP guidance, many pediatricians are aware of the research and can point families toward age-appropriate activities.

What's Next

The AAP will review the governors' letter as part of its ongoing policy development process. No timeline has been announced. The Collaborative for Student Success is continuing to advocate for early numeracy guidance alongside the existing literacy framework. For COS families, the practical takeaway is available now: simple number games and counting activities at home build the same skills that the governors are asking the AAP to promote.
