### STORY 3: High Chair Hazards

#### Long-Form Post — 12:00 PM MT

Two high chair products are on the radar from CPSC this week, and both involve hazards serious enough to warrant immediate action.

The first is a recall. Bicystar Children's High Chairs, model TB-HC6900, has been recalled under CPSC Recall No. 26-290. About 150 units were sold exclusively on Amazon in September 2025. You'd recognize it as a gray chair with a metal frame and a cushioned seat cover. There are two distinct hazards: the chair has no attached crotch restraint, which creates a fall risk, and there's a head entrapment gap between the seat and the tray that presents a fatal hazard. Stop using it immediately and contact the seller for a full refund.

The second situation is a CPSC safety warning — not a traditional recall, because the manufacturer, Ningbo Yibai Children Products Co. Ltd. based in China, refused CPSC's request to recall. Babimoni Multifunctional High Chairs, model YB-H1234, were sold on Amazon from March 2024 through July 2024. About 350 units are in circulation. The issues include tipping, unattached restraints, tray disengagement, lock failures, and a head entrapment gap. If you have one, stop using it. There's currently no established refund process because of the manufacturer's refusal to cooperate.

Head entrapment between a chair seat and tray is a fatal hazard — an infant or toddler's head can become wedged in a gap too small for adults to recognize as dangerous. If you're unsure whether a high chair in your home has a similar gap, check whether you can fit two adult fingers between the seat back and tray edge.

### STORY 4: Governors AAP Early Math

#### Long-Form Post — 3:00 PM MT

Most parents know that pediatricians ask about reading at well-child visits — that's partly because the American Academy of Pediatrics has a program called "Reach Out and Read" that effectively made literacy promotion a standard part of pediatric care. Now six governors, including Colorado's Jared Polis, are asking the AAP to do the same thing for math.

The letter, sent July 23, asks the AAP to develop clinical guidance encouraging early numeracy the same way the existing literacy guidelines work. The governors' specific ask: recommend math-based board games and card games as tools parents can use at home to build number sense in young children. The request is organized by the Collaborative for Student Success, a group that focuses on K–12 academic outcomes.

The research behind this push is solid. Studies consistently show that a child's math skills at kindergarten entry are one of the strongest predictors of their long-term academic outcomes — stronger, in some studies, than early reading skills. The gap between kids who arrive at kindergarten with number sense and those who don't is hard to close later.

The practical takeaway right now, before any AAP guidance changes: simple games you probably already own — dice games, card games like War or Go Fish with number recognition, counting games with board pieces — build exactly the kind of number sense researchers are describing. You don't need to wait for a policy change to start.

### STORY 5: Burn Scar Flash Flood Risk

#### Long-Form Post — 5:00 PM MT

Good news and a new concern from the Aspen Acres Fire this week. The good news: evacuation orders have been lifted across the affected communities — Wetmore, Beulah, Rye, San Isabel, and Colorado City — and the fire is now 61% contained at approximately 101,921 acres. It's the 9th largest wildfire in Colorado history, and it's moving in the right direction.

The concern: burn scars don't behave like normal ground. Soil that's been burned loses its ability to absorb water — it repels rain the way a waxy surface would. During monsoon season, which has arrived in southern Colorado, afternoon thunderstorms can drop enough rain on these areas to trigger flash flooding and debris flows with almost no warning. CDOT has already issued alerts warning drivers of flash flooding, mudslides, and reduced visibility on state highways near burn scar zones south of Colorado Springs.

What this means for families: if you have weekend outdoor plans in the areas south of COS — hiking, camping, or driving through canyon roads near Beulah, Colorado City, or Rye — keep a close eye on afternoon weather. Flash floods in burn scar areas don't require rain to be falling directly above you; a storm miles uphill can send water and debris down a canyon in minutes.

Afternoon thunderstorms during monsoon season are fairly predictable: they build late morning, hit hardest between noon and 4:00 p.m., and can move quickly. If you're heading out early and coming home before noon, the risk is lower. If you're planning afternoon or all-day activities in these areas, have a weather app open and a plan to move to higher ground quickly.
