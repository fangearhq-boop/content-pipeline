CPSC Issues Safety Warning on bblüv Grow High Chair — Stop Use Immediately
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post If you own the bblüv Grow high chair, the CPSC wants you to stop using it right now. On October 15, 2026, the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission issued Warning #27-031 against the bblüv Grow high chair. The hazard is specific: the restraint system was never assembled during manufacturing. That means the straps meant to keep your child secured in the seat weren't functional — creating serious fall and entrapment risks for any child using the chair. Here's the part that matters: this isn't a typical recall. bblüv Group declined to cooperate with a corrective action plan, so CPSC issued a unilateral warning instead. No injuries had been confirmed at the time of the warning, but that's exactly why the CPSC acts before someone gets hurt. The directive from CPSC is clear: stop using it, don't sell it, don't give it away, and dispose of it. If you purchased the chair from a third-party online seller (it was sold through various online platforms), you may want to contact your retailer about a refund, and you can check cpsc.gov for any remedy updates. If you know anyone who has this high chair — especially new parents, grandparents, or caregivers — share this post. Apple and AAP Team Up on Free Screen Time Guide for Families
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post If you've ever looked at your kid's screen time report and just felt defeated — Apple and the American Academy of Pediatrics have something for you. On September 29, 2026, they released a free joint guide: "Building Healthy Digital Habits: A Guide for Families." It's six practical steps that walk parents through the actual tools inside iOS 27, informed by the AAP's current guidance on kids and screens. Here's the short version: 1. Start a conversation about screen time — before the devices, before the rules. 2. Set up a Child Account, which gives your child age-appropriate access and keeps things within your control. 3. Use Ask to Buy and Ask to Browse to approve apps and websites before kids access them. 4. Build a device schedule — daily time allowances and specific off times (homework, dinner, sleep). 5. Manage communication — decide who your child can call, message, and FaceTime. 6. Revisit the plan as your child grows — what works at age 8 doesn't work at age 13. The AAP dropped the old two-hour limit for school-age children in early 2026. Their current focus is on quality over quantity — is the content educational, is a parent nearby, is it replacing sleep or exercise? The guide weaves that thinking into every step. It's free, it's practical, and it lives at apple.com. Worth spending 15 minutes with this week. What's your family's screen time situation these days? --- ### Image Post Caption Apple and the AAP released a free 6-step guide for managing kids' screen time — Child Accounts, device schedules, communication limits, and more. Halloween Night Safety — Last-Week Checklist for COS Families
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Halloween is six days away, which means it's time for the safety prep checklist. The numbers matter here: child pedestrian fatality risk is statistically about 43% higher on Halloween night than on comparable fall evenings, according to Safe Kids Worldwide. It's not about stranger danger — that's largely a myth. It's about the combination of dark streets, excited kids, distracted drivers, and costumes that aren't designed with visibility in mind. A few things that actually help: Reflective tape on costumes goes a long way. A strip on a bag, a jacket, a shoe — it doesn't have to be visible in daylight to matter in the dark. Flashlights and glow sticks serve double duty: they help drivers see your kids, and they help your kids see. Grab them before you go. Skip the full-face masks. If your child's costume includes one, consider swapping it for face paint. Full masks reduce peripheral vision and make it harder to see curbs, cars, and other hazards. CSPD recommends that children under 12 have adult supervision. The fun run of kids going on their own starts at 12 and up, depending on the neighborhood — use your judgment. Cross at crosswalks. This sounds obvious, but Halloween excitement means kids will dart into the street. Reinforce this before you leave the house. The Teal Pumpkin Project: if your family has food allergies, look for homes displaying a teal pumpkin — they're offering non-food treats. FARE (Food Allergy Research & Education) has a map at allergyfamilies.com. The ideal trick-or-treat window in Colorado Springs is roughly 5:30–8 PM. Wrapping up by 8 keeps the risk window manageable. What's your family's one non-negotiable Halloween safety rule? --- ### Image Post Caption Halloween night safety for COS families: reflective tape, flashlights, face paint over full masks, and kids under 12 with adult supervision.
