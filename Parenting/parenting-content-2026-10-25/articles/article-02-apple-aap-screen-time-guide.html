Apple and the AAP Released a Free Screen Time Guide for Families

By Jamie Rivera | COS Parenting | October 25, 2026

On September 29, 2026, Apple and the American Academy of Pediatrics released a joint guide called "Building Healthy Digital Habits: A Guide for Families." It's free, it's practical, and it walks parents through six concrete steps for managing how their children use devices — using the tools already built into iOS 27.

This isn't the first time a tech company has paired with a medical organization on screen time guidance. But the combination of Apple's reach and the AAP's credibility makes this one worth a closer look, especially because it reflects the AAP's updated thinking from early 2026 — thinking that moved away from rigid hour-count limits toward something more nuanced.

The Six Steps

Step 1: Start the Conversation

Before adjusting any settings, the guide recommends talking with your child about how and why you're setting digital boundaries. Kids who understand the reasoning are more likely to cooperate — and more likely to internalize healthy habits as they grow.

Step 2: Set Up a Child Account

Apple's Child Account feature gives parents centralized control over what a child can access, download, and spend money on. The account applies age-appropriate defaults and allows parental visibility into activity without requiring constant monitoring.

Step 3: Set Website and App Safety

Ask to Buy and Ask to Browse are the two key features here. Ask to Buy requires parental approval before a child can purchase or download anything. Ask to Browse applies the same approval gate to websites. Both can be adjusted as children demonstrate responsibility over time.

Step 4: Build a Device Schedule

Screen Time's Downtime and App Limits features let parents set specific hours when devices are off-limits and cap daily usage on specific app categories. The guide suggests building a schedule that protects homework time, family meals, and the hour before bed — recommendations consistent with AAP guidance.

Step 5: Manage Communication

Parents can control who a child can call, message, and FaceTime. For younger children especially, limiting communication to known contacts is a meaningful safety layer.

Step 6: Revisit the Plan as Kids Grow

What works at age seven won't necessarily work at age twelve. The guide emphasizes that screen time boundaries should evolve with your child's age, maturity, and demonstrated responsibility. Building in a regular check-in — even annually — keeps the plan relevant.

The AAP's Evolving Guidance

In February 2026, the AAP updated its screen time guidance for the first time in roughly a decade. The update dropped the two-hour daily limit for school-age children and replaced it with the 5 Cs framework: Content, Context, Child, Connections, and Choices. The shift was a recognition that counting minutes tells parents very little about whether screen time is beneficial or harmful.

What hasn't changed: the AAP still recommends avoiding screens for children under 18 to 24 months (except video calling with family), avoiding screens in the hour before bedtime, and keeping screens out of children's bedrooms.

The Apple and AAP guide builds directly on this updated thinking, emphasizing quality and conversation over strict time limits.

The Free Family Media Plan

The AAP's HealthyChildren.org has offered a free Family Media Plan tool since 2016. It allows families to build a customized set of screen time rules and values, and it has been updated to reflect the 2026 guidance. It's worth completing even if you also use Apple's built-in tools — it prompts conversations that settings menus don't.

What's Next

The "Building Healthy Digital Habits" guide is available free at apple.com. The AAP's Family Media Plan is at HealthyChildren.org. Both are worth reviewing together — the Apple guide gives you the tools, and the Family Media Plan gives you the framework for the conversation behind them.

For families in Colorado Springs who already went through fall break this week, the Monday return to school is a natural reset point — a good time to revisit whatever screen time agreements you had in place before break and update them as needed.
