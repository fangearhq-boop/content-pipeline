Halloween Night Safety — The Week-Before Checklist for COS Families

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting | October 25, 2026

Halloween is six days away. The costumes are picked, the route is loosely planned, and the candy bags are ready. What's left is a five-minute safety check that most families skip — and probably shouldn't.

Child pedestrian fatality risk is statistically about 43% higher on Halloween night than on comparable fall evenings, according to Safe Kids Worldwide. The risk isn't stranger danger — that's largely a myth, and actual candy tampering is extremely rare according to decades of FBI data. The real risk is cars and low-visibility conditions: dark streets, excited kids running between houses, and drivers who aren't always paying attention to the sidewalk.

Most of the checklist below takes under two minutes. Some of it is a conversation worth having before you leave the house.

Visibility — Make Your Kids Easier to See

Reflective Tape

A strip of reflective tape on a costume, bag, or shoe is invisible in daylight and highly effective at night. It doesn't have to look like a safety vest — it just needs to be there. Dollar stores often carry reflective tape in October.

Flashlights and Glow Sticks

Beyond helping drivers see your kids, flashlights help your kids see — curbs, uneven pavement, and steps between houses are common trip hazards after dark. Glow sticks are a great option for younger kids who won't keep track of a flashlight.

Face Paint Instead of Full Masks

Full-face masks reduce peripheral vision and make it harder to see curbs, cars, and other hazards. The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends face paint as an alternative where the costume allows. If your child's costume requires a mask, make sure it has clear eye openings that don't limit side vision.

Supervision and Route

Kids Under 12

Colorado Springs Police Department recommends that children under 12 have adult supervision while trick-or-treating. For older kids going out in groups, know the route, know the group, and agree on a check-in time or place.

Cross at Crosswalks

Halloween excitement means kids will want to dash across the street mid-block. Reinforce before you leave: we only cross at corners or crosswalks. Say it twice. Say it while you're walking.

Stick to Familiar, Well-Lit Streets

Well-lit streets with sidewalks and active porch lights are the safest routes. Avoid streets with no sidewalks, high traffic, or poor lighting even if they have better candy. The neighborhoods you know are always the better choice.

Food Safety and Allergies

Teal Pumpkin Homes

The Teal Pumpkin Project, organized by FARE (Food Allergy Research and Education), encourages households to set out a teal pumpkin alongside their regular decorations to signal that they're offering non-food treats — small toys, glow sticks, stickers — for kids with food allergies. If your family manages food allergies, teal pumpkin homes are worth watching for. FARE's website at allergyfamilies.com has a map of participating homes.

Check Treats Before Kids Eat

The standard guidance: go through the bag before kids eat anything, set aside anything unwrapped or unfamiliar, and check for allergen information on labeled candy if your child has known allergies. Actual candy tampering is statistically rare, but a quick visual check is a reasonable habit.

COS Trick-or-Treat Timing

Most Colorado Springs neighborhoods run trick-or-treating from approximately 5:30 PM to 8 PM. Earlier is safer — better visibility, fewer cars, and kids are less tired. If your HOA or neighborhood association has set specific hours, those take priority. Wrapping up by 8 PM is a reasonable target for families with younger children.
