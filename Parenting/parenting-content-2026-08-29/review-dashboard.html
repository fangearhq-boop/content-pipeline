Kmaier Infant Walker Recalled — Fall Hazard (CPSC)
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Heads up, Colorado Springs parents — there's a baby product recall that needs your attention right now. The Consumer Product Safety Commission has recalled Kmaier infant walkers due to a deadly fall hazard. The recalled walkers (model RV001) can fit through a standard doorway and fail to stop at the edge of a step — meaning a baby in one of these walkers could roll right off a stair. About 243 of these walkers were sold on Target.com in June 2026, in pink, green, and white, for $62 to $70. The model number (RV001) is on a label located under the seat. The fix is straightforward: stop using the walker immediately, disassemble it, destroy it by cutting the fabric seat and writing "Recalled" in permanent marker on the tray, take a photo, and send that photo to TargetplusSJ@outlook.com to receive a full refund. If you bought one of these walkers for your baby — or if you've passed one on to a friend or family member who might have one — please share this post. Did you know infant walkers have been a long-standing safety concern? The American Academy of Pediatrics has recommended against them for years. Drop a comment if you have questions about this recall. ### Image Post Caption RECALL ALERT: Kmaier infant walkers (model RV001) are recalled due to a dangerous fall hazard — they can roll off steps. About 243 sold on Target.com in June 2026. Stop use and contact TargetplusSJ@outlook.com for a full refund. Share to spread the word.

HABA Rainbow Rattle Grasping and Teething Toy Recalled — Choking Hazard (CPSC)
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Here's a recall that's worth a second look, especially if you have a little one at home. HABA, the German wooden toy brand sold at specialty stores and retailers like Amazon, Target, and Nordstrom, has recalled its Rainbow Rattle Grasping and Teething Toy. The issue: a glued knot on the elastic cord can come untied, giving an infant access to small wooden and plastic pieces — a serious choking and ingestion hazard. About 2,000 of these toys were sold between December 2025 and July 2026 for around $13. The affected toy is item number 2012261001, batch code 0925. HABA has received two reports of the knot becoming untied, but no injuries have been reported so far. If you have this rattle, stop using it immediately and keep it away from your child. Contact HABA USA for a full refund: call 800-468-6873 (Monday through Friday, 9 a.m.–4 p.m. ET), email recall@habausa.com, or visit habausa.com/recalls. HABA toys are popular baby gifts — if you gave this to a new parent or received it at a shower in the last year, it's worth tracking down and passing along this information. Do you have a system for keeping up with toy recalls? Drop a comment — we're curious what other families do. ### Image Post Caption RECALL: HABA Rainbow Rattle Grasping & Teething Toy (item 2012261001) recalled due to choking hazard — elastic cord knot can untie, exposing small wooden pieces. ### Long-Form Post We're three weeks into the school year now — and for more than 14,000 Colorado Springs kids, they started it with everything they needed already in hand. Colorado Springs School District 11 partnered with Edukit this year to provide free, grade-specific school supply kits to every K-8 student in the district. The kits were delivered on the first day of school, August 12 — no shopping trips, no supply lists, no scrambling at the last minute. For families, this matters a lot. School supplies are one of those recurring costs that tends to sneak up on people — and in a district like D11, which serves a diverse, mixed-income population across Colorado Springs, the gap between "fully supplied" and "underprepared" can affect how a child feels walking into school on day one. The kits are grade-specific, which means students actually get what their teachers need them to have, not a generic assortment. The logistics behind delivering that at scale, for over 14,000 students, isn't simple — and D11 pulled it off.
