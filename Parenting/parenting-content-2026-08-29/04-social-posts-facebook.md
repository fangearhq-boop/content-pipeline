### STORY 1: Kmaier Infant Walker Recall

**Long-Form Post (8:00 AM MT)**

Heads up, Colorado Springs parents — there's a baby product recall that needs your attention right now.

The Consumer Product Safety Commission has recalled Kmaier infant walkers due to a deadly fall hazard. The recalled walkers (model RV001) can fit through a standard doorway and fail to stop at the edge of a step — meaning a baby in one of these walkers could roll right off a stair. About 243 of these walkers were sold on Target.com in June 2026, in pink, green, and white, for $62 to $70. The model number (RV001) is on a label located under the seat.

The fix is straightforward: stop using the walker immediately, disassemble it, destroy it by cutting the fabric seat and writing "Recalled" in permanent marker on the tray, take a photo, and send that photo to TargetplusSJ@outlook.com to receive a full refund.

If you bought one of these walkers for your baby — or if you've passed one on to a friend or family member who might have one — please share this post.

Did you know infant walkers have been a long-standing safety concern? The American Academy of Pediatrics has recommended against them for years. Drop a comment if you have questions about this recall.

### STORY 2: Little Loves & Co. Drawstring Recall

**Long-Form Post (10:00 AM MT)**

Another recall to put on your radar, Colorado Springs families.

The Consumer Product Safety Commission has announced a recall of approximately 590 Little Loves & Co. youth clothing sets. The hazard is a waist drawstring that can snag on objects — playground equipment, bus doors, fencing — and trap a child. This type of entrapment can cause serious injury. The clothing was sold on Littlelovesandco.com and Whatnot.com between May 2025 and July 2026, for $15 to $30.

If you bought clothing from Little Loves & Co. in that timeframe, check the label and stop letting your kids wear the recalled items. Contact the company directly for information on the remedy — refund or replacement, depending on availability.

Online marketplace shopping (especially via Whatnot.com) can be great for deals, but recall notices don't always reach buyers the way they do with major retailers. This is a reminder that it's worth double-checking any kids' clothing you've bought online recently.

### STORY 3: HABA Rainbow Rattle Recall

**Long-Form Post (12:00 PM MT)**

Here's a recall that's worth a second look, especially if you have a little one at home.

HABA, the German wooden toy brand sold at specialty stores and retailers like Amazon, Target, and Nordstrom, has recalled its Rainbow Rattle Grasping and Teething Toy. The issue: a glued knot on the elastic cord can come untied, giving an infant access to small wooden and plastic pieces — a serious choking and ingestion hazard. About 2,000 of these toys were sold between December 2025 and July 2026 for around $13.

The affected toy is item number 2012261001, batch code 0925. HABA has received two reports of the knot becoming untied, but no injuries have been reported so far.

If you have this rattle, stop using it immediately and keep it away from your child. Contact HABA USA for a full refund: call 800-468-6873 (Monday through Friday, 9 a.m.–4 p.m. ET), email recall@habausa.com, or visit habausa.com/recalls.

HABA toys are popular baby gifts — if you gave this to a new parent or received it at a shower in the last year, it's worth tracking down and passing along this information.

### STORY 4: BASE49 / After-School Programs

**Long-Form Post (3:00 PM MT)**

For District 49 families who are still figuring out after-school coverage, there's still time.

BASE49 — D49's official before- and after-school program — is still accepting registrations for the 2026-27 school year. The program runs at multiple elementary campuses across the district, from August 4 through May 26, 2027. It's designed specifically for working families who need reliable, supervised care during the hours before and after the school day.

If you've been meaning to look into it and just haven't gotten there, now's the time — spots at some campuses are limited, and enrollment tends to close out as the fall settles in.

D11 families also have options. Colorado Springs School District 11 runs after-school programs at a number of its campuses; details vary by school, so your best first call is to your student's principal or front office.

The first weeks of school are always a whirlwind, and childcare logistics don't always get sorted before the first bell. If you're still working it out, you're not alone — and resources are there.

### STORY 5: D11 Free School Supplies (Edukit)

**Long-Form Post (5:00 PM MT)**

We're three weeks into the school year now — and for more than 14,000 Colorado Springs kids, they started it with everything they needed already in hand.

Colorado Springs School District 11 partnered with Edukit this year to provide free, grade-specific school supply kits to every K-8 student in the district. The kits were delivered on the first day of school, August 12 — no shopping trips, no supply lists, no scrambling at the last minute.

For families, this matters a lot. School supplies are one of those recurring costs that tends to sneak up on people — and in a district like D11, which serves a diverse, mixed-income population across Colorado Springs, the gap between "fully supplied" and "underprepared" can affect how a child feels walking into school on day one.

The kits are grade-specific, which means students actually get what their teachers need them to have, not a generic assortment. The logistics behind delivering that at scale, for over 14,000 students, isn't simple — and D11 pulled it off.

Three weeks in, we're curious: for D11 families, how has the back-to-school season been going? Did the kits help?
