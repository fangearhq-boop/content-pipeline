## STORY 2: CPSC Baby Bath Seat Recalls
**Posting Time:** 10:00 AM MT

### Long-Form Post

If your family uses a baby bath seat purchased on Amazon in the past two years, check the model name now.

The Consumer Product Safety Commission has recalled two bath seat models — YCXXKJ and NFSVLB — due to drowning and entrapment hazards.

**YCXXKJ Baby Bath Seats** (sold by BenTalk on Amazon, May 2024–October 2025)
Approximately 9,000 units affected. Available in blue, gray, pink, and yellow. The seat comes with two detachable arms, four suction cups, and duck/turtle/whale bath toys. The problem: it's unstable and tips over, posing a drowning risk for infants.

**NFSVLB Baby Bath Seats** (sold on Amazon, May 2025–October 2025, $25–$40)
This model has leg openings that allow infants to slip through, creating both entrapment and drowning hazards.

What to do: Stop using either product immediately. Contact the seller through Amazon for a full refund.

Full recall details are available at cpsc.gov. If you're unsure whether your seat is one of the recalled models, check the product label against the CPSC recall listings before using it again.

Bath time safety reminder: The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends that a caregiver maintain constant, within-arm's-reach supervision whenever a child under 6 years old is near water — regardless of bath seats or other devices.

If you've checked and you're in the clear, it's worth sharing — a lot of families receive bath seats as shower gifts and may not know the model name.

## STORY 4: AAP 2026 Vaccine Schedule
**Posting Time:** 2:00 PM MT

### Long-Form Post

If you've been confused about which vaccine schedule to follow for your child in 2026, you're not alone.

The American Academy of Pediatrics released its 2026 immunization schedule recommending vaccines for 17 diseases. The CDC's current schedule covers fewer — it no longer includes recommendations for hepatitis A, hepatitis B, meningococcal disease, rotavirus, flu, and RSV (among others).

The gap between the two schedules has left many families — and some providers — uncertain about what's actually recommended.

Here's what Colorado families should know: The Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment (CDPHE) is backing the AAP schedule. So are 12 major national health organizations and 28 states. The AAP's schedule includes COVID-19 vaccination for children ages 6–23 months.

The practical takeaway: The AAP schedule is considered the more comprehensive option, and Colorado's own health authority recommends it. If your pediatrician is still going by the CDC schedule, it's worth asking why — and whether they plan to update.

For more information, visit healthychildren.org (the AAP's parent resource site) or talk to your child's doctor at the next well-child visit.

Is your pediatrician following the AAP or CDC schedule? It's worth asking at your next appointment.

## STORY 5: Cool Science Festival
**Posting Time:** 4:00 PM MT

### Long-Form Post

The Cool Science Festival returns to Colorado Springs for 2026 — and it's running for 16 days, Oct. 3–18.

Co-hosted by the Space Foundation Discovery Center, the festival brings dozens of free and low-cost STEAM events to venues across the city. Here's what families can look forward to:

**Carnival Day at UCCS — Saturday, Oct. 3, 10 AM–4 PM (FREE)**
The flagship event. Dozens of hands-on demos in rockets, slime, robots, biology, and more. Designed for K–8, but worth it for any curious kid or adult. This is the event to put on the calendar first.

**WMMI Gem and Mineral Panning — Oct. 3–18**
The Western Museum of Mining and Industry hosts gem and mineral panning throughout the full festival run. Drop in anytime during the festival dates.

**Science Riot at Ivywild School — Oct. 7**
A science comedy show where scientists share their research — quirks and all — in 10-minute presentations. Surprisingly accessible and fun for older kids and adults.

**Space Science Shows at USAFA — Oct. 8**
The U.S. Air Force Academy hosts space science programming during the festival. Check coolscience.org for ticketing and registration.

Most events are free. Full schedule and details at coolscience.org.
