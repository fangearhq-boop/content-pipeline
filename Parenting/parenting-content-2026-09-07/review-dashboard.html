NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Posting Window: 3:00 PM MT Tier: 2 Have you heard about weight-loss medications being prescribed for kids? Here's what the research actually says. When the American Academy of Pediatrics issued new guidelines in 2023 recommending medication alongside lifestyle changes for children with obesity, it triggered a significant shift in prescribing. According to studies published in 2025 and 2026: 📊 GLP-1 drug prescriptions for kids and teens jumped 65% immediately after the guidelines were released, then continued to grow another 5% per month. 📊 Between October 2022 and September 2024, total pediatric prescriptions increased sevenfold — from about 3,400 to over 24,000 per year. 📊 The drugs in question are the same ones making headlines in adults: semaglutide (Wegovy/Ozempic) and liraglutide (Saxenda). The research shows these drugs do improve blood sugar control and weight in obese children. But gastrointestinal side effects — nausea, vomiting, diarrhea — are significantly more common than with placebo. The good news: fewer than 10% of eligible kids are actually being prescribed these medications. This isn't widespread prescribing — it's a targeted tool for children with clinically diagnosed obesity. If your pediatrician brings this up at your next well-child visit, it's worth an honest conversation about whether it's appropriate for your child's situation. You're not obligated to say yes — and you're also not out of line for asking questions. What questions would you have for your pediatrician about this? ### Image Post Caption Posting Window: 3:00 PM MT Prescriptions for weight-loss drugs like Wegovy for kids jumped 65% after AAP guidelines — and sevenfold in two years. ### Long-Form Post Posting Window: 5:00 PM MT Tier: 2 Your teen might be talking to a chatbot about their mental health. According to an AAP survey, 1 in 5 adolescents and young adults has asked an AI chatbot — like ChatGPT — for mental health advice. That's not inherently bad news. Teens seeking information and support is a healthy impulse. But AI chatbots aren't therapists, and they can't replace the kind of connection and context a real person provides. For a teen dealing with social anxiety, depression, or stress about school — the stakes of bad advice are real. Back-to-school season is when teen anxiety tends to spike. New classes, social dynamics shifting, academic pressure ramping up — it's a lot. And many teens won't initiate the conversation with a parent or counselor. If AI is filling that gap, it's worth knowing. A few things parents can do: ✅ Ask open-ended questions at the end of school days ("What was the hardest part today?" not "How was school?") ✅ Normalize mental health conversations by talking about your own stress sometimes ✅ Know your district's counseling resources — D11, D20, and D49 all have school counselors; make sure your kid knows theirs by name ✅ If your teen won't talk to you, that's normal — but make sure they have *someone*: a coach, an aunt/uncle, a trusted adult Has your family talked about what to do when things feel overwhelming? ### Image Post Caption Posting Window: 5:00 PM MT 1 in 5 teens has asked an AI chatbot for mental health advice. ### Long-Form Post Posting Window: 10:00 AM MT Tier: 3 Happy Labor Day, COS parents. Tomorrow, school is back in session — and a three-day weekend has a way of sliding bedtimes, meal schedules, and morning routines sideways. Here's where things stand for local families: D49 starts week 6 on Tuesday, D11 starts week 4, and D20 starts week 3. The honeymoon phase of the new school year is well behind us at this point. What you set up tonight matters for how Tuesday morning goes. A few simple resets for tonight: 🌙 Bedtime: The AASM recommends 9-12 hours of sleep for ages 6-12 and 8-10 hours for teens. If the long weekend pushed bedtime back an hour or two, aim to close half that gap tonight. Don't try to force the full correction at once — gradual works better than sudden. 🍽 Prep tonight: Pack backpacks, sign permission slips, lay out tomorrow's clothes, prep anything you can for lunch. Tuesday morning chaos is mostly a Sunday night problem. 📱 Screens off earlier: If screens were allowed later over the weekend, start pulling them back tonight. Blue light disrupts melatonin, and tired kids (and parents) don't do their best work on Tuesdays. You've got this. Summer's over. Routine is actually good for kids — they just don't always know it yet. What's your reset ritual before a school week?
