Labor Day Routine Reset: How COS Families Can Get Back on Track Before Tuesday

By Jamie Rivera | September 7, 2026

D49 starts week 6, D11 starts week 4, and D20 starts week 3 on Tuesday. Here's how to use Sunday night to set everyone up for a smoother morning.

A three-day weekend is a wonderful thing, right up until Sunday night when you realize tomorrow is a school day and the bedtime has drifted 90 minutes later than usual. Labor Day is one of the most reliable routine disruptors of the fall school year. Here's how to recover it tonight.

Where COS Families Are in the School Year Right Now

It helps to know what week it is for your district, because the right interventions differ based on where families are in the routine-building process:

  • D49 (Falcon): Started August 3-4. Tuesday is week 6. Routines should be well-established by now — the focus is recovering what slipped over the long weekend.
  • D11 (Colorado Springs): Started August 12. Tuesday is week 4. Getting into the groove, but not fully locked in yet.
  • D20 (Academy): Started August 17-18. Tuesday is week 3. Still in the early stages of building the habit.

For D49 families, a Labor Day weekend disruption is a blip — you've been at this long enough to recover quickly. For D20 families still in week 3, it's worth taking the reset more seriously, because the routine habit isn't fully formed yet.

Tonight's Checklist

Bedtime: aim for halfway back. The American Academy of Sleep Medicine recommends 9-12 hours of sleep for ages 6-12 and 8-10 hours for teens. If bedtime drifted an hour or more over the weekend, don't try to force the full correction in one night — that creates resistance. Instead, aim for halfway back tonight, and the full correction Tuesday. Kids who are 60 minutes off bedtime can handle 30 minutes of correction without too much protest.

Screens off 60-90 minutes before bed. Blue light from phones and tablets suppresses melatonin. If screens ran later over the weekend, pull them back tonight — not for the week, just for tonight. The goal is a smoother morning, not an argument at 9 PM.

Pack the backpack tonight. Lunch, permission slips, library books, PE clothes, snacks — whatever needs to make it out the door Tuesday morning, put it in the bag tonight. This eliminates the most common source of chaos: the 7:45 AM scramble for things that were never located.

Lay out tomorrow's clothes. For younger kids especially, this removes one decision from a morning that will already have enough of them. For teens, even a quick "do you have everything for tomorrow?" conversation tonight goes a long way.

The Bigger Picture

Labor Day is a natural checkpoint. The summer flexibility is fully over. The school year's patterns — sports, activities, homework, family dinners — are all in motion. Families who feel like the routine is still chaotic at this point often find that small, consistent adjustments over the next two or three weeks settle things more than any single dramatic change. The goal isn't a perfect Tuesday morning. It's a slightly better one than last week, repeated until it becomes the norm.
