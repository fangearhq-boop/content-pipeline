September is the quiet high point of outdoor living in Colorado Springs. Average daily highs sit around 74 degrees. On average, there are only three rainy days in the entire month. The summer crowds have thinned at the popular parks. The trails are fully accessible, the light is different from any other time of year, and the temperatures stay mild enough for sustained outdoor time without summer's afternoon thunderstorm risk.

If your family wants to make the most of what Colorado Springs looks like before November changes everything, this is the month to do it.

Garden of the Gods Park

Garden of the Gods is free, open from sunrise to 10 PM, and never gets old. The paved main trail is accessible for strollers and children who are still building their trail stamina. The unpaved routes around the rock formations add challenge for older kids and parents who want more from the visit.

September mornings at Garden of the Gods — before 9 AM — are distinctly uncrowded compared to July and August. The same light that makes the red rocks photogenic in summer looks different (and arguably better) in early September's angle. If your family has been putting off a visit because of summer crowds, this is the window.

America the Beautiful Park

America the Beautiful Park, at Cimarron Street along Fountain Creek, has a free-access art installation called Quirks that is genuinely engaging for children of most ages. The installation includes hammocks, spinner bowls, and turntables built into an open green space. It is low-effort, high-return for families who want outdoor time without the commitment of a full trail hike.

Note that the splash area at the park typically closes by mid-September as temperatures drop. If your family wants the water features, earlier in September is better than later.

North Cheyenne Cañon Park

North Cheyenne Cañon Park is free and offers a range of trail difficulty from easy walks along the canyon floor to longer, more demanding routes. The waterfall visible from the Helen Hunt Falls Trail is one of the more accessible nature experiences in Colorado Springs for young children — it is a short walk from the visitor area and provides a clear payoff for the effort.

The park is more sheltered from wind than Garden of the Gods, which matters on September afternoons when the Front Range can pick up gusts.

Events Coming This Month

Two events worth planning around before September ends:

Dahlia Days at Gather Mountain Blooms (5210 S. U.S. 85/87) — today, September 12, and next Saturday, September 19 — runs 8 AM to noon. Admission is $10; cut flowers are $30 per cup. This is the last chance to pick dahlias from this field in 2026.

Pikes Peak Regional Airshow — September 19-20 at the Colorado Springs Airport. Gates open at 8:30 AM, aerial performances from 10:50 AM to 2:25 PM. Adult tickets start at $53.20; children at $42.18. Tickets are available online only at pprairshow.org and have sold out in previous years.

What's Next

Colorado Springs' outdoor window is real and it is measurable. By late October, most trail conditions are changed by first freezes, shorter days reduce usable afternoon hours, and the Pikes Peak region's weather becomes less predictable. September's mild average conditions make it the easiest month to simply go outside with your family. The parks are open, the trails are accessible, and most of the best options in Colorado Springs cost nothing to access.
