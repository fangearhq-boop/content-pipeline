Every school district in Colorado Springs is past the first month of the 2026-27 school year. D49 families have been in the new routine since August 3. D11 since August 12. D20 since August 17. By now, the first-day excitement has settled, the initial schedule is showing its cracks, and the question most families are quietly asking is: is this actually working?

The short answer is that mid-September is the right time to find out — and, if it's not working, to make small adjustments before October's parent-teacher conferences make those gaps visible to someone else.

The Sleep Schedule Is the Foundation

Pediatric sleep guidelines recommend 9 to 11 hours of sleep per night for children ages 6 to 13, and 8 to 10 hours for teenagers. By September, summer sleep schedules — which tend to run about an hour later than school-year schedules — should be fully corrected. If your child is still struggling to wake up on Monday mornings, the weekend sleep timing is the most likely culprit.

D11 families have a specific asset to use here: the district builds Monday late starts into its calendar. That means Monday mornings are structurally easier than other mornings for D11 students. If your child uses the Monday late start as license to sleep in an extra 90 minutes on Sunday night, the school-week fatigue may actually start on Tuesday rather than getting resolved by Friday.

A consistent wake time — even on weekends — is more effective than an earlier bedtime at stabilizing school-week energy.

After-School Decompression Time

The gap between "arrived home" and "doing homework" matters more than most families expect. Children leaving school after a six-to-seven hour day have spent significant cognitive energy. Jumping directly into homework typically produces worse work and more conflict than allowing even 20 to 30 minutes of unstructured decompression first — a snack, a walk, time outside, or low-demand screen time.

D49 families whose children started the earliest (August 3) are now almost six weeks into this daily rhythm. If the after-school routine is still producing friction, a 20-minute buffer built in before any homework expectations begin is often enough to reset the pattern.

October Is Coming

Parent-teacher conferences are a fall tradition across all three Colorado Springs districts, typically scheduled in October. The conversations that happen in those conferences are shaped by what has been happening in your home over the past six weeks. If a teacher mentions attention, completion rates, or energy in class — those indicators often trace back to sleep and after-school routine patterns established in September.

The routines families set now will be the data that teachers describe in October. A small adjustment in mid-September — an earlier bedtime, a consistent wake time, a decompression window before homework — is much easier to make than a larger intervention after a difficult conference.

What's Next

For Colorado Springs families in all three districts, the next natural check-in point is the week before fall break or the run-up to October conferences. If the current routine is working, stay the course. If it is not, the mid-September adjustment window is open now. D11 late-start Mondays are a consistent opportunity to reset the weekly rhythm. D49 and D20 families have slightly different calendar structures but the same underlying levers: sleep consistency, a decompression window, and reasonable homework pacing through the week.
