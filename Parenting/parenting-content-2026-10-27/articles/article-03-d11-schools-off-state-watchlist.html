No D11 High School on Colorado's State Watchlist for the First Time in About a Decade

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting | October 27, 2026

Colorado Springs School District 11 has reached a milestone that hasn't been true in roughly ten years: no D11 high school is currently on the state's accountability watchlist. Mitchell High School, which had been in the Priority Improvement category, has moved out of that designation. Ten schools across the district improved their performance ratings in the most recent state report, according to The Gazette's August 2026 coverage of Colorado's annual school accountability results.

How Colorado's School Accountability System Works

Colorado rates schools annually on a five-point scale, ranging from "Excellent" at the top down through "Performing," "Performance Watch," "Improvement," "Priority Improvement," and "Turnaround" at the bottom two levels. Schools that land in the bottom two categories for multiple consecutive years are placed on what the state calls the Accountability Clock — a process that can eventually lead to state intervention, including reconstitution or closure.

D11 high schools had been regularly appearing in those lower categories for years, drawing state scrutiny and creating pressure on the district to show improvement. The move off the watchlist is meaningful precisely because the system is designed to be hard to exit — it requires sustained performance gains, not just a single good year.

Mitchell High School's Progress

Mitchell High School's removal from Priority Improvement status is the most prominent individual school result in D11's current accountability picture. Mitchell serves a community on Colorado Springs' west side that has historically faced concentrated poverty and lower academic outcomes. The school's progress doesn't mean challenges are solved, but it reflects measurable gains in the data the state uses to track academic performance.

The change means Mitchell is no longer on a state-mandated improvement timeline at the most serious level. That has operational implications — it reduces the level of state intervention the school faces and gives the district and building more flexibility in how they approach improvement planning.

What the Broader D11 Picture Looks Like

Ten D11 schools improved their ratings in the most recent state report. That kind of broad movement across a district is harder to achieve than isolated gains at a single campus — it suggests something systemic is working, even if the district still has lower-performing schools that need continued attention.

D11 still has schools that aren't at the top of the state's scale. The accountability system will continue to track every building's performance annually, and the results will shift from year to year. But for D11 families who've been watching the district navigate persistent high school accountability pressure, the current status represents a genuine change in trajectory.

What's Next

Colorado's next round of school accountability ratings will be released in the fall of 2027, based on 2026-27 school year performance data. D11 families who want to review current school ratings can find them on the Colorado Department of Education's SchoolView website. COS Parenting will continue to track D11 performance news as the district prepares for its November 3 bond vote, which would fund building renovations and upgrades across the district.
