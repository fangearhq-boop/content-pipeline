CPSC Issues Safety Warning on Lucakuins Infant Bath Seats: Stop Use Immediately

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting | October 27, 2026

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission has issued a safety warning on Lucakuins infant bath seats, directing consumers to stop using the product immediately. The warning identifies drowning and entrapment as the primary hazards. Parents who have this product in their bathrooms should remove it from use now, regardless of whether their baby appears to have been injured while using it.

Warning vs. Recall: What's the Difference

A CPSC warning is different from a formal recall. In a recall, the company typically cooperates with the CPSC, agrees to a remedy, and distributes refund or replacement instructions. In a warning, the agency acts unilaterally — usually because the company has declined to participate in a voluntary recall process. The bblüv Grow high chair, flagged two weeks ago, followed the same path: the company declined cooperation, and the CPSC issued a warning directing consumers to dispose of the product immediately.

That distinction matters practically. When there's a recall, you generally get a refund process. When there's only a warning, the company may not offer any remedy. In those cases, CPSC's guidance is typically to stop use and dispose of the product, and any recovery may require pursuing the company directly.

What Parents Should Do

Stop using Lucakuins infant bath seats immediately. Set the product aside in a location where it can't be accessed by other adults or caregivers who might not know about the warning. Do not donate it, sell it, or pass it on to another family.

Visit CPSC.gov and search for "Lucakuins" to find the full warning text, which will include any available remedy information and the specific product identifiers. The warning number and recall details are the authoritative source for what steps to take and whether any compensation is available.

A Pattern Worth Paying Attention To

This is the fourth baby product safety action in approximately two weeks. ONTHEWEI baby bath seats were recalled on October 9 for tip-over instability and drowning risk. Papablic convertible bedside sleepers were recalled on October 15 for entrapment, suffocation, and fall hazards. The bblüv Grow high chair received a CPSC warning on October 15 after the company refused to cooperate with a voluntary recall. Now Lucakuins bath seats have drawn a warning for the same category of hazard as the ONTHEWEI seats.

Two of these four involve bath seats specifically, which should prompt parents to take a harder look at whatever bath seat they're using, even one that hasn't yet appeared on a CPSC list. The question to ask: does it have a stable base, secure attachment to the tub, and no points of entrapment for an infant's legs or body? If you're uncertain, CPSC.gov maintains a full searchable database of current recalls and warnings.
