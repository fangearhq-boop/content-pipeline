### STORY 2: CPSC Recalls — Play Yard Mattresses + HAUOMS Dressers

**Long-Form Post (10:00 AM MT)**

Two more CPSC recalls came through this week, and both affect items that sit in a lot of Colorado Springs homes.

The first is Mommy & Me Pack & Play mattresses, recalled for suffocation and entrapment hazards in play yards. Play yard mattresses are the kind of product families use from early infancy through toddlerhood, often for travel or as a secondary sleep space. If you have one, stop using it and check cpsc.gov/Recalls for the specific remedy.

The second is HAUOMS eight-drawer dressers — 1,392 units recalled for tip-over and child entrapment risk. Dresser tip-overs are one of the CPSC's highest-priority hazard categories because children can be entrapped underneath, and it happens fast. If you have this dresser, check whether your model is affected.

This is now the third week running with CPSC recalls affecting young children. If you haven't already, it's worth bookmarking cpsc.gov/Recalls — you can search by product category, and it's updated as new recalls are issued.

### STORY 3: D11 Bond Accountability Protest

**Long-Form Post (12:00 PM MT)**

There's been some community pushback on the D11 bond this week, and it's worth knowing about if you're a Colorado Springs family paying attention to where the $775M in approved bond money goes.

Protesters gathered outside D11 leadership around November 10 demanding accountability and transparency on bond spending. The district has proposed an independent citizen oversight committee to monitor how the money is allocated and spent — a structure that, in principle, gives the public a formal mechanism to track progress.

D11 has said an independent investigation found no wrongdoing. The matter is in litigation, and the district has declined further comment.

What families do know: the bond includes an $80M rebuild of Holmes Middle School and $24.18M in planning for MacLaren Elementary, among other projects. The oversight committee, if established, would be the main way the community gets visibility into those timelines and budgets.

### STORY 4: AAP 2026–27 Flu Guidance

**Long-Form Post (3:00 PM MT)**

The American Academy of Pediatrics has updated its flu guidance for the 2026–27 season, and there's one piece of it that many families miss every year: the two-dose rule.

If your child is between 6 months and 8 years old and is getting vaccinated for the first time this season — or only received one dose before July 1, 2026 — they need two doses this year, given at least four weeks apart. The first dose primes the immune system; the second builds the full protection. Getting only one dose leaves kids in that age group only partially covered.

For older kids and adults, one dose is what's recommended.

The urgency here is real. Last season saw 190 pediatric flu deaths nationally, and only 49.4% of children in that age group were vaccinated. Colorado's 2026–27 season has already recorded its first pediatric death — an elementary-aged, unvaccinated child. That's earlier than typical.

If your kids haven't been vaccinated yet, most pediatric offices and many pharmacies have the vaccine in stock now. Calling to confirm before you go in is worth the two minutes.

### STORY 5: Skate in the Park

**Long-Form Post (5:00 PM MT)**

The cold weather just got a little more fun.

Skate in the Park opened its 15th season at Acacia Park in downtown Colorado Springs last Friday (November 13), and it's running daily through January 2027 as part of the Acacia Park Holiday Celebration.

Here's what you need to know:

Admission is $15 per person and includes skate rental — no need to bring your own. Children ages 4 and under skate free when accompanied by a paid adult. If you're active-duty military or a first responder, you get $2 off.

It's a City of Colorado Springs Parks, Recreation & Cultural Services program, which means it's well-run and sticks around. The holiday atmosphere in downtown COS this time of year makes the whole outing feel like an event, not just an errand.

For families with younger kids who haven't tried ice skating yet, this is a pretty low-pressure way to try it — skate rental is included, the rink is managed, and the age-4-and-under free admission removes the financial sting of a short skate session with a toddler.
