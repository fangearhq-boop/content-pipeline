AAP's 2026–27 Flu Guidance: What Colorado Springs Families Need to Do Before December

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting | November 17, 2026

The American Academy of Pediatrics published its 2026–27 flu guidance in Pediatrics this fall, and there are two things Colorado Springs families need to know right now. One is a recommendation most families understand but don't always act on. The other is a rule many families miss — with real consequences.

The Two-Dose Rule for Young Children

If your child is between 6 months and 8 years old and is getting vaccinated for the first time this season — or only received one dose before July 1, 2026 — they need two doses this year. The doses need to be given at least four weeks apart.

This isn't a new rule, but it's one that pediatric providers report is frequently missed. The first dose of the flu vaccine primes a young immune system; the second builds the full protection. Getting only one dose leaves children in that age group only partially protected. If you aren't sure whether your child had one or two doses last season, check your records or call your pediatrician's office before scheduling this year's vaccine.

Children 9 and older only need one dose, as do adults.

Why This Season Matters More Than Last

Last season (2025–26) was difficult. There were 190 pediatric flu deaths nationally, and only 49.4% of children between 6 months and 17 years received a flu vaccine. Those two numbers together make the case for acting early.

Colorado's 2026–27 season has already recorded its first pediatric death — an elementary-aged, unvaccinated child — earlier in the season than typical. The AAP guidance is a recommendation; the Colorado data is a reason to treat it urgently.

What's New in the 2026–27 Vaccines

All 2026–27 U.S. flu vaccines are trivalent with updated compositions to match the strains circulating this season. Baloxavir — an antiviral medication used to treat flu — is now available as a generic, which may reduce cost if your child needs treatment after a confirmed flu diagnosis.

Most pediatric offices and many pharmacies have this season's vaccine in stock now. Calling ahead to confirm before you go in takes two minutes and can save a wasted trip.

What's Next

The AAP recommends getting vaccinated before flu season peaks — in most years, that means before the end of October, but vaccination remains beneficial any time during the season. November is not too late. If your family hasn't been vaccinated yet, this week is a good week to make the call.
