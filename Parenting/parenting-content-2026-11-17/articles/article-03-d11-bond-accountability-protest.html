D11 Community Members Demand Bond Accountability — What Parents Should Know

By Jamie Rivera | COS Parenting | November 17, 2026

Colorado Springs School District 11 families voted to approve a $775 million bond in the November 2026 election — roughly 51.68% yes — and the work of spending that money is now underway. This week, a group of community members gathered outside D11 leadership to demand that the process be transparent and that spending decisions be made publicly accountable.

What the Protest Is About

Protesters gathered around November 10, according to FOX21 News, calling on D11 to be clear about how bond funds will be allocated, what oversight exists, and how the community can track progress. The core concern: a $775 million commitment from taxpayers deserves a formal mechanism for public accountability, not just district updates issued on the district's own timeline.

D11 has responded by proposing an independent citizen oversight committee to monitor how bond money is spent. An independent investigation, the district says, found no wrongdoing in its current operations. The matter remains in litigation, and D11 declined to comment further citing that litigation.

What's Planned for the Bond Money

The major projects families should know about from the bond include:

  • Holmes Middle School: $80 million rebuild
  • MacLaren Elementary: $24.18 million for planning, with construction to follow
  • Additional school upgrades and repairs across D11

The citizen oversight committee, if the district moves forward with it, would be the main public-facing structure through which families can follow the money as these projects move from planning into execution.

What Parents Can Do

If you're a D11 family invested in how this bond is spent, the most direct path is engagement with the oversight process. Watch for D11 announcements about the composition and meeting schedule of the citizen oversight committee. D11 board meetings are public; agendas and minutes are posted on the district's website.

For families who voted yes on the bond, the oversight committee is worth paying attention to — it's the accountability structure that justifies the investment. For families who voted no, it may be the mechanism that changes the equation over time.

What's Next

The bond implementation is in its early stages. Expect more updates from D11 as the oversight committee structure is formalized and project timelines are set. COS Parenting will continue following bond developments as they affect families across D11 schools.
