CPSC Recall — Ricky Joy Sour Crush Candy Bottles (2.3 Million Units) (TIER 1)
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post **Window:** 8:00 AM MT Parents: the CPSC issued an urgent recall Thursday that affects a candy product your children may have in their backpacks, rooms, or school bags right now. The Ricky Joy Company recalled approximately 2.3 million Sour Crush Rolling Liquid Candy Bottles on September 10, 2026. The rolling ball on these bottles can detach and create a serious choking hazard for children. No injuries have been reported, but the CPSC urges consumers to act immediately. What to look for: the bottles come in blue (blue raspberry), red (strawberry), green (green apple), and pink (watermelon). They contain 1.85 oz of sour liquid and were sold individually or in packs of 3 or 12. They were available at H-E-B, Forman Mill, and online at Walmart.com, CandyFunHouse.com, and RickyJoy.com from January 2023 through May 2026, for about $3 each. What to do: Stop use immediately. Take the bottles away from children. Contact the Ricky Joy Company for a full refund — call 404-975-0352 (Monday through Friday, 8 AM to 5 PM ET) or visit rickyjoy.com and click "Recall Information" at the bottom of the page. This product type has a serious track record: a 2024 recall of Slime Licker rollerball candies covered 70 million bottles and was tied to one child death. Treat this recall with urgency. #COSParenting #ColoradoSprings #COSKids #PikesPeakParenting ---

RSV Season 2026-27 Is Starting — What Colorado Springs Families Need to Know (TIER 1)
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post **Window:** 10:00 AM MT September in Colorado means the RSV season is opening — and pediatric experts want families to act before it peaks. The American Academy of Pediatrics updated its 2026-27 RSV guidance, and the biggest changes affect which children qualify for protective immunization. For the 2026-27 season, all infants under 8 months entering their first RSV season should receive RSV immunization — either nirsevimab or clesrovimab, both monoclonal antibodies that reduce the risk of severe RSV illness. September is also the recommended window for pregnant parents to receive the RSV maternal vaccine so that their newborn is protected at birth. For children aged 8 to 19 months, the AAP expanded the high-risk criteria this season. Children in this age range who qualify for continued RSV immunization now include those born preterm at under 32 weeks, those with hemodynamically significant congenital heart disease, children with anatomic pulmonary abnormalities or neuromuscular disorders, and children with Down syndrome or other chromosomal differences. RSV hits hardest in infants and young children, particularly in the October through January window when seasonal activity peaks. Colorado Springs families with young infants or children in the expanded high-risk categories should reach out to their pediatrician now to discuss immunization timing and options before the season intensifies. If your child does not have a current pediatric provider, El Paso County Public Health (elpasoco.com/public-health) can connect families with local resources. ---

Fall Parent-Teacher Conferences Are Coming — How COS Families Can Prepare (TIER 2)
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post **Window:** 5:00 PM MT D49 families are heading into week 7 of the school year. D11 is in week 5. D20 is in week 4. And if patterns hold, fall parent-teacher conferences in Colorado Springs districts are coming up in October. That's 4 to 8 weeks away — close enough to start preparing now. Here is what families can do today to make the most of that time: Start a notes document. Jot down anything you've observed at home — changes in attitude toward school, shifts in sleep or appetite, specific subjects that feel hard or exciting. Teachers see dozens of students; your home observations are unique data they don't have. Email the teacher for an informal check-in before conference day. A brief "how is she/he doing?" message now often surfaces issues much earlier than the formal conference. Most teachers appreciate the proactive outreach. Pull together any relevant documents if your child has a 504 plan or IEP. Conference time is limited, and having documents in hand means you can get to the specific questions that matter. Prepare your questions in advance. A few good ones: Is my child meeting grade-level benchmarks? What does success look like in this class? Is there anything you'd like me to reinforce at home? Are there any social or behavioral patterns I should know about? For families with children who have received services or accommodations in previous years, confirm those are in place before the conference — not during it. ---

Finger Light Toys Recalled Over Battery Danger to Kids (TIER 2)
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post **Window:** 3:00 PM MT If your child has finger light toys purchased on Amazon, the CPSC issued a recall Thursday that requires your attention now. The Consumer Product Safety Commission recalled Cade California Electronic Finger Light Toys on September 10, 2026. These toys contain button cell batteries — the small, flat, coin-shaped batteries found in many small electronics — that can be easily accessed by children. If a child swallows a button cell battery, the battery can lodge in the throat and cause a rapid internal chemical reaction, resulting in severe burns or death. This is not a theoretical risk: button cell battery injuries in children are well-documented. What to do: Remove the batteries from these toys immediately. Do not let children play with the toys until the batteries are secured or the toys are discarded. For full recall details and refund instructions, visit cpsc.gov/Recalls. This is also a good moment to audit other toys and household items for accessible button cell batteries — remote controls, key fobs, musical greeting cards, small flashlights, and similar items are common sources. ---
