### STORY 1: CPSC Button Battery Recalls

#### Long-Form Post

If you've got LED tea lights or those fun Easter light-up headbands at home, take a quick look at them this morning.

CPSC just recalled two products — JINHEZO Mini Waterproof LED Tea Lights and OKK Trading Easter LED Headbands — because the button batteries inside can be accessed too easily by children. A swallowed button battery isn't a "wait and see" situation. It can cause severe, sometimes fatal, burns to the esophagus within hours.

These recalls are separate from the baby lounger recalls earlier this week. This is specifically about decorative LED products that are common in a lot of households.

Head to CPSC.gov, search for the product names, and follow the remedy instructions — that'll tell you whether it's a refund, return, or repair. If you think a child may have swallowed a battery, call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 or go to the nearest emergency room immediately.

### STORY 4: AAP Teens + AI Mental Health

#### Long-Form Post

With school back in session across Colorado Springs, here's something worth knowing.

A new AAP survey asked more than 1,000 adolescents and young adults between the ages of 12 and 21 where they turn for mental health support. Nearly 1 in 5 said they'd asked an AI chatbot — like ChatGPT — for help.

It's not surprising when you think about it. For a teenager who's anxious about starting a new school year, talking to an AI can feel lower-stakes than calling a counselor or even talking to a parent. It's available at midnight. It doesn't judge. It doesn't report back.

But AI chatbots have real limits when it comes to mental health. They can't pick up on signs of crisis the way a trained counselor can. They can give generic reassurance that feels supportive but doesn't actually help. And they're not a substitute for real connection.

The good news is that this doesn't have to be a scary conversation. Asking your teen "I read that a lot of kids are turning to AI when they're stressed — have you ever done that?" is a curiosity question, not an accusation. It opens a door rather than closing one.

If your teen in Colorado Springs ever needs more support, the Pikes Peak Mental Health Center is at 719-572-6100. School counselors are also a good first step.

Have you talked to your teen about how they handle stress this school year?
