COS Weekend: Switchbacks Tonight, Sunday Market Tomorrow, and Yes, It Might Hit 94

By Sarah Morales | August 22, 2026

Local Events

It's the last full weekend of August in Colorado Springs, and there's a lot worth doing — if you time it right with the heat. Here's your quick guide to what's happening and what to plan around.

Tonight: Switchbacks vs. El Paso Locomotive FC

The Colorado Springs Switchbacks take on El Paso Locomotive FC tonight at Weidner Field. Kickoff is at 7 PM, gates open at 6 PM, and tickets start at $30. It's a USL Championship match with genuine rivalry energy — El Paso and Colorado Springs have been going at it for years — and an evening kick in late August means the heat has typically broken by the time you're in your seat.

Weidner Field is a good family venue. The sightlines are solid from most sections, the concession options have improved over the past couple of years, and the atmosphere for a rivalry match gives even non-soccer families a fun outing. If your kids are at that age where they're starting to get interested in live sports, this is an accessible, affordable entry point.

Tickets are available at the Switchbacks' website. Parking fills up close to the field, so arriving at or just after the 6 PM gate opening is a smart move if you want a good spot.

Sunday Morning: Colorado Springs Sunday Market at Acacia Park

Sunday brings the Colorado Springs Sunday Market back to Acacia Park, running from 9 AM to 2 PM. It's free to attend. The market features local produce, artisan food vendors, handmade goods, and live music — and Acacia Park's downtown location makes it easy to tack on a walk or a coffee shop stop.

The Sunday Market is a genuinely nice way to start a Sunday with kids. It's low-pressure, the energy is relaxed, and the combination of fresh food, local makers, and outdoor music tends to hold attention in a way that doesn't require much planning. It's also one of those COS institutions that's easy to overlook until you go and remember why it's worth going.

Morning hours are cooler, and if Sunday is anything like Saturday's forecast, getting there by 9 AM or 10 AM is the right call before the afternoon heats up.

Weather Heads-Up: Possible Record High of 94 Degrees Today

Saturday is the one to watch. Colorado Springs could flirt with a record high of 94 degrees Fahrenheit — a possible tie or break of the daily record. Skies are mainly clear through the morning, but expect gusty winds in the afternoon. The storm threat is actually low for August — there's not a lot of atmospheric moisture around — so this isn't a severe weather day. It's just hot.

The practical guidance: outdoor activities with kids are better scheduled for the morning. Bear Creek Nature Center's 50th party (see our separate article) runs until 2 PM and gives you a morning-into-afternoon window that works well with the forecast. If you're planning anything that involves direct sun exposure in the afternoon, pack extra water, sunscreen, and a plan to find shade.

Overnight lows are expected around 53 degrees, so Sunday morning should feel comfortable before the day heats up again.

Also Worth Knowing: Rock Island Trail Extension

Construction has started on a 2-plus-mile extension of the Rock Island Trail between Sand Creek and Constitution Avenue. If you're a family that regularly uses Colorado Springs' trail network, this is good news for the long run — more connected trail infrastructure means more places to go without a car. In the short term, expect some disruption in that corridor while construction is underway. Check current conditions before heading out to that section of trail.

What's Next

The quick version of the weekend plan: if you're going to Bear Creek Nature Center's 50th this morning (and it's worth going), that handles Saturday daytime. Tonight, Switchbacks at 7 PM if your family likes live sports. Sunday, sleep in slightly, then hit the Sunday Market at Acacia Park by 9 or 10 AM before the day gets hot. August has one more weekend after this — make this one count before the full school-year rhythm locks in.
