### STORY 2: CPSC Toddler Tower Recall

#### Long-Form Post — 10:00 AM MT

If your family has a TOMY Boon PIVOT Collapsible Toddler Tower Kitchen Step Stool, stop using it today.

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission has issued a recall for 116,908 units due to a tip-over and fall hazard that can cause serious injury or death. The stools — sold at Amazon, Target, and Babylist from January 2023 through June 2026 for around $150 — can tip while a toddler is standing on them. Eleven incidents of instability have been reported.

What to do: Stop using it. Contact TOMY, who will provide a free stabilizing repair part. Once the repair is installed, the stool will be safe to use.

Here's the broader picture. This is not an isolated product issue. Since September 2025, 11 different toddler tower brands have been recalled — a total of 434,176 units across all brands, with 54 documented child injuries. Consumer Reports is now pushing the CPSC to finalize a mandatory safety standard for this entire product category. Until that standard exists, it's worth checking any learning tower or kitchen step stool you own against the CPSC recall list at cpsc.gov.

If you know a family with young children who might have this product, please share this post.

### STORY 3: D11 Bond Vote Tomorrow

#### Long-Form Post — 12:00 PM MT

The D11 Board of Education votes tomorrow evening on one of the biggest decisions for Colorado Springs schools in more than 20 years.

The vote — scheduled for Wednesday, August 5 at 5:30 PM — determines whether a $775 million bond measure will appear on the November general election ballot. The board doesn't approve the bond itself; they decide whether voters get to weigh in.

Here's why the numbers matter. The average D11 school building is 58 years old. The district has identified more than $422 million in needed repairs — from failing HVAC systems to aging roofs to safety upgrades. D11's annual capital repair budget is $17 million. At that pace, the backlog grows faster than it can be addressed.

If the bond passes at the November election, the estimated cost to a homeowner would be approximately $6.50 per month per $100,000 of assessed home value. For a home assessed at $400,000, that's about $26 per month.

D11's first day of school is August 12 — one week from tomorrow. Whatever the board decides, COS Parenting will have coverage Wednesday evening.

More information is available at d11.org/bond.

The free supply kits are already handled. The rest is yours. D11 first day: August 12.
