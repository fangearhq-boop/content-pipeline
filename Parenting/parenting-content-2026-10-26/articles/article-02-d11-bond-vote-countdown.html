D11's $775 Million Bond Is One Week Away — Here's What Colorado Springs Families Should Know

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting | October 26, 2026

November 3 is one week away, and Colorado Springs families with kids in District 11 have a significant question on their ballot. D11's $775 million bond measure is the largest school funding vote the district has brought to voters since 2004. Here's a clear-eyed look at what it would fund, what it would cost, and how to make sure your ballot is counted.

What the Bond Would Pay For

The bond covers infrastructure and major capital projects across the district. The headline items:

  • Palmer High School — a full rebuild of one of COS's oldest high school campuses
  • Holmes Middle School — a full rebuild
  • Coronado High School — HVAC system replacement
  • Districtwide upgrades across other D11 campuses

These are not renovation projects — Palmer and Holmes are rebuilds. Palmer High School has served Colorado Springs since 1894. The existing buildings have aged well past their design life, and the district argues that deferred maintenance on them is no longer cost-effective.

What It Would Cost Homeowners

The district estimates the tax impact at approximately $6.50 per month for every $100,000 of a home's assessed property value. For a home with an assessed value of $300,000, that works out to approximately $19.50 per month, or about $234 per year.

Assessed value and market value are not the same thing — assessed value is typically lower. Check your most recent property tax statement to find your home's assessed value if you want to calculate the exact impact on your household.

What It Could Mean for Teachers

Bond funds are restricted to capital projects — they can't be used to pay salaries. But D11 has signaled that if the bond passes and frees up operating funds that have been diverted to building maintenance and emergency repairs, the district intends to pursue a $10,000 raise for all D11 teachers. That is a district commitment, not language written into the bond itself, and families should weigh it accordingly.

Who Has Endorsed It

The Colorado Springs Gazette, the city's major daily newspaper, has endorsed the bond. Support also comes from the D11 Board of Education and the district's parent and teacher advocacy groups.

How to Vote

Colorado's coordinated elections use a mail ballot system. Your ballot was mailed to your registered address. If you haven't voted yet, drop your completed, sealed ballot at any D11 school building or El Paso County Clerk and Recorder drop-off location by 7 PM on November 3. No postage is needed for drop-off. For a full list of drop-off locations and information on same-day registration, visit d11.org/Bond or the El Paso County Clerk's website.

What's Next

Election Day is November 3. Results will be reported by the El Paso County Clerk's office on election night, with additional ballot batches counted through the week as mail ballots are verified. If the bond passes, D11 would begin the project planning process for Palmer and Holmes, with construction timelines expected to span several years. COS Parenting will cover the results and what a yes or no vote means for the district's capital plan.
