CPSC Recalls ONTHEWEI Baby Bath Seats — Tip-Over Risk for Infants

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting | October 26, 2026

The Consumer Product Safety Commission recalled ONTHEWEI baby bath seats in October 2026. Approximately 2,000 units were sold on Amazon. The problem is tip-over instability — the seat doesn't hold its position reliably in the tub, creating a drowning risk for infants. If you have one, stop using it now.

What Makes It Dangerous

Bath seats are designed to keep pre-crawling infants in a stable seated position during bath time. They're not flotation devices, and they're never a substitute for direct supervision — but they're meant to provide enough structural support that a baby doesn't tip over while a caregiver is right there.

The ONTHEWEI seats fail at that basic function. CPSC determined the seats have tip-over instability, meaning they can fall sideways or forward with the infant still in them. In a bathtub with even a few inches of water, that's a drowning hazard. The recall covers approximately 2,000+ units sold through Amazon.

What to Do

Stop use immediately. CPSC and the recall instructions ask that you destroy the product — cut or break it in a way that makes it unusable — so it can't accidentally be passed on or donated. Then request a full refund through your Amazon order history. The process is handled through Amazon's standard return system; you'll need to locate the original order.

The Broader Pattern

This is the third baby product recall or safety warning covered in the past two weeks: the bblüv Grow high chair CPSC Warning came October 15, the Costway convertible high chair recall arrived October 22, and this recall dates to October 9. All three involve products where the failure happens during ordinary supervised use — not misuse.

It's worth doing a quick sweep of the gear you use regularly. CPSC.gov has a recall search function where you can look up any product by brand name. The agency also lets you sign up for email alerts by product category — so if you set up an alert for "bath seats" or "high chairs," future recalls come to your inbox automatically rather than waiting for you to find them.

What's Next

If you're replacing a recalled bath seat, look for models that explicitly meet ASTM F1917, the current voluntary safety standard for infant bath seats. As with all bath products for infants, direct and uninterrupted supervision — not the seat itself — is the primary safety measure. CPSC.gov is the best ongoing resource for tracking new recalls across all baby and children's product categories.
