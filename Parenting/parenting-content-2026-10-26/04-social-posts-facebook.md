### STORY 1: Costway High Chair Recall

**Long-Form Post (8:00 AM MT)**
Heads up, Colorado Springs families — another high chair recall, and this one came from Walmart and Target.

On October 22, 2026, the Consumer Product Safety Commission recalled Costway convertible high chairs. About 3,332 units were sold at both major retailers. The problem: the chairs are missing a passive restraint, which means there's nothing keeping a baby secured if they shift or lunge. CPSC says this creates a fall and entrapment risk that could cause serious injury or death.

This comes just days after the bblüv Grow high chair warning we shared on Saturday. Two different products, two different companies, but the same category of concern: restraint systems.

If you have a Costway convertible high chair from Walmart or Target, stop using it right away. A full refund is available — check CPSC.gov or the retailer's recall page for details.

Have you checked your baby gear recalls lately? It's worth a 5-minute CPSC search by brand name.

### STORY 2: D11 Bond Countdown

**Long-Form Post (10:00 AM MT)**
If you're a Colorado Springs family with kids in D11, November 3rd is one week away — and there's a $775 million question on your ballot.

D11's school bond is the biggest local education vote in over two decades. It's the first new D11 bond since 2004. The projects it would fund include a full rebuild of Palmer High School, a full rebuild of Holmes Middle School, HVAC upgrades at Coronado High School, and districtwide improvements.

The cost to homeowners: approximately $6.50 per month for every $100,000 of assessed property value. A $300,000 home would see about $19.50/month.

What's also on the table: if the bond passes, it frees up operating funds the district has signaled could support a $10,000 raise for all D11 teachers.

Voting is easy — drop your completed ballot at any D11 school building or El Paso County Clerk drop-off location by November 3. No stamp needed.

### STORY 4: ONTHEWEI Bath Seat Recall

**Long-Form Post (10:00 AM MT)**
Bath time is supposed to be one of the safest parts of the day — but a recently recalled baby product is a good reminder to check what's in your tub.

CPSC recalled ONTHEWEI baby bath seats this month. About 2,000 units were sold on Amazon. The problem is tip-over instability: the seat doesn't stay stable in the tub, which creates a drowning risk for babies who can't sit up on their own without support.

If you have one of these, stop using it immediately. Destroy the product and request a full refund through your Amazon order history.

This is the third baby product recall or safety warning we've covered in the past two weeks. It's a good week to do a quick scan of CPSC.gov for any products you use regularly. CPSC lets you sign up for recall email alerts by product category — worth five minutes of setup.

### STORY 5: D11/D49 Back to School

**Long-Form Post (8:00 AM MT)**
Monday mornings after fall break have their own special energy — and not always in a good way.

D11 and D49 are both back in class today after fall break. D11 was out since October 19; D49 since October 13. That's nearly two weeks off for some COS families. And after that long a break, getting everyone back on schedule takes a little intentional effort.

A few things that actually help:

Reset the sleep schedule tonight — not tomorrow. One late night doesn't matter as much as one early morning. Set bedtime 30 minutes earlier than last night.

Pack bags and lunches tonight — morning chaos multiplies when nothing's ready. Do it before you go to bed so tomorrow's Tuesday already feels easier.

Talk through expectations — kids who've been doing their own thing for two weeks may need a quick reminder about homework routines, screen time rules, and when practice starts again.

Go easy on yourself — if today's morning is rougher than usual, that's normal. The groove comes back faster than you think.

D20 families have already been back for a week, which means they can confirm the first few days are always the hardest.
