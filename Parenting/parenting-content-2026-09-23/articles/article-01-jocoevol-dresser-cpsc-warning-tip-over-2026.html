CPSC Warns: Stop Using Jocoevol 6-Drawer Dressers — Tip-Over and Child Entrapment Hazard

By Sarah Morales | September 23, 2026

Product Safety | National Parenting

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission issued a warning on September 17, 2026, urging all consumers to immediately stop using Jocoevol 6-Drawer Dressers. The dressers violate the mandatory federal safety standard for clothing storage units and pose a serious tip-over and child entrapment hazard.

The units were sold on Amazon.com from approximately December 2024 through June 2026 for around $160. If you purchased a Jocoevol 6-Drawer Dresser and haven't securely anchored it to a wall, stop using it now — especially in any room where children spend time.

Why Dresser Tip-Overs Kill Children

Furniture tip-overs are one of the most persistent and preventable causes of serious injury and death among young children in the United States. Children are naturally curious and physically active — they pull on drawers, climb furniture, and explore their environments in ways that can shift the center of gravity on a tall, heavy piece of furniture in seconds.

A six-drawer dresser can weigh 60 to 100 pounds when full. If one tips onto a child, the weight can cause crushing injuries, head trauma, or suffocation. Entrapment — where a child becomes trapped between the furniture and a wall or floor — is equally dangerous. The mandatory federal safety standard for clothing storage units was specifically designed to address this risk by requiring that dressers remain stable under the kinds of forces children typically apply to them. Jocoevol's six-drawer dresser does not meet that standard.

What to Do If You Have This Dresser

Step Action
1 Stop using the dresser immediately
2 Keep children away from it
3 If you can anchor it securely to a wall stud using appropriate hardware, do so now
4 If you cannot safely anchor it, dispose of it according to your local requirements
5 Do not sell or give it away to another family

One important distinction: this is a CPSC warning or advisory, not a formal voluntary recall. That means there's no organized refund or remedy program from Jocoevol or Amazon. The CPSC is telling consumers to stop using the product and take action to eliminate the hazard, but there's no consumer refund hotline to call. Your options are to anchor it or dispose of it.

How to Anchor a Dresser Safely

If anchoring is an option in your home, the CPSC's AnchorIt.gov program has detailed guidance on how to do it properly. The key points are:

  • Use a wall anchor kit with straps rated for the weight of the furniture
  • Anchor into wall studs, not just drywall — drywall alone won't hold under real force
  • Use two anchor points when possible for taller or heavier pieces
  • Check the anchors periodically to make sure they remain secure

Anchor kits are widely available at hardware stores and typically cost between $10 and $30. For families who need assistance, the CPSC's AnchorIt.gov program occasionally offers free anchor kits and installation guidance.

Part of a Broader 2026 Enforcement Pattern

The Jocoevol warning is one of several dresser-related CPSC enforcement actions in 2026. Other brands including Furnulem, FJTJBSI, YITA, and Fixwal have also been cited for similar violations of the clothing storage unit mandatory standard this year. The common thread across all of them is that the products were sold through Amazon at lower price points, and none of them met the federal tip-over stability standard.

If you have any dresser or chest of drawers in your home — regardless of brand — that isn't anchored to a wall, particularly in a child's bedroom or play area, this is a good reminder to address that today.

What's Next

If you own a Jocoevol 6-Drawer Dresser, stop using it immediately and either anchor it securely or dispose of it. For the full CPSC warning, visit cpsc.gov and search "Jocoevol." For free anchoring resources and guidance, visit AnchorIt.gov. For questions, the CPSC consumer hotline is available at (800) 638-2772.
