### STORY 2: Qpancy Children's Pajama Recall

#### Long-Form Post — 10:00 AM MT

```
Heads up for Colorado Springs parents — if you bought Qpancy kids' pajamas from Amazon, there's an active CPSC recall you need to know about.

The recall covers about 1,309 sets sold between March and August 2026. The pajamas fail federal flammability standards for children's sleepwear, which is a serious safety issue. We're talking burn risk. The affected sets include long-sleeve, button-front tops with shorts, in sizes 4-5T through 10-11Y, with prints like multicolor cats, pink bows, and stripes. There's also a matching doll pajama set included in the recall.

The sets sold for about $20 on Amazon. No injuries have been reported, but the CPSC says to stop using them immediately.

Here's what to do: Email qpancyrecall@outlook.com to request your refund. You'll be asked to cut the pajamas in half, take a photo, and then discard them.

Recall number is 27-094 — you can confirm all details at cpsc.gov. Worth sharing this with other parents in your network who might have picked these up.

Have you seen this recall before this post? Drop a comment so we know if this is spreading!
```

### STORY 3: AAP Flu Vaccine Guidance 2026-27

#### Long-Form Post — 12:00 PM MT

```
With the holiday travel season in full swing, there's one thing pediatricians want every COS family to know: if your kids haven't gotten the flu vaccine yet, now is the time.

The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends the flu shot for every child 6 months and older, without exceptions. And this season matters — Colorado is already seeing an early flu surge, and the numbers from last year are sobering. The 2025-26 flu season killed 190 children in the US, and 85% of those children had not been vaccinated.

As of May this year, only 49.4% of kids ages 6 months to 17 years had received last season's flu vaccine. That gap is what the AAP is trying to close.

The 2026-27 flu vaccine is widely available. Call your pediatrician, check your local pharmacy, or visit healthychildren.org for guidance on which vaccine is right for your child's age and history. If your little ones are heading to Grandma's or on a plane this holiday weekend, it's especially worth taking care of this week.

Is your family up to date on flu shots?

### STORY 4: Colorado Springs School Zones

#### Long-Form Post — 3:00 PM MT

```
If you drop your kids off at a Colorado Springs middle or high school, there may be some new signs, flashers, and enforcement in your pickup zone soon.

Colorado Springs has been expanding its school zone safety program — Safe Streets COS — to include more middle and high schools across D11, D20, and D49. It's a change that was largely sparked by a tragedy: a Doherty High School student was struck and killed near the school in 2023, pushing the city to expand zones beyond elementary schools.

Schools that are part of the expansion include Sierra High School, Panorama Middle School, Cheyenne Mountain, Mann Middle School, West High School, Palmer High School, and others. Flashers at some sites are already operational for the 2026-27 school year. Speed safety cameras, operated in partnership with Colorado Springs Police, are also part of the program.

If you haven't seen the updated zone markings at your school yet, they may still be coming — the city has described the full rollout as an "ambitious goal."

Do you feel safer at your school's pickup/dropoff zone this year than in previous years?

### STORY 5: D11 Enrollment Update

#### Long-Form Post — 5:00 PM MT

```
Colorado Springs families: here's a local schools update you may have missed.

District 11 presented its latest enrollment and campus capacity numbers at a board work session last week — and the news is nuanced. Eleven D11 schools currently meet the state's criteria for being "underused," meaning they're operating below expected capacity.

Here's the important part: the district is not planning to close any schools. Instead, D11 is leaning toward "co-location" — which means putting more than one school or program in the same building. The district also acknowledged that it needs to do a better job of communicating with families earlier when co-location is being considered for a school.

One broader context item: D49 (Falcon) has grown to become the largest school district in El Paso County, surpassing D11. Meanwhile, D11 is managing an older building stock, including Holmes Middle School, which is set for an $80M rebuild as part of the voter-approved bond.

The district is using the bond money and an independent citizen oversight committee — also part of the bond approval — to guide how those funds get spent. That transparency piece will be worth watching over the next few years.

What's your read on the D11 situation? Are you in a school that's been part of the capacity discussions?
```
