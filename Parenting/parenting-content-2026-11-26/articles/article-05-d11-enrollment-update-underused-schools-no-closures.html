D11 Says 11 Schools Are Underused But Promises No Closures — Co-Location Plans Ahead

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting | November 26, 2026

Colorado Springs School District 11 presented updated enrollment and campus capacity data at a board work session on Nov. 18, 2026, and the news was notable: 11 D11 schools currently meet the state's criteria for being "underused," meaning they're operating below expected enrollment capacity.

The district's response to this situation, however, is a clear departure from what many families might expect. D11 says it is not planning to close any schools. Instead, the district is pursuing "co-location" — a strategy in which more than one school or program operates within a single building.

What D11 Said

At the Nov. 18 session, district leaders presented the capacity data alongside their approach to managing it. Key points from the presentation:

  • Eleven D11 schools meet the state's criteria for being underused for the 2026-27 school year.
  • The district has actually increased enrollment overall and is not experiencing the enrollment declines that have prompted closures in other districts across Colorado.
  • D11 is not currently planning to close any school buildings.
  • The district's preferred approach is co-location — sharing a facility between two or more programs or schools — rather than consolidation or closure.
  • District leaders acknowledged they need to "communicate earlier and more often" with school communities when a co-location is being considered.

That last point reflects a pattern that has caused tension in some communities. When co-location discussions happen without enough advance notice, families feel blindsided. The district's acknowledgment of this — and its stated commitment to earlier communication — is worth watching.

Bigger Picture: D49 Is Now the Largest District in El Paso County

One backdrop to D11's enrollment situation is a broader shift in where families are choosing to live and send their kids to school. D49 (Falcon/Peyton) has grown to become the largest school district in El Paso County, surpassing D11. Meanwhile, D11 manages an older building stock and a more established residential footprint on the west and central sides of the city.

The $775 million bond that voters approved in the November 2026 election (Ballot Issue 4A) is intended to address some of these infrastructure challenges, including an $80 million rebuild of Holmes Middle School. An independent citizen oversight committee is part of the bond plan to monitor how the money is spent.

What This Means for D11 Families

If your child attends a D11 school, nothing is changing immediately. The district has been clear that closures are not part of its current plan. However, co-location conversations may be coming for some campuses — and the district has committed to giving families more notice when those discussions begin.

If you want to stay informed about enrollment decisions at your school, attending D11 board meetings or following the district's updates at d11.org is the most reliable way to hear things first.

What's Next

D11 is expected to bring more capacity and co-location details to future board presentations as the 2026-27 school year progresses. The bond money rollout will also start to take shape, with the citizen oversight committee's first meetings likely coming early in 2027. For families in D11, the next 12 months will be an important window for staying engaged with district decisions. Watch the D11 board calendar and COS Parenting for updates as they develop.
