New School Zones Are Coming to Colorado Springs Middle and High Schools This Year

By Jamie Rivera | COS Parenting | November 26, 2026

If your family does school pickup or dropoff at a Colorado Springs middle or high school, there's a good chance the zone around your school has changed — or is about to change — this year. The city is continuing its Safe Streets COS program, which has been expanding traffic safety improvements to secondary schools across D11, D20, and D49.

Until recently, school zones with enhanced signage, flashers, and reduced speed limits were largely limited to elementary schools. That began to change after a Doherty High School student was struck and killed by a vehicle near the school in 2023. Since then, the city has been systematically adding zones to middle and high schools where pedestrian safety is most needed.

Which Schools Are Included

The Safe Streets COS expansion has named several Colorado Springs secondary schools as part of the program, including:

  • Sierra High School
  • Panorama Middle School
  • Cheyenne Mountain High School
  • Mann Middle School
  • West High School
  • Palmer High School
  • Vista Ridge High School

Flashing amber warning signs are already operational at some of these schools for the 2026-27 school year. The city has described the full rollout as an "ambitious goal" for 2026, with some installations still being completed at certain sites.

What Safe Streets COS Includes

The program is broader than just school zones. Safe Streets COS is a citywide initiative that includes:

  • Roadway and intersection safety improvements near schools
  • Enhanced school zone signage and flashing lights
  • Trail crossing projects
  • Speed safety cameras, operated in partnership with Colorado Springs Police
  • Safety demonstration projects

The speed safety camera component has gotten the most attention in some areas. Cameras are operational in designated school zones and focused on reducing speeding during school hours. Colorado Springs Police have said they will actively enforce traffic laws in these zones.

What This Means for Families

If you drive near a school with a new or expanded zone, expect reduced speed limits during school hours, flashing amber signs during dropoff and pickup windows, and increased enforcement. The changes are designed to protect kids walking to and from school — not just during the official school zone hours but throughout the school day.

If you haven't noticed zone markings at your school yet, they may still be coming — or may have been installed recently without much fanfare. The city updates its Safe Streets COS information at coloradosprings.gov/SafeStreetsCOS.

What's Next

The Safe Streets COS program is expected to continue expanding in the coming school year. A state bill related to school zone traffic safety was heard by the Colorado legislature in 2026, though its final status wasn't confirmed in time for this article — that's worth tracking as it may affect how cities across Colorado can implement and fund zone improvements. For parents, the key takeaway right now is to allow extra time near secondary schools during dropoff and pickup, and to watch for new zone markings at schools in your area.
