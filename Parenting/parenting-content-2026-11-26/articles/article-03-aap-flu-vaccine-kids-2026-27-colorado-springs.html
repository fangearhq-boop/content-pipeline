AAP Calls for Flu Vaccines for All Kids 6 Months and Up as Season Hits Colorado Early

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting | November 26, 2026

Colorado is seeing an early flu surge this season, and pediatricians are urging families who haven't gotten their kids vaccinated to do it now. The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends the 2026-27 flu vaccine for every child 6 months and older — without exceptions — and the numbers from last season make a strong case for taking that recommendation seriously.

In 2025-26, 190 children in the United States died from the flu. Of those, 85% had not been vaccinated. And as of May 2026, only 49.4% of children ages 6 months through 17 years had received that season's flu vaccine. That means more than half of kids were unprotected during one of the deadlier flu seasons in recent memory.

Why This Season Is Different

Colorado health officials reported an early flu surge this year, with the state's first pediatric flu death of the 2026-27 season announced before Thanksgiving. That's earlier than typical. The timing matters because flu activity tends to peak in winter, and an early start often means a longer, more difficult season overall.

The Thanksgiving holiday weekend — with its travel, indoor gatherings, and crowded spaces — is one of the highest-risk times of year for flu transmission. For families who've been putting off the vaccine, this week is a natural prompt to get it done before the holiday season gets any busier.

What the AAP Recommends

The AAP's 2026-27 guidance is clear and consistent with prior years:

  • All children 6 months and older should receive the flu vaccine as soon as it's available, unless they have a medical contraindication.
  • All flu vaccines available in the US this season are trivalent (protecting against three flu strains).
  • The AAP does not recommend one age-appropriate flu product over another.
  • The recommended number of doses depends on the child's age and flu vaccination history — your pediatrician can confirm the right number for your child.

The vaccine is widely available at pediatricians' offices, pharmacies, and many urgent care clinics in Colorado Springs. The healthychildren.org page from the AAP has a parent-friendly summary of everything you need to know: healthychildren.org.

Beyond the Vaccine

The flu shot is the most important step, but it works best alongside other basics. Regular handwashing, keeping sick kids home from school, covering coughs and sneezes, and staying home when you feel unwell all help slow the spread. If your child develops flu-like symptoms — fever, body aches, fatigue, cough — contact your pediatrician early, especially for children under 2, those with asthma or other chronic conditions, or infants.

What's Next

Flu season in Colorado typically runs from November through March, with peak activity in January and February. The best time to vaccinate was a month ago; the second best time is this week. If you're heading into the holiday season with gatherings, travel, or family visits planned, getting the kids vaccinated now gives their immune systems the best chance to respond before the busiest weeks of flu season arrive. Call your pediatrician or check a local pharmacy — most have same-day or walk-in availability for flu shots.
