### STORY 1: Taleco Gear Baby Jumpers Recall

#### Long-Form Post — 8:00 AM MT

Heads up if you have a baby jumper or swing at home.

The CPSC recalled Taleco Gear Baby Jumpers, Baby Swings, and 2-in-1 Baby Jumper & Swings on August 13 due to fall and strangulation hazards. There have been at least 52 reported incidents — including at least 32 injuries, with head bumps and bloody noses among them.

These products were sold on Amazon.com, Walmart.com, and TalecoGear.com from April 2021 through July 2026, priced between $65 and $120.

If you have one of these products, CPSC says to stop using it immediately. You can find the full recall details at CPSC.gov — search "Taleco Gear" to pull up the recall notice with information on what to do next.

### STORY 2: HARPPA Nordi Toddler Tower Stools Recall

#### Long-Form Post — 10:00 AM MT

A lot of us have toddler tower stools in our kitchens — those little step-up helpers that let kids stand at counter height and "assist" with cooking. If yours is the HARPPA Nordi model, the CPSC has recalled it.

The recall cites entrapment and fall hazards, with the risk of serious injury or death. This is the kind of product safety language that means you should stop using it now and check CPSC.gov for the full recall notice and next steps.

Toddler kitchen helpers are a popular category and many families love them — but this specific model has a design problem serious enough to prompt a federal recall. Check your brand and model before your next kitchen session with your toddler.

### STORY 4: AAP Screen Time Guidelines

#### Long-Form Post — 8:00 PM MT

We're a week into the school year for most Colorado Springs families, which means the summer screen time habits are running into a new daily schedule.

Good moment to revisit what the American Academy of Pediatrics actually recommends.

The AAP has updated its approach to focus on quality over strict time limits. Here's the short version:

- Under 18 months: no screens, except for video chats with family
- Ages 2-5: up to 1 hour per day of high-quality content
- Ages 6 and up: consistent limits, focus on what they're watching, and watch together when you can

The key shift is the emphasis on what kids watch and whether parents are engaged, not just the clock. An hour of an educational show watched together is very different from an hour of autoplay on a tablet in another room.

With homework, after-school activities, and the general chaos of the school year ramping up, now's a good time to set some family guidelines before the habits fully lock in.

### STORY 5: D11 Free School Supply Kits + COS Supply Resources

#### Long-Form Post — 12:00 PM MT

Colorado Springs District 11 pulled off something pretty great at the start of this school year.

Through a partnership with Edukit, D11 delivered free, grade-specific school supply kits to more than 14,000 K-8 students on the first day of school (August 12). No shopping list, no last-minute runs, no "we couldn't afford everything" — kids walked in ready to learn.

If your family is still getting everything sorted, there are other resources available:

The Phil Long Ford Chapel Hills location (1565 Chapel Hills Drive) has an ongoing back-to-school supply drive benefiting Beulah School of Natural Sciences students and families affected by the Aspen Acres fire. Details at shield616.org.

And if you're still working on enrollment — D11 online enrollment is open at d11.org, or you can go in person to 1033 N. Franklin St., Monday through Friday, 8 AM to 4 PM MT.
