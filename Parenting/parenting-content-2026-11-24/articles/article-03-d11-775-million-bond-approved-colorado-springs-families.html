Colorado Springs Voters Approved a $775 Million D11 Bond — What It Means for Your Family

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting | November 24, 2026

Tier 2 | Local News pillar

Colorado Springs School District 11 has a mandate — and a lot of money to work with.

D11 voters approved Ballot Issue 4A in the November 2026 general election: a $775 million school bond that may be the largest in El Paso County history. The measure passed with voter support in a district that serves 23,458 students across a network of aging facilities, 11 of which the district's own board has identified as significantly underused.

What's in the Bond

The most prominent specific commitment in the bond package is the Holmes Middle School rebuild, which carries an approximately $80 million price tag. Holmes has been one of D11's older facilities, and a full rebuild rather than a renovation signals the district's intent to address infrastructure at a foundational level rather than incrementally.

The full spending plan beyond Holmes has not been fully itemized publicly, and that's where the oversight structure becomes relevant. D11 plans to establish an independent citizen oversight committee to monitor how the bond funds are spent — a meaningful accountability layer given that $775 million distributed over a school district's capital program is a multi-year commitment that will outlast the current administration and board.

Context: What D11 Has Been Managing

The bond arrives at a complicated moment for the district. D11 is running at 81% building capacity on average, and that number is projected to drop to roughly 73% over the next 10 years without intervention. Last week, the board reviewed the status of 11 underused campuses — which the district has described as candidates for co-location with other programs, not for outright closure.

The bond money doesn't directly solve the capacity mismatch in the near term. What it does is give the district the capital to make strategic investments in the buildings it wants to keep, while the enrollment and co-location decisions unfold separately.

What Parents Should Track

The oversight committee structure is worth watching. If a citizen oversight body is established as planned, its meetings will likely be open to the public, and its reports will be the primary accountability mechanism for how bond funds are deployed. D11 families who want to stay engaged should watch the district's official communications at d11.org for committee formation announcements.

Parents with students at Holmes Middle School will likely see the most direct near-term impact — but the broader capital program will touch facilities across the district over time.

What's Next

The district will move into implementation planning over the coming months. Holmes Middle School's rebuild is the most clearly defined early commitment. The citizen oversight committee's formation and first meetings will likely take place in early 2027.

COS Parenting will follow the bond implementation, committee meetings, and any school-specific decisions that affect D11 families. For now, the vote itself is a signal that Colorado Springs residents are willing to invest in the district's infrastructure at a scale that hasn't been seen before.
