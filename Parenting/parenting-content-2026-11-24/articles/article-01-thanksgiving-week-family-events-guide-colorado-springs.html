What to Do with Your Kids in Colorado Springs This Thanksgiving Week

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting | November 24, 2026

Tier 1 | Local Events pillar

D11, D20, and D49 schools are out all week, and if you're looking for ways to fill Thanksgiving break without anyone losing their mind, Colorado Springs has a full calendar of options — some free, some ticketed, and one that involves running in the cold on Thanksgiving morning.

Here's what's happening this week, and how to make the most of it.

Turkey Trot 5K — Thanksgiving Morning

The 29th annual YMCA Turkey Trot returns to Briargate YMCA at 4025 Family Place on Thursday, November 26. It's one of the city's most reliable Thanksgiving traditions, drawing 4,000-plus participants each year for a morning run before the cooking begins.

The Tiny Trot — a shorter run for younger kids — starts at 8:15 AM MT. The main 5K starts at 9:00 AM MT. Registration closes at 8:15 AM on race morning, so if you're planning to run, don't wait until Thursday to sign up. Register at runsignup.com before the cutoff.

It's a genuinely fun family event, and even if running isn't the plan, the energy of 4,000 people starting Thanksgiving outside is worth showing up for.

Colorado Springs Philharmonic: The Nutcracker

The Colorado Springs Philharmonic presents The Nutcracker at the Pikes Peak Center for the Performing Arts this Friday through Sunday, November 27–29. If you've been meaning to start a Nutcracker tradition with your family, this is a good year to do it.

Tickets are available at AXS.com or at the Pikes Peak Center box office. It's the kind of experience that lands differently once kids are old enough to follow the story — typically around ages 5 and up — though even younger kids tend to respond to the music and the visuals.

Free Options All Week

Garden of the Gods is open every day, as always, and free year-round. A morning hike among the red rock formations with Pikes Peak in the background is one of the best free things Colorado Springs families can do any time of year. Thanksgiving week is no exception, and the weather in late November tends to be cool and clear.

In Manitou Springs, the Elf Hunt is running through the holiday season — a free scavenger hunt that gets families walking through the shops and streets looking for hidden elves. It's low-stakes, free, and works for a wide age range.

Cheyenne Mountain Zoo is open on Thanksgiving Day, which makes it an option if you want to build a morning around the zoo before the afternoon meal.

What's Next

Thanksgiving break runs through Sunday, November 29 for most COS families, with D11, D20, and D49 schools returning Monday, November 30. The weekend of November 27–29 is the busiest stretch, with the Nutcracker, the Elf Hunt, and the post-Turkey-Trot energy carrying through.

December brings the full holiday event season in Colorado Springs — including holiday lights, winter markets, and more Pikes Peak Center productions. We'll have that roundup in the coming weeks.
