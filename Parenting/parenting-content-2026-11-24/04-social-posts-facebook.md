### STORY 2: Kidsview Bouncer Recall

**Long-Form Post:**
Heads up for anyone with a Kidsview infant bouncer at home.

The Consumer Product Safety Commission announced a recall on November 19 after seven Kidsview infant bouncers collapsed during use, injuring two babies who struck their heads on the floor. About 412 units are affected — these were sold on Amazon.

If you have one of these, stop using it immediately. A full refund is available. Visit cpsc.gov or contact the seller through Amazon to get the recall process started.

This is the third infant product recall we've covered in the past two weeks. It's a good reminder to check your gear regularly and bookmark cpsc.gov/recalls so you can search any product quickly.

Have you ever had to deal with a product recall for something you use with your kids? Let us know in the comments.

### STORY 3: D11 $775M Bond

**Long-Form Post:**
Colorado Springs School District 11 families: voters approved the D11 Ballot Issue 4A bond last election — a $775 million investment that could be the largest school bond in El Paso County history.

Here's what we know so far about where the money goes: Holmes Middle School is slated for a full rebuild at approximately $80 million. The district also plans an independent citizen oversight committee to monitor how the funds are spent — which is a meaningful accountability step given the scale of the investment.

For context, D11 has been dealing with an enrollment and capacity challenge. The district serves 23,458 students across buildings that are running at 81% capacity on average, with 11 campuses identified as significantly underused. The bond creates a path to address those long-term infrastructure needs rather than just managing what's already there.

There's a lot more detail still to come as the district makes implementation decisions. What questions do you have about the D11 bond and what it means for your kids' schools?

### STORY 4: Black Friday Toy Safety

**Long-Form Post:**
Black Friday is two days away, and if toys are on your shopping list, it's worth taking five minutes to run a quick safety check before you buy.

Here's what we're checking in 2026 specifically:

**Age labels.** The "choking hazard — not for children under 3" warning exists for a reason. If a toy has small parts and your household has kids under three, it stays on the shelf.

**Magnets.** This year we've covered multiple recalls involving high-powered magnetic toys — including a recall of over 100,000 magnetic robot sets earlier this month. High-strength magnets in kids' products are banned under CPSC rules, but they still show up in toys. If a toy has accessible magnets, it's worth a double-check at cpsc.gov.

**Button batteries.** A coin-sized battery can cause a severe internal burn injury in under two hours if swallowed. Any toy with a battery compartment should require a screwdriver to open — not just a snap.

**Noise levels.** Toys over 85 decibels can damage kids' hearing over time. If it sounds loud to you at a distance, it's loud for a child holding it.

**Flammability.** The Qpancy pajamas recall earlier this month is a good reminder to check that sleepwear meets flammability standards — that label matters.

What toys are on your kids' wish lists this year? Drop them in the comments and we'll let you know if anything's on the CPSC watch list.

### STORY 5: Holiday Parenting Self-Care

**Long-Form Post:**
The holidays are a lot. The school break, the travel, the family gatherings, the gift logistics, and somehow keeping everyone's emotions regulated including your own.

A study published in the AAP journal Pediatrics found something worth sitting with: children of caregivers who weren't dealing with their own mental health challenges benefited most from parenting support programs. In other words, it's not just that your mental health matters for you — it directly affects how well you're able to be there for your kids.

This isn't a guilt trip. It's the opposite. It's permission to take the mask-on-yourself-first principle seriously.

Some practical options for Colorado Springs families:

If you're feeling overwhelmed, El Paso County Behavioral Health (elpasocountyhealth.org) has resources and referrals. If you're in a D11 school family, the district's family support services team can be a starting point. The YMCA's family wellness programs run year-round and are genuinely accessible.

This week, even one small thing — a 20-minute walk at Garden of the Gods, calling a friend, sleeping one hour earlier — counts.

What do you do to take care of yourself during the holiday season? We'd love to hear what actually works for COS families.
