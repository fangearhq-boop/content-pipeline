Pikes Peak Regional Airshow Returns September 19–20: Your Family Guide to Colorado Springs' Biggest Aviation Event

By Jamie Rivera | COS Parenting | September 8, 2026

Local Events | Pikes Peak Airshow | Colorado Springs | Family Activities

One of the most spectacular family events of the year is coming back to Colorado Springs. The Pikes Peak Regional Airshow returns to the Colorado Springs Airport on September 19 and 20, bringing two full days of aerial demonstrations, WWII warbirds, and a kids' zone to the Front Range.

What to Expect at the Show

The 2026 airshow lineup combines modern military aircraft and legendary WWII warbirds in a way that works for aviation enthusiasts of every age and knowledge level.

On the military demonstration side, families can watch the U.S. Air Force F-35A Lightning II Demonstration Team put the Air Force's most advanced fighter through its paces. The U.S. Navy F/A-18 Demo Team brings Navy precision to the Colorado skies. The USAF Wings of Blue parachute team rounds out the military portion with a high-altitude jump that's one of the show's most crowd-pleasing moments for kids.

The WWII warbird collection is an education on its own. The show features flying examples of aircraft that shaped history: a B-17 Flying Fortress bomber, the iconic Corsair fighter, the rugged P-47 Thunderbolt, a B-25 Mitchell bomber, the F7F Tigercat, the SBD Dauntless dive bomber, and the Skyraider. Seeing these planes flying together is genuinely rare — and the National Museum of World War II Aviation, based in Colorado Springs, provides context that makes the history tangible.

Practical Tips for Families

A few things that make the day smoother with kids in tow. Hearing protection is worth bringing for younger children — the aircraft demonstrations, particularly the fighter jets, produce noise that can startle and distress toddlers and preschoolers. Foam earplugs or child-sized ear defenders are easy to pack and make the experience much more enjoyable for sensitive ears.

The grounds open at 8:30 AM, well before flying begins at 11 AM. Arriving early gives families time to find good viewing spots, explore the ground displays, and get food before the crowds build. Colorado September afternoons can be warm, so sunscreen and water bottles are worth prioritizing.

Tickets are available in advance at pprairshow.org. Advance purchase is recommended — events like this draw large crowds, and having tickets ready avoids entry line waits during the pre-demonstration rush.

What's Next

September 19 is shaping up to be one of the bigger family days of the month in Colorado Springs — the Pikes Peak Regional Airshow runs both days, and the Rocky Mountain PBS KIDS FEST is also happening that Saturday at Panorama Park from 10 AM to 2 PM. Families with younger children who prefer a lower-decibel option have a great alternative that same day.

For full airshow details, performer lineup updates, and ticket purchase, visit pprairshow.org.
