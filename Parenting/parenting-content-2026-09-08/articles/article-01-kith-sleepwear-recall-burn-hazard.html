CPSC Recall: Kith Children's Lounge Sets Pose Burn Hazard — What Colorado Springs Parents Need to Do

By Jamie Rivera | COS Parenting | September 8, 2026

Product Safety | CPSC Recall | Children's Sleepwear | Colorado Springs Parenting

Families who purchased the Kids Kithmas Plaid Lounge Sets from Kith during the 2025 holiday season need to take immediate action: the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission has recalled the sets due to a burn hazard. The garments violate mandatory federal flammability standards for children's sleepwear, posing a risk of serious injury.

What Makes This a Safety Risk

Federal standards for children's sleepwear exist specifically because loose-fitting garments can come into contact with ignition sources — stove burners, candles, and matches — more easily than tight-fitting clothing. Once ignited, loose sleepwear burns rapidly and can cause severe burns over large areas of a child's body.

The Kith lounge sets are made from woven fabric that did not meet the flammability testing requirements required for children's sleepwear. The recall affects the holiday plaid sets sold in sizes for both infants and older children. No incidents or injuries have been reported in the United States or Canada as of the recall date, but the CPSC's guidance is to stop using the product immediately regardless.

Steps to Take Now

The recall process for these sets requires destruction of the product before a refund is issued. Here is what to do:

  1. Stop using the lounge sets immediately. Do not let your child sleep or lounge in them.
  2. Cut the garment in half to render it unusable.
  3. Take a clear photograph of the destroyed garment.
  4. Email the photo along with proof of purchase to recalls@kithnyc.com. Include your contact information and shipping address.
  5. Dispose of the product.

Kith will issue a full refund in the form of a gift card. If you have questions or do not receive a response within a reasonable timeframe, the CPSC provides consumer assistance at cpsc.gov or by calling 1-800-638-2772.

What's Next

If you're in the market for replacement children's sleepwear, look for garments labeled "snug-fitting" — tight-fitting children's pajamas are exempt from flammability testing requirements because they do not pose the same ignition risk as loose styles. Alternatively, look for garments with a tag explicitly stating they meet CPSC flammability requirements.

The CPSC updates its recall list regularly at cpsc.gov/recalls. Bookmarking that page or signing up for email alerts is one of the simplest ways to stay informed about product safety issues affecting families.
