### STORY 1: Kith Children's Sleepwear Recall

**Long-Form Post (8:00 AM MT)**

Heads up, parents — if you purchased the Kids Kithmas Plaid Lounge Sets from Kith during the holidays, there's a CPSC recall you need to know about.

Kith Retail recalled the sets because they violate mandatory flammability standards for children's sleepwear. About 130 sets were sold at Kith stores and online from December 2025 through January 2026 for approximately $75. The hazard: loose-fitting sleepwear can come in contact with stoves, candles, or matches and ignite quickly, causing severe burns.

Here's what to do if you have them: Stop using immediately. Cut the garment in half. Take a photo of the destroyed item. Email recalls@kithnyc.com with your photo and proof of purchase to receive a full refund via gift card.

The good news: no injuries have been reported. Acting now keeps it that way.

### STORY 2: Dresser Tip-Over Recalls

**Long-Form Post (10:00 AM MT)**

Two dresser recalls crossed our radar this week, and both involve the same serious risk: a dresser that can tip over and trap or crush a child.

Gizoon Direct recalled their 6-drawer double dressers due to tip-over and entrapment hazards. Separately, the Alanca-branded 6-drawer wood dressers (sold by Nanjing Wu Hai) were recalled because they don't meet federal standards for clothing storage units.

If you have either of these dressers, stop using them immediately if they're not anchored to the wall. Contact the manufacturer for a full refund.

And even if your dresser isn't on the recall list, this is a good reminder: ALL dressers should be anchored to the wall with an anti-tip kit. It's a simple fix that prevents a tragedy. Anchor kits are widely available for under $10.

### STORY 3: Pikes Peak Regional Airshow

**Long-Form Post (3:00 PM MT)**

Colorado Springs families — put September 19 and 20 on the calendar, because the Pikes Peak Regional Airshow is back.

Two full days of aerial demonstrations at the Colorado Springs Airport, including the USAF F-35A Lightning II Demo Team, the U.S. Navy F/A-18 Demo Team, and an impressive lineup of WWII warbirds — a B-17, Corsair, P-47, and more. There's also a kids' zone and ground displays to explore between the flying demos.

Logistics: Grounds and free parking open at 8:30 AM. Flying demonstrations run approximately 11:00 AM to 2:25 PM each day. The address is 7330 Embraer-Heights, Colorado Springs. Tickets are available in advance online at pprairshow.org — purchasing ahead is a good idea since events like this draw big crowds.

This is one of those Colorado Springs events that works for just about every age. Whether your kids are obsessed with planes or just love a big outdoor spectacle, two hours of fighter jets and vintage aircraft tends to leave an impression.

### STORY 5: Post-Labor Day School Routines

**Long-Form Post (12:00 PM MT)**

D49 families are in week 7. D11 is week 5. D20 is week 4. The school year is well underway — and right after Labor Day is actually one of the trickiest moments of the fall.

Why? Because kids had three days off. Routines that were barely cemented got disrupted. Sleep schedules shifted. And now it's Tuesday, and everyone needs to reassemble.

A few things that tend to help this week: holding firm to wake times even if there's pushback, building in a bit of extra decompression time after school since the post-long-weekend return can bring extra emotional weight, and checking in specifically with kids who've been showing any signs of stress or fatigue about the school year.

Week 4 through 7 is also when after-school activities really kick into gear — sports, clubs, and enrichment programs that were just getting started are now in full swing. If your family is feeling overscheduled, this is a natural checkpoint to assess which commitments are working and which are adding friction.
