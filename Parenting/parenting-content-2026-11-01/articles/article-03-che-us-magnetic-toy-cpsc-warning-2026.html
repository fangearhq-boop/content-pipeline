CPSC Warning: Stop Using CHE-US Magnetic Stick Figures and Fidget Balls Immediately

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting

November 1, 2026

The Consumer Product Safety Commission issued a stop-use warning on October 29, 2026 for two products from CHE-US: magnetic stick figure sets and magnetic fidget balls. If you own either product, the CPSC's instruction is direct — stop using them immediately and keep them away from children.

This is a priority safety action, not a routine advisory. The hazard involved — rare-earth magnet ingestion — is one of the most serious toy-related dangers that pediatric emergency rooms treat, and the CHE-US warning comes because the products contain the specific magnet type responsible for the worst outcomes.

What the Hazard Is

CHE-US magnetic stick figures and fidget balls contain powerful rare-earth magnets. When a single magnet is swallowed, it typically passes through the digestive system without serious harm. The danger arises when multiple magnets are swallowed at different times, or when a magnet is swallowed and another magnet — or a piece of metal — is already in the body: the magnets can attract to each other through intestinal walls, pinching tissue between them.

That pinching can cut off blood supply to sections of the intestine, cause perforations, or create blockages. Serious injuries require surgery. In the most severe cases, magnet ingestion has been fatal. Children, especially those under 5, are at highest risk because they're more likely to put small objects in their mouths and less likely to understand the danger.

Rare-earth magnets — including neodymium magnets found in many magnetic toy products — are significantly stronger than conventional refrigerator magnets. They can attract through multiple centimeters of material, which is what makes ingestion so dangerous even when magnets are swallowed at different times.

Why This Is a Warning, Not a Recall

The CPSC issued a "stop-use warning" rather than a formal recall because CHE-US has not cooperated with the agency's recall process. A formal recall requires coordination with the manufacturer or importer to implement a remedy — typically a refund, replacement, or repair program. When a company does not cooperate, the CPSC lacks the legal authority to compel one unilaterally under current law, so it issues the most urgent action available to it: a public warning directing consumers to stop use immediately.

From a consumer standpoint, the absence of a formal recall does not reduce the urgency. There is no remedy program to participate in, no replacement to wait for. The recommended action is simply to stop using the products and keep them away from children.

How to Identify These Products

Both CHE-US products were sold primarily online, including on Amazon and similar platforms. The magnetic stick figures are sets of small human-shaped figures made from magnetic rods and metal ball connectors. The fidget balls are spherical toys containing rare-earth magnets. Check your home for these descriptions, and check cpsc.gov for photos and additional product identification details if you're uncertain.

If you purchased either product as a gift — including in the past year or two — contact the recipient. These products were sold over a period of time, and they may be in homes beyond your own.

The Broader Magnetic Toy Pattern

The CHE-US warning is not an isolated incident. The CPSC has been tracking hazards from high-powered magnetic toy products across multiple brands for years. Magnet sets marketed as adult fidget or desk toys have repeatedly ended up in homes with young children, and the ingestion injuries have been consistent across brands and product types. If you have other high-powered magnetic toys — including sets not mentioned in this warning — it's worth reviewing whether they're appropriate for a home with young children and whether they're stored out of reach.

What's Next

The CPSC updates its warnings and recall notices at cpsc.gov. If you believe you've purchased a CHE-US magnetic product, stop using it now and check the site for any additional guidance. If a child in your care has swallowed a magnet or you suspect magnet ingestion, go to an emergency room immediately — do not wait for symptoms to develop, as internal damage can occur before pain is apparent.
