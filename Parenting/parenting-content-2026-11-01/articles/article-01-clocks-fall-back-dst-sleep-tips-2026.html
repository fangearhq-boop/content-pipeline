Clocks Fall Back Tonight at 2 AM — Here's How to Keep Kids' Sleep on Track This Week

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting

November 1, 2026

Daylight Saving Time ends tonight. At 2:00 AM on Sunday, November 1, 2026, clocks fall back one hour across most of the United States — including Colorado Springs. The change happens automatically on most phones and devices, but your kids' internal clocks won't receive the update. That discrepancy between the new clock time and your children's body schedule is what makes the first week after the fall-back feel harder than it sounds like it should.

For Colorado Springs families with school-age children in D11, D20, or D49, Monday morning is the real test: the kids are back in class on what their bodies still understand as the old schedule. An early wake-up on a school morning after a time change is one of the more reliably rough parts of November parenting, but there are a few things you can do tonight and over the next several days to make it easier.

Tonight: Keep the Routine Exactly the Same

The most common mistake on time-change night is trying to game the system. Parents see the extra hour and decide to let the kids stay up late, reasoning that they'll sleep until a more convenient time in the morning. It almost never works that way. Children's bodies are not very responsive to one-night schedule shifts, and they tend to wake at roughly their usual biological time regardless of when they went to bed.

The better approach: run tonight's bedtime routine as close to normal as possible. Same wind-down sequence, same darkness level in the room, same time on the clock. If your child usually goes to bed at 8:00 PM, aim for 8:00 PM on the new clock. The room should be dark — blackout curtains earn their cost this time of year, when earlier evening light in November can make it feel like the middle of the day at what is technically bedtime.

The morning will likely still be early. Accept that going in. Monday is going to be a tired morning for a lot of Colorado Springs families, and that's a normal consequence of the time change, not a sign that something went wrong.

The Week Ahead: Gradual Shifting Works Better Than Forcing It

Pediatric sleep guidance is fairly consistent on this point: gradual adjustment over several days works better than trying to reset all at once. If your child normally wakes at 6:30 AM, they'll likely be up around 5:30 AM for the first few days after fall-back. Rather than fighting it, shift the day's schedule slightly: push meals, naps, and activities back by 15 minutes every two days until you're back on the regular schedule.

That means lunch at 11:45 instead of 11:30, dinner at 5:45 instead of 5:30, and bedtime nudged back incrementally over the course of the week. By Thursday or Friday, most school-age children will be close to fully adjusted.

Light exposure helps reset the body clock faster. Getting kids outside in the morning — even for a few minutes — provides the bright light signal that tells the brain the day has started. Morning sunlight is one of the most effective natural tools for circadian adjustment. Evening darkness matters too: keep the bedroom dark when it's time to wind down, since the earlier sunsets this week can actually help by making it feel like bedtime sooner.

Babies and Toddlers Take Longer

For infants and toddlers under 2, the adjustment period tends to run longer — closer to a week to ten days rather than the four to five days that work for most school-age kids. Younger children are more dependent on consistent environmental cues and have less capacity to self-regulate their sleep schedule.

If you have an infant or a toddler, the same gradual shift approach applies, just over a longer window. Be prepared for a more disruptive first week than you might expect with older children. Consistency in the bedtime routine — same sequence, same darkness, same temperature in the room — is especially important during the transition period.

What Usually Works

A few small adjustments make the transition noticeably smoother for most families. Keeping the bedroom dark and cool during sleeping hours is the single most effective change you can make. Dark and cool signal sleep to children's brains regardless of what the clock says, which matters a lot when their bodies are adjusted to a different time than the one on the wall.

Avoid screen time in the hour before bed this week. The blue light exposure from devices delays the body's melatonin production, which is already disrupted by the time change. This matters more in the first few days after fall-back than during the rest of the year.

Most children fully adjust to the time change within five to seven days. For some kids the adjustment is barely noticeable; for others it's a harder week. The key is consistency: keep the routine intact, don't panic about the early mornings, and give the body a week to catch up.

Quick Reference: Fall Back Survival Guide
