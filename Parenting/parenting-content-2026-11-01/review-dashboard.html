Clocks Fall Back Tonight — How to Help Kids Adjust to the Time Change
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Tonight's the night — clocks fall back one hour at 2:00 AM, and while the "extra hour" sounds great on paper, it tends to land a little differently when your kids are up at 5:30 Monday morning like it's totally normal. Here's what actually helps: keep tonight's bedtime routine exactly the same as usual. Don't try to push it late just because you can — your kids' bodies won't cooperate, and you'll still be dealing with the early wake-up. Keep the room dark (blackout curtains help a lot with the earlier sunsets this week), keep the wind-down routine consistent, and expect a rough Monday morning regardless. For babies and toddlers under 2, it usually takes longer — closer to a week — to fully adjust. School-age kids in D11, D20, and D49 head back to class Monday, so it's worth front-loading sleep tonight if you can. The gradual-shift approach works best: shift meals, naps, and bedtimes by 15 minutes every couple of days rather than trying to reset all at once. Most kids are fully adjusted within 5-7 days. ### Long-Form Post If you haven't returned your ballot yet, this weekend is your last real window before Tuesday's election. The D11 $775 million school bond (Issue 4A) is on the November 3 ballot. If it passes, the money goes toward HVAC system repairs, a new Holmes Middle School, updates to Palmer High and Coronado High, and library and classroom renovations across the district — the first major capital funding for D11 since 2004. To return your ballot: drop boxes are open now (find locations at d11.org/bond or govotecolorado.gov), mail ballots must be postmarked by November 3, or vote in person at El Paso County polling places. The estimated cost for average homeowners is approximately $260 per year. Whether you're for it or against it, if you have a ballot at home, today and tomorrow are the days to get it in. CPSC Warning: Stop Using CHE-US Magnetic Stick Figures and Fidget Balls Immediately
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post 🚨 This one is worth a quick check of your toy bins before the kids play today. On October 29, the Consumer Product Safety Commission issued a stop-use warning for two products from CHE-US: magnetic stick figure sets and magnetic fidget balls. Both contain powerful rare-earth magnets that, if swallowed, can attract to each other through intestinal walls — causing serious internal injuries that often require surgery, and in some cases can be fatal. These aren't hypothetical risks; magnet ingestion is one of the most dangerous toy hazards pediatric emergency rooms treat. This is a CPSC warning rather than a formal recall because CHE-US hasn't cooperated with the agency — which actually makes it more urgent, not less, since there's no formal remedy process in place yet. The action step is simple: stop using these products and keep them away from children. Both were sold online, including on Amazon. If you're not sure whether you own these, check cpsc.gov for photos and product details. And if you have young kids who might still be drawn to small magnetic toys in general, it's a good time to audit what's in the playroom. NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post The COS Veterans Day Parade is this Saturday, November 7 — not November 11, so mark it now if it's not already on your calendar. The "In Their Honor" parade steps off at 11:00 AM and marches south along Tejon Street from East St. Vrain to Vermijo Avenue. Road closures begin at 9:00 AM and are expected to reopen by 1:00 PM, so plan your downtown routing accordingly if you're arriving early to get a good spot. This year's Grand Marshal is 103-year-old retired U.S. Navy Commander Jesse Kimber, honored as a Rosie the Riveter figure. Organizers asked women to dress as Rosies along the route as part of the tribute, and a public art display — "Salute from the Springs" — will accompany the parade. Colorado Springs is one of the most veteran-dense cities in the country. This parade draws a real crowd and tends to be a genuinely moving experience for kids and adults alike. If you've never taken your family, it's worth going at least once. A Community and Veterans Job Fair is scheduled for November 10, 10 AM–2 PM at the Colorado Springs Event Center at 3960 Palmer Park Blvd, for military families looking for employment resources. November in Colorado Springs — Family Activities Worth Putting on Your Calendar
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post October's over — so what's actually happening in Colorado Springs for families this month? The big opener is Skate in the Park, the outdoor skating rink at America the Beautiful Park, which opens November 13. Admission is $15 per person including skate rental, and kids 4 and under skate free with a paying adult. It's one of those traditions that feels distinctly COS and tends to get busy on weekends, so weekday evenings are worth considering once school gets out. For families who run: Turkey Trails is November 8 with a kids' dash at 8:00 AM, and the 28th Annual Turkey Trot 5K and Kids' Tiny Trot is November 26 (Thanksgiving week). Both are COS staples. A few things worth noting before they fill up: Kids' Reading & Craft Time at Wild Birds Unlimited is November 7 at 2:00 PM (a day before the parade — good combo if you're already downtown). The Pikes Peak Library District runs Baby Time and Toddler Time weekly throughout November at no cost — check PPLD.org for the branch schedule. November is when COS family life starts shifting into holiday mode. Getting a few of these on the calendar now means you're not scrambling when December arrives.
