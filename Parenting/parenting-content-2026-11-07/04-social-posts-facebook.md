### STORY 1: D11 Bond — What Happens Now

**Long-Form Post (8:00 AM MT)**

Big news from Tuesday's election: D11's $775 million school bond appears to have passed, with unofficial results showing 51.3% yes. If it holds, it would be the first successful D11 capital bond in more than 20 years.

Here's the thing, though: it's not official. El Paso County's canvass process — the formal verification and certification of election results — typically takes two to three weeks. Until that's complete, the district can't start spending money or formally move on any projects. For some context, D11's 2021 bond attempt had a similar margin and wound up failing by just 11 votes once the final recount was done. So while the mood is cautiously optimistic, families should know the certification step matters.

Once certified, the district plans to use the funds to build a new Holmes Middle School, update HVAC systems at every D11 school, and renovate Palmer and Coronado High Schools, among other projects. These are facilities changes that will affect students for decades.

We'll keep tracking the official certification as it comes in. For now, we're cautiously celebrating — and watching the canvass closely.

### STORY 2: CPSC Recalls

**Long-Form Post (10:00 AM MT)**

Two product safety recalls were issued on November 5 that COS families should check right now.

First: the Baby Brezza SuperFast Sterilizer Dryer. About 29,000 units have been recalled after the CPSC received 32 reports of overheating or melting plastic housings. These units were sold for up to $180 at major retailers and online. The fix is a free replacement — contact Baby Brezza and they'll send you a new one with no shipping charge.

Second: Arlopu children's slide playsets. About 10,318 units were recalled due to a serious head and neck entrapment hazard. The design includes openings that allow a child's torso to pass through while preventing the head from getting out — which is exactly the kind of design flaw that leads to tragedies. These were sold on Amazon and Walmart. If you have one of these, stop using it immediately.

For model numbers and registration on both recalls, go to cpsc.gov or call 1-800-638-2772.

If you bought either of these products, what steps are you taking? Let us know in the comments.

### STORY 3: Veterans Day Parade Today

**Long-Form Post (8:00 AM MT)**

This one's happening today, so heads-up: the In Their Honor Veterans Day Parade steps off at 11 AM in downtown Colorado Springs.

The route runs south along Tejon Street from St. Vrain to Vermijo. It's free, it's open to everyone, and it's one of those events that hits differently in Colorado Springs — a city with more active-duty military families per capita than almost anywhere else in the country. Fort Carson, the Air Force Academy, Peterson, Schriever, Cheyenne Mountain — this city is woven with military service, and Veterans Day here feels like a community event in the truest sense.

Bring the kids. If they're old enough, it's a great opportunity to talk about why we set aside this day — and what service actually means in a city where so many families live it every day.

If you're not making the parade today, check out the Turkey Trails 5K at Bear Creek Regional Park tomorrow (Nov 8) — it has a kids' race for ages 15 and under for $15.

### STORY 4: Flu Vaccination

**Long-Form Post (3:00 PM MT)**

We've been covering the flu story closely this month, and it's worth returning to with some fresh numbers that feel pretty sobering.

The American Academy of Pediatrics' 2026-27 guidance recommends that every child six months and older receive an annual flu shot. That's not new guidance — it's been consistent for years. What's new is knowing that only 49.4% of children nationally have actually gotten vaccinated this season. That means roughly half of the kids in Colorado Springs schools haven't been vaccinated yet.

We already reported Colorado's first child flu death of the 2026-27 season on November 3. The child was elementary school-aged and unvaccinated. Flu season typically peaks in December and January — and with Thanksgiving travel just three weeks away, the exposure window is about to get a lot bigger.

Here's the practical part: flu shots are available right now at Walgreens, King Soopers, Safeway, and most pediatric clinics in Colorado Springs. You don't need an appointment at most of these locations.

We know families are busy — this Saturday might actually be a good day to get it done. Have your kids gotten their flu shots yet?

### STORY 5: COS Holiday Season Preview

**Long-Form Post (5:00 PM MT)**

November is moving fast and the holiday season in Colorado Springs is coming in hot.

Here are the dates worth putting on your calendar right now:

The downtown ice rink at Acacia Park opens November 13. Kids 4 and under get in free with a paying adult, which makes this one of the most accessible outings in the city for families with littles.

The Thanksgiving Turkey Trot 5K is November 26, with the kids' Tiny Trot 1K running alongside it. Proceeds go to the YMCA. It's become one of those annual traditions families look forward to.

The Nutcracker at Pikes Peak Center is November 27-29, performed with the Colorado Springs Philharmonic and the Children's Chorale. Tickets start at $40. If you've never gone, this is the year to try it — and if it's already a family tradition, you know to book early.

There's also Snowland at Great Wolf Lodge in Pueblo West, expected to open in late November — a good option if your family is looking for a low-key winter getaway.
