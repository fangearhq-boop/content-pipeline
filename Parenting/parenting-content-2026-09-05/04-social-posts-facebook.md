### STORY 1: Walmart Mainstays Dresser Recall (Tier 1)

**FB Long-Form — 8:00 AM MT**
If you bought a Mainstays 9-drawer fabric dresser from Walmart between September 2023 and March 2026, it's been recalled — and Walmart is reissuing the warning because some of these dressers continued to be sold through liquidators even after the original May 2026 recall.

The dressers have a black metal frame with nine brown or black fabric drawers. They're about 45 inches tall and weigh around 32 pounds. When not anchored to a wall, they can tip over and trap or crush a child. This is a STURDY Act violation, and the CPSC has stepped in to require the recall.

The fix: stop using the dresser immediately if it's not wall-anchored, move it somewhere children cannot access, and contact Walmart for a full refund. You can reach Walmart at 1-800-925-6278 or visit Walmart.com/recalls.

Do you have one of these dressers? It's worth checking before the weekend is over.

This year's airshow features the U.S. Air Force F-35A Lightning II demo team and the U.S. Navy F-18 Hornet demo team — these are the actual military demo pilots who fly these jets at airshows around the country, and watching them perform live is something you don't forget. ### STORY 4: AAP Screen Time Guidelines (Tier 2)

**FB Long-Form — 3:00 PM MT**
The American Academy of Pediatrics updated its screen time guidance in early 2026, and the biggest change is how they're asking parents to think about it.

Instead of obsessing over a specific number of hours, the AAP now asks one central question: "What did this replace?" A show that replaced boredom on a long car trip is different from a tablet that's replacing sleep, outdoor time, or family dinner conversation. The context matters as much as the clock.

Here's the current framework for parents:

No screens at all before 18 months. For toddlers 2–5, up to one hour per day of high-quality content — not just any screen time, but things with genuine educational or storytelling value. For school-age kids and teens, there's no specific hour limit, but the AAP recommends consistent family rules that protect sleep, physical activity, and face-to-face conversation.

The single most protective thing parents can do? Get phones and tablets out of kids' bedrooms at night. That one change, more than any time limit, is consistently associated with better sleep and better mental health in kids.

With school back in session, September is a great time to revisit the household rules around devices. What does your screen time approach look like this school year?

### STORY 5: CMZ Military Appreciation Week (Tier 2)

**FB Long-Form — 5:00 PM MT**
Colorado Springs has one of the largest military communities in the country, and Cheyenne Mountain Zoo is showing its appreciation with 50% off admission during Military Appreciation Week, September 14–20, 2026.

The discount applies to active-duty military, veterans, and retired military personnel — and all dependents living in the same household qualify too. So this is a genuine family discount, not just a single-ticket deal.

To take advantage, families need to purchase advance timed tickets at cmzoo.org/military and bring military ID to verify eligibility at the gate. The tickets are limited and they do sell out — especially as the week approaches — so if you're planning to go, book sooner rather than later.

Cheyenne Mountain Zoo sits at 6,800 feet above sea level and is one of Colorado's most visited family destinations. This is a great opportunity for military families in COS to visit at a significant discount.
