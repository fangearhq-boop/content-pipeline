### STORY 1: HARPPA Nordi Toddler Tower Stool Recall (Tier 1)

**FB Long-Form — 8:00 AM MT**

COS parents — if you have a HARPPA Nordi Toddler Tower Stool at home, stop using it now.

The CPSC just recalled 47,166 of these kitchen learning towers after reports of the stool collapsing or tipping over while in use. Here's the safety concern: a child's torso can fit through the openings on the front and back of the stool, creating a real risk of entrapment, falls, and strangulation. HARPPA has received three reports of the stool collapsing or tipping, resulting in two injuries — scrapes, cuts, and bruises.

The recalled model is the Nordi Foldable Toddler Tower Stool, model HANS0002 (check the label on the underside of the platform). These were sold on harppababy.com and Amazon from September 2023 through June 2026 for around $130.

The good news: HARPPA is offering a free repair kit that includes protective nets and stabilizing feet, with shipping included. You don't need to return the product.

→ Contact HARPPA to request your free repair kit. Stop using the stool until the repair is complete.

### STORY 2: Target Cat & Jack Toddler Sandal Recall (Tier 1)

**FB Long-Form — 10:00 AM MT**

Heads up, Colorado Springs families — if you picked up Cat & Jack toddler sandals at Target this spring, check the closet before putting them on your child.

Target is recalling approximately 211,000 of these sandals after reports that the decorative plastic pearls can detach. A loose pearl in the hands of a toddler is a choking hazard, and the CPSC has classified this as a risk of serious injury or death. Target has received 23 reports of pearls falling off; fortunately, no injuries have been reported.

What to look for: the recalled sandals are tan with two raffia straps, gold buckles, and plastic pearls. They come in sizes 5T to 12T and were sold from January through May 2026 at Target stores and on Target.com for about $20.

The remedy is simple: return the sandals to any Target store for a full refund. You can also request a prepaid mail label and return them from home. Call Target at 1-800-591-3869 (7 AM–10 PM CT, daily) if you have questions.

No receipt needed — just bring in the sandals.

### STORY 5: September Peak Asthma Season (Tier 2)

**FB Long-Form — 3:00 PM MT**

September is one of the most important months to stay on top of your child's asthma — and if you're raising kids in Colorado Springs, the challenge is a little different than elsewhere in the country.

Nationally, pediatric pulmonologists and the American Lung Association have long tracked September as the peak month for childhood asthma emergency visits. The combination hits all at once: kids return to school and spread respiratory viruses at higher rates, ragweed pollen peaks across the Front Range, and mold spores from fallen leaves rise in school yards and parks.

Add Colorado Springs-specific factors — 6,035-foot elevation (drier air that can irritate airways), and September wildfire smoke that regularly drifts in from the mountains — and you have a genuinely elevated asthma environment compared to lower-elevation cities.

A few things to check this week:

- Make sure your child's rescue inhaler is in their backpack every day.
- Confirm a backup inhaler or nebulizer is at the school office.
- Ask whether your child's asthma action plan is on file with the school nurse. Every child with asthma should have one, per AAP guidance.
- Watch for signs of increased coughing, wheezing, or fatigue — especially after outdoor recess.

If you have concerns, El Paso County Public Health can be reached at 719-578-3199.
