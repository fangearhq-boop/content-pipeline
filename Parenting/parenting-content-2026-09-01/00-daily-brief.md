### STORY 1: HARPPA Nordi Toddler Tower Stool Recall — 47,166 Units (TIER 1)
**Status:** NEW STORY
**Pillar:** National Parenting
**Angle:** The CPSC is recalling 47,166 HARPPA Nordi Foldable Toddler Tower Stools (model HANS0002) due to entrapment and fall hazards. The tower stools can collapse or tip over while in use, and a child's torso can fit through openings on the front and back — posing entrapment, fall, and strangulation hazards. HARPPA has received three reports of collapse/tipping, resulting in two injuries (scrapes, cuts, bruises). Sold on harppababy.com and Amazon from September 2023 through June 2026 for about $130. HARPPA is offering a free repair kit (protective nets, stabilizing feet). Families should stop using immediately and contact HARPPA for the repair kit.
**Byline:** Jamie Rivera
**Posting Window:** 8:00 AM MT

### STORY 2: Target Cat & Jack Toddler Sandal Recall — 211,000 Units (TIER 1)
**Status:** NEW STORY
**Pillar:** National Parenting
**Angle:** Target is recalling approximately 211,000 Cat & Jack toddler girls' sandals (tan, raffia straps, gold buckles, plastic pearls, sizes 5T–12T) due to a choking hazard. The decorative plastic pearls can detach from the sandals, posing a risk of serious injury or death. Target received 23 reports of pearls falling off; no injuries reported. Sandals sold January through May 2026 at Target stores and Target.com for about $20. Parents should stop use immediately and return to any Target store or by mail with prepaid label for a full refund. Contact Target at 1-800-591-3869 (7 AM–10 PM CT daily).

### STORY 5: September Is Peak Asthma Season — What COS Parents Need to Know (TIER 2)
**Status:** NEW STORY
**Pillar:** National Parenting
**Angle:** Pediatric pulmonologists and the American Lung Association consistently flag the week after Labor Day as the highest-risk period for childhood asthma attacks, driven by a combination of back-to-school viral spread, fall allergy triggers (ragweed, mold), and end-of-summer air quality. At 6,035 feet, Colorado Springs families face a distinct profile: lower humidity can dry out airways, and wildfire smoke (common September in CO) stacks on top of ragweed. Practical guidance: ensure kids' rescue inhalers are in their backpack and at school, update asthma action plans with school nurses, watch for mold in leaves as school playgrounds accumulate them. AAP guidance on asthma action plans. Contact: El Paso County Public Health 719-578-3199.
**Byline:** Jamie Rivera
**Posting Window:** 3:00 PM MT
