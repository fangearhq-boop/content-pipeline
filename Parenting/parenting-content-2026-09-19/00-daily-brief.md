### STORY 2: SHEIN Recalls Pull & Chew Montessori Teething Toys — Choking Hazard
**Tier:** 1
**Pillar:** National Parenting
**Status:** NEW STORY
**Platforms:** X, Facebook, Article
**Byline:** Sarah Morales
**Posting Window:** 10:00 AM MT

The CPSC has recalled SHEIN Distribution Corporation's Pull and Chew Montessori Teething Toys due to a choking and respiratory hazard. About 644 units were sold on SHEIN.com from December 2025 through April 2026 for approximately $10 each. The silicone strings on the toys are smaller and longer than permitted under mandatory safety standards — they can reach the back of the throat and become lodged, posing a risk of respiratory distress and death. Consumers should stop using the toys immediately, cut the silicone strings, and send a photo to productrecall@shein.com for a refund.

**Key facts:**
- Recall: SHEIN Pull and Chew Montessori Teething Toys
- Units: approximately 644
- Sold: SHEIN.com, December 2025–April 2026, ~$10
- Hazard: silicone strings smaller/longer than permitted; choking/respiratory risk
- Action: stop use immediately; cut strings; photo to productrecall@shein.com for refund
- Source: cpsc.gov

### STORY 4: 6.7 Million Kids Live in Homes with Loaded, Unlocked Guns — New Research
**Tier:** 1
**Pillar:** National Parenting
**Status:** NEW STORY
**Platforms:** X, Facebook, Article
**Byline:** Sarah Morales
**Posting Window:** 12:00 PM MT

New research published in AAP's Pediatrics journal estimates that approximately 6.7 million children in the United States live in homes with a loaded, unlocked firearm — a significant child safety finding with direct relevance for parents across Colorado Springs. The data highlights the importance of safe storage practices and is being highlighted this fall by pediatric safety advocates as part of broader child firearm safety awareness.

**Key facts:**
- Estimate: 6.7 million children in US live in homes with loaded, unlocked guns (AAP Pediatrics)
- Key action for parents: store firearms unloaded, in a locked safe, with ammunition stored separately
- Colorado law: no specific safe storage requirement but Colorado passed child access prevention laws
- Safe storage resources: BeSmartForKids.org, projectchildsafe.org
- Pediatric providers recommend discussing safe storage at well-child visits
