### STORY 2: SHEIN Teething Toy Recall

**FB Long-Form** (post 10:00 AM MT)

Heads up, parents: if you ordered a Montessori-style teething toy from SHEIN in the past year, check your purchase history.

The CPSC has recalled SHEIN's Pull and Chew Montessori Teething Toys. About 644 units were sold on SHEIN.com from December 2025 through April 2026 for around $10. The problem is the silicone strings — they're smaller and longer than what's permitted under mandatory safety standards, which means they can reach the back of a child's throat. That's a choking and respiratory hazard. Not a small risk. Stop using the toy immediately.

Here's what the CPSC says to do:

Take the toy away from your child right now. Cut the silicone strings. Then send a photo of the destroyed toy to productrecall@shein.com to get your refund.

This is a good reminder that toy recalls from online marketplaces are easy to miss. The CPSC's full recall database is at cpsc.gov/Recalls — worth bookmarking if you're a frequent online buyer.

### STORY 4: 6.7 Million Kids — Firearm Safety

**FB Long-Form** (post 12:00 PM MT)

This is one of those stats that's hard to sit with: new research published in the AAP's Pediatrics journal estimates that approximately 6.7 million children in the United States live in homes with a loaded, unlocked gun.

That's not a hypothetical risk. It's a number. And it's a number that pediatric safety experts say is entirely preventable with proper safe storage habits.

If you're a gun owner with kids in your home — or you know someone who is — the guidance is clear: store firearms unloaded, in a locked safe, with ammunition stored separately. The lock on a gun safe is the single most effective step between a curious child and a tragedy.

Two free resources worth bookmarking: BeSmartForKids.org and ProjectChildSafe.org both offer guidance, storage option finders, and free cable gun locks. Your pediatrician can also talk you through this at your child's next well-child visit — it's a conversation they're ready to have.

This isn't a political post. It's a safety one. What's your approach to firearm storage if you have kids at home?

### STORY 5: COS Fall and Halloween Festival Guide

**FB Long-Form** (post 3:00 PM MT)

October is basically knocking on the door, and Colorado Springs has a solid lineup of fall and Halloween events for families. Here's what we're watching:

The Venetucci Farm Pumpkin Fest runs from October 2 through 31, and it's one of the most beloved COS fall traditions — open Fridays 9:30 AM to 2 PM and Saturdays through Sundays 9:30 AM to 4 PM. Admission is $8 (kids under 3 are free).

The Miners' Pumpkin Patch at the Western Museum of Mining and Industry runs Saturdays in October from 9 AM to 4 PM. Online tickets are $13 and include all the pumpkin patch fun plus the museum grounds.

For Halloween specifically: the Emma Crawford Coffin Races in Manitou Springs are always a highlight — October 24, free to watch, and one of the most uniquely Colorado Halloween experiences around. The Old Colorado City Fall Celebration on the same day (October 24, noon to 6 PM) is also free and brings live music, pumpkin painting, cider and hot chocolate, crafts, and vendors.

The Promenade Shops at Briargate is also hosting a Movies Under the Mountains screening of Hocus Pocus this fall — date still to be announced but worth following them for updates.
