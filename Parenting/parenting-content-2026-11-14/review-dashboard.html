Taleco Gear Baby Jumpers and Swings Recalled — 41,100 Units, Fall and Strangulation Hazard
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post If you have a Taleco Gear baby jumper or infant swing at home, stop using it today. The Consumer Product Safety Commission recalled approximately 41,100 Taleco Gear baby jumpers and infant swings (recall #26-688, issued August 13, 2026). These were sold on Amazon, Walmart, and TalecoGear.com from April 2021 through July 2026. The products pose fall, impact, and strangulation hazards. CPSC received more than 52 incident reports and at least 32 injuries — including head bumps from falls. The remedy is a full refund from Taleco Gear. The catch: the item needs to be destroyed, not donated, resold, or passed along to another family. This is an important detail — it means the product shouldn't end up at a consignment sale or thrift store. If you're not sure whether you have a recalled item, go to cpsc.gov/Recalls and search "Taleco Gear" or recall number #26-688. Do you check cpsc.gov/Recalls before buying or accepting baby gear as a gift? 🛡️

What to Actually Buy Kids for the Holidays — Age-Appropriate Toys and What Actually Works
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Holiday shopping is in full swing — and with multiple product recalls active this season, it's a good time to revisit what actually makes a solid toy purchase for kids. Start with the age range label. This isn't just a suggestion — it reflects CPSC testing thresholds for things like small parts and choking hazards. For infants and toddlers specifically, look for no strings or cords over 12 inches, materials that can be sanitized, and no small detachable pieces. If a product's age range doesn't match your child's age, the safety assumptions don't apply. For school-age kids, the research consistently points in one direction: open-ended items hold attention longer. Building sets, art supplies, craft kits, and board games tend to outlast single-function electronic toys by a wide margin. Kids who can direct the play tend to stay more engaged. The simplest gut check before any purchase: Can you describe what the child actually does with it? If the answer is "plays with it," that's a flag. If the answer is "builds towers and knocks them down" or "makes cards for the family," that's a toy. What's one gift that was a huge hit with your kids — and one that totally flopped?
