How to Buy Kids' Gifts That Are Actually Safe and Worth It: Age Labels, Recalls, and What Works

By Sarah Morales | November 14, 2026 | COS Parenting

It's holiday shopping season, and multiple baby and toddler product recalls are active right now — including the Taleco Gear baby jumper and infant swing recall affecting more than 41,000 units sold through Amazon and Walmart over the last five years. Before you buy, before you accept hand-me-downs, and before you wrap anything for a baby or young child this year, here's what actually matters when it comes to toy safety and what's worth the money.

Age Range Labels Are Safety Thresholds, Not Suggestions

The age range on the box is not a marketing decision. For toys intended for children under age 3, the label reflects CPSC testing for small parts, cord length, and structural hazard standards. A toy labeled "3+" has been tested to confirm it doesn't have parts small enough to cause a choking hazard for a child three and older — but that testing didn't happen for a younger child. If your one-year-old receives a toy labeled 3+, the safety assumptions don't apply.

For infants specifically: look for no strings or cords longer than 12 inches (a strangulation hazard threshold that shows up consistently across CPSC recalls), no detachable small pieces, and materials that can be cleaned or sanitized. Many infant toys can't be fully sanitized, which matters for families with multiple children, secondhand gear, and anything that ends up in a baby's mouth — which is everything.

Check CPSC Before Buying or Accepting Baby Gear

The CPSC recall database at cpsc.gov/Recalls is free, searchable by manufacturer and product type, and updated with every new recall. If you're buying a baby jumper, bouncer, swing, or similar product — new or secondhand — a quick search takes two minutes and removes the guesswork entirely. This is especially relevant this season because recalled products regularly show up in secondhand markets and consignment sales. The Taleco Gear recall has been active since August, which means those products have had three months to cycle through buy-sell-trade groups and thrift stores.

What Works for School-Age Kids

For older kids, the safety question is usually simpler — but the engagement question is where most holiday purchases underperform. The consistent finding from child development research is that open-ended toys hold attention longer than single-function ones. Building sets, art supplies, craft kits, and board games outperform single-purpose electronic toys in sustained engagement, almost universally. The reason is straightforward: when a child directs the play rather than following the toy's script, there's more to do and more to discover over time.

That doesn't mean screen-based toys are always bad — it means that "flashy" and "new" are not proxies for "worth buying." A toy that does one thing in one way typically loses its appeal within a few weeks. A set of building blocks or a good board game can last years.

The simple gut-check: Can you describe what the child actually does with it in one specific sentence? "They build towers and knock them down" or "they draw animals and make up stories" means the toy supports real play. "They play with it" is a flag that you're buying the packaging, not the activity.

Quick Reference: What to Check Before Buying

Age Group Check First Red Flags
Infants (0–12 months) CPSC recall status; age range label; no cords over 12 inches Strings, small attachments, non-sanitizable materials
Toddlers (1–3 years) Age range label; small-part test; recall status for any gear (bouncers, jumpers, swings) 3+ labeled toys given to 2-year-olds; secondhand gear without recall check
Preschool (3–5 years) Age range label; choking-hazard parts; durability for active play Single-function electronics; toys that require constant adult management
School-age (6+) Open-ended vs. single-function; can you describe the actual play activity? "Plays with it" descriptions; novelty items without replay value

What's Next

The holiday recall season isn't over. CPSC continues to issue new recalls through December, and the volume typically increases as manufacturers surface problems ahead of the holiday sales surge. Bookmark cpsc.gov/Recalls and check it before any significant purchase or gift acceptance for young children. COS Parenting will continue to flag major recalls as they're issued — the Taleco Gear baby jumper recall (#26-688) is the highest-priority one active right now for families with infants and toddlers.
