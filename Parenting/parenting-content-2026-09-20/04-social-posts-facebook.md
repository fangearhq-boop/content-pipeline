### STORY 1: Jerify Magnetic Toy Recall

**Long-Form [8:00 AM MT]**

If your family has the Jerify Magnetic Stick Figure Toys — the little poseable human figures with magnets in their hands and feet — the CPSC is asking you to stop using them immediately.

These toys were recalled on September 17, 2026, after two children were hospitalized when they swallowed magnets that detached from the figures. About 38,507 sets were sold on Amazon between October 2022 and May 2026 for around $21.

Here's why this matters: when magnets are swallowed, they can attract each other or other metal inside the body. That can cause intestinal perforations, blockage, twisting of the intestines, blood poisoning — and in serious cases, death.

**What to do:**
1. Take the toys away from kids immediately
2. Contact Colerinsec for a full refund (you'll need to dispose of the toy and submit a photo of the disposed item)
3. Find more info at cpsc.gov

If you're not sure whether you bought these, check your Amazon order history. The 30-piece sets feature colorful human figures and were marketed as Montessori-style magnetic toys.

We'd rather share this and have families check than not — please share if you know someone who may have these.

### STORY 2: PRCS Fall Youth Sports

**Long-Form [10:00 AM MT]**

Looking for fall sports options for your kids in Colorado Springs? The city's Parks, Recreation and Cultural Services (PRCS) department has you covered.

The fall season programs include:

**Soccer** — for kids from PreK through 9th grade. One of the best ways to get younger kids moving on the field and building teamwork skills early.

**Flag Football** — for grades 1 through 8. A great lower-contact option for kids who love football without the full pads.

**Tackle Football** — for grades 3 through 8. For families ready for the full game, tackle football is available through the city's sports office.

A heads up: spaces are limited in some divisions, so it's worth registering sooner rather than later if your kid is interested. You can register and find complete program details — including practice start dates, fees, and locations — at coloradosprings.gov/youthsports or call the Sports Office at (719) 385-5940.

### STORY 3: Boo at the Zoo

**Long-Form [3:00 PM MT]**

Boo at the Zoo at Cheyenne Mountain Zoo is one of those COS fall traditions that families count down to every year — and tickets for 2026 are on sale now.

The event runs on seven evenings this October: October 16, 17, 23, 24, 25, 30, and 31 from 4 PM to 8:30 PM. Kids trick-or-treat at more than 20 stations throughout the zoo, with animal exhibits open for an evening experience that's a little different from your usual zoo day.

The night-time atmosphere, the animals, the candy, the costumes — it's a genuinely fun evening out. And it's one of the safer Halloween alternatives for families who want a structured, enjoyable experience for younger kids.

Here's the catch: tickets sell out every year. Advance timed-admission tickets are required, so we'd recommend booking as soon as you can. CMZoo members get a discount.

Grab your tickets at cmzoo.org before they're gone.

### STORY 4: D20 Accreditation

**Long-Form [12:00 PM MT]**

Here's some good news for families in Academy District 20: D20 has earned Colorado's highest district accreditation — "Accredited with Distinction" — for the 18th consecutive year.

That's 18 years in a row at the top rating from the Colorado Department of Education. The district serves more than 26,000 students across 40 schools in Colorado Springs.

If you're a D20 family, it's worth recognizing the teachers, administrators, and staff who show up every day to make that possible.

### STORY 5: Colorado Free Days October

**Long-Form [5:00 PM MT]**

October is a great time to take advantage of free museum access in Colorado — and Colorado Springs families have some solid options.

The Colorado Springs Pioneers Museum at 215 S Tejon Street offers free admission every single day. It's one of the few local museums where there's genuinely no cost barrier to walk in. The exhibits cover COS history, rotating art displays, and hands-on experiences kids enjoy.

Beyond that, Colorado runs a statewide "Colorado Free Days" program where participating institutions offer free admission on specific dates throughout the year. In October, that includes science centers, natural history museums, and other venues across the state — including options within a reasonable drive from Colorado Springs.

It's a genuinely useful program if you're looking to stretch your fall activity budget without sacrificing quality experiences for your kids.

You can find the full list of 2026 free days at coloradoparent.com or uncovercolorado.com — both updated annually. Worth bookmarking before October fills up.
