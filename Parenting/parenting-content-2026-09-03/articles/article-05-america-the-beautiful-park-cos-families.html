America the Beautiful Park: The Free Colorado Springs Spot You Might Not Be Using Enough

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting | September 3, 2026

It's on the western edge of downtown, it's always free, and it's got the kind of interactive play elements that eat up an afternoon without any effort on your part. America the Beautiful Park doesn't get nearly the attention it deserves.

Ask most Colorado Springs families to name their go-to free park, and you'll hear Garden of the Gods or Monument Valley Park. Both are excellent. But America the Beautiful Park — tucked in right next to Prospect Lake on the western edge of downtown — often gets overlooked, and it shouldn't.

The park is part of the broader Memorial Park campus, sitting adjacent to Prospect Lake and the Swim Beach. It's free, it's year-round, and it's stocked with the kind of features that make it worth a dedicated trip rather than an afterthought.

What Makes It Worth Going

The standout feature at America the Beautiful Park is "Quirks," an interactive art installation that's part play structure, part community art project. It includes colorful, tactile elements — spinner bowls that kids (and, let's be honest, grown-ups) love to spin in, hammock structures spread across the lawn, and turntable-style pieces that invite experimentation.

The spinner bowls alone are worth the trip if you've got a toddler or a kindergartner. Something about the motion is deeply satisfying for small kids, and you'll get far more unstructured time out of the installation than you'd expect.

The open lawn is also more generous than it looks on a map. There's room to throw a frisbee, set up a picnic, or let kids run without worrying much about them. It doesn't feel crowded the way some of the more popular COS parks can on a weekend afternoon.

Pairing It with Prospect Lake

One of the underrated moves in Colorado Springs is combining America the Beautiful Park with a lap around Prospect Lake — the walking and jogging path circling the lake is flat, paved, and about 1.5 miles. It's manageable for older kids on bikes, easy for a stroller, and pleasant enough that you might find yourself doing two laps without meaning to.

The lake itself allows fishing with a Colorado fishing license. If you've got kids who are interested in learning to fish, this is a low-pressure spot to try it — the lake is stocked, and it's right next to the park, so if the fishing doesn't hold anyone's attention, there's always the spinner bowls.

On a clear day, the view of Pikes Peak from the lake path is genuinely beautiful. That's not a small thing.

September Is a Good Month to Go

Early fall is one of the better times to visit America the Beautiful Park. The summer crowds have thinned out, the temperatures are more comfortable, and the afternoon light in September has a quality to it that makes the mountain backdrop look especially good. It's the kind of afternoon outing that costs nothing and feels like it should have cost something.

What's Next

America the Beautiful Park is at the northwest corner of the broader Memorial Park campus — the same area where the Labor Day Lift Off happens this weekend. If you're heading to the balloon festival anyway, it's worth arriving early and wandering over to the park before the crowds peak. The interactive installations are a great way to keep young kids occupied while you wait for the morning launch.
