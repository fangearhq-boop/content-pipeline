The AAP Wants Your Kids Vaccinated Against Flu by End of October — Here's Why It Matters

By Jamie Rivera | COS Parenting | September 3, 2026

The American Academy of Pediatrics has released its 2026-27 influenza guidance, recommending annual flu shots for all children starting at 6 months of age. With back-to-school well underway in D11, D20, and D49, September is the right time to book the appointment.

Every fall, the American Academy of Pediatrics releases its updated influenza recommendations. The 2026-27 guidance landed this week, and the core message is the same one pediatricians have been delivering for years — but this year it comes with some harder numbers that make the case more clearly than ever.

The AAP is recommending that all children without medical contraindications receive an annual flu shot, starting at 6 months of age. The goal is to have every eligible child vaccinated by the end of October, before influenza season typically peaks.

Why This Year's Numbers Matter

The 2025-26 flu season was, by CDC classification, a high-severity season. There were 190 pediatric deaths in the United States attributed to influenza. Of those children, 85 percent had not been vaccinated against flu.

That's not an argument pediatricians should need to make twice. And CDC researchers reinforced it with an additional finding: flu vaccines reduce the risk of a child dying from influenza by 80 percent.

In Colorado Springs — at just over 6,000 feet — respiratory illnesses can hit harder than at lower elevations. The combination of altitude, school hallways full of kids, and an approaching flu season makes the timing of this guidance particularly relevant for COS families.

A Note for Families with Young Kids

If your child is under 9 and has never received a flu vaccine before, they'll need two doses this season — spaced at least four weeks apart. That's the group the AAP specifically calls out as a priority for early scheduling, because they need extra lead time to be fully protected before the season picks up.

If your child is 9 or older, or has received a flu shot in a prior season, one dose is all they need. Either way, September is a reasonable time to book the appointment — vaccine supply is typically available by now, and scheduling early avoids the October rush.

Where to Go in Colorado Springs

Flu shots are available at pediatrician offices, UCHealth, Centura Health, Walgreens, King Soopers pharmacies, and most Walmart pharmacies across Colorado Springs. If your child is eligible for the Vaccines for Children program — which covers uninsured and underinsured kids — call your pediatrician or county health department to confirm availability.

What's Next

The flu vaccine is the single most effective thing you can do right now to reduce your child's risk of a serious flu illness. Check in with your pediatrician's office this week to find out when their flu vaccine supply arrives and whether you can schedule appointments in advance. The end-of-October target gives you time — but the earlier you go, the less you're competing with everyone else who waited.
