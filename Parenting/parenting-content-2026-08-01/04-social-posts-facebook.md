### STORY 1: D49 Student Success Center Opens Monday

#### Long-Form Post — 8:00 AM MT

District 49 families: something new opens on Monday, the same morning your children start school.

The D49 Student Success Center is opening its doors at the former Falcon Elementary School of Technology building. It houses four programs, and whether your family is affected depends on which program your child is in — or needs.

The preschool option is the one that affects the widest group. Two new full-time classrooms are opening on a Monday-through-Thursday schedule, and they're open to any D49 family, districtwide. This is not tied to your neighborhood school zone — any family in D49 can enroll their preschooler at the SSC. Registration is open through d49.org.

PEAK is the SSC's program for students who need a higher level of support for disabilities. The goal is a structured environment that supports a path back to the student's neighborhood school.

Elevates is for students ages 18–21 who need transition services. These services were previously provided through outside community agencies; moving them to the SSC means students receive services within the district.

BASE49 is before- and after-school care for SSC preschool students, also available starting Monday.

If any of these programs apply to your family, head to d49.org to get enrolled before Monday.

### STORY 2: CPSC Recalls — Baby Swings, Bath Seats, Magnetic Sets

#### Long-Form Post — 10:00 AM MT

Three product recalls worth checking this weekend — two with hazards serious enough that the CPSC says to stop use immediately.

The first involves baby swings made by Sanven Technology, sold under the VEVOR brand on Amazon. The swings have been recalled because they violate the mandatory standard for infant sleep products and the inclined sleepers ban. The hazard is suffocation. If you have a VEVOR or Sanven baby swing, stop using it and check CPSC.gov/Recalls for the remedy.

The second is the NFSVLB baby bath seat, also sold on Amazon. It violates the mandatory standard for infant bath seats. The hazard is drowning and entrapment. Bath seats were a common purchase for parents of infants in the last few years — this is one to actively check if it's in your home.

The third is a CPSC safety warning — not yet a full recall — on Ocensmice Magnetic Building Stick and Ball Sets sold on Amazon. The concern here is that the magnets in these sets exceed federal strength limits. The reason this matters: if a child swallows more than one high-powered magnet separately — which can happen with small building sets left unsecured — those magnets can attract to each other through intestinal walls. The result can be perforation, obstruction, or worse. The CPSC updated its mandatory magnet standard in 2022 specifically because of this hazard.

For all three: stop use, check CPSC.gov/Recalls for the specific recall page and remedy instructions, and contact the manufacturer for refund or replacement information.

D11: All students begin August 12.
D20: Preschool, elementary, 6th, and 9th graders start August 17. All other students start August 18.

### STORY 5: D11 Bond Vote August 5

#### Long-Form Post — 5:00 PM MT

Four days from now, the D11 Board of Education meets to vote on whether to place a $775 million bond on the November ballot.

This is the largest school infrastructure decision D11 has considered in more than 20 years. The last bond the district brought to voters passed in 2004. Many of the school buildings that would be addressed by this bond have been operating without air conditioning since before most current students were born.

The bond has two primary components. The larger piece is air conditioning for D11 schools between 50 and 100 years old that currently lack it. The second is teacher pay increases.

If the board votes yes on August 5, the measure goes to voters in November. If voters approve, the tax impact is estimated at approximately $6.50 more per month for a property owner with $100,000 of assessed property value.

There is some added context in the news this week about why this matters. Jenkins Middle School — a D11 school that has been closed for roughly 18 months due to structural failures (cracking and separating floors and walls) — is expected to reopen for students this fall. During the closure, 6th and 7th graders attended classes in portable buildings in the parking lot, and 8th graders relocated to Doherty High School. The facility challenges at Jenkins are a visible example of what aging school infrastructure looks like in practice.

The August 5 board meeting details and the bond FAQ are at d11.org.

Is this bond something your family is following?
