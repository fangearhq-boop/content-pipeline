AAP: Developmental Disabilities in U.S. Children Keep Rising — What Colorado Springs Families Should Know

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting | September 27, 2026

A new report from the American Academy of Pediatrics published on September 22, 2026 confirms a trend that many Colorado Springs families are already experiencing on the ground: the rate of developmental disabilities in U.S. children continues to rise. The AAP News report, titled "Number of US Children With Developmental Disabilities Continues to Rise," adds to an ongoing body of research documenting sustained increases in diagnoses over the past two decades.

Nearly 1 in 6 U.S. children currently has a developmental disability. That figure has climbed from about 12.8 percent of children in the late 1990s to more recent estimates near 17 to 18 percent. ADHD, autism spectrum disorder, and intellectual disabilities account for the largest share of that growth.

What's Driving the Increase

The rise in diagnosed developmental disabilities reflects a combination of factors. Greater awareness among parents and primary care providers has led to more children being referred for evaluation. Diagnostic criteria for conditions like autism spectrum disorder have expanded over time. Access to early childhood screening has improved in many parts of the country, catching conditions that might previously have gone unidentified until school age or later.

That said, researchers also note that some of the increase reflects genuine changes in prevalence — not simply better detection. The causes of those changes remain an active area of study.

What This Means for Colorado Springs Families

For families in Colorado Springs, the national trend translates into something very practical: waitlists. Pediatric neuropsychologists, developmental pediatricians, occupational therapists, and speech and language pathologists are in high demand across the country, and COS is no exception. If you've been told the wait for an evaluation is months long, that's not a local failure — it's a national capacity problem that reflects just how many families are seeking services simultaneously.

While you wait, there are concrete steps that can help. Ask your child's pediatrician to document the referral in writing as a medical priority, which can sometimes move timelines. All three of Colorado Springs' major school districts — D11, D20, and D49 — have legal obligations under the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA) to evaluate children who may need special education services. Requesting a school-based evaluation is free and does not require a private diagnosis first.

Community organizations like Pikes Peak Behavioral Health Group and the Autism Society of Colorado Springs chapter also maintain resource lists for families navigating evaluations and early intervention services in the COS area.

What's Next

The full AAP report is available at publications.aap.org. If you're in the early stages of navigating a developmental disability evaluation in Colorado Springs, the Colorado Department of Education's Exceptional Student Services unit maintains a guide to the evaluation and IEP process for families — search "Colorado IEP process guide" on the CDE website at cde.state.co.us. For children under 3, Early Intervention Colorado (eicolorado.org) provides free developmental screenings and services separate from the school system.
