ABC Trading Light-Up Children's Toys Recalled — 43,674 Units, Button Battery Death Risk
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post There's a new CPSC recall that COS parents need to know about before the weekend shopping runs. ABC Trading has recalled approximately 43,674 light-up children's toys — including light-up glasses, crown headbands, sparkle headbands, and light-up ties — because the battery compartments don't meet mandatory safety standards. Children can get to the button batteries inside, and button batteries are one of the most serious hazards out there: if swallowed, they cause internal chemical burns within two hours and can be fatal. These toys sold for about $1 each, which means they ended up in a lot of party favor bags, Halloween treat bags, and stocking stuffers. They were sold at ABC Trading stores in Los Angeles, specialty stores across the country, and on abctradinginc.com. Sales ran from September 2016 through May 2026. If you have any of these in your house — check by the model number on the outer bag (6602, HD-10, or L-508) or by the importer label "ABC TRADING, INC." What to do: Stop using them immediately. Photograph the item in the trash (you don't have to mail anything back), then email recallabc@gmail.com with that photo and they'll issue a full refund. The CPSC recall is live at cpsc.gov — and it's worth doing the check before those bags end up in trick-or-treat hauls. Have you seen these toys before? Any questions about the recall process? ### Image Post Caption ⚠️ RECALL: 43,674 ABC Trading light-up toys recalled — button batteries are accessible to kids and can be fatal if swallowed. Email recallabc@gmail.com to get your refund.

### Long-Form Post Something worth celebrating happened in our community this week. Galileo Middle School in Colorado Springs opened the "Ascend" food pantry and community garden, in partnership with Joint Initiatives for Youth and Families. The pantry operates market-style — families shop for what they need rather than receiving pre-packed boxes. AAP: Rate of Developmental Disabilities in U.S. Children Continues to Rise
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post A new AAP report published this week confirms something that many Colorado Springs parents are already feeling firsthand: the rate of developmental disabilities in U.S. children keeps climbing. The data shows nearly 1 in 6 U.S. children now has a developmental disability. ADHD, autism spectrum disorder, and intellectual disabilities are the main drivers behind the increase. These aren't necessarily new diagnoses appearing out of nowhere — awareness, access to evaluation, and changing diagnostic criteria all play a role. But the underlying numbers are real, and so are the waitlists. If you're in Colorado Springs and you're trying to get your child evaluated, you likely know how backed up the system is. Pediatric neuropsychologists, developmental pediatricians, and occupational therapists are stretched thin here just as they are nationally. Knowing the scale of what's happening across the country doesn't fix the wait, but it does mean you're not alone in it. A few things that can help while you wait: ask your child's pediatrician to document the referral as a priority, look into school-based evaluations through your district (D11, D20, or D49 all have special education support processes), and connect with other COS families who've been through the process. What's been your experience trying to access developmental support in Colorado Springs? ### Image Post Caption AAP: nearly 1 in 6 U.S. children has a developmental disability, and the rate is still rising.
