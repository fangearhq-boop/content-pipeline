Skip Hop Elmo Teether Recall — Choking Hazard for Infants (TIER 1)
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post COS parents — if your baby has the Skip Hop Sesame Street Elmo Silicone Teether, this is worth stopping to check right now. CPSC announced yesterday that Skip Hop is recalling about 22,660 of these teethers (model number 9R263210 — it's printed on the packaging). The issue: the black eyes can detach from the teether if it's been frozen. For an infant, that's a choking hazard. Four families have already reported the eyes coming off. No injuries so far, but the recall is the right call. What to do: Stop using it immediately and contact Skip Hop at skiphoprecall.com. They'll send you a gift card refund that works at Carter's, Skip Hop, OshKosh B'gosh, and Little Planet stores. The quick tip to pass along to every parent you know: silicone teethers should not be frozen — it can compromise the materials over time. This is a good reminder to look up any baby gear you've had sitting in a drawer, too. The CPSC recall database at cpsc.gov/Recalls is searchable by product type. Have you noticed any issues with teethers or silicone toys? Drop a comment below. ### Image Post Caption RECALL: Skip Hop Elmo Silicone Teether — model 9R263210 — recalled for choking hazard. Eyes can detach when frozen. Stop use now and visit skiphoprecall.com for a gift card refund. Pass this along.

### Long-Form Post The 50th Colorado Springs Labor Day Lift Off starts tomorrow — and if you've never taken your kids to see 65 hot air balloons launch all at once, this is the year. A few things worth knowing before you head to Memorial Park: The morning launch happens at 7 AM — get there by 6:30 AM. Seriously, don't miss the launch. That's the moment that makes it worth coming. Bring chairs or blankets. The morning grass is damp and you'll want to be comfortable watching the balloons go up. The Orangetheory 5K runs Sunday morning around Prospect Lake if any of the adults in your crew want to mix in a run. It's free. It runs September 5-7. ### Long-Form Post School is back, and here's something worth checking this week: when did your child last visit their pediatrician for vaccines? The AAP and ACIP updated the recommended childhood immunization schedule for 2026, and it's a good time to pull out your child's shot records and make sure everything's current. We're talking DTaP, MMR, varicella, polio, Hepatitis A and B, and several others — the kind of routine vaccines that tend to slip through the cracks during busy seasons. Colorado school districts including D11, D20, and D49 require proof of up-to-date immunizations for school attendance. Many families realize they're a dose or two behind when they finally dig into those records. If you're not sure where your child stands, Colorado's Immunization Information System (CIIS) keeps records that your pediatrician can access. A quick call to your doctor's office can save a scramble in October. Child Passenger Safety Week Comes to Colorado Sept. 20-26 (TIER 2)
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post A heads-up for COS families: National Child Passenger Safety Week is coming up September 20-26. The CDC reports that more than 1,000 children ages 14 and younger are killed in motor vehicle crashes in the U.S. every year. Of those, 43% weren't buckled up at all. But there's another piece most parents don't hear as often: many children who ARE buckled are in car seats that are installed incorrectly or that no longer fit them properly. Car seats have specific height and weight limits, and kids move through stages — rear-facing, forward-facing with harness, and booster — at different rates. A seat that worked last year may not be the right fit today. During Child Passenger Safety Week, fire stations and Safe Kids chapters across Colorado hold free car seat inspection events where certified technicians check the installation and fit for free. Details for COS events will be shared when announced — watch your local fire department's social pages. Before then, it's worth spending five minutes pulling out your seat's manual and checking the weight and height limits. Is your child still within the rear-facing range? Has your booster-age kid grown past the harness limit? What car seat questions do you have? We'll answer them in the comments. ### Image Post Caption National Child Passenger Safety Week is September 20-26. Colorado Springs Fall Family Activities — What to Put on Your October Calendar (TIER 3)
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Labor Day weekend is the unofficial start of fall in COS — and the calendar ahead is full of good stuff for families. A few things worth putting on your radar: Rock Ledge Ranch typically opens its fall programming in October, with wagon rides, pumpkin picking, old-time music, and tours of the historic buildings and blacksmith shop. It's one of those COS gems that doesn't get the attention it deserves. The Penrose Apple Festival is October 7, about 45 minutes from Colorado Springs. It runs from a pancake breakfast at 7 AM all the way to fireworks at 9:30 PM, with a parade, live music, food trucks, vendors, and an apple pie eating contest. Kids who can handle a long day love it. For hiking, the aspens will start turning gold on Rampart Range Road and the high trails above Woodland Park by late September. If your family's never driven the Gold Camp Road or hiked the lower section of Barr Trail in fall, both are worth adding to the list. There's also a Gather Mountain Blooms celebration on September 26 with flowers, music, food, and the first pumpkins of the season. What's on your family's fall list this year? ### Image Post Caption Fall in COS starts this weekend. Ahead: Rock Ledge Ranch, Penrose Apple Festival (Oct 7), gold aspen hikes, and more.
