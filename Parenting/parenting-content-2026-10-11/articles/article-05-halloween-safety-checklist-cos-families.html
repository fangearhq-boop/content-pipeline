Halloween Safety Checklist for Colorado Springs Families

By Jamie Rivera | October 11, 2026

Evergreen | Safety

Halloween is two weeks away, which is exactly the right time to run through a safety checklist before the night itself gets busy. Colorado Springs Police and the Red Cross put out similar reminders every year, and there's a reason for it: children are more than twice as likely to be hit by a car and killed on Halloween than on any other night of the year. The good news is that most of the risks are entirely manageable with a few minutes of planning.

Here's the checklist we run through for COS families, built from CSPD guidance and Red Cross recommendations.

Before You Leave

Costume Check

Check the costume length. Anything that drags on the ground is a trip hazard — especially on uneven sidewalks and dark driveways. Hem it or swap shoes if needed. Avoid full masks that restrict vision. Face paint and makeup work just as well for most costumes and let kids see clearly when they're crossing streets or navigating yards.

For dark costumes — which are common, especially for superhero and villain costumes — add visibility. Glow sticks tucked into a bucket or attached to the costume are inexpensive and effective. Reflective tape on the back of the costume or on the treat bag adds another layer. Drivers in low-light residential streets are not always paying attention, and a visible kid is a safer kid.

Plan the Route

Know where you're going before you leave the house. Colorado Springs Police recommend sticking to familiar neighborhoods where you know the streets and can navigate without looking at a phone. If older kids are going with a friend group instead of with parents, set a clear return time and make sure everyone has a way to communicate.

While You're Out

Stick to the Sidewalk

Cross only at corners and look both ways before stepping into the street — even on quiet residential roads. Drivers in Colorado have been reminded repeatedly to slow down through neighborhoods on Halloween night, but not all of them do. Assume cars can't see kids until proven otherwise.

Never Go Inside

This is a non-negotiable rule for kids of any age. Candy comes from the door. If a homeowner invites your kids inside to get their treat, the answer is no. This applies even in familiar neighborhoods and with people you recognize.

Adult Supervision

Younger kids need a parent or adult alongside them the entire time. For older kids going with friends, the threshold for what counts as "old enough" varies by family, but Colorado Springs Police specifically recommend adult accompaniment for younger children and a clearly set curfew for older ones who are going independently.

When You Get Home

Inspect Candy Before Eating

Go through the bag before anything gets eaten. Any candy that isn't in its original sealed wrapper goes in the trash. Anything homemade from a stranger — cookies, brownies, anything unwrapped — also goes in the trash. This is standard guidance from CPSC and CSPD alike.
