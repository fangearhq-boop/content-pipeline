Sweet Cubby Children's Loungewear Recalled for Flammability Risk — Over 12,000 Sets Affected

By Jamie Rivera | October 11, 2026

Product Safety | CPSC Recall

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission recalled approximately 12,461 Sweet Cubby children's loungewear sets on September 11, 2026. The two-piece sets — a short-sleeved shirt and matching shorts — fail the federal flammability standard for children's sleepwear, which means they burn more quickly and more dangerously than the law allows.

If you've shopped Shein for kids' clothing in the past year, this one is worth checking. The recalled sets were sold exclusively through Shein.com from June 2025 through May 2026, priced at $8 to $12. No injuries have been reported, but the number of units is large and the price point means many families may have purchased multiple sets without realizing the brand or product type.

How to Identify the Recalled Sets

The recalled loungewear sets come in children's sizes 5-6Y through 13-14Y. The batch number 272779 and the size are printed on the neck label and the seam label — that's the identifier the CPSC is using for this recall. If you have a Sweet Cubby two-piece set from Shein in any of those sizes, check those labels.

The sets are styled as casual loungewear — not specifically marketed as pajamas — but they fall under the children's sleepwear standard because of the style, fabric, and intended use. The federal standard requires children's sleepwear to be either flame-resistant or snug-fitting to reduce burn risk. These sets are neither.

What to Do

Stop using the sets immediately and keep them away from your kids. To get a full refund, you'll need to destroy the sets by cutting them in half and email a photo of the destroyed items to SweetCubby@163.com. The company processes refunds after receiving confirmation of destruction — don't throw them out before getting the photo.

If you ordered these as gifts or received them from someone else, pass the recall information along. The $8–$12 price range and the Shein purchase window mean many of these were likely bought in bulk or as gifts for school-age kids.

Why the Flammability Standard Matters

Children's sleepwear flammability standards have been in place since the 1970s after a wave of burn injuries involving loose, synthetic fabrics. The standard requires that any garment marketed or intended for children's sleep use either be made from flame-resistant material or be snug-fitting — tight enough to reduce the air gap between the fabric and the skin, which limits how quickly the fabric can ignite and burn.

Garments that fail this standard don't just catch fire more easily — they can also sustain a flame longer and spread faster, which increases the severity of burns. For kids who sleep near nightlights, candles, or other heat sources, the risk is real even when the probability of exposure is low.
