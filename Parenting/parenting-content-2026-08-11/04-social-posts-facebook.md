### STORY 1: Liuqibax Bed Rail Recall

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 8:00 AM MT

Heads up, Colorado Springs families — this is one to act on.

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission has recalled about 12,000 Liuqibax adult portable bed rails (Model BR-11) that were sold on Amazon between July 2024 and May 2026 for around $60. The hazard isn't minor: users can become entrapped within the rail or between the rail and the side of the mattress, posing a serious risk of asphyxiation.

The rails also lack the required warning labels that would have flagged this risk at the point of sale.

No injuries have been reported — but the hazard classification here is as serious as CPSC recalls get. If you bought a portable bed rail on Amazon in the last two years, it's worth checking whether it matches the Liuqibax BR-11 description. The rails are black or white, about 30.5 inches wide by 40.5 inches tall, and weigh 6 pounds.

What to do: Stop using it immediately. To get your full refund, you'll need to dismantle the rail, cut the safety strap and storage bag, write "RECALLED" in permanent marker on both the upper and lower rails, and email a photo to Liuqibax@outlook.com.

It's extra steps — but it's the process, and the refund is full.

We'll keep sharing recalls like this as they come through CPSC. Have you seen any other recent recalls that COS parents should know about?

### STORY 4: AAP Screen Time Guidance

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 3:00 PM MT

Parents, the American Academy of Pediatrics has updated its position on screen time — and it's a meaningful shift.

The new guidance, built on a review of hundreds of studies spanning 20 years, says that screen time limits alone are no longer sufficient to protect children's health. What matters more is the type and context of screen use: what kids are watching, whether they're doing it alongside a parent, and whether the content is relational and educational rather than passive or isolating.

Dr. Tiffany Munzer, a pediatric behavioral specialist at the University of Michigan Hospital, puts it plainly: "Simply taking devices away or enforcing rigid rules can backfire for parents."

The practical shift is this: instead of focusing on hitting a two-hour limit, families are encouraged to think about watching alongside kids, asking about what they're seeing, choosing content that promotes connection, and modeling the kind of screen behavior we'd want them to have.

Back-to-school season tends to bring screen time back into focus — with homework on devices, activities on devices, and downtime on devices all suddenly competing. This new framework might give parents a better tool for navigating those conversations than a daily hour count.

What's your household screen time approach — have you found what works for your family?

### STORY 5: D11 First Day Tomorrow

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 10:00 AM MT

Tomorrow is the day, D11 families.

Wednesday, August 12 is the first day of school for all Colorado Springs School District 11 K-12 students. Today (Tuesday, August 11) was Jumpstart Day for kindergarteners, 6th graders, and 9th graders — those transition-year students got a head start with a shorter, orientation-style day before the full school year opens tomorrow.

One thing a lot of families didn't know going into this week: D11 is partnering with Edukit to deliver free, grade-specific school supply kits directly to more than 14,000 K-8 students on the first day. That means if your child is in kindergarten through 8th grade, their teacher will have a kit waiting for them with notebooks, pencils, folders, and other grade-appropriate supplies — at no cost to your family and with no pre-shopping required.

The Edukit program doesn't eliminate every supply list item your child's specific teacher might need, but it covers the foundation. If you've been putting off the back-to-school supply run, this is the news you needed.

For families who still haven't enrolled: D11 enrollment is open at d11.org, or in person at 1033 N. Franklin Street, Monday through Friday, 8 AM to 4 PM MT.

A reminder for the rest of the district: D20 (Academy District 20) starts Monday August 17 for preschool, elementary, 6th and 9th graders, and Tuesday August 18 for all remaining students. D49 students have already been back since August 4.

Is your family ready for tomorrow — and did you know about the Edukit program?
