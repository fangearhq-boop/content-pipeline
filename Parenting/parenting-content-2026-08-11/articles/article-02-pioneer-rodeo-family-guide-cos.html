Pioneer Rodeo Returns Aug. 14–16 at Norris Penrose — A Family Guide for Colorado Springs

By Jamie Rivera | COS Parenting | August 11, 2026

If you're looking for a way to mark the end of the first week of school with something the whole family can enjoy, the Pioneer Rodeo has you covered. The annual event returns to Norris Penrose Event Center in Colorado Springs this weekend, running August 14 through August 16, with three nights of programming that includes rodeo competition, live entertainment, and a Demolition Derby finale on Sunday.

It's one of those Colorado Springs traditions that tends to draw families year after year — partly because of the rodeo itself, and partly because of everything that surrounds it. Here's what to know before you go.

The Schedule

Doors open at 4:00 PM all three nights, giving families a full three hours before the show starts at 7:00 PM to explore the grounds, visit the petting zoo, grab food, and let younger children burn off energy in the inflatable park.

Friday and Saturday nights feature the rodeo competition — bull riding, barrel racing, roping events, and the rest of the traditional rodeo lineup. Sunday night closes the weekend with the Demolition Derby Finale, which tends to be a crowd favorite with older kids especially.

What to Know About Tickets and Pricing

General admission for children under 5 is free. Children 2 and under are also free in box seating, provided they sit on an adult's lap. For everyone else, tickets are available through pioneerrodeo.com.

If you have younger children in the mix, arriving at 4:00 PM gives you time to settle in before the arena fills up and the show begins. The early arrival also means access to the petting zoo and inflatable park during the lower-traffic window before the evening crowd peaks.

Family Activities Beyond the Arena

The Pioneer Rodeo isn't just the competition inside the arena. The event typically includes a petting zoo for younger children, an inflatable park, a mechanical bull, and food vendors throughout the grounds. These extras tend to matter a lot for families with a range of ages — keeping younger siblings entertained while older kids focus on the rodeo, or giving everyone something to do during the pre-show hours.

Norris Penrose Event Center sits at 1045 Lower Gold Camp Road, which places it at the southwest edge of Colorado Springs near the foothills. The location is accessible from several directions and has on-site parking for large events.

Timing With the School Year

The Pioneer Rodeo lands on the first full weekend after D11 students start school on Wednesday, August 12. D20 students follow on August 17 and 18. For many Colorado Springs families, this weekend sits right at that transition point — the first week of school behind you, a weekend stretch ahead.

A Friday or Saturday night at the rodeo, or a Sunday demolition derby, offers a natural end to a big week. It's the kind of outing that doesn't require much planning but delivers the kind of shared experience families tend to remember.
