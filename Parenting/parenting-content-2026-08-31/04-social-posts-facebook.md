### STORY 1: Budget Baby Boost Crib Bumpers (Tier 1)

**FB Long-Form — 8:00 AM MT**
A safety warning that COS families with infants need to see:

The Consumer Product Safety Commission has issued an urgent warning against Budget Baby Boost padded crib bumpers, which were sold on TikTok Shop from March through July 2026 for about $16. The CPSC warns that these bumpers violate the federal ban on crib bumpers and pose a suffocation risk to babies.

Here is what makes this situation more concerning: Budget Baby Boost has not responded to the CPSC's request to recall the products or offer a remedy to consumers. About 141 units were sold before the products were flagged.

If you purchased padded crib bumpers on TikTok Shop in the last several months, check your crib. The recalled bumpers are round and padded, approximately 197 inches long with ties at the ends, and were sold in blue, gray, pink, and white.

The action steps are clear: stop using them immediately, remove them from the crib, and dispose of them. Do not sell or give them away.

Crib bumpers have been federally banned since 2022 because they can obstruct a baby's airway, even if the baby cannot roll over yet. A safe sleep environment means a firm, flat mattress with a fitted sheet — nothing else in the crib.

### STORY 2: Commonwheel Art Festival (Tier 2)

**FB Long-Form — 10:00 AM MT**

The 52nd Annual Commonwheel Artists Labor Day Art Festival runs September 5–7 at Memorial Park in historic Manitou Springs, just minutes west of Colorado Springs. Memorial Park in Manitou Springs sits against the backdrop of Pikes Peak, which means the scenery is spectacular even before you set foot in any booth.

### STORY 4: HPV Vaccine Ages 9–10 Study (Tier 2)

**FB Long-Form — 3:00 PM MT**
If your pediatrician has ever suggested starting your child's HPV vaccine earlier than you expected, this study is worth your attention.

Research published in the August 2026 issue of Pediatrics — the American Academy of Pediatrics' flagship journal — found that starting HPV vaccination at ages 9–10 did not increase parental hesitancy compared with the standard recommended ages of 11–12. Clinicians across the study reported similar levels of questions, concerns, and vaccine acceptance regardless of when they introduced the topic.

What this means in practice: offering the conversation earlier is not going to make the process harder. For families who have been nervous about the HPV vaccine or who have delayed it, this research suggests the early timing itself is not the sticking point.

HPV vaccination protects against cancers caused by the human papillomavirus. Starting at ages 9–10 allows for a longer immune response window before potential exposure.

Back to school is a natural time to catch up on vaccines. If you have questions, your child's pediatrician is the right first call.
