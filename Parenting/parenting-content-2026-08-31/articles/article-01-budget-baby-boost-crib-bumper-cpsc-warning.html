CPSC Warning: Budget Baby Boost Crib Bumpers Sold on TikTok Shop Pose Suffocation Risk

By Jamie Rivera

August 31, 2026

Product Safety

The Consumer Product Safety Commission has issued an urgent warning against Budget Baby Boost padded crib bumpers that were sold on TikTok Shop between March and July 2026. According to the CPSC, the bumpers violate the federal ban on crib bumpers and pose a suffocation risk to babies. Making the situation more urgent: the company has not responded to the CPSC's requests to recall the products or offer a remedy to consumers.

Approximately 141 units were sold through the social media platform's built-in shopping feature for about $16 each. Parents and caregivers who purchased these bumpers should stop using them immediately and dispose of them.

Product Description

The Budget Baby Boost crib bumpers are padded and round, measuring approximately 197 inches long, with ties at the ends. They were sold in blue, gray, pink, and white. If you purchased padded crib bumpers via TikTok Shop in the spring or summer of 2026, check whether the product matches this description and remove it from your child's sleep area immediately.

Why Crib Bumpers Are Federally Banned

Padded crib bumpers have been federally banned since 2022. Research shows that even babies who cannot yet roll over can press their faces into soft padding during sleep, obstructing breathing and creating conditions for suffocation. The federal ban applies to all padded crib bumpers regardless of material, thickness, or marketing claims about "breathability."

The safe sleep standard established by the American Academy of Pediatrics is clear and consistent: babies should sleep on a firm, flat surface with a fitted sheet — nothing else in the crib. No bumpers, no loose blankets, no positioners.

The TikTok Shop Angle

The fact that these bumpers were sold through TikTok Shop highlights a growing pattern the CPSC has flagged in recent years: products sold through social media shopping features and online marketplaces do not always undergo the same safety review processes as traditional retail channels.

If you shop for baby products through social media platforms, the CPSC recommends checking any product purchases against the agency's recall database at cpsc.gov before use. This is especially important for products that go into or near your baby's sleep space.

What to Do Now

Because Budget Baby Boost has not offered a remedy or recall process, the CPSC's guidance is to dispose of the bumpers entirely. Do not attempt to sell them, pass them to another family, or use them in any other context. They are not safe under any circumstances.

If you have questions or want to report an incident, contact the CPSC at cpsc.gov or call 1-800-638-2772.

What's Next

The CPSC continues to monitor Budget Baby Boost's compliance and may take further enforcement action. Colorado Springs families can stay current on product safety warnings by bookmarking cpsc.gov and signing up for email recall alerts. When in doubt about any sleep product for your baby, consult your pediatrician and reference the AAP's safe sleep guidelines.
