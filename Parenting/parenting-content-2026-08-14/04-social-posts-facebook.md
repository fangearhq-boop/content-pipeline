## STORY 1: WPX-4 Slide Playset CPSC Warning

### FB Long-Form Post — 8:00 AM MT

⚠️ URGENT: If you have a WPX-4 Slide Playset in your backyard or home, stop using it right now.

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission issued an emergency warning August 13 urging parents to immediately stop using Model WPX-4 Slide Playsets — indoor and outdoor plastic playsets made by Wenzhou Yidian Amusement Equipment that include a tunnel, slide, and basketball hoop. The hazard: the playset has multiple openings where a young child's torso can slide through — but their head cannot. That creates a head and neck entrapment risk that has already claimed the life of a 17-month-old child.

Here's what you need to do: stop using the playset immediately, and dispose of it. Don't donate it, sell it, or pass it along. The CPSC's instruction is to destroy the product so it can't be used again. This is a warning — not a formal recall — so there's no refund mechanism right now. The action step is simply: get rid of it.

If you're unsure whether your set is affected, check the model number. If it says WPX-4, it's the one.

Please share this post — another family in your neighborhood may have this playset and have no idea.

Have you ever had to dispose of a recalled toy? Drop a 👍 if you're sharing this with someone who might need to know.

## STORY 2: Goody King Magnetic Building Cubes Recall

### FB Long-Form Post — 10:00 AM MT

🚨 Recall alert for parents who ordered magnetic building sets from Amazon: Goody King Magnetic Building Cubes and Blocks are officially recalled.

The CPSC announced the recall August 13. Approximately 213,500 units were sold exclusively on Amazon.com between January 2024 and July 2026. Sets ranged from 45 to 300 cubes with forest, dinosaur, and unicorn themes. The hazard: the cubes can break apart, exposing the high-powered magnets inside. If a child swallows two or more magnets, they can attract to each other through intestinal walls — causing perforations, blockages, or twisting that can become life-threatening. This isn't a standard choke hazard situation. At least 27 reports of broken cubes have come in, and two children required surgery to remove swallowed magnets.

Here's how to get your refund: contact Yi Suen Commerce (the importer) to register for the recall. They'll send you a prepaid shipping label, and once the company receives your returned product, they'll issue a full refund. Stop use now while you get that process going.

If you're not sure your set is affected, look for the Goody King brand name on a magnetic cube set purchased on Amazon in that date range — that's the one.

Tag a parent who might have these in their playroom, and share this post so it reaches as many families as possible. Did you know about this recall before seeing this?

## STORY 3: CMZoo Little Paws Preschool

### FB Long-Form Post — 12:00 PM MT

🦒 Something genuinely new for Colorado Springs families with preschool-aged kiddos: a zoo-based outdoor preschool is opening August 24 at Cheyenne Mountain Zoo — and enrollment is still open.

Little Paws Preschool is Colorado's first state-accredited, nature-based outdoor preschool inside a zoo, accredited by the Colorado Department of Early Childhood. Classes are small — about 15 students — and all learning happens outdoors on the zoo's grounds. For 3-year-olds, class runs Tuesdays and Thursdays, 9 AM to noon, at $220.75 per month. For 4- and 5-year-olds, it's Mondays, Wednesdays, and Fridays, 9 AM to 1 PM, at $460.75 per month. Teachers hold early childhood credentials that meet Colorado state licensure requirements.

The curriculum is play-based and outdoors, built around critical thinking, collaboration, emotional intelligence, and resilience — developed through real exploration of the zoo environment. It's hard to think of a more unique preschool setting in Colorado.

The inaugural class opens August 24. If you're interested in enrolling or just want more information, reach out to littlepawspreschool@cmzoo.org. Enrollment is still open as of today.

## STORY 4: Jenkins Middle School Reopening

### FB Long-Form Post — 3:00 PM MT

After 18 months, Jenkins Middle School is home. 🐺

On August 12 — the same morning as D11's first day of school — Jenkins held a ribbon cutting for its fully rebuilt wing. Students in 6th, 7th, and 8th grade walked back onto their own campus for the first time in a year and a half. Last school year, 6th and 7th graders spent their days in portable classrooms in the Jenkins parking lot. 8th graders attended Doherty High School. It was workable — but it wasn't home.

The original building had never been properly anchored to its foundation, causing floors and walls to crack and separate over time, making the 7th and 8th grade wing structurally unsafe. Crews broke ground in October 2025 and built a replacement: 20,000 square feet, 20 new classrooms across two floors, fully anchored to a new stable foundation.

It's a building that will serve the Wolves community for decades to come. Welcome home.

## STORY 5: Palmer High Renovation

### FB Long-Form Post — 5:00 PM MT

🚧 Heads up for COS families who drive near downtown — Palmer High School's $100 million renovation is active now, and there are a few things worth knowing before your next morning drop-off.

Palmer High School, Colorado Springs' oldest high school (founded 1874), is in Phase 1 of a major renovation funded through certificate of participation funds. The campus is open and fully operational throughout — modular classrooms have been added to keep classes running — but the construction zone will affect traffic around the school. Phase 1 involves demolishing the current tech building and cafeteria and replacing them with a new academic wing, with completion expected in early spring 2028.

The biggest immediate change: Boulder Street, which previously ran adjacent to the Palmer campus, has been permanently closed. The City Council reversed its initial vote on this in May 2026, and the closure lets D11 finally connect both halves of the campus. If you used Boulder Street as a route near downtown, you'll need to find an alternate. On top of that, 12 new school zones are being established at middle and high schools across Colorado Springs this fall — so keep an eye out for new signage on your regular routes.

If you make drop-off runs anywhere near downtown COS, it's worth building in a few extra minutes until the new traffic patterns settle in.

Do you drive near Palmer in the mornings? Let us know which route you've been using — we might be able to share what others have found to be the clearest detour.
