CPSC Warns Parents to Stop Using WPX-4 Slide Playsets — 17-Month-Old Died in Entrapment Incident

By Jamie Rivera | August 14, 2026 | COS Parenting

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission is urging parents to immediately stop using Model WPX-4 Slide Playsets after a 17-month-old child died in an entrapment incident. The warning, issued August 13, 2026, applies to indoor and outdoor plastic playsets made by Wenzhou Yidian Amusement Equipment Co., Ltd., a Chinese manufacturer.

If you have one of these playsets — which include a tunnel, slide, and basketball hoop — stop using it now. Do not donate it, sell it, or give it away. The CPSC's instruction is to dispose of the product entirely so it cannot be used again.

What Makes the WPX-4 Dangerous

The playsets contain multiple openings sized in a specific and dangerous range: large enough for a young child's torso to pass through, but not large enough for their head. When a toddler enters one of these openings head-first or gets into a position where their body slides through, they can become entrapped — their head unable to follow and unable to return.

This type of hazard is classified as head and neck entrapment. It is not a fall hazard or a sharp-edge hazard. The structure of the opening itself is the danger.

One 17-month-old child died in such an incident. The CPSC does not release details of entrapment incidents to protect the privacy of the families involved, but the warning makes clear that this death occurred in connection with the WPX-4 model and the specific entrapment mechanism described.

This Is a Warning, Not a Recall

It's important to understand what the CPSC's August 13 action is and is not. This is a consumer safety warning — an urgent advisory telling families to stop using a product immediately. It is not a formal recall, which would typically include a refund, replacement, or repair mechanism coordinated with the manufacturer.

Because there is no formal recall structure in place, there is no hotline to call and no refund to claim. The action step is simply to get the product out of your home and away from children.

CPSC sometimes issues warnings in advance of formal recalls when the safety risk is immediate and severe enough to warrant getting the message out as quickly as possible. A formal recall, if one follows, would be announced separately.

How to Check Your Backyard and Playroom

The WPX-4 Slide Playset is a specific model from a specific manufacturer. If you have a plastic indoor or outdoor playset with a tunnel, slide, and basketball hoop, check the model number on the product or its packaging.

The model number to look for is WPX-4. It may appear on a label affixed to the playset itself, on the original box, or in assembly documentation if you still have it.

If you're unsure whether your playset is the affected model and cannot find the model number, the CPSC recommends treating it with caution until you can confirm. If in doubt, the safest choice is to follow the same guidance: stop use and dispose of it.

Check garages and sheds as well as living spaces. If a playset has been stored rather than actively used, it may not come to mind immediately when thinking about recalled or warned products — but the hazard applies regardless of whether it's in regular use.

What's Next

The CPSC may issue additional guidance or announce a formal recall of the WPX-4 Slide Playset in the coming weeks. If that happens, it would include information about any remedy — such as a refund or disposal compensation — that may become available from the manufacturer.

For now, the directive is clear: stop use, dispose of the product, and do not pass it along to anyone else.

Families can monitor the CPSC's official website at CPSC.gov for updates on this warning and any future recall actions. The site maintains a searchable database of all active recalls and warnings by product name and category.

If you know another family in Colorado Springs or anywhere else who may have this playset, share this story. In cases involving head and neck entrapment, a few minutes of awareness can make all the difference.
