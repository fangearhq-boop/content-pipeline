Palmer High School's $100M Renovation Is Underway — What COS Families Need to Know

By Jamie Rivera | August 14, 2026 | COS Parenting

If you drive near downtown Colorado Springs during the school day — or during morning and afternoon drop-off — you'll want to know what's happening at Palmer High School right now.

Palmer is in Phase 1 of a $100 million renovation, and the construction is active. The campus is fully open and operational for students, but the project is reshaping the streets and traffic patterns around one of the busiest parts of downtown COS. Here's what families need to know to plan their mornings.

What the Renovation Involves

Palmer High School is Colorado Springs' oldest high school, founded in 1874. The campus has seen additions and updates over its 150-year history, but the current project is the largest-scale renovation in the school's modern era.

Phase 1 calls for demolishing Palmer's current tech building and cafeteria and replacing them with a new academic wing. Construction on this phase is expected to be complete in early spring 2028. The rest of the Palmer campus — classrooms, common areas, athletic facilities — remains open throughout construction. D11 has added modular classrooms to the campus to keep instruction running without interruption.

The project is funded through certificate of participation funds, a financing mechanism D11 has used for major capital projects. No property tax increase was required for this project.

Boulder Street Is Permanently Closed

The single biggest traffic change for families near downtown is the permanent closure of Boulder Street adjacent to the Palmer campus.

Boulder Street previously ran through the area separating two portions of the Palmer campus, creating a physical divide that limited how the school could use and connect its buildings. D11 had sought to close the street for years to unite both halves of the campus. The Colorado Springs City Council voted against the closure initially, then reversed that vote in May 2026. The closure is now permanent.

If Boulder Street has been part of your regular morning route — whether for Palmer drop-off or simply as a cut-through near downtown — you'll need to find an alternate. The closure is not temporary.

12 New School Zones Across Colorado Springs

Beyond the Palmer-specific construction, D11 is establishing 12 new school zones at middle and high schools across Colorado Springs this fall. These are new speed zone designations, not reconfigurations of existing zones.

Families who drive routes near any of D11's middle or high school campuses should watch for new signage going up this fall. Speed limits within school zones are reduced during arrival and dismissal times, and Colorado law includes enhanced penalties for violations in school zones.

The 12 new zones haven't been individually identified in public communications yet, so the practical approach is to drive attentively near school campuses citywide until new markings become visible.

A Note on Palmer's History

Palmer High School takes its name from General William Jackson Palmer, the founder of Colorado Springs, and has served the city since 1874. It's a significant piece of Colorado Springs history — the oldest continuously operating high school in the city — and the scale of this renovation reflects both the building's age and D11's long-term commitment to the campus.

Students attending Palmer today are experiencing something rare: a major institutional renovation of a school that has existed since the city itself was young. When the Phase 1 work wraps in spring 2028, the campus will have a new academic wing that, unlike the tech building it replaces, was designed for the educational needs of the 21st century.

What's Next

For families who drive near downtown COS, the most immediate action is to identify alternate routes around Boulder Street and plan a few extra minutes into morning drop-off while construction zone traffic patterns settle.

D11 will communicate directly with Palmer families as the construction progresses and as new developments affect the campus or surrounding streets. Families can follow updates through D11's official communications channels and the Palmer High School website.

Phase 1 completion in early spring 2028 is the next major milestone. What Phase 2 of the renovation looks like — and what the full campus will be when the project is complete — is a story that will unfold over the coming years. For now, Palmer High School is open, students are in class, and the most important thing for the wider COS community is navigating the construction zone safely and patiently.
