Colorado health officials have confirmed the first child flu death of the 2026-27 season. The child was elementary school-aged and had not received this year's flu vaccine. State public health officials are urging families not to wait — the flu season is already underway, it's starting earlier than last year, and the highest-risk months are still ahead.

Last season was Colorado's worst on record for children. Eight kids died from influenza statewide, and 1,169 were hospitalized — both figures higher than any year since the state began tracking pediatric flu outcomes. That context makes this early-season death a meaningful signal for families in Colorado Springs.

Why This Year Warrants Attention

Colorado's 2026-27 flu season is arriving earlier than the 2025-26 season did. Flu typically peaks in Colorado during December and January, which means the highest-risk window is roughly six to ten weeks away. That gap is exactly why health officials are pushing vaccination urgency now rather than after the holiday rush.

The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends an annual flu vaccine for every child six months of age and older. The recommendation applies year-round, but effectiveness is highest when the vaccine is received before flu circulation peaks. Getting vaccinated in November still provides meaningful protection heading into December and January.

Children under five — and particularly those under two — are at higher risk for serious flu complications. Children with underlying conditions such as asthma or diabetes face elevated risk as well. But pediatric flu deaths can and do occur in otherwise healthy children, which is why the AAP's recommendation extends to all children regardless of health status.

Where to Get Vaccinated in Colorado Springs

Flu vaccines are currently available at most Colorado Springs pharmacies, including major chains like Walgreens, CVS, and King Soopers pharmacy counters. Most pediatric clinics in the area have doses on hand. If your child's regular pediatrician has limited availability, a pharmacy appointment is typically quick and walk-in friendly for children old enough to receive the shot without a separate prescription (generally age 7 and up; confirm with your specific pharmacy).

Parents of infants and toddlers should call their pediatric clinic to schedule. Some younger children need two doses spaced at least four weeks apart if it's their first time receiving the flu vaccine — another reason not to delay.

What to Do If Your Child Gets Sick

If your child develops symptoms consistent with flu — sudden fever, cough, body aches, fatigue, or vomiting — call your pediatrician before heading in. Many clinics ask families to call ahead for respiratory illness cases to manage exposure in waiting rooms. Flu testing is available and can help guide treatment decisions, including whether antiviral medication like Tamiflu is appropriate.

Antiviral treatment is most effective when started within 48 hours of symptom onset. If your child is very young, has underlying health conditions, or seems unusually ill, seek care promptly rather than waiting to see if symptoms improve.

The Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment is tracking flu activity statewide and updates its flu surveillance page weekly. Families can follow along at cdphe.colorado.gov for current season data as it becomes available.

What's Next

Health officials expect flu circulation to increase through November and into December. This week's confirmed pediatric death will likely prompt renewed outreach from CDPHE and local school districts about vaccination. Colorado Springs families who haven't yet gotten flu shots — for their children or themselves — have time to act before the season peaks, but not a lot of it.

If your children are already vaccinated, no additional steps are needed now. If they aren't, this week is a reasonable time to get it done. Most appointments take under 30 minutes from check-in to checkout.
