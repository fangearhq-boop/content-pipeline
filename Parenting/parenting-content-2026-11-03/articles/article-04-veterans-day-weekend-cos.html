Veterans Day weekend brings a few genuinely family-friendly options to Colorado Springs — including one that's free, one that gets kids moving, and one that earns some credit as educational. Here's a practical look at what's worth your time this Saturday and Sunday, November 7 and 8.

In Their Honor Veterans Day Parade — Saturday, November 7

The In Their Honor Veterans Day Parade steps off at 11:00 AM on Saturday, marching south down Tejon Street through downtown Colorado Springs. It's free to attend, it's family-friendly, and it typically runs about an hour — long enough to be meaningful, short enough to hold most kids' attention.

Colorado Springs has one of the largest concentrations of military personnel and veterans in the country. The parade is one of the more visible ways that relationship shows up in the community. For families with kids old enough to ask questions about service members and military history, it's a natural starting point for a conversation that doesn't require a history textbook.

Dress for a November morning in COS — temperatures at 11:00 AM can still be in the low 40s heading into mid-November. The Tejon Street corridor has coffee shops and restaurants nearby if the family wants to warm up before or after.

Colorado Springs Veterans Day 5K — Sunday, November 8

The Colorado Springs Veterans Day 5K runs from 9:00 to 11:00 AM on Sunday, November 8. There's a kids race for participants ages 15 and under, with registration at $15. It's a relatively low-key race — not one of the enormous events that require months of training — but a solid option for active families who want a structured outdoor activity with some built-in community context.

Registration details are available through the race's organizers; check Colorado Springs running event listings for current signup information. If your kids have run a fun run or short race before, this is a reasonable step up.

ANA Money Museum — 818 N. Cascade Ave

The American Numismatic Association Money Museum at 818 N. Cascade Ave. offers free admission for children 12 and under. The museum covers the history of coins and currency — which sounds dry until you're actually standing in front of a collection that spans ancient civilizations through modern U.S. coinage. Kids who have any interest in history, collecting, or "old stuff" tend to find it more engaging than expected.

It's a good add-on if you're already downtown for the parade on Saturday, or a standalone option on Sunday for families who want something indoor after the race. The museum is typically open Tuesday through Saturday; confirm current hours at money.org before visiting.

Talking About Veterans Day With Kids

Veterans Day is one of those holidays that's easy to explain at a surface level — we honor people who served in the military — but harder to make meaningful for young children. The parade is a useful concrete anchor: here are actual people who served, here are the organizations that served alongside them, here is a community that showed up to say thank you.

For older kids who want more context, Colorado Springs is home to Fort Carson, Peterson Space Force Base, Schriever Space Force Base, and the U.S. Air Force Academy — a military footprint that shapes the city's economy, neighborhoods, and community culture. That connection is visible in the parade in a way that's harder to convey from a textbook.

What's Next

After Veterans Day weekend, Colorado Springs families with school-age kids should note that D49 has no school on Friday, November 6 — the professional development day falls just before this weekend. D11 and D20 families should check their own district calendars.

Thanksgiving break for most local districts is still several weeks out. The Veterans Day weekend is one of the last relatively unstructured weekends before the holiday season picks up pace — a reasonable time to get outside, do something with the kids, and let the week's news settle.
