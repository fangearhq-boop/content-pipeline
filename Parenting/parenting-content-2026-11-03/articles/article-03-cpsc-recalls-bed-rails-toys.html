The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission has issued formal recalls on two brands of children's portable bed rails — HOMEAL and WYBITNY — due to a risk of entrapment and asphyxiation. Three additional brands sold on Amazon — KOOLDOO, Famill, and Yulong Tengfei — have received urgent stop-use warnings for the same hazard. Separately, ZMC Group has expanded a recall on light-up children's toys over a battery ingestion risk.

If you've purchased children's bed rails or light-up toys from Amazon in the past one to two years, stop using them and check the details below.

The Bed Rail Recalls and Warnings

Portable bed rails — the mesh or padded barriers that attach to the side of a mattress to prevent children from falling out of bed — can create a dangerous gap between the rail and the mattress if they're not properly designed or installed. The CPSC has identified that five brands sold through Amazon have this hazard. Children can become entrapped in that gap, which in the worst cases can result in asphyxiation.

HOMEAL and WYBITNY are under formal recall, which means consumers can request a remedy — typically a refund or replacement — through the CPSC recall process at cpsc.gov. KOOLDOO, Famill, and Yulong Tengfei have received urgent stop-use warnings. While not formal recalls, stop-use warnings from the CPSC carry the same urgency: the agency is directing consumers to stop using the product immediately.

All five brands were sold through Amazon. If you're unsure whether a bed rail you own is one of these brands, check the product label, your Amazon order history, or search the brand name at cpsc.gov for the specific recall or warning notice. Amazon order history is searchable by brand name under "Your Account" > "Your Orders."

Do not donate a recalled bed rail. Donating a recalled product passes the hazard to another family. Dispose of it or follow the specific remedy instructions from CPSC, which may include returning it to the retailer.

ZMC Group Light-Up Toy Recall

ZMC Group has expanded an existing recall on light-up children's toys due to a battery ingestion risk. These toys contain small, powerful button batteries — sometimes called coin batteries — that can cause serious internal injuries if swallowed. Button batteries can burn through esophageal tissue within hours of ingestion, causing injuries that may not be immediately apparent.

Children under five are most at risk of accidental battery ingestion, but the hazard applies wherever young children have access to toys with battery compartments that can be opened. If you have ZMC light-up toys in your home, stop use immediately and check cpsc.gov for the specific recall notice and remedy instructions.

What to Do

If you have any of the recalled or stop-use bed rails, take them out of use today. Don't wait until you've ordered a replacement — a child sleeping without a bed rail is safer than sleeping with one that poses an entrapment risk.

For the bed rail recalls, visit cpsc.gov and search the brand name to find the specific recall notice and remedy options. The CPSC hotline at 1-800-638-2772 can also help you navigate the recall process if you have questions.

For ZMC light-up toys, check cpsc.gov for the expanded recall notice. If you believe a child may have swallowed a battery, call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 immediately — battery ingestion is a medical emergency.

What's Next

The CPSC regularly updates its recall database as new products are flagged. Colorado Springs families who want to stay current on product safety notices can bookmark cpsc.gov and sign up for email recall alerts through the site's "E-Mail Subscriptions" option. The agency allows you to filter by product category, so you can receive alerts relevant to children's products specifically.

Recalls like these are a reminder that marketplace platforms with third-party sellers move products into homes faster than regulatory review can track. Checking Amazon order history for flagged brands when recalls are announced is a practical habit, particularly for parents of young children.
