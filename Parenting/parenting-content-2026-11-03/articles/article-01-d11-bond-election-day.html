Today is Election Day, and District 11 families are at the center of one of the most consequential local votes in Colorado Springs in more than two decades. Ballot Issue 4A — a $775 million bond measure — would fund significant capital improvements across the district's aging school buildings. Voters decide today.

If you still have your mail-in ballot at home, drop boxes are open until 7:00 PM tonight. The closest location to you is at d11.org/bond. You can also drop your ballot at the El Paso County Election Office if you're unsure of other locations. This is a mail-in and drop-box-only election; in-person voting is not available for this measure.

What the Bond Would Fund

District 11 has not passed a capital bond since 2004. In the more than 20 years since, the district's buildings have continued to age. The $775 million — if approved — would go toward specific projects that D11 has identified as its most urgent infrastructure needs.

The highest-profile item is a new Holmes Middle School. Holmes is the oldest middle school in the district and has needed replacement for years. The bond would also fund HVAC system repairs at every D11 school — a project that affects the air quality and temperature control in every classroom in the district. Palmer High School and Coronado High School would receive facility upgrades, and library renovations are planned across multiple campuses.

D20 families: District 20 also has a bond on today's ballot. Check asd20.org for information specific to your district.

What It Would Cost

District 11 treasurer Jason Jorgenson has estimated that the average homeowner in the district would pay approximately $22 more per month in property taxes if the bond passes — about $260 per year. The actual amount depends on each property's taxable value. Homeowners with higher-assessed properties would pay more; those with lower-assessed homes would pay less.

Context matters here. A 2021 D11 bond attempt failed by just 11 votes. That measure would have funded similar projects. The buildings that needed replacement then still need replacement now — and have had another five years of continued wear. The question on the ballot today is whether Colorado Springs wants to address that deferred maintenance at this scale and at this cost.

What's Next

Results from El Paso County's ballot count typically begin posting after 7:00 PM. Watch the El Paso County Elections website or the Colorado Secretary of State's results page for live updates.

If the bond passes, D11 would begin the formal project planning and bond issuance process in early 2027, with construction timelines varying by project. The new Holmes Middle School would be among the first major projects to enter design. If the bond fails, D11 has stated it would continue addressing the most urgent repairs using existing operational funds — a slower and more limited approach.

Either way, tonight will tell Colorado Springs families something important about how the community wants to invest in its schools.
