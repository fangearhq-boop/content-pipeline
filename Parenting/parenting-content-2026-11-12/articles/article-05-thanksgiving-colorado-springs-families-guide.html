From Gratitude Jars to Giving Back: A Thanksgiving Prep Guide for Colorado Springs Families

By Sarah Morales | November 12, 2026 | COS Parenting

Thanksgiving is two weeks away, and if you're the type of family that likes to build toward it rather than scramble at the last minute, now is a good time to start. Here's a practical guide covering four areas: volunteering in COS, getting kids into the kitchen, a gratitude practice that actually sticks, and the outdoor tradition that Colorado families are uniquely positioned to build.

Volunteer Opportunities in Colorado Springs

Two of the most established Thanksgiving volunteer programs in COS are Care and Share Food Bank and the Salvation Army. Both run Thanksgiving-specific meal and distribution programs that need volunteers in the week leading up to November 27, and both welcome families with older kids who can follow instructions and work alongside adults.

These spots fill up earlier than most people expect. If you want to volunteer with your kids, sign up this week rather than waiting until the week before Thanksgiving. Check Care and Share's website or call their volunteer line directly — search "Care and Share Food Bank Colorado Springs volunteer" for current signup links. The Salvation Army works similarly; their COS location typically coordinates Thanksgiving meal service and toy distribution, so there are multiple ways to help depending on your family's availability and interests.

If your kids are younger — say, under 7 — a food drive is a more manageable alternative. Many D11 and D20 schools run them, and helping a child pick out canned goods at the grocery store with a specific purpose in mind teaches the same lessons as in-person volunteering.

Getting Kids Into the Kitchen

Thanksgiving is one of the best opportunities of the year to give kids real kitchen work, and "real" is the operative word. A three-year-old who washes sweet potatoes or tears bread for stuffing is contributing. A seven-year-old can be fully responsible for the cranberry sauce. A twelve-year-old can own a vegetable side dish start to finish with a little guidance.

The key is matching the task to the age and then stepping back. Kids who have a genuine job — not just "you can stir" or "hold this for a second" — stay engaged longer and feel genuinely proud when the dish ends up on the table. That sense of contribution tends to make them more interested in the meal itself, more willing to try things, and more likely to ask to help again next year.

Some practical age-by-age ideas for Thanksgiving prep:

  • Ages 2–4: Washing vegetables, tearing bread or greens, using cookie cutters on pie dough
  • Ages 5–7: Measuring and mixing dry ingredients, peeling (with a Y-peeler and supervision), setting the table
  • Ages 8–11: Mashing potatoes, making cranberry sauce, full ownership of a simple side dish
  • Ages 12+: Baking a pie, preparing a casserole, coordinating timing on multiple dishes

Start the Gratitude Jar This Week

The gratitude jar is a simple practice that works better when you start it now rather than on Thanksgiving morning. The setup takes about two minutes: find a jar or box, cut some strips of paper, and put a pen nearby. Each day between now and Thanksgiving, everyone in the family writes one thing they're grateful for and drops it in. You read them all aloud at the table on November 27.

What makes it work is the two weeks of accumulation. Kids who've been depositing small notes every day feel invested in the ceremony. Things turn up that wouldn't come up in a conversation — a specific person who helped, a moment that mattered, something a child noticed that the adults had moved past. It tends to be one of the more memorable parts of the meal, and it's genuinely low-effort to maintain once you've started.

The COS Thanksgiving Hike

Colorado Springs families are lucky to have accessible open space year-round, and November hiking in COS is significantly underrated. The crowds of summer are gone, the trails are quieter, and the late-autumn light at elevation can be genuinely beautiful. Thanksgiving morning or the day after are both good options for a family walk that doesn't require much planning.

Palmer Park has a network of trails across skill levels and is easy to reach from most parts of the city. Red Rock Canyon Open Space offers more dramatic scenery with options ranging from flat accessible paths to moderate climbs. Both are free, both are open on Thanksgiving, and both give families a reason to be outside before the food arrives or after the meal.

Starting a COS Thanksgiving walk as a family tradition is easier than it sounds. It doesn't need to be long or ambitious — thirty minutes of moving around outdoors together before or after the meal is enough to establish a pattern that kids will carry forward.

What's Next

Thanksgiving 2026 falls on November 27. This week is the right time to sign up for volunteer shifts before they fill, start the gratitude jar so it has two full weeks to accumulate, and decide which trail you want to make your family's tradition. None of these take more than an afternoon to set up — the return on that two-week runway is worth the small effort now.
