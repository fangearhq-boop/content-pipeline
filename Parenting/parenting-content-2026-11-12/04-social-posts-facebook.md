### STORY 2: D11 $775 Million Bond Passes

#### Long-Form Post — 10:00 AM MT

D11 families, this one's worth bookmarking.

Colorado Springs voters approved the **D11 $775 million school bond** in the November election — the first time the district has passed a bond since 2006. That's a long time between capital investments, and the facilities list reflects it.

The two headline projects: **Holmes Middle School** gets a full $80 million rebuild, and **MacLaren STEM Academy** will see $24.18 million in renovations. A citizen oversight committee will be in place to monitor how the money is spent — which is a structure D11 is leaning into heavily given how long it's been since the last bond. 🏫

This doesn't happen overnight. What families should expect next: district-led community engagement sessions on project timelines, design phases, and how construction will affect day-to-day school operations. Holmes families in particular will want to watch for those communications.

The bond covers a range of schools and projects across the district. If your kid is at a D11 school that wasn't named in the top headlines, it's worth checking the full project list once D11 publishes the breakdown.

What school does your kid attend, and are you hoping to see it on the project list?

### STORY 3: AAP Guidance — Parent Substance Use Disorder

#### Long-Form Post — 12:00 PM MT

The American Academy of Pediatrics published new guidance this week that's worth reading — not because it applies to everyone, but because a lot more families navigate this than we tend to talk about openly.

The guidance, released ahead of the December 2026 issue of Pediatrics, focuses on how pediatricians can support children when a parent has a substance use disorder. The core message: take a nonjudgmental approach, build trust with the family, and treat the child's office visit as an opportunity to assess and support their developmental and emotional needs. 💙

Research is pretty consistent that kids in these households face elevated risks — developmental delays, trauma responses, behavioral challenges — but that early, sustained support from a trusted pediatrician can make a real difference in outcomes.

For parents going through this themselves, or for families caring for kids in this situation, it's a good piece to bring up at your next well-child visit. Your pediatrician has more tools than you might realize.

If you've navigated this, what do you wish you'd known earlier about the support that was available?

### STORY 4: CPSC HAUOMS Dresser Recall

#### Long-Form Post — 3:00 PM MT

Quick heads-up for parents: there's a new CPSC recall you'll want to check.

The **HAUOMS 8-Drawer Dresser** (Recall #27-068) was recalled on November 5. About 1,392 units were sold on Amazon for around $150. The model SKU is AJ_DJ504298_1 — if you bought a dresser matching that description, check the sticker on the back or bottom of the unit.

The hazard: the dresser can tip forward when a drawer is extended, posing a crush risk to young children. This is exactly the type of dresser tip-over scenario the CPSC has been warning about for years — it doesn't require much to trigger, and it can happen fast. ⚠️

**The remedy is a full refund.** Stop using the dresser immediately. To claim the refund, email a photo of the product to **recall@hauoms.com**.

Tip-over anchoring is worth doing on any dresser with young kids in the home — this recall is a good reminder to check whether yours are secured to the wall.

Have you already anchored your dressers, or is this a reminder to get that done?

### STORY 5: Thanksgiving COS Family Guide

#### Long-Form Post — 5:00 PM MT

Thanksgiving is two weeks from today. If your family's the type that likes to build toward it rather than scramble, here are a few things worth doing this week in COS.

**Give back while there's still time:** Both **Care and Share Food Bank** and the **Salvation Army** in Colorado Springs need volunteers for Thanksgiving week. These fill up — if you want to do it with your kids, sign up sooner rather than later.

**Start the gratitude jar now:** A shoebox, some strips of paper, and a pen. Each day until Thanksgiving, everyone in the family writes one thing they're grateful for and drops it in. You read them aloud at the table on the 27th. It takes about 90 seconds and kids remember it for years. 🫙

**Get them in the kitchen:** Even toddlers can wash vegetables. By 6 or 7, most kids can help mash potatoes. By 10, they can own a full side dish with a little guidance. Giving kids a real job — not just "you can stir" — changes the whole dynamic.

**COS tradition:** The day before or after Thanksgiving, families who can get outside on a trail are lucky to live here. Palmer Park and Red Rock Canyon are open year-round. November hiking in COS is genuinely underrated.

What's your family's Thanksgiving tradition — something you do every year that you'd tell other COS families about? 🍂
