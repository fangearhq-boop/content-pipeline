### STORY 1: 5Color Bicycle Helmet Recall — Long-Form

**Post** (8:15 AM MT)

🚨 SAFETY RECALL: If your kid has a 5Color bicycle helmet in size Small, stop use now.

The CPSC issued a recall on September 24 for approximately 324 units of the 5Color bicycle helmet, Model YD-001. The helmet violates federal safety standards for bicycle helmets — meaning it may not protect in a crash.

Here's what to look for: size Small, pink color, head circumference 18–22 inches, Lot number YD-260320 on the product. These were sold on Amazon in May 2026 for around $25–$26.

What to do: stop using it immediately, cut the straps so it can't accidentally be used, and email a photo to FiveColorCS@163.com to receive a refund. You can also call 833-382-6461, Monday–Friday, 9 AM–2 PM CT.

No injuries have been reported, but the product doesn't meet the mandatory safety requirements — which means it's not worth the risk on your child's head.

Have you already bought one of these? Leave a comment so other COS parents can see this warning.

### STORY 2: AAP COVID-19 Vaccine Recommendations — Long-Form

**Post** (10:15 AM MT)

The American Academy of Pediatrics just released its 2026-2027 COVID-19 vaccine guidance, and here's what it means for your family.

AAP recommends that all children ages 6 months through 18 years get the updated 2026-27 COVID-19 vaccine. The key point: it's one dose, regardless of how many shots your kids have had before. Whether they're fully vaccinated or have only had a dose or two, the recommendation is the same — get the current season's shot.

For the youngest kids (6–23 months), AAP specifically calls it out as a priority. For kids 2 and older, one age-appropriate dose of the 2026-27 formulation is the updated standard.

This is also a good time to review the full 2026 immunization schedule, which now includes clesrovimab for RSV protection and an updated HPV vaccine window (ages 9–12). Talk to your pediatrician if you have questions about what your kids may need to catch up on.

### STORY 3: Cool Science Festival — Long-Form

**Post** (12:15 PM MT)

The Colorado Cool Science Festival is coming to Colorado Springs, and the timing could not be better for a Saturday adventure with your kiddos.

The festival runs October 3–18, but the big kickoff is this coming Saturday, October 3: FREE Carnival Day from 10 AM–4 PM. That's free admission and free parking — plus a full afternoon of rockets, robots, slime, drones, virtual reality, and hands-on science labs. Science magic shows, live demos, and interactive stations are all part of the day. It's the kind of event where kids forget they're learning while they're completely absorbed in something that's exploding, spinning, or flying.

### STORY 4: D49 Purple Star Schools — Long-Form

**Post** (3:15 PM MT)

All seven schools in D49's Falcon Zone have earned the Colorado Purple Star designation — and that's a big deal worth celebrating.

The Purple Star designation is awarded by the Colorado Department of Education to schools that demonstrate exceptional support for military-connected students and families. It recognizes schools that have specific staff, resources, and programs in place to help kids who move frequently, experience deployment separations, or face the unique challenges that come with a military family life.

For families in the Falcon area, this means every school in the zone is equipped and recognized for that support. Colorado Springs has one of the highest concentrations of military families in the state, so having all seven Falcon Zone schools hold this designation is genuinely meaningful.

D49 also held its Fantastic 49 recognition event on September 10, honoring two Colorado-based companies and two members of Team 49 for their contributions to the district.

### STORY 5: Fall Events Guide — Long-Form

**Post** (5:15 PM MT)

Two Colorado Springs fall events are coming up fast, and both are worth adding to your family calendar.

First up: Gather Mountain Blooms Oktoberfest on October 3–4, 2026 (yes, this Saturday and Sunday), 10 AM–6 PM at 5210 S. U.S. Hwy 85-87. General admission is $8, but kids three and under are free. The venue will have flower fields, pumpkin picking, hayrides, kids games, an artisan makers market, food trucks, and a biergarten with local craft beers for the grown-ups. It's a full afternoon out.

Then mark October 24 on your calendar: the 32nd Annual Emma Crawford Coffin Races in Downtown Manitou Springs. Teams of five race decorated coffins down Manitou Avenue in full costume — it draws around 10,000 people every year, and it's one of those COS-area traditions that's genuinely unforgettable for kids. The event is confirmed to go ahead this year after the city resolved a security planning dispute in June. Spectating is free; team entry is $100 with a deadline of October 22.
