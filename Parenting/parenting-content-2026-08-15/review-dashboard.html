Taleco Gear Baby Jumpers and Swings Recalled — ~41,100 Units, Fall and Strangulation Hazards
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post 🚨 If you have a Taleco Gear baby jumper or swing at home, please stop using it right now. The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission announced a recall on August 13 covering approximately 41,100 Taleco Gear baby jumpers and swings sold in the United States (and 150 more in Canada). The products were sold on Amazon.com, Walmart.com, and TalecoGear.com between April 2021 and July 2026 for $65 to $120. The recall covers three separate hazards: units that become unstable (fall and impact risk), hanging restraint straps that create a strangulation hazard, and seat openings sized to create an entrapment risk for infants. There have been 52 reported incidents and 32 injuries. Here's how to find out if your product is affected: check the fabric wash/care label inside the seat cover. The model number will be printed there. If your product is recalled, stop using it immediately and contact Taleco Gear to arrange a full refund. The CPSC's instruction is to destroy the product — not donate it, not resell it, not pass it along to another family. Please share this post. These products were sold across multiple major retailers over five years, and not every parent who bought one will see this recall automatically. Did you know this one was recalled? Have you ever had to act on a product recall? 👇 ### Image Post Caption 🚨 RECALL ALERT: Taleco Gear baby jumpers and swings — ~41,100 units recalled August 13. Hazards include falls, strangulation, and entrapment. Sold on Amazon, Walmart, and TalecoGear.com from April 2021 to July 2026. Stop use immediately. Full refund available. Model number is on the fabric label inside the seat cover. Full story at the link.

AAP Updates Screen Time Guidelines — What Colorado Springs Parents Need to Know
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post 📱 The American Academy of Pediatrics has updated its screen time guidance — and it's a lot more nuanced than "no more than X minutes per day." The core recommendations: no screens before 18 months, with the exception of video calls with family. For kids ages 2-5, up to one hour of high-quality programming per day is the guideline. For older children and teens, the focus shifts from strict time limits to balance — making sure screens don't crowd out sleep, physical activity, family time, or unstructured play. The AAP also emphasizes two practical habits: device-free bedrooms and mealtimes (both for kids and parents), and co-viewing — watching together, talking about what you're seeing, asking questions. Research increasingly shows that how families engage with screens matters as much as how much time is spent on them. There's a newer framing the AAP uses that's worth keeping in mind: "balance over burnout." The goal isn't zero screens — it's making sure that when screens are part of the day, they're not the only part. We're all figuring this out in real time. What's your screen time approach at home right now? No judgment — genuinely curious. 💬 ### Image Post Caption 📱 The AAP has updated its screen time guidelines.
