### STORY 1: Gizoon + Alanca Dresser Recalls (Tier 1)

**FB Long-Form — 8:00 AM MT**
If you haven't already checked the dressers in your kids' bedrooms this week, now is the time.

Two more dresser recalls have been added to an already busy week for the CPSC. Gizoon dressers — about 4,396 units — were recalled on September 3, 2026, because they can tip over and trap or crush children when not anchored to a wall. Separately, Alanca-branded 6-drawer wood dressers — about 261 units sold on Wayfair from April 2025 through June 2026 in brown and white — carry the same tip-over entrapment hazard.

That's three separate dresser tip-over recalls in one week: Walmart's Mainstays 9-drawer fabric dresser (covered Friday), the Gizoon, and now the Alanca.

The fix for all three is the same: stop using the dresser immediately if it isn't bolted to the wall, move it somewhere kids can't access, and contact the retailer or CPSC for a remedy. For the Gizoon and Alanca, check cpsc.gov/Recalls for the specific recall pages and instructions.

A furniture strap costs a few dollars and a few minutes. Have you anchored the tall furniture in your kids' rooms?

### STORY 2: Labor Day Lift Off Day 2 (Tier 1)

**FB Long-Form — 10:00 AM MT**
Sunday at the Labor Day Lift Off is usually the best day — and this year is no exception.

Morning balloon launches are at 7 AM from Memorial Park. If you want to watch the balloons actually ascend, arrive by 6:30 AM to get a good viewing spot. This is the second of two launch mornings, and the energy is always different on the last full day — the balloonists know it's almost over.

This evening brings the final Balloon Glow of the 50th anniversary weekend. After sunset, all 65 hot air balloons light up at the same time. It's the kind of thing that sounds like it'll be cheesy and turns out to be genuinely stunning — especially for kids who've never seen it before.

A few reminders for today: admission is free, parking starts at $5. Tethered balloon rides are available for kids 5 and older through Rainbow Ryders. The Wings of Blue skydiving team and the Grizzly drone show are also on the schedule.

Monday is the final day of the festival — a lower-key wrap-up. If you're going to do one day, make it Sunday.

### STORY 5: COS Park System Master Plan (Tier 2)

**FB Long-Form — 5:00 PM MT**
Here's a civic opportunity that directly affects COS families, and the window to participate closes September 22.

The City of Colorado Springs Parks, Recreation and Cultural Services Department has released the draft Park System Master Plan — the document that will guide how the city maintains parks, builds trails, expands recreation options, and makes spending decisions for the next 10 to 15 years. It updates the 2014 plan that's been guiding the city for over a decade.

The public comment period is open now through September 22. The Parks Advisory Board reviews the draft on Thursday, September 10.

This is how playgrounds get built, how trail connections get funded, and how community centers stay open. The PRCS serves over 300,000 Colorado Springs residents annually through three community centers, seven sports complexes, and five program areas. That infrastructure doesn't shape itself — it gets shaped by the plans that the city adopts, and those plans reflect what the community asks for.

If you want to weigh in, visit coloradosprings.gov and search for "Park System Master Plan" to find the draft and the feedback form.

What's one park or trail improvement you'd love to see in your part of Colorado Springs?

### STORY 6: AAP COVID-19 Vaccines 2026–2027 (Tier 3)

**FB Long-Form — 6:00 PM MT**
The American Academy of Pediatrics has released its updated COVID-19 vaccine guidance for the 2026–2027 season, and it's worth knowing before you head into fall wellness visits.

The AAP recommends the 2026–2027 COVID-19 vaccine for all infants and children 6 through 23 months of age. Children 6 months through 18 years who are moderately or severely immunocompromised are also recommended to receive the vaccine. For children ages 2 through 18 who aren't in a higher-risk group, parents can discuss a single dose with their pediatrician.

If your kids are due for fall wellness visits, this is a good topic to raise. Flu vaccine guidance is also updated for this season — combining the conversation saves time.
