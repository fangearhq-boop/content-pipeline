Labor Day Lift Off Day 2: Tonight's the Last Balloon Glow — What to Know Before You Go

By Jamie Rivera | September 6, 2026

Sunday is typically the best single day at the Labor Day Lift Off at Memorial Park — you get the morning balloon launches and the final evening Balloon Glow of the 50th anniversary weekend. Here's what to plan for.

The 50th Labor Day Lift Off is in full swing at Memorial Park, and Sunday has a strong argument for being the best day of the three. You get both the morning launch and the evening Balloon Glow — the part that tends to stick with families long after the weekend is over.

If you made it out Saturday for Day 1, you know what the atmosphere is like. If you're going for the first time today, here's what the day looks like and how to make the most of it.

Morning Launches — 7 AM

Balloon launches are scheduled for 7 AM, weather permitting. Morning ascensions are the most dramatic part of the festival in terms of pure spectacle — 65 balloons in various colors inflating and lifting off from Memorial Park's east fields is something that photos don't fully capture.

Arrive by 6:30 AM if you want to be close enough to watch the inflation up close rather than from a distance. By the time launches begin, the field fills up quickly. The launches depend on wind conditions, so pay attention to the festival's social media channels for any weather-related updates if you're setting an early alarm.

Afternoon Activities

Between the morning launches and the evening glow, there's plenty to keep families occupied. Tethered balloon rides through Rainbow Ryders are available for kids ages 5 and older. The Wings of Blue parachute team from the U.S. Air Force Academy is performing this weekend, and the Grizzly drone show is also on the schedule.

Memorial Park has ample space for families to spread out, and the festival atmosphere around the food vendors and activity areas stays active throughout the afternoon.

The Final Balloon Glow — Tonight at Dusk

The Balloon Glow happens both Saturday and Sunday evenings, synchronized with sunset. Tonight is the final Glow of the 50th anniversary weekend. All 65 balloons simultaneously light up from their burners, creating that golden-amber glow against the darkening sky that's become one of the signature images of this festival.

There's no set clock time for the Glow — it starts at dusk, which shifts by a few minutes each evening. Arrive at least 30 minutes before expected sunset to find a good viewing spot. Admission to watch the Glow is free.

What's Next

Monday is the final day of the 50th Labor Day Lift Off. Monday's programming is typically lower-key than Saturday and Sunday — fewer vendors, no evening Glow — but there is a final morning launch attempt, weather permitting. If you can't make it today and want one more shot at the morning launches, Monday is your option. The festival's social media channels are the fastest place to get weather updates or any schedule changes before you head out.
