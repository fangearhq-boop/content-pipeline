Two More Dressers Recalled for Tip-Over Hazard — Gizoon and Alanca Added to CPSC List

By Sarah Morales | September 6, 2026

The CPSC recalled Gizoon dressers on September 3, 2026, and Alanca-branded 6-drawer wood dressers have also been flagged — both for the same tip-over and entrapment hazard that applies to hundreds of thousands of units already pulled from shelves this week.

If you feel like you've been hearing about a lot of dresser recalls lately, it's because you have. The week of September 3 brought three separate tip-over hazard recalls for furniture sold in or near kids' bedrooms. Yesterday we covered the Walmart Mainstays 9-drawer fabric dresser recall. Today, two more products have been added to the list.

The pattern is worth understanding: furniture tip-overs are one of the leading causes of furniture-related injuries and deaths among young children in the United States. The STURDY Act, passed in 2022, established mandatory stability standards for clothing storage units — and enforcement has accelerated. The recalls you're seeing this week are not coincidences. They're the result of ongoing CPSC testing and compliance checks.

Gizoon Dressers — Recalled September 3, 2026

Approximately 4,396 Gizoon dressers were recalled by the CPSC on September 3, 2026. The recall covers units that can tip over and trap or crush a child when not properly anchored to a wall. Consumers are advised to stop using the dressers immediately if they are not anchored.

For the specific recall page, product identification details, and remedy information, visit cpsc.gov/Recalls and search for Gizoon. If you're unsure whether you have a recalled unit, the recall page includes identifying information to help you confirm.

Alanca 6-Drawer Wood Dressers — Wayfair, April 2025–June 2026

The Alanca-branded 6-drawer wood dressers were sold on Wayfair.com from April 2025 through June 2026 in brown and white finishes. Approximately 261 units were sold. Like the Gizoon, these dressers can tip over and trap or crush a child if not anchored to a wall.

If you purchased a dresser fitting this description from Wayfair in this time period, stop using it immediately unless it's secured to a wall stud, and check cpsc.gov/Recalls for the specific remedy and contact instructions.

The Anchor Solution — and Why It Matters

All three of this week's dresser recalls share the same underlying hazard and the same fix: anchor the dresser to a wall. Furniture anti-tip straps are available at most hardware stores for a few dollars. The installation takes about 10 minutes with a stud finder and a drill.

The reality is that many families have furniture they bought years ago that was never anchored — not because they didn't care, but because it didn't come with a reminder and there was always something more pressing. These recalls are a concrete, urgent reason to do it now.

What's Next

Check every tall piece of furniture in your home — especially anything in kids' bedrooms. If it's not anchored to a wall stud, it's a tip-over risk regardless of whether it's on a current recall list. For the Gizoon and Alanca recalls specifically, visit cpsc.gov/Recalls for the remedy instructions. If you have the Walmart Mainstays dresser covered in Friday's story, contact Walmart at 1-800-925-6278 or Walmart.com/recalls for a full refund. The CPSC updates its recall database regularly — check back if you're unsure about any other furniture in your home.
