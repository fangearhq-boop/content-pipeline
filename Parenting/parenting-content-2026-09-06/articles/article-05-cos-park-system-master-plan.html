COS Families Have Until September 22 to Shape the Next 15 Years of Colorado Springs Parks

By Sarah Morales | September 6, 2026

The City of Colorado Springs has released the draft Park System Master Plan — the document that will guide parks, trails, recreation centers, and open spaces for the next 10 to 15 years. The public comment period is open through September 22, 2026.

If you've ever stood in a neighborhood park and thought "this playground really needs an update" or "I wish there was a trail connection here," this is the moment the city is actually asking for that input.

The Colorado Springs Parks, Recreation and Cultural Services (PRCS) Department has released the draft Park System Master Plan for public review. The plan updates the 2014 document that has guided the city's parks infrastructure for over a decade, and it will shape how the city allocates resources for parks, trails, open spaces, and cultural services for the next 10 to 15 years. The public comment period runs through September 22, 2026.

What the Plan Covers

The Park System Master Plan addresses the full scope of what the PRCS manages: neighborhood parks and regional parks, trail networks, sports complexes, community centers, and natural open spaces throughout Colorado Springs. The department currently operates three community centers, seven sports complexes, and five program areas, serving over 300,000 Colorado Springs residents annually through youth and adult sports, health and wellness programs, and adaptive recreation.

The plan is intended to guide decisions about maintaining existing infrastructure, planning for the growth that's happening across the city's eastern and northern corridors, expanding recreation opportunities where gaps exist, and protecting natural and cultural resources.

Why Families Should Weigh In

Plans like this are the mechanism by which playground replacements, trail extensions, and community center programming get funded. When the city looks at its budget in 2027 or 2029 and decides which parks projects to prioritize, it will be working from this document. The parks that benefit tend to be the ones that showed up in the community feedback as priorities.

The Parks Advisory Board is scheduled to review the draft at their meeting on Thursday, September 10. That's an additional opportunity for community members to attend and observe, or for the board's discussion to surface specific issues that families raised during the comment period.

How to Participate

The public comment period is open through September 22, 2026. To find the draft plan and the feedback form, visit coloradosprings.gov and search for "Park System Master Plan." The city is accepting feedback on priorities, gaps in current services, and recommendations for future investment.

Comments don't need to be formal or lengthy. Something as specific as "the playground at [park name] has outdated equipment that needs replacement" or "we need a safe bike connection between [neighborhood A] and [neighborhood B]" is exactly the kind of input that makes its way into the final plan when enough community members say the same thing.

What's Next

The comment period closes September 22. After that, the PRCS team will review public input, refine the draft, and move toward formal adoption. The Parks Advisory Board presentation on September 10 is open to the public — check coloradosprings.gov/ParkSystemMasterPlan for the full timeline and any additional community meetings scheduled before the comment period closes. This is the clearest window COS families have had in over a decade to shape what parks look like in their neighborhoods over the next 15 years. Two weeks is enough time to read the key sections and submit something.
