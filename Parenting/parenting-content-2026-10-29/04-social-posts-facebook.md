### STORY 1: AAP Flu + RSV Season Guidance

**Long-Form Post (8:00 AM MT)**

Here's the number that stopped us in our tracks: at least 190 children died from the flu last season. And only 49.4 percent of kids were vaccinated.

The American Academy of Pediatrics just issued its updated guidance for the 2026-27 respiratory season, and the message is simple — don't wait. Flu vaccines are available now for all children 6 months and older, and there's no preferred product this year, so whatever your pediatrician has on hand is the right call.

The new RSV guidance (published October 26 in Pediatrics) also expands who qualifies for second-season protection: children aged 8 to 19 months at elevated risk are now recommended to receive RSV immunization in their second season, not just the first. If you have a young child with a chronic health condition, it's worth a quick call to your pediatrician this week to confirm where they stand.

Fall in Colorado Springs means cold air and indoor time — flu and RSV season is here. If your kids aren't vaccinated yet, this week is the week.

Have your kids gotten their flu shots yet this fall?

### STORY 2: Flyboss Infant Bouncer CPSC Warning

**Long-Form Post (10:00 AM MT)**

If you have a Flyboss 3-in-1 Infant Bouncer in your home, please stop using it right now.

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission issued a warning after receiving 191 reports that the bouncers can collapse while a baby is in them. In 115 of those incidents, the baby's head hit the floor or another surface below.

About 14,200 units are affected. If you have one, stop using it today and go to cpsc.gov to find out about your options. This is the fourth time this month the CPSC has flagged a baby product — October has been a busy one for infant safety alerts.

We know it's frustrating when something you bought to keep your baby comfortable turns out to be a hazard. But this one's worth acting on quickly.

Do you check the CPSC website regularly? It's become a habit for a lot of COS parents — especially with so many infant product recalls this fall.

### STORY 3: D11 Bond — What It Funds

**Long-Form Post (10:00 AM MT)**

The D11 bond vote is in five days — November 3 — and we've been hearing from a lot of COS parents who want to understand what the $775 million would actually go toward before they vote.

Here's the short version: the money is targeted at the basics that aging school buildings need. Think HVAC systems that were installed decades ago and are now failing, classrooms that need to be updated to support how kids actually learn today, and library renovations across the district. D11 has 55-plus schools, and a lot of them are showing their age.

The district hasn't passed a bond in 20 years, which means a lot of deferred maintenance has built up. D11 is also the only major COS district with no current bond debt, which is worth knowing when you're evaluating the ask.

If you want to see what this would mean for your specific property tax, there's a calculator at d11.org/bond.

We're not here to tell you how to vote — but we do think it helps to know what's actually on the table. What questions do you still have about the bond?

### STORY 5: Halloween Candy Inflation

**Long-Form Post (3:00 PM MT)**

Let's talk about the elephant in the candy aisle.

A new study found that candy prices have risen roughly four times faster than general inflation over the past few years. That means the bag of mini Snickers that used to cost you nine dollars is now... more than that. You've noticed. We've noticed. The entire checkout line at King Soopers on October 30 has noticed.

And yet we're all still buying it.

Because Halloween is Halloween. But there are a few ways to make it a little less painful:

Buy in bulk from warehouse stores — the per-unit cost is meaningfully lower than grocery bags. Go for candy that kids actually want (no one's raving about those orange peanut-shaped things). Consider a neighborhood bulk buy with a few families on your street. And on the receiving end — let the kids sort, trade, and enjoy the haul rather than immediately supplementing it at the store.

We're two days out. The candy is already in the cart. Just know you're not imagining it — it really is more expensive than it used to be.

What's your strategy for managing the trick-or-treat candy situation in your house?
