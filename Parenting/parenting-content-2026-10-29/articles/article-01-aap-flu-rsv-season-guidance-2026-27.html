AAP Updates Flu and RSV Guidance: Respiratory Season Is Starting Now

By Sarah Morales | October 29, 2026 | COS Parenting

At least 190 children died from influenza last season in the United States — one of the highest totals in recent years. Fewer than half of children (49.4 percent) received a flu vaccine. The American Academy of Pediatrics is urging families to act now, as the 2026-27 respiratory virus season gets underway.

The AAP released updated guidance this month covering both influenza and RSV, the two respiratory viruses that send the most children to the hospital each fall and winter. For parents in Colorado Springs, where cold air and school routines accelerate transmission season, the timing is directly relevant.

Flu Vaccination: No New Rules, But the Urgency Is Real

The AAP's influenza recommendation for 2026-27 is unchanged: all children 6 months and older without a medical contraindication should receive an annual flu vaccine. There's no preferred product this season — all 2026-27 U.S. flu vaccines are trivalent, and the AAP says whatever your pediatrician carries is appropriate for your child.

The reason the guidance is drawing more attention this year is the 2025-26 season's severity. At least 190 pediatric deaths were confirmed — a number that pediatricians have been pointing to as a direct consequence of low vaccination rates. The vaccine doesn't guarantee your child won't get the flu, but it significantly reduces the risk of severe illness, hospitalization, and death.

If your children haven't been vaccinated yet, late October is still well within the optimal window. Flu season typically peaks between December and February in Colorado, which means there's still time to build protection before the highest-risk months.

RSV: Expanded Guidance for High-Risk Toddlers

The AAP also published updated RSV immunization guidance on October 26 in Pediatrics, and this one contains genuinely new information for families with young children.

The existing recommendation — RSV immunization for all infants under 8 months entering their first RSV season — remains in place. What's new is an expansion of eligibility for second-season protection. Children aged 8 to 19 months who are at elevated risk (due to chronic lung disease, congenital heart disease, or other qualifying conditions) are now recommended to receive RSV immunization in their second RSV season as well.

If you have a child in that age range with a chronic health condition, a quick call to your pediatrician this week can confirm whether the updated criteria apply to your family's situation.

What's Next

Most pediatric practices in Colorado Springs offer flu shots during well-child visits and many have dedicated flu shot clinics through November. Call your pediatrician's office to confirm availability — walk-in slots at local pharmacies are also an option for children 4 years and older. The AAP's patient-facing resources are available at healthychildren.org.
