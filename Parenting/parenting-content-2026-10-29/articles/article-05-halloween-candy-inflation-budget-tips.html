Halloween Candy Inflation Is Real — And Families Are Feeling It

By Sarah Morales | October 29, 2026 | COS Parenting

It's October 29. Halloween is in two days. And a new study finds that candy prices have risen roughly four times faster than general inflation over the past few years.

If you've been standing in the candy aisle lately thinking "this used to cost less," you're right. You're not misremembering. The checkout total on your bag of assorted miniatures is genuinely higher than it was a few years ago, and not by a small amount.

We're still all buying it. But here's what's happening, and a few ways to make it a little less painful.

Why Candy Has Gotten So Expensive

Candy is especially vulnerable to ingredient price swings because it's almost entirely made of commodities: sugar, cocoa, corn syrup, and packaging materials. When those input costs spike — and they have — candy manufacturers absorb some of the cost and pass the rest along. The result is smaller bags for the same price, or higher prices for the same bag.

The economics are a little absurd when you look at them squarely: the typical American household buys candy almost exclusively for a few holidays each year, which means the industry can price it at peak-demand moments. Halloween is peak Halloween.

Ways to Manage the Candy Budget

Buy in bulk before the weekend. Warehouse stores like Costco and Sam's Club consistently offer significantly lower per-unit prices on candy compared to grocery store bags. If you haven't gone yet, today is the day. The variety bags are popular and they do go fast.

Go for what kids actually want. Chocolate and name-brand candy gets taken first and traded for value in the after-Halloween candy economy. Candy corn and those little orange peanut-shaped things... don't. You'll end up throwing out the unloved ones anyway. Spend the money where it'll actually get used.

Consider a block buy. If you have neighbors who are also putting out candy, pooling a bulk order and splitting it reduces the per-household cost and generally results in better candy than buying individual bags.

Manage the haul strategically. On the receiving end, a little candy organization goes a long way. The first-night pillowcase sort — where kids trade and negotiate — is genuinely fun and reduces the impulse to immediately go back out for more. Spreading the enjoyment over a few weeks is also just better for everyone's teeth.

The Bottom Line

Halloween candy is expensive and we're buying it anyway. That's fine. It's Halloween. But knowing the prices are higher than you're remembering — and understanding why — at least takes away the confusion at checkout. You're not going crazy. It really does cost more now.
