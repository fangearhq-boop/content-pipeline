Colorado Springs Has 47,000 Kids Under 5 and Only 18,400 Childcare Spots — The City Is Acting

By Jamie Rivera | COS Parenting | November 21, 2026

If you've ever called around trying to find an infant spot and gotten nothing but waitlists, the numbers that explain that experience are significant. Colorado Springs has approximately 47,000 children under the age of 5. The city has approximately 18,400 licensed childcare spots. That's a gap of nearly 28,600 seats — fewer than one available licensed spot for every two young children who need care.

Some areas of Colorado Springs are worse than others. The east side, southeast COS, and the Fountain-Fort Carson corridor are among the city's most severely under-resourced areas for childcare access. These neighborhoods qualify as official "childcare deserts" — areas where the ratio of children to licensed slots exceeds three to one.

Why the Shortage Is So Severe

The childcare shortage in Colorado Springs has multiple reinforcing causes. Provider costs are high — staff wages, liability insurance, licensing requirements, and facility standards all create real barriers for anyone trying to open a new program. At the same time, previous zoning rules meant that childcare facilities could only open in certain building types, limiting where new providers could locate even when they wanted to.

Demand has also been climbing. Colorado Springs has grown substantially over the past decade, adding families faster than its licensed childcare capacity has expanded. The east side and other underserved corridors tend to have fewer existing providers, longer commutes for the providers who do exist, and lower subsidy reimbursement rates that make it harder for providers to serve families who need financial assistance.

Statewide, the picture is similarly strained. As of April 2026, approximately 14,000 Colorado children were on the state childcare subsidy waitlist. For many families, that waitlist isn't a temporary inconvenience — it can stretch for years.

What Colorado Springs Is Doing

The Colorado Springs City Council has passed a new zoning ordinance that allows childcare facilities to operate in more types of buildings, including commercial and mixed-use spaces that previously weren't zoned for childcare use. The intent is to lower one of the most practical barriers to expansion: the difficulty of finding a qualifying location in the neighborhoods that need care the most.

The city has also established a new loan program aimed at helping childcare providers offset the upfront costs of getting licensed and operating. Startup costs — physical build-out, equipment, licensing fees, background checks for staff — have historically been a significant barrier for small providers. A loan program doesn't eliminate those costs, but it can make the gap between intention and actual opening smaller for providers who want to serve underserved areas.

These are early-stage interventions, and they won't close a 28,600-seat gap quickly. But they address real, identified constraints rather than just identifying the problem.

What Families Can Do Now

If you're currently on a waitlist or searching for care in Colorado Springs, knowing the structural context doesn't immediately change your options — but it's worth knowing that the shortage you're experiencing isn't your fault or a failure of persistence. It's a supply problem with documented causes.

The Colorado Department of Human Services maintains a Child Care Assistance Program (CCAP) for income-eligible families, and El Paso County Human Services can help connect families with available subsidized spots. The waitlist situation varies by provider, so reaching out directly to multiple licensed centers in your area — including those slightly outside your first-choice neighborhood — can expand options in ways that a single-call search won't surface.

What's Next

Colorado Springs City Council has passed the new zoning ordinance, and the loan program is active. Implementation and provider uptake will take time before new spots materialize. For families currently searching for care, El Paso County Human Services (719-444-5700) can provide CCAP information, and the state's childcare finder tool at earlychildhood.colorado.gov is a starting point for locating licensed options in your ZIP code.
