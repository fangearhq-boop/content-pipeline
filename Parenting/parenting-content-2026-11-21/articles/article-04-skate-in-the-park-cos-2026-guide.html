Skate in the Park Is Back in Downtown COS — Everything Families Need to Know for 2026

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting | November 21, 2026

Skate in the Park is open for its 12th season at Acacia Park in downtown Colorado Springs, and it's one of the most straightforward family outings of the winter. For $15 per person, you get admission and skate rental included. Kids ages four and under skate free. No advance tickets — everything's handled at the door.

The rink is at 115 E. Platte Ave. in the heart of downtown, which puts it within walking distance of coffee shops and restaurants on Tejon Street if you're making a full afternoon of it. The rink opened in mid-November and runs through approximately January 2027.

What to Expect

Acacia Park is a classic downtown park — open, flat, surrounded by the urban streetscape — and the rink is set up in the open central area. The holiday decoration that goes up around the park during November and December adds to the atmosphere. The rink is outdoor and open-air, which means you'll want to dress for actual cold weather. Layers are the right call, especially for little ones who may need a break from the ice but don't want to leave.

Skate rentals are included in the $15 admission, which is a genuine value compared to the add-on rental fees at a lot of rinks. If you bring your own skates, the admission is still $15 — the rental is included, not a surcharge for not having your own gear.

If you've been before in past seasons, the experience is consistent. If this is your family's first time, it's worth knowing that the rink can get busy on weekend afternoons, especially as the holiday season picks up. Mid-morning or early afternoon on weekdays tends to be quieter.

Tips for Families With Young Kids

The free admission for ages four and under makes this easy for mixed-age families. Kids who are just learning to skate can usually find support rails along the edges of the rink — that's standard at most seasonal outdoor rinks. If your child is nervous about the ice, arriving a little before peak hours can give them more space to move at their own pace without feeling crowded.

Dress in layers and bring gloves — protecting hands from the ice surface is worth the extra prep. If you've got a stroller for a non-skating sibling, the park paths around the rink perimeter work for that.

Acacia Park is about a block from several coffee spots on Tejon Street, so a post-skate warm-up is easy to build in. Parking in the downtown garage on Vermijo or the lots along Platte is usually the most convenient approach on weekends.
