CPSC Recall: Kidsview Infant Bouncers Can Collapse Under Baby — What to Do

By Jamie Rivera | COS Parenting | November 21, 2026

If you've got a Kidsview infant bouncer seat in your home, the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission wants you to stop using it today. The agency announced Recall #27-092 on November 19, 2026, covering approximately 412 Kidsview infant bouncers that can collapse while a baby is seated in them.

Seven collapses have already been reported. In two of those incidents, the infant's head struck a hard surface. One required medical attention. The units involved were manufactured between August and October 2022 and sold on Amazon from August 2022 through November 2023, priced between $60 and $170.

How to Know If Yours Is Affected

The recall covers bouncers manufactured from August through October 2022. The easiest way to check is your Amazon order history. If you purchased a Kidsview infant bouncer between August 2022 and November 2023, it's likely covered. The CPSC's recall page doesn't specify a model number for the affected units, so if you have any Kidsview bouncer from that period, treat it as recalled.

These bouncers were also sometimes given as gifts or passed between families, so it's worth checking with anyone who might have given you one or who you may have passed one on to.

What to Do

The CPSC's instruction is straightforward: stop using the bouncer immediately. Don't set it aside and plan to get back to it — the collapse risk is present any time a baby is in the seat.

To get a refund, contact Kidsview directly by email at haimaoziying@163.com. The company is providing an Amazon gift card for the purchase price. You'll want to have your order information ready. If you purchased through a third-party seller or no longer have the receipt, still reach out — it's worth the email.

The CPSC recall page also provides contact information and can be referenced if you have trouble reaching the company.

Why Infant Bouncer Recalls Matter

Infant bouncers are commonly used during the first few months of life — often as a hands-free option while parents prepare meals, work, or care for other children. Because babies are placed in them repeatedly and sometimes for extended periods, a structural failure during use poses a direct fall or impact risk. The CPSC's threshold for initiating a recall is evidence of real-world harm, and seven collapses meeting that threshold, with two head strikes and one medical visit, is significant for a product with only 412 units sold.

That small unit count also means these may not show up in a lot of parenting social feeds. If you bought yours on Amazon in that window, check today.

What's Next

Stop using the Kidsview bouncer now if you have one. Email haimaoziying@163.com to arrange the Amazon gift card refund. If you have questions about the recall or the company doesn't respond, the CPSC Consumer Hotline is available at 1-800-638-2772. The recall record is searchable at cpsc.gov under recall number 27-092.
