CPSC Warning: FZChenrry Magnetic Fidget Ball Toys Are a Fatal Hazard and There Is No Refund

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting | November 21, 2026

This isn't a recall. That matters, and it makes the situation more complicated for families who bought this product.

On November 19, 2026, the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission issued Warning #27-095 about FZChenrry Magnetic Fidget Ball Toys. The seller, Chanlee, refused to issue a voluntary recall. That means there's no refund program, no replacement option, and no box to send the product back to. The CPSC's guidance is clear: stop using the toys immediately, don't sell them, don't give them away, and dispose of them.

What Makes These Dangerous

The FZChenrry fidget balls contain high-powered magnets that exceed the federal magnet standard under the mandatory toy safety rules. The danger isn't just that a child might swallow one — it's what happens if they swallow more than one, even hours or days apart.

When two or more high-powered magnets are inside the digestive tract, they can attract to each other through the intestinal walls. The force involved can pinch, perforate, and tear the intestine. This kind of injury requires emergency surgery and can be fatal. It's the same mechanism that led to the deaths and serious injuries that prompted federal magnet standards in the first place.

The balls are small, silicone-textured, and might look like a sensory or fidget tool appropriate for children. They're not. The CPSC warning applies regardless of whether children in the home have ever used them.

How to Know If You Have Them

The product is sold as a set of eight textured silicone balls in purple, packaged in a black oval zippered case. If you purchased anything matching that description on Amazon from a seller called Chanlee, Fzchenrry, or FZChenrry between February 2025 and September 2026, treat it as affected.

These were sold at approximately $16 per set, which is a price point common for sensory or fidget toys. They might be in a child's room, a desk drawer, a gift bag, or still unopened. Check Amazon order history for that period if you're unsure.

Because the seller refused the recall, there's no official notification being sent to purchasers. The CPSC warning is the only communication channel, which means parents may not find out unless someone tells them.

No Refund — What That Means Practically

Normally a recall comes with instructions: return the product here, or email here, and you'll receive a refund or replacement. This situation is different. Because the seller refused to cooperate, the CPSC can't compel a remedy program in the same way it handles a voluntary recall.

Parents who bought these on Amazon can try submitting a complaint through Amazon's buyer protection system, though there's no guarantee of a refund outside the standard return window. The more important action right now is simply to remove the product from your home.

What's Next

If you have FZChenrry Magnetic Fidget Ball Toys in your home — the purple set of eight silicone balls in a black zippered case — dispose of them now. Don't pass them on. The CPSC's Warning #27-095 is on record at cpsc.gov. If you have questions or want to report an incident, the CPSC Consumer Hotline is at 1-800-638-2772. Parents who want to pursue an Amazon refund outside the standard window can open a case through Amazon's customer service, though outcomes will vary.
