### STORY 1: Kidsview Infant Bouncer Recall

#### Long-Form Post — 8:00 AM MT

⚠️ Heads up for parents with babies: there's a new CPSC recall on Kidsview infant bouncer seats and you'll want to check your home today.

The recall (#27-092) covers about 412 bouncers made between August and October 2022 and sold on Amazon from August 2022 through November 2023, priced between $60 and $170. The problem is structural — the frame can collapse while an infant is seated in it. Seven collapses have been reported, and in two of those cases, the baby's head hit a surface. One required medical attention.

If you have one of these, the action is straightforward: stop using it immediately, then email Kidsview at haimaoziying@163.com to arrange an Amazon gift card refund. That's the company's remedy — a gift card for the purchase price.

If you're unsure whether yours is included, check your Amazon order history for a Kidsview bouncer purchased in that window. The units involved were manufactured from August through October 2022.

### STORY 2: FZChenrry Magnetic Fidget Balls Warning

#### Long-Form Post — 10:00 AM MT

⚠️ This one is different from the recalls you're used to seeing — and it's actually more urgent.

The CPSC just issued a warning (not a recall) about FZChenrry Magnetic Fidget Ball Toys, which were sold on Amazon between February 2025 and September 2026 for about $16 a set. The product is eight textured silicone balls in purple, packaged in a black zippered oval case. The seller, Chanlee, refused the voluntary recall, so there's no refund, no replacement, and no remedy program of any kind.

Here's why that matters: these balls contain high-powered magnets that violate the federal mandatory toy standard. If a child swallows two or more magnets — even at different times — the magnets can attract to each other through the intestinal walls, causing perforations and tears that can be fatal without surgery.

The CPSC's guidance is to stop using them immediately, don't sell them, don't give them away, and dispose of them safely. If your household had these in a junk drawer, a fidget collection, or as a stocking stuffer from last year, it's time to look.

Have you spotted these in your home or in Amazon orders from that window? Share this post — these are the ones that fall through the cracks because there's no return box to send them back to.

### STORY 3: Colorado Springs Childcare Desert

#### Long-Form Post — 12:00 PM MT

If you've ever had trouble finding childcare in Colorado Springs, the numbers behind your experience are staggering.

Colorado Springs has about 47,000 children under the age of 5. The city has approximately 18,400 licensed childcare spots. That's fewer than one spot for every two young children, and in some parts of town — the east side, southeast COS, and the Fountain-Fort Carson corridor — the gap is even worse. These areas qualify as official childcare deserts, where there are more than three children for every available licensed slot.

The city is trying to close that gap. The Colorado Springs City Council recently passed a new zoning ordinance that allows childcare facilities to open in more types of buildings — including commercial and mixed-use spaces that previously weren't zoned for childcare. There's also a new loan program for providers to help offset the high startup and operating costs that keep many potential providers from getting licensed.

Statewide, about 14,000 Colorado families are on the childcare subsidy waitlist. The wait can run years, not months. The new city policies won't fix that overnight, but they're a step toward making more spots available in a city that's growing faster than its childcare capacity.

### STORY 4: Skate in the Park

#### Long-Form Post — 3:00 PM MT

⛸️ The ice rink is back in downtown Colorado Springs and it's one of the best low-key family outings of the winter.

Skate in the Park is in its 12th season at Acacia Park (115 E. Platte Ave.) and it's open now through approximately January 2027. Admission is $15 and includes skate rental, so there's no extra cost for gear. Kids ages four and under skate free. Tickets are sold at the door — there's no advance online purchase, so plan to have cash or a card when you arrive.

It's right in the heart of downtown, which makes it easy to pair with a stop at a coffee shop or the shops along Tejon Street before or after. The park has that classic outdoor rink atmosphere, and it's especially nice on a clear COS winter evening.

### STORY 5: COS Holiday Weekend Guide

🎄 COS families, this weekend has some good options across a range of price points — here's a quick guide.

Vintage Market Days "Rocking Around the Christmas Tree" runs through Sunday, November 22, at the Norris Penrose Event Center (1045 Lower Gold Camp Road).
