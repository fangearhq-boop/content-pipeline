The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission has issued Recall #27-092 for approximately 412 Kidsview infant bouncer seats manufactured August through October 2022 and sold on Amazon from August 2022 through November 2023 for $60 to $170. The hazard is structural: the frame can collapse while an infant is seated, posing an impact injury risk. ### STORY 2: CPSC Warning — FZChenrry Magnetic Fidget Ball Toys, No Recall, No Refund, Dispose Now
**Tier:** 1
**Type:** NEW STORY
**Pillar:** National Parenting
**Byline:** Sarah Morales
**Posting Window:** 10:00 AM MT

The CPSC has issued Warning #27-095 for FZChenrry Magnetic Fidget Ball Toys — a set of eight textured silicone magnetic balls in purple, sold in a black oval zippered case. Approximately 1,372 sets were sold on Amazon by seller Chanlee from February 2025 through September 2026 for about $16 per set. The seller refused a voluntary recall. The balls contain high-powered magnets that violate the mandatory federal toy safety standard; swallowing two or more can cause intestinal perforations and tears that are potentially fatal without surgery. Because there is no recall, there is no refund and no replacement. The CPSC instructs families to stop using the toys immediately, not to sell or give them away, and to dispose of them.

### STORY 3: Colorado Springs Childcare Desert — 47,000 Kids, 18,400 Spots, City Passes New Zoning Law
**Tier:** 2
**Type:** NEW STORY
**Pillar:** Local News
**Byline:** Jamie Rivera
**Posting Window:** 12:00 PM MT

Colorado Springs has approximately 47,000 children under age five and only about 18,400 licensed childcare spots — fewer than one slot for every two young children. The east side, southeast COS, and the Fountain-Fort Carson corridor are worst affected, qualifying as official childcare deserts where more than three children compete for every available licensed slot. Colorado Springs City Council has passed a new zoning ordinance allowing childcare facilities to open in more building types, and a new loan program is available to help providers offset startup and operating costs. Statewide, about 14,000 Colorado children are on the childcare subsidy waitlist.
