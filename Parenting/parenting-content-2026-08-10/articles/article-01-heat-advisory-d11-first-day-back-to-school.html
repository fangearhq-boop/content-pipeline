El Paso County Heat Advisory in Effect — And D11 Starts Wednesday

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting | August 10, 2026

The National Weather Service out of Pueblo issued a Heat Advisory for El Paso County on Monday morning. It runs from 11:00 AM to 7:00 PM MDT today, with Colorado Springs forecast to reach 95°F. This is the fourth consecutive heat advisory for the area — a stretch that has Colorado Springs tracking toward one of its warmest August stretches in recent years.

The timing matters for families: D11's first day for all K-12 students is Wednesday, August 12. Jumpstart Day for kindergarteners, 6th graders, and 9th graders is tomorrow, Tuesday, August 11. This week is a warm one to navigate.

What the Advisory Means for Today

Today's advisory runs through the peak afternoon heat window. The forecast is for 95°F in Colorado Springs, with Pueblo reaching 101°F. Cloud cover is expected to build by noon to 1:00 PM, and spotty thunderstorms are possible between about 1:30 and 7:00 PM — the same window the advisory covers.

The practical read for Monday: if you have outdoor errands or activities with younger children, the morning hours before 11:00 AM give you the coolest window. Once the heat builds through midday, the afternoon storm activity may bring down temperatures, but it can also bring lightning, which makes outdoor activity unsafe in a different way.

Standard El Paso County heat safety guidance: keep kids hydrated throughout the day, avoid outdoor activity during peak heat hours, watch for signs of heat illness (dizziness, nausea, skin that feels hot but stops sweating), and get into air conditioning if those symptoms appear.

First Week Heat Safety for Kids

The first week back tends to be more physically demanding than kids expect — new routines, more time on their feet, and less time than over summer to rest when they want to. In heat, that combination can catch families off guard.

A few practical things that help: send a water bottle that's large enough to last a full school day without refilling if needed, because access to water fountains during class varies by school. Put the bottle in the freezer the night before so it starts cold. Pack a lunch that doesn't rely on staying cool — sandwiches with mayonnaise or dairy-based sides can become a problem in a warm backpack.

For outdoor activities in the first week: early morning is best. Colorado Springs mornings this week should be in the high 70s before 9:00 AM even with the overall hot pattern, dropping considerably from the afternoon peak. If your family has outdoor weekend plans, Saturday August 15 looks slightly cooler than Sunday.
