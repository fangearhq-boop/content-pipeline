### STORY 2: CPSC Coin Battery Recalls

#### Long-Form Post

Two more products recalled this week for the same hazard — coin and button batteries that children can easily access.

Both were announced on August 6 as part of a large Consumer Product Safety Commission recall batch.

**First: A2batt EEMB Lithium-ion Coin Battery Chargers.** About 4,930 units were sold on Amazon between August 2024 and March 2026 for around $14. The product comes with a charger, four rechargeable CR2032 batteries, and a USB cable. The problem: the packaging doesn't meet child-resistant standards and lacks the warnings required by Reese's Law. Children can access the coin batteries. If you have it: write "RECALL" on the charger and contact A2batt for a full refund.

**Second: KC Imports Mini Laser Pointer Keychains (model KKC-6071).** About 9,600 units sold — not on Amazon, but at gas station checkouts, discount stores, and one website. These sold for about $0.42 each. Same issue: the button battery inside is accessible to children, and the packaging violates Reese's Law. If you find one: email a photo of the disposed product to kcimport90058@hotmail.com for a full refund.

Here's why this matters specifically: coin and button batteries, when swallowed, don't just "get stuck." They react with the moisture in a child's digestive tract and generate a hydroxide chemical burn. This can happen within two hours. It can cause life-threatening internal injuries. Reese's Law — passed in 2022 — is specifically designed to address this after several child deaths.

These are the kinds of products that end up in a junk drawer or a keychain clip at the school store. Worth checking.

### STORY 4: D49 Purple Star Schools

#### Long-Form Post

D49 just added nine more Purple Star Schools for the 2026–27 school year — and if your family has ever done a PCS move, a deployment, or a homecoming while your kids were in school, you'll want to know which campuses made the list.

The Colorado Department of Education's Purple Star designation goes to schools with a demonstrated commitment to supporting military-connected students. In practice, that means a few specific things: a designated point of contact for military families (so you're not starting from zero every time you call), counselors who've been trained to recognize the specific challenges of mid-year school transitions, and peer support programs for students who've moved away from friends and need to re-establish a social foothold.

Nine D49 campuses — seven district-operated and two charter schools — now carry this designation for the coming school year.

This is especially meaningful in east Colorado Springs and the Falcon area. Fort Carson sits at the southern edge of D49's service area, and Peterson Space Force Base and Schriever Space Force Base are both within the broader El Paso County community D49 serves. The research on military-connected students is clear: frequent moves and parental deployments create measurable disruptions to academic progress and social development. Schools that proactively build in support structures see better outcomes for those kids.

If you're a military family in D49, look for the Purple Star designation at your school's profile on d49.org.

Are you a military family in Colorado Springs? Have you found your school district to be supportive of transitions and deployments?

### STORY 5: AAP + Apple iOS 27 Family Media Plan

#### Long-Form Post

Apple announced something at WWDC in June that didn't get as much attention as the hardware, but it directly affects every Colorado Springs family with a kid on an iPhone or iPad: Apple is partnering with the American Academy of Pediatrics to build pediatricians' screen-time guidance directly into iOS 27.

Here's what that means in practice.

iOS 27 — releasing this fall — includes a set of new parental controls shaped by the AAP's Family Media Plan. The features include:

**Ask to Browse:** Your child cannot visit any website in Safari until you approve it. Not filtered by category — literally approve by URL.

**Mandatory Child Account:** Any device user under 13 will be required to have a Child Account. The option is available for users up to 18.

**Contact Approval:** Before a new contact can be added to your child's device, you have to approve it first.

**Time Allowances:** You can manage screen time by app category — social, games, entertainment — not just by total hours.

**Expanded Communication Safety:** The existing feature that detected nudity in messages now also flags gore and violent content.

The AAP's Family Media Plan — the tool Apple is adapting — has been available for free at HealthyChildren.org since 2016. It's an interactive form you fill out with your kids to decide your household rules: device-free zones (bedroom? dinner table?), what happens at bedtime, what content is okay, how much time is too much. The AAP has long said that the best screen-time approach isn't an hour limit — it's a family agreement that everyone actually understands.

With D11 starting Wednesday, this week is a natural moment to revisit what your family's screen agreements actually are, before homework schedules and group chats and school apps start competing for everyone's attention.

You can set up the Family Media Plan now at HealthyChildren.org/MediaUsePlan — the iOS 27 controls aren't live yet, but the conversation can happen today.
