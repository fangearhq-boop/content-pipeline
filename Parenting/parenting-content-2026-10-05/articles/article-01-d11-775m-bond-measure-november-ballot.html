D11's $775M Bond Measure Is on the November Ballot — What Colorado Springs Families Need to Know

By Sarah Morales October 5, 2026

What's on the Ballot

Colorado Springs School District 11 families have a major decision coming in November. On August 5, the D11 Board of Education voted 6-1 to place Ballot Question 4A — a $775 million bond measure — on the November 2026 general election ballot. If approved by voters, it would be the first voter-approved bond for the district since 2004.

The bond would fund infrastructure repairs across every D11 school in Colorado Springs. Projects include replacing aging heating, ventilation, and air conditioning systems; removing hazardous materials including asbestos in older buildings; modernizing classroom spaces; adding capacity at several schools; completing a full rebuild of one school; and installing air conditioning in Coronado High School, which currently lacks it.

What It Would Cost Homeowners

According to the D11 Board, the bond would cost the average Colorado Springs homeowner approximately $260 per year. That figure assumes a typical home in the district and could vary depending on property value. The district's official bond information page at d11.org/bond includes detailed cost breakdowns for families who want to verify the estimate for their own properties.

Bond measures fund capital projects like construction and renovation — they are separate from the mill levy override measures that fund operations and staff salaries. A key talking point from D11 is that redirecting savings from bond-funded efficiencies could help support teacher salary increases over time, but that is distinct from the bond measure itself.

Why Now

D11 serves tens of thousands of Colorado Springs students across dozens of schools, many of which were built decades ago. The district has been making the case for years that deferred maintenance has accumulated to the point where a comprehensive funding mechanism is necessary. The last time D11 voters approved a bond of this scale was 2004 — more than 20 years ago.

The board previewed the proposal in earlier forms — initial plans ranged from $750 million to $775 million — before landing on the $775 million figure approved in August. The measure goes before all registered voters in the D11 service area, not only families with children currently enrolled.

How to Get Informed

October is the window to get informed before ballots arrive. The district has published detailed project lists, cost information, and school-by-school impact summaries at d11.org/bond. Community members can also attend D11 town halls and board meetings as the November election approaches.

Local coverage from the Colorado Springs Gazette and KKTV has also covered the measure in depth since the August vote.

What's Next

November 2026 ballots will be mailed to D11-area registered voters in the weeks before Election Day. Families who want to engage more deeply — or who have questions about how the bond would affect their child's school specifically — can find details at d11.org/bond or contact the district directly. Watch for candidate forums and town halls in October as local media and community groups take up the question ahead of the vote.
