First Conferences of the Year

October marks the first parent-teacher conference season of the 2026-27 school year for most Colorado Springs families. Fall conferences happen early enough in the semester that they're genuinely useful — there's time to course-correct before semester grades close, and teachers have had enough time with students to give meaningful feedback.

Colorado Springs has multiple school districts with their own conference schedules. CIVA Charter School has confirmed dates in October; D11, D20, and D49 families should watch for direct communications from their child's school about specific sign-up windows.

How to Make the Most of 20 Minutes

Parent-teacher conferences are often scheduled in 15- or 20-minute slots. That's not much time if you're not prepared, but it's more than enough if you go in with focus.

Come with a specific question. "How is my child doing?" invites a general answer. "How does my child do during transitions, or when asked to work independently?" invites something actionable. Think about what you've noticed at home and ask teachers whether they see similar patterns at school.

Ask what support looks like at home. Teachers often have specific, practical suggestions — a type of reading practice, a particular app, a quiet-time habit. Ask directly, and you're more likely to get something usable.

Ask about the second half of the semester. Fall conferences are early enough that the semester is still very much in progress. Asking "what should we focus on between now and January?" orients the conversation toward something actionable rather than just reviewing what's already happened.

Take notes. A quick note on your phone during or immediately after is enough. You won't remember everything, especially if you have multiple children and multiple conferences in one evening.

If Your Child Has Concerns About Their Conference

Some kids are nervous about what parents will hear at conferences, and some have legitimate worries about grades or behavior. One approach that works for many families: tell your child before the conference that you're going to listen, not judge, and that anything the teacher brings up is information you'll work on together. After the conference, share what was said in age-appropriate terms. Kids handle transparency better than silence.
