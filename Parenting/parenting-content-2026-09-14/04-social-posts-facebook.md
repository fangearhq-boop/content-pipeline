### STORY 1: Ricky Joy Sour Crush Candy Bottle Recall

**Long-Form — 8:00 AM MT**
Heads up for parents and caregivers: the CPSC has issued a recall covering more than 2.3 million Ricky Joy Sour Crush Rolling Liquid Candy bottles.

The hazard: the rolling ball on top can detach from the bottle, creating a choking hazard. These are the small sour liquid candy bottles you roll across your tongue — the kind you'd find at checkout counters, candy stores, and Walmart. They were sold from January 2023 through May 2026 at H-E-B, Forman Mills, Walmart.com, CandyFunHouse.com, and RickyJoy.com for around $3 a bottle or up to $32 for a 12-pack.

Flavors included: blue raspberry, strawberry, green apple, and watermelon.

No injuries have been reported, but the CPSC has issued the recall because of the risk of serious injury or death. If you have these at home, stop use immediately and take them away from children.

To get a full refund, contact Ricky Joy at **404-975-0352** (Monday–Friday, 8 AM–5 PM ET) or visit **rickyjoy.com/pages/recall**.

### STORY 2: Finger Light Toys Recall

**Long-Form — 10:00 AM MT**
Another product safety alert to know about: several brands of projecting finger light toys sold on Amazon are being recalled because the button cell batteries inside are too easy for children to access.

The CPSC recall from Cade California Electronic covers approximately 179,739 units — 50-count boxes of finger light toys in white, blue, red, and green, sold on Amazon from 2015 through July 2026. Similar recalls were also issued for Oitnlaughter, POPOOO, and Syera-branded versions sold through Amazon and TEMU.

The problem is serious: if a child swallows a button cell battery, it can cause severe internal chemical burns and death — even within a short period of time.

If you have any of these in your home, stop using them immediately. You'll need to throw them away and then send an email with a photo of the disposed product to the seller to receive a full refund. Check cpsc.gov/Recalls for the specific contact information for each brand.

### STORY 5: Back-to-School Screen Time Guide

**Long-Form — 5:00 PM MT**
We're about a month into the school year, and if screen time in your house has already crept up, you're in good company.

Here's what the research says, because it's worth knowing as fall routines settle in. Studies published in major pediatrics journals in 2026 found that screen use before bedtime extended how long it took kids to fall asleep by an average of 23 minutes. That might not sound like much, but over a school week, that adds up to almost two hours of lost sleep.

The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends:
- **Under 18 months:** No screens except video chatting
- **Ages 2–5:** No more than 1 hour per day of high-quality content
- **Ages 6 and up:** Quality and conversation matter more than strict limits — but screens shouldn't replace sleep, movement, or family time

For COS families, the timing is relevant. D49 is now in week 6, D11 is in week 5, and D20 is in week 4 — which means most families are past the "fresh start" energy and into actual daily patterns. Homework routines, after-school wind-down, and bedtime device habits are all being set right now.
