2.3 Million Ricky Joy Sour Crush Candy Bottles Recalled: What COS Families Need to Know

By Jamie Rivera | COS Parenting | September 14, 2026

The Consumer Product Safety Commission and Ricky Joy Co. have issued a recall covering more than 2.3 million Sour Crush Rolling Liquid Candy bottles because the rollerball top can detach from the bottle, creating a choking hazard.

If these small candy bottles have come home in your child's backpack, birthday party bag, or holiday stash, here's what you need to know.

Which Products Are Recalled

Ricky Joy Sour Crush Candy Bottle Recall — Quick Reference
Detail Information
Product Ricky Joy Sour Crush Rolling Liquid Candy Bottles (1.85 fl oz)
Flavors Blue raspberry, strawberry, green apple, watermelon
Sold as Individual bottles and in 3- or 12-bottle packs
Where sold H-E-B, Forman Mills, Walmart.com, CandyFunHouse.com, RickyJoy.com, other candy retailers
Sale period January 2023 through May 2026
Price range $3 (single) to $32 (12-pack)
Units recalled More than 2.3 million
Hazard Rollerball top can detach — choking hazard, risk of serious injury or death
Injuries reported None as of recall date
Remedy Stop use, take from children, request full refund
How to get a refund Call 404-975-0352 (Mon–Fri, 8 AM–5 PM ET) or visit rickyjoy.com/pages/recall

What to Do Right Now

If you have any of these candy bottles in your home, stop using them immediately and take them away from children. The CPSC has issued this recall because the hazard is real even though no injuries have been reported yet.

To get a full refund, contact Ricky Joy at 404-975-0352 Monday through Friday, 8 AM to 5 PM Eastern, or go directly to rickyjoy.com/pages/recall. Do not throw the bottles away before contacting the company, as you may need to provide proof of the product.

Where They May Have Come From

These bottles were a popular impulse purchase at checkout counters, candy specialty shops, and online. They were also commonly included in birthday party favor bags and Halloween candy assortments. The 2.3 million units involved in this recall represent several years of distribution across a wide retail network.

If your children frequent places that sell loose candy, it's worth a quick check of any bottles at home to see if they match this description.

What's Next

Ricky Joy has set up a dedicated recall page and phone line for this situation. The CPSC also maintains a full recall listing at cpsc.gov/Recalls, where you can find official documentation and any updates to the remedy process. If you're unsure whether a product is covered, the recall page is the best starting point.
