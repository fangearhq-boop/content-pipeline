Finger Light Toys Recalled Over Button Battery Danger: Nearly 180,000 Units Pulled From Amazon

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting | September 14, 2026

A wave of CPSC recalls is targeting projecting finger light toys sold through Amazon and TEMU, with multiple brands pulled from shelves because the button cell batteries inside are too easy for children to reach.

Button cell batteries — also called coin batteries — are among the most dangerous small objects a child can swallow. When lodged in the throat or esophagus, they can cause severe internal chemical burns in as little as two hours. The burns can be fatal.

What Was Recalled

The largest single recall involves approximately 179,739 projecting finger light toys sold by Cade California Electronic on Amazon. The toys come in 50-count boxes with lights in white, blue, red, and green. The packaging bears the labels "California Cade Electronic" and "Finger Flashlights."

Additional recalls cover similar products under the brand names Oitnlaughter (sold by Delightmi on Amazon), POPOOO (Amazon), and Syera (sold by Senyu on TEMU). All four recalls cite the same hazard: the three button cell batteries in each toy can be easily accessed by children and swallowed.

Why Button Cell Batteries Are So Dangerous

Button cell batteries are small, shiny, and can look like a coin or a toy to a young child. The CPSC has been issuing warnings about these batteries for years, and federal law now requires that products containing them meet specific safety standards to prevent children from accessing them.

These finger light toys violate those mandatory safety standards. That is the core of the recall: it's not just a design flaw — these products fail to meet a legal requirement that exists specifically to protect children.

What to Do

If you have any of these finger light toys at home — whether purchased from Amazon, found in a party bag, or received as a gift — stop using them immediately. Remove them from any space where children can access them.

For a full refund, you'll need to dispose of the product and send an email with a photo of the disposed item. Check cpsc.gov/Recalls for the specific email address associated with the brand you have, as each company has a separate contact.

What's Next

The CPSC has a standing list of button-battery-related recalls and warnings at cpsc.gov/Recalls — it's worth bookmarking if your household includes young children. When evaluating any new toy that lights up or makes sounds, check whether the battery compartment requires a tool (like a screwdriver) to open. If it doesn't, that's a product worth evaluating more carefully before it gets into the hands of a toddler.
