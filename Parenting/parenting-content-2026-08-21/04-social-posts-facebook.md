### STORY 1: CPSC Multiple Amazon Baby Lounger Recalls

#### Long-Form Post

If you've got a baby lounger at home — especially one you bought on Amazon in the last couple of years — this is worth two minutes of your time.

The CPSC has recalled multiple baby lounger brands in August 2026 for suffocation, entrapment, and fall hazards. Two brands you'll want to check specifically: Vevor Baby Loungers (sold Nov 2024–Jan 2026 on Amazon, VEVOR.com, and Wayfair for about $30) and CooCooBaby Baby Loungers (~2,355 units).

If you have one of these, stop using it now. Go to CPSC.gov, search for the brand name, and follow the remedy instructions. Some recalls include refunds or replacement options.

Baby loungers have been a popular new-parent purchase for a few years now, and this is part of a broader CPSC sweep of lounger-style products in the infant space. When in doubt, check CPSC.gov — it's free, it's current, and it could genuinely make a difference.

Tag a friend who has a new baby at home. Which brands do you look for when checking CPSC recalls? 👇

### STORY 3: Labor Day Lift Off Preview

#### Long-Form Post

Put it on the calendar now: the 50th annual Labor Day Lift Off is coming to Memorial Park on September 5, 6, and 7 — and it's free to attend. 🎈

This is one of Colorado Springs' biggest and most beloved family events. About 200,000 people come through over the weekend to watch hot air balloons inflate and launch, catch live music, hit the food vendors, and soak up the last big weekend of summer. The balloon glow viewings — when the balloons light up against the night sky — are the stuff of COS memories.

The event is free, but parking is another story. Memorial Park fills fast, and pre-ordering your parking spot for $15 is genuinely worth it. Don't wait until the week of.

This year is the 50th anniversary of the event, which makes it a little extra special. Whether you've been going for decades or this is your first time taking the kiddos, it's one of those COS traditions worth making time for.

Parking pre-order and full event details are available through the event's official site. Are you planning to go this year? First-timers or longtime Lift Off regulars — tell us below! 👇

### STORY 4: North Pole Santa's Workshop Weekend Guide

#### Long-Form Post

If you're looking for something fun to do with the kiddos this weekend, North Pole — Santa's Workshop is open Friday through Sunday at 5050 Pikes Peak Hwy in Cascade.

It's one of those places that's a genuine Colorado Springs original. The park sits right at the base of Pikes Peak along a scenic stretch of highway, and it's been a COS family tradition for generations. It's Christmas-themed year-round: 30+ rides, a chance to meet Santa, and all the holiday décor you could want on a warm August afternoon.

Here's what you need to know for this weekend:
- Hours: 10:00 AM–5:00 PM MT (last wristband sold at 4:15 PM)
- Ride wristbands: $35 per person, ages 3–59
- Kids 2 and under: free with a paying adult
- Entry into the park is free — you only pay for rides

The park is closed on Tuesdays and Wednesdays, so this weekend's Friday–Sunday run is a good window before fall schedules kick in fully. Plan to arrive by mid-morning if possible — afternoons can get busy.

### STORY 5: CDC Flu Vaccine Study

#### Long-Form Post

Here's a number worth knowing before flu season starts: flu vaccines reduce children's risk of dying from flu by 80%. That's from a CDC study published this month in Pediatrics, the AAP's flagship journal.

The AAP recommends flu shots for every child 6 months and older, every year. And this year, there's a new piece of information for parents of young kids: injectable flu vaccines for 2026–27 carry updated prescribing information about a slightly increased risk of febrile seizures (fever-related seizures) in children under 5. This risk was identified through postmarketing studies from the 2023–24 and 2024–25 flu seasons.

To be clear — the AAP still recommends flu shots. The update is about giving parents and pediatricians accurate information to choose the best form of the vaccine for their youngest kids (nasal spray is an option for many children 2 and older). Talk to your child's pediatrician before September, when flu shots typically start becoming available.

In Colorado Springs, flu shots are usually available through UCHealth and Centura Health pediatric clinics, as well as CVS and Walgreens pharmacy clinics starting in October.
