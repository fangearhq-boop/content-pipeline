CPSC Recalls Multiple Amazon Baby Loungers Over Suffocation and Fall Hazards

By Jamie Rivera | COS Parenting | August 21, 2026

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission has recalled multiple baby lounger products sold on Amazon and other online retailers in August 2026, citing suffocation, entrapment, and fall hazards. Two of the primary brands affected are Vevor Baby Loungers and CooCooBaby Baby Loungers. If you have a baby lounger at home — especially one purchased online in the last 18 months to two years — it is worth taking a few minutes to check CPSC.gov.

What Is Being Recalled

Vevor Baby Loungers were sold from November 2024 through January 2026 at a price of approximately $30. They were available through VEVOR.com, Amazon, and Wayfair. The CPSC identified entrapment and fall hazards with these products.

CooCooBaby Baby Loungers, representing approximately 2,355 units, were recalled for suffocation and fall hazards. These loungers were also sold through online retail channels.

These two recalls are part of a broader CPSC sweep of infant lounger products in August 2026. Additional brands — including Alinux, Cpzzkq, Bimuva, and others — have also been subject to recall actions in the same category. If you own any pillow-style or cushioned infant lounger, it is worth searching the brand name on CPSC.gov to confirm its status.

Why These Products Are Considered Dangerous

Baby loungers are soft, padded products designed to support an infant in a slightly reclined or flat position. They have become popular with new parents as a place to lay a baby down for supervised rest or feeding support. However, the CPSC has consistently raised concerns about soft surfaces and confined sleep-adjacent products for infants.

Suffocation risk occurs when an infant's face is pressed against a soft surface and the baby does not have the neck or motor strength to reposition. Entrapment risk arises when a baby's body or limbs become caught in a product's structure. Fall hazard occurs when a product placed on an elevated surface tips or when the infant rolls out of a loosely structured product.

For infants, these risks are serious. The safest sleep surface for babies, according to the American Academy of Pediatrics, remains a firm, flat surface on a separate sleep space with no soft bedding, pillows, or lounger-style products. Baby loungers are not intended or approved for unsupervised sleep.

What to Do If You Own an Affected Product

Stop using the product immediately. Do not continue to use the item while waiting for remedy instructions, and do not pass it along to another family.

Visit CPSC.gov and search for the product brand name. The recall listing will include specific model numbers, date ranges, retail channels, and instructions for obtaining a refund, replacement, or repair (depending on the recall's terms).

If you purchased the product through Amazon, you can also check your order history or look for a recall notification in your Amazon account. Amazon has periodically notified buyers of CPSC recalls directly through account messages.

A Pattern Worth Knowing

This wave of baby lounger recalls is not isolated. The CPSC has been active in the infant product category for several years, and baby loungers specifically have faced increased scrutiny since the broader ban on inclined sleepers in 2022. Parents shopping for new products for infants should look for JPMA certification and check that any new product is CPSC-compliant before purchasing.

When in doubt, the simplest and safest option for infant rest remains a firm, flat crib or bassinet mattress that meets current safety standards.

What's Next

Check CPSC.gov now if you own a baby lounger — especially a Vevor or CooCooBaby brand product. The recall pages include the exact remedy instructions. You can also sign up for CPSC email alerts at CPSC.gov to receive future recall notices as they are issued.
