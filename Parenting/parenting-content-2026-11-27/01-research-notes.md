### STORY 3: Helping Kids Through Holiday Stress — AAP Guidance

**Key Facts:**

- Holiday stress affects children differently than adults — kids may show symptoms like changes in sleep or appetite, headaches, stomach aches — HIGH (multiple pediatric sources: University of Michigan, Mayo Clinic, CBS News Texas)
- Symptoms that persist more than 2 weeks warrant a pediatrician visit — HIGH (Michigan Medicine)
- University of Michigan poll of 2,020 parents found holiday logistics (shopping, tasks, finances) are the most common stressors — HIGH (U of Michigan study, cited in search results)
- Nearly a third of parents say stress comes from holiday shopping and tasks — HIGH (U of Michigan poll)
- Almost 50% of parents say most days their stress is completely overwhelming (2024 U.S. Surgeon General's report on parents) — HIGH (cited in October 2025 early-childhood health brief)
- AAP/healthychildren.org guidance: Holiday stress article on "Comfort and Joy" at healthychildren.org covers how families can enjoy holidays together — HIGH (healthychildren.org URL confirmed in search)
- Practical tips: maintain routines, model stress management, limit screen time, keep expectations realistic, involve kids in preparations — HIGH (from multiple AAP-linked sources)
- Persistent low mood in children is a legitimate concern worth discussing with pediatrician — HIGH (Michigan Medicine)
- Parents' own mental health matters and directly affects children — HIGH (Surgeon General's report context)
- Colorado Springs context: Thanksgiving weekend + upcoming holiday season = relevant timing — HIGH (editorial context)
