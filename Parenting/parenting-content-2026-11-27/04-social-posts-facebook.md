### STORY 1: Black Friday Toy Safety

#### Long-Form Post — 8:00 AM MT

```
Before you hit the sales today, here's one thing worth doing first: checking CPSC's recall list at cpsc.gov/Recalls.

This November alone, recalls have included pajamas that failed flammability standards, magnetic toys that pose ingestion risks if they break, and toy sets with button batteries that young children can access. The list gets updated regularly, and it takes less than a minute to search a brand or product category.

A few other things worth keeping in mind as you shop:

Age labels on toys aren't just suggestions — they exist because toys designed for older kids can pose real hazards (small parts, strong magnets, complex assembly) for younger children. If you're buying for a child under 3, look for the "not for children under 3" warning and steer clear.

ASTM F963 is the federal toy safety standard. When you see it on a label, it means the toy has been tested. When you don't see it on an imported toy from an unfamiliar brand, that's worth noting.

Button batteries are increasingly common in toys and games. If a battery compartment doesn't require a screwdriver to open, it's worth considering whether a younger sibling might get to it.

Happy shopping, COS — and happy Thanksgiving.
```

### STORY 3: Helping Kids Through Holiday Stress

#### Long-Form Post — 12:00 PM MT

```
Here's something that doesn't get talked about enough: the holidays can be hard on kids.

Not because they don't love the season — most of them really do. But the disrupted routines, the expectations, the travel, the family gatherings, and the general sensory overload of November through January can all add up. For some kids, that shows up as excitement. For others, it shows up as headaches, stomach aches, or changes in sleep and appetite that parents sometimes attribute to something else.

The American Academy of Pediatrics has guidance on this. The short version: watch for changes in behavior or physical symptoms that are out of the ordinary and that last more than two weeks. If you see them, it's worth a conversation with your pediatrician — not because something is necessarily wrong, but because a good check-in now can help.

What can help at home:
- Maintain as much of the normal routine as possible (bedtimes, mealtimes)
- Let kids help with holiday prep in age-appropriate ways — it gives them a sense of involvement and control
- Talk openly about your own stress. It models healthy coping and gives kids permission to share theirs
- Keep realistic expectations about how the holidays will go. Perfect is the enemy of good, and especially the enemy of your kid's nervous system

What do you do in your family to keep the holidays from getting overwhelming? Share your approach below.
```

### STORY 4: D11 Boundary Changes Community Input

#### Long-Form Post — 3:00 PM MT

```
COS families in District 11: the school district is working through a boundary review process, and community input is part of how that gets shaped.

The backdrop: D11 has seen declining enrollment in recent years — a trend that's partly driven by D-49 growing to become El Paso County's largest district. When schools have significantly more capacity than students, the district starts asking whether attendance zones need to be adjusted. That's where boundary changes come in.

What it means practically: for affected families, a boundary change could mean a different school assignment for your child — potentially a different elementary or middle school than where they're currently enrolled or expected to enroll.

About 20 residents have been attending D11's community input sessions. If you have kids in the district, or kids who will be starting school in the next few years, it's worth knowing these conversations are happening and that your voice counts in the process.

D11's website (d11.org) has information about upcoming meetings. We'll keep an eye on this story as the process moves forward.

If your family has been through a school boundary change before, what was your experience? Let us know.

### STORY 5: Holiday Travel With Kids — Survival Guide

#### Long-Form Post — 5:00 PM MT

```
Happy Thanksgiving, COS families.

If you're doing the holiday drive today — or doing it again on Sunday — here's the unofficial COS Parenting guide to traveling with kids during the most chaotic travel week of the year:

1. Pack more snacks than you think you need. Then pack more. The snacks are not for you.

2. Download everything before you leave. Streaming requires cellular data. Cellular data disappears on I-25 between COS and Pueblo. The kids will not understand why.

3. The first person to say "are we there yet" gets to hold the aux cord. This solves nothing but it creates a game, and games buy you approximately six minutes.

4. Build in a stop. The urge to push through and "just get there" is real. The reality of a 5-year-old who needs to run around at a rest stop is more real.

5. It's okay if it's not perfect. The drive that goes sideways becomes the story you tell at the next Thanksgiving. Probably.

If you're staying in Colorado Springs this weekend, the city's got you covered: Skate in the Park is open at Acacia Park, Garden of the Gods is free and stunning, and next weekend the Christkindlmarket opens in Old Colorado City.

Safe travels, COS. We hope your holiday is exactly as chaotic and wonderful as it needs to be.
