Black Friday Toy Shopping: What COS Parents Need to Check Before Hitting “Buy”

By Jamie Rivera | COS Parenting | November 27, 2026

Black Friday is here, and for many Colorado Springs families, today is the official start of holiday toy shopping. Before you head out or pull up your cart online, there are a few quick checks worth making — the kind that take less than five minutes and can spare a lot of trouble down the road.

This November, the Consumer Product Safety Commission has announced several recalls involving children's products, including pajamas that failed federal flammability standards, magnetic building toys that can release small high-powered magnets when broken, and toy sets with button batteries that young children can access. The CPSC maintains an updated recall list at cpsc.gov/Recalls. It's searchable by brand or product category, and it's worth a quick scan before any major purchase.

The Basics: Age Labels and Safety Standards

Federal law requires toy manufacturers to include age labels on products. Those labels exist because toys designed for older children can present real hazards — small parts, strong magnets, and complex assembly — for younger kids. If you're shopping for a child under three, pay attention to any label that reads "not for children under 3 years."

ASTM F963 is the federal toy safety standard. When you see this certification on a label, it means the toy has been tested against a set of safety requirements. When you're buying from an unfamiliar brand — particularly imported toys — its absence is worth noting. It doesn't automatically mean the toy is unsafe, but it's a signal to look more carefully.

The Button Battery Concern

Button batteries — the small, coin-shaped batteries found in toys, remote controls, greeting cards, and games — are increasingly flagged as a hazard for young children. If swallowed, they can cause serious internal burns within two hours. The CPSC requires toys containing button batteries to have battery compartments that require a screwdriver or tool to open. If a battery compartment opens easily by hand and you have younger children in the home, that's worth factoring into your decision.

Magnets: Know the Difference

Not all magnetic toys are the same. Traditional toy magnets — the kind in refrigerator-letter sets and basic block sets — are generally low-powered and low-risk. High-powered rare-earth magnets (neodymium) are a different category: they're extremely strong, and if two or more are swallowed separately, they can attract through intestinal walls and cause serious injury. Products containing high-powered magnets are legally required to carry a warning label for ages 14 and up. If you're buying any magnetic building set, check the packaging carefully for age labeling and magnet strength information.

Shopping Tips for Black Friday and Beyond

A few more things worth keeping in mind as you shop this holiday season:

  • Keep the age range in mind for every child in the household, not just the one you're buying for. A toy that's right for an 8-year-old may have parts a toddler sibling can reach.
  • Register for recall notifications at cpsc.gov. If a product you've bought gets recalled, you'll be notified by email.
  • Save receipts and packaging through the holiday season, particularly for products from unfamiliar brands. If a recall is issued in January, you'll want to have that information available.
  • Gift wrapping obscures brand names. If you're giving secondhand toys or gifts from unfamiliar sources, check the brand against the recall list before wrapping.

What's Next

The CPSC recall list at cpsc.gov/Recalls is updated continuously and is the most reliable source for current recall information. If you're unsure about any product — toy, clothing, furniture, or otherwise — it's your first stop. Happy shopping, COS families, and happy Thanksgiving.
