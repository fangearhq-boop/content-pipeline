Surviving Holiday Travel With Kids: A Practical Guide for COS Families

By Jamie Rivera | COS Parenting | November 27, 2026

If you're loading up the car today, good luck — you're in excellent company and terrible traffic. Thanksgiving is statistically the most traveled holiday of the year, and if you're heading out of Colorado Springs on I-25, I-70, or US-24 this weekend, every other family in the region has had the same idea at approximately the same time. Here's what actually helps.

Before You Leave

The single most effective thing you can do before any holiday drive with children is download everything. Not "mostly" download it, not "trust that the signal will be fine" — actually download the shows, the audiobooks, the playlist, and the podcasts before you back out of the driveway. Cellular data on Colorado highway stretches, particularly between COS and Pueblo on I-25 and in the mountain corridors, is unreliable in ways that become catastrophic when a four-year-old is mid-episode of their favorite show.

Pack the snacks last, after everything else is loaded. This accomplishes two things: the snacks are accessible immediately, and you won't spend 20 minutes excavating them from under the luggage when someone announces they're hungry 11 minutes into the drive. Pack more snacks than you think you need. The estimate of how many snacks you need is always wrong in the same direction.

On the Road

Build in a stop. The temptation to push through and "just get there" is powerful, and it's also how you end up with a catastrophic meltdown 45 minutes from your destination. Children — particularly those under eight — do not handle extended confinement well, regardless of the quality of their entertainment. A 15-minute stop at a rest area where they can run around, use the facilities, and eat something is an investment that pays off in the second half of the drive.

Colorado has decent rest stops on most major corridors. The Raton Pass area on I-25 south toward New Mexico is actually a reasonable stop if you're heading that direction — there's a visitor center there that breaks the drive nicely. If you're heading north toward Denver, the Castle Rock area has options, and that stretch is also where I-25 tends to compress into slow traffic anyway.

Set expectations honestly before you go. Tell the kids how long the drive actually is, not how long you hope it will be if traffic cooperates. Children who know the drive is four hours manage four hours better than children who were told "about two hours" and are now experiencing the fourth hour with no explanation. Disappointment plus confusion is worse than just disappointment.

Altitude and Mountain Routes

If your holiday travel involves a mountain route — Pikes Peak, Trail Ridge Road (which is closed for the season), or any of the passes — keep altitude in mind for young children and infants. Kids who live in Colorado Springs at 6,000-plus feet are already acclimated, but any travel to significantly higher elevations can produce symptoms in children who aren't used to it: headache, nausea, fatigue. If you're heading above 10,000 feet with young children, hydrate them well before the ascent and don't let the symptoms catch you off guard.

Road Weather

November and December in Colorado are unpredictable. The I-70 mountain corridor and US-24 through Woodland Park are both subject to rapid closures when conditions deteriorate. CDOT's cotrip.org is the best real-time source for road conditions, closures, and camera views on Colorado highways. Check it before you leave and have it bookmarked on your phone. If a pass is closed or showing red conditions, the right call is usually to wait — a two-hour delay at home is better than a multi-hour standstill in a canyon.

Pack a small emergency kit in your trunk during winter travel: a blanket, water, some snacks, and a phone charger. This is true whether or not you have kids in the car, but it matters more when you do.

If You're Staying in COS

If the holiday drive isn't on your agenda this weekend, Colorado Springs is genuinely a good place to be right now. Skate in the Park at Acacia Park runs through the winter. Garden of the Gods is free and spectacular in every season, and the November light on the red rocks is worth making a trip for. The Pikes Peak Summit is open weather permitting, and the drive up is a half-day activity that never really gets old.

Next week, the Old Colorado City Christkindlmarket opens December 4 at Bancroft Park — free admission, noon to 8 p.m. That's a full day of holiday activity without leaving the city. COS has what it needs to do the holidays well without getting on the highway.

What's Next

For road conditions on Colorado highways, check cotrip.org before any significant drive this season. If you're flying out of Colorado Springs Airport, COS has expanded its direct routes in recent years — check their website for updated destinations before automatically routing through DIA. And if the drive goes sideways in some memorable way: those stories tend to become the ones families tell for years. Safe travels, COS.
