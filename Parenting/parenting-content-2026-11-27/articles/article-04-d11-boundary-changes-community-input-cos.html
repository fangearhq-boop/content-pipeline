D11 Is Reviewing School Boundaries — Here's What COS Families Should Know

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting | November 27, 2026

Colorado Springs School District 11 is currently conducting a boundary review process that could result in changes to school attendance zones. If your family lives in D11 and has children in elementary or middle school — or will be starting school in the next few years — this is a process worth paying attention to now, while community input is still being collected.

What a Boundary Review Means

School district boundaries determine which neighborhood school a child attends. When a district re-examines those boundaries, it's typically in response to enrollment shifts: either population growth in one area that outpaces school capacity, or declining enrollment that leaves schools operating significantly below their capacity.

D11's situation falls into the latter category. The district has seen declining enrollment over recent years — a trend that's partly connected to D-49 (Falcon School District) growing to become one of El Paso County's larger districts. When school buildings have significantly more capacity than students, the district faces real operational costs: staffing buildings that aren't full, maintaining facilities, transportation routes. A boundary review is how districts address the structural side of that problem.

What Changes Could Look Like

Boundary changes can take several forms. In some cases, they result in students being reassigned to a different school than where they currently attend or would have attended based on their home address. In other cases, they consolidate attendance zones — meaning two schools' catchment areas are merged into one — sometimes alongside school closures or repurposing of facilities.

At this point in D11's process, specific proposals may or may not have been made public — the district's review is ongoing and community input is a formal part of how proposals get shaped. Families who are potentially affected should monitor D11's communications directly for proposal specifics rather than relying on secondhand accounts.

Why Community Input Matters at This Stage

School boundary decisions are made by district leadership and school boards, not by community vote. But school boards do hold open meetings, and community input sessions — the kind D11 is currently running — are specifically designed to collect feedback before proposals are finalized.

Historically, when community input is concentrated from one part of a district and sparse from another, that imbalance shows up in the feedback record and can influence which options move forward. About 20 residents have been attending D11's community input sessions so far — a relatively modest turnout for a district that serves thousands of students. If you're in D11, your attendance and your perspective have more weight at this stage than they will once proposals are drafted.

What to Watch For

A few things worth tracking if your family is in D11:

  • Which schools are involved. Not every D11 school will be in scope for boundary changes. The district will specify which attendance zones are being reviewed — check d11.org for current information about which campuses are part of the review.
  • The timeline. Boundary change processes typically have public comment periods with specific deadlines. Missing those windows means your input isn't formally part of the record.
  • Transition provisions. When boundary changes do get approved, they usually include provisions for students already enrolled at a school — phased transitions, sibling policies, or grandfather clauses that let current students stay through a grade level. Those details matter as much as the boundary lines themselves.

D49's Role in the Context

It's worth understanding the broader dynamic driving this review. D-49, the Falcon School District covering the eastern part of El Paso County, has grown significantly over the past decade as development has pushed east of COS. As families have moved into the Falcon corridor and new schools have opened there, some enrollment that might have remained in D11 has shifted instead to D49. That growth in D49 is a separate school district with no connection to D11 governance — but it explains part of the enrollment trajectory D11 is responding to.

What's Next

D11's website at d11.org is the most reliable source for information about the boundary review process, upcoming community meetings, and any proposals that have been made public. COS Parenting will continue to track this story as the process moves toward a decision. If your family is in D11 and has questions about how a potential boundary change might affect your child's school placement, the district's enrollment office is the right first contact.
