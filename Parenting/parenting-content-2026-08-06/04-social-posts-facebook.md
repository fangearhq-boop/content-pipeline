### STORY 1: D11 Bond Vote Approved

**Long-Form Post (8:00 AM MT):**
The District 11 Board of Education voted Wednesday night to approve ballot language for a $775 million bond measure.

The bond will appear before Colorado Springs voters in the November 2026 coordinated election. If approved, it would fund repairs at every D11 school — buildings that average 58 years old and carry an estimated $422 million in deferred maintenance. The money would also free up existing budget funds that could then go toward increasing teacher pay.

Supporters of the bond have pointed to the district's limited annual capital budget — approximately $17 million — as inadequate to address the scale of the maintenance backlog. The bond would let the district catch up in a way that ongoing budgets cannot.

For families, the estimated tax impact would be about $6.50 per month for every $100,000 of assessed home value.

D11 families have a direct stake in this vote. The first day of school is August 12 — just one week away.

Do you plan to vote for the D11 bond in November? What would change your mind?

### STORY 2: D11 Edukit School Supplies

**Long-Form Post (10:00 AM MT):**
One less thing to worry about before the first day of school.

Colorado Springs District 11 is partnering with Edukit this year to deliver free, grade-specific school supply kits directly to more than 14,000 K-8 students in their classrooms — on the first day of school, Wednesday, August 12.

There is nothing to order, nothing to pick up, and no shopping required. The kits are handled entirely by the program and arrive ready for students when they walk through the door.

One thing worth noting: the Edukit program does not replace the specific supply list your child's teacher may send home. There may be items a teacher requests that are not in the kit. But for the core supplies — the things that end up on every back-to-school list — families should be covered.

For parents managing a packed August calendar alongside school prep, this is a meaningful relief.

D11 first day is August 12. D49 is already back. D20 starts August 17-18.

### STORY 4: GOPO Toys Teething Recall

**Long-Form Post (3:00 PM MT):**
If you have purchased a GOPO Toys Pull String Teething Toy — especially from Amazon over the past few years — you need to check your home.

The Consumer Product Safety Commission recalled approximately 70,000 of these toys. The hazard is specific: the silicone strings attached to the toy are smaller in diameter and longer in length than what is permitted under mandatory safety standards for toys. That design means the strings can reach the back of a young child's throat, causing choking or respiratory distress.

The CPSC has received three incident reports.

The toy itself looks harmless. It is an off-white disc shape with a grey ball in the center and six pull-string tentacles in different colors, plus seven push buttons on the disc face. It was sold on Amazon.com from August 2023 through March 2026 for between $11 and $15.

If you have this product, stop using it immediately. The remedy: cut all silicone strings, write the word "DESTROYED" on the main body with a permanent marker, photograph it, and send the photo to recalls@gopotoys.com to receive a full refund.

If you are unsure whether you have this product, check Amazon order history for GOPO Toys purchases in that window.

### STORY 5: Colorado Farm & Art Market

**Long-Form Post (5:00 PM MT):**
Looking for a free Wednesday outing that works for the whole family?

The Colorado Farm & Art Market is back at Alamo Square Park in Colorado Springs every Wednesday through October 9, from 3:00 PM to 7:00 PM. The market features local fruits, vegetables, and herbs alongside handcrafted artisan goods from the region.

It is free to attend and designed to be family-friendly.

With District 49 already back in school and District 11 starting August 12, Wednesdays from 3 to 7 PM hit right in the after-school window. A quick stop at the market — picking up local produce or letting younger children explore the artisan tables — fits naturally into the school-week rhythm.

The market runs through the first week of October, which means there are more than two months of Wednesdays left in the season.

Alamo Square Park is central and accessible, and the market has become one of Colorado Springs's reliable warm-season community gathering spots.

### STORY 6: Weekend Family Roundup — August 7–9

**Long-Form Post (8:00 PM MT):**
This is it — the last full weekend before D11 starts Wednesday, August 12, and the best chance for a relaxed family outing before the school-year schedule takes over.

Here are five picks for Colorado Springs families this weekend:

Garden of the Gods is always free and always worth it. August mornings before 10 AM or evenings after 4 PM are the sweet spot — cooler temperatures, dramatic light, and the main loop is stroller-friendly.

Friday evening, Cheyenne Mountain Zoo opens its gates to leashed, vaccinated dogs for Dog Days — a genuinely fun combination for families with dogs and school-age kids. Regular zoo admission applies.

Saturday and Sunday, the Mountain Arts Festival runs in Woodland Park (18 miles west) with 70+ artists. The relaxed outdoor format works well for older kids and families who enjoy browsing without a rigid schedule.

Sunday morning, the Downtown Acacia Park Farmers Market runs 9 AM to 2 PM — free to browse, central location, good for fresh produce and a walk before the afternoon fills up.

And any time this weekend, the Pikes Peak Library District branches are free, air-conditioned, and wrapping up summer programming. A good time to return summer reading books and check fall schedules.

What is your family doing this weekend before school starts?
