With school back in session for D49 families and starting next week for D11, after-school and evening activities that are free, accessible, and genuinely enjoyable become more valuable. The Colorado Farm & Art Market at Alamo Square Park fits that description well.

The market runs every Wednesday through October 9, 2026, from 3:00 PM to 7:00 PM at Alamo Square Park in central Colorado Springs. Admission is free. The market features local fruits, vegetables, and fresh herbs alongside handcrafted artisan goods from the region — a combination that draws both families shopping for produce and families looking for a community outing.

Why It Works as a Family Outing

The market's timing is well-suited to the school-year schedule. A 3:00 PM start catches the after-school window on days when pickup is early, and the 7:00 PM close allows for an early evening visit for families whose afternoon is already full.

Alamo Square Park's location in central Colorado Springs makes it accessible from multiple parts of the city. The outdoor market setting — open air, manageable in size, with clear sightlines — is generally easier to navigate with young children than a crowded indoor event.

For families who shop local produce, the market also serves a practical function. Local fruits and vegetables, seasonal herbs, and artisan food products from the region are available in one place, with vendors who can speak directly to how and where their products were grown.

What to Expect

The market includes a mix of fresh produce vendors and artisan crafters. On any given Wednesday, families are likely to find seasonal Colorado vegetables, fresh herbs, locally made jams or baked goods, and handcrafted items — jewelry, home goods, seasonal gifts — depending on the week.

Bringing a reusable bag for produce is practical. Cash is often welcome at vendor stalls, though many have adapted to accept card payment as well.

How Many Wednesdays Are Left?

With the market running through October 9, there are more than nine Wednesdays remaining in the season from today. The fall stretch — September through early October — often features some of the strongest Colorado produce weeks as the summer harvest comes in and temperatures cool.

What's Next
