Where to Get Flu Shots for Kids in Colorado Springs This Fall

By Jamie Rivera | October 9, 2026

Colorado Springs, Colorado

Colorado had five pediatric deaths from influenza during the 2025-26 flu season — and the state's child vaccination rate last year was approximately 34.4%, well below the national average of around 52%. The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends that all children 6 months and older get vaccinated against flu, ideally by the end of October, to allow immunity to develop before flu season peaks.

For Colorado Springs families, the question isn't whether to vaccinate — it's where to go. Here are the local options.

Where to Go in Colorado Springs

Penrose-St. Francis Health Services

Penrose-St. Francis operates multiple locations across Colorado Springs and runs walk-in flu clinics throughout fall. Call (719) 776-5000 to find the clinic nearest you and confirm current hours. Most visits accept insurance; uninsured patients should ask about cash pricing when scheduling.

UCHealth Memorial Hospital

UCHealth runs flu shot clinics at multiple COS-area locations. Visit uchealth.org to find locations and check current availability. UCHealth accepts most major insurance plans.

El Paso County Public Health

El Paso County Public Health offers free or low-cost flu vaccines for families without insurance coverage. This is the primary local option for uninsured and underinsured families. Call (719) 578-3199 for clinic schedules and eligibility information.

Walgreens, CVS, and Safeway Pharmacy

Most pharmacy locations in Colorado Springs offer walk-in flu shots for children ages 4 and up, with some locations offering shots for children ages 3 and up with a provider note. Most take insurance; out-of-pocket cost without coverage is typically under $25. No appointment needed at most locations — call ahead to confirm pediatric availability at your specific store.

School-Based Clinics

D11 and D49 schools sometimes coordinate flu shot days through the school nurse program. Watch for flyers or emails from your school's health office — school-based clinics are often the most convenient option for families whose kids are already in school full time.

University of Colorado Colorado Springs (UCCS)

UCCS has offered community flu clinics in fall 2026 as part of its public health programming. These may be available to families of students, staff, and members of the public. Check the UCCS campus health page or call the student health center for details on community access.

What to Know Before You Go

Flu vaccines are available as a standard injection or as FluMist, a nasal spray approved for healthy children between 2 and 49 years old. If your child is hesitant about needles, asking about FluMist at your preferred clinic is worth the call.

Children under 9 years old who are receiving a flu vaccine for the first time — or who have only received one flu shot previously — need two doses, given at least four weeks apart. If this applies to your child, schedule the first dose now to allow time for the second dose before flu season peaks.

Scheduling flu shots for all family members in one trip reduces the number of clinic visits. Most providers who see children for flu vaccines will also administer the shot for accompanying adults.

What's Next

The AAP recommends vaccination by the end of October for the best protection during peak flu months, which typically run November through March in Colorado. If October doesn't work, a November or December vaccination still provides meaningful protection — but sooner is better. Colorado CDPHE tracks flu activity statewide at cdphe.colorado.gov; that's the best place to watch for real-time updates on when flu is circulating in El Paso County. For families with children who have underlying health conditions, talk to your pediatrician about timing and any additional precautions for the 2026-27 season.
