Making the Most of 15 Minutes

Parent-teacher conferences are typically 15 to 20 minutes, which sounds like enough time until you're sitting down and the clock is moving. A few ways to use that time well:

Come with specific questions written down. "How is my child doing?" is a reasonable opener but it eats time. More focused questions — "Is my child reading at grade level?" or "How are they handling group work?" — get to information faster. Bring any schoolwork samples or notes you've been keeping at home if you want to show the teacher what you're seeing on your end.

Ask the teacher what they want you to know, not just what you want to ask. Teachers often have observations they won't lead with unless prompted. "Is there anything you've noticed that I should know about?" opens that door.

Before you leave, establish the next step. Whether it's a follow-up email check-in in a month or a specific skill to practice at home, a concrete action keeps the conference from being a one-time event. Most D11 and D49 teachers are accessible via email throughout the year — the conference is a good chance to confirm the best way to reach them.

What's Next

D49 families have fall break coming up in just three days — break begins Sunday, October 12, with students returning on October 20. D11 fall break runs October 19-23. If the conference today surfaces any learning goals or concerns, the break can actually be a good window to work on those things with your child in a lower-pressure environment.

D11 and D49 both offer additional conference opportunities throughout the year. If today's conversation felt rushed or left you with more questions than answers, reach out to your child's teacher directly — most will make time for a follow-up conversation when they know a family wants one.
