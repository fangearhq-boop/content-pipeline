### STORY 1: Palmer Ridge Refuses Forfeit — Lutheran Controversy Escalates

#### Long-Form Post

The Lutheran High School controversy that started in Colorado Springs is getting more complicated — and today we've got a new wrinkle.

Coronado High School and Air Academy High School both forfeited games against Lutheran High of Parker over racism allegations. Students from Sand Creek (D49) and Rampart (D20) alleged racist remarks and gestures during football games on September 24 and October 2. Both forfeiting schools said it came down to safety and values — D11 noted it had requested a statement denouncing racism from both Lutheran and CHSAA and received no response from either at the time.

Now two things have happened: D11 and CHSAA both issued statements this week denouncing racism. And Palmer Ridge, another COS-area school with a game against Lutheran on October 30, says its team will NOT forfeit — breaking ranks with Coronado and Air Academy. 🏈

Meanwhile, the community response is growing. The NAACP branches in both Douglas County and Colorado Springs acknowledged steps Lutheran has taken, but requested a meeting with the administration. State Rep. Regina English expressed concern that adults involved hadn't "intervened appropriately." CHSAA says it's still investigating whether incidents violated its bylaws.

Lutheran exec director Dan Gehrke maintains the school condemns racism and will take disciplinary action if its own investigation finds it warranted.

Where do you stand — should schools continue playing Lutheran while the investigation is open, or wait for findings first?

### STORY 2: Boo at the Zoo Opens Friday

#### Long-Form Post

Halloween season officially kicks off in Colorado Springs this Friday — and if you haven't grabbed your Boo at the Zoo tickets yet, now's the moment. 🎃

Cheyenne Mountain Zoo's beloved Halloween event runs on six nights this year: October 16, 17, 23, 24, 25, 30, and 31, from 4 to 8:30 PM each night. Think 25 trick-or-treat stations (including non-candy options for kids with allergies), giraffe and budgie feedings, a spooky graveyard, and lit pumpkins at Magic Manor. The big thing to know this year: there are NO walk-up tickets. None. You have to purchase in advance at cmzoo.org, and some nights are already close to selling out. Zoo members still need tickets too — but you do get $5 off, so it's worth logging in through your member account.

If you've got kids who love animals and love Halloween (so... basically all kids), this is the one. Friday and Saturday evenings book fastest, so if you've been waiting — stop waiting.

### STORY 3: Sweet Cubby Sleepwear Recall

#### Long-Form Post

If you've bought kids' loungewear from Shein in the past year, this one's worth checking.

The CPSC announced a recall of Sweet Cubby two-piece children's loungewear sets — the kind with a short-sleeve shirt and matching shorts. About 12,461 units are affected, in sizes 5-6Y through 13-14Y. The problem: they fail the mandatory children's sleepwear flammability standard, which means they're a burn hazard.

These sets were sold on Shein.com from June 2025 through May 2026, for roughly $8 to $12. To check if yours are part of the recall, look for batch number "272779" printed on the neck label and the seam.

What to do: stop using them immediately, keep them away from your kids, cut the sets in half (yes — cut them in half), and then email a photo to SweetCubby@163.com. The company will issue a full refund. No injuries have been reported.

It's a quick check that takes 30 seconds — and $8 or $12 back in your pocket isn't a bad deal for a few minutes of digging through the drawer.

Have you bought kids' clothing from Shein before? Drop a note if you've got questions about checking the label.

### STORY 4: D11 Bond 4A — Drop Boxes Open

#### Long-Form Post

If you've got a D11 mail ballot sitting on your kitchen counter, the drop boxes are now open.

El Paso County ballot drop boxes are available now for returning your ballot ahead of the November 3 election. One of the measures on that ballot is D11's Measure 4A — a $775 million school bond that would fund safety and security upgrades, HVAC and asbestos removal, expanded CTE programs, classroom modernization, and library improvements across the district.

For a typical D11 homeowner, the cost would be roughly $260 per year in property taxes. The D11 board voted 6-1 on August 5 to put the measure on the ballot.

Whether you're voting yes or no, drop boxes are the easiest way to return your ballot without standing in line. You don't need a stamp. You don't need to find Election Day parking. You can also still vote in person on November 3 if you prefer.

Head to d11.org/4A for the full measure breakdown, and check the El Paso County Clerk's site for drop box locations near you.

### STORY 5: Ricky Joy Sour Crush Candy Recall

#### Long-Form Post

With Halloween 17 days away, here's a recall worth knowing about before you buy (or hand out) Halloween candy.

The CPSC announced a recall of Ricky Joy Sour Crush candy — those small sour liquid candy bottles with a rollerball on top. About 2.3 million bottles have been recalled because the rollerball applicator can pop off, creating a choking hazard for kids. No injuries have been reported so far, but the CPSC considers the hazard serious enough to pull 2.3 million units.

The product is a 1.85-fluid-ounce bottle of sour liquid candy applied through the rollerball top — the kind you might see at checkout counters, candy stores, or in Halloween variety packs. The recall was announced around September 11, 2026.

If you've got these at home or in your Halloween stash, stop using them and check cpsc.gov/Recalls for the remedy and return instructions.

With candy season kicking into high gear, it's a good time to do a quick sweep of anything you've already stocked up on. A minute of checking beats a trip to the ER.
