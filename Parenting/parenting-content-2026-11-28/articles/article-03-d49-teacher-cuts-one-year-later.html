D49 Nine Months After Its Teacher Cuts: What Colorado Springs Families Are Seeing Now

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting | November 28, 2026

In early 2026, Falcon School District 49 made headlines across Colorado Springs with a move that alarmed families: the district declared a fiscal exigency — a formal declaration of financial emergency — and eliminated 12 teaching positions. For a district that became the largest in El Paso County by enrollment in 2025, those cuts hit close to home for a lot of families.

The 12 positions were far fewer than the roughly 100 that had initially been projected when the fiscal exigency was declared. That was reassuring. But even 12 positions affect real classrooms, real students, and real teachers who lost their jobs. Nine months into the 2026-27 school year, it's worth checking in on what the district looks like now.

What Happened

D49's board declared a fiscal exigency in January 2026 and finalized the elimination of 12 teaching positions in February 2026. The process moved quickly by district standards — from declaration to cuts in roughly a month.

At the time, district leadership pointed to enrollment projections, state funding formulas, and long-term budget sustainability as the driving factors. The original projections of up to 100 possible cuts reflected worst-case modeling; the final 12 represented where the board landed after reviewing exactly which positions were essential to maintaining core academic programs.

Twelve families still had their teachers' positions eliminated. Those teachers had to find new positions, leave the profession, or join the reserve pool. For families whose children had those teachers, there were transitions mid-year or at the start of the new school year. That disruption was real, even if the number was smaller than feared.

D49's Size Makes This Matter

Context matters here: D49 is the largest school district in El Paso County by enrollment. That means it touches more Colorado Springs families than any other district. When D49 has financial instability, the ripple effects are felt widely — by families choosing between districts, by teachers weighing job security, and by the community's overall confidence in public education in the region.

The district's growth in enrollment has been consistent over the past several years, driven largely by the rapid development in the eastern Colorado Springs area. That growth should be a financial positive — more students generally means more state per-pupil funding — but it also creates short-term capacity pressures on staff and facilities that can outpace funding timelines.

What D49 Families Are Watching

For families with kids currently in D49 schools, the questions worth asking your school's principal or attending board meetings about include: how have class sizes changed from last year? Are there support staff or specialists — reading interventionists, counselors, librarians — who weren't replaced when the positions were cut? Have extracurricular programs been affected?

These are the downstream effects of budget cuts that often don't appear in a press release but show up in day-to-day school life. D49 board meetings are public and open, and they're one of the best ways to stay informed about where the district's financial picture stands heading into the second half of the school year and budget planning for 2027-28.

What's Next

D49's next budget cycle will begin taking shape in early 2027. If you have kids in D49 schools and want to stay informed, the district's board meeting schedule is posted at district49.net. The fiscal exigency period is behind them, but the financial decisions made in that period are still being felt. Engaged families asking questions is one of the most effective forms of accountability available in a school district this size.
