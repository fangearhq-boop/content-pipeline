Holiday Pajama Alert: Multiple Children's Sleepwear Brands Recalled for Fire Hazard

By Jamie Rivera | COS Parenting | November 28, 2026

Before you pick up a set of matching holiday pajamas as a gift this year, the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission has an important list for you to check. Several children's sleepwear brands have been recalled this month for failing mandatory federal flammability standards, and some of the items involved were sold widely on Amazon through the summer of 2026.

The recalls cover a range of sellers, from smaller online brands to larger platform retailers. The common thread is the same in each case: the pajamas don't meet federal safety requirements for children's sleepwear, creating a burn hazard for the kids who wear them.

The Qpancy Recall

The most recent significant recall comes from Qpancy (recall No. 27-094), a brand operated by Shanghai Shengkailong Trading Co., Ltd., of China. The company is recalling children's pajama sets that were sold on Amazon.com from March 2026 through August 2026 for approximately $20.

The pajamas violate mandatory federal standards for children's sleepwear. The CPSC found they don't meet the flammability requirements that are designed to give children time to escape or be rescued in the event of a fire. No injuries have been reported, but the agency is asking families to stop using the recalled pajamas immediately.

The remedy for the Qpancy recall has an extra step compared to most: owners need to cut the pajamas in half and email a photo of the destroyed garment to the company for a full refund. That step is designed to prevent recalled items from being resold or given away.

Other November 2026 Children's Sleepwear Recalls

Qpancy isn't alone. The CPSC has issued multiple children's sleepwear recalls in 2026, including actions against Mallimoda (pajama sets sold on Amazon), Black Sheep Fam (children's pajamas), and Veseacky (pajama sets). Each involves the same type of violation: failure to meet the mandatory federal flammability standard for children's sleepwear.

Federal law requires all children's sleepwear to either use flame-resistant fabric or be snug-fitting. Snug-fitting pajamas are less likely to catch fire because they don't have loose fabric that can contact a heat source. When brands skip this requirement, they put kids at risk.

The pattern across these recalls is worth noting for holiday shoppers: many involve smaller brands sold exclusively through Amazon or other marketplace platforms, where product safety vetting is less rigorous than traditional retail. That doesn't mean every small-brand Amazon pajama is unsafe, but it's worth doing a quick check before gifting.

What's Next

With the holiday shopping season now in full swing, this is the right time to check your kids' current pajama drawer, not just the gifts you're planning to give. If you have pajamas purchased from smaller online sellers in the past year, a few minutes at cpsc.gov could matter. For the Qpancy recall specifically, contact information for the company is listed in the CPSC notice. And if you're shopping for new pajamas this season, look for the snug-fit designation or a flame-resistant fabric label — it's the clearest signal that a product has met the safety standard.
