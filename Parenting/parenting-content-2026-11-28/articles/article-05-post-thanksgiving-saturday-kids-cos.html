The Day After Thanksgiving: A COS Parent's Guide to Post-Holiday Saturday

By Jamie Rivera | COS Parenting | November 28, 2026

Thanksgiving happened yesterday. We're not going to relitigate it. The turkey was fine. The mashed potatoes were excellent. Someone at the table said something that required a 20-minute debrief after the guests left. The kids ate mostly bread rolls and a suspicious amount of cranberry sauce. All normal.

Now it's Saturday. The kids woke up at 6:15 because they always wake up at 6:15, the routine is completely unmoored, and you have one day to figure out how to get everyone through it before Sunday planning kicks in and life technically has structure again.

Colorado Springs families, here's your guide.

Option 1: Get Outside (Actually Works)

Bear Creek Regional Park and Red Rocks Open Space are free, they're open, and they're genuinely one of the best things about living in Colorado Springs. Twenty to thirty minutes of cold, fresh mountain air does something for overstimulated kids that no indoor activity can replicate. It doesn't have to be a hike — even a walk to the creek and back is enough to change the energy in the household for the next two hours.

If the kids are old enough and you have the layers for it, Cheyenne Mountain State Park is another option. The trails are quieter after Thanksgiving when most of the town has gone into full couch recovery mode. That's your window.

Pack the thermos. Bring the dog if you have one. Consider this your one guaranteed win of the day.

Option 2: The Leftovers + Movie Afternoon (Also Works)

There is nothing wrong with a deliberate couch afternoon. We've collectively decided that's lazy, but that's a judgment that doesn't survive contact with actual small children who have been off their schedules for three days.

The specific formula: leftover pie, the one movie the whole family agrees on, the couch, and a blanket per person. This is not giving up. This is recharging before the holiday season gets busier. December is coming, and December in Colorado Springs is not slow.

Use the downtime to look up Electric Safari tickets at Cheyenne Mountain Zoo, which opens Tuesday, December 2. Tickets sell out. Buy them now while you're on the couch with nothing else demanding your attention.

Option 3: Head Start on Christmas Decorations

Hear this out: telling the kids "we're setting up the Christmas decorations today" is not just a holiday activity. It's a two-to-three-hour structured project that uses up energy, produces visible results, and results in a house that looks significantly better by bedtime.

The tree is the main event. Kids who are old enough to hang ornaments feel genuinely invested in the outcome, and kids who aren't old enough will be very committed to touching every single ornament repeatedly. Both are fine. The chaos is part of it.

Colorado Springs has good local options for real trees if you don't have an artificial one — several lots are open this weekend, and buying local is always a solid call in a city that cares about its small businesses.

The One Rule

Thanksgiving weekend recovery with kids in Colorado Springs has one rule: don't try to make it too productive. The holiday season runs for another month. This weekend is a bridge, not a beginning.

Whatever you do with the day, do it in a way that leaves everyone — including you — with slightly more energy than you started with. That's the whole goal. You can plan everything else tomorrow.
