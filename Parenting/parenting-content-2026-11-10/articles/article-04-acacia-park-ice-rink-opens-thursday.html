The Downtown Ice Rink Is Opening Thursday — Everything COS Families Should Know

By Jamie Rivera | November 10, 2026

Local Events

The Acacia Park outdoor ice rink in downtown Colorado Springs opens this Thursday, November 13 — and with it, the official start of COS holiday season. The rink is one of the city's most beloved winter traditions, and opening day has a particular energy that's worth experiencing if you've never been.

One of the best features: kids age 4 and under skate free with a paying adult. If you've been waiting for the right moment to introduce little ones to skating, this is it.

What to Know Before You Go

  • Opening day: Thursday, November 13, 2026
  • Location: Acacia Park, downtown Colorado Springs
  • Free skating: Children age 4 and under skate free with a paying adult
  • Skate rentals: Available at the rink — check the official rink listings for current pricing and hours
  • Season run: The rink operates through the winter season; plan multiple visits

Dress in layers — the rink is outdoors, and even a mild Colorado day feels colder on the ice. Wool socks make a real difference in skate comfort, especially for kids who are new to skating.

The Downtown Holiday Experience

The ice rink doesn't exist in isolation — it's the anchor of a broader downtown holiday experience that builds throughout November and December. Boutiques along Tejon Street and the surrounding blocks have holiday programming, pop-up shops, and extended hours timed to the rink season. It's one of those things that makes COS feel like a place with real holiday tradition rather than just a mall parking lot.

Opening weekend is worth making a full outing. Skate first, then walk a few blocks, grab something warm to eat, and take in what downtown has going on. The holiday lights are up, the energy is there.

Tips for Skating with Young Kids

  • First-timers: Most rinks have skating aids (penguin-shaped pushers kids can hold for balance). Ask at the rental counter if they're available.
  • Helmet consideration: Helmets aren't always required at outdoor rinks, but for very young skaters or those just learning, a bike helmet works and can save a lot of grief.
  • Keep sessions short: Young kids tire quickly on the ice. A 30-45 minute session is often the sweet spot before cold and frustration set in.
  • Warm up after: Plan a coffee or hot cocoa stop nearby. It becomes part of the tradition.

What's Next

The rink runs through the winter season, so there's no pressure to cram everything into one visit. But opening weekend has a special first-of-season feeling that's worth catching. Check the official Acacia Park rink listings for current hours, ticket prices, and any opening weekend events before you go. We'll continue to share updates on downtown holiday programming throughout the season.
