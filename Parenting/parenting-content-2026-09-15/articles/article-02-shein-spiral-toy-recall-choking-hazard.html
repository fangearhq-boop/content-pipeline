SHEIN Recalls About 963 Spiral Toys Due to Choking Hazard for Children Under 3

By Jamie Rivera

September 15, 2026

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission has announced that SHEIN Distribution Corporation is recalling about 963 spiral toys because small ball components violate the federal small ball ban for toys intended for children under age 3. If you've ordered this type of toy from SHEIN in the past year, here's what to know.

What the Toy Looks Like

The recalled product is a spiral toy that comes with stackable colored platforms — three, five, or seven platforms depending on the model — and several small yellow plastic balls topped with bear-shaped heads. The toy also has a basket component at the top. It was marketed and sold on SHEIN.com, priced between $6 and $16.

The problem is in those small balls. Federal law prohibits small ball components in toys intended for children younger than 3 because they can lodge completely in a child's airway and cause choking or death. The balls in this toy meet the definition of a prohibited small ball for that age group.

Sales Window

The toys were sold exclusively on SHEIN.com from September 2025 through May 2026. This is SHEIN's fourth recall for violations of the small ball ban since August 2025, according to the CPSC and reporting from Medical Daily. If you've purchased any spinning or stacking toy from SHEIN during that window that includes small ball components, it's worth checking the CPSC recall page to confirm whether you have the recalled product.

No injuries have been reported in connection with this recall.

What to Do

Stop using the toy immediately and take it away from children. Contact SHEIN for a full refund. The official recall page is published on CPSC.gov — search for "SHEIN Distribution Corporation Recalls Spiral Toys" to find the full product listing and contact information for the refund process.

You do not need to ship the toy back — SHEIN is providing refunds remotely. Check the recall page for the current process and any photo requirements.

What's Next

Water bead toys, small ball toys, and other novelty products from online marketplaces have generated a significant number of CPSC recalls in recent years. If your family purchases toys from online retailers, it's worth bookmarking CPSC.gov and checking it periodically for new recall announcements. The CPSC also offers a free email and text alert service at cpsc.gov/Recalls that notifies you when new recalls are announced — a straightforward way to stay ahead of safety issues without having to search manually.
