### STORY 1: D11 Bond — What's Next

**Long-Form Post (8:00 AM MT)**

It's official (or close enough): D11's $775 million bond is on track to pass with roughly 51.7% of voters saying yes. 🏫

For COS families, that "yes" vote is just the beginning. The real story now is what happens next — and there's a lot to track.

The board has committed to redirecting mill levy override funds to teacher pay now that the bond has passed. Holmes Middle School gets an $80 million rebuild. The district is also proposing a citizen oversight committee to watch how the bond money gets spent. That oversight piece matters: at $775 million, this is the largest single bond in El Paso County history.

The construction timeline will play out over years, not months. Families with kids in D11 schools right now are the ones who will feel the first rounds of changes — and they're also the ones most worth keeping in the loop as the board moves from "it passed" to "here's what we're actually building."

### STORY 2: CPSC Recalls — SHEIN + Kakgold

**Long-Form Post (10:00 AM MT)**

Two new CPSC recalls landed on November 12 — just as holiday shopping is ramping up. If you have little ones at home, these are worth a quick check. 🛒

SHEIN baby rattle and teether sets — about 2,657 units sold on US.Shein.com between November 2025 and July 2026. The knobs on these rattles can detach and become a choking hazard for children under 3. The fix: stop use immediately and contact SHEIN for a refund.

Kakgold 4WD remote-controlled cars — about 294 units. These RC toys have button cell and lithium-ion batteries that children can access, which creates a serious ingestion and injury risk. Same action: stop use and contact the company for a remedy.

Both products are now off the shelves, but they may already be in your home or on your holiday list. Full recall details are at cpsc.gov/Recalls — it only takes a minute to check.

### STORY 4: Colorado Flu Season

**Long-Form Post (3:00 PM MT)**

Colorado has confirmed the first pediatric flu death of the 2026–27 season. That's not meant to alarm you — but it is a good reason to act now if you haven't yet. 💉

Last season was Colorado's worst on record for flu hospitalizations: 5,931 statewide, with 1,169 children hospitalized and 8 pediatric deaths. The child in this year's first case was elementary-aged and had not received the current flu vaccine.

Here's the number that keeps coming up: flu vaccination rates for children between 6 months and 9 years old are sitting at about 36%. For comparison, that's not close to what it needs to be to protect the kids who can't get vaccinated at all.

CDPHE is also reminding families with infants under 8 months about RSV immunization — the program that helped bring Colorado's RSV hospitalization numbers down last season.

Your pediatrician or any pharmacy can get your kids vaccinated. The earlier in the season, the better.

### STORY 5: D20 Interim Superintendent + USAFA School

**Long-Form Post (5:00 PM MT)**

If you're a D20 family and you haven't been following the leadership transition, here's the quick version — and there's some genuinely good news in it.

Academy District 20's board voted to part ways with former superintendent Jinger Haberer earlier this year. The board then unanimously appointed Dr. Susan Field as interim superintendent through the 2026–27 school year. Dr. Field was previously the assistant superintendent for learning services — she's not an outside hire, she knows the district.

Meanwhile, D20 broke ground on a new pre-K through 8th grade school at the Air Force Academy, funded by a $42.7 million Department of Defense award. For USAFA families who've been in D20's boundaries, that's a significant addition.

Academic performance held up through the transition: D20 maintained its "accreditation with distinction" rating in the preliminary 2025–26 state performance results.
