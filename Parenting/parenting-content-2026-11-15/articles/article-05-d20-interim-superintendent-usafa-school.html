D20 Families: What to Know About the District's New Interim Leader and School Builds

By Jamie Rivera | November 15, 2026

Local News · Academy District 20

Academy District 20 has had a lot happening this fall — a leadership transition, a major school construction groundbreaking, and strong academic results. If you're a D20 family and you haven't been following the district closely, here's the catch-up.

Dr. Susan Field Is the New Interim Superintendent

The D20 board of education voted unanimously to appoint Dr. Susan Field as interim superintendent through the end of the 2026-27 school year. Dr. Field was previously serving as assistant superintendent for learning services — a role that put her close to instructional programming and school performance across the district.

This appointment follows the board's vote to part ways with former superintendent Jinger Haberer. The transition was handled cleanly from a process standpoint: the board moved quickly, chose an internal candidate with direct district knowledge, and reached a unanimous vote.

What that means practically for D20 families is some continuity. Dr. Field knows the district's schools, programs, and staff. The transition from an external superintendent to an internal interim doesn't typically produce major disruptions in day-to-day school operations, and there's no indication D20 is heading into the kind of extended turbulence that leadership transitions sometimes bring.

The board hasn't yet announced a timeline or process for a permanent superintendent search. That will likely be one of the significant decisions made in the spring semester.

A New Pre-K Through 8th Grade School Is Being Built at USAFA

The other major D20 news this fall is construction: the district broke ground on a new pre-K through 8th grade school on the grounds of the Air Force Academy, funded by a $42.7 million award from the Department of Defense.

This is a significant addition for USAFA families. D20 serves the Air Force Academy community, and the families stationed there have needed more school capacity for some time. A purpose-built pre-K through 8th grade facility on the USAFA grounds removes the need for those families to send kids off-base for schooling through middle school.

The DoD funding mechanism — which directs federal money to districts serving military installations — is a standard channel for this kind of project, but the $42.7 million scale puts this in the larger end of DoD school construction awards. The project is expected to serve both USAFA families and eligible D20 students in the surrounding area.

Construction timelines for a project this size typically run 18 to 30 months depending on scope and weather. Families at the Air Force Academy should follow D20's official communications at d20.org for updates on the construction schedule and opening timeline.

Academic Performance: Accreditation with Distinction

Even during a leadership transition year, D20 maintained its "accreditation with distinction" rating in preliminary 2025-26 state performance results. That's the highest academic accreditation level Colorado assigns to school districts, and D20 has held it consistently.

It's a data point worth noting. Leadership transitions create real uncertainty about direction and culture, and they can affect teacher morale and program continuity. The fact that D20 is coming out of this one with its academic standing intact reflects well on how the district's schools are functioning at the classroom level, regardless of what's happening at the administrative level.

What's Next

Three things are worth following for D20 families over the coming months:

  • Superintendent search timeline. The board will eventually need to announce a process for a permanent hire. That announcement — when it comes — will signal how quickly the district wants to stabilize its leadership situation.
  • USAFA school construction updates. Groundbreaking happened, but construction schedules shift. D20's official channels at d20.org are the best place to track progress and get a realistic opening date.
  • Spring academic performance data. CDPHE releases final performance ratings in the spring. Watching whether D20's "accreditation with distinction" holds through the transition year will give families a cleaner read on how the leadership change is affecting the district.

D20 board meeting agendas and minutes are posted at d20.org. If you want to stay close to district decisions, board meetings are typically held on the second and fourth Mondays of each month.
