### STORY 2: Product Recalls

#### Long-Form Post — 10:00 AM MT

Three more recalls to add to your check list, COS parents. These are all from the CPSC in August 2026 — and they cover products that are probably in your home or on your Amazon order history.

**PIXLABBY Silicone Magnetic Fidget Sliders (sold on Amazon)**
These were recalled after a child accessed and swallowed the magnets inside. If multiple magnets are swallowed, they can attract each other through intestinal walls, causing perforations, blockages, blood poisoning, and in serious cases, death. If you have one of these, stop use immediately.

**Wnttmt Baby Walkers (about 1,310 units, sold on Amazon)**
These walkers were recalled because they can fit through a standard doorway and fail to stop at the edge of a step — creating a fall hazard. If you have one, stop use.

**Michley Children's Pajamas (sold on SHEIN.com)**
These PJs fail mandatory flammability standards. Sleepwear that doesn't meet flammability requirements is a burn injury and fire risk. Stop use immediately.

For the full details on each recall — including photos of the products and return/refund instructions — visit cpsc.gov. And if you bought any of these as gifts for other families, it's worth a quick message to let them know.

### STORY 5: AAP NICU Parental Mental Health

#### Long-Form Post — 8:00 PM MT

This one is for the families who've been through the NICU — and for the pediatricians, grandparents, and support networks who love them.

The American Academy of Pediatrics published new research in August 2026 in its peer-reviewed journal Pediatrics, focusing on the mental health of parents whose babies were born preterm. The finding is important: parental anxiety, stress, and trauma after a NICU stay often go untreated — and that has real consequences for both parents and the child's long-term health.

The AAP is calling on pediatric medical homes to address parental mental health proactively, not just the baby's health. That means your baby's pediatrician's office should be asking how you're doing — and if they're not, you have every right to bring it up.

For COS families with NICU experience, local hospital systems — including UCHealth Memorial Hospital and Centura St. Francis Medical Center — have NICU follow-up programs that can include family support. If you're months or years out from a NICU stay and still carrying it, ask your own doctor or your child's pediatrician about a mental health referral.

We often focus so hard on the baby's milestones that we forget the person who barely slept for months in a hospital waiting room. That person's wellbeing matters too.

Has anyone else navigated the post-NICU mental health piece? We'd love to hear from our community.
