CPSC Recalls Xalirium Portable Bed Rails for Entrapment and Asphyxiation Risk

By Jamie Rivera | COS Parenting

October 18, 2026 — Colorado Springs, Colorado

The Consumer Product Safety Commission recalled approximately 200 usOpal Xalirium portable bed rails on October 15, 2026, due to entrapment and asphyxiation risk. The rails fail the federal mandatory safety standard for adult portable bed rails and are missing required hazard warning labels.

If you have these in your home — or if a grandparent, caregiver, or anyone else in your family uses them — stop use immediately and contact usOpal for a full refund. No injuries have been reported.

What the Recall Covers

The recalled product is the Xalirium brand portable bed rail, sold by usOpal (Opal Vista Ltd., based in China). The specific units recalled are bed rails without vertical bars inside the opening — that design creates the entrapment hazard. The recall does not cover all usOpal products, only this specific model.

The rails were sold exclusively on Amazon.com from February 2026 through August 2026 for approximately $60.

Why This Matters to Families

Portable bed rails are most commonly used by elderly adults or people with mobility concerns who need help getting in and out of bed safely. They're also sometimes used during overnight visits — when grandparents come to stay, or when families set up a bed for an older adult in a guest room.

The entrapment and asphyxiation risk associated with non-compliant bed rails is serious. Federal standards for adult portable bed rails exist specifically because older designs have caused fatalities. The Xalirium rails that are recalled lack the vertical bars inside the opening that modern standards require to prevent a person from slipping into the gap and becoming trapped.

What's Next

If you have Xalirium portable bed rails in your home, check whether they have vertical bars inside the opening. If they don't, they're covered by this recall. Stop using them and contact usOpal through Amazon or directly for a full refund. For the full recall notice, visit cpsc.gov/Recalls.
