COS School Districts Have Embraced Fall Breaks — Here's What to Know

By Jamie Rivera | COS Parenting

October 18, 2026 — Colorado Springs, Colorado

Colorado Springs families with kids in the public school system are navigating three different fall break schedules this year, and the differences between them are worth understanding. The Gazette examined the landscape in a piece published Saturday, and the overall picture is one of cautious adoption — districts that have tried fall breaks are keeping them, and the community has generally been on board.

D11: One Week With a New Addition

Colorado Springs School District 11 holds fall break October 19 through October 23 — one week, following parent-teacher conferences on October 16. It's a relatively recent addition to D11's calendar, and this year the district has added something new: a free, voluntary three-day literacy program for kindergarteners who could use extra reading support during the break.

The literacy program is small in scope but meaningful in intent. It's designed to reach younger readers who are still building foundational skills without making it feel punitive or stigmatizing — the program is opt-in, not assigned. D11 families who are interested in the program should contact their child's school directly this week.

D49: Two Weeks, Longer Year

School District 49 runs the most substantial fall break of the three major COS districts: two weeks off, with D49 schools closed from October 13 through October 23. The tradeoff is a longer school year overall, which the Gazette notes has found general community support.

The two-week structure gives families more flexibility to travel, take on projects, or just decompress, but it also means the school year runs deeper into summer. For families where that tradeoff works, the longer fall break has become a draw. For those who need summer flexibility, it's a consideration.

D20: End of Quarter Break

Academy District 20 structured its fall pause differently. Rather than a standalone break, D20 scheduled an "end of quarter break" immediately following parent-teacher conferences on October 13 and 14. D20 families had two days off at the end of last week and are already back in school this week.

The shorter break reflects D20's calendar philosophy — conferences anchor the pause, and the break is tied to a natural transition point in the school year rather than a standalone rest period.

The Bigger Picture

The move toward fall breaks in Colorado Springs mirrors a national trend. Research on student well-being and academic performance has found that regular recovery periods throughout the year can support learning outcomes and reduce burnout — both for students and teachers. Districts that experimented with fall breaks during and after the pandemic generally kept them once they saw the community response.

For COS families, the practical implication is knowing which district your child is in and planning accordingly. All three major districts have now settled into distinct patterns, and the Gazette's reporting suggests those patterns are likely to continue in the years ahead.

What's Next
