### STORY 1: Two CPSC Recalls — Peony Design Bibs and Bellabu Bear Robes

**Long-Form Post — Publish with Story 1 article (8:00 AM MT)**

If you've ordered personalized baby bibs or stroller bags from Etsy in the last year, this is worth stopping to read right now.

The CPSC has issued two recalls that Colorado Springs parents need on their radar this weekend. First: Peony Design is recalling personalized baby bibs and stroller bags because the snap attachment can detach and become a choking hazard. These were sold on Etsy from October 2025 through July 2026, priced $15–$45. If you have one, remove it from your child immediately and contact Peony Design for a full refund.

Second recall: Bellabu Bear is pulling its children's bamboo robes due to a burn hazard. The products don't meet mandatory federal flammability standards for children's sleepwear, which means they're not as flame-resistant as they need to be. Stop using them right away and check cpsc.gov/Recalls for next steps.

We know recall notices can feel overwhelming, especially when you're juggling a Saturday morning. Both of these are worth the five minutes it takes to check your kids' things. You can always search cpsc.gov/Recalls by product name to confirm details.

Have you ever had to act on a product recall for something you bought for your kids? Share in the comments -- we'd love to know how the process went. 👇

### STORY 3: Gov. Polis Joins Push for Early Math at Pediatrician Checkups

**Long-Form Post — Publish with Story 3 article (12:00 PM MT)**

Here's something that might change how you think about your child's next checkup. 🎲

Colorado Governor Jared Polis signed on with five other governors this week to send a letter to the American Academy of Pediatrics. Their ask: start giving pediatricians guidance on early math development the same way they already have guidance on early literacy.

The idea is that doctors can use well-child visits to talk with families about activities that build foundational numeracy -- things like board games and card games, starting in infancy. The governors are modeling this on the AAP's landmark literacy guidance, which has been integrated into pediatric care nationwide.

It's a small ask with a potentially big payoff. Most of us know that reading to our kids matters from day one -- but math development rarely comes up at the pediatrician's office, even though research shows that early numeracy skills matter just as much. Having your doctor bring it up could change the conversation a lot of families have at home.

The AAP hasn't issued new guidance yet, but this letter moves the ball. In the meantime, your kids' board game collection might just count as homework.

### STORY 4: D11 Adding 8 New School Zones Before August

**Long-Form Post — Publish with Story 4 article (3:00 PM MT)**

Heads up, D11 families -- there are changes coming to the roads near your kids' schools before August. 🚸

Colorado Springs is adding eight new school zone designations at middle and high schools this summer. That means new speed limits and increased enforcement in those areas once the school year starts. If you drive your kids to school, or even if you just pass through those corridors during drop-off and pick-up hours, it's worth knowing which schools are affected before the first day.

The goal is straightforward: reduce car accidents near schools and keep kids safer walking and riding in those zones. That's a goal we can all get behind.

We'll update this story as more specifics are confirmed. In the meantime, d11.org is the best place to check for details on which schools are getting new zones and what the updated speed limits will be.

Are you a D11 family who drives near middle or high schools? Let us know which school you're watching -- we'll flag any updates as they're released. 👇

### STORY 5: Free Water Play in Colorado Springs

**Long-Form Post — Publish with Story 5 article (5:00 PM MT)**

It's 84 degrees in Colorado Springs today, and the week ahead isn't much cooler. It's officially time to find some water. 💦

The good news: Colorado Springs has some genuinely great free and low-cost options for families who want to cool off without spending a lot. Uncle Wilber Fountain at Acacia Park is one of our favorites -- it's free, runs noon to 6 p.m. daily, and is exactly as charming as the name suggests. The kiddos can splash around while you grab a seat in the park and pretend you planned the whole thing in advance.

Deerfield Hills Spray Ground has been open since June 1 and features more than 50 spray nozzles, which is a genuinely impressive number of ways to get wet. Monument Valley Pool and Wilson Ranch Pool are both open daily if your family prefers lanes and lifeguards over fountains and chaos.

Summer in Colorado Springs moves fast. If you haven't made it to the fountain or spray ground yet, this weekend is as good a time as any.
