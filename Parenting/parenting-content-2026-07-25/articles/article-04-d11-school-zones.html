D11 Is Adding 8 New School Zones This Summer — What Colorado Springs Families Need to Know

By Jamie Rivera | July 25, 2026

Colorado Springs is adding eight new school zone designations near D11 middle and high schools before the end of the summer. For families who drive near those schools during drop-off and pick-up hours, that means new speed limits and increased enforcement will be in place by the time the school year begins in August.

If you're a D11 family planning your fall driving routine, it's worth knowing about these changes before the first day of school rather than after.

What's Changing

School zones are designated stretches of road near schools where lower speed limits apply during school hours, typically during morning drop-off and afternoon pick-up windows. They also typically carry higher fines for speeding violations to encourage compliance.

Adding eight new zones at middle and high schools represents a significant expansion of the existing network. High schools and middle schools tend to generate more traffic than elementary schools because students frequently walk, bike, and ride to and from school independently, and because parent drop-off and pick-up volumes at secondary schools are substantial.

The specific schools receiving new zone designations were not confirmed in our research. Families should check d11.org for the full list of affected schools and the corresponding speed limit changes once that information is published.

Why This Matters Before August

The timing of this announcement, during summer when school isn't in session, gives families and drivers the chance to adjust before the zones are active. A driver who doesn't know a new school zone exists could be caught off guard when speed signs appear and enforcement begins at the start of the school year.

This is especially relevant for families who moved into D11 neighborhoods over the summer, families whose children are transitioning from elementary to middle school and entering a new drop-off zone, and anyone who regularly travels through corridors near D11 secondary schools.

Colorado school zone violations carry elevated fines compared to standard speeding tickets, and enforcement tends to increase at the start of each school year as police prioritize zone compliance. Knowing the zones in advance saves families both time and money.

The Safety Goal

The push to add new school zones is part of an ongoing effort to reduce car accidents near schools in Colorado Springs. Traffic incidents in drop-off and pick-up zones, including pedestrian and bicycle accidents involving students, are a persistent safety concern across school districts nationwide.

Lower speed limits near schools reduce stopping distances and collision severity, which is particularly important in zones where children are walking between parked cars or crossing mid-block. The eight new designations at middle and high schools reflect recognition that older students, who are more likely to be navigating school corridors independently, need the same protected zone infrastructure as younger children.

What Families Should Do Now

The most important step for D11 families right now is to check for the specific list of affected schools once D11 publishes it. The district's official website at d11.org is the authoritative source for school-by-school details. Look for communications from D11 in the weeks leading up to the school year, which typically include back-to-school safety notices.

If you drive a regular route through a neighborhood near a D11 middle or high school, consider whether that corridor might be affected and watch for new signage. School zone signs are required to be posted within the designated stretch of road, so new signs will give drivers visible notice before enforcement begins.
