Two CPSC Recalls Colorado Springs Parents Need to Know About This Weekend

By Sarah Morales | July 25, 2026

Two separate product recalls announced by the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission affect items that many Colorado Springs families may have purchased in the last year. Both involve products used with young children, and both carry hazards serious enough that the CPSC is asking families to stop using them immediately.

Here's what you need to know about each recall and what to do if you own these products.

Peony Design Baby Bibs and Stroller Bags: Choking Hazard

Peony Design is recalling its personalized baby bibs and stroller bags after the company found that the snap attachment can detach from the product. A detached snap becomes a small part that poses a choking hazard for infants and young children, with the potential for serious injury or death.

The recalled products were sold exclusively on Etsy.com. If your family purchased a personalized bib or stroller bag from Peony Design between October 2025 and July 2026, your item may be affected. The products were priced between $15 and $45.

The CPSC's guidance is clear: remove the product from your child's reach immediately. Do not continue using it, and do not give it to another family. Consumers should contact Peony Design directly to receive a full refund. The recall is listed at cpsc.gov/Recalls.

Personalized items purchased as gifts are worth a double-check. If you gave a Peony Design bib as a baby shower gift or received one, it's worth reaching out to confirm whether it falls under the recall.

Bellabu Bear Children's Robes: Burn Hazard

The second recall involves Bellabu Bear children's bamboo robes. The CPSC found that these robes violate mandatory federal flammability standards for children's sleepwear, meaning they do not provide the flame resistance that federal law requires for products worn by children at bedtime.

This is a burn hazard. Consumers should stop using Bellabu Bear children's robes immediately. The full recall details, including information on the remedy, are available at cpsc.gov/Recalls under the Bellabu Bear listing.

Bamboo fabric has grown in popularity for children's sleepwear and loungewear because of its softness and breathability. If your family has purchased bamboo robes for children from any retailer, it's worth confirming whether the brand and style are included in this or any other active recall.

Why Safety Recalls Matter Even for Products You Trust

Product recalls happen when a safety defect is discovered after a product is already in homes. A well-made product from a reputable seller can still fail in ways the manufacturer didn't anticipate during design or testing. The CPSC's recall system exists precisely because no product category is immune.

Both of these recalls involve products that are common in households with infants and young children: bibs, stroller accessories, and sleepwear. The hazards -- a snap that comes loose and a robe that doesn't meet flammability standards -- aren't visible during normal use, which makes the recall notification the only reliable way for families to know about the risk.

Signing up for CPSC recall alerts at cpsc.gov is one of the more practical steps families can take to stay on top of these notifications. The agency publishes new recalls regularly, and a free email subscription delivers them directly to your inbox.

What's Next

  • Check cpsc.gov/Recalls for both active listings: Peony Design (personalized baby bibs and stroller bags) and Bellabu Bear (children's robes)
  • Remove recalled products from your home immediately -- do not donate or regift them
  • For the Peony Design recall, contact the company for a full refund if you purchased between October 2025 and July 2026
  • For the Bellabu Bear recall, follow the instructions on the CPSC listing for your remedy options
  • Consider signing up for CPSC recall email alerts so future recalls reach you automatically
  • If you gave either product as a gift, reach out to the recipient to let them know
