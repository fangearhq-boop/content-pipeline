### STORY 1: Harppa Nordi Toddler Tower Recall

**Long-Form (9:00 AM MT)**
Heads up for families with young children — there's an active CPSC recall you'll want to know about.

HARPPA has recalled 47,166 Nordi Foldable Toddler Tower Stools (Model HANS0002). The problem: the stools can collapse or tip over while in use, and a child's torso can fit through the openings on the front and back sides, creating an entrapment and fall risk. CPSC has received three incident reports, including two injuries.

The stools were sold at Harppababy.com and Amazon from September 2023 through June 2026 for about $130. If you have one, stop using it right away.

HARPPA is providing a free repair kit — protective nets, stabilizing feet, and installation instructions — with free shipping. To request yours, call 833-471-9321 (noon to 8 PM ET, Monday through Friday), email recall@harppababy.com, or visit harppababy.com. You can also find official recall details at cpsc.gov/Recalls.

Do you or someone you know have one of these stools? Share this post with Colorado Springs parents in your circle.

### STORY 3: AAP Flu Vaccine 2026–27

**Long-Form (10:30 AM MT)**
Flu season is coming, and the American Academy of Pediatrics has updated its guidance for the 2026–27 season.

The AAP recommends annual flu vaccination for all children 6 months and older. This year, the ideal window to get vaccinated is by the end of October — before flu activity typically picks up. Any licensed flu vaccine appropriate for your child's age and health status will work, so there's no need to wait for a specific brand.

A couple of specifics worth knowing if you have younger kids: children ages 6 months through 8 years who are receiving a flu vaccine for the first time — or who have only received one flu dose ever before July 1, 2026 — need two doses this season, given at least four weeks apart.

The AAP also noted an update to prescribing information for injectable flu vaccines this year. Two postmarketing studies found a small increased risk of febrile seizures in children under 5, estimated at roughly one extra seizure per 22,000 to 41,000 doses. The AAP is clear that the benefits of vaccination still far outweigh this risk, but it's good information to have before talking with your child's pediatrician.

### STORY 5: Uuoeebb Infant Walker Recall

**Long-Form (3:30 PM MT)**
Parents of infants — there's another CPSC recall to be aware of this week.

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission has recalled Uuoeebb Infant Walkers that were sold on Amazon by the seller BaoD. The walkers violate the mandatory federal safety standard for infant walkers and pose a risk of serious injury or death from fall and entrapment hazards.

If you have one, stop using it immediately and check cpsc.gov/Recalls for the full details on the recall, including what remedy options are available.

It's a good reminder that infant walkers — even ones that look sturdy — have a history of causing injuries. If you're unsure whether a walker in your home is covered by this or another recall, the CPSC website is the most reliable place to check.
