### STORY 2: Flu Season Action Guide

**Long-Form Post (Window: 10:00 AM MT):**

If you haven't gotten flu shots for your family yet, here's your gentle nudge: Colorado confirmed its first child flu death of this season just yesterday. The 2026-27 flu season is starting earlier than last year, and we're heading straight into the December-January peak — right alongside Thanksgiving travel.

The good news is that flu shots are widely available right now. Most Walgreens, King Soopers, and Safeway locations in Colorado Springs offer walk-in flu shots, and most pediatric clinics are scheduling them alongside other appointments. The AAP recommends flu vaccination for every child 6 months and older.

What to know about sick days: if your child develops a fever, keep them home from school until they've been fever-free for 24 hours without fever-reducing medication. That's the standard guidance for D11 and D49.

If your kids are bringing home the usual fall sniffles, don't assume it's the flu — but if you see fever, body aches, fatigue, or vomiting alongside respiratory symptoms, those are your signs to watch for.

### STORY 5: Talking to Kids About Veterans Day

**Long-Form Post (Window: 5:00 PM MT):**

With the Veterans Day parade this Saturday and a whole weekend of events ahead, kids across Colorado Springs are going to have questions: Why are there flags everywhere? Why is there a parade? What did veterans do?

Here's a quick age-by-age guide for how to answer:

Ages 3-5: Keep it simple and concrete. "Veterans are people who worked very hard to help keep everyone safe. We say thank you to them." You don't need more than that — and a parade is a great visual for little kids.

Ages 6-10: You can introduce the idea of service: choosing a job that means protecting other people, sometimes in dangerous places. Talk about the different branches (Army, Navy, Air Force, Marines, Coast Guard, Space Force). Fort Carson is Army; the Air Force Academy trains future officers — these are real places your kids might have driven past.

Ages 11 and up: Tweens can handle more nuance — the difference between Veterans Day (honoring all living veterans) and Memorial Day (honoring those who died in service), why wars happen, what service members give up for their families, and why Colorado Springs has such a large military community.

The parade Saturday is honestly one of the best classroom moments you'll find in COS — real veterans, real units, real history rolling down Tejon Street.
