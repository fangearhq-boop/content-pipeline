Flu Season Is Here: What COS Families Should Do Before Thanksgiving

By Sarah Morales

November 4, 2026

Colorado confirmed its first pediatric flu death of the 2026-27 season on Monday, and health officials say this season is tracking earlier than last year. With the flu's typical December-to-January peak approaching — and Thanksgiving travel right in the middle of it — now is the right time for COS families to get vaccinated and have a plan.

What We Know About This Season

The state confirmed the first child flu death of the current season on November 3, 2026. The child was elementary school-aged and had not been vaccinated. Local health officials and UCHealth have noted that the 2026-27 flu season appears to be starting earlier than the 2025-26 season, which itself was a severe year — Colorado recorded 8 child flu deaths and 1,169 hospitalizations statewide during 2025-26.

Flu activity typically peaks in Colorado between December and January. That window lines up almost exactly with Thanksgiving and holiday travel, when families are spending more time indoors with extended family and gathering in airports, holiday parties, and community events. Getting vaccinated now gives the immune system time to build protection before that peak hits.

Where to Get Flu Shots in Colorado Springs

Flu vaccines are widely available in Colorado Springs right now. Most Walgreens, King Soopers, and Safeway pharmacy locations offer walk-in flu shots — no appointment needed in most cases, though you can book online to skip the wait. Many pediatric clinics and family medicine offices in COS can administer flu shots during existing appointments or during a quick vaccine-only visit.

The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends annual flu vaccination for all children 6 months and older. If your child is under 6 months, focus on vaccinating everyone around them — parents, grandparents, siblings, and regular caregivers — to create a protective circle.

Sick-Day Protocol: What D11 and D49 Expect

For families in D11 and D49, the standard sick-day guidance follows Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment recommendations: if your child has a fever, they should stay home from school until they have been fever-free for 24 hours without fever-reducing medication. This applies even if the child seems to feel better.

Flu symptoms to watch for include fever above 100.4°F, body aches, fatigue, headache, and respiratory symptoms such as coughing or congestion. Vomiting and diarrhea can also occur, especially in younger children. If your child develops these symptoms, contact your pediatrician — antiviral medications like Tamiflu can reduce the severity and duration of flu illness when taken within the first 48 hours.

Before Thanksgiving Travel

If your family is traveling for Thanksgiving, consider the vaccination timing: flu shots typically take about two weeks to reach full effectiveness. That means families traveling in late November should ideally be vaccinated by mid-November to get maximum protection. This week is a good window to act.

What's Next

Colorado's flu season will likely intensify through December and January. The Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment publishes weekly flu surveillance reports at cdphe.colorado.gov. COS families can find current local vaccination options through their pediatrician, El Paso County Public Health (epcph.org), or the Colorado Immunization Information System. Keep an eye on school communications from D11 and D49 for any classroom-level updates as the season develops.
