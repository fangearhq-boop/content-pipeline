### STORY 1: CPSC Blossom Loungewear Recall

#### Long-Form Post — 8:00 AM MT

If you have kids in the 12-month to 12-year range, this one is worth a check of the closet.

CPSC has recalled Blossom OCC children's two-piece loungewear sets because the garments violate mandatory federal flammability standards for children's sleepwear, posing a real risk of burn injury. The recall covers short-sleeved sets in white, navy, light blue, light pink, and lilac — in fireworks, multi-stripes, tulips, and sailboat designs — as well as long-sleeved holiday sets including "letter to Santa" and "Santa paws" patterns.

Those holiday designs are worth flagging specifically, because families shopping for fall and holiday clothing may already have them in hand — or find them on shopping sites if they're not aware of the recall.

What to do: Stop use immediately. Destroy the garments by cutting them in half. Photograph the destroyed items. Then email sales@blossomocc.com for a full refund in your original payment form, or store credit if you prefer. Full details at cpsc.gov/Recalls.

A second recall worth knowing about: Bellabu Bear bamboo sherpa children's robes (sizes S/M and M/L) have also been recalled for the same type of flammability violation. Those were sold at Saks Fifth Avenue and online for approximately $60.

Share this post — there's a good chance someone you know has these in the house.

### STORY 2: D11 Galileo Middle School Food Pantry

#### Long-Form Post — 10:00 AM MT

Something good happened in Colorado Springs last week.

Galileo Middle School, part of Colorado Springs School District 11, opened the "Ascend" food pantry and community garden on September 25. It's a market-style pantry offering fresh produce, bread, and household food staples, built in partnership between the school and Joint Initiatives, a local nonprofit.

In just the first days of operation, 22 families had already been served.

The pantry reflects a broader shift happening in Colorado Springs schools — expanding their role beyond academics to become genuine community hubs. The community garden component takes that one step further, giving students a hands-on connection to food and nature right on school grounds.

If you know a family in the Galileo community who could use this resource, the sign-up contact is KMcDaniel@jointinitiatives.org. And if you'd like to support Joint Initiatives' work, their website has more information.

This is Colorado Springs at its best — people helping people. Share this with anyone who might need it.

### STORY 3: Ghost Stories of Old Manitou Walking Tours

#### Long-Form Post — 3:00 PM MT

The 31st Annual Ghost Stories of Old Manitou kicks off this Thursday and Friday — October 2 and 3 — and it's exactly the kind of experience that makes Halloween season in Colorado Springs something special.

The walking tour is led by a Spirit Guide narrator from the Manitou Springs Heritage Center and THEATREdART, with staged vignettes at stops throughout historic Old Manitou Springs. It's rooted in real Colorado Springs history, told through a Halloween lens — not just jump scares, but actual storytelling. Most families find it appropriate for kids around 8 and up (younger ones will depend on the child).

Tours depart from 517 Manitou Avenue at 5:30 PM, every 15 minutes, and run about an hour. Regular tickets are $18.84 (advance tickets are a bit cheaper, around $17). Children under age 10 are free.

Key detail: these tours sell out. If you're thinking about this Thursday or Friday, get advance tickets now at manitouspringsheritagecenter.org or Eventbrite.

They also run October 9-10, 16-17, 23, and 30, so you've got several chances throughout the month.

### STORY 4: CDC Teen Mental Health Data

#### Long-Form Post — 12:00 PM MT

There's a piece of good news in the teen mental health conversation, and it's worth celebrating — carefully.

New CDC data from its 2025 Youth Risk Behavior Survey shows that 33% of high school students reported persistent feelings of sadness or hopelessness — down from 40% in 2023 and a record high of 42% in 2021. The share of teens who seriously considered suicide also dropped from 20% to 14%. These are real improvements that represent hundreds of thousands of young people moving in a better direction.

The caution: rates still remain higher than before the pandemic (around 31.5% in 2017), and the improvements are not evenly distributed. Girls and LGBTQ youth still face significantly higher rates than their male and heterosexual peers. Experts who reviewed the data told EdWeek that while the trend is encouraging, this is not the moment to ease up on support.

For Colorado Springs parents with middle or high schoolers: the data gives you context, and context can be a good starting point for a conversation. Knowing that rates are improving — but that some kids are still really struggling — can help reframe how you approach check-ins with your own teens.

How are you navigating mental health conversations with your family? We'd love to hear what's working.

### STORY 5: October Outdoor Guide

#### Long-Form Post — 5:00 PM MT

October in Colorado Springs might be the best month to be outside with your family. Here's how to make the most of it — and what to plan for before the window closes.

**Early October (Now through Oct 10):** Highs around 67°F and lows in the low 40s. This is the most comfortable stretch for hiking, biking, and outdoor events. Gardens of the Gods, Bear Creek Park, and the trails at North Cheyenne Canyon are spectacular right now and won't be crowded.

**Mid-October (Oct 11-20):** Peak fall foliage. The color change at Garden of the Gods, along North Cheyenne Canyon Road, and on the Pikes Peak Highway is worth planning a weekend around. Note: the Pikes Peak Highway typically closes for the season in November — if you haven't driven it this year, now is the time.

**Late October (Oct 21-31):** Temperatures start dropping fast — highs around 58°F, lows near freezing overnight. Frost is possible. This is the week of Boo at the Zoo at Cheyenne Mountain Zoo (Oct 16-31, timed e-tickets at cmzoo.org) and the Emma Crawford Coffin Races in Manitou Springs (Oct 24, 32nd annual). Fun evening events, but dress in layers — it will be chilly.
