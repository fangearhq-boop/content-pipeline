October Outdoor Guide for Colorado Springs Families: What to Do Before the Cold Arrives

By Jamie Rivera | COS Parenting | September 30, 2026

October in Colorado Springs hits a sweet spot that many families overlook: the summer crowds have cleared, temperatures are pleasant for hiking and outdoor events, and fall color season brings the region to life. For families with kids of any age, this month offers some of the best outdoor conditions of the year — but the window has an expiration date.

Early October typically brings daytime highs near 67°F with low humidity and clear skies. By late October, expect highs in the upper 50s and overnight lows approaching 30°F. Plan outdoor activities with that temperature shift in mind — early and mid-October are ideal for hiking and longer outdoor days, while late October events mean layers for the whole family.

Early October: Catch Peak Fall Color

The Pikes Peak region reaches its fall foliage peak in early-to-mid October. For families who want to experience it, this is the best window before conditions change.

Garden of the Gods provides a classic Colorado Springs setting — red rock formations contrasted with golden cottonwoods and scrub oak turning orange and red. Parking at the main visitor center fills early on weekends; arriving before 9 AM or after 3 PM will make the experience significantly less stressful with children. The Perkins Central Garden Trail (roughly 1.5 miles, paved) is accessible for strollers and younger children and gives a full overview of the park's geology and fall scenery.

North Cheyenne Canyon offers a different experience — a cooler, forested canyon setting with a creek running through it and a trail up to Helen Hunt Falls. The main road through the canyon is a paved drive with multiple parking pullouts, so families can get out at the spots that interest them without committing to a long hike. Both parks are free to enter.

Mid-October: Transition to Evening Events

As temperatures begin shifting, October's signature family events start rolling out. Boo at the Zoo at Cheyenne Mountain Zoo launches October 16 and runs through October 31, operating during evening hours when the zoo is decorated for Halloween and features trick-or-treating stations throughout the grounds. Tickets are required and zoo members receive discounts — if your family has a membership, this is one of the best uses of it. Costumes are encouraged for children and adults alike.

The Ghost Stories of Old Manitou Walking Tours continue through mid-October as well, with October 9 and 10 dates following the opening weekend. These tours offer a way to blend outdoor time, local history, and Halloween season atmosphere in a format designed for families.

Late October: Layer Up for the Final Push

The last week of October sees a real temperature shift. Lows can drop to around 30°F overnight, and evening events will require real winter layers — not just a light jacket. For Boo at the Zoo and the Ghost Stories tours in late October, plan for hats, gloves, and insulated layers for children, especially younger ones who will be outdoors for an hour or more.

The Emma Crawford Coffin Races on October 24 in Manitou Springs are one of the most distinctively Colorado events of the year — teams build decorated coffins and race them down Manitou Avenue while a costumed "Emma" rides on top. It's free to watch and deeply weird in the best possible way. This is the 32nd annual event, and it draws a crowd; arrive early to find a good viewing spot along the course. Manitou Avenue has food and shops nearby, making it a full afternoon outing.

Practical Tips for October Outdoor Days

A few notes that will make your October outings smoother with kids:

Buy tickets in advance for evening events. Boo at the Zoo and the Ghost Stories tours both sell out, particularly on weekends. Don't plan to walk up — buy ahead and skip the disappointment.

Dress in layers, always. October temperatures in Colorado Springs can swing 30 degrees between noon and sunset. What feels warm at 1 PM will feel cold by 6 PM, especially at elevation. Bring an extra layer per child even on "nice" days.

Check trail conditions before you go. Early October snowfall at elevation is possible and can make trails slick. The Colorado Springs Parks, Recreation and Cultural Services website lists current conditions for city parks, and weather.gov/pub covers Pikes Peak area forecasts.

The Monday-Thursday window. If you have any flexibility in your schedule, outdoor spots like Garden of the Gods and North Cheyenne Canyon are dramatically less crowded on weekdays. The fall color experience is significantly more enjoyable without the weekend parking competition.

What's Next

October is a full month of outdoor opportunity in Colorado Springs — fall color into Halloween, with major family events bookending the month. After October wraps, Colorado Springs families can look forward to winter programming at Cheyenne Mountain Zoo, holiday light events, and the transition to ski season at nearby Monarch Mountain and Breckenridge. For city park conditions and event updates, the Colorado Springs city parks site (coloradosprings.gov/parks) and the Pikes Peak Outdoor Recreation Alliance (ppoutdoors.org) are good resources to bookmark for the season ahead.
