### STORY 1: CPSC Recalls Blossom Children's Loungewear — Burn Hazard, Sizes 12M–12Y
**Type:** NEW
**Tier:** 1 (CPSC recall — safety story, mandatory Tier 1)
**Pillar:** National Parenting (Product Safety)
**Byline:** Jamie Rivera
**Angle:** CPSC has recalled Blossom OCC two-piece loungewear sets for children in sizes 12M through 12Y because the garments violate mandatory federal flammability standards for children's sleepwear, posing a burn injury or death risk. The recall affects short-sleeved sets in white, navy, light blue, light pink, and lilac (fireworks, multi-stripes, tulip, and sailboat designs) and long-sleeved sets including holiday "letter to Santa" and "Santa paws" patterns — items likely already in some homes as families begin fall and holiday shopping. Families should stop use immediately, destroy by cutting in half, photograph, and email sales@blossomocc.com for a full refund or store credit. Details at cpsc.gov/Recalls.
**Sources:** cpsc.gov/Recalls/2026/Blossom-Recalls-Childrens-Loungewear-Due-to-Serious-Risk-of-Injury-or-Death-from-Burns-Violates-Mandatory-Standard-for-Childrens-Sleepwear

### STORY 4: CDC Data: Teen Mental Health Is Improving — But Gaps Remain for Girls and LGBTQ Youth
**Type:** NEW
**Tier:** 2 (National parenting study — actionable, relevant to COS parents of teens)
**Pillar:** National Parenting (Health & Wellness)
**Byline:** Sarah Morales
**Angle:** New CDC Youth Risk Behavior Survey (YRBS) data from 2025 show meaningful progress in teen mental health: 33% of high school students reported persistent sadness or hopelessness — down from 40% in 2023 and a peak of 42% in 2021. Teens seriously considering suicide also dropped from 20% to 14%. But experts flag that rates remain higher than pre-pandemic levels, and girls and LGBTQ youth still face significantly higher rates than their peers. The CDC released the full report September 21. COS parents and educators can use this data as a baseline for conversations with their kids.
**Sources:** cdc.gov/media/releases/2026, edweek.org, npr.org, deseret.com
**Posting Window:** 12:00 PM MT

### STORY 5: October in Colorado Springs — Your Family Outdoor Guide for the Best Month to Be Outside
**Type:** NEW
**Tier:** 2 (Evergreen/seasonal — practical planning for COS families)
**Pillar:** Evergreen
**Byline:** Jamie Rivera
**Angle:** October is the sweet spot for outdoor activities in Colorado Springs — warm days (highs around 67°F in early October) with cool evenings, peak fall foliage through mid-month at Garden of the Gods and North Cheyenne Canyon, and a packed calendar of family events. By late October, temperatures drop toward frost-risk territory (highs near 58°F, lows around 30°F), so evening events like Boo at the Zoo (Oct 16–31 at Cheyenne Mountain Zoo) and the Emma Crawford Coffin Races (Oct 24, Manitou Springs) will require layers. This is the week to plan the month before the window closes.
**Sources:** accuweather.com, easeweather.com, cmzoo.org
