### STORY 1: D11 Bond Measure 4A

#### Long-Form Post — 8:00 AM MT

D11 families, there's a big vote coming up: Colorado Springs School District 11 has a $775 million bond measure on the November 3 ballot.

The board voted 6-1 this past August to refer the measure. If it passes, the funds go toward safety and security upgrades, replacing aging HVAC and air filtration systems, removing asbestos and lead pipes, modernizing CTE programs, updating classrooms, and renovating libraries. The bond also prepays some existing lease obligations — which frees up mill levy override money for teacher compensation.

The estimated cost for a typical homeowner is around $260 per year, or about $6.50 per month per $100,000 of assessed property value. An independent citizens' oversight committee would be required by the measure.

November 3 is the vote. If your kiddos are in D11 schools, this one's worth understanding before you fill out that ballot.

### STORY 2: Baby Bath Seat Recalls

#### Long-Form Post — 9:00 AM MT

Two baby bath seat models have been recalled in recent days — and if you have one, it's time to act.

The YCXXKJ Baby Bath Seats, sold on Amazon by a seller called BenTalk, were recalled for drowning and entrapment risk. According to the CPSC, these seats violate the mandatory federal safety standard for infant bath seats. A second recall — issued October 8 — covers ONTHEWEI baby bath seats: more than 2,000 units sold on Amazon that are unstable and tip over, creating a drowning hazard. No injuries have been reported with the ONTHEWEI model, but the risk is real.

For both products: stop use immediately. Head to cpsc.gov to find the specific recall notice and follow the remedy instructions.

Baby bath seats in general require that parents stay within arm's reach at all times — a CPSC reminder worth repeating even with products not under recall.

If you've purchased a baby bath seat on Amazon recently, check your order history and the CPSC recalls page. Better safe than sorry.

### STORY 4: AAP Raw Milk Warning

#### Long-Form Post — 2:00 PM MT

The American Academy of Pediatrics has updated its policy on raw milk — and the recommendation is clear: don't give it to children, and parents who are pregnant should avoid it entirely.

The updated statement, scheduled to publish in the November 2026 issue of Pediatrics, renews the AAP's longstanding call to ban the sale of raw milk and raw dairy products. The new guidance leans on two specific concerns: H5N1 avian influenza, which has been detected in raw milk, and listeriosis — a bacterial illness that's 12 to 14 times more likely to occur during pregnancy when raw dairy is consumed.

For Colorado Springs families who shop at local farm markets, this is directly relevant. Raw milk is sold at some COS market locations, and it's often marketed with health claims that the AAP's data doesn't support for children or pregnant people.

This doesn't mean local farmers are doing anything wrong — it's more about the inherent risk of unpasteurized dairy for the people most vulnerable to foodborne illness.

### STORY 5: BabyBond Gate Recall

#### Long-Form Post — 4:00 PM MT

There's a baby gate recall that may have flown under the radar — and if you bought one from Target in the last couple of years, it's worth checking.

Infant Innovations (sold under the BabyBond brand) recalled approximately 280 retractable mesh safety gates in January 2026 (Recall #26-168). The gates — model SH20.006DB, available in black, gray, and white — are 33 inches high and adjust up to 55 inches wide. The hazard: the gap between the bottom of the gate and the floor is large enough for a young child's torso to pass through, creating an entrapment risk.

The gates were sold exclusively on Target.com from November 2024 through October 2025, priced around $50. No injuries have been reported, but the CPSC issued the recall because the risk is real.

The fix is simple: stop using the gate and return it to any Target store for a full refund. You don't need a receipt.

If you have a retractable mesh gate from BabyBond, check the model number on the label. And if you're not sure, it's better to pull it down and check than to wonder.
