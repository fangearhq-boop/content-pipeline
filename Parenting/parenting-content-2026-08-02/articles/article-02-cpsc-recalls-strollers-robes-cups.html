Three Product Recalls COS Parents Should Know About This Weekend

By Sarah Morales | August 2, 2026

National Parenting

Three new consumer product safety actions from the CPSC cover items that may be in Colorado Springs homes right now: a stroller sold through a major online retailer, children's robes sold at a well-known department store, and LED-lit superhero cups and food containers. Here's what each one involves and what to do if you have it.

B. Childhood "High Landscape" Strollers — Stop Use Immediately

The CPSC has issued a stop-use warning for B. Childhood "High Landscape" strollers sold on SHEIN. The hazard is an entrapment risk: an opening between the seat and the grab bar is large enough to allow an infant to become entrapped. This is a stop-use warning, not just a recall — the CPSC is directing consumers to stop using these strollers right away.

If you have one of these strollers, do not use it until you've reviewed the full notice at cpsc.gov/Recalls. The recall page includes remedy information and contact details for the manufacturer.

Bellabu Bear Children's Robes — Burn Hazard

Bellabu Bear children's robes have been recalled for a burn hazard. The robes were sold at Saks Fifth Avenue and through online retailers between January 2024 and July 2025, and they don't meet mandatory federal standards for children's sleepwear flammability. That means they burn more readily than the standard allows.

If your child received one of these robes as a gift or you purchased one directly, stop using it. Full recall and remedy information — including how to request a refund or replacement — is available at cpsc.gov/Recalls.

Golden Link Fantastic Four Cups and Captain America Containers — Battery Ingestion Risk

The CPSC has recalled Golden Link Fantastic Four cups and Captain America containers with LED lights. The hazard is the LED components: they contain button batteries, which can be accessed by children and pose a serious risk of injury or death if swallowed.

These products are sold as cups and food containers — the kind that might look like a child's water bottle or snack holder. The superhero branding makes them appealing to kids. If you have these products at home, stop using them and check cpsc.gov/Recalls for the recall notice and remedy options.

How to Stay on Top of Recalls

The CPSC maintains a free, searchable recall database at cpsc.gov/Recalls. If you want to check whether any product you own has been recalled, you can search by brand name, product type, or hazard category. The CPSC also offers a free email alert subscription service — signing up means you'll receive recall notices as they're issued rather than finding out after the fact.

What's Next

All three of these recalls are currently active. If any of these products are in your home, take action now: stop use, check cpsc.gov/Recalls for the specific product, and follow the remedy instructions. The stroller warning carries particular urgency given the stop-use designation. For ongoing recall updates, the CPSC email alert service is the most reliable way to stay current.
