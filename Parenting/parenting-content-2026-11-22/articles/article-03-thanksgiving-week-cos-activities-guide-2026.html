Thanksgiving break officially starts this week for most Colorado Springs families, and the city has a solid lineup of activities for every age and budget. Whether you're looking for something to burn off energy on Thursday morning, a ticketed experience for the weekend, or a free outing that won't require much planning, there's enough here to fill the week without leaving town.

Turkey Trot 5K — Thursday Morning

The Briargate YMCA hosts its annual Turkey Trot on Thanksgiving morning, Thursday, November 26. The event opens with a Kids' Tiny Trot at 8:15 AM, designed for younger children, followed by the main 5K at 9:00 AM. Adult registration runs $43–$54 depending on age; confirm current pricing directly with the YMCA since listings varied across sources. It's one of the more family-friendly 5Ks on the local calendar — the energy is festive rather than competitive, and it's a genuinely nice way to start the holiday before the cooking begins.

Chefs' Kitchen Cooking Camp

If your kids are the type who'd rather be in the kitchen than on a running course, Chefs' Kitchen is running a three-day cooking and baking mini-camp during the break. It's open to ages 7–14 at $235 for the full session. The camp typically fills up — if you haven't registered yet, check availability through visitcos.com. It's a practical gift for kids who've shown an interest in cooking, and it keeps them productively occupied for a good chunk of the week.

The Nutcracker at Pikes Peak Center

The Colorado Springs Philharmonic presents The Nutcracker at the Pikes Peak Center on November 27, 28, and 29. The Nutcracker is a reliable choice for families with children who've never seen a live orchestra performance — it's the right length, visually engaging, and culturally familiar enough that kids arrive knowing roughly what to expect. Tickets and showtimes are available at colorado-springs-theater.com. It's worth booking in advance; Thanksgiving weekend performances routinely sell out.

Skate in the Park at Acacia Park

Skate in the Park is running through January at Acacia Park, 115 E. Platte Ave. in downtown Colorado Springs. Admission is $15, which includes skate rental — no need to bring your own. Children ages 4 and under skate free. The rink runs daily, and the downtown location makes it easy to combine with a meal or a stop at the Christmas Market if it's running nearby. Thanksgiving week tends to be busy, so arrive early if you're planning a weekend visit.

Cheyenne Mountain Zoo and Broadmoor Holiday Lights

Cheyenne Mountain Zoo is open every day of Thanksgiving week. If you haven't been recently, their holiday lights display runs in the evenings and is worth the trip. The zoo's regular admission applies; check cmzoo.org for current pricing and holiday hours. For a free option nearby, the Broadmoor holiday lights display is free to walk or drive through and runs through the winter season. It's the kind of thing that takes under an hour and works well as a low-key weekday evening activity when the week starts to run out of structure.

Planning the Week

Thanksgiving break for D11, D20, and D49 runs through Friday, November 28, with students returning Monday, November 30. That's six days counting the weekend — enough time for at least a couple of these activities without over-scheduling. For families who prefer free and low-key, the Broadmoor lights, Acacia Park skating, and a walk at a local trail or park offer flexible options that don't require advance planning. For families who want one signature experience, The Nutcracker and the Turkey Trot are both worth reserving ahead of time.
