CPSC Recalls ~1,309 Qpancy Children's Pajamas Over Burn Hazard — Stop Use Immediately

By Jamie Rivera | November 22, 2026

Quick Reference

Recall number #27-094
Recall date November 19, 2026
Brand Qpancy
Units recalled Approximately 1,309
Where sold Amazon.com, March–August 2026
Price Approximately $20
Hazard Fails mandatory children's sleepwear flammability standard
Risk Serious burn injuries or death
Remedy Full refund — contact Qpancy

The Consumer Product Safety Commission announced a recall of approximately 1,309 Qpancy children's pajama sets on November 19, 2026 (Recall #27-094). The pajamas fail the mandatory federal flammability standard for children's sleepwear — a standard that exists because children who wear non-compliant sleepwear face a real risk of serious burn injuries or death if the fabric comes in contact with a flame or heat source.

If your household purchased Qpancy pajamas on Amazon between March and August 2026 at around $20, the CPSC's guidance is clear: stop dressing children in them immediately and contact Qpancy for a full refund.

Why the Flammability Standard Exists

Federal law requires that children's sleepwear meet one of two safety standards. It must either be made from inherently flame-resistant fabric — in which case the garment is labeled "flame resistant" — or it must be snug-fitting, which limits the fabric's exposure to air and reduces the risk of rapid ignition. Garments labeled "snug-fitting" are designed to fit close to the body for this reason.

These standards apply to children's sleepwear in sizes 9 months through 14 years. They've been in place for decades and have significantly reduced burn injuries related to children's sleepwear. When a product fails to meet them, the CPSC treats it as a serious hazard — not a technical paperwork issue.

The Qpancy recall is the third children's sleepwear recall this fall. While the brand names and specific products differ, the underlying violation is the same: garments reaching consumers without passing the flammability tests required by federal law.

What to Do if You Have Qpancy Pajamas

Parents who purchased Qpancy pajama sets on Amazon between March and August 2026 should take these steps:

  • Stop dressing children in the pajamas immediately.
  • Contact Qpancy for instructions on returning the product and receiving a full refund.
  • Check CPSC.gov or sign up for recall email alerts to stay current on future notices.

Even if the pajamas appear in good condition, appearance doesn't indicate flammability compliance. A fabric can look and feel fine while still failing to meet the required safety standard.

What to Look for When Buying Children's Sleepwear

With the holiday season underway and plenty of cozy pajama gifting ahead, this recall is a useful reminder of what to look for on the tag. Children's sleepwear should carry one of two labels:

  • Flame resistant — the fabric meets the flammability standard through its material properties.
  • Snug-fitting — the garment is designed to fit close to the body, reducing fire risk through fit rather than material.

If a garment's label doesn't include either phrase, it doesn't meet the federal standard for children's sleepwear. This applies to pajamas purchased from any retailer, including third-party sellers on Amazon and similar platforms. When in doubt, check the CPSC database before purchasing — especially from unfamiliar brands.

The Qpancy recall, like others before it, was sold at a price point ($20) that makes it appealing to budget-conscious shoppers. That's not a reason to avoid affordable brands, but it is a reason to verify the label before purchasing any children's sleepwear.

What's Next

The official CPSC recall page for Recall #27-094 is live at cpsc.gov and includes contact information for reaching Qpancy directly about refunds. Parents can register for free CPSC recall email alerts at cpsc.gov — the alerts are product-category specific, so signing up for children's products will notify you of future sleepwear, toy, and nursery recalls as they're announced.
