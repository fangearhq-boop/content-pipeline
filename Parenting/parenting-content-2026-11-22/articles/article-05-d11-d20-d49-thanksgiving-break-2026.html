Colorado Springs's three main school districts — D11, D20, and D49 — all observe a full Thanksgiving week break. For most families, that means schools are closed from Monday, November 24 through Friday, November 28, with students returning on Monday, November 30. Including the weekend, that's six days to plan for before the school routine resumes.

Because individual school calendars can vary — particularly for charter schools and alternative programs operating within a district — parents are encouraged to verify their child's specific school dates on the district's website. D11 at d11.org, D20 at asd20.org, and D49 at sd49.org each maintain current year calendars. If your child's school operates on a modified calendar, those sites are the authoritative source.

Six Days Off: Ideas for Families Who Want to Plan Ahead

A full week off with nothing on the calendar can be great or chaotic depending on your household. For families who prefer to have a few anchor activities, Colorado Springs has solid options this week across a range of ages, budgets, and energy levels.

Thursday: Turkey Trot 5K

The Briargate YMCA hosts its annual Turkey Trot on Thanksgiving morning — a Kids' Tiny Trot at 8:15 AM followed by the main 5K at 9:00 AM. It's a good way to start the holiday, particularly if your Thanksgiving involves a lot of time at the table. Adult registration runs $43–$54 depending on age; confirm current pricing with the YMCA.

Friday and the Weekend: The Nutcracker

The Colorado Springs Philharmonic presents The Nutcracker at the Pikes Peak Center on November 27, 28, and 29. For families with children who haven't seen a live performance before, it's the right introduction — familiar music, manageable length, and visually engaging staging. Tickets at colorado-springs-theater.com tend to go quickly for Thanksgiving weekend shows.

All Week: Skating, the Zoo, and More

Skate in the Park at Acacia Park (115 E. Platte Ave.) is running daily through January — $15 with skate rental included, children ages 4 and under free. Cheyenne Mountain Zoo is open every day of the break. If you haven't been recently, their holiday lights display runs in the evenings and is worth the trip. The Broadmoor holiday lights are free and accessible throughout the winter season.

For kids with a creative streak, Chefs' Kitchen is running a three-day cooking and baking mini-camp for ages 7–14 at $235 during the break. It's a structured option for families who prefer their children to have something purposeful to do mid-week.

Keeping the Week Manageable

Six days off doesn't require a packed schedule. Many Colorado Springs families use Thanksgiving week as a low-key reset — a few planned outings, some downtime, and a Thanksgiving dinner. The activities above range from free (Broadmoor lights, a hike at Palmer Park or Garden of the Gods) to ticketed events that benefit from advance booking. Mixing both tends to work better than trying to fill every day with something new.

If you're managing childcare over the break, the YMCA, Chefs' Kitchen, and similar programs represent the structured weekday options currently available. YMCA member discounts may apply to the Turkey Trot and other programming — check with your local branch.
