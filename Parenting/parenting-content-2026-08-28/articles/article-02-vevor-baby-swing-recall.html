Federal Recall: Stop Using Vevor Baby Swings Immediately — Suffocation Hazard Affects About 1,020 Units

By Jamie Rivera | COS Parenting

August 28, 2026

If you purchased a Vevor baby swing online in early 2025, you need to stop using it and contact the manufacturer for a full refund.

Sanven Technology has recalled approximately 1,020 Vevor baby swings due to a suffocation hazard. The affected models are BB501K, BB702A, and BB005K. These swings have an incline angle greater than 10 degrees, which violates both the mandatory Infant Sleep Products safety standard and the federal ban on inclined sleepers under the Safe Sleep for Babies Act.

Why This Is Dangerous

An infant placed in an inclined seat can experience their head tipping forward, partially blocking the airway. Young infants do not have the neck strength to reposition themselves out of this situation. Even a brief period in an unsafe position can have severe consequences.

The Safe Sleep for Babies Act specifically prohibits inclined sleepers because of this documented risk. Products that angle the infant's body — even at angles that seem slight — have been linked to infant deaths in the United States.

What to Do Right Now

If you own any of the affected models (BB501K, BB702A, or BB005K), stop using the swing immediately. Do not allow infants to sleep or rest in it. Contact Sanven Technology using the following information:

  • Phone: 855-599-6320 (Monday–Friday, 9 a.m.–5 p.m. PT)
  • Email: recalling@vevor.com
  • Online: vevor.com/pages/vevor-baby-swing-recall
  • CPSC recall page: cpsc.gov/Recalls

Sanven Technology is offering a full refund for all affected units. You do not need proof of purchase from a retail store — the swings were sold online.

Safe Sleep Basics

The AAP and the CPSC continue to recommend the same safe sleep environment for infants: flat surface, firm mattress, no incline, on their back, in their own sleep space. Swings, bouncers, and car seats are not approved infant sleep environments — even when your baby falls asleep in them, they should be moved to a flat surface when possible.

For a full list of current CPSC recalls affecting families with young children, visit cpsc.gov/Recalls.
