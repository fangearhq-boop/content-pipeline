### STORY 1: D49 Student Success Center

**Post 1 — Long-Form** — 10:00 AM MT
Big news for D49 families: the Student Success Center is officially open for the 2026-27 school year.

The SSC is housed at the former Falcon Elementary of Technology site and brings together several support programs that were previously spread across the district. There are two full-time preschool classrooms available Monday through Thursday for families throughout D49. The Elevates program, which provides transition services for students ages 18-21, has relocated to the SSC — meaning students receive those services within the district rather than through outside agencies.

Also on-site: the Motor Team (physical therapists, occupational therapists, and assistants), the Assistive Technology Team, the School to Workforce Alliance Program (SWAP) Coordinator, and the homebound special education teacher.

For families who've been working with any of these programs across multiple locations, this is a meaningful consolidation. One address, one hub, better coordination.

Head to d49.org for enrollment information and to connect with the SSC team.

### STORY 2: Vevor Baby Swing Recall

**Post 2 — Long-Form** — 8:00 AM MT
Heads up for parents of infants: there's a new federal safety recall you need to know about.

Sanven Technology is recalling about 1,020 Vevor baby swings sold online in early 2025. The affected models are BB501K, BB702A, and BB005K. The hazard is serious — these swings have an incline angle greater than 10 degrees, which violates both the mandatory Infant Sleep Products safety standard and the ban on inclined sleepers under the Safe Sleep for Babies Act.

Why does incline matter? An angled surface can cause an infant's head to tip forward, partially blocking the airway. Infants don't yet have the muscle control to reposition themselves, and even a few minutes in an unsafe position can be dangerous.

If you own one of these models, stop using it immediately. Sanven Technology is offering a full refund. You can reach them at 855-599-6320 (Monday–Friday, 9 a.m.–5 p.m. PT), by email at recalling@vevor.com, or online at vevor.com/pages/vevor-baby-swing-recall.

And as always, the CPSC's full recalls list is at cpsc.gov/Recalls — worth bookmarking if you have young kids at home.

### STORY 4: AAP Screen Time Guidelines 2026

**Post 4 — Long-Form** — 5:00 PM MT
Something shifted this year in how pediatricians are thinking about screen time — and if you've been stressed about hitting a specific daily minute count, you can take a breath.

In January 2026, the American Academy of Pediatrics officially retired the "two hours or less" rule for children ages 2 and up. The evidence, they said, had moved past simple time caps. What actually matters is quality, context, and what screens are replacing.

Here's the new framework — they call it the "5 C's":
🔹 Child — consider the individual child's age, development, and needs
🔹 Content — what are they actually watching or doing?
🔹 Calm — is it used to calm down, or to slow down for sleep? That matters.
🔹 Crowding Out — is screen time replacing sleep, play, family time, or movement?
🔹 Communication — are you watching together and talking about it?

The core rules for young kids haven't changed much: no screens for babies under 18 months (except video calls with family), and no more than one hour of high-quality content for kids ages 2-5. For kids 6 and up, the AAP recommends building a Family Media Plan together with your kids rather than imposing a universal number.

Device-free bedrooms and mealtimes are still recommended. But the guilt-per-minute accounting? That's officially off the table.

### STORY 5: Liizousuda Paint Thinner Recall

**Post 5 — Long-Form** — 3:00 PM MT
If you or your kids do any oil painting or art projects at home, take a quick look at your supplies cabinet.

The CPSC recently recalled Liizousuda brand paint thinner — sold on Amazon under the name "Odorless Mineral Spirit / Artist Oil Thinning Medium." It comes in a clear bottle with a black cap. The problem: the bottles don't have child-resistant packaging, even though the contents are low-viscosity petroleum distillates, which can cause chemical pneumonia and serious pulmonary damage if a child swallows even a small amount.

The product was sold on Amazon from March 2025 through February 2026 for about $10. If you bought it during that window, the remedy is a replacement bottle with a child-resistant cap — details are at cpsc.gov/Recalls.

This is a good reminder in general: any art supply with solvents, thinners, or strong chemicals should be stored out of reach, ideally locked, even when the product is labeled as "odorless" or "artist-grade." Odorless doesn't mean safe for kids.
