### STORY 1: CPSC Recalls Halloween Toys and Costumes — Battery and Choking Risks
**Tier:** 1 (Breaking/Trending)
**Pillar:** National Parenting
**Byline:** Jamie Rivera
**Type:** NEW

On October 22 — just days before Halloween — the CPSC issued multiple recalls for Halloween-specific children's products. CIYTI Light-Up Princess Costumes (sold on Amazon) recalled because children can access the batteries; violates federal safety standard. BAMAMON LED Finger Light Toys (~6,533 units) recalled for battery ingestion risk. Kaizou Group Squishy Bun / Squeezy Dumpling toys recalled for violating water beads safety standard (choking/ingestion risk in children). Families should check products immediately before trick-or-treating Oct 31.

**Key angles:** Halloween timing makes this urgent (7 days out); battery ingestion is a serious emergency risk; water beads recalls have been recurring CPSC issue; CIYTI costumes sold widely on Amazon.

**Sources:** CPSC.gov, ConsumerAffairs (Oct 23, 2026), ABC27

### STORY 3: CPSC Recalls Two Infant Sleep Products — Papablic and Joyful Journeys
**Tier:** 2 (Today/Upcoming)
**Pillar:** National Parenting
**Byline:** Jamie Rivera
**Type:** NEW

Separate from the Halloween toy recalls, CPSC also recently recalled two infant sleep products with serious hazards. Papablic Bedside Sleeper/Baby Co-Sleeper (recalled Oct 15): entrapment, suffocation, and fall hazards; violates mandatory standard for play yards. Joyful Journeys Baby Lounger (recent): entrapment and fall hazards; violates mandatory infant sleep products standard. Parents and grandparents with infants under 12 months should stop use immediately.

**Key angles:** Infant sleep safety is critical — these failures can be fatal; bedside sleeper products have been a recurring recall category; grandparents who purchased these as gifts may not know about recalls.

**Sources:** CPSC.gov, Oct 15 recall notice

### STORY 4: AAP's New Screen Time Guidelines — The 2-Hour Rule Is Gone
**Tier:** 2 (Today/Upcoming)
**Pillar:** National Parenting
**Byline:** Sarah Morales
**Type:** NEW

The American Academy of Pediatrics updated its screen time guidance in 2026, replacing the rigid "2-hour limit" with the flexible "5 Cs" framework: Content, Context, Child, Connections, and personal Choices. First major update in ~10 years. Focus shifted from time limits to quality of content and family context. Still recommends no screens for children under 18-24 months except video chatting. Practical guidance: co-viewing, discussing content, and ensuring screens don't replace sleep or physical activity. Relevant as Halloween and holiday season bring more screen time.

**Key angles:** Major policy shift that many parents haven't heard about yet; practical and less guilt-inducing guidance; holiday screen time context; replaces advice parents have heard for a decade.

**Sources:** AAP (Feb 2026 update), EdSurge, Jellies App coverage
