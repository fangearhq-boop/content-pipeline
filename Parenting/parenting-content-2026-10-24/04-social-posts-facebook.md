### STORY 1: CPSC Halloween Toy and Costume Recalls

**Long-Form Post (8:00 AM MT)**
Before kids head out trick-or-treating this week, it's worth a quick check of what's in their Halloween bags — because the CPSC just pulled three children's products on October 22.

The first recall: CIYTI Light-Up Princess Costumes sold on Amazon. The concern is that children can access the batteries directly, which creates a serious risk. Battery ingestion — especially small coin or button batteries — can cause severe internal burns within two hours, even when a child doesn't show immediate symptoms.

The second recall: BAMAMON LED Finger Light Toys (~6,533 units sold). Again, battery ingestion risk. These are the glowing finger toys that have become popular costume accessories.

The third recall: Kaizou Group Squishy Bun and Squeezy Dumpling toys, which contain water beads. These violate federal toy safety standards for water beads — the beads expand in the stomach if swallowed and can cause serious internal blockages in young children.

If you purchased any of these items, check cpsc.gov for full recall details and what to do. And do a quick scan of trick-or-treat candy and accessories before kids dig in.

### STORY 2: D11 $775M Bond on Nov. 3 Ballot

**Long-Form Post (10:00 AM MT)**
Colorado Springs School District 11 has a $775 million bond on the November 3 ballot — and it's the first new D11 bond since 2004.

If it passes, the money would fund upgrades that have been needed for a long time: HVAC replacement at Coronado High School, full rebuilds at Holmes Middle School and Palmer High School. These are among D11's oldest buildings, and the district has been working with aging infrastructure for two decades.

The estimated property tax impact is around $6.50 per month for every $100,000 of home value — so a $300,000 home would see roughly $19.50/month in increased taxes.

The Gazette editorial board has endorsed the measure. D20 also has a bond question on the same ballot.

If you have kids in D11, or if you own property in Colorado Springs, November 3 is the date to know. Early voting is already underway in El Paso County.

D11 families — what do you think? Is this a yes vote for you?

### STORY 3: Infant Sleep Product Recalls

**Long-Form Post (12:00 PM MT)**
A reminder that not all CPSC recalls make the news — two infant sleep products were recently recalled that many parents and grandparents may not know about.

Papablic Bedside Sleepers (also sold as baby co-sleepers), recalled October 15, were flagged for entrapment, suffocation, and fall hazards. The products violate the mandatory safety standard for play yards. If you have one of these, the CPSC guidance is to stop using it immediately.

Joyful Journeys Baby Loungers were also recalled for entrapment and fall hazards, cited as violating mandatory standards for infant sleep products. Baby loungers — the cushioned pods many parents use for daytime lounging — have been a recurring recall category because they're not designed for unsupervised infant sleep.

Both of these products carry real risks. Entrapment and positional suffocation are among the leading causes of infant sleep-related deaths, and a recalled product that looks fine is still a safety failure.

If you know a family with an infant under 12 months, it's worth a quick share. The full recall information, including remedy instructions, is at cpsc.gov.

### STORY 4: AAP Screen Time Update

**Long-Form Post (3:00 PM MT)**
Good news for parents who've been ignoring the 2-hour screen time rule: the American Academy of Pediatrics replaced it in early 2026.

The new framework is called the 5 Cs — Content, Context, Child, Connections, and Choices. Instead of watching the clock, parents are encouraged to think about what their kids are watching, whether they're watching together, how it fits their child's individual needs, and whether it's connecting or isolating them. It's the first major update to AAP screen time guidance in about 10 years.

A few things haven't changed: the AAP still recommends no screens for kids under 18–24 months (except video chatting with family), and discourages screens in the hour before bedtime. But the rigid time cap for older kids is gone.

This update feels especially timely heading into Halloween, holiday break, and all the screen time that tends to come with colder months. The goal isn't less screen time for its own sake — it's more intentional screen time.

### STORY 5: COS Parks Master Plan City Council Vote Monday

**Long-Form Post (5:00 PM MT)**
A quick heads-up for families who use Colorado Springs parks: the City Council votes Monday, October 27, on the 2026 Park System Master Plan.

The master plan sets priorities for COS parks, trails, and recreational spaces over the coming years — covering things like maintenance, new amenities, and how the city allocates resources to the parks system. The Parks Advisory Board has already recommended the plan to City Council.

Also on the agenda: the future of the Meadows Park Community Center. The board has recommended the site be preserved for community-focused use, but what that looks like specifically is still being determined.

If parks are part of your family's regular routine — playgrounds, trails, the Westside pool, Monument Valley, Palmer Park — this vote affects those spaces. City Council meetings are open to the public and the agenda is available at coloradosprings.gov.

Which COS parks do your kids love most? Would love to hear what families are using.
