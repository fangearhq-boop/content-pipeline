### STORY 1: AAP Updated Pediatric UTI Guidelines

**Long-Form Post (8:00 AM MT)**

If your child is under five, here's a health update worth knowing about.

The American Academy of Pediatrics just published new clinical practice guidelines for urinary tract infections in young children. The updated guidance covers kids from 8 days old through age 5 — the age range was expanded from the prior version, so this now includes younger infants. It also drops race as a clinical risk factor, which is a significant change from older guidance that some families may have encountered.

The update came with three technical reports covering how UTIs are diagnosed, treated, and monitored through imaging. UTIs are common in young children and frequently missed or misdiagnosed, especially in infants who can't tell you what's wrong. Knowing that the guidance your pediatrician uses is current matters.

You don't need to do anything differently today — this is an update that your child's doctor will apply during visits. But if your infant or toddler has had unexplained fever, fussiness, or other symptoms that haven't been explained, it's worth bringing up at your next appointment.

### STORY 3: Sensory-Friendly Haunted Forest for Neurodivergent Kids

**Long-Form Post (3:00 PM MT)**

Halloween can be genuinely hard for families of neurodivergent children.

The crowds, the strobe lights, the loud sudden noises — these are features that make Halloween fun for many kids, but they're also the things that make it completely inaccessible for others. For parents of kids with IDD — intellectual and developmental disabilities — October 31 is often a night of watching from the sidelines.

A man in Cascade, Colorado has been quietly solving this problem for about five years. He hosts a sensory-friendly Haunted Forest designed specifically for children with IDD, and this week he invited RiseUp School from Colorado Springs to bring students on a field trip. RiseUp principal Kathy Stults praised the experience. Many of the students are non-verbal.

It's not trying to be the same thing as every other Halloween experience — it's a different experience built with specific kids in mind.

This is the kind of thing that doesn't make the news often enough. If you have a family member or know a family with a neurodivergent child, it's worth knowing options like this exist.

### STORY 4: Día de Muertos at Colorado Springs Fine Arts Center Nov. 1–2

**Long-Form Post (5:00 PM MT)**

The weekend after Halloween, the Colorado Springs Fine Arts Center at Colorado College is hosting a Día de Muertos celebration.

Saturday, November 1: 12:30–4:30 PM
Sunday, November 2: 4–8 PM
Location: 30 W. Dale St., Colorado Springs

Programming includes live music and performances, hands-on cultural activities for families, a children's costume parade, and community cultural events throughout both days. Registration is requested at fac.coloradocollege.edu/events/dia-de-muertos. The event is free or low-cost — check the website for current pricing.

Día de Muertos is a Mexican tradition that honors those who have passed, and it's one of the most visually rich and culturally meaningful observances of the year. The Fine Arts Center's celebration is designed as a community event that families can attend together.

After two straight years of Halloween-adjacent programming in Colorado Springs, this is a good option for the first weekend of November that's something different.
