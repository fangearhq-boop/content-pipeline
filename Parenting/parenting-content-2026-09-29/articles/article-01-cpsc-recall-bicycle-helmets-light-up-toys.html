Two CPSC Recalls Colorado Springs Families Need to Know: Bicycle Helmets and Light-Up Toys

By Sarah Morales | September 29, 2026

Safety | Product Recalls

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission issued two recalls on September 24 that parents in Colorado Springs should know about this week. One involves children's bicycle helmets that fail to meet the federal safety standard. The other involves light-up toys with battery compartments that children can access too easily — and the danger there is serious.

Here's what you need to know about both, and what to do if you have either product at home.

5Color Children's Bicycle Helmet and Pads Sets

The CPSC recalled 5Color Children's Bicycle Helmet and Pads Sets because they violate the mandatory federal safety standard for bicycle helmets. That standard exists to ensure helmets absorb impact effectively in a crash. A helmet that doesn't meet it could fail to protect your child's head when it matters most.

If you have one of these helmets at home, stop using it. Contact the seller for a refund or replacement. Full recall details, including how to identify the affected product, are available at cpsc.gov/Recalls.

In Colorado Springs, where cycling — on trails, in neighborhoods, and in parks — is a major part of family life year-round, this recall is worth checking even if you're not sure about the brand. Not every parent knows exactly what brand their child's helmet is.

ABC Trading Light-Up Children's Toys

The second recall covers ABC Trading Light-Up Children's Toys. These are recalled because the button/coin battery compartments can be accessed by children. This is a different category of danger — not just a hazard if something breaks, but a hazard that exists by design.

Button batteries — the small, flat, disc-shaped batteries used in many light-up toys, key fobs, and similar items — are one of the most serious hazards in pediatric emergency medicine. If swallowed, a button battery can lodge in a child's esophagus and begin generating an electrical current. That current can cause severe internal chemical burns within two hours. Children have died from swallowing button batteries that went undetected.

Stop using these toys immediately. Contact the seller for a refund. If you suspect a child has swallowed a button battery, seek emergency medical attention immediately — do not wait for symptoms.

How to Stay Updated on Recalls

The CPSC maintains a free email alert system at cpsc.gov/Recalls where parents can sign up to receive notifications by product category. It takes about two minutes to set up and is one of the easiest ways to stay ahead of recall news that might otherwise take weeks to reach you through social media or news coverage.
