### STORY 2: D11 Bond Measure — $775 Million

#### Long-Form Post — 10:00 AM MT

Colorado Springs School District 11 hasn't passed a bond measure since 2004 — that's 22 years. A new one could change that this fall. 📋

D11's board is meeting on August 5 to vote on whether to put a $775 million bond measure on the November 2026 ballot. If the board says yes, COS voters would decide whether to approve it in the fall election.

What the money would go toward: air conditioning for D11 school buildings that are 50 to 100 years old, and teacher pay increases. If you've ever picked your kid up from a D11 school in August or September and felt the heat the moment you walked in the door, you know what the AC piece is about. Many of D11's buildings were built before air conditioning was standard — they've been running through Colorado summers without it for decades.

The tax math, per reporting from the Gazette, KRDO, and KOAA: approximately $6.50 per month for every $100,000 of property value. So a home assessed at $300,000 would see roughly $19.50 added to the monthly property tax bill if the bond passes.

The August 5 board meeting is open to the public. If you want to weigh in before the board votes, that's your window.

What do you think — would you support the D11 bond measure?

### STORY 4: CPSC Summer 2026 Recalls

#### Long-Form Post — 3:00 PM MT

With back-to-school prep in full swing, it's a good time to do a quick scan of the CPSC's recent children's product recalls. Five products are worth checking this week. 🚨

**Babypark 3-in-1 Soft Infant/Toddler Carriers:** These carriers have leg openings that are too large for infants, creating a suffocation and fall risk. They were sold on Amazon from approximately April 2024 through May 2026. If you have one, stop using it and contact the seller for a refund.

**POPOOO/Delightmi Jungle Safari LED Finger Lights:** These finger lights have accessible button cell battery compartments, which pose a serious ingestion risk for young children. Sold from approximately July 2025 through April 2026. Keep away from children; contact the seller for information.

**TOMY Boon PIVOT Toddler Tower/Kitchen Step Stools:** This one has 11 reported incidents. The product shifts and leans when a child stands on it, creating a fall hazard. Sold at Target, Amazon, and Babylist from January 2023 through June 2026. Stop using it and contact TOMY/Boon for a remedy.

**Target Gigglescape Under the Sea Popping Toys:** The dome on this toy can detach, exposing small balls that are a choking hazard. Nine reported incidents, including one confirmed choking. Sold at Target. Return it to any Target store for a full refund.

**Peony Design Personalized Baby Bibs and Stroller Bags:** The snaps on these products can detach from the fabric, creating a choking hazard for infants. Recall issued July 23, 2026. Contact Peony Design for a refund.

The full details on each recall are at cpsc.gov. If you're not sure whether a product has been recalled before you buy secondhand, a quick search on the CPSC website by brand name takes about 30 seconds.

### STORY 5: Governors AAP Early Math

#### Long-Form Post — 5:00 PM MT

Most parents know that pediatricians now ask about reading at well-child visits — books in the waiting room, questions about whether you read aloud at home. That became standard partly because the AAP developed formal literacy guidance that made it part of routine pediatric care. Six governors, including Colorado's Jared Polis, want the AAP to do the same thing for math. 🔢

On July 23, the governors sent a letter to the American Academy of Pediatrics asking the organization to develop early numeracy guidance modeled on its existing literacy program. Their specific ask: the AAP should recommend math-based board and card games as tools parents can use at home to build number sense in young kids. The initiative is organized by the Collaborative for Student Success, a group focused on K–12 academic outcomes.

The research behind this push is consistent across studies: a child's math skills at kindergarten entry are one of the strongest predictors of long-term academic success. The gap between kids who arrive with solid number sense and those who don't is one of the hardest to close once school has started.

The practical takeaway right now, before any policy change happens: simple games you probably already have — dice games, card games that involve number recognition, matching games with quantities — build exactly the skills these governors are trying to formalize. You don't need to wait for the AAP to update its guidance to start. With D49 starting Monday, the timing couldn't be better for a few counting games over the weekend.

What math games does your family play? We're curious what's working for COS families right now.
