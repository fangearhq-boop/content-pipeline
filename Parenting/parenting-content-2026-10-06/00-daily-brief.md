### STORY 1: ZMC Group Expands Recall of 245,000 Light-Up Toys — Button Battery Ingestion Hazard
**Type:** NEW
**Tier:** 1 (CPSC recall — safety story, mandatory Tier 1)
**Pillar:** National Parenting (Product Safety)
**Byline:** Jamie Rivera
**Angle:** The CPSC has announced an expanded recall of 245,124 battery-powered light-up toys sold by ZMC Group, including yo-yos, maracas, light-up wands, and LED headbands. The hazard: the toys contain button cell (lithium coin cell) batteries. If a child swallows one, the battery can become lodged in the throat or stomach, creating an electrical current that causes severe internal burns, serious injury, or death — sometimes within two hours. Consumers should stop use immediately, destroy the toys (so they cannot be used by other children), and email recallzmctoy@gmail.com for a full refund. The recall covers light-up toys sold at dollar stores, party supply stores, and online retailers.
**Sources:** cpsc.gov/Recalls (ZMC Group notice), CPSC press release

### STORY 4: AAP Issues New AI Guidance for Pediatric Care — What COS Parents Should Know
**Type:** NEW
**Tier:** 2 (National Parenting — new guidelines from major medical organization, directly relevant to parents)
**Pillar:** National Parenting
**Byline:** Sarah Morales
**Angle:** The American Academy of Pediatrics wrapped up its annual conference October 2-6 with new guidance on artificial intelligence in pediatric practice. The AAP's guidance addresses how AI tools are being used in clinical settings and what parents can expect from AI-assisted care. Separately, the October 2026 issue of Pediatrics (the AAP's flagship journal) published "Trends in Parent-Reported Developmental Disabilities 2019-2024" — tracking how diagnoses of ADHD, autism spectrum disorder, and developmental delays have shifted in recent years. The conference also featured research on smartphone ownership in youth, linking it to higher rates of depression, obesity, and insufficient sleep. For COS parents, this is a moment to understand both how AI is entering healthcare and what the latest research says about developmental health.
