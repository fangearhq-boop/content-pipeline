## STORY 1: D11 School Performance
### FB Long-Form Post | 12:00 PM MT

📚 Big news for D11 families this fall — and it's the kind we like to share.

Colorado overall had a strong year too — nearly two-thirds of schools statewide earned a top rating — but the D11 turnaround story is one worth paying attention to. If you've got kids in the district, or you're thinking about it, this is real progress to note. 🏫

## STORY 2: CooCooBaby Baby Lounger Recall
### FB Long-Form Post | 10:00 AM MT

🚨 Parents of infants — please stop and read this.

The CPSC issued a product safety warning on September 10 for CooCooBaby Baby Loungers. If you have the Classic or Deluxe model, stop using it right now. About 2,355 units were sold on Amazon and coocoobabyofficial.com between $35 and $70, and they've been found to violate the federal mandatory standard for infant sleep products.

The hazards are serious: the sides are shorter than the required minimum height, the sleeping pad is thicker than allowed (which creates a suffocation risk), and there's an open foot opening where an infant could fall out or become trapped. These aren't minor technicalities — they're design issues that can cause real harm.

Here's what to do: Take the lounger out of use immediately, cut up both the lounger and the sleeping pad to destroy it, and then email a photo of the destroyed unit to support@coocoobabyofficial.com. You'll receive a full refund to your original payment method. If you bought it as a gift or have already passed it along, please share this with anyone who might have one. You can find the full recall details at cpsc.gov.

Has anyone in your circle heard about this one? Tag a parent who has a young baby — it's worth a heads-up. 💛

## STORY 3: D20 Late Start Day
### FB Long-Form Post | 8:00 AM MT

Good morning, D20 families — just a heads-up if you haven't checked the calendar: today is a 2-hour late start day across all Academy District 20 schools. 🕐

It's a PLC day — Professional Learning Community. These are built into the district calendar throughout the year so teachers can collaborate, review student data, and work on professional development together. They happen a handful of times each school year and are noted on the district calendar at asd20.org.

If you're newer to the district or just getting used to the calendar, PLC days are different from early release days (which end school early) — today just means the morning start is two hours later than your school's normal time. Afternoon dismissal is unchanged.

And if you haven't bookmarked your district's calendar yet, it's worth five minutes to do it now. D11 families can find theirs at d11.org/calendars, and D49 families at d49.org/discover-d49/district-calendar. Parent teacher conferences for D49 are coming up on October 9.

## STORY 4: AAP Screen Time Guidelines
### FB Long-Form Post | 3:00 PM MT

So the AAP quietly made one of the biggest parenting rule changes of 2026, and a lot of families still haven't heard about it. 📱

Back in January, the American Academy of Pediatrics officially retired the decades-old two-hour-per-day screen time cap for children ages 2 and up. Yes, really. The framework that shaped a generation of parenting advice is gone. What replaced it is something much more flexible — and honestly, more realistic.

The new framework is built around the "5 Cs": Child (what works for your specific kid), Content (quality matters more than minutes), Calm (don't use screens to manage behavior), Crowding out (screens shouldn't replace sleep, physical activity, or real conversation), and Communication (co-viewing and talking about what they're watching). The only hard rule that didn't change: no screens at all for babies under 18 months, except video calls with family. For ages 2–5, an hour of high-quality content is still the guideline, but with context. For ages 6 and up, the AAP now says families should agree on consistent limits together — using a Family Media Plan — rather than following a universal number.

The Family Media Plan tool is free at healthychildren.org and lets you build a customized plan for your household. It's worth 20 minutes if you haven't done it yet. The old rules weren't working for most families anyway — these at least meet you where you are. 👍

## STORY 5: COS Pumpkin Patches
### FB Long-Form Post | 5:00 PM MT

Hey, pumpkin season starts THIS weekend in Colorado Springs and we're here for it 🎃

Long Neck Pumpkin Farm kicks things off on Saturday, September 20 and runs Fridays through Sundays (10am–5pm) through October 26. They've got a hay bale slide, corn boxes, tractor swings, a Farmers Market, and a Story Barn for stories and crafts with the littles. It's a great first-of-the-season option if you want to ease into fall.

October brings two more favorites. Venetucci Farm Pumpkin Fest (run by Gather Mountain Blooms) opens on weekends starting in early October — Fridays from 9:30am to 2pm, Saturdays and Sundays from 9:30am to 4pm. Admission is $5 per person ages 3 and up, pumpkins are $10 each, and they've got hayrides, farm animals, a craft fair, and a maze. You can find the full details and special themed weekends at gathermountainblooms.com.

And if you want something with a little more Colorado flavor, the Miners' Pumpkin Patch at the Western Museum of Mining and Industry (225 North Gate Blvd) runs every Saturday in October. Tickets are $11 online ($13 at the door) and include access to hayrides ($5 extra), animal encounters, gold panning, mining machine demos, and — the kids' favorite — the pumpkin catapult. Pumpkins are $6 each ($7 at the door). Find everything at wmmi.org.
