The AAP Retired the 2-Hour Screen Time Rule — Here's What the New "5 Cs" Framework Means for Your Family

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting | September 18, 2026

One of the most-quoted parenting rules in recent decades — the two-hour-per-day screen time limit for children ages two and up — is officially gone. In January 2026, the American Academy of Pediatrics released updated guidelines that retired the rigid hour-based cap and replaced it with something more nuanced: a framework that asks what screen time is actually doing for a specific child, rather than just counting minutes.

If you've been either quietly ignoring the two-hour rule or feeling guilty about not hitting it, the AAP is now officially saying the clock was never the right measurement anyway. Here's what replaced it, and what it means for Colorado Springs families navigating daily life with screens.

What Changed — and What Didn't

The one rule that didn't change: no screens at all for babies under 18 months, with the exception of video calls with family members. That limit is still firm, and the reasoning is the same — early brain development is best served by in-person interaction, language, and exploration, not passive media.

For ages two through five, the guideline of about one hour per day of high-quality content hasn't disappeared — but it's no longer treated as a hard cap. The emphasis has shifted to what the content is, whether a parent is co-viewing and talking about it, and whether screen time is crowding out sleep, outdoor play, or real conversation. An hour of educational content watched with a parent looks very different from an hour of algorithm-driven videos watched alone.

For ages six and up, the AAP has stepped away from universal numbers entirely. The new recommendation is for families to build consistent, agreed-upon limits together — using a tool called the Family Media Plan — rather than following a one-size-fits-all rule.

The "5 Cs" Framework

The AAP's new approach is organized around five questions families should ask about any screen time, rather than watching the clock:

The "C"The Question to Ask
ChildWhat does this specific child need? Every kid is different — their developmental stage, temperament, and how they respond to screens all matter.
ContentWhat is the quality of what they're watching or doing? Educational, interactive, and co-viewed content is different from passive or marketing-driven media.
CalmIs screen time being used to manage behavior or calm a child down? The AAP cautions against using screens as a default emotional regulator.
Crowding outIs this replacing time for sleep, physical activity, in-person play, or family conversation? If it is, that's a problem regardless of how many minutes are involved.
CommunicationAre you talking about what your child is watching or doing? Co-viewing, asking questions, and connecting content to real life make media time more valuable.

The Family Media Plan

The AAP offers a free, customizable Family Media Plan tool at healthychildren.org. It walks families through setting consistent screen time limits, designating device-free times and spaces (bedrooms and mealtimes are the big two), and establishing shared expectations that everyone in the household understands. It's not a lecture — it's a working document you fill in together, ideally with your kids old enough to participate.

Most families already have some version of informal screen rules. The value of the Family Media Plan is making those rules explicit, consistent, and agreed-upon — which makes them significantly easier to enforce without it turning into a nightly battle.

What's Next

The full Family Media Plan is available at healthychildren.org — the AAP's official parent resource site. It takes about 20 minutes to complete and can be updated as your kids get older or your family's situation changes. The complete updated AAP screen time policy is also published at aap.org for families who want to read the source directly. If screen time is a regular point of friction in your household, the Family Media Plan is probably the most practical tool available right now for getting everyone on the same page.
