D20 Is on a Late Start Today — and Here's How to Never Be Caught Off Guard by a PLC Day Again

By Jamie Rivera | COS Parenting | September 18, 2026

If your household has kids in Academy District 20 and your morning felt a little looser than usual, there's a reason for it: today, Friday, September 18, is a two-hour late start day for all D20 schools. Every school in the district begins two hours later than its normal schedule.

This one tends to sneak up on families who haven't had a chance to internalize the district calendar yet. If that's you this morning, you're not alone — and the good news is that it's easy to stay ahead of these going forward.

What a PLC Day Actually Is

PLC stands for Professional Learning Community. These days are built into school district calendars not as a convenience for administrators, but as structured time for teachers to do collaborative, data-informed professional work. During a PLC day, teachers review student performance data together, align curriculum across classrooms, discuss instructional strategies, and work on professional development goals as teams.

The research behind PLCs is pretty strong — teacher teams that regularly review student data and adjust their instruction together tend to produce better outcomes than teachers working in isolation. Districts like D20 build these days in specifically because it's hard to find this kind of protected collaboration time during the regular school day. It's professional time, not a day off.

For families, the practical impact is simple: schools start two hours later than their posted bell time, but dismissal is at the regular time. Lunch, after-school programs, and bus routes are unchanged. It's only the morning start that shifts.

How to Stay Ahead of These Dates

Each of Colorado Springs' major school districts publishes a full annual calendar with PLC days, early release days, no-school days, and holidays clearly marked. Bookmarking your district's calendar is the single easiest thing you can do to avoid schedule surprises for the rest of the year.

For D20 families, the district calendar is at asd20.org. The calendar marks every PLC late start day well in advance. Many D20 schools also send calendar reminders through their parent communication systems (Parent Square, email) a few days ahead of these events.

D11 families can find the district calendar at d11.org/calendars/district-calendar. D49 families should check d49.org/discover-d49/district-calendar. It's also worth noting that D49 has Parent Teacher Conferences coming up on October 9, 2026 — no students that day across the district.

What's Next

D20's full calendar for the 2026-27 school year is available at asd20.org, and it includes all remaining PLC days, holidays, and early release dates through next spring. If you haven't synced it to your phone calendar yet, it takes about three minutes and saves a lot of scrambling. You can also sign up for email or text updates from your specific school through the school's parent communication system — those typically send a reminder the Friday before any unusual schedule week. D49's Parent Teacher Conferences on October 9 are the next major schedule event across the area worth planning around.
