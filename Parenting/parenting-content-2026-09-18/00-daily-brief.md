### STORY 2: CooCooBaby Baby Lounger Recalled — 2,355 Units Pose Suffocation and Fall Risk
- **Tier:** T1
- **Pillar:** National Parenting (Product Safety)
- **Byline:** Sarah Morales
- **Summary:** The CPSC issued a product safety warning on September 10 for CooCooBaby Baby Loungers (Classic and Deluxe models). About 2,355 units sold on Amazon and the brand's own website violate the mandatory Infant Sleep Products standard and pose suffocation and fall hazards. Parents should stop use immediately.
- **Key Facts:**
  - CPSC product safety warning date: September 10, 2026
  - Products: "Classic" and "Deluxe" CooCooBaby Baby Loungers (multiple colors)
  - Units recalled: approximately 2,355
  - Sold: Amazon.com and coocoobabyofficial.com; price range $35–$70
  - Hazards: sides shorter than minimum height limit (infant unsecured); sleeping pad exceeds maximum thickness (suffocation risk); open foot opening (fall/entrapment risk)
  - Violation: mandatory standard for Infant Sleep Products
  - Action: Stop using immediately; destroy unit (cut up lounger and pad); email photo of destroyed unit to support@coocoobabyofficial.com; receive full refund to original payment method
  - No injuries or deaths reported at time of recall
- **Source:** cpsc.gov (Recalls/2026/CooCooBaby-Baby-Loungers...)
- **Article:** article-02-coocoobaby-lounger-recall.html

### STORY 4: The AAP Retired the 2-Hour Screen Time Rule — Here's What Replaced It
- **Tier:** T1
- **Pillar:** National Parenting (Health)
- **Byline:** Sarah Morales
- **Summary:** In January 2026, the American Academy of Pediatrics officially retired the decades-old two-hour-per-day screen time cap for children ages 2 and up, replacing it with a "5 Cs" framework that focuses on quality, context, and family conversation rather than rigid time limits. Here's what it means for Colorado Springs families.
- **Key Facts:**
  - AAP released updated guidelines January 2026
  - Retired: two-hour/day screen time cap for ages 2 and up
  - New framework: "5 Cs" — Child (individual needs matter), Content (quality over quantity), Calm (don't use screens to manage behavior), Crowding out (screens shouldn't replace sleep/activity/play), Communication (co-view and talk about content)
  - Unchanged: no screens for children under 18 months (except video chatting)
  - Ages 2–5: 1 hour/day of high-quality content, with co-viewing
  - Ages 6 and up: family-agreed consistent limits built with a Family Media Plan (not a universal number)
  - Family Media Plan tool available at healthychildren.org
  - Device-free bedrooms and mealtimes recommended for all ages
- **Source:** aap.org, healthychildren.org, prodigygame.com/main-en/blog/screen-time-aap-guidelines-2026
- **Article:** article-04-aap-screen-time-five-cs.html
