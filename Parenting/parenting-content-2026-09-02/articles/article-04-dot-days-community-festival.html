Dot Days Community Festival Returns to Colorado Springs September 9–11

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting | September 2, 2026

Three days of family-friendly programming covering cultural connections, citizen science, and nature — right here in Colorado Springs the week after Labor Day.

The week after Labor Day weekend, Colorado Springs hosts the Dot Days Community Festival — a three-day event built around science, culture, and the natural world. This year it runs September 9–11, with sessions designed for families and kids of all ages.

If you're looking for something to do with kids in the first full week of September that goes beyond the usual after-school routine, Dot Days is worth putting on the calendar.

What Is Dot Days?

Dot Days is a community festival organized around three distinct programming tracks:

  • Cultural Connections — activities and programming that explore diverse cultural traditions, arts, and community stories
  • Citizen Science — hands-on nature observation and data-collection activities that involve kids as active participants in scientific inquiry
  • Our Place in Nature — outdoor and environmental programming that connects families to the natural landscape of the Pikes Peak region

The Colorado Springs Pioneers Museum at Boulder Park is involved in the programming, lending the festival a grounding in the region's history and natural sciences.

Bonus: Colorado at 150 Lecture

Running alongside Dot Days, the Colorado Springs Pioneers Museum is presenting a "We The People: Colorado at 150" lecture series. On September 12 from 2 PM to 3 PM, there's a session at Boulder Park that digs into the state's history and civic story. It's a natural complement to the festival's programming if you have older kids who are studying Colorado history — or if you're curious yourself.

Why It Works for Colorado Springs Families

One of the things Colorado Springs does well is outdoor and nature-based programming for kids. Events like Dot Days take that a step further by pairing outdoor exploration with cultural learning and science engagement — three things that reinforce what kids are doing in school while packaging it in a way that feels more like an adventure than a lesson.

Citizen science programming in particular tends to resonate with kids who want to feel like they're doing something that matters. When kids collect real data and observe real patterns, they see themselves as participants in the world around them — not just students being told what's true.

What's Next

September in Colorado Springs is packed with outdoor and community events — Dot Days is one of the more underrated ones. Worth checking out before the weather turns.
