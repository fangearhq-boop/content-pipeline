D49 Fall Break Activity Guide: What to Do With Kids This Week in Colorado Springs

By Sarah Morales | October 16, 2026 | Local Events

District 49 schools are closed October 12 through 23 — that's 10 days of fall break for families to fill. The good news is that this week overlaps with some of the best free and low-cost fall activities in Colorado Springs, and the timing works in your favor: events that would otherwise require fighting weekend crowds are more manageable on a Tuesday or Wednesday afternoon.

Here's a rundown of what's worth doing before the break ends.

Cool Science Festival — Star Party (Saturday, October 17)

The 2026 Cool Science Festival wraps up this weekend, and the final-night star party at Cheyenne Mountain State Park on Saturday is the highlight. Organized by the Cool Science organization in partnership with UCCS, this event brings out telescopes and astronomers who walk families through what they're seeing in the October sky. It's free, it's outdoors, and it's the kind of thing kids remember.

Star parties are typically scheduled around dusk and run until around 10 PM, weather permitting. Check coolscience.org for the confirmed start time before you go. Cheyenne Mountain State Park is at 410 JL Ranch Heights Road — bring layers; October evenings in Colorado Springs get cold quickly.

Barks & Boos Fall Fest (Sunday, October 18)

On Sunday, Bear Creek Dog Park hosts Barks & Boos from 11 AM to 3 PM. Admission is free. If your family has a dog, this one's a natural — it's a Halloween-themed dog event with costumes, activities, and the usual park energy. Even without a dog, it's a low-key outdoor afternoon that doesn't cost anything. Bear Creek Regional Park is at 21 Bear Creek Road.

Venetucci Farm Pumpkin Picking (Fridays through Sundays through October 31)

Venetucci Farm has been a Colorado Springs fall tradition for decades. The farm opens Friday through Sunday through Halloween, and fall break gives D49 families a chance to go during the week when weekend crowds aren't a factor. Check the farm's schedule for any midweek openings.

Venetucci is located off South Nevada Avenue and has been tied to the Cheyenne Mountain community since the 1940s. It's worth going at least once before pumpkins disappear.

Boo at the Zoo — Cheyenne Mountain Zoo (Through October 31)

Cheyenne Mountain Zoo's Boo at the Zoo runs on select dates through Halloween. Remaining 2026 dates include October 17, 23 through 25, and 30 through 31. Advance tickets are required — the event regularly sells out, and walk-up availability is not guaranteed. Book at cmzoo.org before you head up the hill.

Boo at the Zoo is a paid event; the zoo's general admission pricing applies plus the event add-on. It's one of the more elaborate Halloween experiences in Colorado Springs, with costumed characters, decorated paths, and trick-or-treat stations through the zoo grounds.

Other Options for the Rest of Break

If you've already done the pumpkin patch and the zoo, a few other ideas for the second half of break:

  • Garden of the Gods: Always free, always worth it, and October light in the park is exceptional. No advance tickets needed.
  • Cheyenne Mountain State Park day hikes: While you're there for the star party Saturday, the park's trail system is open during the day for families looking for a lower-effort hike than the Front Range alternatives.
  • Colorado Springs Pioneers Museum (downtown): Free admission, good for a rainy or cold day midweek.

What's Next

D49 fall break runs through October 23 — school resumes Monday the 24th. Halloween falls on Saturday, October 31, which means trick-or-treat weekend is coming up fast. If your family is planning Boo at the Zoo, book tickets now at cmzoo.org; the remaining dates are limited. For more family events in Colorado Springs this weekend, check the full listings at visitcos.com.
