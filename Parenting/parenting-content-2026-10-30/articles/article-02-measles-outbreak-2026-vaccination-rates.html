US Measles Cases Hit 4,588 in 2026 — Here's What Colorado Springs Parents Should Know

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting

October 30, 2026

The United States has recorded 4,588 confirmed measles cases so far in 2026 — the highest annual total since measles was declared eliminated from the country in 2000. That number, confirmed through October 22, is roughly double what the country saw all of last year. Three people have died.

For COS parents, the most relevant part of this story isn't the outbreak itself — it's the vaccination rate data sitting underneath it. Kindergarten MMR coverage has slipped to 92.4% nationally, down from 95.2% in 2019-20. That slide is small in percentage terms but significant in practice, because measles spreads aggressively among unvaccinated populations. Ninety-six percent of 2026 cases were in people who were unvaccinated or had unknown vaccination status.

Who Is Getting Sick

Children ages 5 through 19 make up the largest share of 2026 cases at 42%. Children under 5 account for 17% of cases — and of those young children, 15% required hospitalization. Measles in young kids carries real risks: pneumonia, encephalitis, and in rare cases death.

Adults are not immune. A significant portion of 2026 cases have been in adults who were either unvaccinated or who received only one dose of MMR as children, before the two-dose protocol became standard in the early 1990s.

Why Vaccination Rates Are Falling

Researchers and pediatricians have pointed to several factors: vaccine hesitancy that accelerated during the pandemic, changes in national vaccine guidance that created confusion for parents, disruptions to routine well visits that shifted the timing of when kids get caught up, and growing numbers of non-medical vaccination exemptions filed in some states.

Pennsylvania currently leads the country with roughly 1,222 confirmed cases as of mid-October. But the outbreak has spread across multiple states, and unvaccinated clusters in any community — including Colorado — create a pathway for spread.

What COS Parents Should Do

MMR is a two-dose vaccine. The first dose is recommended at 12 to 15 months of age; the second at 4 to 6 years. Both doses are needed for full protection. If you're not certain your kids received both, there are two easy ways to check: call your pediatrician's office and ask them to pull the records, or look up your child's record in Colorado's immunization registry through coimmunize.org.

You don't need a separate appointment to check records. A phone call or portal message is usually enough. If your child is missing a dose, it can be given at the next well visit or a dedicated catch-up appointment.

For parents of school-age children: public schools in Colorado require MMR for enrollment, so if your child is currently enrolled without a medical or religious exemption, they likely have at least one dose on file. But it's worth confirming the second dose is documented, particularly for kids who started kindergarten between 2019 and 2022 when well-visit scheduling was often disrupted.

What's Next

The CDC is continuing to track the outbreak and updating its case count regularly. Pediatricians across the country are urging parents to verify vaccination records before the holiday travel season, when large gatherings in enclosed spaces make transmission more likely.

If your kids are current on MMR, no action is needed. If there's any doubt, a records check takes five minutes and removes the uncertainty. Your pediatrician's office can help with either catch-up doses or documentation.
