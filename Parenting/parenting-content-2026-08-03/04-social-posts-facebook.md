### STORY 2: CPSC Recalls — Infant Walkers and Bouncer Seats

**Long-Form Post (10:00 AM MT)**

Summer is peak CPSC recall season, and if you have a baby or young toddler at home, it's worth a quick check before the school year picks up and the chaos hits.

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission has flagged infant walkers and infant bouncer/rocker seats in recent recall actions covering products sold through Amazon and other online marketplaces. Infant walkers are one of the most consistently recalled product categories — the CPSC notes they're responsible for thousands of emergency room visits annually, with fall hazards, tip-overs, and access to stairs being the primary concerns. Several models sold under third-party brand names are affected.

Bouncer and rocker seat recalls typically involve structural failure or tip-over risks, particularly when used on elevated surfaces like tables or counters.

Here's what to do: head to cpsc.gov and use the recall search to enter your product name or model number. If your product is listed, stop use immediately. Most recalls offer a full refund or free repair, and the process is handled directly through the retailer or manufacturer.

We'll update with specific model numbers as CPSC publishes additional details.

### STORY 3: D11 Bond Vote — 2 Days Away

**Long-Form Post (12:00 PM MT)**

Two days from now, District 11 voters decide whether to pass the district's first major bond measure in more than 20 years.

The vote is Wednesday, August 5 at 5:30 PM. The amount on the table is $775 million. If approved, the bond would fund HVAC upgrades across D11 schools, safety improvements, classroom modernization, and at least one new construction. The estimated cost to homeowners is approximately $6.50 per month for every $100,000 of home value.

Here's why the timing matters: D11's first day of school is August 12 — exactly one week after the vote. If the bond passes, planning and project timelines begin almost immediately. If it doesn't, the district faces the same aging infrastructure with no clear funding path. Families whose kids attend D11 schools will feel the results of Wednesday's decision in real time.

For more information, including details on what each school would receive, visit d112026bondplanning.com. That site breaks down the bond by project type and school location.

### STORY 4: Beat the Heat

**Long-Form Post (3:00 PM MT)**

It's the first week of school for D49 families, and Colorado Springs is greeting it with a week of upper 90s. If you need somewhere to take the kids that isn't your living room floor in front of a box fan, here are a few options worth knowing about.

Garden of the Gods offers free morning nature programs Monday through Wednesday at 10 AM. The morning hours are cooler, the trails offer shade, and the programs are designed for families. It's one of the better free options in the city right now, and the Visitor Center is air-conditioned if you need a break.

America the Beautiful Park has a splash pad that's a reliable choice on hot days — free entry, central location, and kids can get soaked. The splash pads along Fountain Creek are also an option.

If you want fully indoor, the Westside Community Center and the Monument Valley Park area both offer programs and cool spaces this time of year. The Cheyenne Mountain Zoo has shaded pathways and indoor exhibits that provide relief from the heat, though admission applies.

For parents navigating the gap between D49's start and D11's August 12 start: this week is a good one to plan around the heat rather than fight it.

### STORY 5: AAP Screen Time 5 C's

**Long-Form Post (6:00 PM MT)**

The school year starts and screen time suddenly becomes a whole conversation again. That's not a coincidence — structured days create clear contrasts, and "homework vs. YouTube" becomes a daily negotiation that parents didn't sign up for.

The American Academy of Pediatrics moved away from its old blanket rules (no screens under 2, one hour per day under 5) and replaced them with the 5 C's framework, which is actually designed to fit real family life. Here's the quick version:

Child — What are your child's individual needs, maturity level, and sleep habits? Screen time affects a 4-year-old and a 9-year-old very differently. Content — Is it educational, creative, social, or passive? Not all screen time is the same. Context — Are they watching alone or with you? On a school night or a weekend? Is it a reward or a default? Communication — Are you talking with your kids about what they're watching and why? That conversation matters more than the timer. Crowd — Who's influencing what your child watches? Friends, algorithms, and group chats all play a role.

The AAP doesn't give a magic number anymore because research shows the relationship matters more than the minutes. If any of the C's are off, that's where to start.

Healthychildren.org has the full interactive resource if you want to dig in.
