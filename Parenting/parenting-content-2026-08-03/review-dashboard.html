CPSC Recall Alert — Infant Walkers and Bouncer Seats Recalled for Safety Hazards
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Summer is peak CPSC recall season, and if you have a baby or young toddler at home, it's worth a quick check before the school year picks up and the chaos hits. The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission has flagged infant walkers and infant bouncer/rocker seats in recent recall actions covering products sold through Amazon and other online marketplaces. Infant walkers are one of the most consistently recalled product categories — the CPSC notes they're responsible for thousands of emergency room visits annually, with fall hazards, tip-overs, and access to stairs being the primary concerns. Several models sold under third-party brand names are affected. Bouncer and rocker seat recalls typically involve structural failure or tip-over risks, particularly when used on elevated surfaces like tables or counters. Here's what to do: head to cpsc.gov and use the recall search to enter your product name or model number. If your product is listed, stop use immediately. Most recalls offer a full refund or free repair, and the process is handled directly through the retailer or manufacturer. We'll update with specific model numbers as CPSC publishes additional details. Has a CPSC recall ever affected something in your home? How did you find out? --- ### Image Post Caption CPSC recall alert for infant walkers and bouncer seats. Check your product at cpsc.gov. ### Long-Form Post Two days from now, District 11 voters decide whether to pass the district's first major bond measure in more than 20 years. The vote is Wednesday, August 5 at 5:30 PM. The amount on the table is $775 million. If approved, the bond would fund HVAC upgrades across D11 schools, safety improvements, classroom modernization, and at least one new construction. The estimated cost to homeowners is approximately $6.50 per month for every $100,000 of home value. Here's why the timing matters: D11's first day of school is August 12 — exactly one week after the vote. If the bond passes, planning and project timelines begin almost immediately. If it doesn't, the district faces the same aging infrastructure with no clear funding path. Families whose kids attend D11 schools will feel the results of Wednesday's decision in real time. Screen Time and the School Year — AAP's 5 C's Framework for Getting Back on Track
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post The school year starts and screen time suddenly becomes a whole conversation again. That's not a coincidence — structured days create clear contrasts, and "homework vs. YouTube" becomes a daily negotiation that parents didn't sign up for. The American Academy of Pediatrics moved away from its old blanket rules (no screens under 2, one hour per day under 5) and replaced them with the 5 C's framework, which is actually designed to fit real family life. Here's the quick version: Child — What are your child's individual needs, maturity level, and sleep habits? Screen time affects a 4-year-old and a 9-year-old very differently. Content — Is it educational, creative, social, or passive? Not all screen time is the same. Context — Are they watching alone or with you? On a school night or a weekend? Is it a reward or a default? Communication — Are you talking with your kids about what they're watching and why? That conversation matters more than the timer. Crowd — Who's influencing what your child watches? Friends, algorithms, and group chats all play a role. The AAP doesn't give a magic number anymore because research shows the relationship matters more than the minutes. If any of the C's are off, that's where to start. Healthychildren.org has the full interactive resource if you want to dig in. How do you handle screen time in your house during the school year — is there a system that actually works for your family? --- ### Image Post Caption The AAP's 5 C's for screen time: Child, Content, Context, Communication, Crowd. A more useful framework than the old "no screens under 2" rule — and one that actually fits how families live. School year reset starts now.
