### STORY 2: CPSC Recalls: 4 Children's Products Flagged in Early November

**Long-Form Post**
Before the week gets away from you, check your home for these four products.

The Consumer Product Safety Commission posted four recalls in early November that every parent of young children should know about.

**Magnetopia Magnetic Building Cubes and Blocks** — recalled for magnet ingestion risk. Loose or swallowed magnets can attract through intestinal walls and cause serious internal injury. If you have these, stop use immediately.

**Arlopu Slide Playsets** — recalled because openings in the product can trap a child's torso, creating a head and neck entrapment hazard. The recall covers about 10,318 units.

**Kikea Store X-VESOLJE Nursing Pillows** — recalled for violating mandatory nursing pillow safety standards, posing a suffocation risk.

**Baby Brezza SuperFast Sterilizer Dryers** — recalled because the unit can overheat while operating, creating a burn hazard if the surface is handled.

For refunds or replacements, check cpsc.gov or call the CPSC hotline at 1-800-638-2772. All four recalls were posted on November 5, 2026.

Do you check the CPSC recall list regularly? It updates weekly — worth bookmarking.

### STORY 4: AAP Flu Guidance for 2026–2027: Vaccinate Now, Doses Unchanged

**Long-Form Post**
The American Academy of Pediatrics just released its updated influenza prevention recommendations for the 2026–2027 season, and the main message is: if your child hasn't been vaccinated yet, don't wait.

The number of doses recommended for children remains unchanged from last season. For most children 9 and older who've had a flu vaccine before, that's one dose. For some younger children getting vaccinated for the first time, it may be two doses four weeks apart — your pediatrician can confirm.

The timing matters. Colorado's flu season typically peaks between January and February, which means kids vaccinated now will have full protection when it's needed most. Earlier this month, Colorado reported its first child flu death of the 2026–2027 season — a reminder that flu isn't just inconvenient, it can be serious.

If your family has already gotten vaccinated, you're ahead. If not, this week is a good week to call your pediatrician or stop by a pharmacy.

### STORY 5: Talking to COS Kids About Veterans Day: Age-by-Age Guide

**Long-Form Post**
Colorado Springs is one of the most military-connected communities in the country. Fort Carson, the U.S. Air Force Academy, Peterson Space Force Base, Schriever Space Force Base, Cheyenne Mountain Space Force Station — all within the Pikes Peak region. Many COS kids have a parent, grandparent, aunt, or uncle who served or is currently serving.

That makes Veterans Day feel different here than it does in a lot of places. It's not abstract for a lot of our kids. So how do you talk about it in an age-appropriate way?

**Ages 3–5:** Keep it simple and concrete. "Veterans Day is a day we say thank you to the people who kept us safe." If you know a veteran personally, today is a great day to call them.

**Ages 6–10:** You can go a little deeper. Talk about the different branches of the military, what service means, and why we set aside a day to honor it. COS's military installations give kids here a real connection to these ideas.

**Ages 11 and older:** Teens can handle history and nuance. Talk about why Veterans Day is in November, what Armistice Day was, and what service has meant in your own family's story.

If you're out today, the Memorial Park ceremony at 11 AM is a meaningful place to bring kids of all ages. The WWII Aviation Museum is free today and excellent for school-age kids.

How does your family mark Veterans Day?
