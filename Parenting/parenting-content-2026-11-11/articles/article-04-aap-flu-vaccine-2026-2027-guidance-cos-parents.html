The AAP's Flu Vaccine Guidance Just Updated. Here's What COS Parents Should Know.

By Jamie Rivera | November 11, 2026 | COS Parenting

National Parenting

The American Academy of Pediatrics published its updated influenza prevention and control recommendations for the 2026–2027 season in the November issue of Pediatrics. Here's what changed, what didn't, and what it means for COS families right now.

What's New in the 2026–2027 Guidance

The number of flu vaccine doses recommended for children is unchanged from last season. That means most children 9 and older who've received at least one flu vaccine in a prior season need just one dose. Younger children, or those receiving a flu vaccine for the first time, may need two doses four weeks apart. Your child's pediatrician can confirm the right number based on your child's vaccination history.

The AAP recommends annual flu vaccination for all children 6 months of age and older, without exception for children who've had the flu before. Prior infection doesn't confer sufficient protection against different circulating strains.

Why Timing Matters Right Now

Colorado's flu season typically peaks between January and February. That means children vaccinated in November will have full protection in place during the period of highest risk. The vaccine takes approximately two weeks to reach full effectiveness after administration.

Earlier this month, Colorado reported its first child flu death of the 2026–2027 season — a reminder that flu complications in children aren't rare and that early-season vaccination matters. By the time flu activity is visibly high in the community, it's often too late to get ahead of it.

National flu vaccination uptake among children has been running around 49 to 50 percent — meaning roughly half of all eligible children aren't vaccinated each season. If your family falls in that group and hasn't gotten vaccinated yet, this week is a practical time to do it.

Where to Get Vaccinated in COS

Flu vaccines for children are available at pediatrician offices, family medicine practices, and most major pharmacies in Colorado Springs, including those in grocery stores. Children under 18 may need a parent or guardian present at pharmacy locations — call ahead to confirm age policies.

Uchealth, Centura Health, and Penrose-St. Francis Health Services all offer flu vaccination. El Paso County Public Health also periodically holds community vaccination clinics — check their calendar at elpasoco.com for upcoming dates.

What's Next

The full AAP policy statement — "Recommendations for Prevention and Control of Influenza in Children, 2026–2027" — is available at publications.aap.org for pediatricians and parents who want the full clinical detail. If your family hasn't been vaccinated yet, contact your pediatrician or a local pharmacy this week to schedule. Colorado's flu season is early enough in the fall that November vaccination still provides the full protective window.
