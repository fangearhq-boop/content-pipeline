### STORY 1: CPSC Recalls — HABA Rainbow Rattle and TooyBing Stacking Toys

**Long-Form Post (8:00 AM MT)**

Two toy recalls worth checking before you finalize any holiday wish lists this year.

The first is the HABA Rainbow Rattle Grasping and Teething Toy (recall #26-719). The knot on the elastic cord can come untied, releasing small parts that pose a choking and ingestion hazard. This affects about 2,000 units sold between December 2025 and July 2026 at Target, Amazon, Nordstrom, and specialty toy stores — typically for about $13. The remedy is a full refund. No injuries have been reported, but if you have one, stop use and contact HABA USA at 800-468-6873 to claim the refund.

The second is the TooyBing Wooden Bead Stacking Toy (model TB-MZWJ). These wooden beads violate the federal small parts ban, making them a choking hazard for children under 3. About 21,758 units were sold on Amazon between July 2023 and December 2025 for about $10. The remedy is a free replacement — stop use, destroy the toy, and email a photo to TooyBing to get the compliant version.

These are both August 2026 recalls, which means some families may have them on shelves without knowing. The holiday season is a good time to check: visit cpsc.gov/Recalls and search the product name. If you're shopping for a family with young children, it's also a quick way to vet a gift idea.

### STORY 2: AAP 2026 — Mental and Emotional Health at Every Visit

**Long-Form Post (10:00 AM MT)**

There's a meaningful shift in what the American Academy of Pediatrics is now asking pediatricians to do at well-child visits — and it matters for how you approach your kids' checkups.

In May 2026, the AAP published a framework in Pediatrics called "Framework for Approaching Healthy Mental and Emotional Development in Pediatrics." The core change: mental, emotional, and relational health shouldn't come up only when there's a known concern — it should be woven into every visit, from infancy forward. The approach includes anticipatory guidance, surveillance, screening, and brief interventions, delivered in ways that account for trauma and equity.

This builds on the AAP's 2025 screening guidelines, which already called for anxiety screening annually from ages 8–18, depression and suicide risk screening starting at 12, and substance use assessments annually after 11. The 2026 framework extends the idea: check-ins on how kids are doing emotionally should be a routine part of the conversation, not a special flag.

What does this mean practically? At your next well-child visit, you can ask your pediatrician how they're incorporating social-emotional check-ins — and expect them to have a framework for it. You can also visit healthychildren.org for guidance on what to look for at each developmental stage.

### STORY 4: D49 Purple Star Schools — All 7 Falcon Zone Schools Recognized

**Long-Form Post (3:00 PM MT)**

If your family is military-connected and you're in the D49 zone, this is worth knowing.

All seven schools in District 49's Falcon Zone now hold Colorado's Purple Star designation. That means every school in that cluster has met the state criteria for supporting military-connected students: a designated staff contact for military families, programs to help kids adjust when they transition in or out, and training for staff. The designation is valid for three years.

The recognition came through Colorado's April 7, 2026 ceremony — the state's second annual round under HB24-1076, the law that created the Purple Star program in 2024. D49's Board of Education had unanimously passed a Purple Star resolution back in December 2025, setting the groundwork for this sweep. This year 22 additional schools across Colorado were recognized in the ceremony; multiple D49 Falcon Zone campuses were among them.

For COS, this matters. We're a military city — Fort Carson, Peterson Space Force Base, Schriever SFB, and NORAD all draw families who may PCS multiple times through their kids' school years. Having a school with a designated point of contact and transition support isn't a small thing if you've ever moved midyear with a kid who didn't know anyone.

You can check which other COS schools hold the Purple Star at ed.cde.state.co.us/cdeawards/purplestarschools.

### STORY 5: COS Holiday Season Preview — Flying W, Royal Gorge Santa Express, Festival of Lights

**Long-Form Post (5:00 PM MT)**

The COS holiday season officially starts kicking off in two weeks. Here are three events worth getting on the calendar now — two of them sell out.

**Flying W Ranch Cowboy Christmas Jubilee — Dec. 3–23**
3330 Chuckwagon Road. Multiple dinner show seatings each evening: 3:00, 6:00, 6:30, and 7:00 PM. The Flying W Wranglers do their thing — classic western cowboy show with Christmas music, and dinner is included (smoked Angus steak). Pricing is roughly $65 for adults 13+ and $30 for kids 4–12; check flyingw.com/christmas/ for exact 2026 prices. This is a COS tradition. If you've got family visiting over the holidays who hasn't seen it, this is the booking to make.

**Royal Gorge Route Railroad Santa Express — Select December dates**
Departing from Cañon City (about 45 minutes from COS). Tickets are approximately $114 adult and $104 per child (ages 1–12). This one sells out early; don't wait until December to look. Book at royalgorgeroute.com/santa-express. Note: this is the Royal Gorge Railroad in Cañon City — not a Pikes Peak train.

**Festival of Lights Parade — Saturday, Dec. 5, 5:50 PM**
Tejon Street, downtown Colorado Springs. The 42nd annual parade is free for all ages. Also that day: the Pioneers Museum Family Fun Day runs 10 AM–2 PM on the same block. The parade is an easy one to build an afternoon around.
