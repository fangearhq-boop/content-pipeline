Two Toy Recalls Parents Should Check Before Holiday Shopping: HABA Rattle and TooyBing Beads

By Jamie Rivera | COS Parenting | November 19, 2026

Two toy recalls are worth checking before holiday wish lists get finalized this year. Both were issued by the Consumer Product Safety Commission earlier in 2026, and both affect products that are still in circulation. The holiday shopping season is a practical moment to run through toy bins and double-check anything that was purchased in the past couple of years.

HABA Rainbow Rattle Grasping and Teething Toy (Recall #26-719)

DetailInfo
Recall dateAugust 27, 2026
Units affectedApproximately 2,000
Sold atTarget, Amazon, Nordstrom, specialty toy stores
Sale periodDecember 2025 – July 2026
PriceApproximately $13
HazardChoking and ingestion — elastic cord knot can untie, releasing small parts
InjuriesNone reported
RemedyFull refund ($12.99)
Contact800-468-6873 or habausa.com

The HABA Rainbow Rattle (item #2012261001, batch code 0925) was imported by Habermaass Corporation (HABA USA) in Skaneateles, New York. It was marketed as a grasping and teething toy for infants. The problem is structural: the knot tying the elastic cord can come undone, and the small parts that come loose pose a choking or ingestion risk. HABA USA is offering a full refund. If you have one, stop use immediately and contact HABA to claim the remedy — they'll ask for a photo of the product.

The recall page is at: cpsc.gov — HABA Rainbow Rattle Recall.

TooyBing Wooden Bead Stacking Toys (Model TB-MZWJ)

DetailInfo
Recall dateAugust 13, 2026
Units affectedApproximately 21,758
Sold atAmazon
Sale periodJuly 2023 – December 2025
PriceApproximately $10
HazardChoking — wooden beads violate the federal small parts ban for children under 3
RemedyFree replacement (compliant version available from October 2026 onward)
How to claimDestroy the toy, email a photo to TooyBing

The TooyBing Wooden Bead Stacking Toy was sold on Amazon and marketed as a Montessori-style toy. The issue is that the wooden beads are small enough to violate federal size requirements for products intended for children under 3. With about 21,758 units sold over a two-year window, there are likely many still in homes where younger siblings have come along since the original purchase.

The recall page is at: cpsc.gov — TooyBing Stacking Toy Recall.

How to Check for Any Toy Recall

The fastest way to check: visit cpsc.gov/Recalls and use the search box with the product name or brand. The database includes all active recalls and shows the remedy for each. For toys specifically, you can filter by category. The site also has a recall alert subscription for email or text notifications when new recalls are issued.

What's Next

Both recalls remain active. If you've claimed the remedy on either product, you're done. If you haven't, the holiday season is a reasonable prompt: toy bins get sorted this time of year, and families shopping for gifts for young children can run a quick search before buying. The CPSC adds new recalls weekly — bookmarking the site takes two minutes and pays off quickly when a product you own shows up.
