All 7 D49 Falcon Zone Schools Now Hold Colorado Purple Star Designation for Military Families

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting | November 19, 2026

Every school in District 49's Falcon Zone now holds the Colorado Purple Star designation, which recognizes schools that provide strong support systems for military-connected students and families. The recognition was confirmed through the state's April 7, 2026 ceremony — the second annual Purple Star round since Colorado created the program in 2024.

What the Purple Star Designation Means

The Colorado Purple Star program was created under HB24-1076, signed in 2024. To earn the designation, a school must meet specific criteria:

  • A designated staff contact specifically for military-connected families — someone who knows the processes and can help a family navigate enrollment, records, and transitions
  • Established programs to help military-connected students adjust when they arrive or depart, including connections to counseling and peer support
  • Staff training on the unique challenges of military family life — frequent moves, deployment, and the stress those place on children

The designation is valid for three years, after which schools must re-apply to demonstrate continued compliance.

How D49 Got to All 7

D49's Board of Education unanimously passed a Purple Star resolution on December 4, 2025, setting the groundwork for schools across the district to pursue the designation. The April 2026 ceremony recognized 22 additional Colorado schools statewide; multiple D49 Falcon Zone campuses were among them, completing all seven in the zone.

Partial school names confirmed as Purple Star honorees include Banning Lewis Ranch Academy, Falcon Elementary School of Technology, Falcon High School, and Grand Peak Academy. The full list of all seven Falcon Zone schools is covered in KOAA's report.

The statewide total of Purple Star-designated schools in Colorado is now more than 27.

Why It Matters in Colorado Springs

Colorado Springs is one of the most concentrated military communities in the United States. Fort Carson, Peterson Space Force Base, Schriever Space Force Base, and NORAD/USNORTHCOM all draw active-duty families to the region. Many of those families move every two to three years — sometimes more. For a child, that means changing schools repeatedly, often mid-year, often without time to adjust.

What the Purple Star designation provides, in practical terms, is a first point of contact. A family arriving from out of state can go to the Purple Star coordinator at their new school and get help with records transfer, credit evaluation, extracurricular enrollment, and referrals for support services — without having to figure out from scratch who to call. For families who've experienced the alternative (no designated coordinator, inconsistent policies), the difference matters.

The D49 Falcon Zone is a logical fit for a complete Purple Star sweep given its proximity to these installations. The April 2026 completion of all seven schools means no Falcon Zone family should arrive at a D49 school without that support structure in place.

Checking Other COS Schools

Purple Star designation isn't limited to D49. The Colorado Department of Education maintains a statewide list of designated schools at ed.cde.state.co.us/cdeawards/purplestarschools. If your family is in D11 or D20, that list will show which schools in those districts currently hold the designation.

What's Next

The Purple Star designation must be renewed every three years, so the first D49 schools to receive it will be up for renewal in 2027–2028. The state's program continues to expand — additional schools across Colorado can apply in the next annual cycle. For military families new to Colorado Springs, the D49 Falcon Zone Purple Star sweep means you can start the conversation with your school's designated coordinator from day one.
