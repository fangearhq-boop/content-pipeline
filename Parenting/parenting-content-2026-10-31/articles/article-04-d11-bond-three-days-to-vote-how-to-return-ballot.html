D11 Bond Vote Closes in 3 Days — How to Return Your Ballot Before November 3

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting

October 31, 2026

The D11 $775 million bond vote closes November 3 at 7:00 PM — three days from today. If you have a mail-in ballot sitting at home, this weekend is your best window to return it before Election Day turns into a deadline crunch.

We've covered the bond's substance in prior stories: what it funds, why the district hasn't passed a bond in roughly 20 years, and how the tax impact is calculated. This piece is a straight how-to-return-your-ballot guide for families who've decided how they're voting and just need to get it done.

Your Three Options for Returning the Ballot

Drop Box (Recommended This Weekend)

El Paso County has drop boxes located across Colorado Springs that are accessible outside of regular business hours. Drop boxes are open for ballot returns now and through 7:00 PM on November 3. Find the nearest drop box location at d11.org/bond or at the El Paso County Clerk and Recorder's website.

Drop boxes don't require postage, don't rely on mail timing, and are available evenings and weekends. With three days left, dropping your ballot in a box this weekend eliminates any deadline uncertainty.

Mail-In Return

If you're mailing your ballot, it must be postmarked by November 3. Given today is October 31 and weekend mail pickup is inconsistent, using a drop box is the safer choice if you want certainty. If you do mail it, use a regular post office drop or mail collection point with a confirmed pickup time — don't leave it in a residential mailbox and assume it'll be picked up in time.

In-Person Voting on Election Day

El Paso County vote centers are open on November 3 during standard Election Day hours. If you prefer to vote in person — or if you've misplaced your mail-in ballot — you can request and cast a ballot at any El Paso County vote center. Bring a valid ID. Find vote center locations at the El Paso County Clerk and Recorder's website.

What's on the Ballot

The D11 bond measure asks voters to approve $775 million in bonding authority for Colorado Springs School District 11. The funding is designated for three main areas:

  • HVAC system replacements in aging school buildings — many were built decades ago and haven't been updated
  • Classroom modernization — flexible learning spaces and updated infrastructure
  • Library renovations — updates to school libraries across the district

D11 is the only major Colorado Springs district that currently carries no bond debt. The district hasn't passed a bond in approximately 20 years. The estimated tax impact is around $6.50 per month for a home with $100,000 of assessed value — use the tax calculator at d11.org/bond to find the specific impact for your property.
