Halloween Safety Guide for Colorado Springs Families: Trick-or-Treat Checklist for Tonight

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting

October 31, 2026

Halloween is here. If your family is heading out to trick-or-treat tonight in Colorado Springs, there's not much left to plan — but a quick run through the safety basics can make the night easier for everyone, including the drivers who share those neighborhood streets.

Tonight's sunset in Colorado Springs is around 6:00 PM MT. That means there's a window of actual daylight in the late afternoon, and it's worth using it. Younger kids especially benefit from heading out early, while streets are easy to see and drivers have more warning.

Visibility: The Most Useful Prep You Can Do

Dark costumes on dark streets make kids harder to see. A strip of reflective tape on a costume hem or bag — or a glow stick clipped to a jacket — costs almost nothing and significantly improves how visible children are to drivers once the sun is down.

Costumes should fit well enough that kids can walk normally — trailing fabric and oversized shoes lead to trips, especially on porch stairs and uneven sidewalks. Avoid full-face masks for walking: they limit peripheral vision and make it harder for kids to see cars or curbs. Face paint is a safer alternative for most ages.

Flame-resistant is worth checking if you bought a costume this year. Many costumes carry a flame-resistant label; if yours doesn't, keep it away from porch candles or decorative fires.

Supervision by Age

Younger children — generally under 8 or 10, depending on the child — should be accompanied by an adult or older teenager. Older kids going out in groups should have a clear check-in plan: a meeting point, a time to be back, and a known route.

Make sure kids know their full address in case they get separated. Younger kids should carry a card with a parent's phone number. If they have their own phone, confirm it's charged and they know how to call home.

Stick to familiar, well-lit neighborhoods whenever possible. The excitement of new blocks can lead kids to lose track of where they are.

Candy Inspection When You Get Home

Candy tampering is genuinely rare, and there's no need to alarm kids about it. But spending two minutes checking the haul before anyone starts eating is a reasonable habit. Toss anything that's unwrapped, has an unusual smell, or lacks a factory seal.

For families with young children: choking hazards are a real concern with small, hard, or sticky candy. Gumballs, hard candies, and chewy toffee-style candy should stay out of reach of children under 3 or 4. Children under 2 shouldn't receive candy.

Pets Inside

Costumes and unusual crowds can genuinely stress out dogs and cats. A dog that's normally calm with visitors can react unpredictably to strangers in masks. Keep pets in a back room or crate during peak trick-or-treat hours — it's less about safety for kids (most dogs are fine) and more about reducing stress for the animal.

Keep candy bags and buckets well out of reach of pets. Chocolate is toxic to dogs and cats, and xylitol — found in some sugar-free candies — is particularly dangerous. If a pet gets into candy, contact your veterinarian.

For Drivers Tonight

If you're driving through Colorado Springs neighborhoods tonight, slow down well below the speed limit starting around 5:00 PM MT. Children cross between parked cars, dart across streets mid-block, and aren't always looking. Keep headlights on early. Be especially careful at intersections, where crowds of kids can suddenly cross on a green light or from multiple directions.

Quick Reference: Halloween Safety Checklist

Category What to Do
Visibility Add reflective tape or glow stick to costume; go out before sunset (~6 PM MT) if possible
Costume fit No trailing fabric; swap full-face masks for face paint; check flame-resistance label
Supervision Adult with young kids; older kids have check-in plan, known route, and charged phone
Candy Inspect before eating; nothing unwrapped or unsealed; hard/sticky candy away from under-4s
Pets Keep inside during trick-or-treat hours; candy out of reach (chocolate/xylitol toxic)
Drivers Slow down after 5 PM; lights on early; watch intersections and mid-block crossings
