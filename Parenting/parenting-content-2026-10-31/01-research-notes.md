## Story 3: Toddler Screen Time + Melatonin Study

**Sources:**
- Contemporary Pediatrics article dated October 27, 2026: "Brief evening screen time did not suppress melatonin in toddlers"
  URL: https://www.contemporarypediatrics.com/view/brief-evening-screen-time-did-not-suppress-melatonin-in-toddlers
- Pulse.bot secondary coverage of AAP screen time shift (April 2026)

**Key Facts (with confidence):**
- Study published: October 27, 2026, Contemporary Pediatrics — **HIGH** (confirmed URL)
- Institution: Ruhr University Bochum — **HIGH** (from search summary)
- Study design: home-based crossover study — **HIGH**
- Age range studied: toddlers 15–24 months — **HIGH**
- Intervention: 15 minutes of tablet video in the hour before bed — **HIGH**
- Finding: did NOT suppress evening melatonin rise, did NOT measurably alter sleep — **HIGH**
- Limitation stated by authors: did not test habitual, longer, or unsupervised screen use — **HIGH**
- Context: AAP moved away from strict time-based limits toward content-and-context guidance in early 2026 — **MEDIUM** (Pulse.bot coverage, not direct AAP source)
- Sample size of study: not confirmed — **LOW** (single small crossover study)
- Note: Contrasting older evidence exists (2019 study found everyday tablet use raised odds of shorter sleep in toddlers; 2022 Boulder study found melatonin 70-99% lower after bright light exposure)
