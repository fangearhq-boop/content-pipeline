### STORY 1: Halloween Night Safety

**Long-Form Post (8:00 AM MT)**

It's Halloween — the night all the planning finally pays off. Before the kids hit the sidewalks tonight, here's the quick safety checklist that's actually worth printing.

**Visibility first.** Costumes are fun but drivers often can't see dark-colored fabric in the evening. A strip of reflective tape on a costume or bag, or a glow stick clipped to a jacket, makes a huge difference once the sun goes down. COS sunset tonight is around 6:00 PM MT — try to get outside while there's still some light.

**Supervision by age.** Younger kids should be with an adult, full stop. Older kids going out in a group should have a check-in plan and a known route — "meet back at the corner of X and X by Y time."

**Candy inspection when you get home.** Tampering is genuinely rare, but it takes two minutes to look through the haul before the kids start eating. Toss anything unwrapped or without a factory seal.

**Keep pets inside.** Dogs especially can startle badly at strangers in costumes — and chocolate is toxic to pets, so keep the candy bags out of reach.

**One thing for drivers tonight:** slow down in neighborhoods, even when streets look empty. Kids in costumes can be nearly invisible, and they're not always looking before they step off the curb.

### STORY 2: Costway High Chair Recall

**Long-Form Post (10:00 AM MT)**

If you have a Costway convertible high chair — the kind that converts into a small table and chair set — this is a stop-use recall you'll want to share.

On October 22, the CPSC issued recall #27-040 for approximately 3,332 Costway convertible high chairs. The recall cites entrapment and fall hazards that violate the federal mandatory safety standard for high chairs. The chairs were manufactured in China.

The remedy is a replacement — contact Costway or visit cpsc.gov to get the full details on how to claim it. Until you've resolved the recall, stop using the chair.

This is the sixth significant child product safety action that's come across our feed in October 2026 alone — after the Harkla sensory swing recall (Oct 29), Flyboss infant bouncer warning (Oct 29), Lucakuins product (Oct 27), Papablic/bblüv (Oct 15), and ONTHEWEI (Oct 9). It's been a busy month for CPSC.

If you know someone with a Costway high chair, tag them in the comments or send this their way.

### STORY 3: Toddler Screen Time Study

**Long-Form Post (3:00 PM MT)**

A new study published this week is getting some traction in parenting circles, and it's worth reading carefully — because it's more nuanced than the headlines make it sound.

Researchers at Ruhr University Bochum published findings in Contemporary Pediatrics (October 27, 2026) from a home-based crossover study of toddlers aged 15–24 months. The finding: 15 minutes of tablet video in the hour before bed did not suppress melatonin or measurably alter sleep quality in those toddlers.

That might feel like a relief if your bedtime routine sometimes involves a show. The study's own authors were quick to say it doesn't apply to habitual, longer, or unsupervised screen use. One crossover study of short video is not the same as unlimited evening screen time.

What it does reinforce is a shift that the AAP has been making all year — away from rigid "no screens before bed" rules and toward guidance based on content, context, and duration. A 15-minute wind-down video is a different thing than falling asleep with a tablet running.

As with most parenting research, the honest answer is: it depends. But it's useful to know that short, supervised tablet use in the hour before bed doesn't appear to be the sleep crisis some older guidance suggested.

### STORY 4: D11 Bond — 3 Days Left

**Long-Form Post (12:00 PM MT)**

Three days. That's how long COS families have to weigh in on the D11 $775 million bond measure before ballots close November 3 at 7:00 PM.

If you've got a ballot at home and haven't sent it in yet, here's what you need to know:

**Drop boxes** are open across Colorado Springs — find the nearest one at d11.org/bond. Drop boxes are available outside regular hours, so you don't need to go during business hours.

**Mail-in:** Your ballot needs to be postmarked by November 3. Given how close we are to the deadline, a drop box is safer than counting on mail timing.

**In-person voting** is available at El Paso County vote centers on Election Day (November 3).

What's the bond for? HVAC system replacements across aging school buildings, classroom modernization, and library renovations. D11 hasn't passed a bond in roughly 20 years — it's the only major COS district with no current bond debt. The projected cost is about $6.50 per month per $100,000 of assessed home value (confirm your exact impact at the tax calculator at d11.org/bond).
