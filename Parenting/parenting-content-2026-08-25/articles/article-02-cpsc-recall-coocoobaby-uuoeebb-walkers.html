CPSC Recall Alert: Check Your Baby Lounger and Infant Walker Now

By Sarah Morales | COS Parenting | August 25, 2026

Two CPSC recalls are in effect that Colorado Springs families with infants and toddlers should check immediately. The affected products — a baby lounger line and a line of infant walkers — both violate mandatory federal safety standards and carry serious injury risks. Full refunds are available for both.

Quick Reference

CooCooBaby Baby Loungers

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission recalled CooCooBaby Classic and Deluxe Baby Loungers on June 18, 2026. Approximately 2,355 units were sold from December 2024 through March 2026 at prices ranging from $35 to $70.

Three separate hazards were identified. First, the sides of the lounger are shorter than the minimum height required by federal infant sleep standards, meaning an infant could fall out. Second, the sleeping pad is thicker than the maximum allowed limit — a direct suffocation risk for infants who shift position during sleep or rest. Third, an opening at the foot of the lounger creates a fall hazard if the infant moves toward it.

If you have a CooCooBaby lounger, stop using it immediately. To obtain a full refund, destroy the product by cutting up both the sides of the lounger and the sleeping pad, then email a photo of the destroyed product to support@coocoobabyofficial.com. No injuries had been reported at the time of the recall.

Uuoeebb Infant Walkers

The CPSC also recalled Uuoeebb Infant Walkers sold on Amazon by BaoD. Approximately 2,650 units were sold from December 2024 through September 2025 for $60 to $90. To check if your walker is affected, look under the seat for a label reading "Production Batch: 7654."

The walkers have two primary hazards. They are narrow enough to pass through a standard doorway and do not stop at the edge of a step — a stair-fall risk that can result in serious injury. The leg openings on the walker are also large enough to allow an infant to slip down until the child's head becomes entrapped, which can cause suffocation.

Stop use immediately. Contact BaoD through your Amazon order history to initiate a full refund. The CPSC had not received injury reports at the time of the recall notice, but the hazard profile is serious enough that continued use is not advised for any reason.

How to Stay Current on Recalls

The CPSC publishes new recalls regularly at cpsc.gov. Parents can sign up for email alerts at the CPSC website to receive notifications when new children's products are recalled. The CPSC hotline (800-638-2772) is also available for questions. El Paso County families with concerns about a specific product can contact the CPSC directly or reach out to their pediatrician for guidance on safe alternatives.

What's Next

Check cpsc.gov regularly — the agency publishes new recalls on an ongoing basis. If you have questions about what safe sleep products look like for infants, the American Academy of Pediatrics provides guidance at healthychildren.org. The AAP recommends infants sleep on a firm, flat surface — no inclined products, no padded inserts, and no sleep positioners of any kind.
