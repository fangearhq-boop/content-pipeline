### STORY 1: D49 Student Success Center

**Long-Form Post:**

District 49 families have a new resource this school year.

The D49 Student Success Center is opening at the start of the 2026–2027 school year at the former Falcon Elementary School of Technology building. It is not a neighborhood school — it is a district-wide specialized services hub designed to bring targeted support to D49 students who need it most.

What is inside: Two full-time preschool classrooms, available Monday through Thursday to families throughout the district (not just families in the Falcon area). The Elevates 18–21 Transition Services program is relocating there, giving young adults a more connected and specialized environment. PEAK students will also benefit from a more structured, school-like experience as they work toward returning to their neighborhood schools.

Also new this year: the FESoT Hawks are co-locating at Bennett Ranch Elementary School.

D49 started August 3–4 for most students. Families looking for more information about the Student Success Center can contact D49 directly at d49.org.

### STORY 2: CPSC Recalls — Baby Loungers and Infant Walkers

**Long-Form Post:**

Two product recalls that Colorado Springs parents need to check right now.

CooCooBaby Baby Loungers — the Classic and Deluxe models — were recalled on June 18, 2026 by the CPSC. Approximately 2,355 units were sold between December 2024 and March 2026 for $35 to $70. The problem: the sides of the lounger are shorter than the minimum required height, the sleeping pad is thicker than allowed (suffocation risk), and infants can fall out through an opening at the foot. If you have one, stop use immediately. Destroy it by cutting up the sides and the sleeping pad and email a photo to support@coocoobabyofficial.com for a full refund.

Uuoeebb Infant Walkers — sold on Amazon by BaoD — were recalled by the CPSC. About 2,650 units were sold from December 2024 through September 2025 for $60 to $90. The walkers can pass through standard doorways, fail to stop at the edge of stairs, and the leg openings allow a child to slip down until their head becomes entrapped. Check under the seat for the label "Production Batch: 7654" to confirm if yours is affected. Stop use immediately and contact BaoD on Amazon for a full refund.

For the complete list of all CPSC recalls, visit cpsc.gov or call 800-638-2772.

Did you or someone you know have either of these products? Share this post so other families can check.

### STORY 3: AAP 2026 Screen Time Guidelines

**Long-Form Post:**

The two-hour screen time rule is gone. Here is what replaced it.

In February 2026, the American Academy of Pediatrics officially retired its long-standing recommendation that children ages 2 and up limit screen time to two hours or less per day. The organization concluded that counting hours misses too much of what actually matters.

The new framework is built around the 5 C's: Child, Content, Calm, Crowding Out, and Communication. Instead of watching the clock, the AAP wants families to evaluate the quality of what their child watches, the context in which they watch it (alone vs. co-viewing), and whether screens are displacing sleep, physical activity, or face-to-face time.

A few rules remain clear: no screens before 18 months (video calls being a limited exception), and no more than one hour of high-quality programming per day for children ages 2–5. For older children, the guidance is to build family-agreed limits with a consistent Family Media Plan.

One finding from a March 2026 study stands out: for children with developmental vulnerabilities, even 10 to 30 minutes of daily solo screen time showed measurable negative effects. Co-viewing with a caregiver changed the outcome significantly. That is worth taking seriously regardless of your child's age.

The full AAP Family Media Plan tool is available at healthychildren.org.

### STORY 4: D11 Monday Late Starts

**Long-Form Post:**

D11 families — this one is worth saving.

Colorado Springs School District 11 builds Monday late-start days into its 2026–2027 calendar for every school in the district. Week 3 begins today, and if your carpool or pickup timing has been set based on a standard start time, your Monday mornings may not be matching reality.

Late starts are built in to give teachers dedicated professional development time before students arrive. The exact late-start time varies by school level, so check with your specific school — elementary, middle, and high school start times can differ on these days.

D11 enrollment is still open if you have a child who needs to register. You can enroll online at d11.org or in person at 1033 N. Franklin St., Monday through Friday, 8 AM to 4 PM MT.

D11 also includes Late Starts in the full district calendar at d11.org/calendars/district-calendar — worth bookmarking before the fall schedule gets complicated.

### STORY 5: COS End-of-August Family Weekend Guide

**Long-Form Post:**

Late August in Colorado Springs is worth showing up for.

Before the school-year routine fully takes hold, this weekend offers a solid list of options for families with children of any age:

**US Olympic Museum** — "Discover Legends" guided experience, 90 minutes, daily starting at noon through August. Exclusive memorabilia and artifacts from Olympic and Paralympic champions. Located in downtown Colorado Springs.

**Garden of the Gods** — Free. Always. The red rock formations are one of Colorado Springs' most accessible outdoor spaces, with trails ranging from paved and flat to more challenging routes. Wildlife sightings are common in the early morning.

**Cheyenne Mountain State Park** — Hiking, mountain biking, wildlife viewing. Entry fee applies (America the Beautiful Annual Pass accepted). 410 JL Ranch Heights Rd.

**CMZoo** — Little Paws Nature Preschool just opened on August 24. The zoo's broader programming continues through the season. 4250 Cheyenne Mountain Zoo Rd.

**Pikes Peak Library District (free):**
- Storytime: Friday 10:30 AM at Cheyenne Mountain Library (1785 S. 8th St) and Old Colorado City Library (2418 W. Pikes Peak Ave)
- Sensory Storytime: Saturday 10:30 AM at Library 21c (1175 Chapel Hills Dr)
- Paws to Read: Saturday 10:30 AM at Old Colorado City Library

All library programs are free. Full schedule at ppld.org.
