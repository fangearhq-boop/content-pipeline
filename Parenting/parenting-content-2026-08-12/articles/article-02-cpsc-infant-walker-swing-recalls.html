Stop Use Now: CPSC Recalls Multiple Infant Walkers and Warns Against Baby Swing in August 2026

By Sarah Morales August 12, 2026

What You Need to Check Right Now

The Consumer Product Safety Commission has issued recalls for three infant walker brands and a stop-use warning for an infant swing model this month. If you have a baby walker or infant swing purchased on Amazon or Walmart.com in the past couple of years, check your order history against the product names below. The hazards involved — falls, head entrapment, and suffocation — are serious, and the CPSC's guidance is to stop using these products immediately.

Infant Walkers: Three Brands Recalled

All three walker recalls share the same root safety failure: the products violate the federal mandatory standard for infant walkers. Specifically, these walkers can fit through a standard doorway without stopping, and they fail to stop at the edge of a step. That combination creates a direct fall hazard for any infant using them near stairs or elevated surfaces.

Wnttmt Baby Walkers (sold as WURUI.BABY, Amazon): Approximately 1,310 units were sold. The walkers pose fall and entrapment hazards. The leg openings can allow a child to slide down until their head becomes trapped. Stop use immediately and contact the seller through your Amazon order for a refund.

Uuoeebb Infant Walkers (sold by BaoD, Amazon): These walkers share the same hazard pattern — they can pass through doorways and fail at step edges, plus the entrapment risk from leg opening design. Stop use immediately.

WonderStone Infant Walkers (sold by Wonder Stone Toys, Walmart.com): Fall hazard due to the same doorway and step-edge failures. Stop use immediately.

For all three recalls, the CPSC recommends stopping use immediately. Do not give these walkers away or resell them. Visit cpsc.gov for the specific recall pages, which include instructions for obtaining a refund or remedy from each seller.

Bimuva Infant Swings: Stop Use Warning

The CPSC has also issued an immediate stop-use warning for Bimuva Infant Swings sold on Amazon between October 2024 and August 2025. About 2,970 units were sold at approximately $100 each.

The hazard is suffocation. The Bimuva swing's incline angle exceeds the 10-degree maximum allowed under the mandatory safety standard for infant sleep products. It also violates the Safe Sleep for Babies Act. An infant placed in an inclined sleep product can have their airway compressed in ways that make it difficult or impossible for them to breathe safely.

The CPSC's guidance is clear: stop use immediately. Do not resell the swing. Do not give it away. The agency recommends disposing of it so it cannot be used by anyone else.

What's Next

If you have any of these products, stop using them today. Check your Amazon or Walmart order history if you're not sure whether you purchased one of these specific brands. The full recall details, including photographs to help identify the products and instructions for refunds, are available at cpsc.gov.

For parents looking for alternatives to infant walkers, the CPSC has long noted that stationary activity centers — sometimes called exersaucers — do not carry the same fall and step-edge risks because they don't roll or move across the floor. Consult your pediatrician for guidance on developmentally appropriate products for your infant's age and stage.

To stay current on CPSC recalls affecting families, bookmark cpsc.gov/Recalls and check it periodically, or sign up for email alerts directly from the CPSC.
