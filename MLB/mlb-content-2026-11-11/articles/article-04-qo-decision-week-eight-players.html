QO Decision Week: Eight Players Have Until November 17 to Decide on $23.15M Offers

By Jake Torres | November 11, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Eight MLB players have until 4 p.m. ET on November 17 to accept or reject qualifying offers worth $23.15 million. The list includes Seiya Suzuki, Randy Arozarena, David Bednar, Jake Bauers, Trevor Rogers, Ryan Jeffers, Brandon Lowe, and Adrian Morejon. All eight are expected to decline.

The qualifying offer is a one-year contract for the league-average salary threshold set each offseason — $23.15 million for the 2026-27 cycle. Acceptance locks a player in for one year at that number. Rejection opens the free agent market, with one significant cost attached: the team that eventually signs a QO-declined player must forfeit a draft pick.

Historical Context

The numbers put the expected mass rejection in context. Of 157 players offered qualifying offers since the system was implemented, only 18 have accepted — roughly 11 percent. The players who accept are typically those whose market value aligns closely with the QO number, or those who face limited free agent demand. Players with legitimate star-level markets almost universally decline and test the open market.

None of the eight players on this year's list fit the profile of a likely acceptor. Each has a credible case for a multi-year deal worth more money on the open market than a single year at $23.15 million.

The Eight Players

Seiya Suzuki of the Cubs is the highest-profile name on the list. The 30-year-old outfielder has been one of Chicago's core players since arriving from Japan in 2022. His market will be active, and a multi-year deal is the expected outcome.

Randy Arozarena of the Mariners arrives after two full seasons in Seattle. The outfielder remains one of the most exciting players in the American League. Brandon Lowe with the Pirates and Ryan Jeffers with the Twins both profile as players with multi-year markets at a position of need for multiple clubs.

David Bednar (Yankees), Trevor Rogers (Orioles), Jake Bauers (Brewers), and Adrian Morejon (Padres) round out the list. Morejon is notable because the Padres are actively working to retain him — he was tendered the QO and is one of the names GM A.J. Preller is meeting agents about at the Las Vegas meetings.

The Draft Pick Complication

The team that signs any of these eight players — once they decline their QO — must forfeit a draft pick. That adds a cost that does not appear in the contract value. For some teams, particularly those with top selections, this draft pick tax can slow negotiations or redirect interest toward players who were not given qualifying offers.

It is a system that benefits incumbent teams — who receive the pick — and penalizes acquiring teams. For marquee free agents like Suzuki and Arozarena, the draft pick cost is typically absorbed because the talent level justifies it. For mid-tier players on the list, the QO attachment can meaningfully limit their market.

What's Next

The November 17 deadline is six days away. When all eight decline — as expected — the free agent market begins to move in earnest. The names that have been holding in place pending QO decisions will be in play for multi-year deals almost immediately.

Suzuki and Arozarena are the ones to watch most closely. Both command significant markets, and the teams pursuing them will accelerate contact once the QO formality is resolved. The GM Meetings this week in Las Vegas are setting those conversations in motion.
