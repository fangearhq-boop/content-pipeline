Teruaki Sato Market: Padres Emerge as Five-Team Race Takes Shape

By Marcus Cole | November 11, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The Padres have entered the Teruaki Sato race. Per The Athletic on November 10, San Diego is now among the teams pursuing the Hanshin Tigers outfielder who won the NPB Triple Crown in 2026. They join four previously reported contenders — the Mets, Yankees, Dodgers, and Phillies — in what is becoming one of the most competitive international signing markets in recent offseason history.

Sato was posted November 1 by Hanshin, opening a 45-day negotiating window that expires December 16. Contract projections vary: Jon Heyman has pegged him at $80 million over four years, while ESPN's model projects closer to $115 million over five years. The truth likely lands somewhere in that range, depending on how competitive the bidding becomes.

What Sato Brings

The Triple Crown in NPB's 2026 season — leading the Pacific League in batting average, home runs, and RBI — is the clearest summary of Sato's value. He is a right-handed power hitter with a track record of contact and production in one of the premier professional baseball leagues outside of MLB.

The transition from NPB to MLB carries inherent uncertainty. The best Japanese position players — Ichiro, Hideki Matsui, Shohei Ohtani — made the jump successfully. Others have required significant adjustment periods. For teams betting on Sato, the Triple Crown is the evidence that the talent is genuine. The projection is that the production translates, at least in part.

Five Teams, Five Different Cases

Each of the five reported contenders has a distinct reason to pursue Sato.

The Mets need a first baseman — Stearns named first base as a priority at GM Meetings. Sato profiles as a corner outfielder or first baseman, which fits. The Yankees have a designated hitter opening and power is always welcome in New York. The Dodgers add him as lineup depth to an already-stacked roster — depth that becomes essential in October. The Phillies need right-handed power to balance a left-leaning lineup. The Padres have made aggressive international signings part of their identity and have the outfield spot to plug him in.

The December 1 Deadline

Here is the critical variable in the Sato market: the CBA expires December 1. If a new agreement is not in place before midnight on November 30, negotiations between Sato and MLB teams freeze — and Sato is required to return to Japan under the posting agreement terms.

That means the five teams pursuing him have roughly 20 days to get a deal across the finish line. For an international signing of this magnitude, that timeline is tight. Teams will need to accelerate their evaluation and decision-making process considerably to close before the deadline.

Alternatively, if a lockout occurs and Sato returns to Japan, he would likely be re-posted when a new CBA is in place — but there is no guarantee the same five teams remain in play, and the dynamics of the market would reset entirely.

What's Next

Sato's representation is at the GM Meetings in Las Vegas this week, meeting with teams in person. The next two weeks will determine whether a deal closes before December 1 or whether this market extends into an uncertain labor landscape.

Watch the Mets and Dodgers most closely — both have the financial capacity to close at the high end of the projection range, and both have specific roster needs that Sato addresses. The Padres' emergence is the new wrinkle that could push the market higher than it would have gone otherwise.
