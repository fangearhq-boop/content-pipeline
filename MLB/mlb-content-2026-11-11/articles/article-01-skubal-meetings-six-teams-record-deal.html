Skubal Meetings Begin: Six Teams, West Coast Preference, Record Deal Looming

By Jake Torres | November 11, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Tarik Skubal's free agency is officially in motion. The AP reported Tuesday that the left-hander is set to meet with six teams, including the Mets, Dodgers, and Padres. Three additional teams were not named. The meetings — happening now, against the backdrop of the GM Meetings in Las Vegas — represent the first formal step in what may become the most expensive pitching contract in baseball history.

The projection numbers are staggering. ESPN's Kiley McDaniel projects an 8-year, $352 million deal for Skubal — an average annual value of $44 million per year. For context, Yoshinobu Yamamoto's 12-year, $325 million deal with the Dodgers set the previous record for a pitcher at $27.08 million AAV. Skubal's projected deal would exceed that mark by nearly $17 million.

The West Coast Preference

MLB insider Jim Bowden said Sunday that Skubal "prefers the West Coast," pointing to the Dodgers, Padres, Angels, and Diamondbacks as natural geographic fits. That said, Bowden added a caveat that shapes the entire market: the Mets could overbid everyone.

New York's presence in this market is significant. The Mets have the financial firepower and the stated need — president David Stearns said at the GM Meetings that pitching is a priority. If the Mets decide Skubal is their answer, the West Coast preference becomes a preference, not a destination.

Cubs and Yankees in the Mix

ESPN's Jeff Passan reported that the Cubs are "badly" pursuing Skubal. That tracks with Chicago's clear need for a front-of-rotation arm and their willingness to spend this offseason. The Yankees, meanwhile, are "thinking about" a push — not a commitment, but not a dismissal either.

The unnamed three teams add a layer of intrigue. At least one is almost certainly a club that has not been mentioned in prior reports, which suggests Skubal's market is broader than the public conversation has acknowledged.

The CBA Deadline Changes Everything

Every team pursuing Skubal is operating under the same constraint: the Collective Bargaining Agreement expires December 1. If a new deal is not in place before that date, a work stoppage becomes possible — and any transactions not completed before midnight on November 30 could be frozen until negotiations resolve.

That creates a compressed timeline unlike any typical offseason. Teams that want Skubal have roughly three weeks to get a deal done. For a contract of this magnitude, three weeks is not a lot of runway.

Skubal is 29 years old. He posted a 2.74 ERA over 185 innings in 2026, winning his second Cy Young Award in three seasons. The argument for paying him record money is straightforward — he is one of the five best pitchers in baseball, entering his prime.

What's Next

Meetings are underway. The CBA deadline is December 1. If Skubal follows the trajectory of other marquee free agents who complete deals quickly once their market is fully engaged, a resolution could come before Thanksgiving. If teams blink at the price, or if the CBA situation creates hesitation, this market could stretch into December and beyond — assuming negotiations allow it.

Watch for reporting on which team emerges as a front-runner after the initial meeting round concludes. The West Coast preference is real. Whether it holds when the Mets make their offer is the question that defines this free agency.
