GM Meetings Notebook: Mets on Pitching, Padres Re-Sign Push, CBA Shadow Over Las Vegas

By Ryan Calloway | November 11, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The 2026 GM Meetings at the Aria Hotel in Las Vegas wrap up tomorrow. For two days, more than 180 free agents and their representatives have been in the same building as the executives who might sign them. The conversations happening in suites and hallways this week will shape the first month of the offseason — if there is a first month before the CBA situation forces everything to pause.

Here is a Day 2 look at the most significant team-level developments.

Mets: Pitching and First Base Are the Priorities

Mets president David Stearns named pitching and first base as the organization's top priorities for the offseason. That is not a surprise — New York's rotation needs depth behind its top arms, and first base has been a question mark for two seasons.

The pitching priority connects directly to the Skubal market. If the Mets believe they can land Skubal at the top of the rotation, the remaining pieces of their pitching plan fall into place differently than if they go a different direction. Stearns has not publicly committed to Skubal, but the Mets are in the meeting rotation — and their financial capacity to outbid West Coast teams is real.

Padres: Keeping the Core Together

Padres GM A.J. Preller is working the room on two specific re-sign priorities: Michael King and Adrian Morejon. King was one of the best pitchers in baseball in 2026 — a legitimate No. 1 starter who proved himself over a full season. Morejon is a left-handed reliever whose value to a contending bullpen is significant.

Both were tendered qualifying offers, which means their free agency carries a draft pick cost for any team other than San Diego. Preller's goal is to get deals done before the market fully opens, reducing the risk that another team absorbs the draft pick cost and outbids the Padres for their own players.

Dodgers: The Skubal Decision

The Dodgers enter these meetings with a genuine fork in the road. Committing to Skubal at $44 million per year — if McDaniel's projection holds — changes how they approach the rest of the offseason. That money does not go toward position player upgrades. The Dodgers have needs in the lineup, and an eight-year, $352 million commitment to one pitcher is a decision that shapes roster construction for a decade.

The alternative is letting another team win Skubal and spending aggressively on position players. Los Angeles has made both kinds of moves in recent years, so the choice is genuinely open. The GM Meetings are likely where the internal conversations about this tradeoff are happening most seriously.

The Lockout Shadow

Analysts covering the CBA negotiations estimate roughly 5 percent odds of a deal being reached before December 1. That means roughly 95 percent of market participants believe some form of work stoppage is coming. That shapes every conversation in Las Vegas this week.

Teams are identifying which transactions they can realistically close before the deadline and which ones they should prepare to execute the moment a new agreement is in place. Free agents and their representatives are making similar calculations — sign now at some discount to avoid uncertainty, or wait for the market to fully open after a deal is struck.

The Sato situation is the clearest example. If he is not signed by November 30, he goes home. That is a hard deadline that forces the hand of every team pursuing him, regardless of what the broader market is doing.

What's Next

The meetings end tomorrow. After that, the market either moves fast — teams accelerate to beat December 1 — or signals that they are prepared to wait. The names to watch in the next two weeks: Skubal, Sato, and the QO players who will formally decline their offers by November 17. Those decisions together will tell us what kind of offseason this is going to be.
