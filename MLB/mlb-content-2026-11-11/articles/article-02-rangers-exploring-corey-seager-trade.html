Rangers Exploring Corey Seager Trade: $157.5M Deal, Full No-Trade Clause Complicates

By Ryan Calloway | November 11, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The Texas Rangers are planning to explore Corey Seager trades at the GM Meetings in Las Vegas, per Ken Rosenthal and Evan Grant. The move is driven by payroll math: Rangers GM Chris Young wants the 2027 payroll to look "very similar" to 2026's $193 million, and Seager's remaining contract — $157.5 million over five years — is the most logical place to find relief.

Seager, 32, hit .227 with a .736 OPS in 2026 — career lows across the board. After a stellar first two seasons in Texas that included a World Series ring in 2023, the 2026 campaign raised legitimate questions about where he is in his career trajectory. That decline, combined with the Rangers' payroll situation, has made what once seemed unthinkable now a live conversation.

The No-Trade Clause Problem

Any Seager trade requires one thing above all others: Seager's consent. He has full no-trade protection in his contract, meaning he can veto any deal to any destination. If he does not want to be traded, he will not be traded. Texas can explore the market and generate interest, but the final decision sits entirely with the shortstop.

That changes the nature of the Rangers' effort. This is less a traditional trade exploration and more a conversation — with Seager himself and with potential suitors — about whether a landing spot exists that works for everyone involved. Texas cannot simply package him and move him. They have to find a destination Seager finds acceptable, then negotiate with that team.

Teams Named as Fits

Multiple teams have been connected to Seager in early reporting: the Yankees, Blue Jays, Red Sox, Dodgers, and Braves. Each has a different version of the case.

The Yankees have the payroll flexibility and a shortstop question. However, the New York Post's Joel Sherman pushed back on the fit — a notable skeptical voice given Sherman's track record on Yankees transactions. The Blue Jays have been aggressive this offseason and need a lineup anchor. The Red Sox are rebuilding their roster around younger players but have the financial capacity. The Dodgers adding Seager would be a reunion — he won a World Series there in 2020. The Braves have both the payroll room and the win-now mandate.

The Price Tag

Acquiring Seager means taking on $157.5 million over five seasons — roughly $31.5 million per year. For a shortstop who hit .227 last season, that is a significant bet on a turnaround. The acquiring team would need to believe the 2026 performance was an aberration, not a trend.

The other side of the bet: Seager at his best is a franchise shortstop. His prime seasons in Texas were exceptional. If the 2026 dip was health-related or situational, the right organization might unlock a bounce-back. That is exactly the case a team like the Dodgers — who know him well — might make to themselves.

What's Next

The Rangers are in information-gathering mode. Las Vegas this week is about taking the temperature of the market — which teams will engage, at what price, and on what timeline. The harder conversation — with Seager about his preferences — will follow.

The CBA expiration on December 1 adds urgency to every transaction. A deal of this complexity, requiring Seager's approval and a multi-team negotiation, is unlikely to close in three weeks. If this moves forward, it probably does so after the CBA situation resolves — one way or another.
