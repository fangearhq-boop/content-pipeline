### STORY 2: Schwarber HR Race Leader at 37

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 12:30 PM ET

The four-way tie at 35 home runs is over.

Kyle Schwarber's two-HR night at the Field of Dreams put him alone in the lead with 37. Yordan Alvarez, Matt Olson, and Junior Caminero are each still at 35 — two home runs back, entering a weekend in which Schwarber doesn't appear to be slowing down.

The context makes this race fascinating:

Alvarez hasn't hit a home run since July 28. That's over two weeks. For the most feared hitter in the American League over the past several years, that's an unusual dry stretch. He has the underlying quality to heat up fast, but the calendar is moving.

Caminero has been the hottest bat in the group — 11 home runs and 22 RBI in his last 13 games. At 23 years old, he's on pace for a historically young back-to-back 35-HR season. But his team is playing out the string.

Olson has 8 HRs in his last 14 games, which is a torrid pace. And Olson, playing his 903rd consecutive game, has experience with the slow burn of a long season.

Schwarber hit 56 home runs in 2025. He's not on a 56-HR pace in 2026 — but at 37 HRs with roughly 45 games left, he's on pace for somewhere in the mid-40s. Whether the others can close the gap will define the final month of the race. ⚾

### STORY 3: Misiorowski Historic Cy Young Case

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 12:30 PM ET

Stop what you're doing and look at Jacob Misiorowski's 2026 numbers.

ERA: 1.76.
Strikeouts: 204.
WHIP: 0.73.
Innings: 133 across 22 starts.

He leads all of baseball in ERA, strikeouts, and WHIP simultaneously. He is 24 years old and he pitches for the Milwaukee Brewers.

Misiorowski became the second-fastest pitcher in MLB history to reach 200 strikeouts in a season. The only pitcher to do it faster? Spencer Strider in 2022. That's the company. He's struck out 22 batters in his last two starts alone. His NL Cy Young odds sit at -550 — the most lopsided of any award race this side of Yordan Alvarez in the AL MVP.

Here's what makes this story unusual: the Brewers are not in a meaningful playoff race. That's part of why Misiorowski hasn't dominated the national conversation the way his numbers demand. But the performance is real. The ERA is real. The strikeout pace is real.

If Misiorowski finishes this season the way he's going, it will be one of the best pitching seasons in Major League Baseball in years.

You should be watching. ⚾

### STORY 5: Cubs 7-0 / Cavalli Near No-Hitter

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 10:30 AM ET

The Cubs lost 7-0. But the story from last night wasn't the Cubs.

Cade Cavalli of the Washington Nationals took a no-hitter into the 7th inning against Chicago. He went 8 innings, struck out 10, and threw 103 pitches. Career highs on both innings and strikeouts.

Michael Busch broke the no-hitter with two outs in the 7th — an opposite-field bloop single that ended one of the most dominant individual pitching performances of the season. Cavalli retired 19 of the first 21 batters he faced.

Nationals rookie Abimelec Ortiz made it academic with a 3-run home run in the 5th.

For the Cubs: a loss, but a manageable one. Their NL Wild Card lead over the Padres and D-backs still sits at approximately 5-6 games. The Cubs went 70-50 entering last night; they'll still be well-positioned after absorbing this one.

For Cavalli: a game to remember. At 24, his 2026 has been the breakout season the Nationals have been waiting for from the former top prospect. ⚾
