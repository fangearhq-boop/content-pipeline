Schwarber Plants Two in the Corn — Phillies Beat Twins 7-1 at Field of Dreams on Netflix

By Marcus Cole

August 14, 2026

The ball went into the corn. Twice. Both times, Kyle Schwarber hit it there. The 2026 MLB Field of Dreams Game in Dyersville, Iowa, is now in the books: Philadelphia Phillies 7, Minnesota Twins 1, on a warm Thursday night that delivered everything the event was designed to deliver.

Schwarber's leadoff home run off Taj Bradley — the third pitch of the game — traveled off the bat and into the cornfield in right-center in the 1st inning. His No. 36 of the season, and his 47th career leadoff home run, tying Curtis Granderson for 7th on the all-time list. Then in the 4th, a two-run shot — No. 37 — off the same pitcher. Into the same corn. The Phillies led 5-1 before a full half of baseball had been played.

Three Phillies Home Runs Into the Field

Brandon Marsh joined Schwarber in the corn with the Phillies' third home run of the night. Bryson Stott contributed an RBI single in the 1st inning as Philadelphia built an early cushion. The offense was not just efficient — it was cinematic, exactly what the aesthetics of the game demand.

Taj Bradley — who entered the night 9-4 with a 3.76 ERA and 148 strikeouts — had no answers for Schwarber. The Twins' starter gave up both of Schwarber's blasts and could not recover from the early damage.

Nola Takes the Ball and Delivers

Aaron Nola made his return from a right knee contusion without visible issue. The Phillies' right-hander pitched 5 innings, allowed one run on a solo home run by Luke Keaschall in the 2nd, and struck out 9 — tying his season high. He did not walk a batter. In a game where everything worked for Philadelphia, Nola's performance was the quiet anchor.

Keaschall was the one Twins bright spot in a difficult night: a 357-foot home run off Nola, a double, and a walk — 2-for-3 at the plate. It was the kind of game that gets circled on a prospect's résumé even in a loss. The right fielder nearly caught Schwarber's 37th as it glanced off his glove above the wall in right.

Costner in the Video, Not the Corn

Kevin Costner did not walk out of the corn the way he did in the 2021 inaugural Field of Dreams Game. Instead, he narrated a two-minute pre-game video produced for Netflix that covered baseball history — Babe Ruth, Jackie Robinson, Kirk Gibson — and brought the film's spirit into the broadcast without requiring his physical presence in Iowa. The movie was there in spirit. The corn was very much present in reality.

This was the first Field of Dreams Game since 2022, after a three-year hiatus for the construction of a permanent stadium. More than 25 Baseball Hall of Famers were part of the on-field festivities before first pitch. The capacity crowd in Dyersville — roughly 8,000 — witnessed a game that gave the setting everything it needed.

What's Next

Kyle Schwarber enters August 14 as the sole leader in the MLB home run race with 37 — up two on Yordan Alvarez, Matt Olson, and Junior Caminero, each at 35. The Phillies, now firmly in the NL playoff picture, return to their regular schedule Saturday. Netflix's viewership numbers for the Field of Dreams broadcast were not yet released as of Friday morning.
