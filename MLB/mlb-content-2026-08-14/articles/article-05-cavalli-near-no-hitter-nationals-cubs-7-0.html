Cavalli Dominates — Takes No-Hitter Into the 7th as Nationals Rout Cubs 7-0

By Jake Torres

August 14, 2026

On a night when the national eye was fixed on Iowa, one of the best individual pitching performances of the 2026 season happened in Washington. Cade Cavalli of the Nationals took a no-hitter into the 7th inning against the Chicago Cubs, finished with 8 innings pitched and 10 strikeouts — career highs in both — and helped Washington to a 7-0 win.

Michael Busch ended the no-hit bid with two outs in the 7th — an opposite-field bloop single that dropped into left. Cavalli had retired 19 of the first 21 batters he faced. He finished the 8th, threw 103 pitches, and walked off with the kind of start that gets replayed on highlight reels long after the final standings are set.

Ortiz Provides the Offense

Abimelec Ortiz, the Nationals' rookie, hit a 3-run home run in the 5th inning to break the game open. The lead was 5-0 by the time Cavalli returned for the 6th, and he pitched with the freedom that kind of cushion provides.

The Cubs' offense — the one that has fueled their NL Wild Card lead all season — managed nothing meaningful against the Nationals' right-hander. Gausman, Holmes, and Garrett, the deadline acquisitions who have been contributing to the Cubs' run, were somewhere off to the side watching what Cavalli was doing and wondering how to solve it.

Where the Cubs Stand

Chicago absorbs the 7-0 loss and remains in first in the NL Wild Card standings. They are approximately 70-51 after the defeat, holding roughly a 5-6 game lead over the San Diego Padres (around 65-57) and the Arizona Diamondbacks (around 64-58). Two other Wild Card spots are also available, so the Cubs' position is secure even after a shutout loss like this one.

The team they brought back after the trade deadline — Kevin Gausman, Clay Holmes, and Braxton Garrett — has collectively performed. The deadline additions were the right calls. One loss at the hands of a 24-year-old who was completely locked in doesn't change the picture.

What Cavalli Has Become

Cavalli was a former top prospect for the Nationals who spent time working back from injury. In 2026, the promise behind that prospect status has arrived. His 8-inning, 10-strikeout, 103-pitch performance against a Cubs lineup that has been one of the most productive in the NL all season is the kind of result that goes on a player's career highlight reel.

He has become the Nationals' ace in a way that extends beyond rotation slot. He pitches like someone who expects to dominate. Last night against the Cubs, he did exactly that.

What's Next
