White Sox Enter Detroit With a 2.5-Game Lead — and a Division Race That Just Got Critical

By Marcus Cole

August 14, 2026

Two years ago, the Chicago White Sox lost 121 games. On Friday night, they open a 3-game series at Comerica Park as first-place occupants of the AL Central, holding a 2.5-game lead over the Detroit Tigers. The distance between where this franchise was and where it is right now is one of the great turnaround stories in modern baseball.

And it is being tested. The Detroit Tigers — 60-61 overall, but 37-23 since June and 16-9 since the All-Star break — are one of the hottest teams in the American League. They are not a flawed team in a weak moment. They are a surging team entering a home series with a chance to take over first place.

The White Sox Situation

Chicago enters at 62-58 after dropping a 9-8 game to the Cincinnati Reds on Wednesday night — one night after Luis Castillo struck out 10 batters in a complete-game shutout. The two-day swing illustrates the volatility that has defined the White Sox all season: they are capable of anything in either direction.

Castillo is their most important starter, and how the rotation is deployed in this series will be one of the most closely watched decisions of manager their season. The last time Chicago visited Comerica Park, they dropped three consecutive games. That pattern is not forgotten in their dugout.

The Tigers' Case

Detroit's 37-23 stretch since June is not a small sample — it is more than two months of sustained excellence. Going 16-9 since the All-Star break puts them among the best second-half teams in baseball. They are doing this without a front-line star who has dominated national headlines, relying instead on pitching depth and a lineup that has been consistently productive.

The Tigers have home field for this series, and the math is straightforward: win this three-game set and they could exit the weekend within half a game of first place. Lose it and the White Sox gap grows to five or six games — a cushion that would be difficult to overcome with roughly six weeks remaining in the season.

What Is at Stake

The AL Central will not be decided this weekend. But the shape of the final six weeks of the race almost certainly will be. A White Sox sweep keeps Detroit at a manageable distance. A Tigers sweep flips the division and puts Chicago in the position of chasing the team that just overtook them. A split leaves everything where it is — tight, tense, and unresolved until September.

For a franchise that has not made the playoffs since 2021, and that bottomed out historically just two seasons ago, every game of this series carries the weight of everything the rebuild has been building toward. The White Sox have a chance to prove their first-place record is real. Tonight in Detroit is where that proof begins.
