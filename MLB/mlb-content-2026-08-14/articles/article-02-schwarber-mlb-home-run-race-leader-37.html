The Field of Dreams Four-Way Tie Is Over — Schwarber Leads the MLB Home Run Race at 37

By Jake Torres

August 14, 2026

Four players walked into this week tied at 35 home runs — the most competitive top of the MLB home run race mid-August has seen in years. By Thursday night in Dyersville, Iowa, there is one leader: Kyle Schwarber at 37. Yordan Alvarez, Matt Olson, and Junior Caminero are still at 35, two behind after a night in which none of them homered.

Schwarber's two-home run performance in the 2026 Field of Dreams Game is the most prominent single contribution to the home run race this season. His 36th and 37th home runs both went into the Iowa cornfield off Taj Bradley. It was his second multi-homer game in a week.

The State of Each Contender

The four-way race entering this week told four different stories, and they are all still relevant:

Alvarez — the AL MVP favorite at -900 odds — has not hit a home run since July 28. That is an unusual drought for the Houston Astros slugger, one of the most feared right-handed hitters of his generation. The gap between Alvarez and Schwarber is now two home runs with roughly 45 games remaining. The issue for Alvarez is not talent — it is timing. When he starts hitting them again, it will likely be in clusters.

Caminero has been the most active of the three trailing Schwarber. The 23-year-old Tampa Bay Rays third baseman has 11 home runs and 22 RBI in his last 13 games — a historically torrid pace for a player his age. At this clip, he is on track to become one of the youngest players with consecutive 35-HR seasons in MLB history. The issue is his team is playing out the string, which limits his visibility.

Olson has 8 home runs in his last 14 games, a rate that could close a two-HR gap in less than a week. He is also playing in his 903rd consecutive game, the 8th-longest streak in MLB history — and the Braves are still in the thick of the NL East race. He has motivation and is performing at an elite level.

Schwarber's Context

Schwarber hit 56 home runs in 2025 — one of the best individual home run seasons in Phillies history. His 2026 pace has been more uneven, but 37 home runs through approximately 117 games projects to somewhere in the mid-40s by season's end. Whether he challenges his 2025 total depends heavily on whether he finds another hot streak of the kind he had last year.

His 47 career leadoff home runs now tie Curtis Granderson for 7th most in MLB history. That is a career marker that speaks to the consistency of Schwarber's approach — he is at his most dangerous when he steps into the box before anyone else.

What's Next

Schwarber and the Phillies play Friday through Sunday. Alvarez and the Astros continue their second-half schedule. Olson and the Braves are in the NL East hunt. Caminero and the Rays play on.
