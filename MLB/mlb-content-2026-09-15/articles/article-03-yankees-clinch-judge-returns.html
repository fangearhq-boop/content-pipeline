Aaron Judge's First Homer Since May Sends Yankees to Their Record 61st Postseason

By Ryan Calloway | Ballpark Banter | September 15, 2026

A fractured rib. Three months on the IL. And then, in the 8th inning against Minnesota on September 14, Aaron Judge hit the home run that sent the Yankees to October — and back to themselves.

The timeline matters. Aaron Judge hit a home run on May 24 and did not hit another one for nearly four months. A fractured rib put him on the injured list in mid-June, and what followed was exactly the kind of extended absence that tests a franchise's identity — games without its best player, games where the team has to prove it can function without the face on the billboard outside the ballpark.

The Yankees passed that test. They held their position in the American League standings, kept the lead, maintained their grip on October. Judge returned around September 8, eased back in, and then on September 14 did exactly what Aaron Judge does in moments that matter: he hit a 3-run home run in the 8th inning against the Minnesota Twins, part of an 8-3 Yankees victory that clinched the franchise's record 61st postseason berth.

First homer since May 24. Record clinch. The Captain is back.

Record 61st Berth

The number deserves its own moment. Sixty-one postseason appearances. No franchise in MLB history has made the playoffs more often than the New York Yankees, and September 14 extended that record further. The Yankees have made 9 of their last 10 postseasons — a consistency that speaks to organizational depth, financial resources and, more than anything, the kind of winning culture that has defined this franchise since the days when the record-setting started.

In a sport where even the best organizations struggle to sustain excellence across decades, the Yankees' postseason regularity is its own kind of achievement. That Judge was the one to deliver the clinching blow — in his first significant moment back from a serious injury — felt less like coincidence and more like the franchise asserting its identity.

The Return of the Captain

A fractured rib is not a minor injury. The healing timeline, the difficulty breathing at full lung capacity, the disruption to a hitter's core mechanics — all of it creates uncertainty about what a player will look like when they return. Judge managed the return carefully, and the 3-run homer in the 8th inning against Minnesota was the moment that answered any remaining questions.

He is healthy. He is dangerous. And the Yankees are going to October with their most important player available.

For Yankees fans who watched the summer without Judge — a summer that was still successful but felt incomplete — the September 14 homer had the quality of a reunion. The team was always going to be fine. With Judge, it is something more than fine. It is a contender.

What October Looks Like

The Yankees enter the postseason as one of the American League's top seeds, with home-field advantage in the early rounds. A healthy Judge anchoring the lineup changes the calculus for every opposing pitching staff. The slates who had months to prepare for a Judge-less Yankees lineup now have to recalibrate.

That is the gift of his return: not just what he adds, but what he removes from everyone else's plans. The Yankees were already in October. Now, with the Captain back, they are in October the way October is supposed to feel in New York — with the full roster, the full identity and the full sense that anything is possible.
