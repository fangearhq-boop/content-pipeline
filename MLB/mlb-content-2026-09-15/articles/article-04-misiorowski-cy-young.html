Jakob Misiorowski Is Doing Things No Pitcher Has Ever Done Before

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | September 15, 2026

1.95 ERA. 236 strikeouts. 1,000 pitches at 100+ mph. The Milwaukee Brewers ace is 24 years old and rewriting the record books — and the Cy Young Award is the least interesting thing about what he's building.

The Cy Young Award is supposed to be the headline. A pitcher who leads MLB in ERA, wins and strikeouts at age 24 is a Cy Young Award winner in the way that the sun rises in the east — it is not a question, it is a consequence of the evidence. Jakob Misiorowski's odds reflect exactly that: -700 to win the award, with his nearest competitor listed at +500. The race is not close. The race barely qualifies as a race.

But the Cy Young is almost beside the point, because the thing Misiorowski is building in Milwaukee in 2026 goes beyond any single award. His 19-8 record, 1.95 ERA and 236 strikeouts are the standard numbers — the ones that go on the trophy plaque. The number that does not fit on any plaque is the one that may define his season more than any of them: 1,000 pitches at 100 miles per hour or faster.

That has never happened before. Not in the Statcast tracking era, which goes back to 2015. Not by any pitcher, anywhere in the league, in any of the years we have had the technology to measure it. Misiorowski did it in 2026. He is 24 years old.

Understanding the 1,000-Pitch Threshold

To appreciate what 1,000 pitches at 100+ mph means, it helps to understand how rare triple-digit velocity is at any level of professional baseball. Most pitchers who can touch 100 mph do so occasionally — in the first inning, in a high-leverage situation, at the peak of their repertoire. Sustaining that velocity across a full season, across hundreds of appearances, is a different challenge entirely.

Misiorowski does not just touch 100 mph. He pitches at 100 mph repeatedly, consistently and across enough innings that his season total has crossed a threshold no pitcher had ever reached in the tracking era. The combination of raw arm strength, elite conditioning and the kind of mechanics that allow him to repeat the delivery at maximum effort — all of it has produced something the sport had not seen before.

The 1.95 ERA is impressive. The velocity record is extraordinary.

The Franchise Record Within Reach

Ben Sheets struck out 264 batters for the Milwaukee Brewers in 2004 — a franchise single-season record that has stood for more than two decades. Misiorowski, with 236 strikeouts and several starts remaining, needs 28 more to break it.

The math is favorable. Twenty-eight strikeouts across four or five remaining starts, for a pitcher who leads MLB in the category, is achievable. It is not guaranteed — streaks can end, pitch counts can be managed conservatively as the Brewers protect their ace heading into October — but the record is clearly within range.

If Misiorowski breaks it, he will have accomplished something in a single season that Sheets — a very good pitcher in his own right — did at the peak of his career. Another piece of franchise history, added to a season already full of them.

What Comes Next

The Brewers hold the No. 1 seed in the National League and a first-round bye. Misiorowski, assuming he is pitching in the postseason — and there is every reason to believe he will be — will face the kind of hitters who have had all summer to study his tendencies, his repertoire and his sequencing.

That is the next chapter. The regular season chapter, with the Cy Young and the velocity record and the franchise strikeout record chase, is still being written. But it is almost finished. And what it says about Jakob Misiorowski at 24 years old is already more than enough to know that Milwaukee is watching something special — and so is the rest of the sport.
