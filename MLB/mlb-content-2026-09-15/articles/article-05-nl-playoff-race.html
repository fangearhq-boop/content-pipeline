Twelve Games Left and Three NL Wild Card Spots Still Feel Like Seven

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | September 15, 2026

The top of the National League bracket is settled. Everything below it is still very much alive — and September 25-27 in San Diego could be the most important three games of the season.

Two things are true about the National League playoff picture with approximately 12 games remaining. First: the top of the bracket is done. Milwaukee is the No. 1 seed and will receive a first-round bye. The Los Angeles Dodgers are No. 2, also with a bye. Atlanta is No. 3. Those are facts, not projections.

Second: everything below those three spots is still very much alive, and the three wild card positions that remain are being contested with the kind of urgency that makes September baseball worth watching. The Phillies hold the first wild card. The Cubs hold the second. And the Padres — 2.5 games ahead of the Arizona Diamondbacks — are clinging to the third.

That 2.5-game gap is the number that defines the rest of this month. It is large enough to be comfortable. It is small enough to disappear.

The Series That Changes Everything

Circle September 25-27 on your calendar. The San Diego Padres will host the Arizona Diamondbacks at Petco Park for three games that could decide who plays in the postseason and who spends October watching. If the Padres win the series, or even split it, their lead likely holds through the regular season's final days. If the D-backs sweep, the race becomes a genuine coin flip with fewer than a week remaining.

Neither team can afford to coast to that series. Both have games to play in the next week that matter — and in a race this close, a loss to a sub-.500 team carries the same weight as a loss to a playoff contender. The standings do not discriminate between how a lead is built or lost.

The Padres have the home-field advantage for that series, and Petco Park has historically been one of the better pitcher's parks in the National League — a fact that matters more when October roster implications are attached to every pitch. The D-backs, meanwhile, have the desperation that comes with trailing in September, which is its own kind of advantage.

What the Other Wild Cards Mean

The Phillies' WC1 position matters beyond simply qualifying for the playoffs. The first wild card in the NL bracket means playing the weakest remaining qualifier in the Wild Card round, with a potential path to face either Milwaukee or Los Angeles in the Division Series. For Philadelphia, holding that position through the final 12 games is a priority, not a given.

The Cubs, at WC2 with Pete Crow-Armstrong chasing a 40-40 season and an NL MVP award, have more going on than a simple playoff qualification. Their placement in the bracket — WC2 rather than WC1 — determines their Wild Card opponent and, potentially, their entire October path. A Cubs team with a healthy PCA and postseason experience is a dangerous draw for whoever they face.

Wild Card Starts September 29

The NL Wild Card round begins September 29, which means the teams that earn those spots will have almost no margin between clinching and playing. A team that wins the wild card in the final weekend of the season will be in October before they have had time to adjust their rotation, rest their bullpen or catch a full night's sleep.

That compression is part of what makes the final weeks of a pennant race feel unlike anything else in sports. The Padres, D-backs, Phillies and Cubs are all aware that every game between now and September 28 is not just a game. It is an audition for the version of their team they want to be when October starts.

Watch the standings. Watch September 25-27. Watch Petco Park. Everything could change.
