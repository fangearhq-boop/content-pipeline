Pete Crow-Armstrong Is 3 Stolen Bases Away From Baseball Immortality

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | September 15, 2026

42 home runs. 37 stolen bases. The first Cub to threaten the 40-40 club is doing it in a pennant race — and every stolen base attempt is now a moment of history in motion.

There are six players in Major League Baseball history who have hit 40 home runs and stolen 40 bases in the same season. José Canseco did it in 1988. Barry Bonds in 1996. Alex Rodriguez in 1998. Alfonso Soriano in 2006. Ronald Acuña Jr. in 2023. Shohei Ohtani in 2023. Six players, across nearly four decades, in a list that has never included a Chicago Cub.

Pete Crow-Armstrong is three stolen bases away from changing that.

Entering September 15, PCA sits at 42 home runs and 37 stolen bases — numbers that would have felt impossible at Wrigley Field just one year ago and feel almost too large to comprehend now. He is not just chasing a personal milestone. He is chasing franchise history, MLB history and, with the Cubs holding the NL Wild Card 2 seed, something that matters even more: a postseason berth.

A Franchise First on Two Counts

The 40-40 milestone would be the obvious headline, but there is another number worth appreciating first. PCA's 42 home runs make him the first Chicago Cub to reach the 40-homer mark since Derrek Lee slugged 46 in 2005. More than two decades separated Lee's achievement from PCA's — and the context could not be more different. Lee's Cubs finished third in the NL Central and missed the playoffs. Crow-Armstrong's Cubs are fighting for October.

That combination — the power and the speed — is what makes this chase so remarkable. The 40-40 club is not just exclusive because the thresholds are high. It is exclusive because the players who hit 40 home runs almost never run enough to steal 40 bases, and the players who steal 40 bases almost never hit with the kind of power that generates 40 home runs. PCA does both. That is the whole point of what he is doing.

The Math and the Moment

Three stolen bases over the final two weeks of the season is not a guarantee. It is also not a given. PCA will need opportunities on the bases and the green light from a Cubs coaching staff that is simultaneously trying to win games and manage their best player's workload entering October. The tension between individual achievement and team strategy is real, even if most Cubs fans would probably like to ignore it for now.

What the Cubs do not have to worry about is the home run total. Forty-two is already history. The franchise record, the franchise milestone and the numbers that have never existed in Wrigley's record books are already there. The 40-40 is the piece that would push PCA from remarkable to immortal — and it would do so as part of a playoff chase that gives every Cubs game a weight it rarely carries in September.

The MVP Picture

None of this is happening in a vacuum. PCA is the frontrunner for the NL MVP award, and for good reason: the power, the speed, the defense in center field and the timing — delivering his best season while the Cubs are fighting for their October lives — all point in the same direction. A 40-40 season would not just confirm the award. It would make the vote a formality.

The list of players who have won an MVP award with a 40-40 season is short because the list of players who have had 40-40 seasons is short. PCA has a chance to add his name to both. Three stolen bases. Watch the basepaths tonight.
