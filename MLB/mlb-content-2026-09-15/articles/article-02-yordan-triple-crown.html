Yordan Alvarez Leads the AL in Batting — But His Triple Crown Window Is Closing Fast

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | September 15, 2026

At .311, Alvarez is the best hitter in the American League by average. The problem is that a Triple Crown requires three categories — and a brutal stretch since August has put the other two out of reach.

Yordan Alvarez is, right now, the best hitter in the American League if you measure by batting average. His .311 mark leads the AL entering September 15, a number that reflects what Alvarez can do at his best: patient, powerful, dangerous in the big moment. The kind of hitter who makes pitchers rethink their entire approach.

The problem is that Triple Crown requires three categories, not one. And in the other two, Alvarez is not leading anyone.

Tampa Bay's Junior Caminero leads the AL in home runs at approximately 40. Baltimore's Pete Alonso leads in RBI, somewhere in the neighborhood of 97 to 100. Both gaps are significant. Both gaps were, theoretically, closable. And then August happened.

The August Slide

Since August 2, Yordan Alvarez has hit .242 with 3 home runs and 12 RBI across 35 games. For almost any other player in baseball, that would be a mediocre stretch worth a paragraph in a game recap. For Alvarez, chasing history, it has been devastating.

The slump has not cost him the batting average lead — his lead there is large enough to have absorbed the slide. But it has widened the gap in home runs and RBI to the point where any honest accounting of the Triple Crown odds has to acknowledge what the numbers say: with approximately 12 games remaining, Alvarez would need to nearly catch Caminero in home runs while also closing on Alonso in RBI. Both categories. In less than two weeks.

That is not impossible. It is, however, very unlikely.

The Last Time

The last American League player to win the Triple Crown was Miguel Cabrera in 2012, when the Detroit Tigers first baseman led the league in batting average, home runs and RBI to cap one of the most individually complete offensive seasons in the sport's modern history. The achievement earned Cabrera a unanimous AL MVP award and a permanent place in baseball lore.

Alvarez had the potential to join that conversation. He has the pedigree — a former AL MVP himself, a hitter whose combination of patience and power makes him among the most feared in the sport. A Triple Crown would have been the capstone on an already-great career. Instead, the August slide has likely pushed it just out of reach.

What Still Matters

An AL batting title is not nothing. If Alvarez holds his lead through the final 12 games — and there is no guarantee, as slumps can deepen — he would become the first Astros player to win a batting average title in recent memory, and it would add another individual trophy to a career already full of them.

But the Triple Crown dream, the thing that would have made 2026 truly historic, appears to be slipping. The window was open. August closed it. That is baseball — individual excellence at the mercy of human limitations, cold streaks and the stubborn reality that no one hits well for six straight months.

Alvarez gets approximately 12 more opportunities to change the story. Watch how he uses them.
