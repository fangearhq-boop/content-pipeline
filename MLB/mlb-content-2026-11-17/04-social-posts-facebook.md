### STORY 1: QO Deadline Day — Arozarena Declines, Suzuki Set to Follow

**Long-Form Post (8:00 AM ET)**

Today at 4 PM ET, the qualifying offer deadline expires — and free agency fully opens for seven of baseball's best players.

Randy Arozarena has already confirmed he's declining Seattle's $23.15M offer. Seiya Suzuki's agent Joel Wolfe told reporters at the GM Meetings that Suzuki will decline Chicago's offer as well. Bednar, Rogers, Jeffers, Lowe, and Morejon are all expected to follow.

The Mariners and Cubs each get compensatory draft picks — small consolation for losing cornerstone players. But the bigger story may be what this day represents: the possible final qualifying offer cycle in MLB history. With the CBA expiring December 1, the MLBPA has signaled it wants the QO system eliminated in the next collective bargaining agreement.

Seven star players hitting the open market simultaneously. History watching from the edges. What a day.

### STORY 2: Skubal at the Finish Line

**Long-Form Post (10:30 AM ET)**

The Mets flew to Orange County to make their pitch to Tarik Skubal in person — and according to Jon Heyman of the New York Post, they're "probably the favorite."

Six teams remain in the running: the Mets, Dodgers, Padres, Blue Jays, and two unnamed clubs. Dodgers GM Brandon Gomes has confirmed the team is engaged. Contract ceiling per ESPN: $400 million. AAV floor: $44 million — which would shatter Yoshinobu Yamamoto's previous record of $27 million per year by more than $17 million.

The urgency is real. Skubal turns 30 this month and wants this done before December 1 when the CBA expires and transactions freeze. If a lockout follows, any team that hasn't signed him waits until spring at the earliest.

Mets fans: how are you feeling about those odds?

### STORY 3: Owners Meetings Day 1

**Long-Form Post (12:30 PM ET)**

The owners began their three-day meetings today in what may be the most consequential gathering in a generation. CBA expires December 1 — exactly 14 days from now.

The ownership side is pushing a salary cap and a five-year maximum contract length for free agents. The MLBPA has repeatedly and publicly rejected both proposals. Commissioner Manfred has pledged a lockout if no deal is reached by midnight December 1.

For context: the last MLB lockout ran from December 2021 through March 2022 — 99 days. The 2027 season isn't in jeopardy yet, but it's on the board if this drags.

No progress has been reported from any outlet on Day 1. The Winter Meetings, typically scheduled for December, could be canceled entirely if a lockout takes hold.

Watch this space.

### STORY 4: Jazz Chisholm

**Long-Form Post (3:00 PM ET)**

Jon Morosi of MLB Network reports 11 teams are engaged in Jazz Chisholm's free agency market — and according to Shi Davidi of Sportsnet, the Toronto Blue Jays have emerged as the frontrunner.

Toronto's vision: Chisholm at second base, with Addison Clement sliding into a super-utility role. The Yankees, who had Chisholm last year, are not expected to return to the table.

Here's what makes Chisholm uniquely valuable right now: no qualifying offer. The team that signs him pays no draft pick penalty — something you can't say about most of the marquee names on the board. Combined with his 2026 numbers (.231/.312/.399, 19 HR, 41 SB) and 2025 showing (31 HR, 31 SB), he represents elite athleticism and position flexibility.

Personal meetings are expected this week. If you're a Blue Jays fan, now is a very good time to be excited.

### STORY 5: 14 Days to Freeze

**Long-Form Post (10:00 PM ET)**

Here's where things stand: December 1 is 14 days away. At 11:59 PM ET, the CBA expires. If a lockout follows — and Commissioner Manfred says one will — all transactions stop. No signings. No trades. The offseason pauses.

That means every team with an unmet need is working against the same hard clock. Tarik Skubal ($400M, six teams bidding). Jazz Chisholm (11 teams, Blue Jays leading). Yoshinobu Yamamoto (posted November 1, 45-day window closing December 16 — the freeze may prevent any team from completing that deal). Plus seven QO players who just became fully free today.

The Cubs need an entire rotation. The Orioles need starting pitching. The Yankees need to replace Chisholm. The Red Sox, locked in with Bauers, need SP depth.

Fourteen days. Then nothing moves until spring.

The next two weeks may be the most active transaction stretch in baseball since the last lockout. Buckle up.
