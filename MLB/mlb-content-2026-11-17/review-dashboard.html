### Long-Form Post Today at 4 PM ET, the qualifying offer deadline expires — and free agency fully opens for seven of baseball's best players. Randy Arozarena has already confirmed he's declining Seattle's $23.15M offer. Seiya Suzuki's agent Joel Wolfe told reporters at the GM Meetings that Suzuki will decline Chicago's offer as well. Bednar, Rogers, Jeffers, Lowe, and Morejon are all expected to follow. The Mariners and Cubs each get compensatory draft picks — small consolation for losing cornerstone players. But the bigger story may be what this day represents: the possible final qualifying offer cycle in MLB history. With the CBA expiring December 1, the MLBPA has signaled it wants the QO system eliminated in the next collective bargaining agreement. Seven star players hitting the open market simultaneously. History watching from the edges. ### Long-Form Post The owners began their three-day meetings today in what may be the most consequential gathering in a generation. CBA expires December 1 — exactly 14 days from now. The ownership side is pushing a salary cap and a five-year maximum contract length for free agents. The MLBPA has repeatedly and publicly rejected both proposals. Commissioner Manfred has pledged a lockout if no deal is reached by midnight December 1. For context: the last MLB lockout ran from December 2021 through March 2022 — 99 days. The 2027 season isn't in jeopardy yet, but it's on the board if this drags. No progress has been reported from any outlet on Day 1. The Winter Meetings, typically scheduled for December, could be canceled entirely if a lockout takes hold. Watch this space. ### Image Post Caption Owners meetings started today. No signings. No trades. The offseason pauses. That means every team with an unmet need is working against the same hard clock. Tarik Skubal ($400M, six teams bidding). Jazz Chisholm (11 teams, Blue Jays leading). Yoshinobu Yamamoto (posted November 1, 45-day window closing December 16 — the freeze may prevent any team from completing that deal).
