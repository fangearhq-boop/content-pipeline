Owners Meet, Labor Gap Yawns: 14 Days to Deal or Lockout

By Ryan Calloway | November 17, 2026

The owners are meeting. The players are not there. And December 1 is 14 days away.

The MLB owners' three-day meetings began today in what may be the most consequential gathering since the last lockout talks in 2021. CBA negotiations have produced no reported progress. The positions on both sides remain wide. And Commissioner Rob Manfred has pledged that if no deal lands by midnight on December 1, a lockout begins.

What the Owners Want

The ownership side has proposed a salary cap and a five-year maximum contract length for free agents. These are not new positions — owners have pushed cap-related frameworks in multiple prior negotiations — but the specificity of the current proposal marks a more aggressive posture than in recent cycles.

Reports indicate MLB has proposed limiting free agent contracts to five years and to 15 percent of a team's salary cap threshold. The logic from the ownership side centers on "cost certainty" — a phrase that translates, in practical terms, to limiting the upside of players who reach the market at age 28 or 29 and have historically signed deals running through their late 30s.

Revenue sharing restructuring is also on the table. Smaller-market clubs have pushed for adjustments to how revenue is distributed across the league, arguing that the current system does not adequately support competitive balance.

What the Players Say

No. The MLBPA's position on a salary cap has been consistent and unambiguous for decades: it is not a negotiating starting point, it is a hard line. The union's leadership has repeatedly stated that any cap in any form is unacceptable, and there is no sign that position has softened entering these talks.

The five-year maximum contract proposal presents a different kind of problem. Players entering free agency at 28 or 29 currently sign deals that run eight, nine, or ten years. A five-year cap would dramatically limit their earning potential and shift risk back onto players who experience injury or decline in their early 30s. The union has signaled this proposal is equally unacceptable.

What the players want includes expanded service time protections for pre-arbitration players, changes to the qualifying offer system, and revenue sharing adjustments that flow toward players rather than away from them. The gap between the two sides is not measured in details — it is structural.

The Clock and What It Means

The current CBA expires at 11:59 PM ET on December 1. Commissioner Manfred has been explicit: if there is no new agreement, a lockout begins immediately. That means all transactions freeze. No free agent signings. No trades. No minor league deals. The offseason stops.

The last lockout, which began in December 2021 and ended in mid-March 2022, lasted 99 days. It shortened spring training, delayed the start of the regular season, and created lasting tension between the league and its players. A similar stoppage this winter would have the same effects, with one additional complication: the December window for transactions would be entirely lost, potentially preventing high-profile deals like the Skubal signing from completing on the player's preferred timeline.

The Winter Meetings, typically scheduled for December, would almost certainly be canceled in a lockout scenario. Teams would enter 2027 spring training having been unable to transact for months.

Day 1 Read

No outlet has reported any movement from today's meetings. That is not surprising for Day 1 of a three-day session, but it is not encouraging either. The owners' meetings run through November 19. After that, there are 12 days until the deadline.

Baseball has avoided a work stoppage in every CBA negotiation since 1994-95 until the 2021 lockout ended that streak. The 2022 settlement came after meaningful compromises on both sides. Whether the same kind of resolution is achievable in the current climate — with a cap proposal on the table and a union that has been preparing its membership for a fight — is the central question the sport is asking.

Fourteen days. The owners are meeting. The gap is wide. Watch carefully.

What's Next

Owners meetings continue through November 19. After that, negotiating sessions between the league and union are expected to intensify in the final days before the deadline. Any signal of progress or breakdown will move markets — both in terms of which free agents sign quickly before the freeze and which teams hold off, gambling that a lockout either doesn't happen or ends quickly.

For fans watching the offseason unfold, the next 14 days are the entire game. Signings that don't happen now may not happen until spring. Everything is accelerated. Everything has stakes. The labor situation is the shadow over all of it.
