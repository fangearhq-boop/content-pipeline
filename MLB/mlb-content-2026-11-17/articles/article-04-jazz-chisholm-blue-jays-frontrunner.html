Blue Jays Frontrunner for Jazz Chisholm as 11-Team Market Heats Up

By Marcus Cole | November 17, 2026

Every team that needs a second baseman with power and elite speed is calling Jazz Chisholm's agent. According to Jon Morosi of MLB Network, 11 teams are engaged. And according to Shi Davidi of Sportsnet — the best-sourced reporter covering the Blue Jays — Toronto has emerged as the frontrunner.

Chisholm has become the best pure value on a free agent market that is about to freeze. And the team closest to signing him may be the one that gets the most out of him.

Why Toronto Makes Sense

The Blue Jays' vision for Chisholm is specific: he slots in at second base, with Bo Bichette's backup Addison Clement moving into a super-utility role. That is clean roster-building — a defined position, a clear lineup upgrade, and a path that does not require displacing anyone already entrenched.

Toronto has the financial flexibility to compete in a market with 11 interested teams, the organizational philosophy to value a player with Chisholm's athleticism profile, and an identified fit that many of his suitors probably cannot match as neatly.

Davidi's reporting on the Blue Jays is sourced directly — when he identifies a team as the frontrunner, it carries weight. Toronto being out in front does not mean the race is over, but it does mean they are the team to beat.

The Case for Chisholm

Chisholm's 2026 numbers read as a slight step back from his breakout 2025 (.231/.312/.399, 19 HR, 41 SB versus .242/.332/.481, 31 HR, 31 SB the year before), but the context matters. The stolen base total actually improved significantly — 41 steals in 2026 compared to 31 the prior year. The power dipped, but the speed element accelerated.

What Chisholm offers that few players on this market can match is the combination of above-average power, plus-plus speed, and positional flexibility. He can play second base, third base, and outfield at the major league level. He changes games on the basepaths in ways that box scores undervalue. And at 28 years old, he is entering what should be the prime years of his career.

The absence of a qualifying offer is the other crucial piece. Teams signing Chisholm pay no draft pick penalty — an advantage that becomes increasingly valuable as QO-attached players like Arozarena, Suzuki, and Bednar all carry that cost. Chisholm is a top-tier free agent with no strings attached. That is genuinely rare.

Who Else Is in the Mix

The Yankees were the team most prominently discussed in connection with Chisholm after his time in New York. But according to multiple reports, the Yankees are not expected to return. New York appears to be pivoting toward other options to fill its second base need — Brandon Lowe among them.

Beyond the Blue Jays and the unnamed nine other teams in Morosi's report, specific clubs with identifiable second base needs include the Cubs, Phillies, and several AL contenders in various stages of roster reconstruction. Personal meetings are expected this week, which typically means a decision timeline of one to three weeks from first sitdown.

With the December 1 CBA deadline bearing down, there is external pressure to move quickly. Teams that want Chisholm locked in before a potential lockout will not have the luxury of a prolonged process.

What's Next

Personal meetings beginning this week are the next signpost. If the Blue Jays have a face-to-face meeting with Chisholm and it goes well, a deal could come quickly — possibly within days. With 11 teams engaged, Chisholm's market has real depth, which means his contract value is likely to push into multi-year territory with meaningful guarantees.

For Toronto fans, the frontrunner status is real and meaningful, but not a done deal. The team has work to do. Chisholm will hear from other suitors, weigh his options, and make his decision based on fit, money, and winning probability.

The Blue Jays are answering first. That is a good place to be.
