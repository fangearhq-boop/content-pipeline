QO Deadline Day: Arozarena Declines, Suzuki Out, Full Free Agency Opens at 4 PM ET

By Marcus Cole | November 17, 2026

At 4 PM ET today, the qualifying offer deadline expires — and with it, the last gate holding back seven of baseball's most coveted free agents. Randy Arozarena has already confirmed he is declining Seattle's $23.15 million offer. Seiya Suzuki's agent Joel Wolfe told reporters at the GM Meetings that Suzuki will decline Chicago's offer as well. By the time the clock runs out, every remaining player on the qualifying offer list is expected to follow.

It is, by any measure, a consequential afternoon for the sport. And it may be the last one of its kind.

The Players and the Decision

Eight players received qualifying offers this offseason, each carrying the standard value of $23.15 million for the 2027 season. Jake Bauers was the first to act, signing a four-year, $58 million deal with the Boston Red Sox and effectively turning down a one-year guarantee worth nearly $24 million. That left seven players still holding their offers heading into today's deadline.

Arozarena's decision was the first confirmed decline. The outfielder had a strong 2026 season with Seattle but had long been expected to pursue a multi-year deal in open free agency — a $23.15 million single-year offer was never going to compete with that calculus. The Mariners will receive a compensatory draft pick if he signs with another team that is subject to draft pick penalties.

Suzuki's situation carried added weight given his profile as a franchise cornerstone for the Cubs. His agent's confirmation at the GM Meetings was the clearest signal yet that Chicago's window with Suzuki has closed. The Cubs, in turn, receive a compensatory pick — meaningful compensation, but no substitute for the right fielder they are losing.

Bednar, Rogers, Jeffers, Lowe, and Morejon are all expected to decline as well. Each of their former teams would receive a compensatory draft pick if the player signs elsewhere with a qualifying-offer-eligible club.

The Last QO Cycle?

The qualifying offer system has existed since the 2011 Collective Bargaining Agreement as a mechanism to give teams some compensation for losing premium free agents. Its effectiveness has been debated ever since. Players resent the attachment — a QO designation makes them harder to sign for mid-market clubs unwilling to surrender draft picks. Teams use it as a roster management tool. And the MLBPA has pushed, quietly and then loudly, to have the system eliminated entirely.

With the current CBA expiring at 11:59 PM ET on December 1, there is a real possibility that today marks the final qualifying offer deadline in baseball history. The union's negotiating posture heading into this CBA cycle has included the elimination of QOs as a priority demand. If a new agreement reflects that position, no player will ever again face this specific decision.

That adds a layer of historical significance to what is otherwise a routine calendar event. Historical acceptance rate for qualifying offers: 18 of 157 players offered, roughly 11.5 percent. Players almost never accept. But the system has shaped the market for free agents for 15 years, and its absence would restructure how teams approach compensation for departing players.

What Opens at 4 PM ET

When the deadline passes, seven players enter unrestricted free agency simultaneously. That means teams which previously faced draft pick costs for signing them no longer do — assuming those players had already received and declined their offers by that point.

Arozarena and Suzuki are the headliners. Arozarena, 29, is coming off a productive season with Seattle and figures to draw significant interest from outfield-needy clubs. Suzuki, 32, is a consistent right fielder with solid contact skills and an arm that plays in right. Both figure to sign multi-year deals that clear the QO threshold by a significant margin.

The other five — Bednar, Rogers, Jeffers, Lowe, and Morejon — represent a mix of relievers, a catcher, and infield depth that will find new homes in the weeks ahead. Each adds to what is shaping up as the most compressed free agency sprint in recent memory, given the December 1 CBA deadline bearing down on every transaction.

What's Next

With all seven players now fully free, expect activity to accelerate. The 14-day window before the CBA expires is real, and teams know it. Any deal that isn't signed by midnight November 30 risks getting caught in a lockout that could last months.

Arozarena's market will develop quickly. Suzuki's will too, given the depth of outfield need around the league. For Cubs fans, the consolation prize is a draft pick and the knowledge that the team now has financial flexibility to pursue other options — whether that means rebuilding the rotation or addressing other holes.

For everyone else, today marks the opening of a free agency sprint unlike any that has come before. Seven players, 14 days, and the possible last qualifying offer deadline in baseball history. The offseason is officially underway.
