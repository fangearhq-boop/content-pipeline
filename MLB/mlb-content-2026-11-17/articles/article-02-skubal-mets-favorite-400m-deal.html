Mets Favorite to Land Skubal in $400M Deal Before Dec. 1 Freeze

By Jake Torres | November 17, 2026

The Mets flew to Orange County to make their pitch in person. Jon Heyman of the New York Post says they are "probably the favorite." The contract ceiling, per ESPN, is $400 million. The deadline is December 1.

Everything about Tarik Skubal's free agency is operating at maximum scale — the team interest, the dollar figures, the urgency. And right now, New York is in the pole position.

The Six-Team Race

Skubal's field has narrowed to six finalists: the Mets, Dodgers, Padres, Blue Jays, and two unnamed teams. Each has made their case in some form. The Mets made theirs in person, dispatching a high-powered contingent to California for a face-to-face meeting with the left-hander and his representatives.

The Dodgers, the other marquee name in the mix, are engaged as well. GM Brandon Gomes confirmed publicly that the club expects to have meetings with Skubal. Los Angeles has recent precedent here — the Yoshinobu Yamamoto signing established the Dodgers as willing to pay premium prices for elite starting pitching, and Skubal would represent an extension of that philosophy.

But Heyman's reporting tilts the conversation toward New York. The Mets have Steve Cohen's financial resources and a front office, led by David Stearns, that has shown willingness to commit to starting pitching at the top of the market. They have the money. They have the motivation. And according to the most plugged-in reporter in the game, they have the edge.

The Numbers That Change Everything

The contract projections circulating around Skubal would rewrite the record books for starting pitcher salaries. The floor, per reports, is $44 million per year in average annual value. That would shatter Yamamoto's existing record of $27 million per year — set when the Dodgers signed him before the 2024 season — by more than $17 million. Per year.

The ceiling is $400 million total. If Skubal signs for 9 years at $44 million AAV, that hits the number. A shorter deal at higher AAV could reach it too. Either way, the magnitude reflects what the market now believes elite starting pitching is worth in the post-Yamamoto era.

Skubal earned this valuation. He is one of the best left-handed starters in baseball, and at 29 years old — turning 30 this month — he represents a pitcher at or near the peak of his abilities. Teams bidding on him are not projecting potential; they are paying for a known commodity at an age where the downside risk is manageable on a reasonable-length contract.

The December 1 Constraint

Skubal has stated publicly that he wants to sign before the CBA expires on December 1. That is not a casual preference — it is a meaningful constraint on the timeline of his negotiation, and it creates urgency that benefits teams willing to move fast.

If a lockout follows the CBA expiration, as Commissioner Manfred has pledged will happen without a new agreement, all transactions freeze. Players cannot sign. Teams cannot negotiate. The process stops entirely until a new deal is in place. The last lockout ran 99 days. A similar stoppage would push Skubal's signing to late February or March at the earliest.

Skubal wants certainty before that happens. His preferred scenario is a signed deal and a known home heading into next spring. That aligns with the interests of whatever team lands him — getting the most expensive pitcher in baseball history locked in before the market goes dark is preferable to waiting.

The Dec. 1 clock is not a neutral deadline. It favors teams that are organized, financially committed, and willing to close quickly. The Mets, as described by Heyman, appear to be all three.

What's Next

Expect this to resolve in the next two weeks. With 14 days until the CBA expires, neither Skubal nor his agent has the luxury of an extended process. Teams will make their best offers. Skubal will choose.

If the Mets land him, the rotation around Skubal becomes the most expensive in baseball. If the Dodgers flip the script, they add yet another elite starter to an already deep staff. If a third team emerges from the unnamed two in his finalist group, it would be the offseason's signature surprise.

For now, New York is the team to beat. The in-person pitch happened. The reporting points one direction. The $400 million question is when — not if — Tarik Skubal ends his free agency.
