14 Days to Sign or Wait Until Spring: The Pre-Lockout Sprint Is On

By Jake Torres | November 17, 2026

The MLB offseason has a hard stop. December 1 at 11:59 PM ET, the current Collective Bargaining Agreement expires. Commissioner Rob Manfred has pledged that a lockout follows immediately if no new deal is in place. All transactions freeze. No signings, no trades, no movement until a new agreement restores baseball to its normal operating state.

That means every team with an unsatisfied roster need has the same urgent math: 14 days to do what they can, or wait until spring.

The Biggest Names Still Available

Tarik Skubal heads the list. The best left-handed starter on the market is seeking a contract with a reported $400 million ceiling, and six teams — including the Mets and Dodgers — are still competing. Skubal has stated he wants to sign before December 1. If the Mets close as Jon Heyman suggests they are positioned to do, the sport gets its most expensive pitcher ever signed in the next two weeks. If the process extends beyond the deadline, the resolution waits until spring.

Jazz Chisholm is next. Eleven teams engaged, Blue Jays frontrunner, no qualifying offer attached. Personal meetings are starting this week. For teams that need a second baseman before spring camps open, the window to get him without a draft pick cost closes December 1 regardless of lockout status — but the ability to actually complete the transaction closes too.

Seven QO players entered unrestricted free agency today: Arozarena, Suzuki, Bednar, Rogers, Jeffers, Lowe, and Morejon. Each team that potentially signs them faces draft pick compensation — but the market for all seven will heat up fast now that they are fully free.

Then there is Yoshinobu Yamamoto. Posted by his Japanese club on November 1, Yamamoto's 45-day negotiating window closes on December 16. That window falls after the CBA expiration. There is a genuine question about whether a lockout would prevent his signing entirely, trapping the process in a legal gray area where neither the posting agreement nor a new CBA governs. Teams interested in Yamamoto — the Mets, Yankees, Dodgers, Phillies, and Red Sox have all been mentioned — have extra incentive to move quickly, but the timeline math may be inescapable regardless of urgency.

Teams Under the Most Pressure

The Cubs need a rotation. After losing Suzuki to free agency and with their starting pitching in need of a full rebuild, Chicago is one of the most active teams in the market by necessity. Names like Michael King, Freddy Peralta, and Roki Imai have all been connected to the Cubs. Getting one or more of those deals done before December 1 is a priority — the alternative is entering spring training with a half-built rotation and no transactions to make until the lockout ends.

The Orioles need starting pitching. With Trevor Rogers now fully free after declining his qualifying offer, Baltimore has a hole to fill and limited time to address it. Rogers is exactly the type of arm the Orioles have pursued in recent offseasons, and he may find Baltimore among the teams calling in the coming days.

The Yankees are replacing Chisholm. New York has already moved on from pursuing him directly and is looking at alternatives — Brandon Lowe among them. Getting that piece signed before the freeze cements their middle-infield picture for 2027. Waiting means uncertainty through a potentially long offseason pause.

The Red Sox have Bauers locked in but need starting pitcher depth. With their outfield sorted and bullpen additions like Evan Phillips (three years, $35 million) already in place, Boston's remaining need is in the rotation.

What the Sprint Looks Like

Baseball history has a reference point here. When the last lockout began in December 2021, teams rushed signings in the days leading up to the deadline — a compressed frenzy of activity that filled the transaction wire with movement normally spread across six weeks. Expect something similar over the next 14 days.

The dynamics are different this time. The free agent class is deeper at the top — Skubal represents a generational contract moment — and the labor situation is more contentious, with an owners' proposal for a salary cap that the MLBPA has flatly rejected. If the 2021 lockout lasted 99 days, there is no particular reason to believe 2026-27 would end faster.

Some teams will make their moves. Some will hedge, betting on a quick resolution. Some will get caught flat-footed. The teams that enter December 1 with their rosters substantially addressed will be better positioned for 2027 regardless of how long the stoppage lasts.

What's Next

Watch the transaction wire between now and November 30. Each deal that closes before the deadline is a team securing its position. Each team that waits is rolling the dice on a CBA resolution that has not materialized despite weeks of negotiations.

The owners meetings running through November 19 are the last major scheduled negotiating milestone before the clock runs out. If Day 3 produces no progress, the final 12 days become a true countdown. If there is a surprise breakthrough, the entire market dynamic changes — freed from the December 1 deadline, teams can breathe again.

Right now, no one is breathing. The sprint is on, and there are 14 days on the clock.
