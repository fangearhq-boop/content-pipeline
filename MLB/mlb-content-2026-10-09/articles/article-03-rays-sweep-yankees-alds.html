Rays Sweep Yankees Out of ALDS, Reach ALCS for First Time Since 2020

By Jake Torres | October 9, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Victor Mesa Jr. took the at-bat every team wants to avoid in October — a two-out, bases-loaded situation where one swing changes everything — and delivered. His two-run homer in the sixth inning on Wednesday night at Yankee Stadium put the Tampa Bay Rays ahead 4-2, and they held on for a 4-3 victory to complete a three-game sweep of the New York Yankees in the ALDS.

Tampa Bay is in the American League Championship Series for the first time since 2020. More notably, it is the first time in franchise history that the Rays have swept a postseason series of five games or more. The Yankees, for their part, have now been swept in a best-of-five series just the second time since 1980 — the first being the ALCS that year, before the wild card era of October baseball even existed.

How the Rays Did It

The sweep was not entirely clean — there was a clear fan interference call in the final game that briefly gave New York hope — but Tampa Bay's pitching set the tone throughout the series. The Rays built leads and protected them. Mesa Jr.'s home run in Game 3 was the defining blow, a two-run shot that erased a 2-2 tie and gave Tampa Bay breathing room they would not give back.

The Rays advance to the ALCS as the AL's top seed, earning home-field advantage for the series. The ALCS opener is Monday, October 12 at 8 PM ET at Tropicana Field on TBS, truTV, and HBO Max. The Rays will face either the Chicago White Sox or the Cleveland Guardians, whose Game 5 is Saturday night.

What This Means for the Yankees

For New York, the sweep continues a difficult October stretch for a franchise that expects to compete for championships. Being swept in the ALDS — at home, in Games 1-3 — is the kind of result that generates real questions about the roster's current construction. The Yankees have not won the World Series since 2009. There are no easy answers after a sweep like this.

What's Next

The Rays host the ALCS beginning Monday at Tropicana Field. Tampa Bay has shown through this ALDS that its pitching and timely hitting is more than capable of going deep into October. Game 1 of the ALCS is October 12 at 8 PM ET on TBS. The American League pennant race resumes in St. Petersburg.
