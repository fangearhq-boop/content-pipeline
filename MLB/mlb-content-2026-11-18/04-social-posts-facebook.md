### STORY 1: QO Deadline Results — Bednar and Jeffers Accept; Five Decline

**Long-Form Post (8:00 AM ET)**

The qualifying offer deadline passed at 4:00 PM ET yesterday, and the results produced the Hot Stove's first genuine surprise: Ryan Jeffers is staying with the Minnesota Twins.

Jeffers had reportedly been considering turning down Minnesota's qualifying offer to pursue a multi-year contract on the open market. Reports suggested he was looking for the kind of guaranteed security that a one-year deal can't provide. Then the deadline arrived — and he accepted the $23.15 million guarantee for 2027.

David Bednar made the other acceptance, returning to the New York Yankees on the same terms.

For the five who declined — Randy Arozarena (Mariners), Seiya Suzuki (Cubs), Brandon Lowe (Pirates), Trevor Rogers (Orioles), and Adrian Morejon (Padres) — their former teams will now receive draft compensation if they sign contracts of a certain value with new clubs. That draft pick cost adds another layer of complexity to their free agency.

What changed Jeffers' mind? Market signals, most likely. In a November where top free agents are rushing to close deals before the December 1 CBA expiration, a one-year $23 million guarantee looks better than the uncertainty of an open market that could get complicated quickly. Jeffers made the smart play.

For the five who declined, the clock is now running. They have 13 days to sign or wait out a lockout.

### STORY 2: Skubal Market — Mets Meeting Went "Very Well"

**Long-Form Post (10:30 AM ET)**

If you wanted one data point about how Tarik Skubal's free agency is going, here it is: the New York Mets flew their people to Orange County, California for an in-person pitch, and when it was over, Jon Heyman reported that the meeting "went very well."

Heyman also said the Mets are "probably the favorite."

The Los Angeles Dodgers — Skubal's incumbent team — are also meeting with him. They know what they have, and they know what it would cost to lose him. But the Mets have resources and a clear stated priority to build around elite starting pitching. Skubal is the best starting pitcher available.

The contract projections are staggering. The Athletic projected nine years and $405 million. ESPN's Kiley McDaniel put the range at $325 to $400 million. Either way, this deal will shatter the AAV record for starting pitchers — Yoshinobu Yamamoto's $27 million average is about to look like a floor, not a ceiling.

Jon Heyman expects Skubal to sign before the CBA expires on December 1. That is 13 days from now. The timeline is coming into focus. The price is extraordinary. The only remaining question is the logo on his jersey.

### STORY 3: Seiya Suzuki Declines Cubs' QO — $100M+ Market Opens

**Long-Form Post (12:30 PM ET)**

Seiya Suzuki is a free agent.

The Chicago Cubs' outfielder officially declined their qualifying offer on November 17, turning down a one-year $23.15 million guarantee in pursuit of a longer-term deal. Spotrac projects four years and $109.9 million. Reports have tied the Toronto Blue Jays as one of the frontrunners.

Here is what makes this story particularly painful for Cubs fans: Ian Happ, their left fielder, is also a free agent. The Cubs could lose both corner outfielders in the same offseason.

Add that to the rotation situation — all five of Chicago's 2026 starting pitchers are free agents — and the Cubs front office is facing one of the most complex rebuilding puzzles in baseball. General manager Jed Hoyer has publicly stated that pitching is the No. 1 priority, which is fair. But if the outfield problem doesn't get solved alongside the rotation problem, the team's offensive production will fall off a cliff.

There is still a path where Suzuki returns. His market will develop quickly, and if the price exceeds what other teams are willing to pay, the Cubs could re-engage. But the signal from every report is the same: probably not. The Cubs drafted comp pick, at minimum, is coming.

### STORY 4: Jazz Chisholm Market — Blue Jays Frontrunner

**Long-Form Post (3:00 PM ET)**

Toronto is not waiting around this offseason.

The Blue Jays met with Jazz Chisholm Jr.'s representatives at the GM Meetings, per Sportsnet's Shi Davidi, making their case for why the former Yankees second baseman should choose Canada. Meanwhile, Shi Davidi and others have continued to describe Toronto as the frontrunner in a market that Jon Morosi says involves 11 teams.

The Blue Jays' vision for Chisholm makes sense: he plays second base, with Addison Barger or Ernie Clement filling a super-utility role to cover other positions. Combined with their interest in Brandon Lowe (another middle-infield option) and their connection to Seiya Suzuki, Toronto is quietly assembling what could be the most aggressive shopping list of any team this winter.

Chisholm's 2026 numbers — .231/.312/.399 with 19 home runs and 41 stolen bases — tell a story about a player who provides impact on the basepaths and in the power game, even when the batting average disappoints. His on-base work leaves room for improvement, but at age 29, he is in the core years of his career.

The Yankees are not coming back, per Morosi. That clears the field. With personal meetings starting, the Blue Jays have an opportunity to close this quickly before December 1 — and they appear motivated to do so.

### STORY 5: CBA Countdown — 13 Days, Lockout Looking Inevitable

**Long-Form Post (6:00 PM ET)**

Thirteen days. That is how long baseball has before the collective bargaining agreement expires and the sport enters what could be a prolonged labor work stoppage.

The MLB owners meetings are underway in New York through Thursday, and the working assumption around baseball is that a lockout will begin at 12:01 AM ET on December 2. The sides remain deeply at odds.

MLB's last substantive offer — presented November 3 — proposed a $258.2 million salary cap with the hard number delayed until 2028. The MLB Players Association rejected it almost immediately. The union has refused any form of a salary cap, and that position has not changed despite weeks of meetings.

For historical context: the last MLB lockout ran from December 2021 to March 2022 — 99 days — and threatened to cancel the beginning of the season. It ended with a compromise CBA. This situation appears more entrenched.

What does this mean for free agents and teams? The next 13 days are the entire free-agent window. Deals that might normally stretch into January must be closed now. That is why Skubal's situation is so urgent. It is why the QO results matter more than usual. It is why teams like Toronto are moving on multiple fronts simultaneously.

December 1 is a wall. Baseball knows it. The sprint is on.
