Jazz Chisholm Market: Blue Jays Frontrunner as 11 Teams Engage — Toronto's Aggressive Hot Stove

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | November 18, 2026

Toronto is not waiting. The Blue Jays met with Jazz Chisholm Jr.'s representatives at the GM Meetings in Las Vegas, per Sportsnet's Shi Davidi, putting a formal face on the interest that has been building for weeks. Jon Morosi of MLB Network has reported that 11 teams are engaged in discussions with Chisholm's camp, with personal meetings beginning soon — and the Blue Jays remain the frontrunner.

No qualifying offer was attached to Chisholm when he departed the New York Yankees, which means any team that signs him owes no draft compensation to New York. That removes one of the standard friction points that complicate marquee free-agent acquisitions.

What Toronto Sees in Chisholm

The Blue Jays' organizational vision for Chisholm has been reported by The Score: he plays second base, with Addison Barger or Ernie Clement filling super-utility roles elsewhere in the infield. It is a construction that makes sense for Toronto, which has needed a reliable, impact second baseman and found that Chisholm fits both the profile and the ballpark.

Chisholm's 2026 numbers — .231/.312/.399 with 19 home runs and 41 stolen bases — reveal a player who creates value through baserunning impact and power more than batting average. The .231 is below average. The 41 stolen bases is elite. The 19 home runs in a down offensive year show real power capability. For Toronto, a lineup that has sometimes struggled to generate extra-base threats, Chisholm's ceiling adds something they have been missing.

Toronto's Broader Blueprint

What makes the Chisholm pursuit interesting in context is that it is not Toronto's only move. The Blue Jays are connected to Brandon Lowe as an additional middle-infield option. They have been reported as a frontrunner for Seiya Suzuki. They are aggressively building out a roster that has significant positional needs entering 2027.

The pattern suggests a front office that has concluded this is the winter to spend. The AL East is competitive — the Yankees, Red Sox, and Orioles all have resources and needs — and the Blue Jays appear to have decided that incremental improvements are not sufficient. Chisholm plus Suzuki plus Lowe would represent a substantial offensive overhaul.

The CBA expiration on December 1 adds urgency. All three targets — Chisholm, Suzuki, and Lowe — are free agents who need to sign before the freeze. Toronto has 13 days to execute its vision or wait out a lockout of uncertain length.

The Competition

Eleven teams is a large market for any free agent. The specifics of who the other 10 teams are has not been fully reported, but expect AL contenders who need middle-infield upgrades to be among them. The Padres have also been connected to Chisholm.

The Yankees, notably, are not returning, per Morosi. That is significant — Chisholm made an impact in New York and there was speculation about a reunion — but the Yankees appear to be moving in a different direction at second base.

What's Next

Personal meetings were described as beginning soon. That means Chisholm will be sitting across from Blue Jays decision-makers and hearing their case in full detail over the coming days. Toronto's history of being aggressive in those meetings — and their market size relative to some of the other 11 teams — gives them structural advantages.

Watch for a Chisholm signing in the next week to ten days. If Toronto closes this deal while also landing one of Suzuki or Lowe, the Blue Jays will enter 2027 as a team that has dramatically reshaped its offensive core during one of baseball's most compressed hot stove periods in memory.
