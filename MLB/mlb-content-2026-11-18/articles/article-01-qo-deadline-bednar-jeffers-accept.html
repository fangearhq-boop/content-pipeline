QO Deadline Results: Bednar and Jeffers Accept; Arozarena, Suzuki, Lowe, Rogers, Morejon Decline

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | November 18, 2026

The November 17 qualifying offer deadline passed at 4:00 PM ET, and baseball got one genuine surprise: Ryan Jeffers is staying in Minnesota.

The Twins catcher had reportedly been exploring the open market, looking for the kind of multi-year security that a qualifying offer — by definition a one-year deal — cannot provide. When the deadline arrived, Jeffers accepted the $23.15 million guarantee for 2027. Yankees reliever David Bednar made the other acceptance.

The five who declined — Randy Arozarena (Mariners), Seiya Suzuki (Cubs), Brandon Lowe (Pirates), Trevor Rogers (Orioles), and Adrian Morejon (Padres) — are now free agents, with draft compensation attached to any team that signs them.

The Jeffers Surprise

Reports in the days leading up to the deadline had Jeffers targeting a multi-year contract. MLBTR had reported he declined a multi-year extension offer from the Twins in order to test the market. The calculus changed.

What drives a player to accept a qualifying offer after exploring alternatives? Market signals matter. With the December 1 CBA expiration looming, free agents who wait risk entering a lockout market where activity freezes until the two sides reach a new agreement. The last lockout ran 99 days. A one-year $23 million guarantee that keeps Jeffers in a known organization, with a chance to re-enter the market next year at age 28, may ultimately represent the more pragmatic path.

Bednar's acceptance was less unexpected. The right-hander had a strong case for returning to New York, and $23.15 million for a proven late-inning arm with the Yankees is a sensible outcome for both sides.

The Five Who Said No

Arozarena, Suzuki, Lowe, Rogers, and Morejon all hit the open market. Each declined knowing their former team will receive draft compensation if they sign a qualifying contract with a new club. That draft pick cost — ranging from a first-round selection to a Competitive Balance Round pick, depending on contract size — is the price teams pay to sign them.

Arozarena is the most compelling outfield option. After his postseason breakouts earlier in his career and continued production in Seattle, he is one of the more dynamic players on this market. His defensive versatility adds value beyond the bat.

Suzuki's departure from Chicago becomes its own story. The Cubs also have Ian Happ as a free agent, leaving the organization with two corner outfield vacancies alongside its full five-man rotation need. The North Side has significant roster reconstruction ahead.

Lowe's market is an interesting one. The switch-hitter provides second base and DH flexibility for the right organization. The Blue Jays are connected to both Lowe and Jazz Chisholm, suggesting a middle-infield upgrade is high on Toronto's priority list.

Draft Compensation Implications

Teams receiving compensation picks are not disadvantaged in the typical sense — they're getting an additional draft asset for losing a player they offered to retain. For the Mariners (Arozarena), Cubs (Suzuki), Pirates (Lowe), Orioles (Rogers), and Padres (Morejon), the comp picks soften the sting of losing quality players.

For teams pursuing those players, the draft pick attached to a signing is a known cost that factors into negotiations. Teams below certain revenue thresholds can sign QO-declined players without forfeiting picks — a nuance that shapes which organizations pursue each name aggressively.

What's Next

With 13 days until the CBA expires, the five declined players need to find homes quickly. Suzuki and Arozarena are the most likely to generate immediate bidding wars. Lowe should attract multi-year interest from teams needing infield versatility. Rogers gives rotations a potential mid-rotation starter with upside.

Jeffers and Bednar both return to their teams, adding a small measure of roster stability for the Twins and Yankees while both clubs continue their winter plans. For Jeffers especially, the decision to accept may look prescient if the lockout extends into the new year and free agency stalls out.

December 1 remains the operational deadline for everything in baseball right now.
