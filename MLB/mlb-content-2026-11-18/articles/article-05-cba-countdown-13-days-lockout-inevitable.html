CBA Countdown: 13 Days Until the Freeze, Owners Meetings Underway, Lockout Expected December 2

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | November 18, 2026

Major League Baseball's owners are meeting in New York today, tomorrow, and Thursday. The collective bargaining agreement expires December 1 at 11:59 PM ET. And based on everything reported from the ongoing negotiations, a lockout beginning at 12:01 AM on December 2 is the working assumption around the sport.

Thirteen days. That is what baseball has left before the freeze.

Where Things Stand at the Bargaining Table

The core dispute remains unchanged. MLB ownership wants a salary cap. The MLB Players Association refuses to accept one. That structural disagreement has not moved in weeks of meetings.

MLB's latest formal proposal, presented November 3, offered a $258.2 million salary cap with the hard number delayed until 2028. The union rejected it almost immediately. Players have been clear: a salary cap is not something they will agree to, regardless of the number attached to it or the timeline for implementation.

The owners meetings running this week are not expected to produce a breakthrough at the bargaining table. The focus is reportedly on coordinating lockout protocols — the administrative and operational steps an organization takes when transactions are frozen and the season is in jeopardy.

What a Lockout Means for Baseball

A lockout is management's tool, not labor's. When the lockout begins, MLB prevents players and teams from engaging in any transactions — no signings, no trades, no contract extensions, no minor league deals. The machinery of roster construction stops.

The last time baseball went through this was December 2021 through March 2022. That lockout ran 99 days and threatened to cancel the beginning of the 2022 regular season. It ended with a compromise CBA — but not before significant disruption to spring training and legitimate concern about the season starting on time.

This situation appears more entrenched. The 2021–2022 dispute featured multiple specific issues that could be negotiated through. The current dispute centers on a structural question about whether baseball will have a salary cap — a yes-or-no issue where both sides have dug in.

The union could also elect to decertify during a lockout, which would strip the MLB Players Association of its collective bargaining authority and allow individual players to challenge the league's antitrust exemption in federal court. Decertification is a nuclear option, but its possibility changes the calculus for both sides.

Why This Is the Most Important November in Baseball in Years

Free agency typically runs from November through February and beyond. Teams have time to evaluate, negotiate, and close deals at their own pace. This November is different.

Every transaction that happens before December 1 matters more than it normally would. Tarik Skubal needs to sign now or risk waiting out a lockout of unknown length. Randy Arozarena, Seiya Suzuki, Jazz Chisholm, Brandon Lowe — all of them are racing the same clock. Teams with rotation needs, outfield needs, and bullpen needs are compressing a normal four-month process into 13 days.

That compression creates inefficiencies. Teams may overpay for certainty. Players may accept less than they'd earn in a full market to avoid the lockout. It is not a clean market — it is a market under time pressure, and time pressure changes behavior.

What's Next

The owners meetings conclude Thursday. Expect reporting from those sessions to either confirm the lockout timeline or introduce unexpected developments if the two sides agree to resume substantive negotiations.

November 30 — the last day before the CBA expires — will be the most active day in baseball transactions in decades. Every deal that hasn't closed by then will close then, or wait months.

Thirteen days. The sprint is at full speed.
