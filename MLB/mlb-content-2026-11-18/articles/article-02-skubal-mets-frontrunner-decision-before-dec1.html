Skubal Market: Mets Meeting "Went Very Well," Decision Expected Before CBA Deadline

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | November 18, 2026

The biggest pitching deal in baseball history is approaching. Jon Heyman reported that the New York Mets' in-person meeting with Tarik Skubal in Orange County, California "went very well," and that the Mets are "probably the favorite" to sign the two-time Cy Young Award winner. Jon Heyman also expects Skubal to sign before the collective bargaining agreement expires on December 1.

That is 13 days from now.

The Los Angeles Dodgers — Skubal's incumbent team — are also meeting with him. They know what they have, and what a Skubal departure would mean to their rotation. But with Steve Cohen's resources and a clear organizational priority on elite starting pitching, the Mets have positioned themselves at the front of the most consequential free-agent bid in recent baseball memory.

The Contract That Would Rewrite History

The projections are staggering. The Athletic projected nine years and $405 million. ESPN's Kiley McDaniel put the range at $325 to $400 million. Either figure would obliterate the existing record for average annual value in a starting pitcher contract — Yoshinobu Yamamoto's $27 million AAV from his deal with the Dodgers.

At the lower end of the projections, Skubal's deal would average roughly $36 million per year. At the Athletic's projection, it would average $45 million — a figure that two years ago would have seemed impossible. The market has moved. Skubal is the best pitcher in baseball on a deal that will likely run through his late 30s, and the winning team is betting on sustained dominance.

No qualifying offer was attached to Skubal, which means no draft compensation is owed by the winning team — removing one of the typical friction points in marquee free-agent negotiations.

Mets vs. Dodgers

Heyman named the Mets as "probably the favorite" after the Orange County meeting. One MLB.com report said "nearly everybody in the game expects Skubal to sign with the Mets." A conflicting report suggested widespread belief that Skubal prefers to remain with the Dodgers.

The honest answer is that nobody outside Skubal's circle knows. What is clear is that the Mets flew their decision-makers to California for an in-person pitch and came out of the room encouraged. That is meaningful. Organizations do not fly across the country for meetings that go poorly.

The Dodgers' case rests on familiarity, a championship-caliber roster, and the proven track record of deploying elite pitching well. Their pitching development program is the gold standard. But the Mets under Cohen have demonstrated that money is not an obstacle — and if Skubal's priority is simply to maximize his contract, the Mets can match whatever the Dodgers offer.

The December 1 Factor

The CBA expiration at 11:59 PM ET on December 1 is not an abstract deadline for Skubal's market — it is a defining one. Once the lockout begins, all transactions freeze. Skubal cannot sign until a new CBA is in place. The last lockout lasted 99 days.

Heyman's report that Skubal expects to sign before December 1 aligns with what any rational party would want. Both Skubal and his eventual team have strong incentives to close the deal now. For Skubal, it provides security and continuity. For the winning team, it locks in the best free-agent pitcher before the market freezes.

If the two sides cannot reach agreement before the deadline, Skubal enters a lockout market where the timeline stretches indefinitely. That scenario benefits no one.

What's Next

Meetings will continue. Heyman's reporting suggests the Mets are in a strong position. The Dodgers will not go quietly. At least four other teams are part of the process, some of whom have not been publicly identified.

Watch for the announcement to come sometime in the next week to ten days. When it does, it will not just set a new record — it will reset what the baseball world considers possible in a pitcher contract. The age of the $400 million starting pitcher may be arriving in November 2026.
