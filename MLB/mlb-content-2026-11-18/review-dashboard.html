QO Deadline Results — Bednar and Jeffers Accept; Five Decline
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post The qualifying offer deadline passed at 4:00 PM ET yesterday, and the results produced the Hot Stove's first genuine surprise: Ryan Jeffers is staying with the Minnesota Twins. Jeffers had reportedly been considering turning down Minnesota's qualifying offer to pursue a multi-year contract on the open market. Reports suggested he was looking for the kind of guaranteed security that a one-year deal can't provide. Then the deadline arrived — and he accepted the $23.15 million guarantee for 2027. David Bednar made the other acceptance, returning to the New York Yankees on the same terms. For the five who declined — Randy Arozarena (Mariners), Seiya Suzuki (Cubs), Brandon Lowe (Pirates), Trevor Rogers (Orioles), and Adrian Morejon (Padres) — their former teams will now receive draft compensation if they sign contracts of a certain value with new clubs. That draft pick cost adds another layer of complexity to their free agency. What changed Jeffers' mind? Market signals, most likely. In a November where top free agents are rushing to close deals before the December 1 CBA expiration, a one-year $23 million guarantee looks better than the uncertainty of an open market that could get complicated quickly. Jeffers made the smart play. For the five who declined, the clock is now running. They have 13 days to sign or wait out a lockout. ### Image Post Caption QO deadline results: Bednar ✅ Jeffers ✅ (surprise). Arozarena, Suzuki, Lowe, Rogers, Morejon all head to open market. 13 days until the CBA expires. #MLB #HotStove

### Long-Form Post Seiya Suzuki is a free agent. The Chicago Cubs' outfielder officially declined their qualifying offer on November 17, turning down a one-year $23.15 million guarantee in pursuit of a longer-term deal. Spotrac projects four years and $109.9 million. Reports have tied the Toronto Blue Jays as one of the frontrunners. Here is what makes this story particularly painful for Cubs fans: Ian Happ, their left fielder, is also a free agent. The Cubs could lose both corner outfielders in the same offseason. Add that to the rotation situation — all five of Chicago's 2026 starting pitchers are free agents — and the Cubs front office is facing one of the most complex rebuilding puzzles in baseball. General manager Jed Hoyer has publicly stated that pitching is the No. 1 priority, which is fair. But if the outfield problem doesn't get solved alongside the rotation problem, the team's offensive production will fall off a cliff. There is still a path where Suzuki returns. His market will develop quickly, and if the price exceeds what other teams are willing to pay, the Cubs could re-engage. But the signal from every report is the same: probably not. The Cubs drafted comp pick, at minimum, is coming. ### Image Post Caption Seiya Suzuki officially declined the Cubs' qualifying offer. 4yr/$109.9M projected. Blue Jays among frontrunners. CBA Countdown — 13 Days, Lockout Looks Inevitable
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Thirteen days. That is how long baseball has before the collective bargaining agreement expires and the sport enters what could be a prolonged labor work stoppage. The MLB owners meetings are underway in New York through Thursday, and the working assumption around baseball is that a lockout will begin at 12:01 AM ET on December 2. The sides remain deeply at odds. MLB's last substantive offer — presented November 3 — proposed a $258.2 million salary cap with the hard number delayed until 2028. The MLB Players Association rejected it almost immediately. The union has refused any form of a salary cap, and that position has not changed despite weeks of meetings. For historical context: the last MLB lockout ran from December 2021 to March 2022 — 99 days — and threatened to cancel the beginning of the season. It ended with a compromise CBA. This situation appears more entrenched. What does this mean for free agents and teams? The next 13 days are the entire free-agent window. Deals that might normally stretch into January must be closed now. That is why Skubal's situation is so urgent. It is why the QO results matter more than usual. It is why teams like Toronto are moving on multiple fronts simultaneously. December 1 is a wall. Baseball knows it. The sprint is on. ### Image Post Caption 13 days until the CBA expires. Owners meetings are happening now. Last lockout lasted 99 days. The sprint to Dec. 1 is at peak intensity. Every signing this week counts. #MLB #HotStove
