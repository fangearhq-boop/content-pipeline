Three Days After Making History, Joshua Baez Faces the Real Test in Cincinnati

By Jake Torres | August 18, 2026

Ballpark Banter / FanGear HQ

Every pitcher in the National League has now had 72 hours to study Joshua Báez.

That is both the blessing and the curse of becoming the most talked-about player in baseball. On August 15, the St. Louis Cardinals outfielder made history at Wrigley Field by homering in each of his first three career at-bats — the first player in Major League Baseball history to accomplish that. He went 3-for-4 with five RBI. His first home run traveled 449 feet to dead center. Thirty family members made the trip from the Dominican Republic to watch.

Tonight, in Cincinnati, it is game four of his career. The book is out. The adjustments are coming.

Who Is Joshua Báez?

Before the debut, the baseball world largely knew Báez as an aggressive power-hitting outfielder who had torched Triple-A pitching in 2026 to the tune of 34 home runs before the call-up. At 25, he was past prospect age but had put together a season so dominant that the Cardinals had no choice but to promote him.

He arrived in St. Louis as an outfielder with elite raw power and questions about his approach at the plate — questions that the August 15 game did nothing to answer definitively. Three pitches, three swings, three home runs. That tells you about bat speed and power. It does not tell you what a pitcher discovers when they come back with an off-speed pitch at the knees in the third start.

The Cardinals, looking at a rebuilding 2026 season, had nothing to lose by calling him up. Now they have something to evaluate through the final 44 games that could define their entire 2027 offseason plan.

The Adjustment Problem

Every young power hitter goes through this moment. The first look is always the hardest to defend against — no scouting report, no tendencies, no pattern of at-bats to study. By game four, opposing pitchers will have tried something. They will have found a way to make him uncomfortable.

The question is how Báez responds. The best power hitters in MLB history have all gone through this initial fire. What separates the ones who stay is what they do after the adjustment.

What's Next

Tonight's game at Cincinnati is Báez's fourth in the majors. How he performs over the next two weeks — against the full range of what National League pitching can throw at him — will tell the Cardinals and the rest of baseball more about who he is than his historic debut ever could. Watch the at-bat quality, not just the results. The real evaluation has begun.
