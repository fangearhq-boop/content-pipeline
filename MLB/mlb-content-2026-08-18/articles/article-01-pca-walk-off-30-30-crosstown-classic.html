Pete Crow-Armstrong Walks Off White Sox With 30th Homer — Hits 30-30 Club for Second Straight Year

By Marcus Cole | August 18, 2026

Ballpark Banter / FanGear HQ

CHICAGO — Pete Crow-Armstrong has a flair for the dramatic, and on Monday night at Wrigley Field, he delivered the biggest moment of the Crosstown Classic.

The Cubs center fielder led off the bottom of the first inning with his 29th home run of the season, setting the tone for a wild evening against the White Sox. Then, with the game tied at five in the 10th inning, Crow-Armstrong put Wrigley into full frenzy with a walk-off shot that became his 30th of the year — giving Chicago a 7-5 victory in Game 2 of the three-game series.

In one night, Pete Crow-Armstrong secured his second consecutive membership in the 30-30 club. He also has 31 stolen bases.

A Night for the History Books

The accomplishments stacked up in real time on Monday. Crow-Armstrong became the first Cubs player in franchise history to record consecutive 30-30 seasons — a feat that had never been accomplished in more than a century of Cubs baseball. He also joined an exclusive list as the 19th player in Major League Baseball history to post multiple 30-30 campaigns.

And in a stat line straight out of trivia history, Crow-Armstrong is now just the seventh player ever to hit both a leadoff and a walk-off home run in the same game.

He did all of that in one night, in a game that mattered, against a White Sox team chasing the AL Central title.

The Walk-Off Moment

The game had been back and forth all night. Chicago erased deficits and the White Sox responded. When Crow-Armstrong stepped to the plate in the 10th, Wrigley was loud in the way it only gets in big moments. He drove the ball to right field — his 30th home run of the year — and the Cubs dugout emptied.

The 7-5 win moved the Cubs to 73-53, maintaining their NL Wild Card No. 1 position, five games ahead of the Philadelphia Phillies. The White Sox fell to 65-59 in the AL Central, still leading their division but watching the lead trim slightly.

What's Next

Crosstown Classic Game 3 is tonight at Wrigley. For PCA, the 30-30 milestone is in the rearview mirror, and the conversation is already shifting to what he can achieve in the final 44 games. His MVP candidacy is real, his defense in center remains elite, and the Cubs are playing October baseball. For the White Sox, they have to respond in Game 3 to protect their AL Central position heading into the final stretch.
