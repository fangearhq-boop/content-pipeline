MLB Playoff Picture: 44 Games Left, Two Chicago Teams Heading to October

By Jake Torres | August 18, 2026

Ballpark Banter / FanGear HQ

With roughly 44 games left in the 2026 regular season, the Major League Baseball playoff picture is beginning to sharpen into something approaching a final form.

At the top of the standings sits the Milwaukee Brewers at 77-48 — the best record in all of baseball. In Chicago, for the first time in years, both the Cubs and the White Sox are sitting comfortably in playoff positions. And in Houston, one of the most decorated franchises of the last decade is fighting for its life in the AL West.

Here is where the race stands as of August 18.

American League

The Tampa Bay Rays lead the AL East at 75-49, having built one of the most consistent records in the American League all season. The New York Yankees (69-55) and Boston Red Sox (67-58) are battling for the two AL Wild Card spots — a rivalry-within-a-rivalry for October berths.

In the AL Central, the Chicago White Sox (65-59) hold a lead that is not as comfortable as it once was, but they remain in front. The White Sox entered the year with legitimate playoff expectations and have delivered. They dropped Crosstown Classic Game 2 on Monday — Pete Crow-Armstrong's walk-off was the difference — but the division lead remains intact.

The most vulnerable division leader is the Houston Astros in the AL West at 63-62. They are barely above .500, holding the division by a razor's edge against a bunched field. The Astros have the roster and the organizational history to survive September — but they will need to perform better than they have.

National League

The Milwaukee Brewers (77-48) are running away with the NL Central and baseball's best record overall. The Atlanta Braves (74-51) lead the NL East and have been consistent all season. The Los Angeles Dodgers (75-51) control the NL West in the way the Dodgers tend to — methodically, with depth.

In the NL Wild Card race, the Chicago Cubs (73-53) hold the No. 1 spot with a five-game lead over the Philadelphia Phillies (68-58). The San Diego Padres (67-59) are one game back of Philadelphia in the fight for the third NL Wild Card slot.

The Cubs' NL Wild Card position means they would host a Wild Card series if the bracket holds. After Pete Crow-Armstrong's walk-off on Monday, the North Side of Chicago is feeling good about where this team is headed.

What's Next

The next three weeks will likely determine the playoff bracket in its near-final form. Watch the Astros — a team that slips further below .500 in August is increasingly dependent on September magic. Watch the NL Wild Card race, where two games separate the Cubs, Phillies, and Padres in the two-through-three slots. And watch Milwaukee, which is making a case to be taken seriously as a World Series contender. October is coming fast.
