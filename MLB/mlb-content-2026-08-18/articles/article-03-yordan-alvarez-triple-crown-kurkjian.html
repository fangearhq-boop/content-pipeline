Tim Kurkjian Says Yordan Alvarez Is Winning the Triple Crown — The Numbers Back Him Up

By Ryan Calloway | August 18, 2026

Ballpark Banter / FanGear HQ

When Tim Kurkjian says something quietly declarative about baseball, the sport tends to listen.

This week, ESPN's longtime baseball analyst made his position on Yordan Álvarez and the 2026 Triple Crown chase clear: "At this point, I think he's going to win the Triple Crown."

The numbers make that statement hard to argue with. Álvarez enters this week with a .329 batting average, 36 home runs, and 88 RBI — leading Major League Baseball in all three Triple Crown categories. The last player to lead all three at this point in the season and not win the Triple Crown had a very bad September.

Breaking Down Each Category

Batting Average (.329): Álvarez's lead in batting average is described by analysts as "essentially insurmountable." He is more than .029 points ahead of the next closest competitor. For a player to overtake him at this point, they would need to sustain a historically unusual hot streak while Álvarez slumped. Neither is impossible, but together they are exceedingly unlikely.

RBI (88): His RBI lead is a comfortable seven runs. With 44 games remaining and Álvarez hitting in the middle of a productive Houston lineup, that margin is likely to hold or grow. RBI leaders in mid-August historically retain their lead the vast majority of the time.

Home Runs (36): This is the only category where the outcome is genuinely uncertain. Kyle Schwarber sits at 37 home runs — one ahead of Álvarez — and Junior Caminero has 33, making the three-way race the most competitive element of the entire Triple Crown chase. Álvarez needs to catch Schwarber while keeping Caminero at bay. One swing closes that gap entirely.

Historical Context

The last player to win the Triple Crown in Major League Baseball was Miguel Cabrera, who hit .330 with 44 home runs and 139 RBI for the Detroit Tigers in 2012. Before Cabrera, the previous winner was Carl Yastrzemski of the Boston Red Sox in 1967. The Triple Crown is not simply rare — it is among the most difficult individual accomplishments in baseball because it requires simultaneously being the best hitter, the best power hitter, and the best run producer in the entire league.

Álvarez has legitimate claims on all three simultaneously. The home run race is tight, but with his swing and his lineup, he has every opportunity to take the lead before September ends.

What's Next

Every Álvarez at-bat is now appointment viewing for a different reason than usual. With each home run, he puts pressure on Schwarber to respond. Follow the HR leaderboard daily — when Álvarez reaches 37 and ties Schwarber, the Triple Crown conversation will reach its loudest moment of the year. September is coming.
