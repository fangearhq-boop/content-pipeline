Jackson Chourio Is 22 Years Old and One Home Run From 60 for His Career

By Jake Torres

August 30, 2026

Jackson Chourio is 22 years old. He was born on March 11, 2004. And heading into Sunday's games, he sits at 59 career home runs — one away from a milestone that only a handful of players in baseball history have reached before their 23rd birthday.

The Milwaukee Brewers outfielder has been one of the most compelling young power stories in the game this season, and his proximity to 60 career home runs is the latest chapter in a career that keeps exceeding timelines.

The Power That Got Him Here

Chourio has five career multi-home run games — three of them in 2026 alone. His most recent came August 27 against the New York Mets: a 4-for-5, two-home run, four-RBI, four-run performance that showcased the full package. He hit his first home run in the game off a starting pitcher's first-pitch heater, then added a second deep into the eighth inning off the bullpen — both hit with authority.

That ability to hit home runs in multiple at-bats on the same night, against different pitchers and different pitch types, is what separates Chourio from the typical young power bat. His power is not situational. It travels.

The Historical Context

Reaching 60 career home runs before age 23 places a player in conversation with some of the most prolific young hitters in baseball history. The players who accumulated power at this kind of rate at this age are a short list — most significant home run totals at young ages belong to players who debuted earlier than the typical prospect or who had exceptional first full seasons.

Chourio's path has been steady rather than explosive in any single season, which makes the career total even more remarkable. He has not had one outlier year carrying the number — he has built it through consistent production across multiple seasons in a Brewers lineup that is not always designed to maximize individual offensive output.

What's Next

Home run No. 60 has not arrived yet — but at Chourio's current pace and with weeks remaining in the regular season, it is a matter of when, not whether. When it does arrive, it will mark one of the significant individual milestones of the 2026 MLB season and one of the most meaningful markers of what could be a generational offensive career. Watch his at-bats closely in the coming days. The swing that puts him over 60 will be one worth catching in real time.
