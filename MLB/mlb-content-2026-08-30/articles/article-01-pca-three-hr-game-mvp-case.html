Pete Crow-Armstrong's Three-HR Night Puts His NL MVP Case in Historic Company

By Marcus Cole

August 30, 2026

If there was a moment in the 2026 season that settled the NL MVP race, Saturday night at Wrigley Field was it.

Pete Crow-Armstrong hit three home runs and drove in six runs as the Chicago Cubs demolished the Cincinnati Reds 17-5. The Cubs move to 77-59. And PCA's month of August now occupies a place in baseball history that only three players in the last 50 years can claim alongside him.

He is the fourth player since 1976 to post at least 12 home runs, 25 RBI, and six stolen bases in a single calendar month. The others: Ryne Sandberg in 1990, Eric Davis in 1987, and Bobby Bonds in 1977.

A Month Unlike Any Other

The numbers don't fully describe what August has looked like. PCA has combined his power output with the baserunning threat that has defined his entire season — and he has done it consistently, not in clusters. Saturday's three-homer performance was the exclamation point, but it was built on a foundation of week-after-week production that no other NL position player has matched.

The NL MVP odds reflect it. PCA sits at -1100 following Saturday's game. Shohei Ohtani is at +650.

That gap narrowed through August by the week, but Ohtani's candidacy has faced one significant obstacle: he has not pitched in 2026, limiting his ability to differentiate himself from PCA on the two-way value front. CBS Sports and Yardbarker both framed Saturday's performance as potentially the race-defining moment of the season.

The Remaining Wild Card

MLB insider Jon Heyman issued a notable warning through Yahoo Sports: Ohtani is expected to return to the mound in early September. If he pitches — and pitches well — the MVP narrative could shift in the final weeks. Voters weight two-way performance heavily, and a dominant September from the mound could reframe the conversation in a way that pure hitting numbers might not be able to counter.

That is the one legitimate threat to PCA's case. Everything else favors him. His 7.7 fWAR leads the league. His 32 stolen bases pair with 33 home runs in what is the best Cubs back-to-back 30-30 season in franchise history. And the Cubs — at 77-59 with 30 games left — are in a strong Wild Card position, which matters to voters who weigh team success alongside individual production.

What's Next

The Cubs continue their home stand at Wrigley Field through the final week of August before hitting the road in September. Jacob Misiorowski — Milwaukee's ace and NL Cy Young frontrunner — is projected to start September 2 against Chicago at Wrigley, setting up one of the most anticipated matchups of the final month of the regular season. PCA will look to keep his historic August rolling into a decisive September.
