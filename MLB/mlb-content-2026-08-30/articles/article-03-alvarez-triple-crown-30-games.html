Yordan Alvarez Leads All Three Triple Crown Categories With 30 Games Left

By Ryan Calloway

August 30, 2026

Yordan Alvarez is still in front. Thirty games remain, and the Houston Astros' designated hitter leads the American League in batting average, home runs, and runs batted in — the combination that, if he holds it through game 162, would make him the first Triple Crown winner in the AL since Miguel Cabrera in 2012.

The numbers as of August 30: approximately .320 batting average, 36 home runs, and more than 90 RBI. Alvarez leads all three categories. The question is whether he can maintain that lead for one more month.

The Tightest Race: Home Runs

The batting average and RBI leads are relatively comfortable. The home run race is not. Junior Caminero of the Tampa Bay Rays sits at 35 home runs — one behind Alvarez. One swing from Caminero on any given night can tie the most visible Triple Crown category, and the separation Alvarez needs to hold is thin enough that a cold week or a Caminero surge could flip the standings before September ends.

Alvarez's home run pace has been steady across the entire season, but one source noted he has hit only one home run in August — making the home run lead the category most at risk of disappearing if he does not get hot again in September. A Triple Crown is won across the full season, but the final month is where the margin is made or lost.

What a Triple Crown Means

The last player to win the American League Triple Crown was Miguel Cabrera of the Detroit Tigers in 2012. Before Cabrera, the most recent AL Triple Crown winner was Carl Yastrzemski in 1967 — a 45-year gap between achievements. That context tells you how rare this is. Winning all three categories in a 162-game season requires not just sustained excellence but a degree of fortune across an enormous number of variables.

Alvarez's current lead in all three categories is the product of a full-season effort. He does not need to be the best in any single category in September — he needs to defend what he has already built.

What's Next

The AL Triple Crown race will be watched daily as September opens. Alvarez has 30 games to protect leads of varying sizes. The home run race — with Caminero one back — will be the one to track most closely. Keep an eye on whether Alvarez returns to his earlier HR pace in September after a slower August in that category. If he does, the Triple Crown becomes significantly more likely. If Caminero gets hot, the lead could flip before the final week.
