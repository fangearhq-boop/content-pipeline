### STORY 2: ALCS Game 2 Recap

#### FB Long-Form Post — 11:00 AM ET

It started with a ground ball that hit the wrong seam of turf.

In the fourth inning of ALCS Game 2 at Tropicana Field, with the game scoreless, a grounder bounced off the dirt-turf seam and rolled right through Jose Ramirez's legs. Manager Stephen Vogt said Ramirez didn't move his feet. Ramirez said the ball hit the dirt. Either way, the door was open.

Liam Hicks followed with a 2-run single. Three unearned runs scored. That's all Tampa Bay needed.

Freddy Peralta — a midseason Rays acquisition — threw six scoreless innings, striking out six, allowing four hits. Parker Messick started for Cleveland and worked five innings (83 pitches), the first time this postseason he's gone that deep. But the Guardians couldn't score more than once.

Rays 3, Guardians 1. Series tied 1-1. Jose Ramirez — who was the hero of Game 1 and came back from a hamate surgery to lead Cleveland's ALDS comeback — is now the central figure of his team's first series loss.

Baseball is cruel. And beautiful.

The Tampa Bay Rays are doing this without their best pitcher.

Shane McClanahan, the Rays' ace, is out for the rest of the postseason with a forearm injury. When a staff ace goes down in October, the typical reaction is that a team's chances go down with him.

Not this Tampa Bay squad.

Freddy Peralta — acquired midseason — threw six scoreless innings in ALCS Game 2, striking out six in the most important start of his Rays career. The offense scored three (two of them unearned, but runs count either way). The series is tied 1-1 heading to Cleveland.

The Rays have been built for exactly this kind of adversity. Thin, fast, relentless. Now they're heading to Progressive Field with momentum and a starter who just announced himself on the biggest stage.

McClanahan's absence changes the rotation math for the rest of the ALCS. It might not change the outcome.
