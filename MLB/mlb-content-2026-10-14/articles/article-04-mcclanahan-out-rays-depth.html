McClanahan Is Out for the Postseason — Can the Rays' Depth Carry Them to the World Series?

By Marcus Cole | October 14, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The Tampa Bay Rays are doing this without their ace.

Shane McClanahan — the anchor of Tampa Bay's starting rotation — is out for the rest of the 2026 postseason with a forearm injury. His absence would normally represent a season-defining blow. Instead, the Rays are 1-1 in the ALCS, just won Game 2 on the back of a Freddy Peralta gem, and appear to be doing fine.

This is what Tampa Bay was built for.

What McClanahan Meant

McClanahan was a central figure in Tampa Bay's rotation plan entering October. He is one of the best left-handed starters in the American League — the kind of arm that changes series and forces lineup adjustments from opposing managers. Losing him to a forearm injury before the postseason is the kind of setback that reshapes October plans.

His absence does not show up in the box score. But it is present in every pitching decision the Rays make. Every inning that Peralta throws, every high-leverage bullpen situation that Kevin Cash navigates, is shaped by the fact that McClanahan is not available to absorb innings and shoulder the rotation burden he was expected to carry.

Peralta Steps In

The Rays acquired Freddy Peralta during the season, and in Game 2 of the ALCS at Tropicana Field, he delivered the most important start of his Tampa Bay career. Six innings. Zero earned runs. Four hits. Two walks. Six strikeouts. The performance was the kind of execution that changes a series — it showed Cleveland a different kind of arm than Peralta's reputation might have suggested, and it won the Rays the game.

The depth calculus in Tampa Bay's rotation has shifted significantly since the regular season ended. Peralta is now carrying responsibilities that McClanahan would otherwise share. The rest of the staff — and the bullpen — absorb the residual load. So far, it is working.

The Rays' Blueprint

Tampa Bay has built its franchise identity on doing more with less. The organization has consistently found and developed pitching that outperforms expectations. The Rays have made the playoffs with rosters assembled on fractions of the budget of their ALCS opponent's payroll. This is not new territory for them.

McClanahan's injury is a serious setback. But it is not a program-defining one, because the program's definition was never dependent on any single player. If the ALCS goes to five, six, or seven games, Tampa Bay will need every arm on the roster. They have used those arms before. They know how.

What's Next

ALCS Game 3 is Thursday at 6 PM ET on TBS at Progressive Field in Cleveland. The series is tied 1-1. The Rays are heading to a stadium that will be loud, hostile, and fully charged with the energy of a Guardians crowd that watched its team lose Game 2 in gut-punch fashion.

McClanahan will not be there. Peralta will. The Rays' bullpen will be there. That is enough, they believe. Thursday's game will tell us whether they are right.
