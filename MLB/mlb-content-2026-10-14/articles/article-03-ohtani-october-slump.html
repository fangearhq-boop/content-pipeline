Ohtani's October Disappearing Act: .150, 0 HR, and a Dodgers Problem That Won't Go Away

By Ryan Calloway | October 14, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The best player in baseball has gone missing in October.

Shohei Ohtani entered the 2026 postseason as the most dangerous offensive force in the sport. He is leaving each game as a near-invisible presence in the Dodgers' lineup — hitting .150 (3-for-20) with seven strikeouts, zero home runs, zero RBI, and a .411 OPS through the NLCS.

His last home run was August 18 — a regular-season shot in Colorado. That was before the playoffs started. That was before the Dodgers needed October Ohtani.

The Numbers Are Stark

Even for a player of Ohtani's caliber, these are troubling figures. A .150 average is not a cold stretch — it is a pattern across multiple series. Seven strikeouts in 20 at-bats represents a 35% strikeout rate. Zero extra-base hits. An OPS that would make most bench players uncomfortable.

In NLCS Game 2, Ohtani went 0-for-4 with two strikeouts. Pitchers are working him differently in October — locating breaking balls away, challenging him with velocity up in the zone — and so far, the adjustments are winning. Ohtani has not yet made the counter-adjustment that his ability demands.

The conversation about benching him has started. It is not a fringe take anymore. When the best player on your team is hitting .150 in the postseason, the question is legitimate.

The Physical Component

Context matters: Ohtani has been managing left knee and biceps discomfort for approximately three months. He is not 100 percent. The full physical picture of how those limitations are affecting his swing — his load, his rotation, his bat speed — is not publicly available. But the correlation between three months of discomfort and a .150 postseason average is not a coincidence that should be ignored.

Manager Dave Roberts heard the benching conversation and put Ohtani back in the Game 3 lineup anyway. Roberts' reasoning, publicly, is that he believes in Ohtani breaking out of it — that the talent is there and the results will come. His track record gives Roberts that faith. Ohtani has broken out of slumps before. He has come up with enormous at-bats in huge moments.

But there is a point at which hope and history are not enough. The Dodgers are down 2-1. They do not have unlimited games left to wait for him.

What's Next

Game 4 is Thursday night at Dodger Stadium, 9 PM ET on FS1. The Dodgers need to win or face a 3-1 series deficit that has historically been very difficult to overcome. Tyler Glasnow starts for Los Angeles.

Ohtani will be in the lineup. The question is whether it is Game 4 — at home, under the lights, with the season pressing — where he finally arrives. He hit his last home run in late August. He is due. The Dodgers cannot win a championship without him, and there is a part of every Dodger fan watching each at-bat that still believes this is the one.

October does not wait. Neither does the NLCS.
