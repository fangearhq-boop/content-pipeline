ALCS Game 2 Recap: Peralta Gem and Ramirez Error Carry Rays to 3-1 Win; Series Tied 1-1

By Jake Torres | October 14, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Baseball handed Jose Ramirez one of the cruelest reversals the sport produces Monday night at Tropicana Field.

In the fourth inning of ALCS Game 2, with the game scoreless and the series hanging in the balance, a ground ball hit the turf seam at Tropicana Field and bounced right through the seven-time All-Star's legs. Three unearned runs followed. Freddy Peralta threw six more innings of scoreless baseball. The Tampa Bay Rays won 3-1, evening the ALCS at one game each.

The Moment That Decided It

The sequence in the fourth inning was the kind of play that defines postseason series. Score tied 0-0. A ground ball that takes a bad hop off the dirt-turf seam and finds Ramirez's glove side at an angle he could not handle. Three unearned runs score. All three Tampa Bay runs in Game 2 were unearned.

Manager Stephen Vogt said Ramirez did not move his feet on the play. Ramirez said the ball hit the seam of the turf. Both things can be true simultaneously — a tricky bounce compounded by positioning. Either way, the outcome was the same: Tampa Bay went ahead, stayed ahead, and the Guardians could not score enough to make up the difference.

The cruelty is in the context. Ramirez came back from hamate surgery to be the heart of Cleveland's ALDS comeback from a 0-2 series deficit against the White Sox — only the 11th team ever to complete that kind of comeback in a best-of-five. He hit the go-ahead home run in Game 5. He was the reason the Guardians are in the ALCS. Then, in the biggest game of the next round, he handed the Rays the lead through his own glove.

Baseball is not always fair. It is almost always memorable.

Peralta's Statement Performance

Freddy Peralta — acquired by the Rays as a midseason addition — turned in the most important start of his Tampa Bay career Monday. Six innings. Zero earned runs. Four hits. Two walks. Six strikeouts. The performance was not just good; it was exactly what the Rays needed after losing Shane McClanahan to a forearm injury before the postseason.

Parker Messick started for Cleveland and worked five innings on 83 pitches — the deepest he has gone in his three postseason starts this October. He held his own. But one error changed everything. The Guardians managed just one run in response to Tampa Bay's unearned trio, and that was not enough.

What's Next

The ALCS shifts to Progressive Field in Cleveland for Games 3 and 4. Game 3 is Thursday at 6 PM ET on TBS. The Guardians are heading home with the series tied, which means they have the opportunity to win both Cleveland games and return to Tampa Bay in control.

For Ramirez, the narrative will follow him. One error in one game does not undo what he has done all season or in the ALDS. But it is the image of the moment — a bounce, a ball, a hole — that October tends to remember.

He will get another chance Thursday. Progressive Field will be loud. The series is even. That is all Cleveland can ask for.
