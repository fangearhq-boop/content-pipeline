Jake Bauers NLCS MVP — The Retirement Almost Nobody Knew About
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Before this season, Jake Bauers was ready to walk away from baseball. He and his wife were expecting their first child. He'd had stretches of decent play and stretches of struggle, and the grind of professional baseball was wearing on him. Retirement was the plan — until the baby changed everything. He needed health insurance. He kept playing. Then October happened. Bauers hit a go-ahead two-run home run in the seventh inning of NLCS Game 3 to give Milwaukee the lead for good. In Game 4, he ripped a two-run ground-rule double in the fourth inning to help fuel the Brewers' sweep of the Los Angeles Dodgers. He hit in all four games. He drove in six runs — the most on the team. He became the first Brewer in history to win the League Championship Series MVP Award. No Milwaukee player had ever won that trophy in the franchise's 58-year history. Bauers did it while batting .286 for the series (4-for-14), making clutch plays at the moments that mattered most, against one of the most expensive and decorated rosters in baseball. "We knew we were going to win," Bauers said after the series-clinching Game 4 victory at Dodger Stadium. Sometimes a pregnant wife keeps you from retiring. Sometimes that leads to a World Series. ### Image Post Caption Jake Bauers was ready to retire this year. His wife's pregnancy convinced him to stay for the health insurance. He just won NLCS MVP — the first Brewer ever to earn that trophy. 6 RBI, 4 for 14, a go-ahead homer, a series-clinching double. Life comes at you fast.

NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Through three ALCS games, Tampa Bay's most dangerous hitters had gone quiet. Junior Caminero, Yandy Díaz, and Jonathan Aranda — three of the most feared bats in the Rays lineup — were a combined 5-for-34 with just one RBI as the Guardians took a 2-1 series lead. Tampa's season was in danger of ending not with a bang but with a quiet series of groundouts and strikeouts. Then came Saturday's Game 4 at Progressive Field. Caminero stepped to the plate in the sixth inning with the game tied 1-1 and did what he does at his best: he hit the ball harder than most humans can swing a bat. Exit velocity: 110.7 mph, which according to Statcast analysts was the defining hit of the game. The ball didn't just leave the park — it sent a message. Final score: Rays 7, Guardians 3. Series tied, 2-2. For Tampa Bay, Caminero's breakthrough couldn't have come at a better time. He's the Rays' most dangerous power source, a player analysts have called the key to the pennant if Tampa is going to advance. Now, heading into Game 5 today, the question is whether Saturday was a turning point or a single hot night. Given what Caminero is capable of — and what he showed Saturday — the Guardians have to respect that lineup in a way they haven't had to this series. ### Image Post Caption Before Game 4: Caminero, Díaz, and Aranda were a combined 5-for-34 with 1 RBI in the ALCS.
