ALCS Game 5 Preview: Rasmussen vs. Messick, World Series Spot on the Line Today at 5 PM ET

By Marcus Cole

October 18, 2026

After nine days of baseball, the American League Championship Series is exactly where it should be: tied 2-2, with everything decided by a single game.

The Tampa Bay Rays and Cleveland Guardians meet in ALCS Game 5 today at Progressive Field in Cleveland. First pitch is 5 p.m. Eastern on TBS and HBO Max. The winner earns a trip to the 2026 World Series to face the Milwaukee Brewers. The loser ends their season here.

The Pitching Matchup

Tampa Bay turns to Drew Rasmussen, who enters the game as Tampa's most reliable October arm. In two postseason starts, he's posted a 1.29 ERA and a 1-0 record, including a dominant eight-inning performance in the Division Series opener. He's been Tampa Bay's best starter and most consistent producer on the mound all fall.

Cleveland counters with Parker Messick, who is carrying a career-high 198 innings into the start after a regular season in which he was one of the Guardians' most dependable arms. Messick's postseason numbers don't match his regular-season form — he enters Game 5 at 0-2 with a 3.09 ERA — but the Guardians' bullpen is deep, and Messick doesn't have to win the game by himself.

Behind Messick, Cleveland will look to Hunter Gaddis, Erik Sabrowski, and closer Cade Smith to preserve any lead.

The Offensive Question

Tampa Bay's lineup woke up in Game 4, and the timing could not have been better. Junior Caminero, Yandy Díaz, and Jonathan Aranda were a combined 5-for-34 with just one RBI through the first three games of the ALCS. Then Caminero stepped in the sixth inning of Game 4 and hit a 110.7 mph laser that broke a 1-1 tie. The Rays went on to win 7-3, evening the series.

The question now: was Saturday a breakthrough or a one-game hot stretch? If Caminero carries that energy into today's game, the Rays have the offensive ceiling to take a commanding series lead.

Cleveland, for its part, has Jose Ramírez — one of the best hitters in the American League — anchoring a lineup that has been reliable all postseason.

Historical Stakes

In ALCS history, the team that wins Game 5 to take a 3-2 series lead has advanced to the World Series roughly 66.7 percent of the time. That context alone tells you how important tonight's game is — and why both managers will be pushing their rosters hard.

The winner meets the Milwaukee Brewers in the World Series, beginning October 23 at American Family Field. The Brewers swept the Dodgers in the NLCS and will have had more than two weeks off by the time the series begins.

What's Next

Game 5 is today at 5 p.m. ET from Progressive Field in Cleveland, airing on TBS and HBO Max. If the series is not decided today, Game 6 moves to Tropicana Field in St. Petersburg on Monday. Game 7, if necessary, would also be in Tampa on Tuesday. The World Series begins Friday, October 23, regardless of who wins tonight.
