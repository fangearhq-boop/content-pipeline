Caminero's 110.7 MPH Laser Breaks Tampa's Drought — Rays Even ALCS at 2-2

By Marcus Cole

October 18, 2026

Junior Caminero had been quiet for three games. Then he wasn't.

The Tampa Bay Rays' most dangerous power hitter stepped to the plate in the sixth inning of ALCS Game 4 at Progressive Field on Saturday with the game tied 1-1, and he hit a baseball the way only he can — hard enough to end conversations. Exit velocity: 110.7 mph. The ball left the park, broke a tie, and sent a message to a Cleveland Guardians team that had begun to believe it had Tampa's lineup figured out.

Final score: Rays 7, Guardians 3. ALCS tied 2-2. The series resumes today at 5 p.m. Eastern on TBS.

The Drought Before the Storm

Through the first three games of the American League Championship Series, Tampa Bay's heart of the order had gone eerily quiet. Caminero, Yandy Díaz, and Jonathan Aranda — three of the most feared bats in the Rays lineup — entered Game 4 a combined 5-for-34 with just one RBI. The Guardians had built a 2-1 series lead by controlling Tampa's power threats, keeping the ball away from Caminero's wheelhouse and forcing the Rays into a situation where they had to win or go home.

For three games, it had worked. Then came the sixth inning on Saturday.

What 110.7 MPH Looks Like

Caminero's go-ahead home run was measured at 110.7 mph exit velocity by Statcast analysts, making it the defining hit of the game by any metric. It wasn't just the number that mattered — it was what the hit represented. A Tampa Bay team that had been grinding out quiet losses suddenly had its engine running, and the Guardians' bullpen couldn't hold the dam.

The Rays scored seven runs in the game. Caminero's blast broke the tie; his teammates did the rest. By the final out, Tampa Bay had a blowout win and a series reset heading into the most important game of the season.

The Question Heading Into Game 5

The question every analyst is asking today: was Saturday a breakthrough, or a one-game hot stretch?

Caminero is the kind of hitter whose cold stretches can last weeks and whose hot streaks can win series. If the energy he brought to the sixth inning on Saturday carries into today's winner-take-all game, Tampa Bay has the offensive ceiling to match any team in the American League. The Rays' lineup, when clicking, is built on exactly this kind of explosion — not the quiet manufacturing of runs, but the sudden and decisive power swing that changes the entire complexion of a game.

Cleveland has to account for him now in a way it didn't have to in Games 1 through 3. That adjustment, or the lack of one, may decide the series.

What's Next

ALCS Game 5 is today at 5 p.m. Eastern at Progressive Field in Cleveland, airing on TBS and HBO Max. The winner advances to the 2026 World Series to face the Milwaukee Brewers beginning October 23 at American Family Field. Drew Rasmussen starts for Tampa Bay.
