Almost Retired. Now an NLCS MVP. The Remarkable Story of Jake Bauers.

By Jake Torres

October 18, 2026

The decision to keep playing wasn't really about baseball.

Before the 2026 season, Jake Bauers was ready to walk away. He'd spent years as a professional baseball player — a solid contributor, not a superstar — and the question of whether to keep going had become genuinely uncertain. Then his wife told him she was pregnant. They needed health insurance. He kept playing.

This week, he became the first Milwaukee Brewer in franchise history to win the National League Championship Series Most Valuable Player Award.

What He Did to Earn It

Bauers was the most clutch hitter in a four-game Milwaukee sweep of the Los Angeles Dodgers. He drove in a team-high six runs on four hits in 14 at-bats, collecting a hit in each of the four games. The numbers themselves are respectable. The moments behind those numbers are what elevated him to MVP status.

In Game 3, with the Brewers holding a 2-0 series advantage, Bauers stepped up in the seventh inning and hit a two-run home run that gave Milwaukee the lead for good. Two nights later in Game 4 at Dodger Stadium, he delivered a two-run ground-rule double in the fourth inning that helped the Brewers fuel the series-clinching 6-4 victory.

"We knew we were going to win," Bauers said after the final out landed in Milwaukee's glove.

History Made in Milwaukee

The Milwaukee Brewers have been in the postseason before, made deep runs before, and come close before. But in their 58-year history, no Brewer had ever won the League Championship Series Most Valuable Player Award. That changed when Bauers stepped to the plate in October 2026 and delivered exactly when the franchise needed it most.

In 1982, the Brewers reached the World Series, and they lost. Fred Lynn of the California Angels won the ALCS MVP that year — in a losing effort. Since then, the Brewers have never been this close to a championship.

A Quiet Hero in a City Ready to Celebrate

Bauers isn't the biggest name on the Brewers roster. He won't be on the posters. But in a series against a Dodgers team whose salary dwarfed Milwaukee's entire payroll, he was the player who made the critical plays at the critical moments.

The story writes itself, and he didn't need to be a Hall of Famer to write it. He just needed to stay in the game long enough to get there. A baby on the way will do that.

What's Next

Bauers and the Brewers begin World Series preparations for Game 1 on October 23 at American Family Field in Milwaukee. The city of Milwaukee is hosting its first World Series game in 44 years. For Bauers, it's an unlikely October sequel to an unlikely October run — and a reminder that sometimes the best stories are the ones nobody saw coming.
