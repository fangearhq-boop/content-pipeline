The Polar Bear Is on the Move

Pete Alonso declined his $24 million Mets option, and the first baseman who has hit more home runs through age 28 than almost anyone in baseball history is now a free agent.

At 28 years old with 226 career home runs, three consecutive seasons with 40 or more home runs, and Scott Boras as his representative, Alonso is positioned for a contract in the five-to-six-year range worth somewhere between $150 million and $200 million. The market for a power bat of this profile is thin — there are not many first basemen in baseball who can do what Alonso does — which works in his favor in a negotiation.

The Career Numbers Make the Case

Alonso's offensive profile is built entirely around power, and he has delivered consistently. Three straight seasons at 40-plus home runs. A career trajectory that projects him to reach historically significant totals if he can stay healthy through a long-term contract. He also offers DH flexibility — his agent Scott Boras has said publicly that Alonso is open to the designated hitter role — which expands the field of teams that might pursue him beyond those that need a first baseman specifically.

The Scott Boras factor matters in any conversation about this negotiation. Boras is the sport's most accomplished agent at extracting maximum value on long-term contracts, and he rarely settles early or takes a discount. Teams entering this process should expect a full-market negotiation that runs deep into the offseason — or, given the December 1 CBA deadline, potentially a rapid conclusion if both sides want to get ahead of a lockout.

The Three Most Realistic Destinations

Boston Red Sox: USA Today's Bob Nightengale reported that multiple general managers around the league view Boston as the perfect fit for Alonso. The Red Sox need power, have payroll flexibility heading into next season, and Fenway Park's short left-field wall — the Green Monster — is tailor-made for a right-handed hitter who generates elite exit velocity. The organizational alignment is strong.

New York Yankees: SI's Pat Ragazzo reported that the Yankees are Alonso's top destination. New York would give him a chance to stay in the city where he became a star while playing for the other New York franchise. The Yankees have their own first base need heading into 2027, financial capacity to compete at the top of the market, and a roster built to contend now.

Mets return: A reunion in Queens is possible, but only at a team-friendly structure. Multiple reports indicate the Mets under owner Steve Cohen plan to let Alonso walk if he demands full market value. The relationship between Alonso and the organization has been positive, but ownership appears unwilling to pay top dollar for a player whose defensive limitations at first base are well-documented at the major league level.

The Dark Horses

The Cardinals and Diamondbacks have been mentioned in preliminary conversations as potential suitors, and the Mariners have also been linked. None appears to be at the top of Alonso's preference list, but in a Boras negotiation, every additional bidder raises the floor and creates leverage against the frontrunners.

The Timeline

The December 1 CBA deadline compresses everything. Boras and his clients historically prefer to run negotiations deep into the winter, but a lockout changes the calculus entirely. If the CBA expires without a new deal, no contracts can be signed during the work stoppage. That means Alonso's market — and potentially his entire 2027 season timeline — gets frozen until a new CBA is ratified.

Both sides have reason to move faster than normal. Where Alonso ends up in the next 30 days figures to be one of the defining storylines of this accelerated offseason.
