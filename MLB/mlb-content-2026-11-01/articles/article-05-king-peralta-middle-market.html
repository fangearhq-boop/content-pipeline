Beyond Skubal — The Second Tier Has Value

Every conversation this offseason starts with Tarik Skubal, and for good reason. A back-to-back Cy Young winner projected at $350 million to $500 million is a once-in-a-generation event. But the free agent starting pitcher market does not begin and end with Skubal — and in a winter where a December 1 CBA deadline threatens to freeze all transactions, the second tier of the market may move faster than anyone expects.

Two names to know: Michael King and Freddy Peralta.

Michael King — The Reliable Mid-Rotation Arm

King declined the San Diego Padres' $28 million option for 2027, triggering a $5 million buyout and making himself a free agent at age 31. What he brings to his next team is a simple, consistent track record: 32 starts, a 12-10 record, and a 3.21 ERA in 2026. He is not a strikeout-heavy ace, but he eats innings, limits damage, and gives managers a reliable No. 3 or No. 4 starter on a nightly basis.

The timing of his opt-out is interesting. Declining $28 million suggests King and his representatives believe he can exceed that figure on the open market — a reasonable bet given the state of starting pitcher supply relative to demand. Teams that miss on Skubal will be aggressive about securing the next best available options, and King sits near the top of that second tier.

His market could move quickly after the signing window opens November 2. Teams operating with rotation needs but unable to compete at the Skubal price point will be looking for exactly what King offers.

Freddy Peralta — The Bounce-Back Candidate

Peralta's 2026 season told two very different stories depending on which half you look at. With the New York Mets, he struggled, posting a 4.99 ERA across 22 starts before being traded to the Tampa Bay Rays at the deadline. In 10 starts for Tampa Bay, he was excellent — 3.25 ERA — flashing the ceiling that made him a coveted arm earlier in his career.

That split is the whole argument for Peralta. The Mets version — 4.99 ERA, inconsistent — is the floor. The Rays version — 3.25 ERA, sharp — is the ceiling. One outlet has projected him at 5 years and $140 million based on the combination of his upside, his age, and his post-trade performance. Whether teams buy the narrative of a revitalized pitcher or remain skeptical of the Mets-era struggles will determine where his market lands.

The San Diego Padres have been linked as a potential suitor, which carries some irony: they just let Michael King walk, and now they may be in the market for his replacement at the top of the second tier.

The Lockout Premium

Both King and Peralta benefit from the same dynamic: the December 1 deadline creates artificial urgency that pushes teams to act before they normally would. In a normal offseason, teams might let the second-tier market develop slowly over November and December. This winter, that strategy risks everything freezing before any deals get done.

The result is likely a compressed, fast-moving middle market where teams that want quality starting pitching have to be aggressive and decisive in a short window. King and Peralta are well-positioned to benefit from that dynamic.

The full 2026-27 free agent starting pitcher class also includes Sonny Gray and Kevin Gausman, rounding out a rotation-starter market that, beyond Skubal, offers genuine depth. But King and Peralta figure to be the most discussed names in the $100 million-to-$200 million range — and that range is likely to get expensive in a hurry.
