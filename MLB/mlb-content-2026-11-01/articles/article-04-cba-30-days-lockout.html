The Clock Is Running

Exactly 30 days from today, the Major League Baseball Collective Bargaining Agreement expires — and a lockout is widely expected to follow.

The current CBA, which governs the economic relationship between MLB owners and the MLB Players Association, expires on December 1 at 11:59 PM ET. Negotiations between the two sides have not produced meaningful progress, and some analysts now put the odds of reaching a deal before the deadline at roughly 5 percent. That is not a typo. The gap between what owners are proposing and what players are demanding is fundamental, not cosmetic.

What Each Side Wants

The owners' proposal centers on structural economic reform. They are seeking a $245 million salary cap — a hard cap that would limit total player compensation on any roster — combined with a $171 million salary floor that would force lower-spending clubs to invest more. They have also proposed increasing the minimum salary from $780,000 to $1 million per year and shortening the regular season from 162 games to 154 games.

The players' union is not interested in any version of a hard salary cap. The MLBPA's position is built around a luxury tax threshold of approximately $300 million, which would continue the existing system that penalizes but does not prohibit high spending. The union wants to preserve the ability of big-market teams to outspend their competitors, which has historically driven the salaries of premium free agents to record levels.

These are not positions that lend themselves to easy compromise. The dispute between a hard cap and no hard cap is a structural argument that has divided the sport before, and it typically takes a prolonged work stoppage before one side blinks.

The Last Lockout — A Preview of What Could Come

The 2021-22 lockout lasted 99 days and delayed the start of the regular season by one week, requiring the cancellation of several early-season games. That dispute also froze the free agent market for the entire duration — no player could sign a new contract, no trades could be completed, and no transactions of any kind were permitted while the lockout was in effect.

If a lockout begins December 1, the same restrictions apply. Tarik Skubal, Teruaki Sato, Pete Alonso, and more than 150 other free agents would see their markets frozen indefinitely. Spring Training 2027 would be at risk. The 2027 regular season schedule could be affected. Everything downstream from the December 1 deadline depends on how quickly a new CBA can be negotiated once the work stoppage begins.

The Next Milestones

The GM meetings scheduled for November 10-12 represent the next meaningful negotiating milestone. Commissioner Rob Manfred and union executive director leadership are expected to discuss the broad outlines of a deal in the margins of those meetings. Historical precedent suggests that GM meetings produce more informal signal than formal progress, but they are a temperature check that will tell observers something about where each side actually stands.

The Qualifying Offer deadline is November 17, which will clarify the free agent landscape for a handful of players who received QOs and must decide whether to accept or reject them.

After that, there are no formal checkpoints before December 1. The 30 days between today and the deadline are the window in which this offseason either gets completed at historic speed — with Skubal, Sato, Alonso, and dozens of others signing before the door closes — or gets frozen in place before it ever really gets started.

What It Means for Fans

A lockout in December means no free agent movement, no trades, and no roster building for any team from December 1 until a new CBA is ratified. The 2027 season cannot be put together while the work stoppage is active. For fans tracking their team's offseason moves, the practical impact is a complete halt to all roster news for an unknown period — potentially months.

The optimistic scenario is a deal before December 1 that does not exist yet. The realistic scenario is a prolonged negotiation that carries into 2027 before the sport gets back to normal. Thirty days to find out.
