The Biggest Pitcher Contract in Baseball History Is Coming

Tarik Skubal's free agency becomes official tomorrow, and two teams have separated themselves from the pack: the Los Angeles Dodgers and the New York Mets.

The Dodgers received what multiple sources describe as a significant boost to their pursuit of the back-to-back AL Cy Young Award winner in recent weeks. The Mets, meanwhile, have reportedly told insiders they are fully committed to landing him. Contract projections from analysts and team executives range from $350 million to as much as $500 million — a figure that would make Skubal the highest-paid pitcher in the history of professional baseball.

Why Skubal Is Worth Every Dollar

The case for Tarik Skubal at $400 million starts with his body of work, which over the last two seasons has been the best sustained run by any starting pitcher in the American League.

In 2026, he went 16-5 with a 1.80 ERA and 252 strikeouts. That followed his 2025 Cy Young season, which established him as the best pitcher in baseball. Back-to-back Cy Young Awards place him in elite historical company. His combination of stuff, command, and durability — 32-plus starts in each of the last two years — is the profile every contending team wants anchoring a rotation.

The signing window officially opens at 5:00 PM ET on November 2, meaning negotiating teams can begin formal contract discussions with Skubal's representatives starting tomorrow afternoon. The expectation is that this process moves quickly given the December 1 CBA deadline.

The Elbow Caveat

There is one wrinkle for any team committing north of $400 million: Skubal underwent an elbow procedure in 2026 to remove loose bodies from the joint. The procedure is relatively common and typically does not cause long-term structural concerns, but for a pitcher at this contract level, it adds a layer of medical scrutiny that teams will want to address thoroughly in their physical evaluations.

Skubal pitched through the issue and maintained his elite performance, which is a favorable data point. But a seven-to-eight-year commitment at $50 million per year requires teams to be fully comfortable with every element of his health picture. Teams bidding at the top end of the range will likely require comprehensive medical review before finalizing any offer.

Dodgers vs. Mets — The Main Event

The Dodgers have become the standard destination for elite free agents in recent years, combining financial capacity with a roster built to win immediately. Adding Skubal to a rotation that already features significant talent would be a statement. The "boost" their pursuit reportedly received suggests something in the dynamic shifted in their favor — whether that is financial flexibility, a player preference signal, or competitive positioning, the details have not been confirmed.

The Mets, under owner Steve Cohen, have demonstrated a willingness to spend at the very top of the market. Their reported "commitment" to landing Skubal signals that they view this as a franchise-defining acquisition. The Cubs, Phillies, and Red Sox have also been mentioned as lurking suitors, though they appear to be chasing the two frontrunners at this point.

The CBA Factor

The December 1 CBA expiration creates a hard deadline that both sides want to beat. If a lockout begins, no new contracts can be signed until a new collective bargaining agreement is reached. The 2021-22 lockout lasted 99 days. A comparable work stoppage starting December 1 would push Skubal's signing into the spring and delay the start of his contract by months — a scenario neither he nor the winning team wants.

That urgency accelerates everything. Teams will need to complete their medical evaluations, finalize offer structures, and close negotiations in roughly 30 days. Given the contract scale involved, that is an aggressive timeline — but both the Dodgers and Mets have the infrastructure and motivation to make it happen.
