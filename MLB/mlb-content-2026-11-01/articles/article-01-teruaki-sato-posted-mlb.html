The 45-Day Clock Is Running

Teruaki Sato is officially available to MLB teams, and the race to sign the 2026 NPB Triple Crown winner begins today — with a hard deadline looming on the calendar.

The Hanshin Tigers third baseman was posted to the MLB system on November 1, opening a 45-day window for teams to negotiate with him directly. The Mets, Yankees, Dodgers, and Phillies have all been reported as interested parties, according to multiple insiders. But there is a complication that adds genuine urgency to this process: the posting window freezes on December 1 when the current Collective Bargaining Agreement expires and a potential lockout is expected to begin.

What Sato Did in 2026

Sato's 2026 season with the Hanshin Tigers was exceptional by any standard. He won the NPB Triple Crown — leading the league in batting average, home runs, and RBIs — which places him in the elite tier of Japanese players who have made the transition to Major League Baseball. The Triple Crown is exceedingly rare in Nippon Professional Baseball, and Sato's accomplishment generated significant interest from scouts who had been tracking him throughout the season.

While Sato's primary position in Japan was at third base and corner outfield, analysts expect him to shift to first base once he arrives in the major leagues, a positional adjustment that mirrors what was anticipated for other top NPB imports. The move makes sense given the athleticism demands at the hot corner in MLB compared to NPB, and Sato's profile projects well at first base.

The Lockout Shadow

This is where the calendar becomes critical. The MLB CBA expires on December 1 at 11:59 PM ET, and a lockout is widely anticipated. Under the standard NPB posting rules, a player's signing window freezes if a lockout begins — meaning Sato cannot sign with an MLB team while the work stoppage is in effect.

This is not a hypothetical scenario. It is exactly what happened to Seiya Suzuki following the 2021-22 offseason. Suzuki was posted in December 2021, the lockout began shortly after, his window froze, and he did not sign with the Chicago Cubs until March 2022 after the labor dispute was resolved 99 days later. If the same sequence plays out this winter, Sato could be left waiting until the spring — potentially delaying his MLB debut to 2027 even after all parties want to get something done.

That shared urgency is one reason Sato's market figures to move quickly. Teams with genuine interest have roughly 30 days to finalize a deal before the December 1 deadline becomes a problem. Given the complexity of negotiations at this contract level, 30 days is not a lot of time.

The Landing Spot Race

The reported field of suitors — Mets, Yankees, Dodgers, Phillies — represents four of the biggest spenders in the sport, and all four have need at or around first base in one form or another. The Dodgers have become the default destination in any international player conversation given their resources and track record. The Mets, under owner Steve Cohen, have shown a willingness to spend aggressively on international talent. The Yankees have their own financial firepower and a roster need. The Phillies have been active in international markets.

The Cardinals and Blue Jays have also been mentioned as potential suitors by CBS Sports, though they are considered secondary to the four main reported bidders.

No posting fee amount has been confirmed. Under the current system, the fee is calculated as a percentage of the contract Sato ultimately signs — so the larger the deal, the larger the fee paid to the Hanshin Tigers.

The Bottom Line

The December 1 deadline turns what would normally be a deliberate, months-long negotiation into something closer to a sprint. Both Sato and the teams pursuing him have incentive to get something done before the calendar forces a long delay. The next 30 days figure to be among the most eventful of this offseason — and Sato's market could establish the tone for everything that follows.
