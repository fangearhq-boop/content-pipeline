Pope Hat Night Couldn't Stop Them: White Sox Still Lead AL Central at 61-56

By Jake Torres

August 12, 2026

They lost in ten innings on Pope Hat Night. They're still in first place in the American League Central.

The Cincinnati Reds beat the Chicago White Sox 5-4 in extra innings on Tuesday night at Guaranteed Rate Field, with Elly De La Cruz delivering a walk-off sacrifice fly in the 10th inning to spoil the giveaway night. The loss snapped a two-game White Sox winning streak, but it did not dent Chicago's lead in the division. The White Sox are 61-56 heading into Wednesday, with the Detroit Tigers sitting 3.5 games back and the Minnesota Twins 4.0 back.

The Context Is Everything

To understand what 61-56 in August means for this White Sox team, you have to remember where they were in August of 2024. Chicago finished that season 41-121 — one of the worst records in modern MLB history. They lost more games than any team since the 1962 expansion Mets. They were historically, comprehensively bad.

Now they are leading a division in the middle of August. The same franchise. Barely two years later. However this season ends for Chicago, the turnaround is one of the legitimate stories of the 2026 baseball season.

The Division Picture

With 50 games remaining, Chicago's 3.5-game lead over Detroit is comfortable but not insurmountable. The Tigers and Twins are both close enough to keep the Central competitive through September. The White Sox's 36-22 home record at Guaranteed Rate Field gives them an edge if the race goes down to the wire — they play most of their remaining schedule with the benefit of that home environment.

Tuesday's walk-off loss was deflating in the moment — De La Cruz is exactly the kind of player you don't want up in the 10th inning of a close game — but the White Sox have been resilient enough all season that one extra-inning loss on giveaway night is not going to define where they're headed.

What's Next

The White Sox continue their homestand at Guaranteed Rate Field. The AL Central standings are updated daily at mlb.com. With 50 games remaining, Chicago will need to keep winning to hold off Detroit and Minnesota through September.
