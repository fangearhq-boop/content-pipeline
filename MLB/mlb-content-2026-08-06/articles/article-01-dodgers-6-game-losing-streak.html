Dodgers Lose Their 6th Straight as Pete Crow-Armstrong Homers Twice in Cubs Win

By Marcus Cole | August 6, 2026

The Los Angeles Dodgers entered 2026 as the presumptive World Series favorite. On Wednesday night at Wrigley Field, they dropped their sixth consecutive game — a season-high losing streak — falling to the Chicago Cubs 7-6 in a game that encapsulated everything that has gone wrong during this skid.

Pete Crow-Armstrong hit two home runs and drove in four runs to carry Chicago. It was the kind of performance that made the Cubs' lineup look dangerous and the Dodgers' pitching look ordinary, which is not a sentence that anyone expected to write in August.

Ohtani Delivers. The Team Doesn't.

Shohei Ohtani hit a leadoff home run — his 29th as a Dodger — in the loss. The blast passed Davey Lopes for second place on the Dodgers' all-time franchise leadoff home run list, a milestone that would normally generate significant attention. In the context of a sixth straight loss, it was buried.

Ohtani extended his hitting streak to nine games with the home run. He has been one of the few consistent bright spots during the skid. The problem is that individual excellence hasn't translated into runs, and runs haven't held up when the Dodgers actually scored them.

Los Angeles left eight runners on base Wednesday night. That number — eight — tells the story of an offense that keeps getting to the point but can't finish. Over the course of six losses, the Dodgers have stranded runners in critical situations repeatedly, and the pattern has been the same each time: opportunity, failure to convert, loss.

Back-to-Back Sweeps

The defeat completed a series sweep for the Cubs, the Dodgers' second straight sweep. Los Angeles came in having already lost five games in a row; they couldn't stop the bleeding in Chicago. The Skubal debut loss (Cubs 5, Dodgers 1) opened the series on August 5 and set the tone for what followed.

Two consecutive series without winning a single game. That is not the profile of a team playing like a World Series contender. It is the profile of a team that has lost its footing.

Still in First Place — For Now

Here is the context that matters: the Dodgers are 69-45. They remain in first place in the NL West. A six-game skid is a skid, not a collapse. The division hasn't caught up, and Los Angeles still has the talent to win the World Series.

But a losing streak of this length — this sustained, this patterned — forces questions. Are there pitching concerns? Is the lineup being mismanaged in high-leverage situations? Is the fatigue of a long season starting to show?

Those are the questions that will define the next two weeks in Los Angeles.

What's Next

The Dodgers leave Chicago and will have to reckon with what happened over this road trip. Their next series will be closely watched — not just for wins and losses, but for signs of whether this is a momentary wobble or something that requires a structural fix. For a team built to win in October, August matters. The skid ends now, or the questions get louder.
