Sonny Gray Improves to 14-2 as Red Sox Reach 30-5 Since June 25

By Ryan Calloway | August 6, 2026

The number that matters most on Wednesday night in Boston is not the final score, though the Red Sox did blank the Chicago White Sox 4-0. It is the line in the standings that now reads: 30-5 since June 25.

Thirty wins. Five losses. Forty-one games. That is what sustained excellence looks like, and the Boston Red Sox have been delivering it for six weeks and counting.

Gray Does It Again

Sonny Gray was the engine of Wednesday's win. He threw six scoreless innings, allowing three hits and two walks while striking out eight. The performance improved his record to 14-2 — the best win total in the American League.

Wins are an imperfect pitching metric, depending as they do on run support and bullpen performance. But Gray's 14-2 record is not a product of luck. He has been dominant across this stretch, and the Red Sox have given him runs to work with because the entire roster has been performing at a high level simultaneously. That alignment — elite pitching supported by an elite offense — is what a pennant race looks like from the inside.

Abreu Powers the Offense

Wilyer Abreu went 2-for-3 on Wednesday, scored three runs, and hit his 20th home run of the season. The milestone marker — 20 HR — puts Abreu firmly in the conversation among the AL's best outfielders. He has been one of Boston's most reliable producers during the surge, combining plate discipline with power in a lineup that has become genuinely difficult to navigate for opposing pitchers.

The final score of 4-0 tells the story efficiently: Gray gave the offense time to work, and the offense delivered enough runs to make it comfortable. That formula has repeated itself throughout the Red Sox's run since late June.

The White Sox Stay Afloat

The White Sox absorbed Wednesday's shutout but remain in first place in the AL Central by three games. Both Cleveland and Minnesota also lost Wednesday, which kept Chicago's cushion intact despite the defeat. The White Sox are a team managing their division lead rather than expanding it, but managing it is enough for now.

If Boston is coming, the White Sox will need their own surge to absorb it. The AL Central standings right now are a quiet drama — one that 30-5 makes unavoidably loud.

What's Next

The Red Sox's seven-game winning streak sets up a second half of August that could define their season. The question is whether this level of performance is sustainable — and whether the pitching depth behind Gray can carry the load through September. At 30-5 since June 25, Boston has answered every question so far. The American League is paying attention.
