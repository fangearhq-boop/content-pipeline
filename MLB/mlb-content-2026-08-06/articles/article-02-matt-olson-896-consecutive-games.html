Matt Olson Passes Stan Musial with 896th Consecutive Game, Moves Into 8th All-Time

By Jake Torres | August 6, 2026

It happened quietly, the way durability milestones usually do. On Wednesday night in Atlanta, Matt Olson played in his 896th consecutive game as the Braves beat the Miami Marlins 4-1 — and in doing so, he passed Hall of Famer Stan Musial for sole possession of 8th place on MLB's all-time consecutive games played list.

Musial played in 895 consecutive games across a career that made him one of the most beloved figures in baseball history. Olson is now one game ahead of him, and counting.

A Streak Five Years in the Making

Olson's consecutive games streak began on May 2, 2021 — when he was a member of the Oakland Athletics, before his path led him to Atlanta. Through trades, free agency, injuries to teammates, changes in role, and the daily grind of a 162-game season across multiple years, Olson has not missed a single game.

That kind of consistency is rare. The all-time list of consecutive games played is, by definition, a list of players who showed up regardless of circumstances. Olson has now earned a place alongside some of the sport's most durable names.

The list above him is short and illustrious. Cal Ripken Jr.'s record of 2,632 consecutive games stands as one of baseball's most untouchable marks. Lou Gehrig's streak of 2,130 remains iconic. Below those, the names grow less familiar — Everett Scott, Steve Garvey, Miguel Tejada — but each represents a remarkable commitment to availability.

Olson is 8th on that list now, and still active.

The Braves Are Rolling, Too

The milestone didn't happen in isolation. The Braves extended their own winning streak to seven consecutive games — a season high — with the 4-1 victory over Miami. Bryce Elder held the Marlins to one hit across seven innings, a performance that might have been the night's headline in any other context. Drake Baldwin drove in two runs.

Atlanta is playing well on both sides of the ball, and Olson has been part of that. His presence in the lineup every day is not just a statistical footnote — it provides the Braves with a reliable bat in the heart of the order, game after game, without the uncertainty that comes when a key player sits.

What the Streak Represents

Consecutive games played records get complicated in the modern era. Teams manage rest more carefully. The schedule's physical demands are better understood. Players are increasingly encouraged to prioritize health over streak maintenance.

That context makes Olson's streak more impressive, not less. He has maintained it in an environment that no longer treats such streaks as inherent goods. Whatever his reasons — competitive drive, durability, good fortune — he has been available every single day for more than five years. Stan Musial, one of baseball's all-time greats, managed the same across a stretch of his career. Olson just went one better.

What's Next

The streak continues. There is no finish line in sight for Olson, and the Braves' seven-game winning streak gives his milestone a strong backdrop. The question going forward is how far up the all-time list Olson can climb — and whether he'll keep showing up to answer it. So far, the answer has been consistent: every single day.
