George Lombard Jr. Homered in His First Major-League At-Bat. The Yankees Won 2-0.

By Ryan Calloway

August 5, 2026

Tuesday night in the Bronx, the New York Yankees answered a 13-7 loss to the Cardinals — a game where Alec Burleson hit three home runs — with a 2-0 shutout win. George Lombard Jr. hit a home run in his first major-league plate appearance. David Bednar came in and locked it down for his 25th save in 27 chances. The Yankees recorded their AL-best 12th shutout of the 2026 season.

That is a good night. Those do not always happen the night after a very bad one.

Lombard's Debut Moment

A home run in your first major-league at-bat is a rare thing. It is the kind of debut that stays with a player for their entire career — a story told at retirement ceremonies, at Hall of Fame speeches, in every feature written about the arc of a career. For Lombard, who arrived in New York as a depth piece while the team waits for Aaron Judge to return from a rib stress fracture, the moment arrived faster than expected and was delivered in the cleanest possible way.

The Yankees' lineup is doing remarkable things without their best player. At 62-49, they remain the top AL wild-card team. The roster construction that allowed the Yankees to absorb Judge's absence — now extending into what looks like a late August or early September return at best — is one of the underappreciated stories of the first half of this season.

Bednar's Case for the Year

David Bednar has 25 saves in 27 chances on the season. He recorded a four-out save on Tuesday, entering in the eighth to close the door. The Yankees' bullpen has been a major part of how they've stayed competitive without Judge. Bednar at 25 for 27 in save opportunities is elite work, and his consistency in high-leverage situations has given the team something dependable at the back of the 'pen regardless of what else is happening.

The Cardinals' Perspective

Monday night's 13-7 Cardinals win featured Alec Burleson's three home runs and a seven-run eighth inning. Luis García Jr. — acquired at the deadline — hit a home run in his first Yankees at-bat during the same game. The Cardinals won that one. Tuesday, the Yankees answered. These back-to-back games between New York and St. Louis are worth noting in the context of the AL wild card race: the Yankees are holding the top spot, and they bounce back from bad nights quickly.

What's Next

Aaron Judge's return timeline continues to be described as late August or early September. GM Brian Cashman has been "optimistic" publicly; Judge himself has said he does not see a reason he would not return this season. The latest imaging showed "some progress" but he has not yet been cleared to begin baseball activities. When he does return, he joins a lineup that has been managing 62-49 without him. The combination of Judge, Lombard, and García Jr. in the second half is a legitimate offensive weapon. The Yankees are building toward something. Tuesday night's shutout win is one more data point.
