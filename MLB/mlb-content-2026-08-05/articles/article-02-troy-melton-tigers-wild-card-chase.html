Troy Melton Is the Most Important Player You're Not Watching

By Marcus Cole

August 5, 2026

While baseball was focused on Wrigley Field on Tuesday night — watching Tarik Skubal make his Dodgers debut in front of a packed house — something equally compelling was happening 2,000 miles northwest in Seattle. The Detroit Tigers beat the Mariners 8-0. Troy Melton threw seven innings, allowed three hits, and has not given up a run in 14 consecutive innings. His ERA is now 1.58. His record is 7-1.

Detroit called it the "post-Skubal era" opener. They won by eight runs.

Who Is Troy Melton?

Melton is a right-hander who spent time on the injured list to start the 2026 season and has been one of the best pitchers in baseball since his return. Through 12 starts, he has a 7-1 record and a 1.58 ERA. He has gone at least six innings in every appearance since returning and topped seven innings on Tuesday night against Seattle. He is not a household name. He is pitching like one.

Riley Greene homered in the fifth inning to extend Detroit's lead. The Tigers' lineup has been relentless — Tuesday was their sixth consecutive game scoring at least eight runs, which ties a franchise record. The offense that helped make Skubal the ace he became in Detroit has not slowed down in his absence.

The Tigers Are a Legitimate Wild Card Team

Detroit entered Tuesday's game with a 55-58 record — which, on paper, sounds like a team playing out the season. But the standings tell a different story. The Tigers are 1.5 games behind Cleveland for the final AL wild-card spot. The Mariners, whom Detroit just shut out 8-0, are 3.5 games behind Houston in the AL West and have lost nine of their last 13 games.

The Tigers are moving in the opposite direction. Their recent run — eight or more runs in six straight games, Melton at 1.58 ERA, the team playing its best baseball of the season — coincides exactly with the period after the trade deadline, the period after Skubal left. That is not nothing.

The Skubal Narrative Gets Complicated

The conventional wisdom was that Detroit traded its best player, accepted a rebuild runway, and shipped off the ace who was keeping this team relevant. The Dodgers won the deadline. Detroit lost.

Forty-eight hours after the deadline closed, the Tigers are playing their best baseball of the year and sitting within striking distance of a playoff spot. That does not mean the trade was wrong — the prospects the Dodgers sent have significant value. But the Tigers' performance in the "post-Skubal era" is at minimum a reminder that depth matters, that chemistry matters, and that sometimes a team plays differently when the pressure changes.

Melton is part of that story. A 1.58 ERA from a pitcher who started the season on the IL, in a rotation that just lost its ace, is a story that deserves more attention than it is getting.

What's Next

The Tigers continue their current road trip before returning to Comerica Park. Every game from here has playoff implications — 1.5 games with roughly 50 games remaining is not a gap, it is a race. Melton's next start will be closely watched. If the offense continues at this pace and Melton keeps pitching like a No. 1, Detroit is going to be a problem for whoever ends up as the top AL wild card seed. The season is not over in Detroit. Not even close.
