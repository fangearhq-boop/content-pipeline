Skubal's Dodgers Debut: Six Innings, 99 Miles Per Hour, and No Offense

By Ryan Calloway

August 5, 2026

Tarik Skubal made his Los Angeles Dodgers debut on Tuesday night at Wrigley Field and did everything you would expect from a pitcher of his caliber. He threw six innings, touched 99.3 miles per hour, struck out six batters, and allowed two earned runs on four hits. The Cubs had 5. The Dodgers had 1. Skubal took the loss.

This is the part of baseball that makes no logical sense and requires no explanation. Skubal pitched well. His team did not score. The Cubs won, and the Dodgers extended what is now a five-game losing streak — a season high — entering August.

How the Game Unfolded

Skubal retired his first six batters. Through two innings it looked like exactly the debut the Dodgers envisioned when they gave up Zyhir Hope, River Ryan, and Brady Smith to get him from Detroit. Then came the third inning, and then came Dansby Swanson.

Swanson, on the very first pitch Skubal threw in the third, hit a solo home run — his 17th of the season. One pitch, one swing, one run. The Dodgers responded by going nowhere offensively. Skubal kept rolling through the fourth and fifth innings before an oddly-bounced infield hit in the sixth plated the go-ahead run. He was done after six. The bullpen held but the damage was done.

Kyle Tucker hit a solo home run for the Dodgers — the only run they would score all night. Los Angeles managed four hits total. That is not enough to win a baseball game, regardless of who is pitching.

What Skubal Showed

Whatever concerns there were about Skubal adjusting to a new league, a new team, and a new city after years in Detroit — none of that showed up on Tuesday. He was sharp. He was hitting 99 miles per hour in the middle innings. He attacked hitters with the same aggression that made him one of the best starters in the American League. The 2.79 ERA and the elite strikeout rate that made the Dodgers trade for him were visible from the first pitch.

Dave Roberts, when the deal was announced, had said the Dodgers' approach to this deadline was "Skubal or nothing." The talent delivered on that standard on night one. The production did not.

The Dodgers' Bigger Problem

This loss was the fifth in a row for Los Angeles — a season-high skid that started before Skubal arrived. The Dodgers were swept in a series against the Red Sox at Dodger Stadium before heading to Chicago, and the offense has been quietly struggling for a stretch that now extends beyond the Skubal debut.

Shohei Ohtani remains limited to designated hitter duty with a knee issue, not playing the field or pitching. Will Smith is on the injured list with an inflamed disc in his neck, out approximately two months. The Dodgers supplemented by acquiring catcher Ben Rortvedt at the deadline, but this is a roster with real depth issues behind the top of the lineup right now.

The talent ceiling of this team, when healthy, still makes them the World Series favorite on paper. The version currently playing — five straight losses, Skubal debuting to no run support, Ohtani limited — is not that team.

What's Next

Skubal will take his next start in the Dodgers' rotation on his normal turn, likely later this week or early next week. The debut loss does nothing to change what he brings to this team. The Dodgers have Tyler Glasnow beginning a minor league rehab assignment as well, with a potential return on the horizon. The rotation, once healthy, is elite. The question is when they start playing like it.

For Cubs fans, a 5-1 win over the guy the Dodgers gave three prospects to get is a memorable night at Wrigley. For everyone watching the NL West, the Dodgers' five-game slide is worth monitoring. Great teams have bad weeks. The question is how long this one lasts.
