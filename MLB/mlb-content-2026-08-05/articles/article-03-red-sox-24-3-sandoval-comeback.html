Red Sox Are 24-3 in Their Last 27 Games. Patrick Sandoval Just Got His First Win Since May 2024.

By Jake Torres

August 5, 2026

The Boston Red Sox beat the Chicago White Sox 14-2 on Tuesday at Fenway Park. The final score tells you what kind of game it was. The box score tells you more: Patrick Sandoval started for Boston and picked up the win — his first major-league victory since May 7, 2024. Wilyer Abreu hit two solo home runs. The Red Sox extended their winning streak to six games.

That winning streak is part of something larger. Boston has gone 24-3 over their last 27 games. That is not a hot week. That is a transformation.

Patrick Sandoval's Road Back

Patrick Sandoval's last win before Tuesday came more than a year ago. What followed was the kind of road that tests a pitcher's relationship with the game: Tommy John surgery, months of recovery, setbacks, a full season watching from the sideline, and then the slow grind back through the minor leagues before returning to a major-league roster.

He was the starting pitcher Tuesday at Fenway and picked up the win in a 14-2 game. The margin makes it look routine. It was not routine. A pitcher coming back from Tommy John surgery and winning his first major-league game in more than a year, in a season that has seen Boston emerge as one of baseball's best teams, is a story that deserves to be told on its own terms. He was there for the right team at the right time.

Abreu and the Lineup

Wilyer Abreu hit two solo home runs on Tuesday, giving the Red Sox four home runs for the second consecutive game. This is a lineup that has been doing this for a month — the 24-3 stretch didn't happen because the pitching carried an average offense. The hitting and the pitching have been running together, feeding each other.

The Red Sox also acquired Adley Rutschman at the trade deadline, bringing in one of the best catchers in baseball. He is only now being integrated into the everyday lineup. The idea that Boston's best baseball might still be ahead of them — even after a 24-3 run — is a genuinely uncomfortable thought for the rest of the American League.

The Bigger Picture

The Red Sox were a team that seemed like a fringe contender entering the summer. They are not that team anymore. Twenty-four wins against three losses over 27 games does not happen to fringe teams. It happens to teams that have figured something out — the rotation, the lineup construction, the bullpen usage, something — and are executing at a level that earns results like this.

They swept the Dodgers at Dodger Stadium before coming home to Fenway. They beat the White Sox by 12 runs in the opener of this series. They are playing the best baseball of any team in the American League right now, and Patrick Sandoval getting his first win since Tommy John surgery is one more piece of a story that keeps getting more interesting.

What's Next

The Red Sox continue their home stand at Fenway Park. The integration of Rutschman into the everyday lineup is a process that is still developing — the returns on that acquisition are not yet fully visible. Boston's next tests will come against teams with winning records. The 24-3 run was built against a range of opponents. If it continues into more competitive matchups, the argument for the Red Sox as a genuine AL pennant contender will be impossible to dismiss. The second half is just beginning.
