### STORY 1: Tarik Skubal's Dodgers Debut

**Long-Form Post (8:30 AM ET)**

Tarik Skubal's Dodgers debut was exactly what you expected from the pitching — and nothing from the offense.

Skubal went six innings at Wrigley Field on Tuesday, touched 99.3 mph, struck out six, and allowed two earned runs. He retired his first six batters. The only real damage: a Dansby Swanson solo home run (Swanson's 17th) on the very first pitch Skubal threw in the third inning, and an oddly-bounced infield hit that plated the go-ahead run in the sixth.

The Dodgers gave him four hits of support. They lost 5-1. It was the team's fifth consecutive defeat and a season-high losing streak.

Dave Roberts called this trade "Skubal or nothing." The talent is obvious — Skubal is a 99 mph ace with elite command. The team around him just needs to start hitting. They still look like the World Series favorite on paper. They need to start playing like it.

### STORY 2: Troy Melton / Tigers

**Long-Form Post (11:00 AM ET)**

While every baseball fan was watching Tarik Skubal's debut in Chicago Tuesday night, something equally interesting was happening in Seattle.

Troy Melton threw seven innings, didn't allow a run, and lowered his ERA to 1.58 in 12 starts. The Tigers beat the Mariners 8-0. It was Detroit's sixth consecutive game with at least eight runs scored — tied for the longest such streak in franchise history.

Melton is 7-1. He hasn't allowed a run in 14 consecutive innings. He started the season on the IL and has been one of the best stories in baseball since returning. He came up through the Detroit system while everyone was focused on Skubal.

The Tigers are 55-58 — which sounds like a bad team. But they're 1.5 games behind Cleveland for the final AL wild-card spot, and they just played one of their best games of the year in the "post-Skubal era" opener.

Maybe they didn't need him as much as everyone thought.

### STORY 3: Red Sox 24-3 in Last 27

**Long-Form Post (1:00 PM ET)**

Patrick Sandoval's last MLB win before Tuesday was on May 7, 2024.

That's over a year of Tommy John surgery recovery — watching games from the dugout, going through rehab, wondering when the feel would come back. He returned to start for the Red Sox on August 4 and picked up the win as Boston crushed the White Sox 14-2. Wilyer Abreu added two home runs. Six-game winning streak extended.

The Red Sox are 24-3 over their last 27 games. That is not a misprint. They've also added Adley Rutschman at the trade deadline and are only just now integrating him.

There are a lot of teams that look good on paper heading into the second half. The Red Sox are proving it with actual results.

### STORY 4: George Lombard Jr. Debut HR

**Long-Form Post (3:30 PM ET)**

Tuesday night, the Cardinals beat the Yankees 13-7. Alec Burleson hit three home runs. New York gave up seven runs in the eighth inning.

Twenty-four hours later, the Yankees came back with a shutout and a debut home run.

George Lombard Jr. hit a home run in his first major-league plate appearance. David Bednar locked it down with a four-out save — his 25th in 27 chances. Yankees 2, Cardinals 0. New York's 12th shutout of the year, the most in the American League.

The Yankees are 62-49 without Aaron Judge, who remains on the 60-day IL with a rib stress fracture. They're the top AL wild-card team right now. Depth is doing that — and Tuesday night, a kid hitting a home run in his first major-league at-bat is what depth looks like.
