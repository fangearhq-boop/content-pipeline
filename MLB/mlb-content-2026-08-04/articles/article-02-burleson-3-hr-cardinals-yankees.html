Alec Burleson Hits Three Home Runs, Cardinals Outlast Yankees 13-7 in First Post-Deadline Game

By Marcus Cole

August 4, 2026

The 2026 trade deadline closed at 6 PM ET on Sunday. Less than four hours later, the first game of the post-deadline era was underway at Yankee Stadium — and it belonged to Alec Burleson. The 27-year-old Cardinals first baseman hit three two-run home runs off three different pitchers, capping the night with a 418-foot, second-deck blast that closed a seven-run eighth inning. Final score: St. Louis 13, New York 7.

Three Pitchers, Three Home Runs

Burleson's first shot came in the first inning against Yankees starter Cam Schlittler — a 350-foot pull shot that put the Cardinals on the board with a two-run lead. The Cardinals kept building. In the fourth, Burleson connected again, this time off reliever Tim Hill into the short right-field porch, pushing St. Louis to a 6-0 lead.

The Yankees came back. Luis García Jr., making his debut in pinstripes following his acquisition at the trade deadline, hit a two-run home run off George Soriano in the seventh inning that gave New York a 7-6 lead. The crowd at Yankee Stadium roared. It looked like a story about a debut, a comeback, and a new beginning.

Then came the eighth inning.

Seven Runs in the Eighth

St. Louis scored seven runs in the eighth inning. Burleson's third home run of the night — a 418-foot, second-deck blast off Angel Chivilli — capped the eruption and finished the Yankees in the same breath. Cardinals 13, Yankees 7. The comeback was erased. The debut story was buried.

It was the first time a Cardinals first baseman had hit three home runs in a single game since Paul Goldschmidt did it on May 7, 2023. Burleson had not been mentioned as one of the names circulating in trade deadline conversations — he was simply the best player on the field in the game that opened the post-deadline stretch.

García Jr.'s Debut Moment

Luis García Jr.'s first at-bat as a Yankee produced a two-run home run and a brief lead. In the context of a 13-7 loss, that sequence is mostly a footnote — but it matters for what it signals about the player the Yankees acquired. Getting to this lineup, with Aaron Judge on the way back from injury, García Jr. is the kind of addition that looks better as October approaches. Sunday night wasn't the full picture. It was the first frame.

What's Next

Cam Schlittler — who gave up Burleson's first home run in the first inning — entered Sunday leading the AL Cy Young voting. He now has a rough outing on his record heading into August. The Yankees absorb a deflating loss the night the deadline closed. And Alec Burleson walks out of Yankee Stadium with one of the best individual nights any Cardinals hitter has had this season. The roster moves made their statement before the deadline. Burleson made his after it.
