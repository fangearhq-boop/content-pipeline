The AL Cy Young Race Is Now a Two-Man Story: Dylan Cease vs. Cam Schlittler

By Jake Torres

August 4, 2026

Tarik Skubal is a Dodger now. The AL Cy Young Award race — which had looked like a three-man conversation through most of the summer — became a two-man story the moment Detroit shipped Skubal to Los Angeles at the trade deadline. What's left is a genuine competition between two pitchers who have been among the best in the American League all season, and who are pushing each other toward one of the more interesting award finishes in recent memory.

Schlittler's Case: Numbers and the Ballot

Cam Schlittler of the New York Yankees is the current leader — in every formal sense. He's at 14-9 with a 2.36 ERA and 249 strikeouts, leading the AL in ERA and in fWAR among pitchers. In the formal Cy Young balloting, Schlittler has 154 points and 23 first-place votes. He has been, by most measures, the best pitcher in the American League this season.

But his last start complicated the narrative. On August 3 — the night the trade deadline closed — Schlittler gave up Alec Burleson's first home run of a three-homer night in the first inning, and the Cardinals knocked him around early in what became a 13-7 New York loss. One start doesn't define a season. But in a two-man race, one start can shift the energy of the conversation.

Cease's Case: Strikeouts, Narrative, and a Statement Start

Dylan Cease of the Toronto Blue Jays is 7-5 with a 2.46 ERA, 148 strikeouts, and a 36.9% strikeout rate. His ERA is higher than Schlittler's, but his strikeout rate is elite — one of the highest in the league. And he has the moment to point to: a recent one-hit complete game shutout against the Red Sox, striking out 12. That kind of start changes how voters think about a pitcher. It's the demonstration of what Cease can do when everything clicks.

The counterargument on Cease runs through his team. The Blue Jays are 48-58, well out of playoff contention and fading. Cy Young voters have historically been reluctant to give the award to pitchers on non-competitive teams, even when the individual numbers are exceptional. That bias — fair or not — gives Schlittler a structural advantage that has nothing to do with his ERA.

Team Context and What August Decides

The Yankees are 62-49, sitting atop the AL Wild Card standings. Schlittler is pitching in a pennant race. Cease is pitching well for a team that's already planning for next year. Voters know the difference.

With Skubal now in Los Angeles and officially out of the AL race, the Cy Young conversation centers entirely on these two. Schlittler has the formal lead. Cease has the strikeout rate and the statement start. August will add roughly five starts for each of them. By September, the race should be clear. Right now, it's good.
