Kyle Schwarber Has 33 Home Runs Entering August — and the 60 HR Conversation Is Already Real

By Marcus Cole

August 4, 2026

Kyle Schwarber did not homer on August 3. The Phillies beat the Washington Nationals 6-3, with Dylan Hill and Bryce Harper each going deep, and Schwarber's bat stayed quiet. His home run total entering August is 33 — but the pace he's been on since Opening Day is generating a conversation that has moved well beyond the usual summer homer-watch chatter.

ESPN has already published a dedicated piece asking whether Schwarber can join the 60 home run club. That's not a fringe take. That's a mainstream conversation about a real possibility.

The Numbers Behind the Chase

Schwarber led the National League with 56 home runs in 2025. This year, he had 20 home runs through his team's first 49 games of 2026 — a pace that represents one of only 18 instances in MLB history at that clip. Thirty-three through the end of July puts him on a trajectory that, if maintained through August and September, would land him in range of the 60 home run threshold.

The historical weight of that number is significant. The 60 home run mark has been crossed exactly five times in major league history, by four different players. Mark McGwire hit 70 in 1998. Sammy Sosa hit 66 that same year, then 63 in 1999 and 64 in 2001. Roger Maris hit 61 in 1961. Barry Bonds holds the all-time record at 73, set in 2001. Five crossings. Four players. In the more than two decades since Bonds' record, no one has reached 60.

What Entering August With 33 Actually Means

Schwarber's 33 home runs through July is ahead of any prior season at the same point. His 56-HR year in 2025 was remarkable. His 20-HR pace in the season's first 49 games put him in historically rare company. The month of August — historically a strong home run month for power hitters at the peak of their swing — is when the pace either holds or fades.

The Phillies are in a pennant race, which adds context on both sides. Playing in meaningful games generates focus; it also means that pitcher approach adjustments, rest management, and the physical grind of a September stretch run are all real factors. Schwarber isn't chasing the record in a vacuum — he's doing it while trying to help Philadelphia win.

What's Next

The chase is real enough for ESPN to write the dedicated feature. The history is real enough to make the threshold feel enormous. Schwarber's numbers are real enough to make the conversation feel earned rather than speculative. Thirty-three home runs through July, on a pace that exceeds every season he's had before. Whether August and September confirm it or slow it down is the story of the next two months. The 60 HR club has four members and five entries. If Schwarber stays on this track, the conversation about a sixth gets louder every week.
