2026 Trade Deadline Report Card: The Dodgers Win Biggest, the Orioles and Twins Pay the Price

By Ryan Calloway

August 4, 2026

The 2026 MLB trade deadline closed Sunday at 6 PM ET, and the verdict from across the baseball landscape is clear. The Los Angeles Dodgers were the biggest winners. The Baltimore Orioles and Minnesota Twins were the most criticized losers. Everything else falls somewhere in between — but the distance between the top and the bottom of this deadline's ledger tells you everything about how October is likely to shape up.

The Dodgers Get an A+

Los Angeles was already the team everyone was building toward stopping. Then they acquired Tarik Skubal from Detroit — 2.79 ERA, 31% strikeout rate — and made the conversation feel almost unfair. Skubal joins a rotation that features Tyler Glasnow, who began his Minor League rehabilitation assignment on August 4, and a potential return to pitching from Shohei Ohtani later this season. The Dodgers were already the World Series favorite. They widened the gap.

The consensus across ESPN, The Athletic, and MLB.com gave Los Angeles the top grade of any team at the deadline. Not just among the buyers — among every team in baseball. They addressed the one real question anyone had about their roster and did it with one of the better starting pitchers in the American League.

The Red Sox and Cubs Impressed

Boston earned high marks for landing Adley Rutschman from Baltimore — a catcher of his caliber doesn't come available often, and the Red Sox moved to get him. Chicago took an aggressive approach to its starting rotation, acquiring Kevin Gausman from Toronto and Clay Holmes from the Mets in a double-dip that signals the Cubs are serious about their window.

And then there's the Chicago White Sox, who did something they haven't done in years: they bought. Acquiring Luis Castillo and Brenton Doyle among others, the White Sox positioned themselves as a legitimate contender rather than an obstacle. For a franchise that has spent recent seasons in rebuild mode, operating as buyers at the deadline is a genuine statement.

The Losers: Baltimore and Minnesota

The Orioles went full seller mode. That decision was called out publicly across the baseball media landscape — and the criticism is worth understanding in context. Baltimore had pieces that other contenders wanted. They moved them for future value. The debate is whether that trade was necessary, or whether the Orioles had more to compete for than their front office believed.

The Minnesota case is more immediately painful. The Twins sat just one game out of a playoff spot at the deadline. One game. They made only minor moves. Teams hovering at the edge of playoff contention have a narrow window to push themselves over the line — that window is shorter with every passing week of August. The Twins passed on it. That decision will either look cautious or disastrous depending on how September plays out.

What's Next

The post-deadline landscape is shaping up with Los Angeles as the clear favorite and a cluster of contenders trying to figure out whether their additions are enough. The Dodgers' rotation, if fully healthy, is as deep as anything the sport has seen in years. Everyone else is playing for second place — and trying to find a reason to believe that second place is good enough. The games that matter most are still ahead.
