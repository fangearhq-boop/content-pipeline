Aaron Judge Is Coming Back — and the Yankees Know What That Means for October

By Ryan Calloway

August 4, 2026

Aaron Judge has been on the injured list since June 5, 2026, with a stress fracture in his first right rib. The New York Yankees have gone 18-47 in the 47 games they've played without him. That record tells you more about what Judge means to this team than any individual statistic. He is the single most important player on this roster, and his absence has exposed exactly how much ground the Yankees lose when he isn't in the lineup.

Now he's coming back.

The Medical Update

Re-imaging conducted during the All-Star break showed "some progress," according to GM Brian Cashman, who described himself as "optimistic" about Judge's recovery timeline. Judge himself addressed reporters directly: "Yeah, definitely [returning]. I don't see why I wouldn't."

The realistic window for Judge's return is late August to early September. That timing puts him back on the field with several weeks of regular season play ahead — enough time to get his timing right, enough games to matter in the standings, and enough runway into October if the Yankees hold their position.

Where the Yankees Stand

New York is 62-49 overall, sitting atop the AL Wild Card standings. They have survived the Judge absence through pitching and timely performances from a depleted lineup — but the 18-47 record without him reveals how much they've been operating below their ceiling. At full health with Judge healthy, this is a different team.

The trade deadline reinforced that. The Yankees acquired Luis García Jr., who homered in his very first at-bat as a Yankee on August 3, giving New York a brief 7-6 seventh-inning lead against the Cardinals. García Jr. is the kind of lineup depth addition that looks increasingly valuable when you add back the best player in baseball around him. The Cardinals eventually won 13-7 — Burleson's three-homer night overshadowed everything else in the Bronx — but the lineup piece was real.

What a Healthy Judge Means

The best version of the Yankees this year isn't the team that went 18-47 without their best player. It's the team that opens October with Judge healthy, rested, and in rhythm — with García Jr. behind him in the lineup and a rotation that has pitched well enough to stay afloat through the summer.

The question for the next several weeks is whether Judge can return with enough time to establish that rhythm before October. Late August would give him a full month of at-bats. Early September still leaves several weeks. Either way, the return itself is now the expectation — not the hope. Judge said so himself.
