## STORY 2: Burleson 3 HRs — Cardinals 13-7 Yankees

### FB Long-Form

⚾ **ALEC BURLESON STOLE THE NIGHT AT YANKEE STADIUM**

The first game after the 2026 trade deadline featured one of the individual performances of the season. Alec Burleson — 27-year-old Cardinals first baseman — hit three two-run home runs off three different pitchers and carried St. Louis to a 13-7 win over the Yankees.

It started in the 1st: a 350-foot pull shot off starter Cam Schlittler. Then in the 4th, a short-porch blast off Tim Hill to push the Cardinals to a 6-0 lead. The Yankees came back — Luis García Jr. hit a 2-run home run in his very first at-bat as a Yankee off George Soriano in the 7th, giving New York a 7-6 lead. The crowd went electric.

Then came the 8th inning. Seven Cardinals runs. Burleson finished it with a 418-foot, 2nd-deck bomb off Angel Chivilli that made the score 13-7. It was the first time a Cardinals first baseman hit three home runs in a game since Paul Goldschmidt on May 7, 2023.

The trade deadline had just closed. The standings are shifting. But for one night, Alec Burleson was the only story that mattered.

## STORY 3: AL Cy Young Race

### FB Long-Form

⚾ **THE AL CY YOUNG RACE IS NOW CEASE VS. SCHLITTLER — AND IT'S GOOD**

Tarik Skubal is a Dodger now. The American League Cy Young Award just became a two-man conversation.

On one side: Dylan Cease of the Blue Jays. He's 7-5 with a 2.46 ERA, 148 strikeouts, and a 36.9% K rate. His recent one-hit complete game shutout against the Red Sox — 12 punchouts — reminded everyone what his ceiling looks like.

On the other side: Cam Schlittler of the Yankees. He leads the AL in ERA (2.36), leads in fWAR among pitchers, and currently leads the Cy Young voting with 154 points and 23 first-place votes. He's been the best pitcher in the league most of the season.

But then came August 3. Alec Burleson hit a home run off Schlittler in the first inning, the Cardinals knocked him around early, and St. Louis won 13-7. It's one start. But in a two-man race, one start can shift the narrative.

The Blue Jays are 48-58 and fading. The Yankees hold the top AL Wild Card spot at 62-49. Context always matters in Cy Young voting. This race is wide open heading into August.

## STORY 4: Aaron Judge Return

### FB Long-Form

⚾ **AARON JUDGE IS COMING BACK — AND THE YANKEES KNOW WHAT THAT MEANS**

Aaron Judge has been on the 60-day injured list since June 5, 2026, with a stress fracture in his first right rib. In the months since, the Yankees have gone 18-47 in the 47 games he's missed. That's what it looks like to lose the best player in baseball.

But the news is improving. Re-imaging during the All-Star break showed "some progress," according to GM Brian Cashman — who called himself "optimistic." Judge himself told reporters: "Yeah, definitely [returning]. I don't see why I wouldn't."

The realistic window is late August to early September. The Yankees are currently 62-49, sitting atop the AL Wild Card standings. On August 3 — the night the trade deadline closed — Luis García Jr. homered in his very first at-bat as a Yankee, giving New York a 7-6 lead before the Cardinals took over.

A healthy Judge returning to a lineup that now also includes García Jr. makes the Yankees a fundamentally different team in October. The question is no longer whether he comes back — it's whether he comes back with enough time to matter.

## STORY 5: Schwarber 60 HR Club

### FB Long-Form

⚾ **KYLE SCHWARBER HAS 33 HOME RUNS — AND THE 60 HR CONVERSATION IS ALREADY STARTING**

Kyle Schwarber enters August with 33 home runs. He didn't hit one on August 3 as the Phillies beat the Nationals 6-3 — but the pace hasn't slowed enough for anyone to stop watching.

Schwarber led the National League with 56 home runs in 2025. This year, he had 20 home runs in his team's first 49 games of 2026 — one of only 18 instances in MLB history at that pace. The ESPN feature asking whether he can join the 60 HR club is already up.

Here's the historical weight of that club: the 60 HR threshold has been crossed exactly five times, by four players. Mark McGwire in 1998 (70). Sammy Sosa in 1998 (66), 1999 (63), and 2001 (64). Roger Maris in 1961 (61). Barry Bonds holds the all-time record at 73, set in 2001.

Schwarber entering August with 33 puts him in striking distance — but August and September will tell the story. The Phillies are in a pennant race and the schedule will push everyone. The conversation is real. Whether the record follows depends on what the next two months look like.
