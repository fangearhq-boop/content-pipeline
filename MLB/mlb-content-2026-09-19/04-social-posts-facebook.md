### STORY 1: PCA 40-40 Chase

**Long-Form Post**
Three. That's all that stands between Pete Crow-Armstrong and baseball history. 🔥

With the Cubs winning 5-4 in Cincinnati on Saturday, PCA continues one of the most compelling individual pursuits in the game right now. He stands at 47 home runs and 37 stolen bases — three steals from joining the most exclusive club in baseball: the 40-40 season.

Only six players in the history of the major leagues have ever hit 40 home runs and stolen 40 bases in the same season. Jose Canseco did it in 1988. Barry Bonds in 1996. Alex Rodriguez in 1998. Alfonso Soriano in 2006. Ronald Acuña Jr. in 2023. Shohei Ohtani in 2023. That's it. Six players. Six times in more than 140 years of professional baseball.

PCA would be No. 7.

And it would be the first 40-40 season by a Cub. Ever. In franchise history.

He's already broken Billy Williams' all-time record for home runs by a left-handed hitter in a Cubs season (Williams set the mark at 42 in 1970). PCA is sitting at 47. He broke that record by five. He's the first Cub to hit 47 home runs.

Nine games remain in the regular season. The Cubs play through September 28, then Wild Card opens September 29. Every game PCA steps to the plate and every time he reaches base, the possibility is there. Three stolen bases. Nine games. Do the math.

This is a front-row seat to something very rare. ⚾

### STORY 2: Judge 10-Day IL

**Long-Form Post**
Aaron Judge is on the injured list again — and this time, the postseason timeline is genuinely uncertain. ⚾

The Yankees officially placed Judge on the 10-day injured list on September 18, retroactive to September 17, with a right calf strain. He was lifted for a pinch-hitter in the September 16 game against the Twins after experiencing stiffness in his lower right leg.

The timing is brutal. Judge had just returned from the injured list on September 8 after missing 99 days with a rib stress fracture. He played 10 days before the calf issue ended his regular season — at least for now. He is eligible to return by the final days of the regular season, which means his status for the postseason is genuinely in question.

When reporters asked manager Aaron Boone whether Judge would be back for the postseason, Boone gave the most honest answer he could: "I don't know."

The Yankees have clinched their 61st postseason berth — a record. They will play in October regardless. But the version of the Yankees that enters October without Aaron Judge is a very different team than the one that enters with him. He's the gravitational center of their lineup, the player who changes how opposing pitchers operate from the first batter of the game.

The calf injury is different from the rib fracture — it's a muscle issue rather than a bone injury. Whether it heals on Judge's accelerated timeline or takes longer is something nobody can answer right now. Including Aaron Boone. 🗓️

### STORY 3: Misiorowski vs. Orioles

**Long-Form Post**
Every Jacob Misiorowski start in September is a tick closer to history. Today's is no different. ⚾

The Brewers' 24-year-old ace takes the mound at Camden Yards in Baltimore on Saturday, carrying a 1.89 ERA (best in the majors) and approximately 243 strikeouts (also the most in baseball) into a start that could push him even further toward the most dominant pitching season in Milwaukee Brewers history.

The milestone within reach: Ben Sheets' franchise record of 264 strikeouts, set in 2004. Misiorowski needs approximately 21 more to break it. With a few starts remaining, getting there is entirely realistic — and it would cap a season that already includes one of the most jaw-dropping stat lines in the game.

He became the first pitcher in MLB Statcast history to throw 1,000 pitches at 100 mph or higher in a single season — by a wide margin. His fastest recorded pitch this year: 105.5 mph. That number alone separates him from virtually every pitcher who has ever played the game.

The Cy Young market says everything. Misiorowski is listed at -700. The next closest pitcher is Chris Sale at +500. That is not a race. That is a formality. When November arrives, the award will go to Lubbock — to a 24-year-old who rewrote what we thought was possible on a pitcher's mound. 🏆

### STORY 5: Alvarez Triple Crown

**Long-Form Post**
The Triple Crown chase was one of the great storylines of the 2026 season. The math has finally caught up with it. 📊

Yordan Alvarez still leads the American League in batting average, sitting at .310. That part of the crown is his. But the other two pieces — home runs and RBIs — have slipped out of reach.

Alvarez has 38 home runs, trailing Yandy Caminero of the Tampa Bay Rays, who is sitting at approximately 40-41. He has approximately 99 RBIs, trailing Pete Alonso of the Baltimore Orioles, who is at approximately 102. With about nine games remaining in the regular season, catching either player would require both a historic hot streak for Alvarez and a historic collapse from his rivals at exactly the same time.

The reason the gap exists is a second-half slump that Alvarez has been unable to shake. Since August 2, he has hit .242 with just three home runs and approximately 13 RBIs in more than 35 games. The timing was devastating for his Triple Crown chances — the exact weeks when he needed to separate himself, he went quiet.

If Alvarez had won it, he would have been the first Triple Crown winner since Miguel Cabrera in 2012 and only the second since Carl Yastrzemski in 1967. No Astros player has ever won the Triple Crown. Those facts remain facts — the chase was real, the historic company would have been real, and Alvarez gave fans something worth watching all summer.

He still leads the AL in batting average. That's not nothing. But October is arriving, and the Triple Crown chase is closing its books. 📊
