### Long-Form Post Three. That's all that stands between Pete Crow-Armstrong and baseball history. 🔥 With the Cubs winning 5-4 in Cincinnati on Saturday, PCA continues one of the most compelling individual pursuits in the game right now. He stands at 47 home runs and 37 stolen bases — three steals from joining the most exclusive club in baseball: the 40-40 season. Only six players in the history of the major leagues have ever hit 40 home runs and stolen 40 bases in the same season. Jose Canseco did it in 1988. Barry Bonds in 1996. Alex Rodriguez in 1998. Alfonso Soriano in 2006. Ronald Acuña Jr. in 2023. Shohei Ohtani in 2023. That's it. Six players. Six times in more than 140 years of professional baseball. PCA would be No. 7. And it would be the first 40-40 season by a Cub. Ever. In franchise history. He's already broken Billy Williams' all-time record for home runs by a left-handed hitter in a Cubs season (Williams set the mark at 42 in 1970). PCA is sitting at 47. He broke that record by five. He's the first Cub to hit 47 home runs. Nine games remain in the regular season. The Cubs play through September 28, then Wild Card opens September 29. Every game PCA steps to the plate and every time he reaches base, the possibility is there. Three stolen bases. Nine games. Do the math. This is a front-row seat to something very rare. ⚾ ### Image Post Caption 47 HR. 37 SB. 3 steals from history. PCA is chasing what no Cub has ever done. 🔥 #Cubs #LetsGo

### Long-Form Post The Triple Crown chase was one of the great storylines of the 2026 season. The math has finally caught up with it. 📊 Yordan Alvarez still leads the American League in batting average, sitting at .310. That part of the crown is his. But the other two pieces — home runs and RBIs — have slipped out of reach. Alvarez has 38 home runs, trailing Yandy Caminero of the Tampa Bay Rays, who is sitting at approximately 40-41. He has approximately 99 RBIs, trailing Pete Alonso of the Baltimore Orioles, who is at approximately 102. With about nine games remaining in the regular season, catching either player would require both a historic hot streak for Alvarez and a historic collapse from his rivals at exactly the same time. The reason the gap exists is a second-half slump that Alvarez has been unable to shake. Since August 2, he has hit .242 with just three home runs and approximately 13 RBIs in more than 35 games. The timing was devastating for his Triple Crown chances — the exact weeks when he needed to separate himself, he went quiet. If Alvarez had won it, he would have been the first Triple Crown winner since Miguel Cabrera in 2012 and only the second since Carl Yastrzemski in 1967. No Astros player has ever won the Triple Crown. Those facts remain facts — the chase was real, the historic company would have been real, and Alvarez gave fans something worth watching all summer. He still leads the AL in batting average. That's not nothing. But October is arriving, and the Triple Crown chase is closing its books. 📊 ### Image Post Caption .310 BA leads the AL.
