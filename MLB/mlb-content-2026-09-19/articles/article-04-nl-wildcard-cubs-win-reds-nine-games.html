Cubs Win 5-4 in Cincinnati: Nine Games Left to Lock Up NL Wild Card 1

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | September 19, 2026

Chicago defeated the Reds 5-4 on Saturday, extending their lead in the NL Wild Card race with nine games remaining before the postseason opens September 29.

One more. The Cubs won 5-4 in Cincinnati on Saturday, September 19, adding another win to a tally that has them sitting in the No. 1 Wild Card position in the National League with nine games left to play before October.

The NL Wild Card race has been one of the defining storylines of the 2026 second half. Three teams — the Cubs, Phillies and Padres — have jockeyed for position since late July, with the Diamondbacks occasionally threatening before fading in the final weeks. Saturday's win extends Chicago's position at the top of the Wild Card standings and tightens the grip on home-field advantage in the Wild Card round.

Where the Wild Card Stands

The Cubs occupy the No. 1 Wild Card position with approximately nine games remaining for all teams in the race. They hold tiebreaker advantages over both the Philadelphia Phillies and the San Diego Padres based on head-to-head results — which means even if one of those teams catches Chicago in the win column, the Cubs would retain the higher seed in a three-way tie.

The Phillies sit approximately one game behind in the loss column. The Padres are approximately 1.5 to 2.0 games back. The Arizona Diamondbacks, who were in the conversation earlier in the month, have fallen 4+ games off the pace and appear to be eliminated from Wild Card contention.

The Wild Card round begins September 29, with the three-game series format that was introduced to prioritize regular-season performance. The No. 1 Wild Card team hosts all three games if necessary — and for the Cubs, that means Wrigley Field in October.

The Larger Bracket Picture

The Milwaukee Brewers clinched the NL Central for the fourth consecutive season and appear positioned for the No. 1 NL seed, which comes with a first-round bye. The Los Angeles Dodgers are expected to earn the No. 2 seed and another bye. The Atlanta Braves are likely positioned for the No. 3 seed, which would mean a Division Series against the No. 2 Wild Card team.

For the Cubs, holding WC1 means a first-round home series and, potentially, a matchup with the No. 1 or No. 2 seed in the Division Series round. Neither matchup is ideal — but home field in the Wild Card gives them a genuine chance to advance through the bracket, particularly with Pete Crow-Armstrong creating problems for opposing pitchers and defenders every inning he plays.

What PCA Means to October

The Cubs' postseason hopes are inextricably linked to what Pete Crow-Armstrong does. He is not just the team's best hitter — he is the player who forces opposing managers into decisions they would prefer not to make. Do you throw to his power? Risk the stolen base? Give him a free base and pitch around him?

His 47 home runs, 37 stolen bases, and .956 OPS make him one of the most dangerous two-way offensive players the game has seen in years. In a three-game Wild Card series, he could be the difference between a swift exit and an October run worth remembering.

What's Next

Nine games remain before the regular season closes. The Cubs will play through September 28, then open Wild Card play on September 29. Every win through that stretch tightens their grip on WC1 and builds the momentum heading into a postseason bracket that could extend their season well into October — or end it after three games. Saturday's 5-4 win in Cincinnati was one more step in the right direction.
