Judge on IL Again: Calf Strain Puts Yankees Captain's Postseason Status in Doubt

By Ryan Calloway | Ballpark Banter | September 19, 2026

Aaron Judge was placed on the 10-day injured list with a right calf strain — retroactive to September 17 — just ten days after returning from a 99-day absence for a rib stress fracture. Manager Aaron Boone won't commit to a postseason timeline.

Nine days. That is how long Aaron Judge played between returning from one injured list and being placed on another.

The New York Yankees officially placed Judge on the 10-day injured list on September 18, retroactive to September 17, with a right calf strain. He had returned to action on September 8 after missing 99 days with a rib stress fracture — the longest absence of his career. Now, with nine regular-season games remaining and the postseason starting September 29, the question of whether Judge will be available for October is entirely open.

Manager Aaron Boone offered the most honest answer he had when reporters asked about Judge's postseason outlook: "I don't know."

How It Happened

Judge was lifted for a pinch-hitter in the September 16 game against the Minnesota Twins after experiencing stiffness in his lower right leg. He exited early in the game after going 0-for-2 at the plate. Boone described the decision to remove him as precautionary in the immediate aftermath — "Hopefully it's something we were able to catch" — but the subsequent diagnosis told a different story.

A right calf strain is a muscle injury and, in theory, heals on a different timeline than the rib stress fracture that cost Judge the bulk of his regular season. He is eligible to return by the final days of the regular season, which would put him in line for the Wild Card round if the team's medical staff clears him in time. But "eligible" and "ready" are different things, and Boone's "I don't know" reflects genuine medical uncertainty rather than caution.

The Yankees Without Judge

The Yankees have already clinched their 61st postseason berth — a franchise record. They will play in October regardless of Judge's availability. But the version of the Yankees that enters the Wild Card or the Division Series without Judge is significantly less dangerous than the one that enters with him.

Judge is the gravitational center of the New York lineup. Opposing pitchers spend entire series gameplanning around how to limit his impact — which in turn creates opportunities for the hitters around him. Without that gravitational pull, the entire lineup operates differently. Ben Rice has contributed meaningfully in the second half, and Anthony Volpe has been solid throughout the year, but there is no functional equivalent to what Judge provides.

The Yankees have the depth to survive a Judge absence in a short series. Whether they can go deep in the bracket without him is the question that the next two to three weeks will answer.

A Difficult Season's Latest Chapter

The 2026 season has been an exercise in frustration for Judge. He injured his rib in late June and missed more than three months. He returned September 8 with the kind of fanfare that accompanies a player of his magnitude returning to a pennant race. He hit a three-run home run in his first game back. He played nine days. And now the calf.

The injuries do not diminish what Judge is as a player — or what he will be when fully healthy. But they make 2026 a season of almost-what-could-have-been for the Yankees captain, who now faces the possibility of watching October from the dugout instead of driving it from the batter's box.

What's Next

Judge is eligible to come off the injured list by the final days of the regular season. The Yankees' medical staff will evaluate his progress over the next several days. If he returns healthy before September 28, he could be ready for the Wild Card round that starts September 29. If his return is delayed, the Yankees will enter the postseason without their best player and manage accordingly. The answer to "I don't know" will come soon enough.
