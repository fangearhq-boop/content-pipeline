The Triple Crown Math Is Brutal: Alvarez Leads BA but Trails Where It Counts

By Ryan Calloway | Ballpark Banter | September 19, 2026

With nine games remaining, Yordan Alvarez leads the American League in batting average but trails in home runs and RBIs — a gap too large to close in the final stretch of 2026.

Yordan Alvarez is still hitting .310. He still leads the American League in batting average. He is still one of the best hitters in baseball.

But the Triple Crown — the rarest individual achievement in a sport full of them — is not going to happen in 2026. The math, with nine games remaining, has finally made that official.

Where the Numbers Stand

Alvarez enters the final nine games of the regular season at .310 batting average, approximately 38 home runs and approximately 99 RBIs. Those are numbers that would define a career year for nearly any other hitter in the American League.

In the Triple Crown race, they are not enough. Yandy Caminero of the Tampa Bay Rays leads the AL in home runs at approximately 40-41, trailing Alvarez by 2-3 dingers entering the final push. Pete Alonso of the Baltimore Orioles leads the AL in RBIs at approximately 102 — three ahead of Alvarez. Closing either gap in nine games while relying on the other leaders to simultaneously collapse is not a realistic scenario. Both gaps would need to close at once, and that has not happened.

The August Collapse

The reason the gaps exist is not a mystery. Alvarez has been cold since August 2 in a way that has been nearly impossible to reverse. Since that date, he has hit approximately .242 with just three home runs and roughly 13 RBIs in more than 35 games. The timing was devastating — the exact stretch of the season when a Triple Crown contender needs to be adding separation, Alvarez went quiet.

The causes of offensive slumps at the major league level are rarely simple. Pitchers adjust. Scouting reports evolve. The physical toll of a 162-game season accumulates. Whatever combination of factors contributed to Alvarez's August and early September output, the result is the same: a Triple Crown bid that peaked in July and has been losing ground ever since.

The Historical Company He Would Have Kept

The significance of what Alvarez was chasing this summer is worth acknowledging, even as the chase ends. The Triple Crown — leading the league in batting average, home runs and RBIs simultaneously — has been won just twice in the last 59 years. Miguel Cabrera did it with the 2012 Detroit Tigers. Carl Yastrzemski did it with the 1967 Boston Red Sox. In the era of modern specialization, defensive shifts, and pitcher-first roster construction, the Triple Crown has become essentially impossible.

No Houston Astros player has ever won the Triple Crown. If Alvarez had completed the feat, he would have done it against the weight of an organization's entire history and in one of the most competitive offensive eras the AL has seen in years.

He still leads the American League in batting average with nine games left. That is a genuine achievement. But the home runs and RBIs are not coming — and the history that was within range in July is not coming back.

What's Next

Alvarez and the Astros will play out the final nine games of the regular season. While the Triple Crown is out of reach, the AL batting title remains within Alvarez's grasp — and there is still a postseason to be played. The Astros' October path will determine whether this season is remembered for what Alvarez nearly did statistically, or for what the team accomplishes once the bracket is set. For now, the Triple Crown chapter closes. The October chapter opens September 29.
