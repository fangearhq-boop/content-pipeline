Three Steals from History: PCA's 40-40 Chase Enters Final Nine Games

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | September 19, 2026

Pete Crow-Armstrong sits at 47 home runs and 37 stolen bases — three steals from becoming just the seventh player in MLB history to hit 40 HR and steal 40 bases in the same season.

The Cubs won 5-4 in Cincinnati on Saturday. That matters. But the number everyone is watching is not on the scoreboard — it's the three stolen bases that separate Pete Crow-Armstrong from the most exclusive individual achievement in baseball.

With nine games remaining in the 2026 regular season, PCA stands at 47 home runs and 37 stolen bases. He needs three more steals to join the 40-40 club — a list so short that you can count its members on one hand and still have a finger left over.

The Company He Would Join

Only six players in major league history have ever hit 40 home runs and stolen 40 bases in the same season. Jose Canseco did it first in 1988. Barry Bonds followed in 1996. Alex Rodriguez in 1998. Alfonso Soriano in 2006. Then a 17-year gap before Ronald Acuña Jr. in 2023 and Shohei Ohtani in 2023 joined the list in the same season.

That is six players across more than 140 years of professional baseball. If PCA gets three steals over the next nine games, he becomes No. 7 — and he does it as a 22-year-old center fielder who still has a decade of prime baseball ahead of him.

He would also become the first player in Cubs franchise history to ever accomplish the feat. The Cubs have had legends in their uniform since 1876. No one wearing that blue and red has ever reached 40-40. Not Banks. Not Santo. Not Dawson. Not Sosa. Not Lee. PCA would be the first.

The Home Run Record Already Broken

The stolen base chase tends to overshadow what PCA has already accomplished on the home run side of the ledger. At 47 home runs, he has already broken Billy Williams' Cubs record for the most home runs by a left-handed hitter in a single season. Williams set that mark at 42 in 1970 — the year the Hall of Famer won the National League batting title. PCA cleared it by five.

He is also the first Cub to hit 47 home runs in a season — and the first to reach 40 home runs since Derrek Lee hit 46 in 2005. The power numbers alone make this one of the most productive offensive seasons in Cubs history. Add the stolen bases and the MVP case, and it is something altogether rarer.

The Math Going Forward

Nine games. Three steals. The math is simple. The execution is harder. PCA averaged just under one stolen base attempt every three games in September — a pace that, over nine games, projects to roughly three attempts. Whether all three succeed, and whether the Cubs put him in position to run, depends on game situations, pitching matchups and the granular decisions that September baseball demands.

What is not in doubt is that PCA will keep competing at the level that got him to this number. His .282 batting average, .375 on-base percentage, and .956 OPS in 2026 represent a complete offensive player who reaches base at the frequency necessary to steal bases at volume. The opportunity will be there. The question is whether the moment delivers.

What's Next

The Cubs play their final nine regular-season games before the Wild Card round opens September 29. Every game PCA plays carries the possibility of a stolen base, a home run — or both. The Wild Card itself, if the Cubs hold their current position, could extend the season further. But the 40-40 window closes with the regular season. Nine games. Three steals. The countdown is on.
