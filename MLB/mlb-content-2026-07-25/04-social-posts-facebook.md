### STORY 1: Baseball Hall of Fame Weekend — Beltran and Andruw Jones

#### Long-Form Post — 8:00 AM ET

Tomorrow, July 26, in Cooperstown, New York, Carlos Beltran and Andruw Jones will walk across the stage and into the Baseball Hall of Fame.

They arrive by different roads and carry different legacies, but both belong.

Beltran was one of the most complete players of his generation. A switch-hitter with power, speed, and a center fielder's instincts, he hit .279/.350/.490 over twenty seasons, belted 435 home runs, drove in 1,587 runs, and stole 312 bases. He won nine Gold Gloves and went to nine All-Star Games. His 2004 NLCS with the Astros — 8 home runs and 14 RBIs in eight games — remains one of the great postseason performances in memory. His candidacy carried the weight of the Astros sign-stealing investigation, where his name appeared in the commissioner's report. The BBWAA voted to induct him based on what he did between the lines over a long career. He earned it.

Jones' case is different — and in some ways more compelling for those who follow the sport deeply. His .254/.330/.486 career line and 434 home runs are Hall of Fame numbers on their own. But what Jones did in center field is what makes him unique. Ten consecutive Gold Gloves from 1998 to 2007. Advanced defensive metrics show him as arguably the greatest defensive outfielder in the history of the game. The conventional eye told you the same thing if you watched him long enough: no one got to balls Jones got to. No one threw like that. He was something different in the outfield.

Two very different players. Both Hall of Famers. See you in Cooperstown tomorrow.

### STORY 2: Ohtani Pitching — No Timetable

#### Long-Form Post — 10:30 AM ET

Dave Roberts said Saturday what Dodgers fans have been bracing to hear: there is no timetable for Shohei Ohtani's return to the mound.

The Dodgers manager confirmed Ohtani will not make his next scheduled pitching start and that his return is indefinitely unknown. Ohtani has been dealing with left knee irritation since early July — an issue that may be connected to a previous surgery. He threw approximately 31 pitches in a bullpen session at Citizens Bank Park on July 22. Roberts called it a "normal side session." But after that session, the Dodgers took more time to evaluate, and Saturday's answer was: we don't know when he pitches in a game again.

What Ohtani was doing before the injury is worth acknowledging. He was 8-2 with a 1.79 ERA, 95 strikeouts, and 85.2 innings pitched before he last took the mound on July 3. On pace, those numbers had him in the center of the Cy Young conversation in the American League — and making that case from a team that was already building a case for the best record in baseball.

He continues to hit. Roberts confirmed that there is no IL consideration; Ohtani will remain in the lineup as a designated hitter. He is slashing .288/.397/.536 this season. The best pitcher in baseball is still doing elite work at the plate every day. But the mound date remains unknown.

### STORY 3: Phillies Records — Sanchez and Schwarber

#### Long-Form Post — 12:30 PM ET

The Phillies have had a remarkable week of individual performances that deserve more attention than they've gotten.

Cristopher Sanchez extended his scoreless innings streak to 41⅔ innings, breaking a 115-year-old Phillies franchise record that had been held by Grover Cleveland Alexander. Let that sit for a moment. Alexander is one of the greatest pitchers in the history of the sport — a Hall of Famer who went 373-208 over 20 seasons and set records that have stood for over a century. Sanchez just cleared one of them.

Scoreless streaks of this length are not built on one great pitch. They come from consistent sequencing, defending the inner half of the plate, trusting your defense, and executing on the days when everything tells you it might unravel. Sanchez has done this over an extended stretch and is now in territory that has belonged to one of the greatest pitchers who ever lived.

Meanwhile, Kyle Schwarber hit his 30th home run this week, reaching that mark in the fewest team games in Phillies franchise history. He leads all of Major League Baseball in home runs. Going into the final days of July with 30 home runs, leading the league, is a statement about what Schwarber has become in Philadelphia.

Two performances. Both franchise-defining. Both this week. If you're a Phillies fan, these are the kinds of weeks you tell people about later.

### STORY 4: Trade Deadline — August 3, Nine Days Away

#### Long-Form Post — 3:00 PM ET

August 3 is nine days away. The MLB Trade Deadline is accelerating.

Here is where the market stands today.

**Mason Miller** — The Padres closer is one of the most coveted arms available. Under team control through 2029, a 2026 All-Star, and dominant in the ninth inning. The Yankees "love the idea" of acquiring him, per reporting. A Miller-to-New York deal would reshape the bullpen of one of baseball's strongest rotation teams.

**Tarik Skubal** — The Tigers ace is the biggest name in the deadline market. ESPN's Jeff Passan puts trade odds at 60%, though rival executives believe Detroit may hold him. Skubal made his preference known publicly last week — he would rather finish the season in Detroit — but the franchise is 6.5 games back in the AL Central. When you're outside a Wild Card spot with nine days to the deadline, the phone calls change. The Dodgers are the frontrunner if Detroit moves him.

**Angels in Fire-Sale Mode** — Los Angeles is openly available to trade controllable players. Jose Soriano and Reid Detmers are available starters. Zach Neto at shortstop is a priority target for multiple teams, including the Red Sox. The Angels cleaning house before August 3 could produce several of the deadline's most significant deals.

**Rays Buying Big** — Tampa Bay at 59-43 (best record in the AL) is expected to spend at the deadline. The Rays' ability to acquire talent cheaply and develop it quickly makes them dangerous when they add to a roster that's already this good.

Nine days. The phone calls are constant.

### STORY 5: The Milestones Notebook

#### Long-Form Post — 10:00 PM ET

July 2026 has been one of the great statistical months in recent baseball history. Several players are either approaching records, breaking them, or doing things that have rarely been done.

**Jose Ramirez (Guardians):** On pace to become only the second player in baseball history — after Barry Bonds — to hit 30 home runs and steal 30 bases in three consecutive seasons. Ramirez did it in 2024 and 2025. He's doing it again in 2026. The 30/30 season is rare. Three consecutive? Only Bonds has done it.

**Freddie Freeman (Dodgers):** Tracking toward 400 career home runs and 2,500 career hits — a combination only 18 players in baseball history have ever achieved. Those 18 include some of the game's all-time greats. Freeman is in that conversation.

**Jacob Misiorowski (Brewers):** The Brewers starter registered a 105.5 mph pitch this week — the fastest ever recorded from a starting pitcher under modern pitch-tracking technology. It ties Ben Joyce for the third-fastest pitch in tracked history. A starter hitting 105.5 mph changes what scouts and coaches think is possible for the position going forward.

**Aroldis Chapman:** Earlier this month, Chapman recorded career strikeout No. 1,364, becoming the all-time career strikeout leader among relief pitchers. The record had been held by Hoyt Wilhelm — a Hall of Famer — for more than 50 years. Chapman has now cleared it.

Records fall in clusters sometimes. This is one of those months.
