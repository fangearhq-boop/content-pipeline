MLB Trade Deadline: Nine Days Out, the Market Is Moving

By Marcus Cole | July 25, 2026

August 3 at 6 PM ET is the MLB Trade Deadline. Nine days away as of Saturday. The phones are active, the names are leaking, and the market is taking shape around a handful of players whose availability could reshape what October looks like for multiple organizations. Here is where things stand today.

Mason Miller

The San Diego Padres closer is the most coveted relief pitcher available, and possibly the most coveted pitcher period. Miller is under team control through 2029, was named to the 2026 All-Star team, and has been dominant closing games in the ninth inning for San Diego. The Yankees have emerged as a serious suitor — multiple reports indicate New York "loves the idea" of adding Miller to a bullpen that already supports one of the strongest rotations in baseball.

Miller's profile is exactly what contenders look for: young, controllable, with elite stuff that has proven durable. The cost to acquire him will be significant. The teams willing to pay it are teams that believe they are close enough to a championship to justify thinning their prospect pipeline.

Tarik Skubal

The Detroit Tigers ace is the headline starter on the market. ESPN's Jeff Passan reported trade odds at 60%, and rival executives around the league have suggested the number may be higher. The situation is complicated by Skubal's public statement last week, when he indicated his preference is to finish the season in Detroit. That preference matters, but it does not change the arithmetic: the Tigers are 6.5 games out in the AL Central and outside the Wild Card picture with nine days to the deadline.

When a franchise is sitting outside a playoff spot this late in the season, the calculus for keeping an ace on an expiring (or near-expiring) deal changes. The Dodgers have been identified as the frontrunner if Detroit moves Skubal. Los Angeles has the prospect depth to make Detroit's front office take notice and the rotation situation — with Ohtani's mound timeline now indefinitely unknown — that makes adding a true No. 1 starter logical.

The Angels Are Selling

Los Angeles is in full fire-sale mode. The Angels have made clear that controllable, tradeable players are available, and the names attached to that availability are significant. Jose Soriano and Reid Detmers are both available starting pitchers who could help contending rotations. Zach Neto at shortstop has drawn interest from multiple teams, with the Boston Red Sox identified as a priority suitor. The Angels cleaning house before August 3 has the potential to produce several of the deadline's most meaningful transactions.

The Rays Are Buying

Tampa Bay sits at the top of the American League standings and is expected to be one of the deadline's most active buyers. The Rays have built a 59-43 record — the best in the AL — and rival executives have indicated the organization is prepared to spend to reinforce a roster already strong enough to compete for a championship. Tampa Bay's ability to acquire and develop talent efficiently makes them a dangerous team when they are adding to a healthy roster rather than rebuilding around one.

The Mets and Phillies

New York is in full sell mode. Clay Holmes and Freddy Peralta are available starters, along with bullpen pieces that could interest contenders. The Phillies, meanwhile, have become "more willing to discuss higher-caliber arms," per reporting — a sign that Philadelphia is engaged in the deadline market from a position of strength rather than necessity.

What's Next

The next nine days will determine whether the 2026 postseason field holds its current shape or gets reshuffled by deadline moves that shift competitive balance. The deals that involve Miller, Skubal, and Neto specifically could change the structure of playoff matchups in both leagues. August 3 at 6 PM ET. The clock is running.
