Dave Roberts Says No Timetable for Ohtani Return to the Mound

By Jake Torres | July 25, 2026

The question Dodgers fans have been dreading received its clearest answer yet on Saturday. Dave Roberts confirmed that Shohei Ohtani will not make his next scheduled pitching start and that there is no timetable for his return to the mound. Indefinitely unknown were the words, and the silence around them was loud.

What Roberts Said

The Dodgers manager addressed the media Saturday and left no room for optimism on a timeline. Ohtani is not on the injured list — Roberts confirmed that point specifically — but he will not pitch on the team's current road trip, and the Dodgers are not prepared to say when he pitches in a game again. The organization expects him to take the mound again in 2026. That expectation is the only direction they would give.

Ohtani has been managing left knee irritation since early July, an issue that Roberts and the organization have linked to a previous surgery. On July 22, he threw approximately 31 pitches in a bullpen session at Citizens Bank Park in Philadelphia. Roberts called it a normal side session at the time and indicated a follow-up session could come in about a week. What came instead was a pause and then Saturday's confirmation that there is no next date.

What He Was Doing Before the Injury

The context of what Ohtani was doing before the knee became an issue makes this a particularly painful story to track. When he last took the mound on July 3, Ohtani was 8-2 with a 1.79 ERA, 95 strikeouts, and 85.2 innings pitched. He was squarely in the center of the American League Cy Young conversation — possibly leading it. A starter going 8-2 with a sub-2.00 ERA before the All-Star break, while also serving as a middle-of-the-order designated hitter for the best team in baseball, is something the sport has never seen from any player in any era.

The Dodgers were building a case for the best record in baseball when Ohtani last pitched. His absence from the rotation has not derailed that case, but it has changed the texture of what this team is trying to do heading into October.

He Continues to Hit

Roberts was clear on one thing: Ohtani's knee situation does not affect his availability as a designated hitter. He is in the lineup every day, and the offensive production has not dropped. Through 96 games, Ohtani is slashing .288/.397/.536. He is drawing walks, hitting for power, and doing everything a team could ask of a DH.

That distinction matters. The Dodgers are not losing Ohtani; they are losing one half of what makes him the most valuable player in baseball. The offensive production of an elite hitter remains in their lineup every night. What is missing is the elite starting pitcher — the one who was tracking toward a Cy Young Award before the knee forced his absence.

What's Next

The Dodgers will continue to evaluate Ohtani's knee on a day-by-day basis, with no public target date for a bullpen session or a game start. The trade deadline is August 3, and the organization will have to decide whether to add rotation depth in light of Ohtani's uncertain status. The best-case scenario remains a return to the mound at some point in 2026. What that looks like — a regular-season start, a postseason role, or nothing at all this year — is a question the Dodgers cannot yet answer.
