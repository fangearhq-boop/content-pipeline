Sanchez Breaks 115-Year Phillies Record, Schwarber Hits 30th Home Run Faster Than Anyone in Franchise History

By Ryan Calloway | July 25, 2026

Philadelphia had a remarkable week of individual baseball performances, and most of it happened quietly while the rest of the league focused elsewhere. Two Phillies franchise records fell in the same stretch of games. Both deserve more attention than they have received.

Cristopher Sanchez and a Record That Stood for 115 Years

Cristopher Sanchez extended his consecutive scoreless innings streak to 41⅔ innings this week, breaking a Phillies franchise record that had belonged to Grover Cleveland Alexander for 115 years. Let the weight of that number settle for a moment.

Alexander is one of the greatest pitchers in baseball history. He went 373-208 over 20 seasons, won three Cy Young-equivalent pitching titles in a single season in 1916, and was elected to the Hall of Fame in 1938. The records he set with the Phillies in the early twentieth century were never expected to fall — they had stood for over a century through generations of pitchers who had access to better training, longer seasons, and deeper medical staffs. Sanchez just cleared one of them.

Scoreless streaks of this length are not built on dominant stuff alone. They require execution on imperfect days. They require sequencing, the ability to defend the inner half of the plate, and the discipline to trust a defense that will not always convert every opportunity. Sanchez has maintained all of those qualities over an extended stretch, and he is now in territory that had exclusively belonged to one of the greatest pitchers who ever lived.

The 28-year-old left-hander has been one of the most reliable starters in the National League this season, operating with a combination of deception, command, and ground-ball generation that makes him effective without the triple-digit velocity that dominates contemporary pitching discussions. His streak is a different kind of statement — one built on craft rather than raw power.

Kyle Schwarber: Fastest to 30 in Franchise History

On the other end of the offensive spectrum, Kyle Schwarber hit his 30th home run of the 2026 season this week, reaching that mark in the fewest team games in Phillies franchise history. He leads all of Major League Baseball in home runs heading into the final days of July.

That combination — leading the league in home runs at the pace of roughly mid-season — says something meaningful about what Schwarber has become in Philadelphia. He arrived as a left-handed power hitter who drew walks and struck out frequently, and that profile remains accurate. But his ability to maintain elite home run production across a full season, consistently, has elevated him into a conversation about the best power hitters in the game today.

Leading the league in home runs with the calendar approaching August is a statement. Reaching 30 faster than any Phillies player in history — a franchise that includes Hall of Famers, Silver Slugger Award winners, and multiple MVP candidates — is something more specific. It belongs in the record books, and it belongs there for a long time.

Two Records in One Week

What makes this week significant is not that one record fell or one milestone was crossed — it is that both happened on the same team in the same week. Pitching and power, on opposite ends of the skill spectrum, both producing franchise-first moments simultaneously. For a team positioning itself as a legitimate National League contender heading into the August 3 trade deadline, the timing is not lost on anyone in the clubhouse.

What's Next

Sanchez will continue to push his scoreless streak as the Phillies rotation carries the team through a stretch of meaningful games. The longer the streak extends, the more historical company he keeps. Schwarber, already at 30 with two months of regular season remaining, is in a realistic position to reach 40 or beyond and contend for the NL home run crown. The Phillies' deadline activity — whether they add or stand pat — will tell the rest of the league whether Philadelphia believes in this group enough to push all the way through October.
