The Milestones Notebook: July 2026 Is One for the Record Books

By Jake Torres | July 25, 2026

Records fall one at a time. Sometimes, though, they cluster. July 2026 is one of those months — a stretch in which multiple players are either approaching historic thresholds, breaking them, or doing things that have never been done at all. Here is what has been happening while the rest of the sports world looked elsewhere.

Jose Ramirez and the Three-Consecutive-Season 30/30

Jose Ramirez of the Cleveland Guardians is on pace to become only the second player in baseball history — after Barry Bonds — to hit 30 home runs and steal 30 bases in three consecutive seasons. Ramirez accomplished the 30/30 combination in 2024 and repeated it in 2025. He is doing it again in 2026.

The 30/30 season has always been rare. The combination of power and speed required to reach both thresholds in one year places a player in a short list of the most complete offensive talents the sport has ever seen. Doing it in back-to-back seasons doubles the difficulty. Doing it three consecutive times has happened exactly once in the history of the sport, and the player who did it was Barry Bonds — one of the greatest offensive players in the history of baseball.

Ramirez is not a footnote. He has been one of the most productive hitters in the American League for nearly a decade. But if he finishes 2026 with at least 30 home runs and 30 stolen bases, he will be standing beside Bonds in one of the most exclusive statistical neighborhoods in baseball history.

Freddie Freeman Tracking 400 and 2,500

Freddie Freeman of the Los Angeles Dodgers is on a career path toward 400 home runs and 2,500 career hits — a combination that only 18 players in baseball history have ever achieved. That list is composed almost entirely of Hall of Famers and players widely regarded as all-time greats.

Freeman has built his career on consistent, elite offensive production rather than any single dominant skill. He hits for average, draws walks, provides power, and has done all of it at a high level across parts of 16 major league seasons. Reaching both thresholds simultaneously would formalize what those who have watched him closely already understand: Freeman belongs in the conversation about the best first basemen of his generation, and more broadly, among the best hitters the sport has seen.

Jacob Misiorowski: 105.5 Miles Per Hour From a Starting Pitcher

The Milwaukee Brewers starter registered a 105.5 mph pitch this week — the fastest pitch ever recorded from a starting pitcher under modern pitch-tracking technology. The number ties Ben Joyce for the third-fastest pitch in tracked history, regardless of role.

Starters do not throw 105 mph. That is the established boundary, the working assumption of scouts, coaches, and front offices who have tracked velocity for decades. Starting pitchers manage their pitch counts, pace their effort across six or seven innings, and generally operate with velocity that preserves their arm over a long season. The assumption that starters top out well below the upper limits of what relievers can produce has been embedded in how the position is understood.

Misiorowski's 105.5 mph changes that conversation. If a starting pitcher can sustain that level of velocity — even occasionally — then the ceiling for what the position can produce has to be reconsidered. Scouts and pitching coaches are paying attention.

Aroldis Chapman and the Relief Strikeout Record

Earlier this month, Aroldis Chapman recorded career strikeout No. 1,364 and became the all-time career strikeout leader among relief pitchers. The record had belonged to Hoyt Wilhelm — a Hall of Famer who pitched from 1952 to 1972 and helped establish what a dominant relief career looks like — for more than 50 years.

Chapman arrived in the major leagues throwing triple-digit heat in a way that had rarely been seen at any level, and he has sustained that ability across a long career of closing games for multiple organizations. The strikeout record is the mathematical accumulation of decades of dominance from a position that typically sees careers end earlier than starters. Chapman has outlasted the record by a wide margin now, and his name sits alone at the top of that particular list.

What's Next

The final two months of the 2026 regular season will determine whether Ramirez completes the three-peat, whether Freeman reaches both thresholds this year or next, and whether Misiorowski can maintain 105 mph velocity through a full starting rotation cycle. Chapman's record is already set. These are the kinds of statistical moments that baseball remembers long after the standings are forgotten. July gave us several of them at once.
