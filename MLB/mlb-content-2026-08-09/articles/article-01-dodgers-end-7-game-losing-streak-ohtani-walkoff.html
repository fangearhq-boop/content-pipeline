Dodgers End 7-Game Losing Streak: Ohtani Walk-Off in the 10th Beats Arizona 2-1

By Marcus Cole

August 9, 2026

The Dodgers needed ten innings and a play that will live somewhere in the highlight archive of improbable baseball moments, but they finally broke through Friday night in Phoenix. Los Angeles ended a 7-game losing streak — the franchise's longest since 2017 — with a 2-1 walk-off victory over the Arizona Diamondbacks at Chase Field.

The losing streak was a genuine story: the Dodgers, with the game's biggest offseason acquisition in Tarik Skubal now in their rotation and Shohei Ohtani still unavailable to pitch, had sputtered at a moment when the NL West race demanded more. Friday changed the narrative.

Tucker, the Triples, and the Tenth

Kyle Tucker put Los Angeles on the board with a solo home run in the eighth inning, giving the Dodgers a 1-0 lead heading into the final two frames. It looked like enough — until Arizona's ninth inning turned the game into something else entirely.

Geraldo Perdomo tripled. Corbin Carroll stepped up and tripled again. Back-to-back triples in the ninth, and just like that, the game was tied at 1-1 and headed to extra innings.

Then came the tenth. Tucker, installed as the automatic runner at second base, advanced to third on a wild pitch. Shohei Ohtani stood in the batter's box with a chance to end it. He lined a sharp infield single — the ball deflected off first baseman Tim Tawa's glove — and Tucker came home with the winning run. Dodgers 2, Diamondbacks 1.

Ohtani: Still Winning Games Without a Mound

It was a reminder that even as a designated hitter, Ohtani occupies a unique category. He has not pitched since July 3 due to a knee injury and biceps issues; he has not given a specific timeline for returning to the mound and has expressed a desire not to rush the process. But as a hitter, he remained dangerous throughout the losing streak — including two home runs in the stretch — and delivered again Friday when the moment demanded it.

A walk-off hit in the tenth inning of a game that ended a seven-game slide is exactly the kind of moment that resets a clubhouse. The Dodgers needed it.

What's Next

Los Angeles snapped the losing streak, but the NL West standings will require continued attention. The Dodgers remain one of the favorites in the National League, particularly with Skubal joining a rotation that already had significant talent. They'll need the kind of production Friday demonstrated — especially from Ohtani, who is still delivering at the plate even as his pitching return remains open-ended.
