White Sox Are 60-56 and Leading the AL Central — One Year After Going 41-121

By Marcus Cole

August 9, 2026

Munetaka Murakami homered. Tristan Peters and Chase Meidroth each drove in two runs in a five-run sixth inning. Braden Montgomery added an RBI single in the eighth. The Chicago White Sox beat the Cleveland Guardians 6-3 on Friday to improve to 60-56 — first place in the American League Central by 3 games.

A year ago, the White Sox were 41-121. The worst record in modern baseball history.

The Turnaround

What the White Sox have done in twelve months is difficult to overstate. The 2024 team didn't just struggle — they set a record. The kind of record that follows a franchise. It was a full teardown, the kind of rebuild that suggests years of irrelevance before competitive baseball returns.

It took one year.

The 2026 White Sox did something that defined the turnaround: at the trade deadline, they were buyers. A team that spent 2024 shedding salary and accumulating prospects acquired Luis Castillo and Kyle Doyle — not sellers, not standing pat, but buying. That decision, made by a front office betting on itself, is why the White Sox stand where they do in August.

Murakami's Moment

Munetaka Murakami's home run on Friday was part of a broader story in Chicago. The Japanese slugger has been one of the White Sox's most watchable performers this season. His ability to drive the ball and his presence in the middle of the lineup have given Chicago a credibility it lacked in 2024.

The five-run sixth inning against Cleveland was a statement at-bat. Peters and Meidroth delivering in back-to-back contributions in the same frame represents exactly what a contending team's middle of the order should produce. Montgomery's eighth-inning RBI finished the scoring and the statement.

What's Next

The White Sox lead the AL Central at 60-56 with approximately two months of the regular season remaining. They are 3 games ahead of Cleveland and Minnesota. October baseball in Chicago is not a guarantee — the AL Central is competitive — but the White Sox have earned the right to play for it. One year after setting a record no franchise wants to own, they are in first place. Baseball's best turnaround story of 2026 continues.
