Red Sox 9-Game Win Streak Ends With 7-3 Loss to Oakland — But What a Run It Was

By Jake Torres

August 9, 2026

The Boston Red Sox lost to the Oakland Athletics 7-3 on Friday, ending a 9-game winning streak that was on the verge of something historically unprecedented. Boston left the bases loaded in the ninth inning during a comeback attempt that ultimately fell short.

The loss matters. But to understand what the Red Sox have done this season, you have to look past the final score.

Two Streaks in One Season

Earlier this summer, the Red Sox went on a 15-game winning streak that tied the 1946 franchise record — the Ted Williams era of Boston baseball. When that streak ended, the team was unrecognizable from the club that had started 32-46. They had turned their season around in one sustained run of excellence.

Then they won nine more in a row.

Had Friday's game gone differently — had they converted the bases-loaded opportunity in the ninth — the Red Sox would have been the first team in Major League Baseball history to post multiple 10-game winning streaks in a single season. They reached nine. It wasn't enough for the record, but it was plenty to announce that this team means something in the American League.

The Transformation

The number that puts everything in context is 32-46. That's where Boston stood before the first streak began. A team heading toward irrelevance by late June, they found something — a rotation coming together, a lineup producing, a clubhouse that believed in what it was building — and they haven't looked back since.

The 15-game streak in July and the 9-game run through early August represent two distinct chapters of the same story. The Red Sox were a rebuilding club at the deadline. Now they are competing for October.

What's Next

The streak is over, but the Red Sox's position in the American League is not. They are one of the hottest teams in baseball over the past two months, and the work they've done from 32-46 is not erased by a single loss to Oakland. The question now is whether they can sustain the level that made the streaks possible. Friday's loss suggests they're human — the rest of August will reveal the rest.
