Matt Olson Is 8th All-Time in Consecutive Games Played — The Iron Man Nobody Talks About

By Jake Torres

August 9, 2026

Matt Olson played in another baseball game on Friday. He has been doing this — without missing a game — since May 2, 2021. That's when his current streak began, when he was still a first baseman with the Oakland A's.

He has now played in approximately 899 consecutive games. Earlier this week, he passed Hall of Famer Stan Musial's 895 to claim sole possession of 8th place on the all-time MLB consecutive games played list.

Passing Musial

Musial played 895 consecutive games across parts of his career with the Cardinals. That's the kind of milestone that attaches itself to a Hall of Fame legacy for a reason. Olson is now past it — alone in 8th place on the all-time list.

For context, here's the list Olson has climbed into:

  • Cal Ripken Jr. — 2,632 (all-time record)
  • Lou Gehrig — 2,130
  • Everett Scott — 1,307
  • Steve Garvey — 1,207
  • Miguel Tejada — 1,152
  • Billy Williams — 1,117
  • Joe Sewell — 1,103
  • Stan Musial — 895 (now surpassed by Olson)

Olson's streak began with the Oakland A's and has continued through his move to Atlanta via trade in 2022. He broke the Braves franchise record at game 741, surpassing Dale Murphy's mark. He kept going.

What It Takes

Playing every day in Major League Baseball for more than five consecutive years requires a combination of physical durability, preparation, and luck that is genuinely rare. Injuries derail even the healthiest athletes. Slumps create lineup decisions. A manager under pressure sometimes makes the call to sit someone who has been struggling, regardless of a streak.

Olson has navigated all of it. He has played in games where he wasn't at his best. He has played through the kind of normal physical attrition that comes with a 162-game schedule — repeated annually. He has been available every day the Braves have needed him.

The streak is a background story in a baseball season full of louder ones. Olson plays first base, does his job, and adds a game to the count without fanfare. On Friday, the Braves lost to the Yankees 5-4 — Gerrit Cole pitched seven strong innings against Chris Sale in a matchup of former Cy Young Award winners. Olson was there, in the lineup, as always.

What's Next

The next name on Olson's horizon is Joe Sewell at 1,103 consecutive games — more than 200 games away. That's not an imminent milestone. It would require Olson to play every game for roughly another year and a half. He's shown no signs of stopping. For now, he's 8th all-time — a number worth knowing, in a story worth following, even if nobody is saying it loudly.
