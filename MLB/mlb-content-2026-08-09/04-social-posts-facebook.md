### STORY 1: Dodgers End 7-Game Losing Streak

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 8:00 AM ET

The Dodgers are back.

Los Angeles ended their 7-game losing streak — the longest since 2017 — with a 2-1 walk-off win over the Arizona Diamondbacks in 10 innings Friday night at Chase Field.

Here's how it played out: Kyle Tucker put the Dodgers ahead with a solo home run in the 8th inning. Then Arizona came storming back in the 9th, when Geraldo Perdomo and Corbin Carroll hit back-to-back triples to tie the game at 1-1.

Then it was Shohei Ohtani's moment. In the 10th, with Kyle Tucker at third base as the automatic runner after advancing on a wild pitch, Ohtani lined a sharp infield single that deflected off Tim Tawa's glove. Tucker scored. Dodgers win.

Ohtani hasn't pitched since July 3 due to knee and biceps issues — and he's given no specific timeline for a return to the mound. But as a designated hitter, he remains one of the most dangerous hitters in baseball, and Friday proved he can still be the reason a team wins.

The Dodgers move forward. The losing streak is over.

### STORY 2: Red Sox 9-Game Win Streak Ends

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 10:30 AM ET

The Red Sox lost Friday night. The 9-game win streak is over.

But before you feel too bad about it, consider the full picture of what Boston has done this season.

The Red Sox were 32-46 when they went on a 15-game winning streak that tied the 1946 franchise record — a team from the Ted Williams era. When that first streak ended, most people figured the lightning-in-a-bottle moment had passed.

Then they won nine more in a row.

The 9-game streak ended Friday with a 7-3 loss to the Oakland Athletics. Boston left the bases loaded in the ninth during a late comeback attempt that fell short. Had they won one more game, they would have been the first MLB team ever to post multiple 10-game winning streaks in a single season.

They got to nine.

That's still historically remarkable. And it's still one of the best stories in the American League this year. The Red Sox aren't done — they're still playing meaningful baseball in August, which is the whole point.

### STORY 3: Field of Dreams — 4 Days Away

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 12:30 PM ET

Four days.

The 2026 Field of Dreams Game is Thursday, August 13, and it might be the most unique event in baseball this year.

The Minnesota Twins host the Philadelphia Phillies at the 8,000-seat ballpark in Dyersville, Iowa — the original site of the 1989 film. First pitch is 7:30 PM ET, live on Netflix, with a pre-game ceremony starting at 6:30 PM ET.

And that ceremony is something special. More than 25 Hall of Famers are confirmed to attend. On the Phillies side: Mike Schmidt, Steve Carlton, and Scott Rolen. On the Twins side: Joe Mauer, Rod Carew, Bert Blyleven, and Paul Molitor. Jim Thome and Jim Kaat — who played for both clubs — appear for the two franchises. Kevin Costner, who played Ray Kinsella in the film, will appear during the broadcast.

This is the first Field of Dreams Game since 2022. The site underwent a major renovation and expansion before returning to the schedule. Gates open at 3:30 PM ET for ticketholders, with complimentary parking.

"Is this heaven?" Thursday, we find out.

**Window:** 3:00 PM ET

On August 8, 2026, the White Sox beat the Cleveland Guardians 6-3 to improve to 60-56 — first place in the AL Central by 3 games.

Munetaka Murakami hit a home run. Tristan Peters and Chase Meidroth each drove in two runs during a five-run sixth inning. Braden Montgomery added an RBI single in the eighth. Dominant, complete.

They lead the AL Central. They're legitimate October contenders. And they were 41-121 twelve months ago.

### STORY 5: Matt Olson's Iron Man Streak

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 6:00 PM ET

Matt Olson plays every day.

Not "every day" as a figure of speech — literally every day, for more than five years now. Earlier this week, Olson passed Hall of Famer Stan Musial at 895 consecutive games to take sole possession of 8th place on the all-time MLB consecutive games played list. His current streak is now approximately 899 games and counting.

It started May 2, 2021, when Olson was still with the Oakland A's. He has not missed a game since. He moved to Atlanta via trade in 2022 and hasn't sat since. At game 741, he passed the Braves' own franchise record held by Dale Murphy.

Now he plays in rarefied air. The all-time list:
1. Cal Ripken Jr. — 2,632
2. Lou Gehrig — 2,130
3. Everett Scott — 1,307
4. Steve Garvey — 1,207
5. Miguel Tejada — 1,152
6. Billy Williams — 1,117
7. Joe Sewell — 1,103
8. Stan Musial — 895 (now passed by Olson)

The next name on the list is Joe Sewell at 1,103 — more than 200 consecutive games away. That is not an imminent milestone. But then again, nobody thought Olson was going to still be here at 899 either.

He plays. Every day. That's the whole story.
