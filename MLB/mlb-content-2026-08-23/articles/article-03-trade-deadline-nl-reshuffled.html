Trade Deadline Closed: How the NL Playoff Picture Changed This Week

By Ryan Calloway | Ballpark Banter | August 23, 2026

The 2026 trade deadline has closed, and the National League looks different than it did a week ago. Four significant moves reshuffled rotation depth, bullpen construction, and lineup stability across the NL playoff field. Here is what each acquisition means and what the October picture looks like now that the window is shut.

Tarik Skubal to the Los Angeles Dodgers

This is the move that changes the most. Tarik Skubal gives the Dodgers a legitimate ace to anchor a rotation that was already capable of going deep in October. In a best-of-five or a best-of-seven series, the team that can send an ace in Game 1 and a reliable starter in Game 2 has a structural advantage that shows up repeatedly across playoff formats.

The Dodgers already had offensive firepower. Adding Skubal gives them a rotation that can match or exceed what any team in the NL sends out in a playoff series. If the Dodgers reach the NLCS or the World Series, Skubal is pitching in multiple elimination games. That is a different team than the one that entered August.

Clay Holmes to the Chicago Cubs

The Cubs made the move that most directly strengthens their October viability. Holmes has been one of the most reliable late-inning arms available at the deadline, and the Cubs' bullpen depth going into September was a legitimate question mark for a team holding the NL Wild Card No. 1 seed.

Adding Holmes means the Cubs now have a clearer late-game picture: reliable bridge options leading to a trusted closer situation. In a wild card round, where a single game or a short series can hinge entirely on one eighth-inning sequence, that acquisition may matter as much as any the Cubs have made all season.

Luis Arraez to the Philadelphia Phillies

The Phillies needed what Arraez provides: elite contact, a .300-plus batting average, and a stabilizing presence in a lineup that has been inconsistent in its ability to make contact during pressure stretches. Arraez is not a power bat, but his .329 career batting average and ability to put the ball in play consistently are exactly what a lineup needs from a No. 2 or No. 3 hitter entering September.

The Phillies hold the NL Wild Card No. 2 seed. They have the pitching to compete deep into October if the lineup can stay productive. Arraez improves that probability meaningfully.

Dustin May to the Milwaukee Brewers

The Brewers did not need much. At 80-49, the best record in baseball, Milwaukee is already running away with the NL Central. Adding Dustin May gives them rotation depth behind Jacob Misiorowski — which, given how dominant Misiorowski has been all season, means the Brewers are approaching October with as much pitching depth as any team in the league.

May has the stuff to be a postseason contributor when healthy. The Brewers took a calculated addition at a moment when they could afford patience. If May is right, this deal looks better in October than it does today.

The NL Playoff Picture

The NL Wild Card field currently stands at Cubs (No. 1), Phillies (No. 2), and Padres (No. 3). The Brewers hold the NL Central title. The Dodgers are positioned as the team that most improved its October ceiling at the deadline.

The deadline is closed. What each team does with its acquired roster from here determines who is still playing in October — and the NL field heading into September is as competitive as it has been at this stage of the season in years.

What's Next

September opens in nine days. Every team in the NL playoff picture now has its full roster in place. The wild card standings will tighten as teams jockey for positioning and home field. The Dodgers, with Skubal now in the fold, are the team that changed its playoff ceiling the most. How the rest of the NL responds over the next 33 games will determine the seeding order when October arrives.
