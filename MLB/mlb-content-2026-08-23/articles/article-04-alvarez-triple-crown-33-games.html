Yordan Alvarez Is One Home Run from History. Thirty-Three Games to Get There.

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | August 23, 2026

Yordan Alvarez is one home run away from leading every Triple Crown category simultaneously, and there are 33 games left to close the gap. He leads the American League in batting average at .329. He leads in RBI at 88. On home runs, he sits at 36 — one behind Kyle Schwarber's 37. One swing separates Alvarez from the top of all three categories at once.

The last player to accomplish the Triple Crown — leading the league in batting average, home runs, and RBI at the end of a full season — was Miguel Cabrera in 2012. Fourteen years. No one has done it since.

Where the Race Stands

The RBI lead is the most secure of Alvarez's three categories. At 88, he holds a lead that would require an extraordinary collapse to relinquish in 33 games. The batting average lead at .329 gives him meaningful cushion — erasing that gap over a month of play would require a near-historic run from a challenger combined with a significant downturn from Alvarez.

The home run race is the live element. Schwarber's 37 is one ahead. That number can flip in a single at-bat. It can also extend — Schwarber is a power hitter who does not go cold for extended stretches. The home run category is not just the closest; it is the one where the lead can change on any pitch Alvarez sees.

The Historical Weight

The Triple Crown is not simply a statistical achievement — it is a statement about a player's dominance across every dimension of offensive production. Batting average reflects consistent contact and on-base ability. Home runs reflect raw power. RBI reflects a combination of power, timing, and performance in run-producing situations.

Leading all three simultaneously means that no other player in the league was better at any single offensive category. That is extraordinarily rare in an era of specialized hitting approaches, deeper pitching staffs, and the analytic management of at-bats. The last fourteen years have produced dozens of great offensive seasons. None has produced a Triple Crown.

Cabrera's 2012 season — .330 average, 44 home runs, 139 RBI — is the benchmark. Alvarez is not there yet. But the window is open, and the math is close enough to be real.

Who Alvarez Is

Yordan Alvarez has been one of the most productive power hitters in baseball since his arrival in Houston. He has played in October pressure every year of his career. He has hit in elimination games. He knows what is at stake at this stage of a season — and he has the track record to suggest that awareness sharpens him rather than tightening him.

The Astros need him to be himself. The Triple Crown needs him to do something no player in baseball has managed in over a decade. The two things are not as far apart as they sound.

What's Next

Thirty-three games remain. One home run closes the gap on Schwarber. The batting average and RBI leads require only maintenance. If Alvarez plays his game, the Triple Crown is within reach — and if he wins it, 2026 ends with one of the rarest individual achievements in the sport. The question is whether September delivers the one swing that makes it happen.
