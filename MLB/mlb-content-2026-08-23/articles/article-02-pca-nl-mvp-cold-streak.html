Pete Crow-Armstrong's Cold Streak Is Real. His MVP Case Still Isn't Broken.

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | August 23, 2026

Pete Crow-Armstrong has gone 4-for-36 over his last eight games. That is a .111 batting average over that stretch, and the narratives that follow cold streaks in MVP races are arriving on schedule: multiple outlets are now reporting that PCA is "falling behind" as Shohei Ohtani's candidacy gains momentum in the final month of the season.

Before writing off the case, it is worth spending a moment with what the full season actually looks like.

The Season-Long Case

Pete Crow-Armstrong leads all of baseball in fWAR at 7.7. That number is not a product of the last eight games — it reflects five months of play across every facet of the game.

He has 22 outs above average in the outfield, along with 24 fielding run value — numbers that no other MVP candidate is close to matching. On offense, he has hit .281 with 30 home runs, 78 RBI, and 31 stolen bases. He is the first player in Cubs history to post back-to-back 30-30 seasons. He is 24 years old and has been the most complete player in the National League since April.

A cold streak does not erase that. It never has, for any player in any MVP race.

What the Ohtani Argument Requires

For Ohtani to overtake PCA, the argument has to clear a specific bar: that Ohtani's offensive production and two-way value outweigh the 7.7 fWAR lead that PCA holds. At the moment, the fWAR gap is significant enough that it would take a sustained collapse from PCA — not eight games — to shift the numbers entirely.

The narrative in MVP races has a way of moving faster than the underlying statistics. A cold streak at the right moment, covered by the right outlets, can reshape perception without reshaping the actual case. That is what is happening right now.

PCA's 7.7 fWAR is built on defense that does not disappear when the bat goes cold. The outs above average number is accrued every inning, every game. A slump at the plate does not subtract from it.

The History of Late-Season Slumps

This is not the first time a leading MVP candidate has hit a wall in August. The pattern is almost predictable: a player builds a dominant first five months, a stretch of poor results arrives, the coverage turns, and suddenly the race is called competitive again. Some of those candidates recover. Some don't.

What separates the ones who win is what they do next. There are 33 games left in the regular season — more than twenty percent of the schedule still on the table. PCA has enough games in front of him to turn this into a footnote. He has also had cold stretches before this one, within this same season, and come through them.

The players who win MVP awards are the ones who respond when the narrative shifts against them. Right now, the narrative has shifted against Pete Crow-Armstrong.

What's Next

The Cubs have 33 games remaining as they defend their NL Wild Card No. 1 seed. They need PCA to be the player he was from April through mid-August — and PCA has every reason to be motivated by a storyline that says he is falling behind.

Cold streaks end. The question is whether this one ends soon enough to keep the season case intact. With 7.7 fWAR banked and a month to go, the math still leans his direction. September is where the argument gets made official.
