### STORY 1: Misiorowski — Historic Start vs Rockies

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 8:00 AM ET

Jacob Misiorowski did something today that nobody in Brewers history has ever done.

He walked to the mound at American Family Field, looked in at the Colorado Rockies lineup, and struck out seven consecutive batters to open the game. Seven straight. A Brewers franchise record. He retired 14 of the first 15 batters he faced — 12 of those via strikeout.

By the time the final line was set — 5 innings, 1 hit, 1 earned run, 12 strikeouts, 83 pitches — the Brewers had won 11-2, and the only thing anyone in Milwaukee was talking about was pitch velocity.

It was Misiorowski's 9th game this season with 10 or more strikeouts, tying the Brewers single-season record previously held by Corbin Burnes in 2022.

He is 1.76 ERA. He is the -550 Cy Young favorite. He is pitching like he belongs in a conversation about the all-time single-season records.

The Milwaukee Brewers are 80-49 and running away with the NL Central. And their ace just posted one of the most electric starts in franchise history.

There are 33 games left. The Cubs need PCA to be the player he was from April through mid-August — and PCA has every reason to be motivated by a storyline that says he is falling behind.

Cold streaks end. Let's see how this one does.

Alvarez has been one of the most productive power hitters in baseball since he arrived in Houston. He knows what is at stake. He has played in October pressure. This is the kind of thing he is built for.

33 games. One home run.

### STORY 5: Joshua Baez One Week In

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 6:00 PM ET

One week ago, Joshua Baez walked to the plate at Wrigley Field for the first at-bat of his major league career and hit a 449-foot home run off the Cubs' starter. Then he did it again. Then he did it again. Three home runs in three consecutive plate appearances in his MLB debut — a feat no player in baseball history had ever accomplished.

The week since has been a more complicated story — and a more interesting one.

After the historic debut, Baez went 0-for-15 with 6 strikeouts over his next three games. The adjustment by pitching staffs was immediate and predictable. Rookies with prodigious power get attacked in specific ways. The slump was expected by people who have watched baseball long enough.

Then Baez recovered. His current line — .251/.332/.552 with 12 home runs and a 32.7% strikeout rate — reflects a player who absorbed the initial wave of adjustments and responded. The K rate is the concern. But at .552 slugging, the power is unquestionably real.

This is what the chess match looks like at its beginning: pitching staffs have found some things that work. Baez's job is to find countermeasures. The players who make careers out of historic beginnings are the ones who can win multiple rounds of this adjustment cycle.

One week in. The most interesting story in baseball is still the most interesting story.
