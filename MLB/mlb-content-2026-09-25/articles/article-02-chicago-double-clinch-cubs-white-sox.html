Cubs and White Sox Both Clinch — The Night Chicago Baseball Made History Twice

By Marcus Cole | September 25, 2026

Wednesday night, Chicago had two baseball teams and both of them made the playoffs.

The Cubs clinched first, at Wrigley Field, with Pete Crow-Armstrong's 40th stolen base and a 2-1 win over the Miami Marlins. Then, minutes later on the South Side, the Chicago White Sox finished off the Kansas City Royals 9-1 and clinched a postseason berth of their own. Two teams. Same city. Same night. Both heading to October.

The Cubs' story is remarkable. The White Sox story is something that baseball had never seen before.

What the White Sox Just Did

Two seasons ago, the 2024 Chicago White Sox lost 121 baseball games. That is not the most losses in a recent era, or within living memory, or since expansion. It is the most losses in the history of Major League Baseball. The record had stood since the 1962 New York Mets. The 2024 White Sox broke it by six games.

A full year later, the 2025 White Sox lost more than 100 games again. Back-to-back triple-digit loss seasons, the most recent following one of the worst single-season records ever recorded. For most franchises, that is the beginning of a rebuild measured in years, not months.

General Manager Chris Getz built around young players. Manager Will Venable gave them a culture and a structure. Seven of the nine everyday starters on this year's team have under two years of MLB experience. They didn't have an established core. They built one on the fly, out of players who were barely in the organization when the record-breaking losses were happening.

And they went to the playoffs.

It is the first time in the history of professional baseball that a team has followed back-to-back 100-loss seasons immediately with a postseason appearance. The first time. That is not a narrative — it is a historical fact, and it happened Wednesday night in Chicago.

What It Means for the Cubs

The Cubs are back in the playoffs for the second straight year. Crow-Armstrong's 40-40 season is the engine, and it happens to coincide with a roster that is deeper and more balanced than Chicago's Wild Card appearance last season. They drew the San Diego Padres in the first round — a team playing the best baseball in the NL over the past two weeks. The Cubs have home-field advantage as the higher seed.

Both Chicago teams head into October in very different places. The Cubs know what they are and what they're capable of. The White Sox are the most improbable playoff team in baseball, built almost entirely from scratch over two years. That story doesn't stop Wednesday night.

What's Next

Both teams play out the final three regular season games before October 1 arrives. The White Sox can still win the AL Central — they trail the Cleveland Guardians by just one game entering the final weekend. The Cubs begin Wild Card play September 29 against the Padres. Two franchises. Two paths. One extraordinary night in the history of the sport they share a city to play.
