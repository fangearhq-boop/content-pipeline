NL Wild Card Bracket Preview — Cubs vs. Padres, Braves vs. Phillies Starting September 29

By Marcus Cole | September 25, 2026

The bracket is set.

Four days from now, the National League Wild Card Series begins — best-of-3, no margin for error, and four teams that spent weeks fighting for position now knowing exactly what they're up against. The Milwaukee Brewers and Los Angeles Dodgers hold first-round byes as the No. 1 and No. 2 seeds. The four Wild Card teams tip off September 29.

Cubs vs. Padres

The Chicago Cubs (No. 5 seed, 88-71) host the San Diego Padres (No. 4 seed) in one Wild Card Series. Chicago clinched Wednesday night — Pete Crow-Armstrong's 40th stolen base and a 2-1 win over the Marlins completed the job. The Padres have been the hottest team in the NL Wild Card race, going 9-1 over their last 10 games entering the final weekend.

This is an evenly matched series. The Cubs have home field advantage despite being the lower seed, which matters in a three-game format. The Padres enter riding genuine momentum. Crow-Armstrong against a team that has been locked in for two weeks — that's a compelling first-round matchup by any measure.

Braves vs. Phillies

The Atlanta Braves (No. 3 seed) host the Philadelphia Phillies (No. 6 seed) in the other first-round series. The Phillies qualified for the third consecutive year — their consistency as a franchise has been one of the quieter NL stories of the past few seasons. Atlanta enters with the highest seed among the four Wild Card teams and home field in all three games if the series goes the distance.

The Phillies have Zack Wheeler, who reached 2,000 career strikeouts earlier this week against the Brewers, making him the 96th pitcher in MLB history to reach that mark. Wheeler pitching at Citizens Bank Park in the first round — that's a matchup Atlanta will have to solve.

What the Winners Face

The survivor of Cubs-Padres goes to the NLDS to face the Milwaukee Brewers, who finished the regular season as the No. 1 seed and have been the best team in the NL for most of 2026. Jacob Misiorowski and the Brewers rotation is the prize waiting on the other side of the Wild Card. The survivor of Braves-Phillies heads to the NLDS to face the Los Angeles Dodgers — Shohei Ohtani, Freddie Freeman, and a lineup that has consistently been one of the deepest in baseball.

Neither path is easy. Neither Wild Card team will enter the Division Series as a favorite. But that's what makes October baseball worth watching. Three regular season games remain. Then we find out who's ready.

What's Next

Regular season ends Saturday, September 27. Wild Card Series begins Tuesday, September 29. Four teams play for the right to advance. Two go home after three games or fewer. The bracket is set, the matchups are clear, and Chicago is ready for October baseball on both sides of town.
