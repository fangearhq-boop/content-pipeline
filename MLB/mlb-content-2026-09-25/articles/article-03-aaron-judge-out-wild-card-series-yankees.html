Yankees Manager Says Team Is 'Planning On' Aaron Judge Missing Wild Card Series

By Jake Torres | September 25, 2026

The Yankees are going to the playoffs. Aaron Judge is almost certainly not going with them — at least not at the start.

Manager Aaron Boone delivered the clearest public signal yet this week when he said the organization is "planning on him not being a part of" the Wild Card Series against the Boston Red Sox, which begins September 29. Judge has a moderate grade right calf strain affecting the soleus muscle and has not resumed hitting. He is on the 10-day injured list and his status for the opening round of the playoffs is, effectively, resolved.

A Season of Injuries

This is Judge's second stint on the injured list in 2026. Earlier in the year, he missed an extended stretch with a right rib fracture — a serious structural injury that required significant time off. He returned from that absence, played seven games, and then went directly back to the IL with the calf strain. The sequence of injuries is not a coincidence — it's the kind of cascading physical breakdown that happens when a body is working through recovery from one problem while another one develops.

Judge himself said the right things when asked about his timeline. "I'd like to be back by the postseason. That's definitely the plan." That sentence has an implied caveat: not this round. If the Yankees can beat the Red Sox without him, the Division Series — which would begin approximately October 4 if New York advances — represents his most realistic return window.

The Roster Around Him

The Yankees' situation is complicated further by the placement of Clarke Schmidt on the 15-day IL with right bicep neuritis. Schmidt's absence depletes a rotation that was already navigating questions about depth entering October. The Yankees won enough games during the regular season to reach the playoffs as the No. 4 AL seed. Now they're being asked to beat a Red Sox team in a best-of-3 without their best position player in the lineup.

It's not impossible — the 2026 Yankees have enough players capable of contributing in a short series. But the margin for error in a three-game Wild Card format is thin under any circumstances, and removing Judge from the equation makes it thinner.

What's Next

The Yankees have three regular season games remaining before the Wild Card Series begins September 29. The Red Sox know what Boone said. Everyone in Boston's dugout will show up to Game 1 knowing they won't have to face Aaron Judge. For New York, the question is whether the offense can compensate, the rotation can hold, and the season doesn't end in three games at Fenway Park. Judge will be watching. The plan is for him to return after that, if there's an after that.
