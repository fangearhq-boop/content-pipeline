Pete Crow-Armstrong Joins the 40-40 Club — The Seventh Member in Baseball History

By Ryan Calloway | September 25, 2026

The stolen base wasn't dramatic in the conventional sense. No close play. No diving tag. Pete Crow-Armstrong drew a leadoff walk from Tyler Phillips in the first inning, stood at first base for one pitch thrown to Seiya Suzuki, and took off. He was standing on second base before most of Wrigley Field fully processed what had happened.

Then the videoboard lit up. Then the crowd understood. Then came the chants.

With that stolen base — his 40th — Crow-Armstrong became the seventh player in the history of Major League Baseball to join the 40-40 club. The final line as of September 24: 45 home runs. 40 stolen bases. Cubs win 2-1. Playoff berth clinched. History made on one play in the first inning.

The Club He Just Joined

The 40-40 club does not have many members, and the years between them are long. Jose Canseco opened it in 1988 with the Oakland A's — 42 home runs, 40 stolen bases, that year's World Series. Barry Bonds reached the milestone in 1996 for the Giants. Alex Rodriguez joined in 1998 for Seattle. Then Alfonso Soriano in 2006 for Washington. That was a 17-year gap before Ronald Acuña Jr. added his name for the Braves in 2023. Shohei Ohtani followed in 2024 for the Dodgers — the first player to ever do it while also pitching.

Now Crow-Armstrong. He is 24 years old, playing in his fourth big-league season, and has just joined a list that spans 38 years of baseball. The pace of membership has accelerated: three of the seven members joined in the last four seasons. But none of that diminishes what he did.

The Night Chicago Needed

The stolen base came in the first inning of a game the Cubs needed. They had been denied a Wild Card clinch the night before. They needed a win, and they got one — Matthew Boyd went seven innings giving up one run, Ryan Zeferjahn closed it with a perfect ninth for save No. 7, and Seiya Suzuki added a home run of his own. Chicago 2, Miami 1. The Cubs' second consecutive postseason appearance is confirmed.

The Wild Card Series begins September 29. Crow-Armstrong will carry a 45-40 season into October — the most compelling individual statistical line in the 2026 regular season, and almost certainly a National League Most Valuable Player award when the votes are counted in November.

What's Next

The Cubs play three more regular season games before October. Crow-Armstrong will take a lineup with 45 home runs and 40 stolen bases into what figures to be a Wild Card Series against the San Diego Padres — a team that has gone 9-1 in its last 10 games entering the final weekend. October baseball in Chicago, with the seventh member of the 40-40 club in centerfield. Watch closely.
