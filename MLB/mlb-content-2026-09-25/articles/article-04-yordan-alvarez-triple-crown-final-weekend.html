Yordan Alvarez Has Three Games Left to Win the AL Triple Crown

By Ryan Calloway | September 25, 2026

Three games. That's what Yordan Alvarez has left to complete one of the rarest achievements in baseball.

The Houston Astros first baseman leads the American League in batting average. He has led it most of the season. That category is his. The other two are the problem.

In home runs, Junior Caminero of the Tampa Bay Rays has pulled ahead. In RBI, Pete Alonso of the Baltimore Orioles holds the edge. For Alvarez to win the Triple Crown, he needs to gain ground in both of those categories across the final three games of the regular season. That is not an impossible task. It is a genuinely hard one.

The Last Time Someone Did This

Miguel Cabrera won the American League Triple Crown in 2012 — the first AL Triple Crown since Carl Yastrzemski in 1967. That was the statistic that anchored Cabrera's first MVP award and created a year-long baseball conversation about whether traditional counting stats or advanced metrics should drive MVP voting. Cabrera, who also had a historically dominant WAR that season, won. The Triple Crown was the centerpiece of his argument.

No AL hitter has won it since. That's 14 seasons of someone leading in two of the three categories but not closing out all three. Alvarez has led in batting average long enough that it's safe to call it his. The home run and RBI gaps entering the final weekend are real but not insurmountable, depending on what happens in the games ahead.

What the Math Looks Like

Three games is roughly six to nine plate appearances for a player in his spot in the batting order. In that window, one or two home runs is achievable. A few RBI opportunities could go either way. Caminero and Alonso are also playing out those same three games, though, and neither is likely to sit out a meaningful final series. The positions are fluid. Everything changes with each at-bat over the next three days.

What Alvarez has done regardless of the final outcome: he has produced one of the great offensive seasons in recent American League history. His batting average has been dominant. His power numbers are elite. He's been the best hitter in the AL for most of 2026. The Triple Crown distinction is the one remaining question, and it gets answered by Saturday.

What's Next

The Astros play out the final three games of the regular season. After that, Houston is in the AL postseason bracket regardless of where the Triple Crown math lands. Alvarez will be in the lineup either way. But this weekend, every at-bat he takes comes with a leaderboard attached. Watch the numbers. Three games to settle it.
