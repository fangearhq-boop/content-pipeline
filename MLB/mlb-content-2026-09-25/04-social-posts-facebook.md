### STORY 1: PCA Makes History — 40-40 Club

#### FB Long-Form Post

It happened last night at Wrigley Field.

Pete Crow-Armstrong drew a leadoff walk off Tyler Phillips in the first inning. Took off on the very first pitch thrown to Seiya Suzuki. Ball barely reached the catcher. PCA was already standing on second base with stolen base No. 40.

The Wrigley crowd erupted. The videoboard lit up. And just like that, a 24-year-old Cubs centerfielder became the seventh player in the history of baseball to join the 40-40 club.

The full membership list: Jose Canseco (1988, Oakland A's). Barry Bonds (1996, San Francisco Giants). Alex Rodriguez (1998, Seattle Mariners). Alfonso Soriano (2006, Washington Nationals). Ronald Acuña Jr. (2023, Atlanta Braves). Shohei Ohtani (2024, Los Angeles Dodgers). And now Pete Crow-Armstrong (2026, Chicago Cubs). PCA is the third player in four consecutive seasons to accomplish the feat — a run that has no precedent in baseball history.

The Cubs won the game 2-1. Matthew Boyd went seven innings giving up one run. Ryan Zeferjahn closed it out. And with that win, Chicago clinched its second consecutive Wild Card berth. A historic night by any measure.

### STORY 2: Chicago's Double Clinch Night

#### FB Long-Form Post

This might be the most remarkable night in Chicago baseball history.

Wednesday evening, both Chicago MLB teams clinched playoff berths — within minutes of each other.

The Cubs did it at Wrigley with a 2-1 win over the Marlins, punctuated by Pete Crow-Armstrong's historic 40th stolen base. The White Sox did it on the South Side with a 9-1 rout of the Kansas City Royals.

The Cubs story is extraordinary. The White Sox story is unbelievable.

Two seasons ago, the 2024 Chicago White Sox lost 121 games — the most in the history of Major League Baseball. Not the most that decade. The most. Ever. The baseball world treated it as a cautionary tale of organizational failure, a franchise in freefall. Then something happened. General Manager Chris Getz rebuilt the roster around young players. Manager Will Venable gave them structure and identity. Seven of the nine starters have under two years of big-league experience.

And on Wednesday night, a team built almost entirely of players who weren't even in the majors when the 121-loss season unfolded — clinched a postseason berth.

It's the first time in the history of baseball that a team has gone from back-to-back 100-loss seasons directly to the playoffs. The first time.

Chicago had its night. And baseball gave us one to remember.

### STORY 3: Aaron Judge Wild Card Status

#### FB Long-Form Post

The Yankees are going to the playoffs. Aaron Judge almost certainly is not — at least not for the opening round.

Manager Aaron Boone made the situation as clear as he's been when he said the team is "planning on him not being a part of" the Wild Card Series against the Boston Red Sox, which begins September 29. Judge has a moderate grade right calf strain — a soleus injury — and has not resumed hitting. This came just seven games after he returned from a right rib fracture that cost him months. He went directly from one IL stint to another.

Judge himself said his goal is to be back for the postseason, and the most realistic target is the Division Series, if the Yankees can advance past Boston without him. That's the plan.

It's a massive blow. The Yankees are the No. 4 seed in the AL, facing the Red Sox in a best-of-3 where every lineup decision matters. Adding to the concern: Clarke Schmidt was placed on the 15-day IL with right bicep neuritis, depleting the team's rotation depth further.

The Red Sox have to feel better about their matchup today than they did two weeks ago.

### STORY 4: Yordan Alvarez Triple Crown — Final Three Games

#### FB Long-Form Post

Three games. That's what Yordan Alvarez has left to make history.

He leads the American League in batting average. He trails in home runs and RBI. To win the Triple Crown, he needs to gain ground in both categories over the final weekend of the regular season — against teams that have nothing to play for, or everything, depending on their playoff situation.

The last time an AL hitter won the Triple Crown was Miguel Cabrera in 2012. That was 14 years ago. The Triple Crown is the rarest sustained achievement in offense — not a single play or moment, but a sustained performance across three different statistical categories over a full 162-game season.

Alvarez has had a dominant offensive year. His batting average lead is the most secure piece of the puzzle. The home run and RBI gaps are tighter but real. Three games isn't much time to close them.

If he falls short, he still had one of the great offensive seasons in recent AL memory. But three games is enough for anything to happen in baseball. Watch the leaderboard this weekend.
