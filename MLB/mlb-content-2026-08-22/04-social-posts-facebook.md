PCA has both the WAR and the counting stats. But Ohtani has the name and the narrative.

Thirty-four games remain. Pete Crow-Armstrong needs to make these games count — not just for the Cubs' playoff run, but for the award that would define his breakout 2026 season.

### STORY 2: Joshua Baez — The Adjustment Period

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 10:30 AM ET

Joshua Baez arrived in the major leagues like almost no one ever has — three home runs in his first three career at-bats on August 15. MLB first. Full stop. A 449-foot shot to open his career, two more before the game was over, and an instant legend was born.

One week later, the adjustment game has begun. Not from Baez — from the pitchers.

This is how the league works. Every pitcher gets film on every rookie, and Baez now has more film than most rookies will ever accumulate in a week. His 32.7% strikeout rate is an early data point that pitching staffs will continue to probe. They're looking for the pitch he can't handle, the sequence that breaks down his timing.

Here's what makes this interesting: Baez has 12 home runs since his debut — the power is genuine. The Cardinals are working through the lineup deployment question, trying to find the spot that protects him from the toughest matchups while maximizing his impact. Leadoff or cleanup. High-count or early-count at-bats.

Every great hitter goes through this. The ones who last are the ones who adjust back when the league adjusts to them. The next 30 days will tell us what kind of player Joshua Baez actually is.

They want to win the World Series, not just the Cy Young Award — and they need their best pitcher healthy for October.

Strike out three in relief Saturday. Start Sunday. That's Jacob Misiorowski's weekend. The Brewers are managing something special.

### STORY 5: NL Wild Card — Cubs Hold No. 1 With 34 Games Left

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** (evening)

Thirty-four games remain in the 2026 regular season, and the NL Wild Card race has taken shape.

The Cubs (74-54) are the No. 1 Wild Card seed. The Phillies (70-58) sit in the No. 2 spot, four games back. The Padres (68-60) hold the third and final Wild Card, six games behind Chicago. Meanwhile, the Brewers (79-49) have the NL Central and the best record in baseball — they are not competing for a Wild Card spot.

The goal is not just to hold the No. 1 seed — it's to avoid a potential first-round matchup with Milwaukee, which has the best record in the National League.

The Cubs want to win in October. But what they really want is to not face Jacob Misiorowski in a best-of-three. Seeding is how they control that.

Thirty-four games to figure it out.
