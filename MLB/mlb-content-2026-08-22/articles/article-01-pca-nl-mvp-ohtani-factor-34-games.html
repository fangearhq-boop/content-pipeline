PCA vs. Ohtani: The NL MVP Race Is Tightening With 34 Games Left

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | August 22, 2026

Pete Crow-Armstrong leads all of baseball with 7.7 fWAR. Not all Cubs players. Not all center fielders. All of baseball. His candidacy for the 2026 National League Most Valuable Player Award has been one of the season's dominant storylines, built on a combination of elite offensive production and historically exceptional defense. But with 34 games left in the regular season, the race is not settled — because Shohei Ohtani's candidacy is gaining ground.

Recent reporting indicates Ohtani's MVP case has strengthened, applying pressure on what had looked like a PCA runaway. The gap remains real. But it is not the same gap it was in July.

What PCA Has Built

The offensive numbers are legitimate: .281 average, 30 home runs, 78 RBI, 31 stolen bases. PCA is the first player in Cubs history to post back-to-back 30-30 seasons. He is one of a small group of players in major league history to hit a leadoff home run and a walk-off home run in the same game — he did it on August 17 against the White Sox in a 7-5 Cubs victory.

The defensive numbers go beyond legitimate — they are historically rare. Pete Crow-Armstrong has 22 outs above average and 24 fielding run value through more than 1,055 innings this season. These are numbers that rarely exist at this scale. The defensive dimension gives him a value calculation no other MVP candidate in the National League can match.

His 7.7 fWAR leads every position player in baseball. In most seasons, that number wins the award.

The Ohtani Problem

Shohei Ohtani's candidacy rests on 6.4 fWAR and a combination of offensive production and name recognition that carries weight in MVP voting that does not always align perfectly with advanced metrics. Voters have historically shown a preference for traditional counting stats — home runs, RBI, and batting average — when two candidates are reasonably close.

Ohtani's advantage is not statistical. PCA leads in WAR by a significant margin. The challenge for PCA is that WAR, outs above average, and fielding run value are numbers that require explanation — while a home run count is immediate. Voters who see two compelling cases sometimes default to the one that is easier to articulate.

That does not make a PCA win less likely. It makes it less automatic than a 7.7 WAR lead would suggest in a vacuum.

What the Next 34 Games Mean

Both candidates will accumulate more statistics between now and the end of the regular season. For PCA, the goal is simple: keep producing. Every home run, every stolen base, every elite defensive play adds to a case that is already strong. The Cubs are 74-54 and locked into the NL Wild Card No. 1 seed — PCA is playing meaningful games that voters will be watching.

In 34 games, WAR can shift meaningfully in both directions. The player who finishes the season on the stronger trajectory tends to benefit from recency bias in voting that happens after the regular season ends. PCA needs to close this season the way he has played it — at the level of the best player in the National League.

What's Next

The Cubs continue their 2026 playoff push with 34 games remaining. Pete Crow-Armstrong's NL MVP case is the leading individual storyline in the National League, with Shohei Ohtani's surging candidacy adding genuine drama to an award race that looked more settled a month ago. For fans watching the Cubs and following the NL MVP race, every PCA plate appearance and every defensive highlight between now and the end of September carries weight.
