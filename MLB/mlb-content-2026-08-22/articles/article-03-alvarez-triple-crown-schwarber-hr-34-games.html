Alvarez's Triple Crown Window: Schwarber Leads HR by One With 34 Games Left

By Ryan Calloway | Ballpark Banter | August 22, 2026

Yordan Alvarez is one home run away from leading all three categories that make up the Triple Crown. He leads baseball with a .329 batting average. He leads in RBI with 88. And he sits one home run behind Kyle Schwarber — 37 to 36 — in the one category that would complete the rarest achievement in individual baseball offense.

The last player to win the Triple Crown was Miguel Cabrera in 2012. No one has done it in the 14 years since.

The Home Run Race Within the Race

Schwarber's lead of one home run heading into August 22 is the thinnest possible margin. A single swing by Alvarez that leaves the yard, combined with a Schwarber homerless game, closes the gap entirely. The math is extremely favorable for Alvarez — not just because one home run is an achievable objective, but because 34 games is a significant runway for a power hitter of his caliber.

Alvarez is one of the most dangerous left-handed power hitters in baseball. His swing was built for this — for the type of multi-category dominance that occasionally emerges and gives a generation of fans something to talk about for decades.

The batting average lead is essentially secure. .329 is a substantial gap over the nearest competitors, and batting average leaders with that kind of cushion rarely lose it in the final month. The RBI lead of approximately seven runs is meaningful but will require protection as other hitters in the top tier of production continue their seasons.

Why the Home Run Race Is the Key Variable

Schwarber is not a passive participant in this race. He is an experienced, elite power hitter who will continue to do what he does — hit home runs at a high rate regardless of what is happening in any title race. He does not defer to the narrative. The home run differential will be determined entirely by production: who hits more home runs over the final 34 games.

If Alvarez hits home runs at the same rate he has all season, the math says he should close and potentially overtake Schwarber's total. If Schwarber maintains his pace, the gap could widen. This is the inherent drama of the Triple Crown chase — three categories moving simultaneously, with dozens of games left and dozens of competing hitters producing every day.

The last time a player was in a comparable position to Alvarez — leading two of three categories within reach of the third — was Cabrera in 2012. He won. It is reasonable to ask whether Alvarez is about to join him in the history books.

What's Next

Yordan Alvarez and the Houston Astros continue their 2026 regular season with 34 games remaining. The Triple Crown chase — .329 batting average, 36 home runs, 88 RBI — is now one of the defining narratives of the final month of baseball. Every Alvarez at-bat carries additional weight. Every home run Schwarber hits or doesn't hit reshapes the math. Follow the home run race daily as the window narrows toward the end of the regular season.
