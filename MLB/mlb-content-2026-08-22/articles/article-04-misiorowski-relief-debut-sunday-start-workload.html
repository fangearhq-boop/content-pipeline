Misiorowski Makes Relief Debut Saturday, Then Starts Sunday — Workload Math in Focus

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | August 22, 2026

Jacob Misiorowski struck out three batters in a relief appearance on Saturday — his first time pitching out of the bullpen in his major league career. Then he is scheduled to start on Sunday against the Colorado Rockies.

This is the challenge of managing something unprecedented. Misiorowski leads the National League in ERA (1.76), strikeouts (204), and WHIP (0.74). He is the second-fastest pitcher in MLB history to reach 200 strikeouts in a season — only Spencer Strider got there faster in 2022. The Brewers are in the middle of a playoff push with the best record in baseball at 79-49. They need their ace, and they are going to use him — but they are going to be deliberate about how.

The Workload Math

Through 19 starts, Misiorowski has thrown 115 innings. His pitch counts in recent outings have been capped — 83 pitches in one start, 65 in another. These are not numbers that reflect Misiorowski's capability; they reflect the Brewers' management philosophy for a pitcher they consider their most important asset heading into October.

The relief appearance Saturday was also a managed situation. Three strikeouts in what was likely a short, high-intensity outing — the type of appearance that keeps a pitcher game-sharp without adding meaningful stress to an arm that the organization wants healthy in October. The decision to then start him Sunday is consistent with a five-day rotation with a controlled pitch count — his body clock is not being disrupted by Saturday's work.

Still, the broader workload question remains. The Brewers need Misiorowski to pitch into the postseason at something close to full capacity. Every inning and pitch count decision between now and the Wild Card round is part of that equation.

The Cy Young Picture

With a 1.76 ERA, 204 strikeouts, and a 0.74 WHIP leading all National League starters, Misiorowski's Cy Young case is the strongest in either league. The -550 odds reflect the consensus view that barring a historic collapse, this award belongs to him.

The Brewers' workload management does not change that calculus. Even if Misiorowski throws fewer than his full allotment of innings in the final weeks of the regular season, the numbers he has already accumulated are historically dominant. The award is decided by what has been done, not by what is projected.

What's Next

Jacob Misiorowski starts Sunday, August 23, against the Colorado Rockies at American Family Field in Milwaukee. Following Sunday's outing, the Brewers will make decisions about his pitch count, workload, and rotation schedule for the final push toward the postseason. Misiorowski's Cy Young case is essentially sealed at 1.76 ERA / 204 K / 0.74 WHIP. The only remaining question is whether he arrives in October at full strength. For Brewers fans, that is the only number that matters now.
