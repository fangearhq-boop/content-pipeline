34 Games Left: Cubs Hold Wild Card No. 1 and the Seeding Race Against Milwaukee Begins

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | August 22, 2026

With 34 games remaining in the 2026 regular season, the National League playoff picture has taken its clearest shape of the year. The Cubs (74-54) are the No. 1 Wild Card seed. The Phillies (70-58) and Padres (68-60) hold the other two Wild Card spots. And the Brewers (79-49) — with the best record in all of baseball — have clinched the NL Central and are entirely separate from the Wild Card conversation.

For the Cubs, the next 34 games are about something more specific than just making the playoffs: they are about who they would face in the Wild Card round.

The Seeding Math

In the current MLB postseason format, the top two division winners in each league receive division series byes. The other four teams — one third-place division winner and three Wild Cards — play in the best-of-3 Wild Card Series, with the higher seed hosting all three games.

The Cubs are a Wild Card team. Their path into October runs through a Wild Card Series. Seeding determines who they play and where. The No. 1 Wild Card plays the lowest-seeded Wild Card or the No. 3 division winner in the first round.

What the Cubs cannot do, under the current format, is draw the Brewers in the Wild Card round — Milwaukee has a division title and the potential for a top-two seeding bypass. The Cubs' actual goal is to protect their Wild Card No. 1 position and avoid a first-round draw against the highest-seeded team they could face — which, depending on final standings, could be a division winner from another NL division.

In practical terms: Cubs at No. 1 Wild Card means home games in an elimination round and the most favorable first-round matchup available among Wild Card slots. Every game the Cubs win between now and the end of the season protects that position.

The Challengers

The Phillies (70-58) are four games behind the Cubs. The Padres (68-60) are six back. Both teams are capable of a late-season run that could close the gap or overtake Chicago. The Cubs' four-game cushion over Philadelphia is meaningful — but 34 games is plenty of runway for movement in both directions.

The Cubs are a 74-54 team that has been playing consistent baseball behind Pete Crow-Armstrong's historically strong season. They have not faded when it mattered. But nothing in baseball's final month is guaranteed, and the Wild Card race is being decided against a backdrop of three teams all playing meaningful games every night.

What's Next

The Cubs play 34 more games, beginning with their current series and running through the end of the 2026 regular season. Wild Card No. 1 is theirs to hold. Pete Crow-Armstrong's individual awards race and the team's playoff seeding race are running simultaneously — and both will be decided by what happens on the field over the final weeks of the regular season.
