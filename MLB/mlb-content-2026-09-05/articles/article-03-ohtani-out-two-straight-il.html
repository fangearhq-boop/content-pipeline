Roberts: Missing a Week "Certainly a Possibility" for Ohtani

By Jake Torres September 5, 2026

The Words Dave Roberts Said

For the second consecutive game on Friday, Shohei Ohtani was not in the Los Angeles Dodgers' starting lineup. Dodgers manager Dave Roberts addressed the media before the game, and the quotes are worth reading carefully.

Roberts said an injured list stint for Ohtani was "unlikely, but not impossible." He said missing nearly a week was "certainly a possibility." When asked directly whether he was confident Ohtani would avoid the IL, his answer was: "Hopeful, but I wouldn't say confident."

Those are not the words of a manager who expects his best player back in the lineup tomorrow. Those are the words of a manager navigating something he does not fully control, trying to be honest about a situation that is evolving day by day.

The Physical Picture

Ohtani has not pitched since July 3, when a combination of left knee and biceps soreness pushed the Dodgers to shelve his pitching plans for the season. He has continued to hit throughout August, though the results have been alarming. Since August 18, he is 7-for-51 with 20 strikeouts — a .137 batting average across 51 at-bats.

During Wednesday's loss to the Cardinals, he was visibly uncomfortable after some at-bats and expressed neck soreness after the game along with pain along his right arm. That added a new physical concern to the list that already included the knee and the biceps.

Roberts' response was to hold Ohtani out Thursday. Then Friday. The missed games are now consecutive, which marks the first time this season Ohtani has sat out back-to-back games.

What This Means for October

The Dodgers are 82-57 and in control of the NL West. The postseason is not in doubt. But October is exactly what makes this situation concerning.

A player hitting .137 with 20 strikeouts in 51 at-bats is not a postseason weapon, regardless of what his talent level is. If Ohtani goes to the IL now and comes back in two weeks, the Dodgers are betting he can reset physically and rediscover his swing in a short window before the playoffs start. If he does not go to the IL and continues to play through the discomfort, the Dodgers risk both the physical situation and Ohtani's confidence at the plate.

The rest-versus-rhythm tradeoff that Roberts described back in early September has now become more urgent. There is no comfortable answer, and the clock is running.

What's Next

Watch Roberts' pregame availability in the coming days for any update on Ohtani's status. The Dodgers have not placed him on the injured list as of Friday, but Roberts' language leaves that door clearly open. The next medical evaluation and Roberts' characterization of it will be the biggest Dodgers storyline of the weekend.
