One Home Run. That's It. Pete Crow-Armstrong Is This Close to Baseball History.

By Ryan Calloway September 5, 2026

Six Names in 130 Years

Six players have ever hit 40 home runs and stolen 40 bases in the same MLB season. Six. In 130 years of professional baseball, across thousands of roster spots and tens of thousands of individual seasons, six people have done it.

Jose Canseco started it in 1988. Barry Bonds did it in 1996. Alex Rodriguez joined the club in 1998. Alfonso Soriano got there in 2006. Then a 17-year gap before Ronald Acuña Jr. in 2023 and Shohei Ohtani, also in 2023, accomplished it back to back.

Now Pete Crow-Armstrong needs one home run.

He hit his 39th Thursday, adding to a season that was already historic by any reasonable measure. He has 32 stolen bases. He has 24 games remaining. The Cubs hold the No. 1 National League Wild Card spot. And every time he steps into the box at Wrigley Field, the entire stadium is aware of the number hanging over the at-bat.

The Stolen Base Path Is the Harder Half

The math is manageable. One home run in 24 games is not difficult for a player who has been averaging better than a home run every four games this season. But the stolen base path is more complicated. PCA needs eight more steals with fewer than a month remaining, and his green lights are not guaranteed.

The Cubs are in a comfortable spot in the wild card standings, but they are not yet clinched, and managers tend to pull back on aggressive baserunning as the postseason approaches. The calculus shifts when a stolen base attempt gone wrong could end a season-defining moment. Every base PCA takes from here carries that tension.

The home run does not carry the same tension. A swing is a swing. He does not need permission.

What This Season Has Already Done

Even before the 40-40 chase became the dominant story of the final month, Crow-Armstrong's 2026 was already one of the great individual campaigns in Cubs franchise history. He entered September leading the National League MVP race. His defense in center field has been exceptional. He has hit for average and power while providing the kind of impact on the basepaths that changes games.

The 40-40 distinction would add a permanent historical marker to a season that did not need one to be remembered. But that is exactly why it matters. History has a way of framing everything around it, and right now, the most important single swing left in the 2026 season might belong to a 24-year-old center fielder in Chicago who needs exactly one home run to do what only six players have ever done.

What's Next

The Cubs play 24 more regular season games. Every PCA at-bat is worth watching. His next home run will be covered coast to coast regardless of who is pitching or what the score is. The stolen base total is the slower-building storyline. Watch for the Cubs' baserunning philosophy to be tested in the coming weeks as the standings tighten and October approaches.
