Misiorowski Allowed 5 Earned Runs for the First Time All Season. Now What?

By Marcus Cole September 5, 2026

The Streak Is Over

Through 26 starts this season, Jacob Misiorowski had never once allowed more than three earned runs. Not in April when the weather was bad. Not in June during a seven-day road trip. Not in August when the pennant race tightened and every start carried more weight. Three earned runs was the ceiling. He never touched it.

Then he went to Wrigley Field on Wednesday night and allowed five.

The details: four innings pitched. Five runs. Five hits. Four walks. Those four walks matched a career high. Hot, humid conditions. A leadoff walk to Pete Crow-Armstrong in the first inning, then a two-run home run from Alex Bregman before he recorded an out. The Cubs chased him by the end of the second inning and Misiorowski never recovered.

The Brewers won 9-5 anyway. Their offense bailed him out. It was a Milwaukee win. But it was a Misiorowski loss in the sense that matters for the Cy Young conversation.

What the Numbers Still Say

His ERA rose from approximately 1.73 to 1.97 after Wednesday's start. That 1.97 is still the lowest ERA in baseball. He is still the overwhelming NL Cy Young favorite. No one start erases what he has built over 26 outings.

The comparison throughout this season has been to Pedro Martinez's 2000 campaign for the Red Sox, when Martinez went 18-6 with a 1.74 ERA, 0.73 WHIP, and 284 strikeouts. That season is widely considered one of the two or three greatest pitching seasons in the modern era. Misiorowski has been in that zip code all year.

Martinez had rough starts too. Every pitcher does. The question is whether Wednesday at Wrigley was weather and circumstances, or whether it signals something that could carry into September and affect the award vote or, more importantly, the postseason.

The Larger Context

The Brewers are 87-53 and sitting atop the NL Central. They are in fine shape. Misiorowski's next start will likely come around September 8 or 9, and how he looks then will tell more than Wednesday's stumble told by itself.

One bad start in September is noise. Two bad starts in September is a signal. Misiorowski's body of work is deep enough to absorb one rough night in hot Wrigley conditions. But the people watching the Cy Young race will be watching his next outing more closely because of what happened Wednesday, and that scrutiny is fair.

What's Next

Misiorowski's next scheduled start is around September 8 or 9. His ERA at 1.97 still leads all MLB starters. Watch for how he responds — his September starts will be the most scrutinized of his season, and the Cy Young Award tends to weight late-season performance heavily in close races.
