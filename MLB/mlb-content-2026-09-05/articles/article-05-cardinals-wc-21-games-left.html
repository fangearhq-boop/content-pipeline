The Cardinals Believe. The Math Says They Need 14-7.

By Marcus Cole September 5, 2026

What the Numbers Say

The St. Louis Cardinals are 70-70. They have 21 games remaining. They are 3.5 games out of the third National League wild card spot, which currently belongs to the San Diego Padres. The Arizona Diamondbacks are 0.5 games behind the Padres, meaning St. Louis is essentially 4 teams away from the last playoff berth.

To have a realistic shot at the postseason, the Cardinals need to go approximately 14-7 over their final 21 games. That pace — .667 winning percentage — would put them on track for a final record around 84-78. Whether that is enough depends entirely on how the Padres and Diamondbacks finish. The Cardinals cannot control San Diego or Arizona. They can only control their own games.

Why They Still Believe

Before the math sounds too cold, here is what Wednesday night in Los Angeles looked like: Nolan Saggese broke up Yoshinobu Yamamoto's no-hitter with a two-run home run in the sixth inning, then delivered a walk-off single in the tenth to beat the Dodgers 8-6. Leo Bernal hit his first career MLB home run. The Cardinals rallied from behind against one of the best rotations in baseball and beat them on their home field.

That is the version of this team that can go 14-7. The one that finds a way against elite pitching. The one where Saggese, still relatively new to this roster, keeps producing at-bats like that.

Saggese's emergence has been the one consistent bright spot of the Cardinals' second half. He is not a name that was on many national radar screens at the start of the season. He is now. A middle-of-the-order bat who can hit for average and drive in runs gives the Cardinals a different look than they had in April, and that is what makes the final 21 games interesting instead of academic.

The Gauntlet Ahead

The schedule is not soft. The Cardinals will face teams fighting for their own positioning in September, and a .667 pace is genuinely difficult to sustain. The Padres have experience in tight September races. The Diamondbacks have proven they can win when it matters.

But the Cardinals are not dead. A .500 team that goes 14-7 to close the season is not playing above itself — it is playing like a playoff team. The question is whether they can sustain it for three more weeks when the pressure is at its peak and every game carries consequences.

What's Next

Track the Cardinals' daily results against the Padres and Diamondbacks' records as September continues. Any Cardinals winning streak accompanied by a San Diego or Arizona slump is the scenario St. Louis needs. Watch for Saggese's production to continue being a key variable — he has been the most impactful Cardinals bat in the second half.
