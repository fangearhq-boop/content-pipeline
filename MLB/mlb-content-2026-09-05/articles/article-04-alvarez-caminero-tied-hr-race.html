37-37: Caminero Ties Alvarez, and the Triple Crown Just Got Complicated

By Ryan Calloway September 5, 2026

The HR Lead Is Gone

For weeks, the home run category looked like Yordan Alvarez's clearest Triple Crown advantage. He led Junior Caminero 36 to 35 entering September. A one-homer cushion is not comfortable, but it is a lead.

Then Caminero hit his 37th. With a fourth-inning solo shot that tied the American League home run leaderboard, the Triple Crown race shifted. The one category that was Alvarez's clearest win is now a tie. Every swing Caminero takes now has the potential to put Alvarez in a deficit for the first time all season.

Where Alvarez Stands

Despite losing the HR lead, Alvarez still controls two of the three categories. His batting average is .316, which gives him an 8-point lead over Chandler Simpson, who is hitting .308. In RBI, Alvarez is at 94, holding off Pete Alonso of Baltimore.

The last American League Triple Crown winner was Miguel Cabrera in 2012. That was 14 years ago. To win the Triple Crown, a player must lead the league in all three categories at season's end. A tie does not count. If Alvarez and Caminero finish with 40 home runs each, neither of them wins the home run title outright — they share it, and neither can claim the Triple Crown.

That is the specific danger of a tie. A tie in home runs is not a safe outcome for Alvarez. He needs to be ahead.

What the Next 24 Games Look Like

With 24 games remaining, the scenarios multiply daily. Caminero could go cold and Alvarez could separate. Alvarez could go cold and Caminero could take the lead. Simpson could close the batting average gap. Alonso could overtake the RBI race.

The Triple Crown has been chased dozens of times in the modern era. What makes it so rare is that holding three simultaneous leads over 162 games requires three separate streaks of good luck to coexist. Alvarez has come closer than almost any player in a decade. The Caminero tie changes the probability significantly. To finish with the Triple Crown, Alvarez now needs to do what he hasn't had to do all season — pull ahead in the home run race while maintaining his leads in two other categories at the same time.

What's Next

Watch the Astros' and Rays' schedules over the next two weeks as the HR race between Alvarez and Caminero plays out daily. Any change in the batting average or RBI standings will be worth tracking as well — all three races are now live simultaneously with 24 games to go.
