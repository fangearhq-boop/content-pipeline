🐻 PETE CROW-ARMSTRONG IS FOUR STOLEN BASES FROM BASEBALL HISTORY

Here's where PCA stands heading into Monday, September 14:

41 home runs. 36 stolen bases. 97 RBI.

Four stolen bases separate him from the 40-40 club — one of the most exclusive in baseball history. Only six players have ever done it:

• Jose Canseco (1988)
• Barry Bonds (1996)
• Alex Rodriguez (1998)

If he gets those four steals in the 13-14 games left on the schedule, he redefines what a Cub center fielder can be.

The Cubs (81-64) are locked into the NL Wild Card as the No. 4 seed, set to face the Phillies in the Wild Card Series starting September 29. PCA is the reason this team is still in the playoff picture — and he's not done making history yet.

### STORY 2: Alvarez Triple Crown — HR Race Tightening

**Long-Form Post (10:30 AM ET)**
⚾ YORDAN ALVAREZ'S TRIPLE CROWN BID IS ENTERING CRUNCH TIME

For most of 2026, Yordan Alvarez looked like a lock for the American League Triple Crown — leading the league in batting average, home runs, AND runs batted in at the same time. If he held on, he'd become just the second player since 1967 to do it.

But September has tightened things up.

Junior Caminero of the Tampa Bay Rays now has 39 home runs — one ahead of Alvarez. Pete Alonso of the Baltimore Orioles leads RBI at 97 to Alvarez's 95. Alvarez's batting average (.311) remains his most secure category — nobody is close to him there.

But the Triple Crown requires leading in all three. Right now, Alvarez is on top in only one.

With roughly 13 games remaining in the regular season, Alvarez needs to surge in the power categories while playing some of the final games against tough AL competition. The Houston Astros ace's bat hasn't been as hot lately — just three home runs and 13 RBI since the beginning of August, according to tracking sources — but 13 games is plenty of time to turn things around.

The last player to win an AL Triple Crown? Miguel Cabrera in 2012. The last time ANYONE won one? Cabrera, in 2012. The drought is 14 years old. Alvarez has a chance to end it — but he has to get there first.

### STORY 3: Misiorowski — Best Brewers Season in Modern History

**Long-Form Post (12:30 PM ET)**
🔥 JACOB MISIOROWSKI IS DOING SOMETHING NOBODY HAS EVER DONE

Let's talk about what Jacob Misiorowski has accomplished in 2026 — because it's genuinely historic.

The 24-year-old Brewers ace is 19-8 with a 1.95 ERA. That ERA is the best in Major League Baseball among qualified starters. He has 236 strikeouts. He's the heavy favorite for the NL Cy Young Award.

And then there's this: On September 8, Misiorowski became the first pitcher in MLB history — since pitch tracking began in 2008 — to throw 1,000 pitches at 100+ mph in a single season.

One thousand pitches. At triple digits.

In comparison, most elite power pitchers top out at a few hundred such pitches per season. Misiorowski is in a category entirely by himself.

Now he's chasing history with the Brewers' all-time strikeout record in his sights. Ben Sheets set the franchise mark with 264 strikeouts in 2004. Misiorowski has 236. He needs 28 more to break it. With the Brewers in the NL Central pennant race, he'll get the starts — and the opportunity.

Milwaukee hasn't seen a season like this in a long time. Maybe ever.

### STORY 4: Ben Rice — Subway Series Hero

**Long-Form Post (3:00 PM ET)**
⚡ BEN RICE IS DOING SOMETHING NOBODY SAW COMING — AND IT'S BEAUTIFUL

On Sunday afternoon, the Yankees beat the Mets 2-0 in the Subway Series rubber game. The headlines belong to Cam Schlittler (6 innings, 1 hit, no runs — another dominant AL Cy Young contender start) but the most meaningful moment came in the first inning when Ben Rice launched a solo home run.

No. 38.

Rice's career high last year was 26 home runs — already an impressive number. This season, he has 38. He made his MLB debut on June 18, 2024. He's a second-year player putting together a 40-homer pace that nobody projected.

He needs just two more to join the 40-homer club with 13 games left. That's very doable.

Meanwhile, Aaron Judge is also back in the lineup. Judge returned September 8 from a 99-day IL stint caused by a stress fracture in his rib — an injury that cost him roughly two-thirds of the season. He has 17 home runs in his shortened season, but more importantly, he has 385 career home runs. He needs 15 more to reach 400 — a milestone that would rank him among the greatest power hitters in Yankees history.

The Yankees have a lot to play for in these final 13 games. Two of those storylines are wearing pinstripes right now.
