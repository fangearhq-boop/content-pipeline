NL Playoff Picture With 13 Games Left: Byes, Brackets, and a Wild Card Race Worth Watching

By Ryan Calloway | September 14, 2026

With 13 games remaining in the 2026 National League regular season, the playoff picture is largely settled — but not entirely. Six teams are in, the bracket is taking shape, and one wild card spot remains genuinely competitive.

Here is where everything stands heading into the week of September 14.

The Teams With Byes

The Milwaukee Brewers hold the NL's top seed, courtesy of their NL Central lead. The Brewers' case was made all season by Jacob Misiorowski (19-8, 1.95 ERA, 236 K) and a lineup that has produced steadily throughout the year. Milwaukee will receive a first-round bye and await the winner of the Wild Card Series involving the No. 3 and No. 6 seeds.

The Los Angeles Dodgers hold the No. 2 seed after winning the NL West, earning the other first-round bye. The Brewers vs. Dodgers potential NLCS matchup is the headline matchup that a large portion of the baseball world is watching for.

The Wild Card Field

The Atlanta Braves hold the NL East lead as the No. 3 seed. The Chicago Cubs (81-64) sit as the No. 4 seed, holding Wild Card No. 2. The Philadelphia Phillies are the No. 5 seed (Wild Card No. 1).

That sets up the bracket: No. 3 Braves vs. No. 6 Padres in one Wild Card Series, and No. 4 Cubs vs. No. 5 Phillies in the other. Both Wild Card Series begin September 29 in a best-of-three format.

For the Cubs, the matchup means facing the Phillies — a dangerous team in a short series — at a time when Pete Crow-Armstrong is 4 stolen bases from the 40-40 club and the North Side fan base is energized.

The One Race Still Alive

The sixth and final NL playoff spot is the only remaining drama. The San Diego Padres hold a 2.5-game lead over the Arizona Diamondbacks for NL Wild Card No. 3. A critical three-game series between San Diego and Arizona is scheduled for September 25-27 at Petco Park — a direct head-to-head that could settle the race.

If the Padres win the series, they advance. If Arizona takes the series, the gap narrows dramatically. Both teams know what's at stake.

What's Next

The regular season ends September 27. Wild Card Series begin September 29. Division Series begin October 3. The NL's most compelling remaining question is the Padres-D-backs race, but for most of the bracket's six teams, the focus has already shifted to playoff preparation. Thirteen games from now, October baseball begins for half the National League — and the other half goes home. Keep an eye on the Petco Park series September 25-27 as the season's final major inflection point.
