Yordan Alvarez's Triple Crown Bid Is Under Pressure With 13 Games Left

By Ryan Calloway | September 14, 2026

When September began, Yordan Alvarez was one of the most compelling stories in baseball. The Houston Astros slugger was leading the American League in batting average, home runs, AND runs batted in — a combination so rare that it had only happened once in the last 14 years.

Now, two weeks into the final month, the Triple Crown is still possible — but it's no longer comfortable.

Alvarez leads the AL with a .311 batting average, and nobody is close to him there. But his home run lead is gone. Junior Caminero of the Tampa Bay Rays has pulled ahead at 39 home runs, one more than Alvarez's 38. And Pete Alonso of the Baltimore Orioles holds a slight lead in RBI, 97 to 95.

For the Triple Crown to happen, Alvarez needs to lead in all three categories when the final out is recorded. He currently leads one of three.

A September Slump at the Worst Time

Part of the challenge is timing. Alvarez's power numbers have slowed since the beginning of August — just three home runs and 13 RBI over that stretch, according to tracking sources, in a season where he'd been consistently producing at an elite rate. That's not a collapse, but it's enough to allow Caminero's September push to take the home run lead.

The good news: 13 games is still enough time for a surge. Alvarez is one of the most powerful hitters in the game, and a one-game home run explosion can shift all three categories simultaneously.

But the pressure is real. Caminero has been productive in September, and Alonso — who spent the early part of the season with the Mets before landing in Baltimore — has been one of the steadier run producers in the AL all year.

What the Triple Crown Would Mean

The last AL Triple Crown winner was Miguel Cabrera of the Detroit Tigers in 2012. Yastrzemski's 1967 crown was the one before that. In the 59 years since Yastrzemski and the 14 years since Cabrera, no one has held all three categories simultaneously at season's end.

Alvarez winning would end the drought and cement his case as the greatest hitter in the American League. His batting average lead is historically significant — .311 with enough plate appearances to qualify is elite — but the home runs and RBI will be decided in the final two weeks of the season.

What's Next

Thirteen games remain. Caminero holds the HR lead by one (39 vs 38) and Alonso holds the RBI lead by two (97 vs 95). Alvarez's .311 batting average is his most secure category. The next week and a half will determine whether 2026 adds a chapter to Triple Crown history or files this as the one that got away. Track the numbers daily — in a race this tight, every game matters.
