If he held on, he'd become just the second player since 1967 to do it. But September has tightened things up. Junior Caminero of the Tampa Bay Rays now has 39 home runs — one ahead of Alvarez. Pete Alonso of the Baltimore Orioles leads RBI at 97 to Alvarez's 95. Alvarez's batting average (.311) remains his most secure category — nobody is close to him there. But the Triple Crown requires leading in all three. Right now, Alvarez is on top in only one. With roughly 13 games remaining in the regular season, Alvarez needs to surge in the power categories while playing some of the final games against tough AL competition. The Houston Astros ace's bat hasn't been as hot lately — just three home runs and 13 RBI since the beginning of August, according to tracking sources — but 13 games is plenty of time to turn things around. The last player to win an AL Triple Crown? Miguel Cabrera in 2012. The last time ANYONE won one? Cabrera, in 2012. The drought is 14 years old. Alvarez has a chance to end it — but he has to get there first. ### Image Post Caption Alvarez leads AL BA. But Caminero leads HR. And Alonso leads RBI.
