Ohtani at the Crossroads: Can He Save the Dodgers in Game 4?

By Jake Torres | October 15, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The question surrounding the Los Angeles Dodgers tonight is the same one everyone in baseball is asking.

Where is Shohei Ohtani?

Through the first three games of the NLCS, Ohtani is hitting .150 with zero home runs, seven strikeouts, and a .411 OPS. His last home run came during the regular season on August 18 — nearly two months ago. The player who redefines expectations every time he steps on a field has been conspicuously absent in the biggest moments of the Dodgers' postseason.

The Context of the Silence

None of this means Ohtani is broken. October is where the best pitchers in baseball are deployed in optimal matchups against opposing lineups' most dangerous hitters. Ohtani's at-bats have been competitive — he's not chasing pitches recklessly — but the results have not been there. He has looked mortal in a way that fans who watched him throughout the regular season have not been accustomed to seeing.

The Brewers' pitching staff has been excellent in this series. That context matters. Multiple Dodgers hitters have struggled, not just Ohtani. But because of who Ohtani is — the face of the franchise, the player the organization rebuilt around — his absence is the one that draws the most attention.

What the Moment Demands

Tonight is Game 4. The Dodgers trail the series 3-0. If they lose, their season is over. There is no simpler statement of stakes than that.

Great players have bad weeks. But they also have the moments where the circumstances demand everything and they answer. Ohtani has been in those moments before — his two-way performance in the 2023 World Baseball Classic, his regular-season games where he has hit and pitched in the same week and dominated both. The instinct for the big moment is part of who he is.

The Dodgers need Glasnow to pitch well. They need their bullpen to hold. But they also need their best hitter to be their best hitter. Not someone who's okay in a tough series, but the player who made the Dodgers believe they could win another title. Tonight is when that player either shows up or the season ends.

What's Next

NLCS Game 4 is tonight at Dodger Stadium, 8 PM ET on FS1. If Los Angeles wins, the series continues Friday night with Game 5 in Los Angeles. If Milwaukee wins, the Brewers advance to the World Series for the first time since 1982.

Whatever happens, tonight is likely the most important game of Ohtani's first two seasons in a Dodgers uniform. The moment has arrived.
