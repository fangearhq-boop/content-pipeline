NLCS Game 4 Preview: Brewers on the Brink of the World Series, Dodgers Face Elimination

By Ryan Calloway | October 15, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The Milwaukee Brewers are 27 outs away from the World Series.

After routing the Los Angeles Dodgers 6-2 in Game 3 — punctuated by back-to-back home runs off Blake Snell in the seventh inning from Jake Bauers and Garrett Mitchell — Milwaukee holds a 3-0 series lead in the NLCS. Tonight, Game 4 comes to Dodger Stadium. Start time: 8 PM ET on FS1, moved one hour earlier from the originally scheduled slot.

The Dodgers, two-time reigning World Series champions, face elimination in front of their home crowd. Tyler Glasnow starts for Los Angeles. The Brewers send their Game 4 starter for a team that has won three games and needs one more.

What the Dodgers Need

For Los Angeles, the calculation is simple but demanding. Glasnow needs to pitch deep into the game and limit Milwaukee's offense. The Brewers have been hitting everyone this series — they knocked around Walker Buehler in Game 2, then torched Snell in Game 3. Glasnow is the Dodgers' best remaining arm, and they need a starting performance that changes the tone of the night.

The offense needs a reset. Shohei Ohtani, the centerpiece of everything Los Angeles does, has been held to a .150 batting average with zero home runs and seven strikeouts through the first three games. His OPS stands at .411 — a fraction of what the two-time MVP produced in the regular season. If the Dodgers are going to extend this series, they almost certainly need Ohtani to look like the player he has been all year.

Mookie Betts, Freddie Freeman, and the rest of the lineup need to find ways to put up crooked numbers against a Milwaukee pitching staff that has been suffocating.

The Brewers' Perspective

Milwaukee does not need to be perfect tonight. They need to be steady. The pressure belongs to Los Angeles.

The Brewers have won this series by playing complete baseball — pitching first, limiting mistakes, and capitalizing when big moments arrive. Bauers and Mitchell's back-to-back shots off Snell in the seventh inning of Game 3 are the defining image of this series: a team that won't stop swinging, against a pitcher who was supposed to be the answer.

A sweep of the two-time defending World Series champions, in their ballpark, after going down 0-3, would be one of the most remarkable things any team has done in October in quite some time.

What's Next

NLCS Game 4 is tonight at Dodger Stadium, 8 PM ET on FS1. If the Brewers win, they advance to the World Series for the first time since 1982. If the Dodgers win, Game 5 shifts to Friday night (Oct. 16) at Dodger Stadium, 9 PM ET.

The Brewers have given every indication they are ready to close. The Dodgers are playing their best when their backs are against the wall. Tonight finds out which of those things is more true.
