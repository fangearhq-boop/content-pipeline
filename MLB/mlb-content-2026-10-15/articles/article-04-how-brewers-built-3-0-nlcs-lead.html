How the Brewers Built a 3-0 NLCS Lead: The Story of Milwaukee's October

By Ryan Calloway | October 15, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

When the Milwaukee Brewers entered the 2026 postseason with the best record in the National League at 103-59, the expectation was that they were a serious contender. What nobody quite anticipated was that they would look like this in October.

Three games into the NLCS, the Brewers have outscored the Los Angeles Dodgers 15-4. They lead the series 3-0. Tonight they can complete a sweep of the two-time defending World Series champions — and do it in the Dodgers' own ballpark.

How did Milwaukee get here?

The Foundation: 103 Wins and a Franchise Built for October

The 2026 Brewers finished with a franchise-best 103 wins. That run through the regular season was built on the same principles that have made them so dangerous in October: pitching depth, smart roster construction, and a lineup that hits with discipline rather than relying on one or two marquee names to carry everything.

Milwaukee's model is sustainable. They don't have a single player who makes or breaks their season. Every night, a different piece delivers — and in a seven-game series, that depth is extraordinarily difficult to prepare for as an opposing staff.

The NLCS: Three Games, Three Wins

The Brewers won Game 1 by 2-1. They won Game 2 by 7-4, with William Contreras delivering the go-ahead hits. Game 3 was their most emphatic statement yet.

With the Dodgers throwing Blake Snell — their most trusted October arm, posted a 1.13 ERA across eight starts since returning from surgery — Milwaukee waited for their moment and then delivered it decisively. Jake Bauers and Garrett Mitchell hit back-to-back home runs off Snell in the seventh inning to turn a close game into a 6-2 Milwaukee victory. That inning is the defining image of this series: Milwaukee refusing to be impressed, refusing to be shut down, doing what they have done all October when the game is on the line.

The Road to 1982 Becoming 2026

The Brewers last reached the World Series in 1982, when they lost to the St. Louis Cardinals. The 44 years since have included playoff runs, near-misses, and one or two breakthrough moments. But the World Series has remained out of reach.

This team is built to end that. They're not a flash in the pan. They're a 103-win club, managed carefully, constructed with purpose, playing their best baseball in October. If they can close out the Dodgers tonight — and the road ahead is one win in a stadium where LA has played some of its best baseball — Milwaukee will take that 44-year wait and finally put it to rest.

What's Next

Game 4 is tonight at Dodger Stadium, 8 PM ET on FS1. Win tonight and the Brewers are World Series-bound. The franchise, the fan base, and the whole city of Milwaukee are 27 outs away from something they haven't felt in four decades.
