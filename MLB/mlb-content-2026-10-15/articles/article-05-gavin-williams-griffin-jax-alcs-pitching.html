Williams vs. Jax: The Pitching Matchup That Could Define the ALCS

By Marcus Cole | October 15, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The ALCS has been evenly matched through two games. The Guardians won Game 1 in a 7-3 comeback at Tropicana Field. The Rays won Game 2 with a 3-1 victory behind Freddy Peralta's brilliant six innings, with all three Tampa Bay runs coming on unearned errors. Both teams are 1-1 and know exactly what the other is capable of.

Today's Game 3 at Progressive Field — moved to 1 PM ET due to weather — features the most consequential pitching matchup of the series. Gavin Williams starts for Cleveland. Griffin Jax starts for Tampa Bay. The winner of today's game takes a 2-1 series lead that statistically has been extremely difficult to surrender in LCS play.

Gavin Williams: Cleveland's October Answer

Williams arrived at this postseason with a clear credential: he started and starred in the ALDS Game 5 clincher against the Chicago White Sox, the game that completed Cleveland's remarkable comeback from an 0-2 series deficit. The Guardians had no business winning that series. They came back anyway, and Williams was a central reason why.

That experience matters in Game 3. Williams has already proven he can hold together when elimination pressure is highest. The Guardians are not in elimination territory today — they're tied and playing at home — but the stakes are enormous regardless. A Williams gem that pushes Cleveland to a 2-1 series lead would shift the balance of this ALCS significantly.

Williams will need to handle a Rays lineup that is disciplined and well-designed to expose pitchers who fall behind in counts. His ability to work ahead and avoid walks early in the game is the key tactical question for the afternoon.

Griffin Jax: Tampa's Trade Deadline Investment

Griffin Jax was acquired from the Minnesota Twins at the trade deadline — a deliberate move by Tampa Bay's front office to add starting depth for exactly this kind of postseason scenario. He was brought in not as a rotation anchor for a full season but as a piece capable of delivering high-leverage starts when the margins are thinnest.

The question is whether he can deliver under pressure he has not faced before. Starting a playoff game on the road, in a stadium that will be loud and hostile, against a lineup with Jose Ramirez hitting from the middle of it — that's a different context than anything in Jax's prior experience. How he handles his first inning will be telling. Pitchers who come out and throw strikes early tend to settle; pitchers who are squeezed or wild in the first frame tend to be in trouble all afternoon.

Tampa's decision to go with Jax here reflects their confidence in his preparation and the quality of their supporting cast. If he can give the Rays five or six innings and keep Cleveland to three runs or fewer, Tampa's bullpen is capable of closing it out.

What's Next

ALCS Game 3 is today at 1 PM ET at Progressive Field in Cleveland. TBS has the broadcast. The winner takes a 2-1 series lead heading into Game 4, which will likely be scheduled for Thursday night or Friday afternoon depending on today's timing and weather. In a series this close, one game of separation could be everything.
