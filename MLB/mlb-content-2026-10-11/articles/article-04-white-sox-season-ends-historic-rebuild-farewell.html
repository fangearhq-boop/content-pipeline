The White Sox Season Is Over — Remember What They Actually Did

By Marcus Cole | October 11, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The Chicago White Sox are out of the playoffs. Their 2026 season ended in Cleveland on Saturday night, eliminated in the ALDS after blowing a 2-0 series lead. The Guardians were the better team when it mattered most. That is the end of the story.

But the story deserves more than an ending.

Where They Came From

Two years ago, the White Sox lost 121 games. One year ago, they lost more than 100. In back-to-back seasons, they were the worst team in major league baseball — a program in the midst of a teardown rebuild that looked like it might take a decade to complete. Most preseason projections in 2026 had Chicago finishing at the bottom of the AL Central again.

Instead, they went 81-81 and became the first team in MLB history to clinch a playoff berth with a non-winning record. They beat the Houston Astros — a franchise that has been to the ALCS three times in a decade — in the Wild Card round. And then they took the Guardians to a fifth game in the ALDS, with a chance to reach the American League Championship Series for the first time since 2005.

The People Who Built It

General Manager Chris Getz and manager Will Venable oversaw a roster where seven of the nine starters have fewer than two years of major league experience. They did not paper over the rebuild with expensive free agents. They developed from within, supplemented with targeted additions, and produced a team that competed in October ahead of schedule by several years.

That core is still here. This is not the end of the White Sox. This is the beginning of it.

What's Next

The White Sox enter the offseason with a young core intact, a front office that has proven it can develop players, and a fan base that watched its team play meaningful October baseball for the first time in 21 years. The 2027 roster will be built around the players who just competed against the Guardians. What they proved in 2026 is that the timeline has been accelerated — and that October should not feel like a surprise much longer.
