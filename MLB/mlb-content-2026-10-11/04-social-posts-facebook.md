### STORY 1: Guardians Complete ALDS Comeback

**FB Long-Form — 9:00 AM ET**
One of the most improbable comebacks in recent postseason history just happened in Cleveland.

The Guardians were down 0-2 to the Chicago White Sox. The numbers were brutal — teams leading 2-0 in a best-of-five postseason series go on to win 83 of 93 times, according to historical data. Everything pointed to a White Sox ALCS appearance.

And then José Ramírez showed up.

Playing through the lingering effects of left hamate surgery that he underwent in June, Ramírez hit a home run and drove in three runs in Cleveland's 9-5 Game 4 win. He had been everywhere in this series — with his bat, his glove, and the presence that has made him one of the most important players in baseball when October arrives. That performance forced a decisive Game 5 at Progressive Field, where the Guardians closed it out on Saturday night.

The last team to complete a 0-2 comeback in a best-of-five postseason was the 2017 New York Yankees — and they did it against these same Cleveland Guardians (then the Indians). Poetic justice comes slowly in baseball, but it does come.

Now Cleveland heads to the ALCS, where they will face the Tampa Bay Rays in Game 1 on Monday, October 12, at Tropicana Field.

### STORY 2: NLCS Game 1 Tonight

**FB Long-Form — 12:00 PM ET**
Tonight is what baseball's postseason was built for.

The Los Angeles Dodgers — two-time defending world champions — travel to Milwaukee to face the Brewers in Game 1 of the National League Championship Series. The first pitch is at 8 PM ET on FOX. American Family Field will be as loud as it has ever been.

For the second straight year, these two teams are meeting for the NL pennant. The Dodgers won last year's series. The Brewers, who went 103-59 this season — the best record in the National League — are hosting an NLCS for the first time in franchise history. The 1982 pennant is the closest Milwaukee has come to the World Series. They want to change that tonight.

On the mound for Milwaukee: Jacob Misiorowski, who finished the regular season 11-5 with a 1.76 ERA — one of the best numbers in baseball. He was outstanding through the Brewers' NLDS run against San Diego and gets the ball in the biggest start of his career.

And on the Dodgers side: Shohei Ohtani is in the pitching rotation for this series. After a regular season in which he managed his workload carefully, Ohtani takes the mound in the NLCS — adding a dimension to the Dodgers' rotation that no team in baseball can match.

This is the matchup of the postseason. Tonight is Game 1.

### STORY 3: ALCS Preview — Guardians vs. Rays

**FB Long-Form — 3:00 PM ET**
The American League Championship Series is set.

On one side: the Tampa Bay Rays, who were so dominant in their first-round series against the New York Yankees that it almost looked easy. Victor Mesa Jr. homered in the clincher. The Rays allowed just five runs in a three-game sweep. They are rested, healthy, and waiting.

On the other side: the Cleveland Guardians, who spent the last week of their lives fighting off elimination. Down 0-2 against the White Sox, they won three games in a row — the hardest thing to do in postseason baseball. Every one of those wins required something from their roster that they had to reach for.

Game 1 is Monday, October 12, at 8 PM ET on TBS at Tropicana Field in St. Petersburg. The pitching matchup to watch: Parker Messick (2.68 ERA, 20 starts) is expected to start for Cleveland, while Drew Rasmussen (3.28 ERA) likely goes for Tampa Bay.

In the Rays lineup, Junior Caminero, Yandy Díaz, and Jonathan Aranda anchor a middle-of-the-order that scored runs in bunches against New York. Cleveland has to shut down that production while Ramírez — still nursing a hand that required surgery five months ago — leads the offense.

Two teams. Two very different paths to the same place. The best-of-seven begins Monday.

### STORY 4: White Sox Farewell

**FB Long-Form — 5:30 PM ET**
The Chicago White Sox are done. Their 2026 season is over.

But before we turn the page, let's acknowledge what they actually did — because no one who watched this team should forget it.

One year ago, the White Sox lost 121 games. The year before that, they lost more than 100. In two consecutive seasons, they were the worst team in baseball. The roster was young, unproven, and under construction. Most projections had them finishing last in the AL Central again.

Instead: they went 81-81. They became the first team in Major League Baseball history to clinch a playoff berth without a winning record. They beat the Houston Astros in the Wild Card round — a legitimate powerhouse — and then they went up 2-0 on the Cleveland Guardians in the ALDS. For four days, they were the story of October.

General Manager Chris Getz and manager Will Venable built something from next to nothing. The young roster — seven of the nine starters have fewer than two years of MLB experience — performed under October pressure. They fell short in the end. The Guardians are better. But 81-81 followed by 121 losses is one of the most remarkable single-season turnarounds in recent baseball history.

Remember this team.
