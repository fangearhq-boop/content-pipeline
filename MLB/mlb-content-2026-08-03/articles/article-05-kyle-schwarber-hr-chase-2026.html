Kyle Schwarber Entered August With 33 HR. The Barry Bonds Record Is Now in the Conversation.

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter

The number sits out there quietly, not yet screaming but impossible to ignore once you see it: 72. That's the pace Kyle Schwarber is on for home runs in 2026. Barry Bonds' all-time record is 73, set in 2001 — the year that many assumed would end the era of genuine 70-home-run threats in baseball.

Twenty-five years later, Schwarber is making it look possible again.

Where He Stands

Schwarber entered August with 33 home runs. He is the first Philadelphia Phillies player to reach the 30-home-run mark before August in franchise history. That alone would be worth noting. But the pace is what turns this from a good season into a potential piece of baseball history.

He has now hit 30 or more home runs in six consecutive seasons — the longest active streak in Major League Baseball. He didn't start that streak with one exceptional year and fade; he built it, maintained it, and in 2026 has pushed it into territory nobody expected from a player who has always been more feared than dominant over a full year.

The June and July performance is what powered the pace projection. His first-half pace has been relentless in a way that his previous seasons haven't always been — fewer cold stretches, fewer multi-week droughts where the power dried up. The consistency has been different this year, and that consistency is what produces a 72-HR pace rather than the 40-something that has been his actual finishing number most years.

The Derby That Didn't Help

Schwarber was the runner-up at the 2026 Home Run Derby, losing to St. Louis Cardinals outfielder Jordan Walker 12-11 in the final. It was a legitimately close contest. It also served as a reminder that Schwarber can generate power at a rate that gets attention in a showcase environment.

Derby performance doesn't predict second-half numbers, but it does speak to the quality of the swing. Schwarber's swing still generates elite exit velocity. The contact rate has never been his strength — his career average reflects a pull-happy approach that produces pop but also strikeouts — but the power tool that drives everything is clearly still operating at full output.

The Record, Put in Context

To actually reach 73 home runs, Schwarber would need to maintain roughly his current pace through the remaining two months of the season. That's approximately 39-40 home runs in the second half alone — a number only a handful of players have ever approached in any single half of a season.

The more realistic and still historically significant conversations are in the 55-65 range:

Roger Maris hit 61 in 1961, which stood as the American League record for decades and the NL-style record in many reference points. The 60-homer club — Bonds (73, 2001), Sammy Sosa (66, 64, 63 across multiple seasons), Mark McGwire (70, 65) — is the short list of the most prolific single-season power performances in history. If Schwarber reaches 60, he joins a group of four players who have done it in over 150 years of professional baseball.

That's the number worth watching in August. Not 73, but 60. And then 65. And then see what's left on the calendar.

What the Second Half Looks Like

Schwarber's historical second-half splits have occasionally shown a slight decline — not a collapse, but a reversion toward a slightly lower pace than his white-hot first halves. The Phillies lineup around him remains excellent; Bryce Harper, Nick Castellanos, and the rest of the order give opposing pitchers very few places to put Schwarber on base intentionally without consequences.

He will get pitches to hit. Whether he hits them at the rate needed to stay on pace for 70-plus is the question August will begin to answer.

For now, though, 33 home runs entering August with the Phillies in the thick of a pennant race is a remarkable place to be. The second half of Kyle Schwarber's 2026 season is going to be worth watching. All of it.
