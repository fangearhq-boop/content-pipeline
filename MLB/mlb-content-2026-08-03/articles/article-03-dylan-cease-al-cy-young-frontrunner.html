Dylan Cease Is the AL Cy Young Frontrunner — And the Trade Deadline Just Made It Official

By Ryan Calloway | Ballpark Banter

The trade deadline has a way of clarifying things. When Tarik Skubal was traded from Detroit to Los Angeles on August 2, it didn't just change the World Series odds. It changed the AL Cy Young race.

Dylan Cease is now the frontrunner. And after watching what he did in July, that designation feels entirely earned.

The July That Defined His Case

Cease's July ERA was 1.00. Not 1.09, not 1.14. One-point-zero-zero, in a full calendar month of starts in the American League. That figure ranks as the fourth-lowest single-month ERA in Toronto Blue Jays franchise history for any pitcher with at least 30 innings pitched. He is in rare company in a franchise that has iced Roger Clemens in his prime.

His signature moment in July: a one-hit complete game against the Boston Red Sox, striking out 12. It was the first one-hit complete game by a Blue Jays pitcher in 16 years. That's not just a good outing — that's a franchise-history-worthy performance, tucked into what has been the best month of pitching Toronto has seen in years.

Season-wide, his numbers hold up to any scrutiny. Cease is 7-5 with a 2.46 ERA, 167 strikeouts in 113.1 innings across 17 starts. His K/9 rate of 13.26 leads the American League. He has been, quietly or not, one of the three or four best pitchers in the sport all season.

Why the Trade Changes Things

Before the deadline, the AL Cy Young race was at minimum a two-horse conversation. Tarik Skubal, with a 2.79 ERA in 16 starts for Detroit, had a legitimate case of his own — better numbers on a per-game basis, with the full-season Cy Young narrative in play.

That narrative fractured the moment Skubal boarded a plane to Los Angeles.

Cy Young voters evaluate pitchers' full seasons with one club. A mid-season trade — even a trade that gives Skubal a legitimate shot at a ring — breaks the season in half. His Detroit numbers count, but his Dodgers numbers will be compiled in a different league, with different opponents, as a newcomer to a new system. That's not an advantage.

Cease, by contrast, is going nowhere. The Blue Jays signed him in December 2025 to a 7-year, $210 million contract. They did not accept any trade offers at this deadline. Cease will finish 2026 in Toronto, with every start in one uniform, compiling one continuous body of work for Cy Young voters to evaluate.

Who's Chasing Him?

Cam Schlittler sits second in AL fWAR at 4.2, just ahead of Cease's 4.1. That gap is close enough that the race remains genuinely competitive. Schlittler has his own excellent season to point to. But fWAR is one input among many for Cy Young voters — ERA and strikeouts carry real weight, and Cease's 2.46 ERA and 167 Ks provide a compelling case in those traditional categories.

The field beyond Schlittler is largely a second tier. No other AL starter has separated himself the way Cease has over the course of a full season.

The Second Half Question

The Cy Young is won in the second half. Cease has to prove that July wasn't a peak. He has to sustain — not necessarily 1.00 ERA territory, but the level that makes him the best pitcher in the league entering September.

Based on what he's shown, that's not a stretch. His stuff hasn't wavered. His command has been consistent. And the Blue Jays, fighting for their own playoff position, will give him every opportunity to make his case start by start.

The deadline is done. Skubal is in Los Angeles. The AL Cy Young race has a clear leader.

His name is Dylan Cease, and he's just getting started.
