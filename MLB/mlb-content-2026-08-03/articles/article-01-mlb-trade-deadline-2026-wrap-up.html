2026 MLB Trade Deadline: Every Major Move, Ranked by Impact

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter

The 2026 MLB trade deadline closes at 6 PM ET today — and it has already been one of the most active markets in recent memory. Teams across both leagues spent the final days loading up for October or cashing in prospects while the price was right. Here's a breakdown of every major confirmed move, and what each deal means for the second half.

The Blockbuster: Tarik Skubal to Los Angeles

The deadline's defining deal was already done before the calendar turned to August 3. Tarik Skubal — two-time AL Cy Young Award winner, 2.79 ERA in 16 starts this season — is now a Los Angeles Dodger. Detroit got a premium package in return: Zyhir Hope (the Dodgers' No. 5 prospect and a top-25 overall talent per MLB Pipeline), right-hander River Ryan (No. 7 in the system), and Brady Smith (No. 17). The Dodgers paid a genuine price for a genuine ace. Skubal is a free agent after 2026, making this a win-now move at full cost.

The Rotation Moves

Freddy Peralta to the Rays: The Mets sent Peralta to Tampa Bay as part of the ongoing sell-off, receiving prospects Aidan Smith, Gary Gill Hill, and Émilien Pitre. Peralta gives a Rays rotation that constantly finds ways to compete another arm with legitimate swing-and-miss stuff.

Luis García Jr. to the Yankees: Washington continued to sell. New York added García Jr. from the Nationals, returning Ben Grable, Yovanny Cruz, Jake Bird, and Jack Cebert to the capital. The Yankees needed rotation depth for an October run, and García provides length.

Luis Castillo to the White Sox: The surprise of the deadline. Castillo is 3-9 with a 5.06 ERA in 2026 — not the line you expect to trigger a trade. But Seattle wanted the $24.2 million salary off the books after sliding out of Wild Card contention, and Chicago sent Seranthony Domínguez, Nolan Jones, and Boston Smith to get him. The White Sox are betting on pedigree: Castillo was a back-to-back All-Star in 2021 and 2022 and the ace of Seattle's 2022 playoff run. The stuff is still in there somewhere.

Bailey Falter to the Braves: Came over from Kansas City alongside Lane Thomas in a two-for-one deal. The Braves sent Lucas Braun and Carter Holton to the Royals. Falter joins an Atlanta rotation anchored by Chris Sale, who is 12-6 with a 2.08 ERA at age 37.

Dean Kremer and AJ Minter to the Twins: Minnesota made two separate deals — acquiring Kremer from Baltimore and Minter from Atlanta. The Twins are in a tight AL Wild Card race and needed both rotation depth and bullpen help. They got both.

Camilo Doval to the Pirates: San Francisco continued to sell its veterans while Pittsburgh adds a proven high-leverage arm. Doval has the closing experience Pittsburgh needs as a young team tries to stay competitive down the stretch.

The Position Player Deals

Lane Thomas to the Braves (part of the Royals package) gives Atlanta an outfielder who has shown he can contribute in a bench or platoon role for a pennant contender.

Logan O'Hoppe and Chase Silseth to the Rangers: The Angels continued a sell-off, moving the catching prospect O'Hoppe and right-hander Silseth to Texas. The Rangers sent Angel Arredondo back to Anaheim.

Bo Naylor and Codi Heuer to the Brewers: Cleveland traded the catcher and reliever to Milwaukee, receiving Craig Yoho and Blake Perkins. The Brewers added depth for a postseason push.

Curtis Mead to the Red Sox: Tampa Bay's versatile prospect heads to Boston in a prospect-for-depth deal as the Rays continue their annual roster churn.

The Veteran Rotation Swap

In one of the more unusual moves of the deadline, Toronto and Chicago swapped veteran starters: the Blue Jays acquired Jameson Taillon from the Cubs, while Kevin Gausman went from Toronto to Chicago. Both are rentals, both teams get a change of scenery for proven arms.

The One Who Didn't Move: Adley Rutschman

Baltimore catcher Adley Rutschman had been the most-discussed player not yet on the move heading into deadline day. He landed on the 10-day IL with wrist inflammation — effectively taking him off the market before 6 PM ET. Whether Baltimore was truly prepared to trade him remains unclear. He's under team control through 2028.

When the deadline buzzer sounds this evening, the teams that made their moves will begin the work of integration. The real evaluation begins in October.
