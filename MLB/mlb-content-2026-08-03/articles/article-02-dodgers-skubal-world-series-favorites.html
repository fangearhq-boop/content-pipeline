Dodgers Add Skubal to an Already Loaded Rotation — They're the World Series Favorites

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter

When the Los Angeles Dodgers entered the 2026 season, the plan was to let Shohei Ohtani run their rotation. He posted a 1.79 ERA in 14 starts before a left knee and right biceps combination pulled him from the mound. The Dodgers needed a contingency plan. They didn't settle for one. They acquired the best starting pitcher available on the trade market.

Tarik Skubal — two-time reigning AL Cy Young Award winner, 2.79 ERA in 16 starts this season — is now a Los Angeles Dodger. The deal, confirmed August 2, sent outfielder Zyhir Hope (the Dodgers' No. 5 prospect and a top-25 overall talent per MLB Pipeline), right-hander River Ryan (No. 7 in the system), and Brady Smith to Detroit. It was a real price, paid for a real ace.

What Skubal Brings

Skubal's 2026 numbers with Detroit — 2.79 ERA, 31 percent strikeout rate, 16 starts — are dominant. He missed time earlier in the year but returned to the level he's been at for two Cy Young cycles. His stuff is legitimate: a high-spin fastball that plays up in the zone, a slider that misses barrels at an elite clip, and the command to work deep into games against lineup top-to-bottom.

Five teams were bidding for Skubal when the market opened. The Dodgers won that bidding war, and the price tells you something: this was a genuine contest among serious organizations. Los Angeles simply paid the most.

There is a significant caveat. Skubal is a free agent after 2026. This is a one-year rental. The Dodgers are not acquiring him for a dynasty — they're acquiring him to win a World Series this October, and they're making the financial and prospect math work on that timeline alone.

The Rotation Picture

Here's what the Dodgers' October rotation looks like, pending Ohtani's return:

Tarik Skubal. Tyler Glasnow. Shohei Ohtani — if and when he returns from the biceps and knee issues that have kept him off the mound since July 3.

That is not a normal baseball rotation. That is a problem for every other team's lineup card.

Dave Roberts confirmed last week, after Ohtani completed a 31-pitch bullpen session, that it would be "at least a week to 10 days" before Ohtani pitches in a game. He will not pitch on the current road trip. His 2026 ERA before going down was 1.79. If he returns healthy and progresses on schedule, he could pitch in games through August and be ready for a full October workload.

The Dodgers don't need Ohtani to get back. With Skubal and Glasnow, they have two legitimate aces for a potential playoff rotation. Ohtani returning would simply make them historical.

The World Series Picture

The Dodgers were already one of baseball's best teams before the Skubal trade. They lead the NL West by a comfortable margin, their lineup remains among the best in baseball, and their bullpen has been reliable throughout the season.

Adding Skubal removes the only genuine question mark anyone had about Los Angeles: rotation depth in a seven-game series. The Dodgers now have an answer for Games 1 and 2 in any playoff round, with Glasnow available for Game 3 and Ohtani — if healthy — available for Games 4 and 5.

The rest of the field still exists. The Braves have Chris Sale. The Phillies have a lineup that could beat anyone. The American League has multiple contenders. But there is one team that separated from the rest of the field at this deadline.

The Dodgers are the team to beat. They chose to be. They paid the price to earn it.
