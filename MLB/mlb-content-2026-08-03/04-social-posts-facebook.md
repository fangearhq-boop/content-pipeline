## STORY 2: Dodgers + Skubal — World Series Favorites

### FB Long-Form Post — Post Time: 12:30 PM ET

Let's take stock of what the Los Angeles Dodgers now have.

Starting rotation: Tarik Skubal (2.79 ERA, 2x AL Cy Young), Tyler Glasnow, and — whenever he returns from his biceps/knee issues — Shohei Ohtani (1.79 ERA in 14 pre-injury starts this season).

That's not a rotation. That's an October embarrassment of riches.

The price was steep: Zyhir Hope (a top-25 overall prospect per MLB Pipeline), River Ryan, and Brady Smith. Those are real pieces, not spare parts. The Tigers got genuine value.

But the Dodgers didn't pay that price to be a 90-win regular season team. They paid it to win a World Series. And with Skubal added — even as a rental, even with Ohtani still uncertain — they are now the clearest favorite in baseball.

The only question is whether Ohtani gets back in time for October. Dave Roberts said last week it would be "at least a week to 10 days" from his July 29 bullpen session. He last pitched July 3. If his timeline holds, Ohtani could be back on the mound in August.

If he is, the Dodgers won't just be favorites. They'll be overwhelming favorites.

## STORY 3: Dylan Cease — AL Cy Young Frontrunner

### FB Long-Form Post — Post Time: 2:30 PM ET

With the trade deadline now behind us, the AL Cy Young race has gotten clearer.

Dylan Cease is the frontrunner.

The Blue Jays left-hander has been exceptional in his first season in Toronto: 7-5, 2.46 ERA, 167 strikeouts in 113.1 innings. His July was otherworldly — a 1.00 ERA that ranked as the fourth-lowest single-month figure in Blue Jays franchise history (minimum 30 innings pitched). He also threw a one-hit complete game against the Red Sox, striking out 12 — the first one-hit CG by a Blue Jays pitcher in 16 years.

Toronto did not trade him. That matters.

With Tarik Skubal now splitting his 2026 season between Detroit and Los Angeles, Cease is the only true ace to have pitched for one team all year. That full-season continuity narrative is exactly what Cy Young voters reward.

His closest competition is still out there — Cam Schlittler is second in AL fWAR at 4.2 to Cease's 4.1 — but the gap will widen if Cease's second half mirrors his July.

## STORY 4: White Sox — The Fastest Rebuild Nobody Saw Coming

### FB Long-Form Post — Post Time: 3:30 PM ET

Two years ago, the Chicago White Sox lost 121 games. It was the worst single-season record in MLB history — a franchise bottoming out in plain sight.

Yesterday, they were a trade deadline buyer.

The marquee move: acquiring Luis Castillo from the Seattle Mariners for a package including Seranthony Domínguez, Nolan Jones, and Boston Smith. Castillo is 3-9 with a 5.06 ERA this season, so this isn't a stat chase. This is a bet on pedigree — and on absorbing the $24.2M salary that Seattle wanted off their books.

Castillo was an All-Star in 2021 and 2022. He was the ace of Seattle's 2022 playoff rotation. The stuff is still there. The results in 2026 haven't matched it, but the White Sox are betting that changes under new context.

Two years from 121 losses to deadline buyer. That's an accelerated arc — and a signal that Chicago believes its window is opening.

## STORY 5: Kyle Schwarber's HR Chase

### FB Long-Form Post — Post Time: 5:30 PM ET

Kyle Schwarber entered August with 33 home runs.

Per projections, that puts him on pace for approximately 72 — one shy of Barry Bonds' all-time record of 73, set in 2001. Nobody has seriously threatened that number since Bonds put it on the board.

Schwarber isn't just threatening. He's doing it with company: the Phillies' lineup around him gives opposing pitchers very few places to hide. He's been walked intentionally this season, but nowhere near enough to meaningfully slow the pace.

What he's already done: he's the first Phillies player to reach 30 home runs before August. He's hit 30+ in six consecutive seasons — the longest active streak in MLB. He lost the 2026 Home Run Derby final to Cardinals outfielder Jordan Walker, 12-11, which probably hurt more than it should have given the context.

The second-half history: Schwarber has sometimes slowed after the All-Star break in past years. But his overall trajectory in 2026 has been different — more consistent, with fewer cold stretches. If the pace holds, the conversation around 60 becomes real in September.

And then 70 becomes real. And then you're looking at 73 in the rearview.
