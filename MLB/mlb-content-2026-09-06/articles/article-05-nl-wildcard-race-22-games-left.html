22 Games Left: The Only NL Wild Card Spot Still Up for Grabs

By Jake Torres September 6, 2026

The Picture at September 6

The NL Wild Card race is beginning to resolve itself. Entering September 6, the Chicago Cubs sit in WC1 with a roughly half-game lead over the Phillies, who hold WC2. Both teams have built approximately five games of cushion over the true bubble, which means the top two spots are not quite locked in mathematically but are approaching that point. Neither team would need to play extraordinary September baseball to hold on.

The actual race — the only spot that's still genuinely competitive — is WC3. The Arizona Diamondbacks hold it entering today. The San Diego Padres are half a game behind them. That is the margin separating two franchises from the postseason and from watching from home. Half a game. Twenty-two left to play.

The Bubble

The D-backs and Padres are running side by side for the third Wild Card spot, and neither team can afford to look past individual games at this point. Every series matters. Every road trip is a potential turning point. The Padres have been within striking distance of Arizona all month without being able to overtake them. The D-backs have held on without putting the Padres away.

The Cardinals, entering Saturday at 71-72, are the other team watching this closely. They're playing at Colorado today against a Rockies team trying to avoid 100 losses — one of the more favorable matchups on anyone's September schedule. If the Cardinals can't pick up ground against the Rockies, the path gets very narrow. They've gone 1-2 since their walk-off win earlier in the week, and any illusion of a sustainable run is now measured in fractions of a game against teams that are also fighting for their lives.

What WC3 Means

The third Wild Card spot comes with a first-round matchup against the NL Central winner — almost certainly the Milwaukee Brewers, who lead the NL Central and have Misiorowski anchoring their rotation. That's not an impossible task, but it's not a favorable draw. Both the D-backs and the Padres have the offensive firepower to compete in a short series, and the Wild Card round is famously unpredictable.

What matters most in the next three weeks is simply staying in the conversation. Every team on the bubble has enough talent to surge at the end. What separates them is pitching depth, health, and the ability to take series from opponents who are also fighting for playoff spots.

What's Next

Track the Arizona-San Diego game-by-game differential over the next week — that's where September gets decided. The Cubs and Phillies will likely clinch their spots sometime in the final two weeks. WC3 is the story. If the Padres can flip the D-backs and take the spot, or if the Cardinals can run off seven or eight straight wins and re-enter the picture, this race gets genuinely compelling in the final days of the regular season. Right now, it's Arizona's to lose.
