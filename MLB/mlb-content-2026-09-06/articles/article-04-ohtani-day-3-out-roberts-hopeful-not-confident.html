Ohtani Has Now Missed Three Straight. Roberts Is Hopeful — Not Confident.

By Marcus Cole September 6, 2026

Three Games and No Timetable

Shohei Ohtani did not play Saturday against the Washington Nationals. That makes it three consecutive games missed, with no injured list designation and no timetable given for his return. Manager Dave Roberts was asked directly ahead of Saturday's game: "Hopeful. I wouldn't say confident, but hopeful."

That is not a reassuring answer for a franchise heading toward October with Ohtani as the centerpiece of everything they do offensively. "Hopeful but not confident" in early September, with multiple ailments and no return date in sight, is exactly the kind of answer that raises legitimate concern about whether he'll be fully functional — or even playing — when the playoffs begin.

The Ailments Are Multiple

The Dodgers have confirmed left knee discomfort, right bicep issues, and neck soreness as contributing factors in Ohtani's absence from the lineup. No single injury would be disqualifying in September for a player of his caliber — you play through things at this stage of the season. The fact that it's three separate ailments, combined with the fact that he's already missed three games, suggests the cumulative toll is significant.

Roberts' earlier comments during the stretch added more context: he had described the IL as "unlikely but not impossible" and a week-long absence as "certainly a possibility." Those quotes, taken with Saturday's "hopeful but not confident," paint a consistent picture of a team managing its most important player through a multi-system breakdown in the worst possible month to have one.

The Slump Before the Absence

Before he stopped playing, Ohtani was in the middle of one of the worst stretches of his career. From August 18 through his last game, he went 7-for-51 with 20 strikeouts — a .137 batting average over that span. That is not the Shohei Ohtani the Dodgers brought to Los Angeles. That is a player who, in retrospect, was likely already dealing with something significant before the team made the call to rest him.

The Dodgers are comfortable in the NL standings and do not need him to win regular-season games in September. But the difference between Ohtani at 100 percent in October and Ohtani managing through a left knee, a bicep, and a neck issue is the difference between the Dodgers being a dynasty and the Dodgers being a disappointment. Their margin for error in the postseason is not as wide as their regular-season record suggests.

What's Next

The Dodgers play through September 27 and have a comfortable enough position that they can afford to keep Ohtani out for another week if needed. The more important date is the start of the NLDS — the Dodgers would likely enter as the No. 1 or No. 2 seed in the NL, which means a first-round series beginning in early October. Whether Ohtani is fully healthy for that series is now the most significant health question in baseball. Watch for Roberts' pregame lineups and injury updates through the coming week — if Ohtani doesn't appear in the lineup by midweek, the concern will escalate further.
