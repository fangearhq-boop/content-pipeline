Yordan Alvarez Is Chasing a Triple Crown — and Every Lead Is Contested

By Ryan Calloway September 6, 2026

The Leads

Yordan Alvarez enters September 6 leading the American League in batting average (.317), home runs (37), and runs batted in (94). That's the Triple Crown — leading your league in all three major offensive categories in the same season. It has happened 17 times in major league history. The last AL player to do it was Miguel Cabrera in 2012. What Alvarez is attempting is that rare, and that difficult, and that close to actually happening.

But the leads are not comfortable. Chandler Simpson is five points back in batting average at .312. Junior Caminero of the Tampa Bay Rays has tied Alvarez at 37 home runs. Pete Alonso has tied him in RBI at 94. Every single lead is being contested by someone, and there are 22 games left to play.

The Home Run Race Is the Wild Card

Of the three leads, the home run tie is the most unstable. Caminero tied Alvarez for the AL lead on a home run off Robbie Ray, and neither player has added to their total since in confirmed reports. The AL HR race at 37 is itself inside a bigger MLB picture — Kyle Schwarber of the Phillies leads the entire sport at 40-plus home runs, though as a National League player he's competing for a different crown. In the AL, it's Alvarez and Caminero at 37, and 22 games of September power hitting to sort it out.

The batting average lead is actually Alvarez's most secure, though five points in late September can evaporate in a single bad week. Simpson hitting .312 is not going away. The RBI tie with Alonso is a function of both players being in high-run-scoring lineups that have continued to produce through the summer.

What It Would Mean

The Triple Crown is the sort of achievement that stands alone in a player's career, separate from awards and championships. It requires a specific kind of consistency that goes beyond any individual skill — power, contact, and run production all sustaining simultaneously for six months. Cabrera's 2012 Triple Crown is still talked about as one of the great individual seasons in modern baseball. Alvarez winning one in 2026 would put his name in the same sentence.

He is a generational offensive talent who has been one of the most productive hitters in baseball for four consecutive seasons. The Triple Crown would put a single-season stamp on a career that already has World Series credentials. It would also require 22 more games of leading all three categories simultaneously — which is very much not guaranteed.

What's Next

Watch the home run number first. If Caminero goes ahead of Alvarez, the pressure compounds immediately — Alvarez would need to answer, while also protecting his BA and RBI leads. The most likely path for Alvarez is to focus on what he controls: his at-bats, his approach, his plate discipline. That's what has gotten him to .317 and 37 home runs in the first place. September is no time to change it.
