The Cubs Got to Him First. Now Misiorowski Goes Back to Wrigley.

By Jake Torres September 6, 2026

What Happened on September 3

For 26 straight starts, Jakob Misiorowski had been untouchable. Three earned runs or fewer, every single time, from the first start of the season through the end of August. It was the kind of run that made comparisons to Dwight Gooden feel not just generous but accurate — the first pitcher in a long time to sustain that level of dominance for an entire half-season and then some.

Then the Cubs came to Miller Park on September 3, and in four innings of work they did something no team had managed all season: they got to him. Five earned runs. Four walks. Career highs in both categories. By the time the Brewers pulled him, the streak was done. ERA: 1.97. Still the best in baseball. But the aura of invincibility had its first crack.

The Extra Rest Decision

The Brewers' decision after the rough outing said something important about how they view him. Rather than putting him right back out there on his normal schedule, they gave him extra rest. The next start got pushed back. The message from the organization was clear: this is a 24-year-old pitcher who just had the first really difficult game of his professional career. Take a breath. Get right. Then go back out there.

That extra rest lands him in the Brewers-Cubs series at Wrigley Field, which runs September 7 through 9. His next start comes somewhere in that window — most likely Monday or Tuesday. Which means Jakob Misiorowski walks back into Wrigley Field and faces the exact same lineup that roughed him up, in the exact same park where that lineup is most comfortable.

Why This Start Matters More Than Any Other

Misiorowski's 14-5 record and 1.97 ERA make him the NL Cy Young frontrunner regardless of what happened September 3. One rough start doesn't undo six months of historic pitching. But the narrative has shifted. Before September 3, the story was whether anyone could touch him. After September 3, the question is what the Cubs saw, and whether they saw it again.

This start is the answer to that question. It's the most important start he's made all year — more important than the big shutouts, more important than the deep game in St. Louis, more important than the back-to-back dominant outings against the Cardinals in July. Because this one has context. The Cubs already have a blueprint. They know they can get him loose. Misiorowski knows they know. And October is three weeks away.

What's Next

Misiorowski takes the ball at Wrigley sometime between September 7 and 9. Watch his pitch selection and command through the first two innings — that's where the September 3 breakdown started, with walks and elevated pitch counts before he found any rhythm. If he comes out sharp and challenges hitters early, the narrative flips back. If the Cubs touch him again, the Cy Young conversation and the Brewers' October outlook both get a lot more complicated.
