Pete Crow-Armstrong Is One Home Run From Becoming the Seventh

By Marcus Cole September 6, 2026

The Number Is Six

Since 1988, exactly six players have hit 40 home runs and stolen 40 bases in the same season. Jose Canseco did it first, turning a physical freakishness that seemed impossible into a statistical fact. Barry Bonds joined in 1996, then Alex Rodriguez in 1998. Alfonso Soriano got there in 2006. Ronald Acuña Jr. did it in 2023. Shohei Ohtani is on the list. Six names. Six seasons. One hundred and thirty-plus years of baseball.

Pete Crow-Armstrong is sitting on 39 home runs and 33 stolen bases, and he has 22 games left to get there. He is, by any reasonable measure, the front-runner to become the seventh.

The Clock Is Ticking

Friday night against the Marlins, PCA went 1-for-3 with a walk and an RBI single. No home run. No stolen base. The Cubs won 6–5 to hold their position in NL Wild Card 1. PCA was efficient, professional, and exactly the kind of quiet game that doesn't change his place in history — not in either direction.

He added his 33rd stolen base Thursday, on a stolen base that was confirmed in the Cubs' 6–1 win over the Marlins. The September pace is there. The only outstanding item is the home run, and PCA knows it. He needs one more to join the club and seven more stolen bases, which his track record suggests is entirely achievable in the remaining schedule.

What 40-40 Actually Means

The 40-40 club is not just a milestone — it's a statement about a specific kind of baseball player. Power hitters get to 40 home runs. Speed players get to 40 stolen bases. The ability to do both in a single season requires an athleticism that only appears once or twice in a generation. Every member of the six-man club is considered one of the most gifted players of his era. Every one of them was a major force in shaping what that position could be.

PCA, at whatever age he is doing this, is making that argument at the highest level in the sport. The Cubs are in the playoffs. He's batting in a lineup that protects him and creates real opportunities to run. He's healthy. The conditions are right. All that's left is the execution.

What's Next

The Cubs play through September 27. Over the final 22 games, PCA has every reason to stay healthy and active — the Cubs aren't resting anyone with a Wild Card spot to protect. The combination of one more home run and seven more steals is not a tall order for a player with his skillset at this stage of the season. Watch the next Cubs home stand closely. History could happen any night.
