### STORY 1: PCA — Still One Swing Away

**Long-Form Post** [9:00 AM]
Pete Crow-Armstrong is one home run from baseball history.

Through last night's Cubs 6–5 win over the Marlins, PCA sits at 39 HR and 33 stolen bases. He didn't add to either category Friday — 1-for-3, a walk, an RBI single. But the bigger picture is this: 22 games remain, and he needs 1 HR and 7 stolen bases to join the most exclusive club in baseball.

Six players have ever hit 40 home runs and stolen 40 bases in the same season. Jose Canseco did it in 1988. Barry Bonds in 1996. Alex Rodriguez in 1998. Alfonso Soriano in 2006. Ronald Acuña Jr. in 2023. Shohei Ohtani. That's the full list — six names, 130+ years of baseball.

PCA has an above-average chance of being next. The Cubs are locked into the NL Wild Card, which means manager Craig Counsell has every reason to keep PCA healthy and in the lineup. He has 22 games to get it done.

That's actually very achievable — IF he heats up. He's had months-long hot streaks this season. One well-timed surge could take care of all seven stolen bases before the month is out. The one HR is the easier ask.

The question is when, not if, as long as he stays healthy.

### STORY 2: Misiorowski — Rematch at Wrigley

**Long-Form Post** [10:00 AM]
For 26 straight starts, Jakob Misiorowski was untouchable.

Three earned runs or fewer, every single time, from Opening Day through August. That's not a hot streak — that's a historically dominant stretch of starting pitching that had the entire baseball world watching.

Then the Cubs happened.

On September 3, the Cubs tagged Misiorowski for five earned runs in four innings — four walks, career-high in both categories. The 26-start streak ended. His ERA ticked up to 1.97.

And now, after the Brewers gave him an extra day of rest to collect himself, Misiorowski gets to walk back into Wrigley Field and face the same lineup that roughed him up. The Brewers-Cubs series runs Sept. 7–9, and his next start comes somewhere in that window.

The stakes are enormous. This is the most important start of his entire season — and possibly of his young career. The NL Cy Young Award is still his to lose, his ERA is still the best in baseball, and his team is holding onto the NL Central lead. But the Cubs exposed something. Whether that was a one-time outlier or the beginning of something else, September at Wrigley will help answer the question.

### STORY 3: Alvarez — Triple Crown Hanging by a Thread

**Long-Form Post** [12:00 PM]
The last time a player won the AL Triple Crown was 2012 — Miguel Cabrera, one of the most iconic individual seasons in baseball history.

Yordan Alvarez is chasing that same territory. And as of September 6, he's still in the lead for all three categories. But every lead is contested.

BA: .317 — Chandler Simpson is 5 points back at .312.
HR: 37 — Junior Caminero (Rays) is tied with him.
RBI: 94 — Pete Alonso (Mets) is tied with him.

Twenty-two games remain. Any one of those three leads could flip on a single night. The HR tie is the most unstable — Caminero is swinging hot and one night could put him ahead. The RBI battle with Alonso is a coin flip. Even the BA lead, the one that felt most secure, is now just five points.

What Alvarez is attempting is extraordinarily rare. It requires being the best hitter in your league across three completely different disciplines for 162 games. He's done that through 140. The last 22 games are where it either gets finished or falls apart.

Keep an eye on Caminero.

### STORY 4: Ohtani — Day 3, No Timetable

**Long-Form Post** [2:00 PM]
Shohei Ohtani did not play Saturday. That's three consecutive games now.

He's not on the injured list. But he's also not playing. Manager Dave Roberts was asked about his status ahead of the game and gave the most sobering answer of the September: "Hopeful. I wouldn't say confident, but hopeful."

The ailments are multiple — left knee, right bicep, and neck discomfort. No single injury would be alarming in isolation. Together, with no timetable given and three games already missed, it starts to feel like something more significant.

Here's the context that makes it worse: before he stopped playing, Ohtani was in the middle of one of the worst stretches of his career. He went 7-for-51 with 20 strikeouts since August 18 — a .137 average over that span. That's not Ohtani. That's someone who was already dealing with something.

The Dodgers are six weeks from October. They can survive the regular season without him — their lead is comfortable. But if he goes into the postseason nursing these ailments, or worse, if he ends up on the IL at all, their entire October calculus changes.

"Hopeful but not confident" is not the answer anyone in Dodger blue wanted to hear in early September.
