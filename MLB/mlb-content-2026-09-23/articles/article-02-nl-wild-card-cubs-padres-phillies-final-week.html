NL Wild Card Final Sprint: Cubs and Padres Tied, Phillies One Back — Six Games Remain

By Jake Torres | September 23, 2026

Three teams. Two tickets. Six games left. Welcome to the best race in baseball.

The National League Wild Card standings entering the final week of the 2026 regular season deliver exactly the kind of theater baseball was built for. The Chicago Cubs and San Diego Padres are deadlocked at 87-69. The Philadelphia Phillies sit one game behind at 86-70. When the dust settles in six days, two of these teams will be playing October baseball. One will be watching from home.

Where They Stand

The Cubs hold a critical edge: the head-to-head tiebreaker over both teams. If the standings end in a tie, Chicago advances. In the current two-spot scenario, the Cubs would be the No. 1 Wild Card seed and host the Phillies in a three-game Wild Card Series; the Padres would hold the No. 2 spot and travel to face the Braves.

But San Diego is making life uncomfortable. The Padres have gone 9-1 over their last 10 games — one of the hottest stretches in the majors. A team playing this kind of baseball in late September is genuinely dangerous, and they have the schedule in front of them to keep climbing.

The Phillies own their own tiebreaker advantage — over the Padres specifically. Philadelphia went a perfect 6-0 against San Diego in the 2026 regular season, which could be decisive if those two teams finish even.

The Star Power on Display

This isn't just a standings race — it's a showcase of some of the best players in the National League.

In Chicago, Pete Crow-Armstrong is chasing a 40-40 season — two stolen bases away from joining one of baseball's most exclusive clubs. The Cubs go as he goes.

In Philadelphia, Zack Wheeler is 8 strikeouts away from 2,000 career Ks, pitching every fifth day with playoff stakes on the line.

The Padres, meanwhile, have built their late run on collective execution — a team playing at its highest level precisely when it matters most.

What Happens to the Loser

There's no consolation prize here. Whichever team finishes third in this race goes home. Baseball's regular season doesn't offer mulligans. A team that was one game out of the playoffs will have to wait until 2027 to try again — and in a sport where rosters turn over, nothing is guaranteed.

That reality makes the next six days as meaningful as almost any regular-season games get.

What's Next

Watch the standings daily. Each game in this final week could shift the picture. The Cubs, Padres, and Phillies all play through Sunday. If the Padres finish hot, they could pull ahead. If the Cubs get a stumble, the Phillies are right there. The tiebreaker scenarios are layered, but the simplest path is straightforward: win more games than the other two teams.
