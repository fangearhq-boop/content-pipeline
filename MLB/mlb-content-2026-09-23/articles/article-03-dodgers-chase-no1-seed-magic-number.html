Dodgers Chase NL's No. 1 Seed With Magic Number of 3 — 14 Straight Postseasons on the Line

By Ryan Calloway | September 23, 2026

The Dodgers have already won. Now they want to win everything.

Los Angeles clinched the NL West title on September 17, defeating the Cincinnati Reds to end any suspense about their division. But there's a larger prize still within reach: the National League's No. 1 overall seed, which comes with a first-round bye and home-field advantage throughout the postseason. Their magic number over the Atlanta Braves is 3.

A September to Remember

The Dodgers are playing their best baseball of the year at exactly the right time. They carried a 10-game home winning streak into the final week of the regular season, and went 14-5 in September before the final week began — one of the hottest records in the majors during that stretch.

This is a team that has learned how to flip the switch in September, and they're doing it again.

Chasing the All-Time Record

The division title brought a significant piece of history: the Dodgers' 14th consecutive postseason appearance, tying the Atlanta Braves' MLB record. Five straight NL West titles. Fourteen straight trips to October.

For a franchise that has been defined by consistency over the past decade-plus, these numbers are a reminder of just how remarkable this run has been. Building a roster capable of competing for a championship every single year — through roster turnover, injuries, and the ebb and flow of a 162-game season — requires organizational depth that few teams can match.

What a No. 1 Seed Means

In the modern MLB postseason structure, a first-round bye is significant. While wild card teams play a three-game series just to advance to the Division Series, the No. 1 seed sits and waits — fully rested, at home, with extra preparation time. Against a deep Dodgers roster with Shohei Ohtani and Freddie Freeman leading the way, those advantages compound.

The magic number is 3. With the Dodgers' current form, that number is likely to reach zero before the weekend is over.

What's Next

The Dodgers play their final regular-season games through Sunday. If they secure the No. 1 seed, they'll rest and prepare for the NL Division Series while the wild card teams fight it out. October in Los Angeles is a tradition at this point — and this year's team looks capable of extending the run well into the postseason.
