Pete Crow-Armstrong Is 2 Steals Away From the 40-40 Club — And MLB History

By Marcus Cole | September 23, 2026

The number is simple: 2.

Two stolen bases. That's what stands between Pete Crow-Armstrong and one of the rarest individual achievements in the history of baseball. The Chicago Cubs center fielder enters the final week of the 2026 regular season with 40 home runs and 33 stolen bases. If he can add just two more steals before the final out, he will become the seventh player in MLB history to record a 40-40 season.

At 24 years old, he's doing this in the middle of a three-team wild card race.

What Makes This Historic

The 40-40 club is genuinely exclusive. Jose Canseco opened it in 1988. Since then, Barry Bonds, Alfonso Soriano, Alex Rodriguez, Carlos Beltran, and Ronald Acuña Jr. have joined. Six players in total — over nearly four decades of modern baseball. Crow-Armstrong would be No. 7.

But the record-breaking extends beyond that single milestone. With his 40th home run, Crow-Armstrong became the first Cubs player to hit 40 or more in a season since Derrek Lee hit 46 in 2005. He also became the first left-handed Cubs batter to reach 40 homers since Hall of Famer Billy Williams hit 42 in 1970. And his 40 home runs mark the second-highest single-season total by a Cubs player age 24 or younger — only Ernie Banks' 44 homers in 1955 rank higher.

These are not small details. These are franchise milestones attached to legends of the game.

The Context Makes It Better

Crow-Armstrong isn't doing this in a meaningless September. The Cubs sit at 87-69, tied with the San Diego Padres for the top NL Wild Card position with six games remaining and one game ahead of the Phillies. Every at-bat, every stolen base attempt, every swing carries playoff implications.

His 2026 slash stats reflect elite production: an average exit velocity of 90.7, a 49.1 hard-hit percentage, and a .402 wOBA. Crow-Armstrong is widely considered the NL MVP frontrunner, and nights like these make clear why.

The question is whether his legs will carry him two more bases before the regular season ends. Every Cubs fan — and every baseball fan watching history unfold — will have an eye on the basepaths this week.

What's Next

The Cubs play their final six games of the regular season, with every game carrying double weight: playoff seeding and Crow-Armstrong's chase. If he steals two bases and reaches 40, the Cubs will be sending a 40-40 club member into the postseason. That's the kind of storyline that defines a baseball season — and a career. Watch closely.
