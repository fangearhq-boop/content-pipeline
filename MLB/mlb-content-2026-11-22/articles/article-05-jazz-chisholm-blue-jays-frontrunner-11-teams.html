Jazz Chisholm's Market Heats Up: Blue Jays Frontrunner as 11 Teams Circle the Former Yankee

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | November 22, 2026

Jazz Chisholm Jr.'s free agency is now one of the most active situations on the market. MLB Network's Jon Morosi reported that 11 teams have engaged with Chisholm's representatives, with in-person meetings scheduled to begin this week. The Toronto Blue Jays are the frontrunner, according to Shi Davidi of Sportsnet, who described Toronto's interest as serious and their intentions as aggressive.

The New York Yankees, who employed Chisholm in 2026, are out of the running per multiple reports.

The Blue Jays' Vision

Toronto's interest in Chisholm is not simply about adding an offensive piece — it is about a specific defensive alignment. Davidi reported that the Blue Jays envision Chisholm playing second base alongside shortstop Ernie Clement, with Clement providing defensive versatility and super-utility coverage. That would give Toronto a dynamic, athletic infield capable of covering multiple positions with legitimate offensive production from both spots.

The Blue Jays have been active across the offseason, also pursuing outfield options and reportedly tracking other market developments. Adding Chisholm would represent a significant commitment to competing in 2027 and beyond.

What Chisholm Brings

Chisholm hit .231/.312/.399 in 2026 with 19 home runs and 41 stolen bases. The batting average is a concern — his contact quality has been inconsistent across his career — but the power-speed combination at the .399 slugging level is legitimate, and 41 steals put him among the fastest baserunners in the game. He also offers flexibility, having played both second base and center field at the major league level.

Critically, Chisholm comes without draft pick cost. He either declined or was not given a qualifying offer, which means any team that signs him retains its full complement of draft picks. That eliminates one of the most significant disincentives for smaller-market teams entering a bidding situation against larger markets.

The Clock Is Running

The CBA expires December 1. That is nine days from today. Any team that wants Chisholm before the lockout needs to close in the next week. The Blue Jays are reportedly motivated to move quickly. With 11 teams engaged, the competition is real — and teams that wait for the lockout to clear risk losing Chisholm to whoever moves decisively now.

What's Next

In-person meetings beginning this week should produce early reads on where Chisholm is leaning. If the Blue Jays are willing to offer the years and dollars that match Chisholm's market value, they should be able to close before December 1. If the market pushes the value above what Toronto has budgeted, one of the other 10 engaged teams may move ahead. Either way, this situation should resolve in the next nine days or get frozen until spring alongside everything else in baseball.
