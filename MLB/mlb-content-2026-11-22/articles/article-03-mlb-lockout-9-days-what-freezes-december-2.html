9 Days to the Freeze: What the December 2 MLB Lockout Means for Every Free Agent

By Ryan Calloway | Ballpark Banter | November 22, 2026

The collective bargaining agreement between Major League Baseball and the Major League Baseball Players Association expires at 11:59 PM Eastern on December 1. That is nine days from today. When it expires, owners are expected to impose a lockout at 12:01 AM on December 2 — and when that happens, every transaction in baseball stops.

No signings. No trades. No waiver claims. No arbitration negotiations. No team-player communication. The hot stove shuts off completely.

What Happens to the Big Free Agents

Tarik Skubal has a $400 million offer from the Mets but is not expected to sign before December 1. His contract — whatever it ends up being — goes into the lockout freeze. The Mets' offer will still be there when the lights come back on, but no new negotiations happen until a new CBA is in place.

Jazz Chisholm Jr. has 11 teams engaged and in-person meetings starting this week. If he wants to sign before the freeze — and avoid potentially waiting months for his market to restart — nine days is the window. The Blue Jays, reportedly his leading suitor, need to close quickly.

Randy Arozarena's market has been stalled by a $100 million-plus asking price that teams are balking at. He can either take a deal in the next nine days or wait out the lockout and hope the spring market is kinder. History suggests the spring market rarely improves a player's negotiating leverage.

Teruaki Sato is the one exception to the lockout freeze. His posting agreement with Hanshin is an NPB arrangement that sits outside the CBA framework, meaning teams can sign him even during a lockout. His window, however, closes December 16 regardless — so the urgency is similar.

What the Owners and Players Are Fighting Over

The core dispute is a salary cap. MLB has proposed a payroll ceiling of $258.2 million and a floor of $180.2 million, with implementation delayed to the 2028 season. The MLBPA has refused to accept any form of salary cap. Owners have also proposed limits on contract length. The players' union views both as fundamental restrictions on earning power, and both sides have signaled no willingness to move.

Commissioner Rob Manfred has said the lockout decision will be made at the "last possible moment." Multiple analysts have described a lockout as near-certain in the absence of a dramatic late breakthrough.

What's Next

The 2021 lockout lasted 99 days and cost the opening days of the regular season, though a full 162-game schedule was eventually played. A similar timeline in 2026-27 would see a new CBA reached around March 10, pushing spring training back and potentially threatening the beginning of the 2027 season. Nine days remain. Fans who want to watch free agency in real time should pay close attention this week — it may be the last action for months.
