Giants Hot Stove: Logan Webb Available and Freddy Peralta in Play Before the Freeze

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | November 22, 2026

San Francisco is shaping up as one of the most active organizations in the final nine days before the lockout, with two major storylines developing simultaneously. Per FanSided's Robert Murray, the Giants are open to listening to trade offers for Logan Webb. At the same time, San Francisco is pursuing free agent starter Freddy Peralta, a move that would reshape the organization's rotation picture heading into 2027.

The Logan Webb Situation

Webb is under a five-year, $90 million contract extension that runs through the 2028 season. He is controllable, affordable, and one of the better right-handers in the National League when healthy. Those qualities make him exactly the kind of asset that contending teams want to acquire before a lockout freezes the trade market.

The Chicago Cubs have reportedly expressed interest in Webb. Chicago needs a frontline starter and has the prospect depth to put together a compelling package. Murray's report that the Giants are "open to listening" is not the same as the Giants being motivated sellers — it means they would hear out what teams are willing to offer before deciding. No trade has been completed, and there are conflicting signals from within the organization about how genuinely available Webb is.

The Freddy Peralta Angle

Peralta is an intriguing free agent target. The right-hander was with the Milwaukee Brewers before a midseason trade to Tampa Bay last season. Because that trade happened mid-season, he was ineligible to receive a qualifying offer from Tampa Bay — meaning any team that signs him pays no draft pick compensation. For a team like San Francisco trying to build rotation depth without sacrificing draft capital, that matters.

If the Giants sign Peralta, he would slot behind Webb and starter Landen Roupp as the No. 3 option in the rotation. That would leave San Francisco with one remaining rotation spot to fill, either through a trade, another free agent signing, or a promotion from the system.

The Internal Logic

Here is the scenario that makes both moves make sense simultaneously: if the Giants sign Peralta, their rotation becomes deeper and more resilient, which makes trading Webb feasible. San Francisco would be moving from a situation where they need Webb to stay put to a situation where they can absorb his departure. A Webb trade for a package of young talent — combined with Peralta as the new ace — would give the organization flexibility and a refreshed prospect pipeline.

None of this is confirmed. It is organizational logic based on what Murray's report and the Peralta pursuit suggest when read together. The Giants have nine days to decide before the lockout makes all of this moot until spring.

What's Next

If the Giants sign Peralta before December 1, watch for the Webb trade conversation to accelerate immediately. If they don't sign Peralta, the Webb trade talk likely stalls — San Francisco needs him too much to deal him without rotation replacement. The Cubs and other interested parties are watching the same clock. Nine days is enough time to close deals. It is not enough time to hesitate indefinitely.
