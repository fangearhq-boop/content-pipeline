Mets Put $400 Million Offer on Table for Skubal — But the Lockout Stands in the Way

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | November 22, 2026

The New York Mets have made a formal contract offer to Tarik Skubal: nine years, nearly $400 million, according to multiple reports including MLB Network's Jim Duquette. The offer would be the richest pitching contract in baseball history, surpassing Yoshinobu Yamamoto's deal with the Dodgers at $325 million over 12 years — an average annual value of approximately $27 million. The Mets' offer to Skubal would work out to roughly $44 million per year.

There is one significant problem: Skubal is not expected to sign before the CBA expires on December 1. With a lockout expected to begin December 2, the negotiations — record money included — will get frozen alongside every other transaction in baseball.

The Mets' Case

Skubal is 29 years old, a two-time American League Cy Young Award winner, and entering what should be the peak years of an elite starting pitcher's career. The Mets have spent aggressively on pitching in recent offseasons, and adding Skubal would give them arguably the best rotation in the National League.

ESPN's Buster Olney has gone on record saying he believes Skubal ends up in New York. The Mets were described as the "industry favorites" following a California meeting that multiple sources said went well. With the Dodgers trading for Royce Lewis on the same day the Mets' offer was reported — a move that suggests Los Angeles is redirecting its offseason resources — the field around Skubal appears to be narrowing.

Who's Still In

The Blue Jays remain engaged. Toronto has been in the discussion throughout Skubal's free agency, though they appear to be competing at a price point that makes matching a $400 million Mets offer difficult. The Padres were also reported to be in the mix earlier in the process, though their position in the final stages is less certain.

What's clear is that the Mets have the resources and the stated commitment to outbid the field. The $44 million average annual value represents a step-change from previous records. If Skubal's priority is maximizing contract value, New York has made the strongest case.

What's Next

The parties will continue negotiations, but Skubal is not expected to sign before December 1. Once the CBA expires and the lockout begins, all discussions are frozen — no new offers, no contract signings, no movement of any kind until a new collective bargaining agreement is reached. The last lockout lasted 99 days. If a similar timeline plays out, Skubal's signing may not come until early spring 2027. The record money will still be on the table when the lights come back on. The Mets appear willing to wait.
