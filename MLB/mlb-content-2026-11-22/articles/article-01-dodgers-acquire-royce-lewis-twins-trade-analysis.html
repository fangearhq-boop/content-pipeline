Dodgers Acquire Royce Lewis From Twins: What the Deal Means for Both Clubs

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | November 22, 2026

The Los Angeles Dodgers traded for Royce Lewis on November 20, acquiring the former No. 1 overall pick from the Minnesota Twins in exchange for pitcher Bobby Miller and 19-year-old catching prospect Francisco Espinoza. Lewis, who is under team control through the 2028 season, hit .227/.287/.380 with 15 home runs and 16 stolen bases in a career-high 125 games in 2026.

Minnesota also non-tendered outfielder Trevor Larnach and starter Bailey Ober on the same day. Combined with the Lewis trade, the Twins cut approximately $18.3 million in payroll in one afternoon. The message from Minnesota's front office is clear: a rebuild is underway.

What the Dodgers Get

Los Angeles needed a right-handed infield bat. With longtime utility piece Miguel Rojas retired, the Dodgers were thin at the infield depth level heading into 2027. Lewis can play first base, second base, and cover multiple positions — the kind of versatile, right-handed threat that fits naturally into a Dodgers lineup that already leans left-handed at the top.

At 25 years old and entering his second year of arbitration, Lewis is controllable and affordable. His 2026 season was his healthiest, appearing in 125 games for the first time in his career after years of ACL and injury interruptions. The power-speed combination — 15 home runs and 16 stolen bases — speaks to a player who could develop into a legitimate middle-of-the-order contributor if the contact issues improve.

The cost is reasonable. Bobby Miller is a former top Dodgers pitching prospect who recently transitioned to the bullpen. Espinoza is a teenage catcher with upside but years away. Los Angeles gave up organizational depth, not impact talent.

The Skubal Subtext

The Dodgers were reported to be in the mix for Tarik Skubal's free agency. The same live update that announced the Lewis trade included news that the Mets had made a formal offer to Skubal — nine years, nearly $400 million. The Dodgers acquiring Lewis instead of staying aggressively at the Skubal table suggests Los Angeles may be comfortable letting the two-time Cy Young winner go to New York.

That interpretation isn't certain — the Dodgers could still re-enter the Skubal market. But the resource allocation signal is real. Spending on a controllable infielder and trading a former starter the same week the Skubal offer was formalized tells a story about where LA's offseason priorities lie.

What's Next

Lewis will join a Dodger infield that includes Freddie Freeman and Max Muncy as the primary options at first and third. His role projects as a utility starter and right-handed bat off the bench who can step in at multiple positions. Minnesota, meanwhile, begins life after Larnach, Ober, and Lewis with a younger, cheaper roster — and a prospect haul that could anchor their next competitive window. The CBA expires in nine days. Any further roster movement before then will happen quickly.
