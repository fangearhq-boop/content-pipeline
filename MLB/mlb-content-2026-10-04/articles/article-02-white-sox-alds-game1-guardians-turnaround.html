White Sox Blank Guardians 3-0 in ALDS Game 1: From 121 Losses to October Wins

By Jake Torres | October 4, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Two years ago, the Chicago White Sox lost 121 games. It was the most losses in a single season in the history of Major League Baseball, the low point of a franchise era defined by consecutive years of rebuilding, bad luck, and on-field failure. No team in baseball history had lost 100 or more games in consecutive seasons and then made the postseason. The White Sox had done it three times in a row.

On Saturday night, they blanked the Cleveland Guardians 3-0 in ALDS Game 1.

The 2026 Chicago White Sox are winning playoff games.

The Numbers Behind the Turnaround

The transformation that produced this result did not happen overnight, but it happened faster than almost anyone outside the organization predicted. Chicago clinched their playoff spot on September 24 with a win over the Kansas City Royals at Kauffman Stadium — the same organization that had been fighting for the same playoff position just years earlier.

Seven of the nine players in Chicago's starting lineup have less than two years of Major League experience. This is not a veteran squad leaning on postseason-tested players — it is a young roster that was assembled with a vision of what this organization could become, and that vision is now materializing in October.

Munetaka Murakami, who has been central to the White Sox's surge, represents the kind of high-upside acquisition that defines successful rebuilds: international talent, immediate impact, and the ability to carry a lineup when the young core needs carrying.

Why This Result Matters

Winning Game 1 on the road is one of the most statistically significant advantages a team can establish in a best-of-five series. The White Sox did not just take Game 1 — they shut out one of the American League's most consistent run-producing lineups. Cleveland entered this series as the team that the division-champion White Sox had been battling all season. The Guardians are not a soft opening-round opponent.

A 3-0 shutout in a playoff game is a statement. It says your pitching is ready. It says your defense is sharp. It says the moment is not too big for a roster that, statistically speaking, has barely existed long enough to form collective memories.

What's Next

The ALDS continues Sunday when the Guardians host the White Sox in Game 2. Cleveland's back will be against the wall at home, in front of their fans, needing to respond or fall into a 2-0 hole that few teams escape.

If the White Sox win this series, it will rank among the most remarkable stories in modern baseball history — not just as a turnaround, but as validation that the process of building from the bottom up, as painful and public as it was, can produce exactly what the organization promised it would. They are no longer a rebuild. They are a contender. Saturday night, they proved it.
