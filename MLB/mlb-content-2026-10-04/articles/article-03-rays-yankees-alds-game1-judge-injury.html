Rays Edge Yankees 1-0 in ALDS Game 1 as Judge Remains Sidelined

By Ryan Calloway | October 4, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The Tampa Bay Rays defeated the New York Yankees 1-0 on Saturday in ALDS Game 1, a result that was shaped as much by who was absent from New York's lineup as by what Tampa Bay did on the field. Aaron Judge — the Yankees' captain, franchise cornerstone, and the player most responsible for New York reaching this round — was not available.

One run from Tampa Bay's lineup was all it took. Their pitching staff, as it so often does, made it look like one run was always going to be plenty.

Judge's Difficult 2026

The timeline of Aaron Judge's season is worth understanding in full, because it explains the weight of his absence. Judge broke a rib making a diving catch in late April — an injury that, for most players, would end a season without controversy. He tried to play through it before being placed on the injured list on May 31. He did not return until September 8, missing more than three months of the regular season.

His return was brief but meaningful: Judge gave New York a window of what this postseason could look like if he was healthy and available. Then came the calf strain. Yankees manager Aaron Boone confirmed before the Wild Card round that Judge would not be available for those games. His status for the ALDS has remained uncertain, with the organization declining to rule him out but unable to promise anything either.

For the Yankees, this is baseball's cruelest timing — a superstar who spent months fighting back into shape, finally returning, only to face another setback when it matters most.

What the Rays Did

Tampa Bay's approach to this series is exactly what you would expect from an organization that has made the postseason consistently with a fraction of the resources of large-market clubs. They pitch, they defend, they take advantage of every mistake, and they do not make many themselves. One run was enough on Saturday because their pitching staff gave up nothing.

The Rays hold a 1-0 ALDS lead heading into Game 2 in New York. The Yankees will have their home crowd behind them on Sunday, but they will also need their lineup to produce against a staff that showed Saturday it can hold an opposing offense scoreless for nine innings in October.

What's Next

Game 2 of the ALDS is Sunday evening in New York. The Yankees' most pressing question — whether Judge can be activated and whether his body will allow him to play — hangs over everything. A New York win to even the series at 1-1 would reset the narrative and bring Judge's availability back into focus. Another Rays win would put New York on the edge of elimination with or without their captain.

The Rays have been here before. The Yankees have been here before too, but never quite like this — never with this much uncertainty surrounding their most important player at the most important moment. Game 2 will tell us a great deal about what this series is going to be.
