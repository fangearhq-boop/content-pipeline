Four Days to the Deadline — And Tarik Skubal Might Not Be Going Anywhere

By Marcus Cole

July 30, 2026

When the August 3 MLB Trade Deadline entered the summer calendar, it came with an assumed headline: Tarik Skubal to the Dodgers. The Tigers' left-handed ace was the most coveted trade target in the sport, with Los Angeles viewed as the overwhelming frontrunner and Jeff Passan pegging the trade probability at 60 percent. That deal was supposed to define the 2026 deadline.

Four days out from that deadline, a different story is emerging. Among rival executives, a growing sense has taken hold that Skubal stays in Detroit. And the Tigers' July has given general manager Chris Ilitch every reason to hold.

Why the Tigers Are Holding

Detroit has gone 10-4 in July and 25-15 since June 1, clawing their way to within 4.5 games of a Wild Card spot. That's not a team staging a fire sale. That's a team that sees a window — and Skubal, under team control and pitching like a Cy Young candidate in his own right, is the reason that window exists.

The Dodgers remain the most aggressive suitor. They've reportedly dangled prospects Justin Wrobleski, Emmet Sheehan, and River Ryan in conversations — a package that would represent a significant haul. Baltimore, Tampa Bay, the Brewers, and the Braves have all been linked to discussions. But for Detroit to move Skubal, the return would need to justify blowing up a contending team's timeline — and right now, the math doesn't clearly work in favor of a trade.

Mason Miller: The Other Name Not Moving

The other major name on the board looks similarly unlikely to move. Mason Miller, San Diego's closer, is posting one of the most dominant reliever seasons in recent memory: a 0.84 ERA, 26 saves (the NL-best), 79 strikeouts in 42.2 innings, and a 2.5 bWAR that would be elite for a starter. He is under team control through 2029.

The New York Yankees made their interest explicit. Reports tied them to an offer of prospects Spencer Jones and Jasson Dominguez — a significant package. The Padres said no. Per Yahoo Sports, Miller is "likely" staying in San Diego, and the Padres' reluctance makes sense: you don't trade a generational closer with four years of control unless someone makes a truly unmatchable offer.

The Ohtani Factor

The other thread running through every Dodgers conversation is Shohei Ohtani, who has not pitched since July 3 due to a left knee issue compounded by right biceps discomfort. No timeline has been given for his return to pitching. The Dodgers' urgency to acquire starting pitching — Skubal being the dream target — is real, and Ohtani's absence makes it more acute. But urgency doesn't automatically close deals if the selling team's asking price doesn't move.

What to Watch Over Four Days

Smaller deals are always possible — the Max Scherzer, Clay Holmes, and Luis Arraez names remain in circulation. A mid-level acquisition by the Dodgers wouldn't be surprising. But the blockbuster Skubal-to-LA trade that everyone expected to define this deadline? It may not happen.

Four days. Every hour brings the deadline closer. And right now, the biggest story might be what doesn't happen.

What's Next

The deadline is August 3 at 6 PM ET. Any trade completed after that moment will be vetoed. Follow MLB.com and the usual reporting corps for real-time updates — by August 3 morning, the picture should be nearly complete. If Skubal stays, the Tigers' Wild Card push becomes the story of August. If he moves, the Dodgers become an even scarier October proposition.
