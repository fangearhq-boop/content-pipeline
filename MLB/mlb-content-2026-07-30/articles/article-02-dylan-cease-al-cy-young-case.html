Dylan Cease Is Pitching Like the Best Arm in the American League — And the AL Cy Young Case Is Building

By Jake Torres

July 30, 2026

In a recent start at Fenway Park, Dylan Cease did something that starters simply do not do anymore: he went out and finished a game. Not just any game — he threw a complete game shutout against the Boston Red Sox, allowing one hit, striking out 12 batters, and throwing 120 pitches in a 6-0 Toronto win. It was the kind of performance that reminds you why complete games still matter as a measuring stick, even in 2026.

That outing is the punctuation mark on what is becoming one of the more compelling pitching seasons in the American League. Dylan Cease, the Blue Jays' right-handed ace, is in the middle of a Cy Young campaign that his peers are starting to take very seriously.

The Numbers

Cease's 2026 season: a 2.56 ERA and 167 strikeouts, with consistency that has made him one of the most reliable starters in baseball. The complete game shutout over Boston — 1 hit, 12 K, six innings of run prevention before Toronto even needed a bullpen arm — is the headline start of a rotation performance that has been building all summer.

Before the CG shutout, Cease threw eight innings against the San Francisco Giants, punching out 11. He has established himself as a pitcher who doesn't just miss bats — he dominates extended sequences of innings at a time. For a Blue Jays team with legitimate playoff ambitions, Cease is the reason they feel confident about a deep October run.

The Cy Young Race

The 2026 American League Cy Young Award is a two-horse race. On one side is Cease. On the other is Cam Schlittler of the New York Yankees, who has made his own compelling case across a strong second half. Bleacher Nation's July tracker put Cease's odds at +145 — making him the narrow frontrunner, but leaving plenty of room for Schlittler to close.

Last Word on Baseball's July 29 piece called Cease's 2026 a "Dominant Cy Young-Caliber Season," and NBC Sports' awards tracker noted he was "heating up" in the race. The conversation around Cease isn't just "he's been good" — it's "this is genuinely historic pitching and the award is his to lose."

The difference-maker between him and Schlittler may come down to the final six weeks. Cease's next several starts will either seal the award or open the door for the Yankees' starter to make a late push.

What Toronto Is Getting

For the Blue Jays, Cease represents something the franchise hasn't had in years — a genuine No. 1 starter who is performing like an ace at a moment when it matters most. Toronto's playoff hopes run through this rotation, and with Cease leading the charge, those hopes feel very real.

He arrived in Toronto with the expectation that he would be exactly this — an elite striker at the top of a competitive rotation. He has delivered. And if the second half maintains this trajectory, there's an award-caliber season at the end of it.

What's Next

Watch Cease's next start carefully. His rotation spot should line him up for mid-August, when he'll have another chance to either extend his Cy Young lead or allow Schlittler to chip away. The award will almost certainly not be decided until September. But the complete game shutout against Boston was the kind of start that shifts momentum in an award race — and Cease just threw one.
