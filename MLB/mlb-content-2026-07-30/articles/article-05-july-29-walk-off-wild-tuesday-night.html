Three Walk-Offs, One Wild Tuesday: What July 29 Looked Like Across MLB

By Jake Torres

July 30, 2026

Every so often, baseball delivers a night where the drama isn't contained to a single game — it sprawls across three time zones and keeps fans from coast to coast on edge until the final out. Tuesday, July 29 was one of those nights. Three games, three dramatic finishes, each with its own narrative weight headed into the August 3 deadline.

Baltimore 10, Detroit 9 — 12 Innings

The Tigers needed a win to stay alive in the Wild Card race. They pushed the Orioles to 12 innings in a game that featured back-and-forth scoring through the night — and eventually came out on the wrong side of a 10-9 loss.

The loss keeps the pressure on Detroit's front office in both directions. On one hand, the Tigers being competitive enough to push Baltimore to 12 innings suggests they're a real team. On the other hand, losing that game in extras is precisely the kind of result that might convince the front office to hold Skubal — or that might finally convince them to deal him. Four days of deadline calculus just got more complicated.

St. Louis 3, Chicago Cubs 2 — 10 Innings

Ivan Herrera's 2-run double in the 10th inning sent Busch Stadium home happy after a tightly contested game in which the Cardinals clawed back from a deficit to stun the Cubs. It was the kind of walk-off that changes the feeling of an entire series — and the Cardinals' win keeps them within range in the NL Central while Chicago takes a step back.

For the Cubs, who have been fighting to stay relevant in the Wild Card picture, a loss in extras to a division rival always stings. They'll need a bounce-back Wednesday to keep their momentum intact.

Miami 8, Philadelphia 6

The Marlins had no business winning this game — and then Griffin Conine walked off the bench in the 8th inning and hit a pinch-hit go-ahead home run to change the conversation completely. Miami finished with an 8-6 win that dealt the Phillies a frustrating setback.

For Philadelphia, the loss is particularly noticeable in the context of Kyle Schwarber's historically significant season. The Phillies need to win games to make an October run worthy of the power performance at the top of their lineup. A loss to Miami via a pinch-hit home run is the kind of game that should not happen to a playoff contender — and it did.

What's Next

Wednesday's games will tell us whether Tuesday's drama carried over or whether the teams shook it off. With the August 3 deadline approaching, every game has playoff-race implications that are more direct than usual. The Tigers need wins to make the "don't trade Skubal" argument stronger. The Cubs need to rebound to stay in the Wild Card picture. And the Phillies need their lineup to start finishing games rather than giving them away. July 29 was a wild night. The next four days will be wilder.
