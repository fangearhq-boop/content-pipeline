Kyle Schwarber Is on Pace for 72 Home Runs. Barry Bonds Hit 73. Let That Sink In.

By Marcus Cole

July 30, 2026

The most famous offensive record in baseball history is 73. Barry Bonds hit 73 home runs in 2001, and in the quarter-century since, nobody has come within 12 of that number. Aaron Judge hit 62 in 2022, the closest anyone has come. The 60 HR club is the most exclusive in the sport.

As of July 18, Kyle Schwarber had 33 home runs. At his current pace, he will finish the season with 72.

One fewer than the record.

The Case Isn't Projection — It's Performance

What makes Schwarber's pace notable isn't just the pace number. It's how he got here. He was the first player in MLB to reach 20 home runs in 2026. The first to 25, which he hit against the Miami Marlins on June 16. The first to 30, which he accomplished in just 84 games. At every milestone, he arrived before anyone else.

Six consecutive seasons of 30 or more home runs — the longest active streak in baseball. Last year, he led the NL with 56 HR. This year, he's already being asked whether he can reach 60. The answer, per his current trajectory, is not just yes — it's "maybe even 70."

His full mid-July slash line: .255 batting average, .935 OPS, 61 RBI. He is not chasing home runs at the expense of everything else. He is simply hitting with a frequency and force that hasn't been seen in more than two decades.

The 60 HR Club

Only five players in baseball history have hit 60 or more home runs in a season:

  • Barry Bonds: 73 (2001)
  • Mark McGwire: 70 (1998)
  • Sammy Sosa: 66 (1998)
  • Aaron Judge: 62 (2022)
  • Roger Maris: 61 (1961)

That's the club Schwarber is being projected into. ESPN ran a feature this summer asking directly: "Will Phillies' Kyle Schwarber join the 60 home run club?" Based on his pace, the answer is almost certainly yes. The question now is how far above 60 he goes — and whether 73 is actually within reach.

Age 35, Doing This

The context that makes Schwarber's 2026 even more extraordinary is his age. He is 35 years old. Most power hitters decline meaningfully by 33 or 34 as bat speed degrades. Schwarber is accelerating. He is not just maintaining power at 35 — he is posting potentially the best power season of his career at an age when most sluggers have already peaked and are managing the slow decline.

There is no precedent for what he is doing. The nearest comparison in recent memory is Aaron Judge at 30 in 2022, but Schwarber is doing it five years older. It defies what we understand about aging curves in baseball.

What's Next

Schwarber's home run total updates with every Phillies game. The numbers to watch: 40 HR (likely by mid-August at this pace), 50 HR, and then the unprecedented territory of 60+. If he stays healthy, every milestone from here forward becomes a news event. The most historic power season in over two decades may be happening right in front of us — and we should be paying closer attention.
