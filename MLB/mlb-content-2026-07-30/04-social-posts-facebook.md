### STORY 4: Schwarber HR Chase

**FB Long-Form — Post Time: 3:00 PM ET**

🤯 **KYLE SCHWARBER IS ON PACE FOR 72 HOME RUNS. LET THAT SINK IN.**

Barry Bonds hit 73 home runs in 2001. It is the most famous offensive record in baseball. It has stood for 25 years.

Kyle Schwarber — 35 years old, left-handed, playing for the Philadelphia Phillies — is on pace to hit **72 home runs** this season.

He reached 33 HR as of July 18. He was the first player in MLB to reach 20 HR, 25 HR, and 30 HR in 2026 — hitting the 30-HR mark in just **84 games**. Six consecutive seasons of 30+ home runs, the longest active streak in baseball. Current slash line: **.255 batting average, .935 OPS, 61 RBI** at mid-July.

The 60 HR club currently has five members:
- Barry Bonds: 73 (2001)
- Mark McGwire: 70 (1998)
- Sammy Sosa: 66 (1998)
- Roger Maris: 61 (1961)
- Aaron Judge: 62 (2022)

ESPN is already asking "Will Kyle Schwarber join the 60 home run club?" — and that conversation is based on math that works out. At his current pace, he doesn't just flirt with 60. He flirts with the greatest single-season home run total ever recorded.

We may be watching the most historically significant power season in over two decades happen right now. And not enough people are talking about it.
