Yordan Alvarez Is 17 Games From Making AL History

By Ryan Calloway | September 9, 2026

Houston Astros

The last time an American League player won the Triple Crown, Barack Obama was in his first term, Justin Verlander was 29 years old, and Miguel Cabrera finished his season at .330 with 44 home runs and 139 RBI. That was 2012.

Yordan Alvarez leads the AL in batting average, home runs, and RBI entering September 9 — with approximately 17 games remaining.

Leading All Three

The numbers aren't dominant in the way Cabrera's were — nothing about winning the Triple Crown is ever comfortable at this stage of the season. Alvarez's batting average sits at approximately .315, roughly 5 points ahead of his nearest competition. His home run total of approximately 38 is tied with a hitter from Tampa Bay. His RBI total of approximately 95 is shared with a Baltimore first baseman.

That's the Triple Crown at its most nerve-wracking: leading in three categories simultaneously while three different opponents are close enough to take any one of them in a good week. Alvarez has to outperform a different player in each race, for every game left, to finish with all three.

The last AL player before Cabrera to do it was Carl Yastrzemski in 1967. More than a half-century passed between those two. If Alvarez holds on, he'll be the third AL player in 59 years to accomplish it.

What Makes It So Hard

The Triple Crown is harder to win than any individual offensive title because it requires excellence across three unrelated skills. Batting average is a contact and on-base skill. Home runs require power and plate discipline. RBI is a function of power combined with run-producing situations that aren't fully under a hitter's control.

Players who lead in one category often don't lead in the others. The list of players who have led in all three simultaneously for a full season and not won the Triple Crown because a rival surged in the final weeks is longer than the list of Triple Crown winners. Alvarez has done the hardest part — maintaining all three leads into September. The question is whether he can maintain them for 17 more games.

What's Next

Every Alvarez at-bat from here carries the weight of 14 years of history. His batting average lead is thin enough that a cold stretch could cost it. His home run and RBI leads are shared, meaning a rival hitting a few more while he goes quiet could tip either category.

The Astros' remaining schedule and Alvarez's health are the two factors that matter most. If he stays in the lineup and produces at his season average, the probability is real. The last time a player led all three with 17 games left, he won.

That was Cabrera in 2012. The AL has been waiting since.
