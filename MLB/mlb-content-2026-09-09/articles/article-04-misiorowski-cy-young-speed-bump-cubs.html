Misiorowski's Cy Young Case Just Hit a Speed Bump — Twice, Against the Cubs

By Marcus Cole | September 9, 2026

Milwaukee Brewers

Tobias Misiorowski came into September as the clearest NL Cy Young frontrunner in baseball. He had a 14-5 record, an ERA sitting just below 2.00, and a streak of 26 consecutive quality starts that ran from early April through the first week of September. His case was straightforward.

Then came the Cubs. Twice.

Two Bad Outings, One Pattern

On September 2 at Wrigley Field, Misiorowski allowed 5 earned runs in 4 innings — his first non-quality start of the season after 26 straight. The explanation could have been a road trip, a bad day, an afternoon game at a hitter's park. Those things happen to good pitchers.

On September 8 at American Family Field — his home park, in the best possible conditions — the Cubs came to Milwaukee and Misiorowski had what his own team described as the worst start of his season.

One rough outing is a data point. Two consecutive rough outings against the same opponent is a pattern. The Cubs have identified something, and the timing is poor for a pitcher who needed clean September appearances to lock up the award.

The Good News

The Brewers won both games. Milwaukee sits at 89-56, one of the best records in baseball, and their offense and bullpen have enough depth behind Misiorowski that his rough nights haven't cost them. That's a credit to the team he's on — and it also means his Cy Young case, while complicated, isn't destroyed.

The 14-5 record and sub-2.00 ERA don't evaporate because of two bad starts. The voters who decide the Cy Young award have a full season to evaluate. Two rough outings in the final weeks can shift the narrative, but they can't erase what came before them.

What they can do is raise questions that didn't exist a week ago.

The October Implication

The more interesting angle isn't the Cy Young — it's October. The Cubs are in first place in the NL Wild Card race. If both teams reach the playoffs, and if they end up in the same bracket, the question of whether Chicago has solved Misiorowski becomes a legitimate scouting conversation.

Two outings is a small sample. But teams notice when a pitcher struggles against them in consecutive appearances, especially in September. If the Cubs and Brewers meet in October, the Misiorowski matchup will be one of the most-discussed in the series.

What's Next

Misiorowski has approximately 4-5 starts remaining in the regular season. If he pitches like he did for the first 145 games, the Cy Young is his. If the recent pattern against Chicago represents something more systematic — a sequencing issue, a pitch he's tipping, a vulnerability in his arsenal — the award race and the postseason implications both shift.

The next start will be the most-watched appearance of his season.
