Seven From History: Pete Crow-Armstrong and the 40-40 Club

By Jake Torres | September 9, 2026

Chicago Cubs

Six players in the history of Major League Baseball have hit 40 home runs and stolen 40 bases in the same season. José Canseco in 1988. Barry Bonds in 1996. Alex Rodriguez in 1998. Alfonso Soriano in 2006. Ronald Acuña Jr. in 2023. Shohei Ohtani in 2023.

Pete Crow-Armstrong enters September 9 with 40 home runs and 33 stolen bases.

He needs 7 more steals in approximately 17 remaining games. The math is real. The history is on the line.

The Pace Question

PCA's 33 stolen bases over roughly 145 games works out to a pace that makes 40 achievable without requiring anything extraordinary. He doesn't need to steal two bases per game. He needs to stay healthy, keep getting on base at the rate he has been, and run when the moment presents itself.

What makes the 40-40 club historically rare isn't that the players who belong to it were obscure talents. Canseco, Bonds, Rodriguez, and Ohtani are among the most celebrated players in the sport's history. The rarity comes from the combination itself — the power that produces 40 home runs and the foot speed and baserunning instinct that produces 40 steals require different physical tools operating at an elite level simultaneously.

PCA, at 24, appears to have both.

The Cubs Dimension

The milestone doesn't exist in a vacuum. When PCA hit his 40th home run, he also became the first Cub to reach that total since Derrek Lee did it in 2005 — and the youngest player in Cubs franchise history to hit 40 in a season. Two franchise records came attached to the same swing.

The broader context matters too. The Cubs are 81-64 and sitting in the first NL Wild Card spot. PCA's historic individual season is happening inside a team that's heading somewhere. The NL MVP race has him as the -1100 favorite. The 40-40 chase is one subplot in a summer that has multiple stories worth telling.

What's Next

The next 17 games will determine whether PCA's name appears on one of baseball's shortest and most exclusive lists. The stolen base is the variable — home runs come from power that doesn't evaporate, but stolen bases require the right game situations, the right reads, and the right decisions to run.

PCA has been making those decisions well all summer. Seven more times and he'll be in company that only six players in baseball history have kept.
