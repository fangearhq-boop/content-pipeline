Four Teams, Three Spots, 17 Games: The NL Wild Card Race Is Here

By Jake Torres | September 9, 2026

MLB

The NL Wild Card standings entering September 9 tell you everything you need to know about why the next three weeks of baseball exist.

The Chicago Cubs hold the first Wild Card spot at 81-64. The Philadelphia Phillies are a half-game behind in second. The Arizona Diamondbacks are in third. The San Diego Padres are a half-game behind the D-backs. Four teams. Three playoff spots. Half-game margins between each of them.

The Wild Card Series begins September 29. That's 20 days away. Three weeks for four teams to decide which one goes home early.

The Cubs' Advantage

Chicago enters September with the most compelling individual story in baseball attached to their playoff push. Pete Crow-Armstrong's 40-40 chase — 40 home runs and 33 steals, 7 more needed in 17 games — is running parallel to a pennant race that matters. When those two narratives intersect, the Cubs become the most-followed story in the NL.

The Cubs' half-game lead over the Phillies means nothing is guaranteed. Wild Card Series matchups are decided by seeding, and the difference between WC1 and WC2 in terms of opponent and home field advantage in the series can be significant. Chicago has a reason to play hard for first.

The Phillies' Case

Philadelphia is the team with the most postseason experience in this cluster. Half a game is a gap that can disappear in two days. If the Phillies run the table while the Cubs stumble, the seedings flip. They're close enough to flip twice before September ends.

The Bottom Half

Arizona and San Diego are separated by half a game from each other and roughly a game from WC2. Either team can find itself in third or out of the field entirely by the end of any given week. The Padres in particular have the offensive depth to go on a run — they've just needed to do it.

The team that falls out will be the one that had its chances and didn't convert. September doesn't wait for good intentions.

What's Next

Check the standings daily. In a race this tight, every game is effectively a one-game swing in the standings — the team that wins gains ground on whoever lost that night. The Wild Card Series bracket won't be clear until the final weekend, and at these margins, the final day of the regular season might decide who makes it.

Three weeks. Four teams. Three spots. This is what September baseball is for.
