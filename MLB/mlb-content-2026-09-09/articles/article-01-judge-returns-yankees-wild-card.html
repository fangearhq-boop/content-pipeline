Aaron Judge Is Back. Now Comes the Hard Part.

By Marcus Cole | September 9, 2026

New York Yankees

There were 99 days between the last time Aaron Judge played baseball and the moment he walked into the batter's box at Yankee Stadium on Monday night. The crowd stood before he swung.

He went 0-for-3 with two strikeouts. The Yankees beat the Colorado Rockies 5-3 anyway.

That scoreline is, in a narrow sense, the whole story. Judge returned. He was rusty, which is what 99 days on the injured list does to any hitter, including the best one in the American League. His team won without him carrying the offense. And now, with approximately 17 games remaining in the regular season, the Yankees have what they've been waiting for since June.

What the 99 Days Cost

The fractured rib that sent Judge to the IL in early June was the kind of injury that doesn't have a clean timeline. You wait until the bone heals, until the pain subsides, until the baseball activities that aggravate the injury can be done without hesitation. The Yankees reinstated him from the 10-day IL on September 7 and put him back in the lineup the following night.

To create the roster space, they optioned Elmer Rodriguez and designated Chase Hampton for assignment. Those are the kinds of decisions that require a team to be confident the returning player is actually ready — not just healthy enough to play, but healthy enough to perform.

The 0-for-3 line won't settle that question. What will settle it is the next two weeks.

The Wild Card Picture

The Yankees held first place in the AL Wild Card race all summer without their best player. That's a remarkable statement about what this team has built — and it also sets up Judge's return as something more complicated than a simple upgrade.

New York entered Monday with a 3-game lead over the Boston Red Sox in the first Wild Card spot. That's a meaningful cushion, but not a comfortable one. Seventeen games is enough time for a lot to change. The Yankees are going to need Judge to be himself — not getting-his-swing-back Judge, but the version that led the AL in most offensive categories for three months before the rib gave out.

The standing ovation before his first at-bat told you everything about what the fanbase understands: his return matters more than one game's line. Monday was just the beginning.

What's Next

The Yankees' remaining schedule will tell you quickly whether Judge's return changes the calculus of the AL Wild Card race. If he finds his timing in the first few games back, New York's lead could extend. If the rust lingers, the Red Sox are close enough to apply pressure.

Judge's presence in a playoff series, when it comes, is a different conversation. Postseason at-bats with a recently healed rib are a test of more than swing mechanics. For now, the Yankees have him back. That's what they needed first.
