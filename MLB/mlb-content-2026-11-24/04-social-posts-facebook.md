### STORY 1: MLB Sends Revised Proposal — 7 Days to Stop the Freeze

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 10:30 AM ET

Baseball's offseason window is closing faster than you think — and it has nothing to do with free agents.

On Monday, Major League Baseball sent the Players Association a revised salary cap proposal. The union rejected it. The collective bargaining agreement expires December 1 at 11:59 PM Eastern, and if there's no deal, a lockout begins one minute later.

Here's what that means in plain terms: all transactions stop. No signings. No trades. The Rule 5 Draft — canceled. Players can't even talk to their own teams.

MLB's proposal sets a hard salary cap at $258.2 million and a spending floor at $180.2 million. The union has never accepted a cap in 50-plus years of collective bargaining. That's not a small gap to bridge in 7 days.

We've been here before. The 2021-22 lockout lasted 99 days and ended March 10, just barely saving the opening of that season. Commissioner Rob Manfred has said he "can't imagine" owners ratifying a deal without a cap and floor. The MLBPA executive has called a lockout "all but guaranteed."

What's on the line in the next 7 days is nothing short of the 2027 season itself. Last time, it took until late February for real negotiations to begin. Teams are rushing to finish their offseason business before the freeze.

Stay with Ballpark Banter for daily updates as the deadline approaches.

### STORY 2: Skubal Physical Underway — The Final Gate on a $450M Deal

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 12:30 PM ET

There's one more step between Tarik Skubal and the most expensive pitching contract in baseball history.

The physical.

Skubal and the New York Mets agreed in principle to a 9-year, $450 million deal last weekend — $50 million per year, with a $45 million signing bonus and a full no-trade clause. It shatters Yoshinobu Yamamoto's previous pitching record of $27 million per year. It is, by any measure, an extraordinary bet on one pitcher's arm.

That arm is now being examined by doctors.

A physical at this price point is comprehensive: MRI of the pitching arm, full musculoskeletal review, cardiovascular evaluation. Scouts and executives who saw Skubal throughout the 2026 season have reportedly raised no medical concerns. The reports describe the exam as a formality, but no $450 million commitment is ever truly routine.

The timing matters. The CBA expires December 1. If the physical clears and the deal is officially announced before then, the Mets will have their franchise cornerstone locked up before the market freezes. If it drags past Dec. 2, the lockout could technically delay the official signing — a scenario the Mets almost certainly want to avoid.

The Mets went 74-88 in 2026. Skubal is the beginning of something new in Queens. Baseball is waiting to hear those test results.

### STORY 3: Chisholm — Blue Jays Closing In, But Clock Is Ticking

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 3:00 PM ET

Jazz Chisholm has not signed. And that matters more by the hour.

The Blue Jays remain the frontrunner, according to Shi Davidi of Sportsnet. Personal meetings between Chisholm's representatives and Toronto are ongoing. Jon Morosi of MLB Network has reported 11 teams engaged with the speedster, and despite all that interest, the Yankees — his most recent employer — are out.

The fit in Toronto makes sense. Chisholm hit 19 home runs and stole 41 bases for the Yankees in 2026. The Blue Jays as a team stole just 75 bases all season. Chisholm alone would have accounted for more than half that total. He slides into second base and immediately transforms Toronto's offensive profile.

There's no qualifying offer attached, which means no draft-pick compensation for the team that signs him. That should make this easier, not harder.

And yet — no deal.

The clock is what should be concentrating minds. If Chisholm isn't signed by December 2, he enters a frozen market. The 2021 lockout lasted 99 days. That means teams waiting out a lockout before signing Chisholm could be looking at March before he has a home. For a player entering what should be his prime free-agency years, that's an expensive wait.

The Blue Jays need to close.

### STORY 4: Brandon Lowe — The Best Value on the Board

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 6:00 PM ET

Everybody is talking about Skubal. About Chisholm. About the lockout.

Nobody is talking about Brandon Lowe — and that's exactly why he's the best value on the free-agent market right now.

Lowe is a second baseman with real pop, an on-base approach, and no qualifying offer attached. The Pirates, Rays, Blue Jays, and Yankees have all been connected to him. No draft-pick compensation comes with signing him. For a player of his caliber, that combination — legitimate skill, no QO cost — is rare.

The Pirates make the most sense. Pittsburgh has needed a middle-infield upgrade for years. Lowe would immediately become their most productive hitter at the keystone, give the lineup another threat teams have to account for, and do it without costing a draft pick.

The quiet urgency is real. If Lowe isn't signed before December 2, he joins the frozen market along with Chisholm, Suzuki, and everyone else still unsigned. For a player who isn't a headline name, waiting out a 99-day lockout until spring isn't just inconvenient — it's disruptive to whatever team eventually signs him, too.

The deals that look smart in April are often the ones nobody noticed in November. Brandon Lowe is one of them.

### STORY 5: 7 Days to Sign or Wait Until Spring

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 10:00 PM ET

Seven days. That's what's left.

December 2 is when the lights go out on baseball's offseason. The lockout kicks in. No signings. No trades. The Rule 5 Draft disappears. Teams can't even talk to their own players.

In seven days, some teams will have finished their offseason. Others will be left staring at empty rosters until a new CBA is reached — potentially not until March.

Here's who needs to move fastest:

**The Mets** are waiting on a Tarik Skubal physical before his $450M deal is official. If it clears in the next 48 hours, they can pivot to building a roster around their new ace. If it drags, they may have one of the most expensive agreements in baseball history stuck in limbo.

**The Cubs** have a bigger problem. Seiya Suzuki declined his qualifying offer and is now projected for a 4-year, $90-110 million deal on the open market. They also lost Ian Happ and pitcher Kelly to free agency. That's roughly a third of their offense and rotation depth gone — before the freeze. Kevin Gausman came over from Toronto, but significant holes remain.

**The Blue Jays** are chasing both Chisholm and potentially Suzuki. Toronto's window is real, but it requires executing quickly on multiple targets in the same 7-day sprint.

The 2021 lockout lasted 99 days. Teams that finished their business before December 2 had a 99-day head start on planning. That's the entire spring training window.

Seven days. The rush is on.
