MLB Sends Revised Proposal — 7 Days to Stop the Freeze

By Jake Torres | November 24, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Major League Baseball sent the Players Association a revised salary cap proposal on Monday, and the union hasn't moved. With seven days remaining before the collective bargaining agreement expires on December 1 at 11:59 PM Eastern, the gap between the two sides has not narrowed. A lockout, beginning at 12:01 AM on December 2, looks more certain by the hour.

Commissioner Rob Manfred has said he "can't imagine" owners ratifying a new deal without a cap and floor in place. The union has said it will not negotiate economics solely within a cap-and-floor framework — a position it has held for decades. These are not talking points designed for public consumption. They are the actual negotiating stances, and they are incompatible.

What MLB Is Proposing

The revised proposal keeps the core structure of MLB's earlier offer: a hard salary cap set at $258.2 million, with a mandatory spending floor of $180.2 million. Both thresholds would take effect in the 2028 season, with the current competitive balance tax system continuing through 2027. MLB is also seeking a 12-round international amateur draft beginning in 2027.

For teams spending near the luxury tax threshold, a hard cap would be an operational constraint they haven't faced before. For small-market teams, the floor would be a mandate to spend — something not all franchises have done voluntarily. The owners believe both provisions create a more competitive landscape. The union believes a cap suppresses player earnings and that a floor, without guaranteed raises to minimum salaries and pre-arbitration pay, is insufficient compensation for accepting one.

Why the MLBPA Won't Move

The Players Association's objection is not philosophical stubbornness. Baseball players operate in the only major American professional sports league without a salary cap, and the absence of one has allowed top players to reach free-agent contracts that dwarf what NFL and NBA counterparts earn in capped markets. Accepting a cap would change the ceiling of what baseball's best players can earn — permanently.

The 2021-22 lockout, which lasted 99 days and ended March 10, produced a five-year deal that improved pre-arbitration bonuses and minimum salaries but did not include a cap. The union's position coming out of that negotiation was that it had preserved the open market. Its position entering this one is the same: the open market is non-negotiable.

What Happens on December 2

If no deal is reached before the December 1 deadline, a lockout begins immediately. All transactions freeze. Signings stop. Trades stop. The Rule 5 Draft — which allows teams to select unprotected prospects — is canceled. Players and teams cannot communicate about contract matters. Free agents already agreed to deals in principle but not officially signed — including Tarik Skubal, whose $450 million agreement with the Mets is pending a physical — could face legal uncertainty about how the lockout affects their paperwork.

The 2021 work stoppage ended just before spring training would have been irrevocably disrupted. Baseball lost no regular-season games. That outcome required a February agreement, which required negotiations that did not seriously begin until late February. If this lockout follows a similar timeline, teams will have lost the entire offseason and most of spring training before play resumes.

What's Next

Formal negotiating sessions are expected to continue this week. The two sides have never been further apart on a core economic issue, but no one is walking away from the table yet. The next 72 hours will reveal whether MLB's revised proposal was a genuine attempt to bridge the gap or a final pre-lockout filing. Watch for a MLBPA response by Wednesday — if the union publicly declares it has seen nothing new worth engaging with, the December 2 freeze becomes a near-certainty. The 2027 season's opening day is now a question mark.
