Skubal Physical Underway — The Final Gate on a $450M Deal

By Ryan Calloway | November 24, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Somewhere in a medical facility, Tarik Skubal is being examined by doctors, and the entire sport is watching. The New York Mets and the left-handed pitcher agreed in principle last weekend to a 9-year, $450 million contract — the largest pitching deal in baseball history. The agreement includes a $45 million signing bonus, a full no-trade clause, and an average annual value of $50 million that more than doubles Yoshinobu Yamamoto's previous pitching record of $27 million per year.

None of it is official until the physical clears.

What a $450 Million Physical Covers

At this dollar figure, the medical examination is comprehensive by design. For a pitcher, it centers on the throwing arm: MRI scans of the elbow and shoulder, full range-of-motion testing, and a detailed review of any prior structural history. Teams commissioning examinations at this level also typically conduct full musculoskeletal evaluation, cardiovascular assessment, and a review of any medical records from the player's time with his previous club.

Skubal dealt with a procedure in the spring of 2026 to remove loose bodies from his left elbow, a surgery that placed him on the injured list and briefly complicated his trade market value. By the time he returned to competition, however, scouts and executives who evaluated him extensively reported no ongoing concerns. Multiple reports describe the exam as procedural rather than precautionary — the Mets are not expecting bad news.

What the Mets Are Betting

The Mets went 74-88 in 2026. They are a team that has spent heavily before — sometimes wisely, sometimes not — and this commitment represents a franchise reset built around elite pitching rather than assembled rosters of aging stars. At 25 years old heading into the contract, Skubal would be in his prime for the first five seasons, with a reasonable expectation of sustained performance through year seven or eight.

The calculus is simple: if Skubal stays healthy, the Mets have a No. 1 starter who can anchor a staff for a decade. If the arm doesn't hold, $450 million becomes a monument to the difficulty of projecting pitcher health over nine years. The Mets are betting on the first scenario. The physical is the last procedural step before that bet is formally placed.

The Lockout Deadline Adds Pressure

The collective bargaining agreement expires December 1. If the physical is not complete and the deal is not formally signed by that point, the lockout could affect the paperwork's finalization. Neither the Mets nor Skubal's representatives have signaled any concern about timeline, and the examination is widely expected to conclude before the week is out. But the seven-day window is real, and both sides have a shared interest in moving quickly.

Baseball history is not short on cautionary tales about agreed deals that fell through or were complicated by physical findings. No one is expecting that outcome here. What they are expecting — from fans in New York, from rivals around the league, and from every team that chased Skubal and lost — is official confirmation that the most expensive pitcher in baseball history is a Met.

What's Next

Expect the official announcement within 48 to 72 hours, barring any unexpected findings. Once the deal is signed, the Mets will pivot immediately to building the rest of their roster — likely addressing lineup needs and bullpen depth before December 2. Skubal himself is expected to speak publicly for the first time about the contract once it is official. For the Mets, their rebuild just found its cornerstone. Now they need to build the building around it.
