Brandon Lowe Is the Best Value on the Free Agent Market

By Jake Torres | November 24, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

When baseball fans discuss the most important names on this offseason's free-agent board, they talk about Tarik Skubal. About Jazz Chisholm. About the lockout. What they rarely discuss is Brandon Lowe — and that oversight is exactly what makes him the best value available.

Lowe is a second baseman with real offensive production, a track record of power from the middle infield, and no qualifying offer attached. The Pittsburgh Pirates, Tampa Bay Rays, Toronto Blue Jays, and New York Yankees have all been connected to him. Any of those teams would be getting a legitimate upgrade at a position that many clubs treat as a placeholder. And they'd be doing it without surrendering a draft pick.

What Lowe Brings

Second base is one of baseball's most historically undervalued positions. Teams routinely deploy below-average hitters there in exchange for defensive stability, accepting an offensive drain that compounds over 162 games. Lowe has never fit that mold. His combination of on-base skill and plus power — including multiple seasons of 15 or more home runs — makes him a genuine lineup contributor, not a glove-first placeholder.

The absence of a qualifying offer is significant. QO-attached players come with a draft-pick cost for any team outside the top-10 payrolls, which suppresses the market. Lowe carries no such burden. A team can sign him cleanly, without the QO penalty, which theoretically invites more competition and raises his price. In practice, because he lacks the headline appeal of Chisholm or Skubal, that competition has been quiet.

The Best Fit: Pittsburgh

Among the teams connected to Lowe, the Pirates represent the most compelling fit. Pittsburgh has needed a productive middle infielder for years, and Lowe would immediately become the most dangerous bat at the keystone they have deployed in recent memory. He provides lineup protection, a threat teams must respect, and does it at a cost that fits the Pirates' operating model — meaningful without being franchise-altering.

The Rays, Lowe's former team, understand his value as well as anyone. Tampa Bay is typically efficient in managing roster continuity. The Blue Jays and Yankees are larger-market clubs also pursuing higher-profile targets — Lowe would represent depth or a secondary priority rather than a lead acquisition.

The Lockout Urgency

Lowe is not a headline player, which means the pressure to sign him before December 2 is less acute for teams that believe he will be available on the other side of a lockout at a discounted rate. That calculation is not necessarily wrong, but it assumes the lockout resolves in days rather than weeks. The 2021 work stoppage lasted 99 days. Teams that waited for lockout discounts in that cycle found themselves doing business in March — in the middle of a compressed spring training — rather than at their leisure over the winter.

The smart move is to close before December 2. For the Pirates especially, adding Lowe this week costs nothing beyond the contract itself, provides immediate roster clarity, and locks in one of the better 2B options in the sport before he gets priced into someone else's rebuild.

What's Next

Expect the Lowe market to accelerate in the final days before the lockout deadline. Teams that have been monitoring him will need to make decisions by the end of the week. The Pirates are the best fit. The Blue Jays are a logical alternative if their Chisholm pursuit stalls. The Yankees are unlikely to lead the bidding with larger priorities on their board. Watch for a contract announcement before December 2 — the value is clear, and the clock is running.
