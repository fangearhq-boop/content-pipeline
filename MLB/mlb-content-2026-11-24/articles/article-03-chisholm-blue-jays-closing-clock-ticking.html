Jazz Chisholm: Blue Jays Closing In, But Clock Is Ticking

By Marcus Cole | November 24, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Jazz Chisholm Jr. has not signed. The Blue Jays are the frontrunner. The lockout freeze is seven days away. Those three facts, in combination, make Chisholm's free agency the most time-sensitive transaction in this offseason's compressed final act.

Shi Davidi of Sportsnet has Toronto leading the pursuit, with personal meetings between Chisholm's representatives and Blue Jays officials ongoing. Jon Morosi of MLB Network has reported 11 teams engaged in his market. The New York Yankees — the team Chisholm played for in 2026 — are out per multiple reports. The field has narrowed. The question is whether Toronto can close.

Why the Blue Jays

The fit is demonstrably real. Chisholm hit 19 home runs and stole 41 bases for the Yankees in 2026. The Toronto Blue Jays as a team stole just 75 bases all season. Adding Chisholm at second base would have accounted for more than half that total by himself, transforming a lineup that was notably static on the basepaths into one of the most dynamic in the American League East.

There is also a critical structural advantage for the Blue Jays: no qualifying offer. Because Chisholm declined a qualifying offer in a prior offseason and is now a true free agent without compensation attached, the team that signs him owes nothing in draft-pick compensation. That zero-cost feature makes aggressive bidding more rational. Toronto isn't trading away future assets to win this race.

The Lockout Is the Real Deadline

Free agency typically operates without hard external deadlines, but this offseason has one: December 2 at 12:01 AM. When the collective bargaining agreement expires and a lockout begins, all transactions freeze. Players who are unsigned on December 2 do not sign until a new CBA is in place — which, based on 2021 precedent, could be as late as March.

For Chisholm, that creates a specific urgency. At 29 years old, entering what should be a contract year representing his peak earning power, waiting out a 99-day lockout before finding a team is not ideal. The leverage cuts both ways: the Blue Jays know that Chisholm wants to sign, and Chisholm knows that Toronto wants to sign him. What remains is the number and the years.

What Toronto Needs

The Blue Jays are not simply chasing Chisholm as a luxury addition. Toronto has several roster needs entering what could be a pivotal offseason — including addressing their baserunning deficiencies and lineup depth. If the lockout hits before they sign Chisholm and potentially other targets including Seiya Suzuki, they would begin spring training with significant unresolved business. That's the scenario Toronto's front office is working urgently to avoid.

The personal-meetings phase is typically the final step before a deal is agreed. Teams do not usually invite a free agent's representatives into direct discussions unless they are prepared to make a serious offer. The Blue Jays are there.

What's Next

Watch for a Chisholm signing announcement in the next three to five days. If Toronto does not close by the weekend, the risk of a freeze-interrupted deal increases substantially. If another team — likely from among the 11 engaged — emerges with a competing offer that reshapes the market, all bets are off. For now, the Blue Jays hold the pole position. They need to use it.
