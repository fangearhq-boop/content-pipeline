7 Days to Sign or Wait Until Spring — The Hot Stove Rush Is On

By Ryan Calloway | November 24, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

December 2 is not a date on baseball's calendar. It's a wall. When the collective bargaining agreement expires at 11:59 PM on December 1 and a lockout begins one minute later, every transaction in the sport freezes. No signings. No trades. No Rule 5 Draft. Teams cannot even contact their own players about contract matters.

What happens in the next seven days will determine which franchises enter 2027 with their rosters built and which ones spend the winter waiting for a labor deal that history suggests could take 99 days to reach. The seven-day scramble is on — and some clubs are far more exposed than others.

The Mets: One Physical Away From Their Foundation

The New York Mets agreed in principle to a 9-year, $450 million contract with Tarik Skubal last weekend, pending a physical. If that examination clears — expected within days — the Mets will officially have the most expensive pitcher in baseball history anchoring a rotation that went 74-88 in 2026. With Skubal secured, their remaining offseason priorities involve building around him: lineup depth, bullpen construction, and depth starting options. The seven-day window is their chance to do that work before the freeze arrives. If the physical is approved this week, they'll have time. If it drags toward the weekend, their flexibility shrinks rapidly.

The Cubs: A Third of a Lineup Is Missing

Chicago entered this offseason needing to manage transitions. It has become something more urgent. Seiya Suzuki declined his qualifying offer and is now a free agent projected for a 4-year, $90 to $110 million contract. Ian Happ and Kelly also departed. The Cubs have lost roughly a third of their offensive lineup to free agency in a matter of weeks. Kevin Gausman arrived via trade from Toronto to address some rotation needs, but the holes in the batting order are significant and unfilled.

Seven days is not a lot of time to replace that production. The Cubs need to decide quickly whether they pursue Suzuki — their own departed player, now with a qualifying-offer compensation cost attached — or pivot to alternative targets. Every day of delay increases the probability that they begin spring training with the roster gaps the lockout freeze will capture.

The Blue Jays: Multiple Races, One Window

Toronto is running multiple acquisition tracks simultaneously. The Blue Jays are the frontrunner for Jazz Chisholm, with personal meetings ongoing. They are also among the teams connected to Seiya Suzuki. These are not independent pursuits — the same payroll dollars fund both conversations, and signing one affects the feasibility of the other.

The Blue Jays are a legitimate contender that needs to get better. Their baserunning deficit in 2026 makes Chisholm an urgent priority. Their lineup depth makes Suzuki a compelling option. Closing both before December 2 is ambitious. Closing neither would leave the roster substantially unchanged entering whatever version of spring training the lockout eventually permits.

The 2021 Lesson

The last lockout ended March 10 after 99 days. Teams that had signed their priority free agents before December 2 of that year had a planning and preparation advantage that compounded over the entire spring. Teams that expected quick resolution — that believed the lockout would be a short pause — found themselves scrambling in compressed spring training with new signings who had barely reported before opening day.

Baseball does not reward waiting when the alternative is clarity. The seven days remaining on this offseason's clock represent the last window to operate without the uncertainty of a labor stoppage hanging over every move.

What's Next

Watch for a cascade of announcements in the next 72 hours. Teams do not let artificial deadlines pass without action if they can avoid it, and the December 2 wall is the most consequential deadline in baseball in five years. Skubal's physical is the first domino. If it falls cleanly this week, the Mets get to work. The Blue Jays need to close on Chisholm. The Cubs need to decide on Suzuki. The rest of the unsigned market — Lowe, Rogers, and a lengthy list of relievers and depth starters — will either find homes before December 2 or wait for spring. The rush is on. Act accordingly.
