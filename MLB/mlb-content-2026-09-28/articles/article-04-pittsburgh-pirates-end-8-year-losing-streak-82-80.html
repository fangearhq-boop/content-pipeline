Pittsburgh Pirates End Eight-Year Losing Streak — 82-80 in 2026, First Winning Season Since 2018

By Marcus Cole | September 28, 2026

It took eight years, and it finally happened on a Sunday afternoon in Detroit.

The Pittsburgh Pirates beat the Detroit Tigers 4-2 in the final game of the 2026 season at Comerica Park, finishing 82-80 — their first winning record since 2018. Seven consecutive losing seasons, from 2019 through 2025, are now behind them. The streak that felt like it might never end is over.

The Context of the Streak

To understand what Sunday means, it helps to understand what the last eight years felt like in Pittsburgh.

After finishing 82-79 in 2018, the Pirates entered a rebuilding era that stretched far longer than most rebuilds. They went through multiple core rotations — young prospects developed, traded, or simply didn't pan out. Roster turnover was constant. The fan base, which had seen winning baseball in the early-to-mid 2010s, endured season after season of finishing in the bottom half of the NL standings, watching other franchises compete for titles while Pittsburgh remained a work in progress.

By entering 2026 with seven consecutive losing seasons, the Pirates had tied for the second-longest active losing streak in baseball. That number represents an enormous investment of patience from an organization, a fanbase, and a city that loves its Pirates even when the Pirates haven't given them much to love in return.

The Rarity of the Achievement

In historical context, Sunday's finish is only the fifth winning season for Pittsburgh in the last 34 years. The other four: 94-68 in 2013, 88-74 in 2014, 98-64 in 2015, and 82-79 in 2018. Two generations of Pirates fans have grown up watching a franchise that finishes above .500 roughly once every seven or eight years. The 2026 season belongs in that short list now.

82-80 doesn't deliver a playoff spot in a league with 12 postseason berths concentrated at the top of the standings. But it is above .500. It means more wins than losses in a season that stretched from April to September. And it ends a streak that was becoming a defining characteristic of one of baseball's historic franchises.

What's Next

The Pittsburgh Pirates will enter the 2027 offseason with a winning 2026 season as their foundation. Whether that foundation translates into playoff contention will depend on how the organization builds on it — whether key contributors return, how the roster develops, and whether the direction of the franchise has genuinely turned. For Pirates fans who have waited since 2018, the immediate question is not about October. It is about whether 82-80 is the beginning of something, or just a one-year exception to a long pattern. Sunday was the right start. The rest depends on what comes next.
