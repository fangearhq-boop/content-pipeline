Houston Clinches AL West at 81-81 — The First Sub-.500 Division Champion in MLB History

By Marcus Cole | September 28, 2026

The Houston Astros finished 81-81 in 2026. They are also the American League West champions.

Those two sentences have never been possible in the same paragraph, until now. The Astros became the first team with a non-winning record to win a division in MLB's 162-game era on Sunday, when the Texas Rangers lost 6-4 to the Minnesota Twins — a result that clinched the AL West for Houston via tiebreaker before the Astros had even completed their own 9-0 win over the Sacramento A's.

How It Happened

Houston and Texas entered the final weekend of the regular season tied at 79-81. Two games left for each team. The math was straightforward: the Rangers needed to win AND see the Astros lose. The Astros, holding the tiebreaker by virtue of their head-to-head season record against Texas, needed only to win once or see the Rangers lose once.

Sunday, the Rangers played Minnesota. The Twins won 6-4. At that point, the calculation was complete. Houston — still in the middle of their afternoon game against Oakland's relocated franchise — had clinched the division on a scoreboard they didn't control. They went on to win 9-0 anyway, finishing the season properly.

The Rangers go home. Their elimination eliminates any chance of a Wild Card backdoor as well. For Texas, finishing 80-82 after entering the final weekend of the season tied for first place is an ending that will require significant offseason reflection.

What It Means Historically

No team in the 162-game era — dating back to 1962 — had ever won a division with a non-winning record before the Astros did it Sunday. Divisions have produced some ugly winners before, but none had ever crossed the finish line at .500 or below in a standard full season.

The AL West has always been a difficult division. The Astros and Rangers spent most of the season trading the lead at or near the .500 mark, both dealing with the weight of a division that could not produce a dominant team in 2026. The result is an historically strange champion — a team that finished the regular season with exactly as many wins as losses, earning a postseason berth and a division banner through the calendar and the tiebreaker.

For the Astros, this marks their ninth postseason appearance in eleven years — a remarkable sustained run for a franchise that was historically the sport's most dysfunctional just over a decade ago. They will enter October having made history for reasons nobody anticipated.

What's Next

The Houston Astros enter the 2026 postseason as the No. 3 AL seed, meaning they receive a first-round bye and will face one of the three Wild Card teams in the ALDS. Their playoff rotation, health, and the dynamics of the AL bracket will shape their chances — an 81-81 record provides limited room for optimism, but October has always had a way of making regular-season records irrelevant. The postseason begins Tuesday, September 29, with the Wild Card round. The Astros open divisional play shortly after.
