White Sox Make History — First Team From Back-to-Back 100-Loss Seasons to Reach the MLB Playoffs

By Jake Torres | September 28, 2026

The Chicago White Sox are going to the playoffs.

They finished 84-78 in 2026, claiming the No. 6 AL Wild Card seed. It is not just a good story. It is a historic one: the White Sox became the first team in MLB history to make the postseason after consecutive 100-loss seasons. The turnaround is complete. The rebuild worked.

Where They Came From

The depth of the White Sox's recent struggles is important context for appreciating what the 2026 season represents. The organization endured back-to-back seasons of 100-plus losses, finishing deep in the AL standings and exhausting the patience of a fanbase in a city that has seen this team win a World Series within living memory.

In those difficult seasons, the organization drafted, developed, and acquired the building blocks of a competitive roster. The process was not elegant. It was not fast. And at multiple points during those losing seasons, the path forward was genuinely unclear. The standard line about rebuilds — "trust the process" — wears thin after losing 100 games once. After losing 100 games twice, it requires real faith to believe the organization knows what it is doing.

The 2026 Chicago White Sox are the proof that the organization knew what it was doing.

The 84-78 Record That Made History

The Cleveland Guardians won the AL Central at 85-77, their third straight division title. That pushed the White Sox to the Wild Card, where they finished as the No. 6 seed. The specific seeding — sixth in the AL — means they'll face a tough matchup in the Wild Card round, likely against one of the higher-seeded Wild Card teams.

But context matters: 84-78 is the record of a team that improved by more than 15 games from their worst recent seasons. The 2026 White Sox outperformed their pre-season projections. They stayed competitive through a September that could have collapsed into another losing year. They are in October.

No team in MLB history had gone from consecutive 100-loss seasons to a playoff appearance. The White Sox just did it. That's not a footnote — it's the headline.

What's Next

The Chicago White Sox enter the AL Wild Card round as the No. 6 seed. Their postseason journey starts Tuesday, September 29. Whether they can extend the October run depends on how the Wild Card Series matchup unfolds — the White Sox's pitching and defense will be tested against playoff-caliber offenses in a best-of-three format with no margin for error. For a fanbase that sat through 100-loss seasons and waited for the rebuild to pay off, Tuesday night's first pitch is already the answer to every question they spent those years asking.
