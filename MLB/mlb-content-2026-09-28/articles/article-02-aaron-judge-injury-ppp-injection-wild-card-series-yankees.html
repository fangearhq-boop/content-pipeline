Aaron Judge Gets PPP Injection Monday — Yankees "Planning on Him Not Being a Part Of" Wild Card Series

By Jake Torres | September 28, 2026

The New York Yankees are entering the postseason with an enormous question at the center of their lineup.

Aaron Judge, who suffered a moderate-grade right calf strain during a game against the Minnesota Twins, received a platelet-poor plasma (PPP) injection in his calf Monday — the first day off before the Wild Card Series begins Tuesday, September 29, against the Boston Red Sox. Yankees manager Aaron Boone told reporters Monday that the team is "planning on him not being a part of" the upcoming series. He did not officially rule Judge out.

The Injury and the Timeline

Calf strains are graded by severity, and a moderate-grade strain represents a meaningful setback without being a season-ending injury. The PPP injection — which delivers concentrated growth factors to the affected area — is a common treatment used to accelerate soft-tissue healing. The limitation is time: PPP injections require rest in the immediate aftermath to allow the treatment to take effect, and the Wild Card Series starts the following day.

Boone's framing — "planning on him not being there" combined with "not ruling anything out" — is the standard injury-update double-speak of postseason baseball. It communicates that Judge is unlikely to play while leaving the door open for a dramatic change if he progresses faster than expected. Given the injection Monday and games starting Tuesday, the realistic window for Judge to appear in this series narrows considerably if it ends before Thursday, October 1.

Judge has had limited activities since the calf strain was diagnosed. He will continue that approach in the immediate days following the PPP injection.

What His Absence Would Mean

Aaron Judge is not just a presence in the Yankees' lineup. He is their offensive identity. The team's ability to generate runs at the level required to beat Boston in a three-game series shifts meaningfully without his bat in the middle of the order.

The Red Sox finished as the No. 5 AL seed at 87-75, a team that can score runs and is not going to collapse in a three-game series regardless of who is pitching for New York. The Yankees have the pitching depth to compete without Judge — their rotation is legitimately strong — but offense generates runs, and Judge generates more of them per at-bat than virtually anyone in the American League.

The Yankees are not helpless without him. They made the playoffs as a team. But "planning on him not being there" heading into Game 1 is not the statement any fan or front office wanted to start October.

What's Next

The Wild Card Series between the Yankees and Red Sox begins Tuesday, September 29. The series is a best-of-three format, meaning the full series could wrap by Thursday, October 1. Watch for Judge's status update Tuesday morning — if he was cleared to do any baseball activity following Monday's PPP injection, that would shift Boone's communication. If he remains on limited activity, the Yankees are playing Game 1 without one of the best hitters in baseball. The postseason starts tomorrow either way.
