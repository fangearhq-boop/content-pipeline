### STORY 4: Pittsburgh Pirates End 8-Year Losing Streak

#### Facebook Long-Form — 3:00 PM ET

Eight years is a long time to wait.

The Pittsburgh Pirates hadn't finished with a winning record since 2018, when they went 82-79. From 2019 through 2025, they had seven consecutive losing seasons — the second-longest active such streak in baseball entering 2026. For Pirates fans, "next year" had been said too many times.

Sunday at Comerica Park, it finally happened. Pittsburgh beat the Detroit Tigers 4-2 in the season finale to finish 82-80. That's it. That's enough. A winning record for the first time since Andrew McCutchen was the face of the franchise.

In context, this is only the fifth time in the last 34 years that Pittsburgh has finished above .500. Their other winning seasons: 94-68 in 2013, 88-74 in 2014, 98-64 in 2015, and 82-79 in 2018. They weren't just bad for eight years — they were largely irrelevant in the standings, year after year.

82-80 won't bring a playoff spot. It won't bring a parade. But it represents something real: a franchise that was buried has climbed back above .500, and Pittsburgh baseball has something worth believing in again heading into 2027.
