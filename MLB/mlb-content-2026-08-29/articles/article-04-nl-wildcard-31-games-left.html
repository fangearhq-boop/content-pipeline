NL Wild Card: 31 Games Left and the Cardinals Are Making It Interesting

By Marcus Cole

August 29, 2026

The NL Wild Card race was supposed to be a three-team affair. The Cardinals may have different ideas.

Heading into play on August 29, the Wild Card standings look straightforward on the surface: Cubs (76-59), Phillies (74-60), and Padres (72-62) hold the three postseason spots. The Arizona Diamondbacks are 1.5 games back of the final slot and remain a threat.

But the story of the past two weeks in the National League is St. Louis. The Cardinals have won five consecutive series — a run that has quietly positioned them as the team nobody in the Wild Card race wants to see heating up in September.

The Cubs Hold, But Not Comfortably

On August 28, the Cubs dropped a 10-8 decision to the Cincinnati Reds, a game they should have been able to win. Chicago's lead over the Phillies is now two games — comfortable enough, but not the cushion they had 10 days ago.

The No. 1 Wild Card position matters in the NL. It brings home-field advantage in the three-game Wild Card Series, a meaningful edge against any opponent. The Cubs have held that spot for most of the second half. Keeping it requires finishing their schedule at or above the Phillies' pace — and with September series against playoff-quality opponents on the horizon, nothing is guaranteed.

Phillies and Padres Are Not Going Anywhere

Philadelphia (74-60) has played well down the stretch and has the pitching depth to close out the season strong. San Diego (72-62) has been inconsistent but possesses the talent to go on a run — and their schedule down the stretch includes games against teams that are out of contention and playing loosely.

Three teams in the Wild Card. Four teams (if you count St. Louis) with meaningful momentum entering the final 31 games. This is exactly the kind of chaos that makes late September must-watch baseball.

What's Next

The final 31 games of the regular season begin in earnest this weekend. Watch the Cubs' series results closely — each series win or loss has outsized implications for seeding. The Cardinals' streak is the wildcard within the Wild Card: if St. Louis can overtake the Padres or D-backs and make a formal charge at the field, this race becomes genuinely unpredictable. Check back daily as the standings shift.
