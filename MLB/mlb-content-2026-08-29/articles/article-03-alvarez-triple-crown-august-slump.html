Alvarez's Triple Crown Bid Is Alive — But August Has Been Brutal

By Ryan Calloway

August 29, 2026

Yordan Alvarez still leads the American League in batting average, home runs, and RBIs. But if you watched him in August, you might not know it.

The Houston Astros slugger has been in a pronounced power slump in August, hitting just one home run in the month after carrying a torrid pace through July. Despite that, he enters the final 31 games of the regular season in first place in all three Triple Crown categories: a .320 batting average, 36 home runs, and 90-plus RBIs — a position that puts him within sight of matching Miguel Cabrera's 2012 Triple Crown, the last time an AL hitter accomplished the feat.

The Challengers Are Closing

One home run in August opens the door for anyone in the HR race to walk through. Junior Caminero (Tampa Bay Rays) is right there at 35 home runs — just one back. New York's Ben Rice has also hit 35, matching Caminero for second place. Both players are younger, hotter in August, and have the momentum that Alvarez currently lacks.

In batting average, Tampa Bay's Chandler Simpson is tracking at .310 — 10 points back of Alvarez's .320 mark. That's a closeable gap over 31 games, particularly if Simpson continues a hot August and Alvarez's bat doesn't fully come around.

In RBIs, Alvarez's lead is more comfortable at 90-plus, with competitors trailing by several RBIs. But RBI production drops in September if a player's home run production dries up — and Alvarez's one-homer August is precisely the kind of extended drought that erodes RBI totals.

The Historical Weight

The Triple Crown is one of baseball's most coveted individual achievements — and one of its rarest. Cabrera's 2012 season with the Detroit Tigers was the first AL Triple Crown since Carl Yastrzemski in 1967 — a 45-year gap. Alvarez is trying to bring the feat back after another 14-year drought.

ZiPS projections give Alvarez a 66.5% chance to hold on across all three categories through the end of the season. Those odds have likely shifted with August's struggles. But he still leads. He still has more than a month to play. And his track record suggests that extended cold streaks aren't his natural state.

What's Next

With 31 games remaining, Alvarez needs a strong September to put this race away. Watch Caminero and Rice in the HR race — if either catches him, the Triple Crown bid ends there. In batting average, Simpson is the threat. The Astros, hovering around .500, need Alvarez to be their offensive driver heading into the final month. Whether he can flip the switch after a sluggish August will determine whether 2026 joins 2012 in the record book.
