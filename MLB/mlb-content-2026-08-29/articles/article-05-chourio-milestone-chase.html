Chourio Is One Home Run From History — And He's 22 Years Old

By Jake Torres

August 29, 2026

Jackson Chourio is about to join some rare company — if he can track down one more home run before the calendar turns to spring.

The Milwaukee Brewers outfielder (born March 11, 2004) needs one more career home run to become just the fourth player in National League history to reach 60 career home runs, 50 stolen bases, and 200 career RBIs before his 23rd birthday. He does not turn 23 until March 11, 2027. He has all of September, and if the Brewers make the postseason, potentially October.

The three players who got there before him: Orlando Cepeda, Cesar Cedeño, and Andruw Jones. Each was considered among the most explosive young talents the game had ever produced at their respective eras. Chourio is now on the doorstep of their company.

The Game That Got Him There

On August 27 in Milwaukee, Chourio went 4-for-5 with two home runs, four RBIs, and four runs scored in the Brewers' 8-2 victory over the New York Mets. It was his fifth career multi-homer game and his third of the 2026 season — a pace of production that has been his defining trait throughout his early career.

That performance put him within one swing of the 60-career-homer mark. Everything else — the stolen bases, the RBIs — is already in place. He needs one home run.

Why This Milestone Matters

The combination of home runs, stolen bases, and RBIs before age 23 is not just a statistical curiosity — it identifies a specific type of complete offensive player who can both generate power and create opportunity. Cepeda, Cedeño, and Jones were all five-tool players who dominated offensively at ages when most prospects are still working their way up through the minor leagues.

Chourio made his MLB debut at 20 and has never looked like he needed more time. His career arc — the pace of his power development, his stolen base totals, his accumulation of RBIs — has tracked ahead of almost every historical comparison you could make for a player his age.

What's Next

The Brewers have 31 games remaining in the regular season, and Jacob Misiorowski's rotation gives them legitimate postseason aspirations. For Chourio, the hunt for No. 60 adds an individual storyline to what is already a compelling team race. He will not be thinking about the milestone — he is not that kind of player — but his fans should. Every at-bat in September could be the one. Watch Chourio.
