PCA Leads the NL MVP Poll — But the Cubs Need to Win to Seal It

By Jake Torres

August 29, 2026

Pete Crow-Armstrong has the NL MVP race by a commanding margin in the polls. He has the analytics to back it up. And there is still something that could unravel the entire case.

The fifth MLB.com NL MVP poll of the 2026 season has Crow-Armstrong with 29 of 37 first-place votes and 177 total points. Shohei Ohtani is second with 8 first-place votes and 154 points. PCA leads on every meaningful metric: his MLB-best 7.7 fWAR, his 22 Outs Above Average in center field (the best defensive figure in baseball), and his .278/.376/.554 slash line with 33 home runs, 83 RBIs, and 32 stolen bases.

Since July 3, he has posted a 171 wRC+, a 1.014 OPS, and 14 home runs — numbers that make the case for him virtually unanswerable on pure value grounds.

The Team-Win Problem

On August 28, the Chicago Cubs lost to the Cincinnati Reds 10-8 at Wrigley Field. Chicago drops to 76-59, still holding the No. 1 position in the NL Wild Card standings. But with 31 games remaining, every loss matters — and MVP voters have historically factored team success into their decisions.

PCA is the Cubs' offensive engine. When Chicago wins, he is at the center of it. But when they drop games against teams like Cincinnati, the narrative around his candidacy gets complicated — even if his individual performance on a given night was productive.

The Cubs need wins. Not because PCA has done anything to lose the award, but because the MVP calculus in a two-man race often comes down to the team storyline.

The Ohtani Threat

Ohtani's numbers this year (.288/.389/.545, 30 HR, 80 RBI) are excellent — perhaps deserving of the award in any other season. But what makes him a genuine challenger is his pending return to pitching. He was 8-2 with a 1.79 ERA before pausing, and his pitching contributions represent a form of value that Crow-Armstrong simply cannot match.

If Ohtani returns to the mound in September and continues to pitch effectively while also hitting, the narrative around the MVP race shifts in ways that polling and fWAR cannot fully account for. Voters will be watching both players in September with the full context of what the award represents.

What's Next

The Cubs have 31 games remaining. PCA enters this stretch as the clear MVP frontrunner — but the race won't be settled until the final week of the regular season. Watch Ohtani's return to the mound and watch the Cubs' record. If Chicago keeps winning and Ohtani's September is inconsistent, PCA becomes the runaway winner. If the Cubs struggle and Ohtani pitches brilliantly, this race goes to the wire. All signs point to one of the more compelling MVP finishes in recent NL history.
