Misiorowski Velocity Watch: Brewers Ace's Next Start Raises Questions

By Marcus Cole

August 29, 2026

The Cy Young Award is already Jacob Misiorowski's to lose. But the conversation around Milwaukee's 24-year-old ace shifted this week from "how dominant is he?" to "how healthy is he?"

In his August 27 start against the New York Mets — a game the Brewers won 8-2 — Misiorowski averaged 98.7 mph on his fastball. That's down 1.8 mph from his 2026 season average of 100.5 mph. Reporters covering the game noted the dip, and it became the central talking point around a performance that was otherwise solid: 6 innings, 2 hits, 2 runs, 2 walks, 6 strikeouts, and a win.

His next start is scheduled for Tuesday at Wrigley Field against the Chicago Cubs.

The Numbers That Make This Matter

Misiorowski's 2026 season has been one of the best pitching performances in recent memory. He enters his Tuesday start at 14-5 with a 1.68 ERA, 216 strikeouts, and a 0.76 WHIP across 24 starts. The Cy Young Award market has him at -550 — a number that reflects near-universal consensus that the award is his barring something dramatic.

But a 1.8 mph velocity drop is not nothing. For pitchers who generate as much of their value from elite fastball velocity as Misiorowski does, a sustained drop in the 90s can mean the difference between a swing-and-miss pitch and a ball in play. His ability to miss bats at an elite rate depends partly on that premium velocity.

The Brewers have been attentive to Misiorowski's workload throughout the season. They gave him extra rest at multiple points, skipped starts during the second half, and have been deliberate about managing his innings total. This is not a team that has ignored the warning signs.

History as a Cautionary Tale

Late-season fatigue is one of the sport's recurring narratives — young aces who rack up innings into September and then either fade or get shut down. The Brewers are aware of the pattern. Their handling of Misiorowski this week — essentially declining to extend him beyond his normal start day — suggests they are paying close attention.

Whether Tuesday's start against Chicago provides clarity on his health or raises more questions will shape how Milwaukee manages the final month of the regular season. The Cy Young is secure regardless. The more pressing concern is what condition he is in for October.

What's Next

Misiorowski takes the mound Tuesday at Wrigley Field against the Chicago Cubs. Watch his fastball velocity in that start — if it trends back toward his 100.5 mph season average, the August 27 dip is likely an isolated data point. If it stays in the 98-99 range, the Brewers will have difficult decisions ahead about how to manage their ace through the stretch run. With 31 games left and the playoffs approaching, Milwaukee cannot afford to be surprised.
