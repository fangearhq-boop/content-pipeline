72 Hours to Lockout: The Last Rush Before Baseball Freezes

By Ryan Calloway

November 28, 2026

The countdown is real: 72 hours from now, baseball's collective bargaining agreement expires and the market freezes. Teams that haven't solved their roster problems are making phone calls today. Players who haven't found contracts are waiting by the phone. And the baseball business — the transactions, the negotiations, the offseason machinery — is about to grind to a halt.

The CBA expires at 11:59 p.m. ET on Tuesday, Dec. 1. Owners are expected to impose a lockout Wednesday morning — Dec. 2 — which immediately stops all activity. No free agent signings. No trades. No minor league contracts. No Rule 5 Draft preparations. Everything stops.

What Teams Are Doing Right Now

The pre-lockout sprint has been underway for weeks, but Saturday feels different — the urgency is palpable across the sport. Teams committed more than $1.7 billion in November alone, a figure that reflects not just the quality of this free-agent class but the structural pressure of a looming market freeze.

When the lockout hits, there's no precedent for how long it lasts. The parties' positions remain far apart. MLB has been pushing for a salary cap. The Players Association's response has been categorical rejection. MLB filed a preemptive antitrust lawsuit in federal court on Nov. 25, asking for a declaration that a lockout would be lawful — an escalatory move the union called "completely frivolous." Neither side is signaling flexibility.

Against that backdrop, every team that still needs to add a pitcher, a corner bat, or any other specific roster piece is working the phones on a Saturday afternoon in late November.

The Two Players Who'll Be Waiting

Two free agents stand out as the most significant players likely to be unsigned when the freeze begins.

Michael King, who declined the San Diego Padres' $28 million qualifying offer, is the best starting pitcher remaining on the market. He's 31, posted a 3.21 ERA across 32 starts, and has already been connected to multiple teams. King turned down $28 million — not a small sum — which suggests his camp believes the open market will return something better. Whether it does depends entirely on whether a deal materializes before Tuesday at midnight.

Seiya Suzuki is the top remaining position player in the class. His contact ability, outfield defense, and consistent production make him an appealing addition for teams with outfield vacancies. With the lockout looming, the market window to get a deal done is essentially the next 72 hours.

The 2021 Template

The last time baseball had a work stoppage, in December 2021, the lockout lasted 99 days. Spring training was canceled. Opening Day was pushed back a week. The sides spent most of that stretch arguing over the same salary cap question that is at the center of the current dispute — a cap that the union rejected then and is rejecting now.

The 2027 Opening Day is projected around March 25. That means approximately 115 days between Dec. 2 and the start of the season — giving a buffer before games would actually be threatened. But spring training, minor league deals, and the entire preparation structure for a baseball season go on ice the moment the lockout begins.

Baseball's 72-hour window is real. And it's closing fast.

What's Next

The CBA expires Tuesday, Dec. 1 at 11:59 p.m. ET. The final 72 hours will see a flurry of signings and announcements as teams race to close deals. Michael King and Seiya Suzuki are the two biggest remaining names; watch for movement on both. If no deal is reached by Tuesday, lockout begins Wednesday, Dec. 2.
