Jazz Chisholm Jr. Lands in Toronto on 1-Year, $20M Deal

By Jake Torres

November 28, 2026

Jazz Chisholm Jr. is a Toronto Blue Jay.

After one of the most closely watched free-agent pursuits of the offseason — with more than 11 teams involved in negotiations — Chisholm agreed to a one-year, $20 million deal with the Blue Jays, with a player option for the 2028 season. The deal makes Toronto one of the busiest teams in the pre-lockout rush, landing the most coveted free agent without a qualifying offer on the market.

The No-QO Advantage

The defining feature of Chisholm's market was what he didn't bring with him: a qualifying offer. Players attached to qualifying offers require the signing team to surrender a draft pick if they accept — a meaningful disincentive for teams that prioritize their amateur pipeline. Chisholm opted out of his previous contract without triggering a qualifying offer, which meant Toronto could sign him without any draft-pick penalty.

That distinction mattered. Players like Seiya Suzuki, Randy Arozarena, and Brandon Lowe each came with qualifying offer complications. Chisholm was the rare elite free agent where teams could make a full commitment without the compensatory cost. It's a significant reason why 11-plus teams were reportedly involved in discussions — there was no structural barrier to signing him, only competition.

Toronto wins that competition. The Blue Jays get their impact player without giving anything up beyond the contract value itself.

What He Brings to the Blue Jays

Chisholm is a table-setter and a lineup disruptor at the same time. His 41 stolen bases in 2026 ranked among the best in the American League, and his 19 home runs gave the Blue Jays the power-speed combination that makes lineups difficult to plan against. He's versatile defensively — capable at second base, capable in the outfield — which gives Toronto's manager flexibility to deploy him wherever the lineup construction demands.

At 25, Chisholm is entering what should be his prime. The one-year deal gives him a platform year: perform well, and the player option for 2028 kicks in, potentially allowing him to either exercise it or test a market that might be more expansive after a new CBA is signed. For a player with legitimate franchise-caliber upside, the structure makes sense — a bridge to a potentially much larger long-term deal.

Blue Jays Context

Toronto enters 2027 needing to push into the AL Wild Card picture. The addition of Chisholm gives the lineup an explosive element that the club had been lacking since losing key contributors in recent offseasons. Combined with the foundation already in place, the Blue Jays now have a lineup that can compete with the top of the American League.

The player option structure also protects both sides. If Chisholm struggles, he doesn't get to opt out and test the open market — the club holds control. If he thrives, the player option represents fair value, and Chisholm can either stay in Toronto or bet on himself again in the winter of 2027.

What's Next

Chisholm's deal is pending a physical. With the lockout expected to take effect Dec. 2, Toronto completed this signing just inside the pre-freeze window. His 2027 season with the Blue Jays begins with spring training in February — assuming the lockout resolves before spring camp.
