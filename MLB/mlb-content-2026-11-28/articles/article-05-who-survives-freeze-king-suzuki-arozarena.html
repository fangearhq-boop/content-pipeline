Who's Still Unsigned When the Freeze Hits? The Hot Stove's Leftovers

By Ryan Calloway

November 28, 2026

The pre-lockout rush has claimed most of the marquee names. Tarik Skubal signed a nine-year, $450 million deal with the Mets. Freddy Peralta went to Baltimore. The Cubs added Trevor Rogers. Jazz Chisholm is a Blue Jay. Dustin May is a White Sox. Teams committed more than $1.7 billion in November.

And yet, three prominent players are likely to still be unsigned when the market freezes Dec. 2 — waiting out a lockout of unknown duration before the market reopens.

Michael King — The Last Elite Starter

Michael King turned down the San Diego Padres' $28 million qualifying offer. The 31-year-old right-hander posted a 3.21 ERA across 32 starts, making him the best starting pitcher left on a market that has already seen its top options claimed.

King's decision to decline $28 million signals confidence — his camp believes the open market will return something better. And it very well might have, in a normal offseason. But the lockout looming over Tuesday changes the calculation. If King doesn't sign before midnight Dec. 1, he enters an indefinite waiting period. Teams that need a starter will want to close quickly before the freeze.

The puzzle is that teams desperate enough to pay above the qualifying offer threshold are either out of money after the November spending surge, or uncertain about committing to a multi-year deal when the entire structure of the sport's business model is under negotiation. King may be the most affected player in this class by the lockout's timing.

Seiya Suzuki — Elite Contact Waiting for a Home

Seiya Suzuki entered this offseason as the top remaining position player on the market. His contact-first approach, strong outfield defense, and consistent production in both Japan and the United States made him appealing for teams with outfield needs or lineup gaps. Several clubs reported interest throughout November.

What's held Suzuki's market back may be a combination of factors: the qualifying offer attached to him requiring a draft pick cost, the overall depth of the outfield market this year, and teams redirecting resources to other needs. Regardless of the reason, Suzuki stands a real chance of entering the lockout period without a contract — waiting for a market that could emerge weeks or months from now.

Randy Arozarena — Power Bat Still Available

Randy Arozarena declined a $15.65 million option with the Seattle Mariners and entered free agency as one of the more intriguing power bats on the market. His track record in October specifically — multiple postseason series where he was the most dangerous hitter on the field — gives him an appeal beyond his regular-season numbers.

Like Suzuki, Arozarena's market hasn't materialized at the speed some expected. As the lockout closes in, he's another candidate to still be unsigned when transactions stop.

The 2021 Template and What to Expect

The 2021-22 lockout lasted 99 days — from Dec. 2 through March 10. During that period, the Commissioner's office could not communicate with players, teams could not negotiate with free agents, and the entire machinery of the sport's business sat dormant. When it ended, there was a compressed flurry of transactions: signings, trades, and extensions that had been backlogged for three months all processing in a matter of days.

If this lockout follows a similar timeline, players still unsigned on Dec. 2 could be waiting until March before they find contracts. With the 2027 Opening Day projected around March 25, there's a narrow window for a lockout that runs 99 days before games themselves would be threatened.

The players on the list above aren't minor free agents. They're established big-leaguers who deserve better than an indefinite holding pattern. But that's where the calendar has put them — 72 hours from a freeze they can't control.

What's Next

The CBA expires Tuesday, Dec. 1 at 11:59 p.m. ET. Teams have 72 hours to complete signings before the lockout freeze begins Dec. 2. King, Suzuki, and Arozarena are the most significant names who may still be waiting when the market stops.
