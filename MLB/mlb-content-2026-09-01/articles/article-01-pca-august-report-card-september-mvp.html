Pete Crow-Armstrong's August Was Historic. September Decides the MVP.

By Marcus Cole

September 1, 2026

Pete Crow-Armstrong just had one of the best individual months in the National League this season. In 27 August games, the Cubs center fielder hit 14 home runs and posted a 1.098 OPS — a stretch of sustained dominance that moved him from NL MVP contender to NL MVP frontrunner.

He enters September at .283, 38 home runs, 91 RBI, and 32 stolen bases. With 28 games remaining in the regular season, no one in the National League is producing a more complete line. The Cubs are 78-60, locked into one of the top two NL Wild Card seeds alongside the Phillies.

What August Looked Like

Fourteen home runs in 27 games. A 1.098 OPS. Those are the kinds of numbers that force the MVP conversation to shift — not just in volume, but in certainty. PCA entered August as a candidate. He exits it as the standard-setter.

The full-season line — .283 BA, 38 HR, 91 RBI, 32 SB — is unusual in its breadth. Home run hitters at this pace rarely contribute at the base-running level PCA does. Players who steal 30+ bases are rarely threatening 40 home runs. The combination places him in a category of his own among active NL position players this season.

The MVP Landscape

The primary competition for the NL MVP is Shohei Ohtani, whose two-way performance has been the benchmark of MVP conversations for years. The calculus is familiar: voters must weigh Ohtani's unique contribution as both a pitcher and hitter against a positional player who may lead in one dimension.

PCA's August may have made that calculus simpler. A position player who hits 38 home runs, drives in 91, steals 32 bases, and plays premium defense in center field — while contributing to a winning team — is the kind of candidate that MVP ballots are built for. The Cubs' Wild Card positioning gives him the team-success context that voters often require.

What September Needs to Look Like

The Cubs have 28 games remaining. PCA needs September to do two things: hold his statistical position and help the Cubs secure their seed. Every game down the stretch that Chicago wins contributes to the case. Every additional home run and RBI strengthens it.

History suggests that MVP races can shift dramatically in September — a cold stretch at the plate, an injury, or a late surge from a challenger can undo months of work. For PCA, the challenge is maintaining a pace through the final month that leaves no ambiguity for voters. August made the argument. September closes it.

What's Next

Pete Crow-Armstrong and the Cubs open September at 78-60, tied with the Phillies for the top two NL Wild Card seeds. The Cubs' regular season schedule runs through September 28 before the postseason begins. PCA's pursuit of 40 home runs — and the NL MVP — will be one of the central storylines of the final month of the 2026 regular season.
