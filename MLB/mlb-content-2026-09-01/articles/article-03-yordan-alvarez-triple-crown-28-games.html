Yordan Alvarez Is Leading Every Triple Crown Category. 28 Games Left.

By Ryan Calloway

September 1, 2026

Yordan Alvarez enters September leading all three American League Triple Crown categories. His .322 batting average leads the AL by 14 points. His 36 home runs lead the AL by one over Junior Caminero and Ben Rice. His 91 RBI are tied for the AL lead with Pete Alonso. Twenty-eight games remain in the regular season.

The last player to win the Triple Crown was Miguel Cabrera in 2012. That was 14 years ago. No one has done it since. Alvarez is close — but close in a Triple Crown race and close to actually winning one are very different things.

The Three Categories

The batting average lead is Alvarez's safest margin. A 14-point cushion over Chandler Simpson (.308) is substantial with less than a month to play. For Simpson to close that gap, he would need to sustain a significant hot stretch while Alvarez cools off. Possible, but not probable at this stage of the season.

The RBI category is a tie. Alvarez sits at 91 alongside Pete Alonso. Any RBI he drives in that Alonso does not immediately gives him the lead. Any Alonso produces without a matching Alvarez RBI puts Alonso ahead. The tied position means neither player carries a buffer.

Home runs are the most precarious. Alvarez has 36. Caminero and Rice both sit at 35. A one-HR lead over two challengers with 28 games left is a razor's edge. Either player could erase that margin in a single series. Alvarez was relatively quiet in August on the home run front — that's how the gap narrowed to one.

What Would a Triple Crown Mean

Cabrera's 2012 Triple Crown was the first in 45 years at the time — the previous winner was Carl Yastrzemski in 1967. In the fourteen seasons since Cabrera, no player has come close enough to generate this kind of sustained conversation through September. The achievement is rare because all three categories require different types of production. Power hitters run up RBI but don't always maintain average. High-contact hitters maintain average but don't always threaten for the home run title. The player who does both — and sustains it across 162 games — is exceptional by definition.

Alvarez at 29 is in the middle of his prime. An AL Triple Crown in 2026 would put him in a conversation that includes some of the best hitters the game has ever seen.

What's Next

Yordan Alvarez and the Astros play out the final 28 games of the regular season with the Triple Crown on the line in all three categories. The home run race is the one to watch most closely — any day Caminero or Rice goes deep while Alvarez doesn't changes the math immediately. Alvarez's next major benchmark is holding all three leads through the final two weeks of September. If he does, the conversation shifts from whether he can win the Triple Crown to when it gets formally confirmed.
