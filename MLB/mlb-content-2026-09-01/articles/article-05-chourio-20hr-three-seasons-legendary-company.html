Jackson Chourio Just Joined Ted Williams, Mickey Mantle, and Three Other Hall of Famers

By Jake Torres

September 1, 2026

When Jackson Chourio hit his 20th home run of the 2026 season on August 29 against the Texas Rangers, it marked more than a personal milestone. It made him only the 17th player in MLB history to post 20 or more home runs in each of his first three big-league seasons.

The company he joined: Ted Williams, Eddie Mathews, Mickey Mantle, Frank Robinson, and Orlando Cepeda — all Hall of Famers. The list is not long. Getting on it at 22 years old is something almost no one does.

What the Milestone Means

Three consecutive 20-homer seasons to open a career requires two things most young players don't have simultaneously: power at the big-league level and the durability to stay on the field for three full seasons before age 23. Most prospects who have the power don't stay healthy. Most who stay healthy don't yet have the power. Chourio has had both.

In 2024 — his first MLB season at age 20 — he hit 20 home runs. In 2025 at 21, he hit 20 or more again. Now in 2026 at 22, he's already hit 20 with time remaining in the season. The consistency is the point. Any player can have a great month. Doing it across three full seasons, across development, roster changes, and the natural adjustments pitchers make to young hitters, is harder to sustain.

In Brewers History

Chourio is also only the second player in Milwaukee Brewers franchise history to accomplish the feat in their first three seasons. The first was Ryan Braun, who powered through similar numbers at the start of his career before becoming the franchise's all-time home run leader.

The comparison is worth noting for what it says about Chourio's trajectory. Braun went on to hit 352 career home runs over 14 seasons with Milwaukee. Chourio, at 22, is several seasons away from that kind of career totals conversation — but the trajectory map has the same early shape.

The Player He Is Right Now

Beyond the home run milestone, Chourio's 2026 season has shown development at the plate. He is batting .282 with a .343 on-base percentage, 22 doubles, and 14 stolen bases through 101 games. The power is there. The plate discipline is improving. The speed element gives him a floor that pure power hitters don't have.

He also had a two-homer game on August 27 against the Cubs — two weeks before the milestone home run against Texas. The power is not coming in isolated bursts. It is sustained through a full season's workload.

What's Next

Jackson Chourio and the Brewers — who have had the best record in baseball for much of the second half — play out the final stretch of the regular season as the NL's top seed. For Chourio personally, the remaining games are an opportunity to push his season total beyond 25 home runs for the first time, and to continue building a career résumé that is already generating comparisons most players never earn before their mid-twenties.
