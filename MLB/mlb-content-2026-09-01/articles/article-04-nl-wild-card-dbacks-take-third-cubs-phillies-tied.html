Arizona Takes Third Wild Card. Cubs and Phillies Tied at 78-60. 28 Games Left.

By Marcus Cole

September 1, 2026

The NL Wild Card picture shifted on Sunday. Arizona surged past San Diego on August 31 to claim the third NL Wild Card spot, leaving the Padres a fraction of a game behind entering September. At the top of the table, the Cubs and Phillies both finished August at 78-60 — tied for the top two seeds, separated by nothing.

Twenty-eight games remain. Five teams still have meaningful stakes in the NL playoff picture. The month ahead will be dramatic.

Cubs: Seeding Is the Goal

The Cubs are already in. At 78-60, their Wild Card spot is all but secured. What remains is the question of seeding — specifically, whether Chicago can avoid a first-round matchup with Milwaukee.

The Brewers have had the best record in baseball for much of the second half and have effectively wrapped up the NL Central. If Milwaukee finishes with the league's best record, they earn the No. 1 seed and host the Wild Card round. A Cubs team that ends up as the No. 3 seed would potentially face the Brewers in the opening round — exactly what Chicago's front office has been working to avoid since the All-Star break.

The path to avoiding that matchup runs through September. Every Cubs win pushes the seeding picture in their favor.

The Third Spot: Arizona vs. San Diego

The most urgent race in the NL Wild Card picture is the battle for the third spot. Arizona's surge on August 31 moved them ahead of San Diego, but the margin is thin — fractions of a game separate the two teams entering September.

Both teams have work to do. The D-backs need to hold what they took. The Padres need to close ground immediately to avoid falling further behind before the final weeks. With 28 games remaining for each club, the third Wild Card is entirely in play.

What It Means for October

The NL Wild Card round is a best-of-three series hosted by the higher seed. Seeding matters. A No. 1 seed gets home games against the No. 6 seed. A No. 2 seed hosts the No. 5. The order in which the field is arranged comes down to how the next 28 games play out.

For the Cubs and Phillies, tied at the top, the tiebreaker in head-to-head matchups may ultimately determine who gets No. 1 and who gets No. 2. Both clubs know what the goal is. The schedule ahead will test both lineups across the final month.

What's Next

The Cubs and Phillies open September at 78-60, locked in a tie for the top two NL Wild Card seeds. Arizona holds the third spot over San Diego by a half game. The final 28 games of the regular season determine seeding, and for Chicago, the priority is keeping Milwaukee out of the first-round bracket if possible. The NL Wild Card race is the most competitive it has been since early summer.
