### STORY 1: MLB Files Federal Lawsuit Against MLBPA

#### Long-Form Post

Major League Baseball did not just prepare for a lockout this week. It filed for one in federal court.

MLB filed a lawsuit against the MLB Players Association in U.S. District Court in Manhattan, asking a judge to declare that an anticipated work stoppage would not violate federal antitrust laws and could not be stopped through a court injunction. The concern driving the lawsuit is that the union could disclaim its status as the players' collective bargaining representative — which would potentially allow players to file antitrust claims against the league and its 30 clubs.

Eight players from the MLBPA executive subcommittee were named as defendants, including newly signed Mets ace Tarik Skubal and Pirates pitcher Paul Skenes.

The union's response was immediate: "completely frivolous." The MLBPA said MLB's decision to run to court in the middle of active negotiations rather than bargain in good faith is "incredibly disappointing."

The CBA expires December 1. A lockout is expected December 2.

What do you think — is this a negotiating tactic or a genuine legal concern from the league?

### STORY 2: Skubal's $450M Deal Finalized

#### Long-Form Post

A day after finalizing the richest contract ever given to a pitcher, Tarik Skubal was named in a federal lawsuit by Major League Baseball.

That is the kind of week the new Mets ace is having.

Skubal's nine-year, $450 million deal with New York was finalized November 25, topping the previous record for a pitcher (Yoshinobu Yamamoto, $325M/12yr with the Dodgers). At $50 million per year on average, Skubal becomes the highest-paid pitcher in baseball history. The contract includes a full no-trade clause and $30 million in deferred payments.

The numbers on the field justify it. Over his last four seasons combined, Skubal has gone 43-17 with a 2.42 ERA. He is a two-time Cy Young Award winner and a two-time All-Star. He finished the 2026 season with the Dodgers after being traded from Detroit at the deadline.

The Mets, despite spending $366 million on payroll in 2026 and finishing last in the NL East at 74-88, are betting that Skubal gives them the kind of rotation anchor they have never had. When you're committing $450 million, you believe you're getting it.

The lawsuit came next.

### STORY 3: 5 Days to Lockout

#### Long-Form Post

Five days. That's how much time is left before the collective bargaining agreement expires and, almost certainly, baseball enters a lockout.

The CBA between MLB and the Players Association expires at 11:59 PM ET on December 1. Unless a last-minute deal materializes — which almost every analyst covering the negotiations views as extremely unlikely — Commissioner Rob Manfred will impose a lockout at 12:01 AM on December 2. All player activity would immediately freeze: no trades, no free-agent signings, no contract extensions, no communication between players and teams.

That means the top unsigned names on the market have five days to find a landing spot.

Jazz Chisholm (.231/19HR/41SB, 2026) has drawn interest from more than 10 teams, with the Toronto Blue Jays reported as the frontrunner. Corey Seager has reportedly said he would waive his full no-trade clause for the Philadelphia Phillies, but a pre-deadline deal is considered unlikely. Seiya Suzuki and Randy Arozarena are also still on the board.

The rush is on. The window is closing.

### STORY 4: Pre-Lockout Blitz

#### Long-Form Post

Teams aren't waiting.

With the December 1 CBA deadline creating an urgency that has not been seen since the last lockout in 2021-22, clubs are signing available players at a pace that mirrors the final days before that work stoppage began. In the last 24-48 hours alone:

— Kevin Gausman agreed to a one-year, $25 million deal with the Detroit Tigers
— Gary Sanchez signed a one-year, $6.5 million contract with the Miami Marlins
— Left-handed reliever JoJo Romero agreed to terms with the San Diego Padres
— Bryan Ginkel agreed to a two-year, $18 million deal

This is exactly how the 2021 pre-lockout market moved: deals piling up rapidly as teams secured what they could before the freeze. That window saw more than $1.2 billion spent in the week before the CBA expired.

For teams with remaining needs and the financial flexibility to act, the next five days are as important as any in the offseason. After December 1, the roster you have is the roster you're stuck with — however long that turns out to be.

### STORY 5: What Would a 2026 Lockout Mean?

#### Long-Form Post

If you've been here before, you know what's coming.

The 2021-22 MLB lockout lasted 99 days. It canceled spring training games, delayed opening day by one week, and shortened the regular season schedule. It was the sport's first work stoppage in 26 years. Now, five years later, the league and the Players Association are back at the same impasse over the same core issue: the salary cap.

MLB has proposed a cap-and-floor system, with the most recent offer delaying its implementation until 2028, setting the cap at $258.3 million and the floor at $180.2 million, and guaranteeing all contracts under it. The union's position has not changed: a salary cap is a nonstarter. MLBPA executive director Bruce Meyer has used that exact word repeatedly.

When both sides are at this distance and the deadline is five days away, the history here is not encouraging. The 2021 lockout took 99 days to resolve. It required the involvement of a federal mediator and lasted past the original opening day target.

A lockout starting December 2 would immediately freeze all player movement. No trades. No signings. No free-agent negotiations. Players and teams cannot even communicate. The 2027 season's opening day is March 25, giving the parties roughly 115 days to reach a deal before games are threatened.

If the 2021 lockout is the template, the sport is in for a long winter.
