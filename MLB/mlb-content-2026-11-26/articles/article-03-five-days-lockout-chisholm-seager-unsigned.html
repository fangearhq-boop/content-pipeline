5 Days to Lockout: Jazz Chisholm, Seager, Suzuki, Arozarena Still Searching for a Home

By Ryan Calloway | November 26, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The collective bargaining agreement between Major League Baseball and its players expires in five days. When it does, all player movement stops.

No free-agent signings. No trades. No contract extensions. No player-team communications of any kind. The market freezes the moment the lockout begins — expected at 12:01 AM on December 2 — and stays frozen until the two sides reach a new deal. Given that the central dispute is a salary cap the union has called a nonstarter, that freeze could last weeks or months.

That leaves the top unsigned free agents with a narrow and rapidly closing window to find a landing spot before the baseball world goes dark.

Jazz Chisholm: The Hottest Name on the Board

Jazz Chisholm is the most high-profile unsigned player on the market. The dynamic infielder-turned-outfielder posted a .231 batting average with 19 home runs and 41 stolen bases in 2026, combining power and speed at a position of broad demand. More than 10 teams have engaged in discussions with Chisholm's representatives, per reports from Jon Morosi of MLB Network.

The Toronto Blue Jays have been identified as the frontrunner. Toronto met formally with Chisholm's representation during the GM Meetings earlier this month. The fit makes sense: the Blue Jays need a dynamic, high-ceiling player at the top of their lineup and in their outfield, and Chisholm's combination of athleticism and offensive potential addresses both. The Yankees, who employed Chisholm earlier in his career, have reportedly not shown serious interest in a reunion.

With five days remaining, the expectation is that a deal gets done before the lockout. The question is where.

Corey Seager: The Trade That Probably Won't Happen in Time

Corey Seager is a different kind of situation. He is under contract — a 10-and-5 player with a full no-trade clause — and would need to waive that clause for any deal to go forward. Reports from Philadelphia-based baseball writer Jim Salisbury and ESPN's Alden Gonzalez indicate that the Phillies have interest and that Seager would likely waive his clause for them. The Rangers have been open to dealing him.

But the complexity of the transaction — financial restructuring, the NTC waiver process, agreement between three parties — has led multiple reports to describe a pre-lockout deal as "unlikely." The more probable timeline has the trade continuing as a discussion topic after the lockout ends and free movement resumes, however long that takes.

Seager batted .227/.312/.424 in 2026 and has $155 million remaining on his contract.

The Others Still on the Board

Seiya Suzuki, the Cubs outfielder who opt-out language in his contract made a free agent this winter, remains unsigned and drawing interest from multiple teams. Randy Arozarena is also still available, as are several mid-tier starters and relievers who chose not to sign before the market accelerated in the past week.

For all of them, the calculus is the same: sign before December 1, or wait through an indeterminate lockout and re-enter a market that may look very different on the other side.

What's Next

The CBA expires December 1 at 11:59 PM ET. A lockout is expected to begin December 2 at 12:01 AM. Any player who does not have a signed contract before that moment cannot receive one until the lockout ends. The 2021-22 lockout provides the clearest precedent: 99 days elapsed before a new deal was reached. If that timeline repeats, the market would not reopen until early March — just weeks before the expected start of spring training for position players.
