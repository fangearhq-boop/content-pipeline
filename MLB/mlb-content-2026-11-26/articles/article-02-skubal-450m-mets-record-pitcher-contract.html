Skubal's $450M Mets Deal Finalized: The Record Contract That Reshaped Baseball's Pitching Market

By Jake Torres | November 26, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

When Yoshinobu Yamamoto signed a 12-year, $325 million deal with the Los Angeles Dodgers, baseball insiders expected the record for a pitcher would stand for years.

It lasted less than two seasons.

Tarik Skubal finalized a nine-year, $450 million contract with the New York Mets on November 25 — the richest contract ever given to a pitcher in baseball history. At $50 million per year on average, Skubal surpasses not just Yamamoto's total value but also his average annual value. The deal includes a full no-trade clause and $30 million in deferred compensation structured to pay out between 2038 and 2046.

The Case for the Contract

The numbers that justified a half-billion-dollar commitment are real. Over his last four seasons combined, Skubal has gone 43-17 with a 2.42 earned run average. He is a two-time Cy Young Award winner, a two-time All-Star, and one of a small handful of pitchers in the sport capable of carrying a rotation by himself.

Skubal finished the 2026 season with the Los Angeles Dodgers after being acquired at the trade deadline from the Detroit Tigers. Because he was traded rather than released, he was ineligible for a qualifying offer from Los Angeles — meaning the Mets owe no compensatory draft pick for signing him. That provision, combined with his availability as a free agent in a thin pitching market, gave New York a rare window to acquire an elite starter without attached cost.

What It Means for the Mets

Context matters: the Mets finished last in the National League East in 2026, posting a 74-88 record despite the second-highest luxury tax payroll in baseball at $366 million. That is not a foundation that inspires confidence in a half-billion-dollar investment.

But the Mets' logic is straightforward, even if the price is staggering. Without an elite ace, their spending is scattered across supplementary players who collectively underperform. With Skubal anchoring a rotation that also includes Nolan McLean and Christian Scott — two promising young starters — the equation changes. A rotation built around a true No. 1 starter is fundamentally different from one that isn't.

The Mets owners expressed publicly that they were "thrilled" to add Skubal. That enthusiasm will be tested over nine years if the rotation around him does not develop into genuine contenders.

The Lawsuit Footnote

The day after Skubal finalized his record deal, MLB named him as a defendant in its federal lawsuit against the Players Association. As a member of the MLBPA executive subcommittee, Skubal holds a formal union leadership position — which is why his name appears in the complaint alongside seven other players, including Paul Skenes of the Pittsburgh Pirates.

The irony is not subtle: the sport's highest-paid pitcher, freshly signed to a record contract by a team that is part of MLB's ownership group, is simultaneously named as a defendant by that same ownership group. It is the kind of contradiction that only a labor dispute can produce.

What's Next

Skubal will be formally introduced as a Met once the team schedules a press conference. His contract is pending a successful physical. The CBA expires December 1, and if a lockout begins December 2 as expected, all player-team communications will freeze — though Skubal's deal was finalized before that deadline. The 2027 Mets rotation will be built around Skubal alongside McLean, Scott, Sean Manaea, and Kodai Senga. Spring training in Port St. Lucie is expected to begin in mid-February, assuming a lockout does not delay it.
