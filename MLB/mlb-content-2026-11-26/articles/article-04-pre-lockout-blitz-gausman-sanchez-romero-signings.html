Pre-Lockout Blitz: Gausman to Tigers, Sanchez to Marlins, Romero to Padres as Teams Race the Clock

By Marcus Cole | November 26, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The market is moving. Teams know why.

With the collective bargaining agreement set to expire December 1 and a lockout expected to follow hours later, clubs are accelerating their free-agent activity at a pace that mirrors the final week before the 2021-22 work stoppage began. In the last 48 hours alone, multiple signings landed across the league as teams secured players they want before baseball goes into a freeze that could last weeks or months.

The Moves

Kevin Gausman — Detroit Tigers, one year, $25 million. The veteran right-hander brings experience and innings capacity to a Tigers rotation that has been building steadily around young arms. Gausman, whose splitter remains one of the most effective pitches in the game, gives Detroit a reliable No. 2 or No. 3 option behind their young core. The one-year structure at $25 million is market-rate for a pitcher of his profile.

Gary Sanchez — Miami Marlins, one year, $6.5 million. The veteran catcher adds depth and offensive potential to a Marlins roster that has been in rebuild mode. Sanchez, whose career has been defined by big power numbers alongside defensive questions, provides a low-risk, high-upside option for Miami. The Marlins have invested in young pitching and need someone capable of handling the staff.

JoJo Romero — San Diego Padres. The left-handed reliever agreed to terms with San Diego, giving the Padres bullpen depth from the left side. Terms were not publicly disclosed as of the agreement's announcement, but San Diego has been active in building out their bullpen ahead of what they hope will be a postseason run in 2027.

Bryan Ginkel — two years, $18 million. The reliever agreed to a two-year commitment worth $18 million. The multi-year structure is a reflection of his value on the market and of the team's desire to lock in an arm before the lockout freeze removes the ability to do so.

The Pattern

None of these moves would look out of place on a typical November offseason day. What is not typical is the volume and pace. Deals that in another year might have been spread across two or three weeks of deliberate negotiation are being compressed into a 72-hour window defined entirely by the looming December 1 deadline.

The 2021 pre-lockout market provides the precedent. In the week before the last CBA expired, more than $1.2 billion was committed across the league in a rush that caught some teams unprepared. Players and agents who waited — hoping for better offers later — found themselves negotiating into a lockout with no deal on the table.

That lesson is not lost on this year's market participants. When the deadline is real, speed is leverage.

What's Next

The pre-lockout signing window closes December 1 at 11:59 PM ET. Teams with remaining free-agent needs — pitching, infield depth, bullpen arms — have five days to close any remaining business. The top unsigned names still on the market include Jazz Chisholm, Seiya Suzuki, Randy Arozarena, and Corey Seager (trade). After December 1, all player movement freezes until a new CBA is ratified.
