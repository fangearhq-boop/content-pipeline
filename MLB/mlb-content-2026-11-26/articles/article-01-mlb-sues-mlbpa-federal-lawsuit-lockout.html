MLB Files Federal Lawsuit Against MLBPA to Shield Anticipated Lockout From Antitrust Challenge

By Marcus Cole | November 26, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Major League Baseball is not simply preparing for a lockout. It is suing for the right to impose one without legal interference.

MLB filed a federal lawsuit against the MLB Players Association in U.S. District Court in Manhattan this week, asking a judge to declare that an anticipated work stoppage would not violate federal antitrust laws and could not be stopped through a court injunction. The filing came five days before the current collective bargaining agreement expires on December 1 at 11:59 PM Eastern time — and four days before a lockout is widely expected to begin at 12:01 AM on December 2.

The union called the suit "completely frivolous." The league called it a legal safeguard. And eight players, including newly signed Mets ace Tarik Skubal and Pirates pitcher Paul Skenes, found themselves named as defendants.

What MLB Is Asking For

The core of the lawsuit is a preemptive declaration. MLB wants a federal judge to rule, before any work stoppage begins, that a lockout is a legal labor action protected by federal labor law and cannot be challenged as an antitrust violation by players or the union.

The league's underlying concern: if the MLBPA were to disclaim its status as the players' collective bargaining representative — formally dissolving the union as a bargaining entity — individual players might then have standing to file antitrust claims against MLB and its 30 clubs under the Sherman Antitrust Act. That is a scenario that has played out in other sports, most notably in the NFL in 2011, when the players' union decertified ahead of that league's lockout to pursue antitrust claims.

By filing first and asking for the declaration now, MLB is attempting to remove that legal avenue before the union can consider using it.

Who Is Named

The complaint names the MLBPA itself and eight members of the union's executive subcommittee — the governing body that makes decisions on behalf of the full player union. Among the named players are Tarik Skubal, the two-time Cy Young Award winner who finalized a nine-year, $450 million contract with the Mets just the day before the suit was filed, and Paul Skenes, the Pittsburgh Pirates ace who is under team control and not a free agent.

Neither player violated any rule by being named. They are on the executive subcommittee as player representatives — the roles that come with their union leadership positions. But the optics of suing the game's newest $450 million pitcher within 24 hours of him signing his deal are not lost on anyone watching the labor situation unfold.

The Union's Response

The MLBPA did not mince words. The union called the lawsuit "completely frivolous" and said MLB's decision to pursue legal action in the middle of ongoing negotiations rather than bargain in good faith is "incredibly disappointing."

The union's position throughout negotiations has been consistent: the players will not accept a salary cap under any framing or timeline. MLBPA executive director Bruce Meyer has described any cap structure as a nonstarter. Legally, the union believes that an anticipated lockout — imposed by the owners, not by the players striking — cannot be an antitrust violation because it is a permissible lockout under existing labor law.

The lawsuit, in the union's view, is a pressure tactic designed to intimidate rather than a genuine legal concern. The league disagrees.

What's Next

The CBA expires December 1 at 11:59 PM Eastern time. Unless a new deal is reached before then — which virtually no one close to the negotiations expects — MLB will impose a lockout at 12:01 AM on December 2. All player movement will freeze: no trades, no free-agent signings, no extensions, no player-team communications. The federal lawsuit will proceed through the courts on its own timeline, separate from bargaining. Both sides have indicated they will continue negotiating even after a lockout begins, though the 2021 precedent suggests resolution could take months.
