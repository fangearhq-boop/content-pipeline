Robert Gasser's Quiet Masterpiece: Six Innings, One Hit, Zero Runs in World Series Game 3

By Jake Torres | October 27, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Everyone will remember the eight-run fifth inning. The seven consecutive two-out hits. Jackson Chourio going 4-for-5. These are the memorable things from World Series Game 3.

But behind all of it — foundational to everything that followed — was Robert Gasser. Six innings. One hit. Zero runs. In the biggest game of his career, in the World Series, at Tropicana Field, Gasser was exactly what Milwaukee needed him to be.

Command, Movement, and Composure

Gasser is not a power pitcher. He doesn't throw 103.5 mph like Jacob Misiorowski. He doesn't overpower lineups with pure stuff the way Drew Rasmussen can on his best nights. What Gasser does is something different: he commands his fastball to both sides of the plate, works multiple pitches in any count, and never gives opposing lineups a predictable look.

Against Tampa Bay on Monday, that approach produced one of the most efficient pitching performances of the 2026 postseason. He navigated a Rays lineup that had scored 13 runs in Games 1 and 2 combined without allowing them to threaten. His one allowed hit came in the middle innings, isolated and stranded.

A Postseason Built on Big Moments

Monday wasn't Gasser's first October statement. In NLCS Game 3 against the Los Angeles Dodgers, he pitched five scoreless innings, allowing just three hits, as the Brewers took a 3-0 lead in the series. That performance put the Dodgers on the brink of elimination.

His postseason ERA, factoring in all appearances, now sits below 1.00 across the Milwaukee run. In a rotation that has gotten significant attention for Misiorowski's velocity records and Peralta's storyline as a returning ex-Brewer, Gasser has quietly been the most consistent arm. He's 25 years old and pitching like a veteran who has done this before.

The Starter Who Made the Blowout Possible

An eight-run inning only happens when the starter keeps the game close long enough for it to matter. If Gasser had given up three runs early, the fifth-inning rally would have been a comeback rather than a historic blowout. Instead, he gave Milwaukee a 2-0 lead to work with, and kept it there through six innings.

The records that were set Monday night belong to the whole lineup. The platform for those records was built by one pitcher, quietly doing his job in one of the biggest moments of his career.

What's Next

Gasser is not expected to start Game 4 tonight, as his work is done after 97 pitches on Monday. Milwaukee will lean on its bullpen — one of the deepest in the game — to close out the Rays at Tropicana Field. First pitch is 8 PM ET on FOX.

If the Brewers win their first World Series tonight, Gasser's Game 3 start will be remembered as a crucial foundation. Quiet performances like his don't always get the credit they deserve in the moment. They always show up in the final box score.
