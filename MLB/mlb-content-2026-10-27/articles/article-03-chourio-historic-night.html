Jackson Chourio Goes 4-for-5 in Game 3: Youngest Player With 4 WS Hits Since 1946

By Ryan Calloway | October 27, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

When the Milwaukee Brewers scored eight runs in the fifth inning Monday night at Tropicana Field, the historical footnotes were about two-out hits and two-out runs. Those records, as significant as they are, almost obscured another piece of history being made in the same game.

Jackson Chourio went 4-for-5. He added a double, stole a base, and became the youngest player to record four hits in a World Series game since Joe Garagiola did it in 1946 — 80 years ago.

A Night to Remember

Chourio turned 22 earlier this year and has spent the postseason systematically building a case for himself as the best young player in the American League — and maybe in all of baseball. In the NLCS, he set a Milwaukee franchise record for postseason home runs. In the World Series, he has recorded multiple hits in each of the first three games, with a combined line that would be impressive for any player at any stage of their career.

His 4-for-5 performance in Game 3 brought him to a level historically reserved for baseball legends. Joe Garagiola, who had four hits in a 1946 World Series game for the St. Louis Cardinals, was 20 years old. Chourio, at 22, is the youngest to match that feat in eight decades.

A October Built on Consistency

What makes Chourio's World Series special isn't just the individual performance — it's the consistency. Multiple hits in Game 1. Multiple hits in Game 2. Multiple hits in Game 3. No player in this World Series has been more reliable than the Brewers' young outfielder.

Chourio is the 26th player in baseball history to record multiple hits in each of the first three games of a World Series. He joined that list at an age when most players are still trying to establish themselves at the major-league level.

The Making of an October Star

The Brewers have a lineup full of contributors in this World Series. William Contreras leads all postseason players with 18 hits — the most in Milwaukee playoff history. Jackson Chourio is right there with him, adding the youth and energy that makes the Brewers' offense feel limitless.

The best part for Milwaukee fans: Chourio is 22. He will be back next October, and the October after that. The Brewers may have found something rare — a player who rises to the biggest moments and delivers consistently, at an age when the franchise can build around him for years.

What's Next

Game 4 of the 2026 World Series is tonight at Tropicana Field, 8 PM ET on FOX. If Milwaukee wins, Chourio will have his first World Series ring — at 22. If the series extends, he'll have more chances to add to a statistical legacy that's already writing itself into the record books.

Whatever happens next, Jackson Chourio's October 2026 will be remembered for a long time in Milwaukee.
