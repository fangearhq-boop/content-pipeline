Brewers 10, Rays 1: Historic 8-Run Fifth Puts Milwaukee One Win From First World Series Title

By Marcus Cole | October 27, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The Milwaukee Brewers didn't just win World Series Game 3 on Monday night. They made history.

Milwaukee beat the Tampa Bay Rays 10-1 at Tropicana Field, taking a 3-0 series lead and putting the franchise one win away from its first World Series championship. But the game will be remembered not for the final score, but for what happened in one fifth-inning eruption: seven consecutive two-out hits and eight two-out runs in a single frame — both World Series records.

An Inning That Rewrote the Record Books

Through four innings, Game 3 was a tightly contested affair. Milwaukee led 2-0 heading into the fifth, but a 2-0 lead at Tropicana felt tenuous against a Rays lineup that had scored 14 runs in the first two games combined. Then came the turning point.

A replay review overturned a double-play call at second base on a hustle play by Christian Yelich. The ruling left the bases loaded with two outs, and what followed was a sequence that no team in World Series history had ever managed: seven consecutive hits, all with two outs, all while the pressure was on Milwaukee to extend. Eight runs scored. The Brewers turned a 2-0 lead into a 10-0 advantage in one inning, all with two outs on the scoreboard.

Eight two-out runs in a single inning is a World Series record. Seven consecutive two-out hits is a World Series record. Both fell on Monday night in St. Petersburg.

Gasser Delivers Six Shutdown Innings

Before the historic fifth-inning rally, Robert Gasser had been quietly magnificent. The 25-year-old left-hander worked through Tampa Bay's lineup with precision — one hit through six innings, no walks, no runs. His final line: 6 IP, 1 H, 0 R. It was the kind of start that doesn't grab headlines when your offense scores ten, but it was the foundation on which the blowout was built.

Gasser's postseason ERA now sits below 1.00 across the full Milwaukee run. He delivered five scoreless innings against the Dodgers in NLCS Game 3, and he delivered again Monday. He has been the most consistent starter not named Misiorowski in Milwaukee's October rotation.

Chourio: 4-for-5, Historic Again

Jackson Chourio continued his remarkable World Series with a 4-for-5 night, including a double and a stolen base. He has now recorded multiple hits in each of the first three games of the 2026 Fall Classic. At 22 years old, he became the youngest player to record four hits in a World Series game since Joe Garagiola did it in 1946 — a span of 80 years.

William Contreras added three hits to bring his postseason total to 18 — the most hits by any player in Milwaukee postseason history.

The Rays' Only Run

Tampa Bay's lone run came in the ninth inning on an RBI double by Diaz, well after the outcome was decided. The Rays are now 0-3 in the series and have been outscored 18-4 across three games. They have never won a World Series, and they now face the bleakest possible statistical outlook.

The sellout crowd of 40,388 at Tropicana Field — the venue's first World Series crowd since 2008 — witnessed the Rays' season hanging by a thread.

What's Next

World Series Game 4 is tonight at Tropicana Field, with first pitch scheduled for 8 PM ET on FOX. Milwaukee needs one win. No team in World Series history has ever come back from a 3-0 series deficit. The Brewers' bullpen is fully rested after throwing minimal pitches Monday.

Games 5 through 7, if necessary, would also be at Tropicana Field (Oct. 28-30) and American Family Field (Oct. 31, Nov. 1). But if Milwaukee wins tonight, the 2026 World Series is over — and the city of Milwaukee gets its first championship in franchise history.
