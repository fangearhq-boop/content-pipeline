World Series Game 5 Preview: Brewers One Win From First Title as Misiorowski Takes the Mound

By Ryan Calloway | October 28, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The Milwaukee Brewers are one win from the first World Series championship in franchise history. Game 5 is Wednesday night at Tropicana Field in St. Petersburg, with first pitch at 8 PM ET on FOX. Jacob Misiorowski starts for Milwaukee in what could be the deciding game of the 2026 Fall Classic.

The Brewers lead the series 3-1 after Tampa Bay survived elimination with a walk-off win in 12 innings on Tuesday night. That resilience buys the Rays one more game. It does not change the mountain they face.

Misiorowski Gets the Ball Again

There is no mystery about why the Brewers are sending Misiorowski to the mound with a championship on the line. He led the entire Major Leagues in ERA (1.80) and strikeouts (252) during the regular season. In Game 1 of this World Series, he threw five scoreless innings and registered a 103.5 mph pitch — the fastest in the Statcast era for a World Series game.

His postseason ERA heading into Game 5 is under 1.00 across his previous starts. He is pitching on regular rest. He is 25 years old and has been the best pitcher in baseball for most of the calendar year.

If there is a more favorable situation for a starting pitcher in a clinching game, it is hard to construct one.

The Rays' Problem

Tampa Bay survived Tuesday thanks to the kind of situational baseball that has defined this franchise for two decades. A 12th-inning walk-off, with the wrong lineup spot coming up and the season on the line, and somehow the Rays manufactured two runs to win 4-3. That kind of late-inning resilience is why this team has won more than anyone expected when October arrived.

But tonight is different. The Rays do not have a fully rested starter to match Misiorowski. Shane McClanahan is on the injured list with a forearm issue and will not pitch in this Series. The bullpen carried a significant load Tuesday night. Facing the best pitcher in baseball on limited rest, with no healthy ace, is an enormous ask.

The betting line reflects the gap: Brewers -147, Rays +119. The over/under sits at 6.5 runs.

History and What It Would Mean

The Milwaukee Brewers have never won the World Series. They reached the Fall Classic in 1982 and lost in seven games. They came close in 2011 in the NLCS and in various strong regular-season years since. Every October has ended without the final celebration.

Tonight could end that. The Brewers have the series lead, the starting pitcher, and the lineup depth to close this out. If they do, Jackson Chourio, Misiorowski, and a roster that was built through one of the more patient and deliberate development processes in modern baseball will celebrate the franchise's first title.

What's Next

Game 5 is tonight at 8 PM ET on FOX and FOX Deportes at Tropicana Field. If the Rays extend the series, Game 6 would be Friday, October 30, and Game 7 on Saturday, October 31, both in Milwaukee. The Brewers would prefer to end this tonight.
