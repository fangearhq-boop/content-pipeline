Jacob Misiorowski Starts World Series Game 5: The Best Pitcher of 2026 Has the Ball

By Ryan Calloway | October 28, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Jacob Misiorowski pitched in the World Series opener and the Brewers won. Now the Brewers want a title, and they are giving him the ball again.

The 25-year-old right-hander starts Game 5 tonight at Tropicana Field on regular rest. He led the Major Leagues in ERA (1.80) and strikeouts (252) during the regular season. He has spent all of October reminding anyone who was not paying attention during the summer why those numbers matter.

What He Did in the Regular Season

Leading the MLB in two of the most important pitching categories is rare. ERA and strikeouts in the same season, for the same pitcher — it happens, but not often. Misiorowski did it while pitching for a team that had genuine World Series aspirations, which means he did it under pressure, in high-leverage starts, against lineups that were prepared for him.

He missed time early in the season due to shoulder inflammation but returned to pitch at a level that made everything before his absence look like a warmup.

The World Series G1 Record

In Game 1 of this World Series, Misiorowski registered a 103.5 mph pitch — the hardest throw ever recorded in the Statcast era for a World Series game. The Brewers won Game 1 behind his five scoreless innings.

His postseason ERA heading into tonight sits under 1.00. Across multiple starts, facing lineups that have had time to prepare and scout him, he has been nearly unhittable. His whiff rate has declined from the regular season, as expected for postseason hitters, but the movement and velocity have remained elite.

The Matchup Tonight

The Rays do not have a fully rested starter to oppose him. Shane McClanahan is on the injured list. Tampa Bay's bullpen worked into the 12th inning on Tuesday night. The mismatch in starting pitching tonight is among the starkest the World Series has produced in years.

That does not mean the Brewers win. Games do not get played on paper. But it does mean the Rays need to score against one of the hardest pitchers to score against in baseball — with a bullpen running low after a draining extra-innings game less than 24 hours ago.

What's Next

If Misiorowski delivers tonight and the Brewers win Game 5, he will have delivered the clinching-game start for a team that has never won a World Series title. That is the full scope of what tonight means for him and for Milwaukee. First pitch at 8 PM ET on FOX.
