Rays Walk Off Brewers in 12 Innings to Force World Series Game 5

By Marcus Cole | October 28, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The Tampa Bay Rays are not done yet. They walked off the Milwaukee Brewers Tuesday night at Tropicana Field, beating them 4-3 in 12 innings to force a Game 5. Ben Williamson's single ended it — a walk-off that sent the Tropicana crowd into a frenzy and kept Tampa Bay's championship dream alive for one more night.

It was the kind of October game that gets replayed in highlight reels. A Brewers lead surrendered. A 12th-inning home run to take it back. And then the Rays responding with five consecutive plate appearances — error, single, single, single, walk-off single — to do the impossible.

The 12th Inning

Cooper Pratt led off the top of the 12th with a solo home run to give Milwaukee a 3-2 lead. With the Rays down to their final six outs, that felt like the killing blow. The Brewers had the lead. The bullpen was locked in. All they needed was six outs.

They could not get them.

The Rays started the bottom of the 12th with what looked like a routine defensive error — then a single. Then another single. Then another. Five batters reached base in sequence, with each advancing the go-ahead run. Williamson's single was the fifth and final, a quiet hit that carried the weight of an entire season.

Final: Rays 4, Brewers 3. World Series: Brewers lead 3-1.

The Rays Keep Defying Expectations

This is not a team with a marquee offense. The Rays have won all October by stringing together exactly these kinds of innings — multiple consecutive baserunners, patient at-bats, manufacturing runs without a single defining home run or star moment. David Hamilton had been part of the Brewers' near-miss earlier in the game, contributing both an error defensively and a key offensive contribution that underscored how many players have factored into this series.

Tampa Bay became just the fifth team in World Series history to win Game 4 after trailing 3-0. Whether that becomes a footnote or a part of a legendary comeback remains to be seen. The Rays still trail by a game and face Jacob Misiorowski tonight. The math has not changed.

What This Means for Game 5

The Brewers had a chance to win it in Game 4 and could not. That matters psychologically. The Rays proved they can manufacture runs against Milwaukee's bullpen in extra innings. But tonight's challenge is entirely different: Misiorowski is starting, fully rested, with a chance to clinch the franchise's first title.

The Rays need to match that energy. They need another game like Tuesday night. They need five more consecutive hits at exactly the right moment.

What's Next

Game 5 is tonight at Tropicana Field, 8 PM ET on FOX. The Brewers lead 3-1 and start their best pitcher. The Rays need to do what they did Tuesday — and do it again against a fully rested Misiorowski. The margin for error has disappeared.
