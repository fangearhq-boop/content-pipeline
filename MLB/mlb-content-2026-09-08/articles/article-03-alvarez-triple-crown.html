Fourteen Years Later: Yordan Alvarez Is Chasing the AL Triple Crown

By Ryan Calloway | September 8, 2026

Houston Astros

The last American League Triple Crown was Miguel Cabrera, Detroit Tigers, 2012. That was fourteen years ago. Yordan Alvarez, entering September 8 with approximately twenty games left in the regular season, leads the AL in batting average, home runs, and RBIs. All three categories. At the same time.

The Triple Crown is the rarest statistical achievement in baseball's positional game — rarer than a batting title, rarer than a home run crown. To lead all three simultaneously requires sustained excellence across multiple disciplines for six full months. Alvarez has done exactly that.

The Numbers

As of September 7, Alvarez's Triple Crown numbers are approximately this: batting average around .314 to .317, leading Chandler Simpson by roughly five to nine points. Home runs around 37 to 38, tied with Tampa Bay's Junior Caminero. RBI around 94 to 95, tied with Baltimore's Pete Alonso.

None of those leads are comfortable. In batting average, a cold week and a hot week from Simpson could erase the gap entirely. In home runs and RBI, Alvarez is tied — one big series from Caminero or Alonso reshuffles the board. The Triple Crown at this point in the season is more a daily race than a settled achievement.

What Makes This Difficult

The Triple Crown requires protecting three leads simultaneously, which means every category matters on every night. A walk that prevents an RBI. A strikeout that drops the batting average. A pitcher who neutralizes the power. No hitter controls all three levers perfectly across 20 games in September, when pitching staffs are at their deepest and opposing managers are managing matchups aggressively.

Cabrera held all three leads in 2012 because he was both dominant and fortunate — his challengers faded at the right time. Alvarez needs the same combination: continued quality at-bats and a little September luck.

The Historical Weight

Triple Crown winners are a short list in the modern era. Frank Robinson. Carl Yastrzemski. Cabrera. The achievement crosses eras because the skill set required has never been easy — hit for average, hit for power, and drive in runs at the highest rate in the league, all in the same season. In an era of launch-angle optimization and shifting defensive alignments, the batting average component is arguably harder than it was in Yastrzemski's time.

If Alvarez finishes this season leading the AL in all three, he joins a conversation that spans baseball history.

What's Next

Approximately twenty games remain in the regular season, ending September 27. Alvarez and the Astros will play out the schedule as Houston determines its postseason positioning. Every Alvarez plate appearance from here is a Triple Crown at-bat. The race in each category is close enough that the final standings won't be clear until the final days of the season.
