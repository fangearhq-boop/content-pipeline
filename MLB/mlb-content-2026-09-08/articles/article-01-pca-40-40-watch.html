Seven Steals Away: Pete Crow-Armstrong Is Chasing the 40-40 Club

By Marcus Cole | September 8, 2026

Chicago Cubs

On September 6, Pete Crow-Armstrong drove a 403-foot home run to right field against the Miami Marlins — his 40th of the season. The Cubs fell in that game anyway, but the number mattered more than the result. Forty home runs. Thirty-three stolen bases. Seven steals needed. Roughly eighteen games to get them.

The 40-40 club has had exactly six members in the history of Major League Baseball. Jose Canseco in 1988. Barry Bonds in 1996. Alex Rodriguez in 1998. Alfonso Soriano in 2006. Ronald Acuña Jr. in 2023. Shohei Ohtani in 2024. PCA is positioned to become No. 7.

What No. 40 Meant

The home run itself was a game-tying hit in the top of the fifth inning. Chicago still lost to Miami, but reaching 40 HR unlocked a different conversation — one that had been building since PCA crossed 35 home runs in August and started running more aggressively on the bases.

At 24 years, 165 days old, Crow-Armstrong is now the youngest Cub ever to hit 40 home runs in a season, passing Ernie Banks, who accomplished the feat at 24 years, 214 days in 1955. He's also the first Cub to reach 40 home runs since Derrek Lee in 2005, and the fourth player aged 24 or younger in MLB history to put together a 40 HR and 30-plus stolen base season — joining Acuña, Rodriguez, and Canseco.

The Math on 40-40

Seven stolen bases in eighteen games is not a given. PCA steals at a high clip when he's going, but stolen base totals fluctuate with game situations, lineup construction, and pitching matchups. Some games don't offer the opportunity. Some games, the Cubs are protecting a lead. The math requires consistent aggressiveness on the basepaths through the final stretch of a pennant race.

The Cubs are 81-63 and hold the first Wild Card spot in the National League. That's the competing pressure: Chicago needs wins more than individual milestones, and PCA's value is measured in both. He will not be sacrificing wins to collect steals — but he won't have to. His game already runs that way.

MVP Picture

NL MVP odds have PCA at -1100, reflecting how far ahead of the field the sportsbooks see him. The power-speed combination, the age milestone, the historic context, and the Cubs' contending record all point in the same direction. The 40-40 chase isn't just a personal milestone — it's the last piece of an MVP season that was already largely written.

If he gets there, PCA joins a list that includes four players who are in or will enter the Hall of Fame. If he falls short by two or three, the season is still historic. But the number is right there.

What's Next

The Cubs have approximately eighteen games remaining in the regular season, ending September 27. Every game Crow-Armstrong gets on base and reads a pitcher's move is another chance to shrink the gap. He needs seven. The 40-40 club has waited 62 years for a Cub. PCA has eighteen games to change that.
