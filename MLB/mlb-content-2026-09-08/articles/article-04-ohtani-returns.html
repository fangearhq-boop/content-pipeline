Back but Not Right: Ohtani Returns to the Dodgers Lineup With Questions Unanswered

By Marcus Cole | September 8, 2026

Los Angeles Dodgers

Shohei Ohtani returned to the Los Angeles Dodgers' lineup on Sunday after a four-day absence and went 0-for-3 with two strikeouts and a walk in a 6-3 win over the Cincinnati Reds. He is healthy enough to play. He is not healthy enough to pitch. And he has not been healthy enough to hit like Shohei Ohtani since mid-August.

The four-day absence was caused by three overlapping ailments: left knee soreness, right biceps soreness, and neck soreness. Manager Dave Roberts and Ohtani, speaking through his interpreter, confirmed Sunday that he will not pitch again in the 2026 season. His return to the mound is now targeted for 2027.

The Slump Context

The four-day rest didn't fix the larger issue. Since August 18, Ohtani has gone 7-for-51, a batting average of .137, with 20 strikeouts. That is not a short-term cold streak. Over three weeks of full lineup participation, he has been one of the least productive hitters in the Dodgers' order.

The reasons are layered. Multiple injury areas affect a hitter's mechanics and pitch recognition in ways that compound over time. Pitchers around the league adjust quickly when a hitter's swing changes, and Ohtani's aggressiveness at the plate — normally a strength — becomes a liability when the mechanics are off. The strikeout rate over this stretch, 20 in approximately 55 plate appearances, reflects a hitter who is not in sync.

The Pitching Decision

The announcement that Ohtani will not pitch in 2026 was expected given the injury picture, but it removes the most electric element of his game from this postseason run. The Dodgers signed him to be the most complete player in baseball. For the remainder of this season, he is a DH managing three sore body parts.

That still makes him valuable. Ohtani in any form creates lineup complications for opposing pitching staffs. His presence in the order protects the hitters around him even when he's not producing at his ceiling. But a .137 BA over three weeks is not what the Dodgers built their October around.

The Dodgers' Approach

Los Angeles is focused on protecting Ohtani's health through the end of the season. The plan is a full DH role — no fielding exposure, no pitching risk — with the explicit goal of arriving at the postseason with him healthy enough to be a factor at the plate. That plan requires the slump to end, or at least the swing to stabilize, before the games matter most.

Roberts has been measured in his public statements, emphasizing availability over output. That framing makes sense for a team that still has the talent to win a championship. But at some point, availability means productivity too.

What's Next

Ohtani will continue in the DH role through the remainder of the regular season. The Dodgers' goal is to have him healthy and swinging well by the time the postseason begins. The 2027 pitching return is confirmed. The remaining question for 2026 is whether Ohtani can find his swing before October and make his presence in this lineup matter the way Los Angeles expected.
