Four Teams, Three Spots, 18 Games: The NL Wild Card Race Enters Its Final Chapter

By Jake Torres | September 8, 2026

MLB Standings

Entering September 8, four National League teams are competing for three Wild Card spots with approximately eighteen games remaining in the regular season. The Chicago Cubs hold the first Wild Card at 81-63. The Philadelphia Phillies are half a game back at 80-63. The Arizona Diamondbacks hold the third spot at 76-68, with the San Diego Padres half a game behind at 75-68. One of these four teams is going home at the end of the month.

The NL West has the Brewers locked up in the Central. The Dodgers, Mets, and Braves are sorting out the remaining division races. Down in the Wild Card column, the race is as tight as the standings show — and that tightness does not resolve itself until the final weekend of September.

The Cubs' Position

Chicago holds the top Wild Card spot at 81-63, giving them a comfortable enough buffer that a short losing streak won't cost them a postseason berth — but not enough comfort to play freely. The Cubs have Pete Crow-Armstrong in the middle of a 40-40 chase and a rotation that has been consistent through the second half. Their Wild Card positioning is the least fragile of the four contenders, but fragile is relative in a field this close.

The Phillies, 0.5 back at WC2, have the second-best record in the group and enough pitching to make a run at overtaking Chicago for the top spot. Philadelphia has been one of the NL's steadiest teams all season.

The WC3 Battle

The third Wild Card spot is the most volatile. Arizona at 76-68 holds the position, but the Padres at 75-68 are half a game back. San Diego has the offensive talent to string together wins quickly. Arizona has the pitching depth to weather a difficult stretch. Both teams know that the other can make up half a game in a single series — and the remaining schedule for both includes meaningful games against division rivals who are also fighting for position.

At the outer edge, a team six games back can mathematically get in with the right combination of wins and losses across the field. The math tightens every night.

What the Wild Card Format Means

The Wild Card Series is a best-of-three format — a quick cut with high variance. A team that barely survives to get in can match up favorably against a higher seed. The NL Central-leading Brewers will host one of the Wild Card teams, and their rotation — anchored by Misiorowski at 14-5 — is built for a short series. The seeding matters. WC1 hosts the first two games of its series; WC3 plays on the road.

For the four teams still fighting for these spots, the home/away distinction in a best-of-three is meaningful. Every half-game in the standings over the next eighteen days carries postseason real estate with it.

What's Next

The regular season ends September 27. Wild Card Series begins September 29. With eighteen games left and four teams within six games of each other, the NL Wild Card race will not be resolved until the final days of the regular season. Every series starting tonight has direct implications on who is still playing in October — and who is packing up for the winter.
