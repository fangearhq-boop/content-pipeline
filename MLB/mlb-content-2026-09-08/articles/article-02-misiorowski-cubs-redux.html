One Bad Start, One More Chance: Misiorowski Faces the Cubs Again

By Jake Torres | September 8, 2026

Milwaukee Brewers

For the first 26 starts of his 2026 season, Jacob Misiorowski allowed three earned runs or fewer. Every single time. Through April, May, June, July, August — twenty-six consecutive quality starts that built a case for one of the most dominant individual pitching seasons in recent National League history.

Then on September 2, the Chicago Cubs came to Wrigley Field and took him apart. Five earned runs. Five hits. Four walks. Four innings. The Brewers won 9-5 anyway, but Misiorowski's line matched career worsts. The streak was over. The conversation shifted.

Today, pitching at American Family Field in Milwaukee, Misiorowski faces those same Cubs for the fourth time this year. His season record: 14-5, 1.97 ERA. His Cy Young case: still the frontrunner, with one dent in it. The rematch is here.

The September 2 Start

The September 2 outing was notable not just for the result but for how it happened. Four walks in four innings is unusual for a pitcher with Misiorowski's command profile. The Cubs attacked counts, worked deep into at-bats, and forced him to throw pitches he would normally avoid. The entire outing had the look of a team that had done its homework on a pitcher it was seeing for the third time in one season.

It ended a 26-start streak that had been among the longest of its kind in the NL this season. After that stretch of complete dominance, one rough outing against one quality opponent is not a trend — but it created a question that today's start answers.

His Track Record With Adversity

One of the quieter storylines in Misiorowski's season is that the September 2 start is the only rough outing he's had all year. Through 30-plus starts, he has not had a second rough game — not after early blowups, not after tight losses, not after long stretches of dominance. A Hall of Famer reportedly sent him an uplifting message before today's start, per SI.com.

He is pitching at home, with a season-long track record that says bad outings don't compound. The Brewers are MLB's best team at approximately 86-53. Misiorowski is the reason they pitch with such confidence this deep in the season.

Today's Matchup

The Cubs send left-hander David Peterson to the mound. Chicago comes in at 81-63, chasing the first Wild Card spot. They hit Misiorowski hard six days ago and will enter American Family Field with some confidence from that outing. But confidence built on one start in four meetings can only carry so far.

Misiorowski has faced Chicago three times in 2026 with strong results in two of those starts. Today's game will determine whether September 2 was a momentary crack or something the Cubs can replicate.

What's Next

Misiorowski has several starts remaining before the postseason. How he pitches today — and in the outings that follow — will shape the final narrative of his Cy Young campaign. The Brewers are already in the playoffs. The question is whether their ace arrives in October sharp or shaken. Today is the first answer.
