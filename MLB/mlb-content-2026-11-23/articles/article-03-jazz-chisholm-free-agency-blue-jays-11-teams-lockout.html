11 Teams Are In on Jazz Chisholm. The Clock Is 8 Days.

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | November 23, 2026

Jazz Chisholm Jr. has 11 teams engaged in his free agency, per MLB Network's Jon Morosi. That is a significant number for a free-agent infielder — and it reflects what Chisholm brings to the market: a switch-hitting second baseman with legitimate power, elite speed, and no qualifying offer attached to his name. No team signs him and loses a draft pick.

The Blue Jays have been the most prominently linked club across multiple reports. The fit makes structural sense. Toronto ranked near the bottom of the American League in stolen bases last season — just 75 as a team. Jazz Chisholm had 41 stolen bases by himself. His arrival would address one of Toronto's clearest on-field deficiencies in one transaction.

What Chisholm Offers

Chisholm's 2026 line: .231/.312/.399, 19 home runs, 41 stolen bases. The batting average is modest, the OBP is functional, and the slugging reflects a player whose power plays in a real way at any ballpark. But the speed is the differentiator. Forty-one stolen bases in the current era of base-running, with a high success rate, is the kind of asset that changes lineup construction and pressures opposing pitchers in ways that don't show up in a traditional stat line.

He is also versatile. Chisholm can play second base, center field, and third base at the major league level. That flexibility matters for a club managing injury risk over a multi-year contract. You are not locked into one defensive assignment with him on the roster.

The switch-hitting ability adds further value. Left-handed and right-handed pitching don't split the same way against Chisholm that they would against a platoon-dependent player. Teams that sign him don't need to worry about being exploited in late-game matchups.

The Blue Jays' Case

Toronto's interest in Chisholm goes beyond filling a hole. The Blue Jays are in a window — a roster built around Vladimir Guerrero Jr., Bo Bichette, and a core that is approaching its competitive peak. Adding Chisholm gives them a third dynamic offensive player who can bat anywhere from first to sixth in the lineup, steal bases at the top, or provide power from the middle. His arrival reshapes what the Blue Jays can do offensively against elite pitching in a playoff series.

No qualified offer means no compensation. Eleven teams engaged means there is a real market. Toronto's window to close this deal is the same as everyone else's: eight days before the December 1 lockout freezes everything.

What's Next

Chisholm is expected to meet with interested teams this week. The Yankees are out of the running. The Blue Jays are out front. But 11 teams engaged means the bidding is competitive, and competitive bidding means the final number will push past initial projections. Chisholm's market will be settled in the next eight days — either by a deal before the lockout or by the freeze that sends everyone into a holding pattern until spring. For a player who has never won a postseason series and wants to prove himself on a contender, the urgency to get this done now is real.
