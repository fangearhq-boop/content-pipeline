The White Sox Bet $52.5M on Dustin May — and They're Still Not Done

By Ryan Calloway | Ballpark Banter | November 23, 2026

The Chicago White Sox signed right-hander Dustin May to a three-year, $52.5 million deal on November 17. For a franchise that spent years at the bottom of the American League Central, it's a signal: they are building something, they are spending money on it, and they are not finished.

May, 29, is coming off a career that includes real big-league innings — 102 appearances, a 4.12 career ERA — alongside the kind of injury history (Tommy John surgery) that creates legitimate uncertainty about his ceiling. The Sun-Times called the deal "a gamble on untapped upside." That framing is honest. This is not a signing of a proven No. 1 or even a proven No. 2. This is a bet that May's best years are ahead of him, that the stuff translates into more consistent performance, and that the White Sox rotation becomes something more than a collection of question marks.

Where May Fits

The depth chart slots May as the No. 2 starter behind Sean Burke. Burke is the anchor, the guy the organization has built around — a young arm with frontline stuff who the White Sox intend to control for years. May gives him a credible follow in the rotation rather than asking Burke to carry innings load while everyone else pitches catch-up ball.

The deal is the largest free-agent investment by Chicago since they signed Andrew Benintendi in 2023. That context matters: a franchise that has been careful with big commitments just made one at the rotation level, which is where rebuilding teams eventually have to bet their future.

May's Contract Sets the Market

Beyond what May does for the White Sox, his contract sends a signal to everyone else in the starting pitcher market. At $52.5 million over three years, May represents the going rate for a mid-tier starter with upside but health question marks. Teams that need rotation help without the budget for top-of-market arms now have a reference point. Free agents in that tier who have not yet signed know what their market will bear.

The White Sox had to designate reliever Jordan Leasure for assignment to open a 40-man roster spot for May. That's the kind of quiet roster math that happens around every signing — but it confirms the deal is official and the club is committed to it.

What's Next

Chicago has stated publicly that they are still pursuing rotation additions before the December 1 lockout. That means May is not the ceiling of their offseason ambitions — he's the floor. With Sean Burke at the top and May settling in at No. 2, one more credible arm would give the White Sox a rotation the American League Central will have to take seriously. The window is eight days. If the front office can close another deal before December 1, the rebuild could officially announce itself as something bigger.
