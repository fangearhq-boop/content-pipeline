### STORY 1: Skubal $450M Mets Deal

**Long-Form Post (10:30 AM ET)**

The Mets just made the largest pitching commitment in baseball history.

Nine years. $450 million. $50 million per year. Full no-trade clause.

Tarik Skubal agreed to the deal Sunday, pending a physical. The Dodgers, Padres, and Blue Jays all made serious runs — the Dodgers were considered a frontrunner for weeks — and they all came home empty. Steve Cohen's willingness to spend past what any owner has spent on a pitcher before is what ended this.

For context: Yoshinobu Yamamoto's $325M/12-year record was considered staggering two years ago. Skubal just obliterated it. His $50M AAV is nearly double Yamamoto's $27M mark.

The Mets went 74-88 last season, last in the NL East. They had the highest payroll in baseball on Opening Day and finished in last. Skubal is the kind of signing that changes the math — a true No. 1, two-time Cy Young winner, in his prime. Paired with the rest of a remade roster, Skubal makes the Mets a genuine contender from day one.

The question now is what happens if the lockout runs long. His $45M signing bonus was reportedly structured to be shielded. But the rest of the roster still can't be built until baseball is back in business.

The Mets bet everything on Tarik Skubal. It's the biggest swing in this franchise since Soto.

### STORY 2: CBA Countdown — 8 Days

**Long-Form Post (8:00 AM ET)**

Eight days. That's what's left before baseball freezes.

The CBA expires at 11:59 PM ET on December 1. At 12:01 AM on December 2, owners are expected to impose a lockout — just like they did in 2021. That means no signings, no trades, no waiver claims, no arbitration hearings. The market goes completely dark.

Owners want a salary cap: $258.2 million ceiling, $180.2 million floor, implemented in 2028. The MLBPA's position has never changed — no salary cap in any form. MLBPA executive leadership has already called a lockout "all but guaranteed."

No one who covers this sport expects a deal before December 1.

The 2021 lockout lasted 99 days and ended March 10, 2022 — without losing a single regular-season game. That's the best-case scenario. The cap demand this time is more fundamental than 2021's disputes, which centered on pre-arb salary. A structural fight over the cap could run much longer, pushing Opening Day back.

The only window is now. Skubal's deal just got done. Jazz Chisholm, Seiya Suzuki, Roki Sato (NPB posting), and dozens of other free agents are racing the clock. Teams have eight days to close what they want closed before the market freezes — potentially for months.

### STORY 3: Jazz Chisholm Free Agency

**Long-Form Post (12:30 PM ET)**

Eleven teams are in on Jazz Chisholm Jr.

That's the number Jon Morosi reported for engaged clubs as of this week. The Blue Jays have been the most prominently linked team across multiple outlets. The fit makes structural sense: Toronto ranked near the bottom of the American League in stolen bases last season — just 75 as a team. Chisholm had 41 stolen bases by himself.

He also adds 19 home runs, switch-hit ability, and middle-infield versatility at a position where the Blue Jays have an open competition. No qualifying offer was attached to Chisholm, so any team that signs him keeps its draft picks. That removes a significant barrier.

The Yankees are out. That narrows the top of the market.

The clock is what makes this urgent. Eight days until the lockout. If Chisholm doesn't sign before December 1, he waits out the freeze — which could run deep into 2027. For a player who wants to be on a roster, playing, the incentive to get a deal done this week is as strong as it gets.

Eleven teams are in. Eight days left. The Blue Jays are out front. This one gets decided before the month ends.

### STORY 4: White Sox Dustin May

**Long-Form Post (3:00 PM ET)**

The Chicago White Sox made their biggest free-agent investment in years — and they say they're not done.

Dustin May. Three years. $52.5 million. The deal was announced November 17 and is pending a physical. For a White Sox franchise that has been methodically rebuilding its roster, this is the most significant outside pitching acquisition since they started the rebuild in earnest. It's the largest free-agent outlay by Chicago since the Andrew Benintendi signing in 2023.

May is 29 and carries a 4.12 career ERA across 102 big-league appearances. The Sun-Times called the deal a "gamble on untapped upside." He slots as the No. 2 starter behind Sean Burke. He's not an ace, and by most analyses, he can't be the best starting pitcher this White Sox team adds this winter — but he's a real, durable rotation piece for a team that hasn't had many of those recently.

The more interesting signal from Chicago is that they're publicly saying the work isn't finished. The Sun-Times reported the club is still actively pursuing more rotation additions before December 1. With Sean Burke anchoring the top, May providing middle-rotation depth, and presumably one more arm in play, this could be the winter the White Sox rotation starts to look like something.

They have eight days to close whatever comes next.

### STORY 5: Seiya Suzuki Market

**Long-Form Post (6:00 PM ET)**

Seiya Suzuki turned down $23 million from the Cubs, and he's about to test a market that could pay him four times that.

Contract projections for Suzuki run from $90 million (SI) to nearly $110 million (Spotrac) over four years. He declined the qualifying offer November 17, triggering a process where any team that signs him must give the Cubs a draft pick. That adds a small cost of entry — but for a player of Suzuki's caliber, it's a cost most teams will accept.

The teams linked so far: Mariners, Red Sox, Blue Jays, and Phillies. The Blue Jays in particular are doing double-duty — they're in on Chisholm and reportedly interested in Suzuki, which speaks to how aggressively Toronto is trying to load up before the lockout.

For the Cubs, the picture is disorienting. Suzuki is leaving. Ian Happ is leaving. Carson Kelly is a free agent. The team could realistically enter 2027 needing to replace a third of its lineup. Rebuilding and contending at the same time is hard enough. Doing it in a lockout winter is harder.

Suzuki's window to sign is the same as everyone else's: eight days. For a player who turned down guaranteed money to bet on himself, there's real urgency to get this done before the freeze.
