White Sox Sweep Tigers at Comerica — AL Central Lead Reaches Season-Best 4.5 Games

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | August 16, 2026

The Chicago White Sox swept the Detroit Tigers at Comerica Park on Sunday, winning Game 2 of the series 4-3 on the strength of a go-ahead single from Andrew Benintendi in the seventh inning. The victory pushes Chicago's AL Central lead to a season-best 4.5 games and sends a clear message about who runs this division.

One year ago, the White Sox finished 41-121. Today, they are 64-58, they have won 5 of their last 7 games, and they hold a 5-3 season series advantage over the Tigers. Detroit sits at 60-62, now below .500 and losing ground fast.

Benintendi's Seventh-Inning Moment

The go-ahead hit captured the identity of this White Sox team. Benintendi, working a count with runners on base, put a ball in the gap for a single that moved Chicago into a 4-3 lead. The Tigers could not answer in the final two innings, and the series sweep was complete.

It was a one-run game decided by execution in the middle of a lineup that has learned to produce when it matters. The White Sox did not blow the Tigers out of Comerica — they ground out two one-run wins and took the series in the kind of baseball that holds up in September.

The Division Math

The numbers behind Chicago's lead are worth sitting with. The White Sox are 22-13 against their AL Central opponents. That intra-division performance is the foundation of a 4.5-game cushion that was not supposed to exist at this point in the year — not given where this franchise was 12 months ago.

Detroit had positioned itself as the main challenger coming into the series. The Tigers have been 37-23 since June 1 — one of the best records in baseball over that stretch. To face them in their home park, in what was being called a must-win series for Detroit, and to take both games, is a performance that belongs at the top of any conversation about whether the White Sox are for real.

The Turnaround in Full View

The number that keeps coming up in any discussion of the 2026 White Sox is 41-121 — the final record of their 2024 season, the worst in the modern history of Major League Baseball. What has happened since then is the result of both rapid player development and a front office that built toward this moment without sacrificing the future.

The White Sox with a season-best 4.5-game AL Central lead in August of 2026 is not a storyline that many people had circled before the season. It is now the storyline of the American League.

What's Next

The White Sox and Tigers play one more game in the series on Monday. Chicago will look to build on the 4.5-game lead as the AL Central race enters its final six weeks. Detroit, now 60-62, needs to win to stay in the conversation. Watch for the White Sox to pursue their second consecutive series win and tighten their grip on the division.
