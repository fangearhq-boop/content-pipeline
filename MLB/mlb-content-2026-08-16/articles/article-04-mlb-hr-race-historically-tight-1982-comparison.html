The 2026 MLB Home Run Race Is Historically Tight — And It Just Got More Interesting

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | August 16, 2026

Kyle Schwarber leads the 2026 MLB home run race with 37. That sentence would seem to suggest comfortable separation from the field. It does not. Yordan Alvarez returned from a 13-game drought on Saturday with his 36th home run. Matt Olson has hit five home runs in the first nine games of August. Junior Caminero is still in the conversation.

The last time the home run race was this tightly contested, this late in a season, was September 8-9, 1982 — when Gorman Thomas, Reggie Jackson, Dale Murphy, and Dave Kingman were all tied at 34 home runs. That season ended with Thomas leading at 39. This one has six weeks remaining and multiple legitimate contenders.

Schwarber's Position

Schwarber leads at 37, extending the advantage he built by hitting two home runs in the Field of Dreams game on August 14. Before that game, the four-way tie at 35 home runs had been the defining image of the race — the first bunching of that magnitude this late in a season in more than four decades.

At 37, Schwarber has proven he is not merely a pace-setter who will fade in August. He has held the lead through multiple challengers taking runs at it. The next milestone — 40 home runs — would put him in a range where the Phillies can start discussing what a truly historic season might look like.

Alvarez Is Back

The most significant development in the home run race over the past 24 hours is Alvarez's drought-breaker. After going 13 games without a home run — a stretch that generated considerable concern among Astros fans and HR race observers — Alvarez hit No. 36 off George Kirby in Seattle on Saturday night.

At 36, Alvarez is one behind Schwarber. And with a .323 batting average and 88 RBI, he is also a Triple Crown candidate. His return to home run production does not just revive the HR race — it complicates the entire conversation about who the best hitter in the American League is right now.

Olson's August

Matt Olson had been the hottest hitter in the race entering this weekend. Five home runs in the first nine games of August was a pace that no other contender was matching. His consecutive games streak continues, a testament to durability that gives the Braves a player who shows up every day and contributes regardless of what the calendar says.

Olson's surge from what appeared to be a mid-race plateau into genuine contention for the lead has changed the math. He is not a sideshow to the Schwarber-Alvarez matchup — he is a co-lead story with a recent track record that no other candidate can match over the last two weeks.

What's Next

The home run race enters its final six weeks with Schwarber at 37, Alvarez at 36, Olson surging, and Caminero still in range. Watch for milestones at 40 and 45 home runs as the season approaches Labor Day — those are the numbers that will crystallize the race and determine whether 2026 produces the kind of finish that keeps casual fans watching every game of September.
