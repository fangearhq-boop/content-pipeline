Alvarez Ends 13-Game HR Drought — And the Triple Crown Chase Is Back On

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | August 16, 2026

Yordan Alvarez had not hit a home run in 13 games. For a hitter of his caliber, that kind of drought attracts attention — not because it signals decline, but because everyone is waiting for the moment it ends. On Saturday night in Seattle, it ended.

Alvarez hit a solo home run off George Kirby in the Astros' 10-7 extra-innings win over the Mariners. It was a straightforward swing from the left side — the kind that has been his signature for years — and it sent the ball well over the fence and reset an entire narrative around the 2026 AL MVP conversation.

The Kirby Detail

The home run carried a layer of statistical significance beyond just ending the drought. George Kirby had started 12 games against Houston and faced 255 Astros hitters. In all of those appearances, no Astros hitter had ever taken him deep. Alvarez ended that on Saturday with one swing.

Before the game, Alvarez had been spotted taking on-field batting practice — a routine he rarely uses, but which signaled that he was actively working through whatever mechanical adjustment the drought required. The BP session visible to fans and media was the kind of public accountability that superstars rarely display. The home run that followed validated the work.

The Triple Crown Numbers

When the dust settled on Saturday night, Alvarez's season line read: .323 batting average, 36 home runs, 88 RBI. Those are the kind of numbers that invite comparison to baseball's rarest achievement — the Triple Crown, which requires leading the league in all three categories simultaneously.

No player has won the Triple Crown since Miguel Cabrera accomplished the feat in 2012. In the years since, the combination of home run power, contact consistency, and run-production efficiency required to contend in all three categories simultaneously has eluded every candidate who came close. Alvarez, with a .323 average that leads the AL, a home run total back in the conversation at 36, and an RBI count that places him among the AL's elite run producers, is one of the few players in the sport capable of making a real run at Cabrera's standard.

Six weeks remain. The drought is over. The race is on.

Where the HR Race Stands

With 36 home runs, Alvarez is one behind Kyle Schwarber, who leads MLB with 37. Matt Olson has been the hottest hitter in the chase, having hit five home runs in the first nine games of August. The race is historically close — the kind of bunching in August that recalls the 1982 season when four players were tied at 34 home runs late in the year.

For Alvarez to compete in the home run category, he needs to sustain the power output that made him a pre-season Triple Crown candidate. Saturday's home run suggests the mechanics are back. The next six weeks will show whether the drought was a blip or a warning.

What's Next

Alvarez and the Astros continue play this week. The Triple Crown conversation will intensify if he strings together home runs and maintains his batting average through August. Track the HR race and the AL batting average standings as September approaches — this story has plenty of runway left.
