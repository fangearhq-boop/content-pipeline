Misiorowski Beats Dodgers 4-1 — Strikes Out Ohtani With Bases Loaded, Brewers Give Him Extra Rest

By Ryan Calloway | Ballpark Banter | August 16, 2026

There was one moment in Jacob Misiorowski's start against the Dodgers on Saturday that will define how people remember this particular outing. Eighth inning. Bases loaded. One out. Shohei Ohtani standing in against the best pitcher in the National League. Misiorowski went slider, fastball — 104.1 mph, registered as a swinging strike, one of the fastest whiffs of Misiorowski's career — slider. Ohtani sat down. Then a popout to escape the jam. Brewers won 4-1.

That is what 204 strikeouts in 133 innings looks like under maximum pressure.

The Historic Statistical Context

Misiorowski is the second-fastest pitcher in Major League Baseball history to reach 200 strikeouts in a season. He got there in 133 innings pitched. Only Spencer Strider's 2022 season, when Strider reached 200 strikeouts in 123.1 innings, was faster in modern MLB history.

The Cy Young odds around Misiorowski sit at approximately -550 in his favor. On a Milwaukee team that is not in playoff contention, he is the lone national story. Every fifth day, baseball people stop what they are doing and watch. Saturday's Dodger game — on the road, against the best team in the sport, with the best hitter in baseball coming up with the game on the line — was precisely the kind of moment that separates candidates from the real thing.

He passed it.

The Extra Rest Decision

On Sunday, the Brewers announced that Misiorowski's next start would be pushed back — he will pitch on approximately Sunday, August 23, rather than on his normal four-day schedule. The decision is organizational wisdom. Protect the arm, manage the workload, bring him back fully rested with six weeks remaining in the season.

For a franchise that is not chasing a playoff spot, the calculus is entirely about Misiorowski. His health and performance through September are the most important things the Brewers can manage. Getting him extra rest in August, after a stretch of high-leverage starts, is exactly the kind of decision that keeps a Cy Young campaign on track rather than burning it out in a meaningless pennant context.

The Cy Young Case

One loss to the Dodgers earlier in the month did not move the needle. Neither will extra rest between starts. What moves the Cy Young needle is what Misiorowski has been doing all season: strikeouts at a historic rate, an ERA that remains among the lowest in the league, and a WHIP that is practically nonexistent.

The ballot is his to lose. The Ohtani moment on Saturday gave every voter a specific, cinematic image to attach to the case. Bases loaded. Best hitter alive. Punchout at 104 miles per hour. That image does not leave the room.

What's Next

Misiorowski's next start is projected for Sunday, August 23. Track his strikeout total as he approaches what would be historic end-of-season numbers. The Cy Young vote is not official until after the season, but the conversation has been ongoing for months — and every start either locks it in further or opens a window for a challenger.
