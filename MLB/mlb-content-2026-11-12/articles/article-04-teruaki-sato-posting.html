Teruaki Sato Posted: MLB Teams Race Before CBA Lockout Pauses Window

By Marcus Cole | November 12, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Teruaki Sato's posting window opened November 1, and the race to sign him is now fully underway — with a complication nobody anticipated when the Orix Buffaloes posted him. The 45-day window was scheduled to run through December 16. But the MLB collective bargaining agreement expires December 1. If a lockout follows, as is widely expected, the posting window pauses until a new CBA is in place. That turns what looked like a straightforward process into a sprint.

Five MLB teams are confirmed to have expressed serious interest: the Mets, Yankees, Dodgers, Phillies, and Padres. The Giants, Blue Jays, and Pirates have also been mentioned. Contract projections range from Jon Heyman's $80 million over four years to Jim Bowden's five-year/$180 million estimate, with ESPN landing in the middle at five years/$115 million. The variance reflects genuine uncertainty about how the market values a 25-year-old who won the NPB Triple Crown in 2026 but has never played professional baseball in the United States.

The Case for Sato

The numbers from Sato's 2026 NPB season are exceptional by any standard. He batted .308 with a .385 on-base percentage and a .613 slugging percentage, hitting 39 home runs to win the Triple Crown. He is 25 years old and produced this season in one of the most competitive leagues outside MLB. The comparison pool for NPB-to-MLB transitions has grown over the past decade, and the conversion rates for high-end contact and power hitters from Japan have been encouraging.

The age matters. At 25, Sato is significantly younger than the typical NPB posting candidate. If his power translates at even a 70-to-80 percent rate against MLB pitching, he projects as a legitimate middle-of-the-order bat. The teams bidding on him are not doing so out of curiosity — they believe the power is real.

The Scout Concerns

The gap between Heyman's $80 million floor and Bowden's $180 million ceiling reflects a real split in how evaluators see Sato. The primary concern: defense at third base. Scout reports from NPB observers describe his range and arm as below average for the position at the MLB level, which could force a move to a corner outfield spot or first base. A player who can only play first base and DH is a materially different market profile than a third baseman who hits .300 with 40 home runs.

The defensive question won't be resolved until Sato is in an MLB spring training camp and put through workouts at multiple positions. Teams bidding now are essentially betting on the bat and factoring in defensive uncertainty. The range in contract projections reflects exactly that uncertainty.

The CBA Timing Problem

Sato's situation shares the CBA complication with every other significant transaction this offseason. The posting window technically runs 45 days from the November 1 posting date — through December 16. But that date falls 15 days into an expected lockout. Under the posting agreement rules, the window pauses during any work stoppage and resumes when a new CBA is ratified. Teams that want Sato cannot wait for the lockout to end. They need to get a deal done in the next 19 days or accept that the process goes on indefinite hold.

That urgency benefits Sato. Teams that might otherwise play a slower game are compressed into a short window, which creates upward pressure on offers. The five-plus confirmed teams gives his representatives genuine leverage. And unlike a qualified free agent with draft pick compensation attached, Sato's signing comes at no additional cost to the acquiring team beyond the posting fee — roughly $3.2 million based on projected contract size.

What's Next

Formal meetings between Sato's representatives and interested teams are ongoing. The CBA deadline of December 1 creates the real pressure point — a deal needs at least informal agreement before that date, with documentation following. Watch for a signing or a formal announcement of a preferred team in the next two weeks. If December 1 arrives without a deal signed, Sato's market effectively freezes until MLB and the Players Association reach a new agreement. For a 25-year-old Triple Crown winner, that delay works against nobody more than the teams that waited.
