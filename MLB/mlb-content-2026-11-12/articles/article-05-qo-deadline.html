QO Deadline Nov. 17: Eight Players, All Expected to Decline

By Jake Torres | November 12, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Eight players have until November 17 at 4:00 PM ET to decide whether to accept or decline their qualifying offers. The value of the QO this year is $23.15 million — a one-year contract at the average of the top 125 MLB salaries. All eight are expected to decline. That would be historically consistent: since the qualifying offer era began, only 18 of 157 players have ever accepted one.

The eight players who received qualifying offers this cycle: Seiya Suzuki (Cubs), Randy Arozarena (Mariners), David Bednar (Yankees), Jake Bauers (Brewers), Trevor Rogers (Orioles), Ryan Jeffers (Twins), Brandon Lowe (Pirates), and Adrian Morejon (Padres).

What Declining Means

A player who declines a qualifying offer becomes a free agent — but with a catch. The team that extended the QO receives draft pick compensation when that player signs elsewhere, provided the signing team is a qualifying team (above the luxury tax threshold) or the deal meets certain thresholds. That draft pick compensation can slightly depress a player's market, since signing teams factor in the cost of losing a pick.

For most of these eight players, the calculus is straightforward: a one-year deal at $23.15 million is likely less than their projected multi-year value. A starting catcher of Ryan Jeffers's caliber, for example, can expect a multi-year commitment that totals well above what a single QO year would pay. Same logic applies to Bednar, Bauers, Rogers, Lowe, and Morejon. For a player certain to get more years and more total money as a free agent, accepting the QO makes no financial sense.

The Mariners Situation

Seattle sent QOs to both Seiya Suzuki and Randy Arozarena — but only wants one of them back. Reports from the GM Meetings confirm the Mariners are interested in re-signing Suzuki. They have stated clearly they will not be re-signing Arozarena. Both are expected to decline, with Suzuki then potentially entering negotiation with Seattle and Arozarena entering an open market his former club is explicitly not participating in.

Arozarena, who came to Seattle at the trade deadline in 2025, had an inconsistent run with the Mariners. The public statement that they won't bring him back is unusual but clear. He will be a free agent by the end of business November 17.

The CBA Deadline Connection

The QO deadline lands five days before the CBA expires. For the eight players involved, the sequence matters: they decline on November 17, become free agents, and then have roughly two weeks to find a team before the lockout takes effect. If the CBA expires December 1 and a lockout follows, all free agent negotiations cease until a new agreement is reached. That compresses the timeline for these players considerably — a declined QO on November 17 leaves about 13 workdays to get a deal done before the freeze.

Most of these players will not get deals done in that window. Historical norms suggest QO-declining free agents sign in December or January after teams have addressed higher priorities. This year, those signings get pushed past the lockout. For players on the lower end of the market — Rogers, Bauers, Morejon — that delay is real but manageable. For a player like Jeffers, who projects as one of the better catchers in this free agent class, a delayed market isn't necessarily a negative if the right team is waiting on the other side.

What's Next

The November 17 deadline is largely procedural. All eight players decline. All eight become free agents with compensation attached. The market for each of them begins in earnest after the QO formalities conclude — and then immediately encounters the CBA expiration two weeks later. For Ballpark Banter readers tracking these players: none of them are likely to sign anywhere in the next two weeks, unless a team moves unusually fast to lock in a player before the lockout. Watch the Mariners on Suzuki as the most likely exception, given their stated interest in re-signing him.
