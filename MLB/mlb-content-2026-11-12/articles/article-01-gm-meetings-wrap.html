GM Meetings Wrap: White Sox, Giants, and the CBA Clock

By Marcus Cole | November 12, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The 2026 MLB GM Meetings at the Aria Resort in Las Vegas ended Wednesday with more questions than answers — which is exactly how it always works at this stage. The deals get made elsewhere, in hotel lobbies and agent offices and eventually on the phone in December. But the intelligence gathered here is worth cataloging.

Three threads emerged from the final day that will shape the offseason narrative heading into the winter meetings: the White Sox's rotation plans, the Giants' trade openness, and the shadow of a collective bargaining agreement that expires in 20 days.

White Sox: Grant Taylor Heads to the Rotation

Chicago's most significant organizational announcement was quiet but meaningful: reliever Grant Taylor will transition to the starting rotation for 2027. Taylor spent 2026 as a high-leverage bullpen arm, posting strong strikeout numbers but limited to multi-inning outings by design. The White Sox, whose starting pitching depth remains a rebuild priority, are betting Taylor's stuff can translate over longer stints.

Starting pitching acquisition remains the organization's top offseason goal. Taylor's move to the rotation is addition-by-conversion — a cost-controlled option who doesn't require a trade or free agent signing. Whether it sticks will depend on Taylor's ability to extend outings and develop a third reliable pitch. The White Sox have made it clear they won't wait to find out in spring training.

Giants: Posey Opens the Trade Book

San Francisco president of baseball operations Bob Posey was direct at GM Meetings: the Giants are willing to listen on essentially any player. "You've got to listen on pretty much anyone," Posey told reporters, a statement that covers the full 40-man roster and signals an organization recalibrating after a third consecutive non-playoff season.

Names that surfaced in trade conversation circles around the Giants: Willy Adames and Matt Chapman, both of whom carry significant contracts and represent the kind of veterans a rebuilding club might move for prospects or controllable talent. Posey did not confirm specific discussions, but his language was notable for its breadth. This is not a team holding a core intact while selling excess depth — it's a team genuinely exploring the value of its entire roster.

The CBA Clock

Phillies general manager Dave Dombrowski described GM Meetings as "productive" — a word that in the context of baseball negotiations means roughly that everyone got in the same rooms and acknowledged the timelines. The more important number Dombrowski mentioned was 20: the approximate number of days remaining before the current collective bargaining agreement expires on December 1.

A lockout is widely expected to follow the expiration. Upward of 180 free agents were in Las Vegas this week, with agents and executives exchanging preliminary interest at a market stage that multiple sources described as early. The free agent market rarely produces major signings before Thanksgiving under normal circumstances. Under a CBA expiration, the window for deals closes December 1 — and doesn't reopen until a new agreement is reached. For players like Corbin Skubal, Teruaki Sato, and other premium names, the next 19 days represent the entire offseason window for the foreseeable future.

What's Next

The GM Meetings produced intelligence, not transactions. The deals that follow will come from what was learned here: which teams are buyers, which are sellers, where the market values players, and who is willing to move before the CBA clock runs out. The White Sox have a rotation plan. The Giants have an open trade book. And the entire industry is watching the calendar. The winter meetings in December will either be a flurry of post-CBA activity or a placeholder event in the middle of a lockout — and nobody in Las Vegas this week seemed confident it would be the former.
