Corbin Skubal Free Agency: Mets and Dodgers Lead as California Meetings Begin

By Jake Torres | November 12, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Corbin Skubal is heading to California next week to meet with teams, and the picture of his free agency is beginning to take shape. The Associated Press has confirmed at least six clubs are in serious pursuit. ESPN's Kiley McDaniel has identified the New York Mets and the Los Angeles Dodgers as the primary bidders. And now Jon Heyman reports the Mets are "probably" the favorite — which means the team run by Steve Cohen is prepared to go further on money than the defending World Series champions across town.

The numbers being discussed are unlike anything the pitcher market has seen. Contract projections consistently land around eight years and $352 million, with an annual value of approximately $44 million. For context: Yoshinobu Yamamoto set the previous record for starting pitcher annual value at $27 million per year when he signed with the Dodgers two winters ago. Skubal would shatter that record by more than 60 percent.

The Field

The AP's report confirms six teams. The Padres are confirmed as one of the six. The Mets and Dodgers are named. Three additional clubs remain unidentified. What is known from subsequent reporting: the Cubs have withdrawn from the process, per Yardbarker, and the Blue Jays entered the picture as of November 11, per MLB.com's offseason roundup. That accounts for movement in a field that started with broad interest and is now narrowing.

McDaniel's identification of the Mets and Dodgers as "primary bidders" tracks with what has been reported about each team's offseason posture. The Mets have operated at the top of the market in recent years; losing out on Juan Soto last winter only increased the pressure to land a generational player. The Dodgers, despite their rotation losses to injury in 2026, have the payroll flexibility and organizational infrastructure to absorb a commitment of this size.

Why Mets Are Probably the Favorite

Heyman's framing — Mets "probably" the favorite — implies the Mets are the most likely team to exceed the Dodgers on the financial terms. This is a meaningful signal. The Dodgers lost a Cy Young finalist to injury this season and have rotation depth concerns heading into 2027. They have every reason to pursue Skubal aggressively. For the Mets to be the financial favorite over a team of the Dodgers' resources suggests Steve Cohen is willing to commit to a contract in a range that the Dodgers may consider a step too far.

That said, widespread belief in reporting circles holds that Skubal may prefer the Dodgers — their organization, their roster, their culture — which creates a scenario where preference and money pull in different directions. The California meetings next week will be the first meaningful signal of which direction Skubal is leaning.

The CBA Complication

Skubal turns 30 on November 20. The collective bargaining agreement expires December 1. A lockout is expected December 2. The window for a deal before the market freezes is narrow: approximately 19 days from today.

No qualifying offer is attached to Skubal's name — the Tigers traded him during the season, before the QO deadline. That means any team can sign him without surrendering draft compensation, which removes one of the more common obstacles in the premium free agent market. The only obstacle is the CBA clock. If the California meetings next week produce a framework, a deal before December 1 is possible. If they produce interest but not urgency, Skubal's signing gets pushed past the lockout — and no one knows when that ends.

What's Next

California meetings begin next week. The timeline from here: meetings produce a preferred destination or a bidding structure; offer letters or term sheets follow; and a signing announcement comes either before December 1 or not until a new CBA is in place. Watch for reporting on Skubal's preferred destination — if the preference-versus-money divide firms up, the outcome of this market becomes significantly more predictable. For now, the Mets hold a financial edge and the Dodgers hold the preference edge, and the most consequential pitching contract in baseball history is two weeks from being decided.
