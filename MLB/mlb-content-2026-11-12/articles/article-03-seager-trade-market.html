Rangers Exploring Corey Seager Trade — But It's His Decision

By Ryan Calloway | November 12, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The Texas Rangers are actively exploring a trade of Corey Seager, according to reporting from Ken Rosenthal and Evan Grant at GM Meetings in Las Vegas. The conversations are real. The Rangers are willing to move their franchise shortstop. There is just one complication that overrides everything else: Seager has a full no-trade clause, earned through his 10&5 rights — 10 years of MLB service time and five with the same club — and no deal happens without his consent.

The Rangers want to do this trade. Whether Corey Seager agrees is a different question entirely.

What Texas Is Working With

The Rangers signed Seager to a 10-year/$325 million contract before the 2022 season. Approximately five years and $155 to $157.5 million remain on that deal. At the time of signing it was one of the richest contracts in shortstop history and a foundational piece of the organization's championship window. That window closed faster than expected.

Seager's 2026 season represented the clearest evidence of the problem: a .227 batting average, a .736 OPS, and career lows across nearly every offensive category. He underwent elbow surgery after the season concluded. He will turn 33 in April. The combination of declining performance, a significant remaining commitment, and a post-surgical recovery makes the Rangers' desire to trade him understandable. It also makes the trade difficult to execute at anything approaching full contract value.

The Yankees Angle

Jon Heyman has identified the Yankees as the most engaged team in Seager discussions. The reported framework: Seager moves to third base in New York, with the Yankees absorbing some portion of the remaining contract. The positional shift makes sense on paper — the Yankees have their own shortstop situation and don't need to displace an incumbent — but it also asks Seager to change positions at 33 while recovering from elbow surgery, which is not a minor ask.

Joel Sherman is skeptical of the Yankees' interest materializing into a completed deal. His reporting points to the injury history and the down 2026 season as reasons the financial case for a trade doesn't hold up under scrutiny. The Yankees would be acquiring years five through nine of a contract signed when Seager was at or near peak value — a commitment that looked reasonable for a 28-year-old World Series hero looks considerably harder to justify for a 33-year-old recovering from elbow surgery with a career-low recent season.

Other Suitors

Beyond the Yankees, teams with reported interest include the Blue Jays, Red Sox, Dodgers, and Braves. In each case, the same calculus applies: how much contract does a acquiring team want to absorb, and what does it get in return from Texas? The Rangers would be selling a player in decline on a long-term deal — the return in prospects or talent would be limited unless Texas eats a significant portion of what remains. No reports have surfaced yet of Texas offering to do that.

The Dodgers are an interesting name. They have used Seager effectively before and understand his profile. Whether they'd want to bring him back given his 2026 and the elbow surgery — and whether Seager himself would approve a move back to Los Angeles — is unknown.

What's Next

The key variable isn't which team offers the best trade package. It's whether Seager decides he wants to leave Texas. If he's comfortable in Arlington and doesn't see a compelling destination, the trade doesn't happen regardless of what the Rangers negotiate. His agent will almost certainly seek clarity on the landscape before Seager makes any decision — and that landscape needs to include a team where Seager believes he can be effective again. Watch for reporting on Seager's own posture toward a move. That, more than anything else happening in Las Vegas, determines where this goes.
