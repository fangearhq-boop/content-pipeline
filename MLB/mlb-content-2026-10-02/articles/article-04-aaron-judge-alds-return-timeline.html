Aaron Judge Targets ALDS Return — Will He Play vs. the Rays on Saturday?

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | October 2, 2026

Aaron Judge has not played since September 19. The New York Yankees swept the Boston Red Sox without him in the Wild Card round. And now the American League Division Series begins Saturday against the Tampa Bay Rays — and every answer about Judge's availability remains unclear.

What is clear is that Judge wants to be on that field. The question is whether his right calf will cooperate.

The Injury

Judge suffered what the Yankees described as a moderate-grade strain of the soleus muscle in his right calf. He was placed on the 10-day injured list on September 19. The soleus is a smaller calf muscle that plays a key role in running, cutting, and tracking balls in the outfield. A moderate-grade strain is a meaningful injury — not the kind that heals in a week — but not necessarily one that keeps a player out for a full month.

The Yankees declined to add Judge to their Wild Card roster against the Red Sox. Instead, they relied on a lineup led by Ben Rice, who rose spectacularly to the occasion. Rice launched multiple home runs across the two-game sweep, including a grand slam, as New York outscored Boston 18-2 without its best player. The Yankees won Game 1 by nine and Game 2 by seven. It was as comfortable a Wild Card series as anyone could imagine.

What Judge Wants

Under Major League Baseball's postseason roster rules, Judge is eligible to be activated for the ALDS. Whether the Yankees add him — and whether Judge himself passes the physical standards needed to take the field — is a decision being made in real time as Saturday approaches.

One key detail from recent reports: Judge has said he wants to return as a right fielder, not just as a designated hitter. That distinction matters. Playing DH removes the risk of tracking fly balls and running routes in right field, but Judge is not just a bat — he is one of the best defensive outfielders in the American League. Testing the calf in a playoff game against a live pitcher, with real base-running situations, is different from controlled rehabilitation sprints.

The Yankees would not push him into an ALDS situation unless the medical staff and Judge himself were confident the risk was manageable. A re-injury mid-series would be far more damaging than holding him out through Game 1.

The Yankees Without Judge

New York's Wild Card performance answered one of the questions that lingered when Judge went down in mid-September: could this lineup compete without him? The answer was a resounding yes.

Jake Schlittler was dominant in Game 1, going 6.1 innings with 12 strikeouts. The bullpen backed him up. Rice gave the lineup an unexpected jolt from the middle of the order. The Yankees looked like a fully functioning postseason team, not a group held together by one superstar's presence.

That depth makes Judge's return feel like a bonus rather than a necessity. If he is healthy, he makes the Yankees arguably the most dangerous team in the American League. If he is not ready, they have shown they can win without him.

What's Next

The Yankees face the Tampa Bay Rays in ALDS Game 1 on Saturday, October 3 at 6:30 PM ET. A final roster decision on Aaron Judge is expected ahead of the series opener. If he is added and plays, it will be his first game action since September 19. If he is held out again, the Yankees have enough to compete — though they will want to get their best player back as soon as the medical staff signs off.

Judge's status will be the most-watched injury update in baseball between now and first pitch Saturday.
