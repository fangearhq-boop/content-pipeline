Braves Eliminate Phillies 6-2 in Game 3, Advance to NLDS Against Dodgers

By Marcus Cole

October 2, 2026

Ballpark Banter

Braves Power Past Phillies at Citizens Bank Park

Philadelphia came into Wednesday night's winner-take-all contest riding high. The Phillies had stolen Game 2 on a walk-off by Alec Bohm, extending the Wild Card series to a decisive third game and putting the crowd at Citizens Bank Park firmly in their corner. What followed was not the dramatic home finish Philadelphia had envisioned.

The Atlanta Braves silenced the park early and never let the Phillies back in, defeating Philadelphia 6-2 in Game 3 to claim the Wild Card series and earn a berth in the National League Division Series against the Los Angeles Dodgers. Atlanta's three home runs did all the damage against a Phillies pitching staff that had been among the best in the NL all season.

Harris Sets the Tone in the First

The Braves needed no warm-up time. Center fielder Michael Harris II stepped to the plate in the first inning against Aaron Nola with two runners aboard and turned on a fastball, sending a three-run home run into the left-field seats. Atlanta led 3-0 before Philadelphia had recorded three outs.

Nola had been one of the best starters in the National League this season, and the Phillies were counting on him to give them length in a short series. Instead, the early damage knocked the wind out of Citizens Bank Park and put Atlanta's starter in a comfortable position from the jump. The Braves did not need to manufacture runs against one of the league's elite pitching staffs. They simply hit the ball over the fence.

Albies Pads the Lead, Phillies Never Recover

Atlanta pushed the lead to five in the fourth inning when second baseman Ozzie Albies connected off Zack Wheeler for a two-run home run, pushing the score to 5-0. Wheeler, the Phillies' Game 2 winner, was called on in relief and could not hold the line.

The two-run shot all but ended Philadelphia's evening. The Phillies scratched across a pair of runs in their half of the fourth to make it 5-2, briefly giving Citizens Bank Park something to cheer about. But Atlanta's bullpen slammed the door, and any hope of a Phillies comeback dissolved inning by inning as the night wore on.

First baseman Matt Olson added a solo home run in the eighth to push the final margin to 6-2, putting an emphatic punctuation mark on the series. The three-homer performance was a testament to Atlanta's lineup depth, with different contributors carrying the load at different moments throughout the game.

Ending a Painful Streak

The victory carried added weight in the Braves' clubhouse. Atlanta had lost six consecutive winner-take-all games heading into Wednesday's contest, a stretch that had cast a long shadow over a franchise that considered itself a perennial contender. That streak is over. The Braves proved they could close out a series when it mattered most, and they did it on the road against a hostile crowd and a capable Phillies team that had every reason to believe it would force a different outcome.

The Phillies had been favored in many circles to advance after winning Game 2 so dramatically. Bohm's walk-off single in 10 innings had the feel of a turning point, the kind of moment that shifts momentum in a short series. Atlanta responded by playing their cleanest baseball of the series when the pressure was highest.

Philadelphia's offense, which ranked among the NL's most feared units during the regular season, managed only two runs on Wednesday. The Braves kept them off balance all night, and by the sixth inning, the outcome was no longer in doubt.

What's Next

The Braves advance to the National League Division Series to face the Los Angeles Dodgers. Game 1 of the NLDS is scheduled for Saturday, October 3, with first pitch set for 4 p.m. ET on Fox. Atlanta will enter as the lower seed, meaning the first two games of the best-of-five series will be played in Los Angeles. The Dodgers were the top seed in the NL and figure to be a formidable opponent, but the Braves carry momentum and confidence after ending their winner-take-all drought in convincing fashion.
