Padres vs. Brewers NLDS Preview: It Has Never Happened Before — Here's What to Expect

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | October 2, 2026

The San Diego Padres and Milwaukee Brewers have played against each other hundreds of times across their franchises' histories. Not once have they done it in October, in the postseason, when everything is on the line. That changes Saturday night when the NLDS opens in Milwaukee.

How They Got Here

Milwaukee's path was simple: be so good that you did not have to play any Wild Card games. The Brewers finished 103-59, the best record in all of baseball, and earned a first-round bye that gave their rotation a full week of rest before the Division Series.

San Diego's path was scrappier. The Padres knocked off the Chicago Cubs in two games in the Wild Card round, sweeping the series. Gavin Sheets delivered the key blow in Game 2, a pinch-hit two-run home run that sealed the win. The Padres' lineup has not been perfect all season, but they are a postseason-tested group that knows how to win elimination games.

Why the Brewers Are Favored

Milwaukee's pitching staff is the best argument for the Brewers entering this series. Their rotation posted a 3.61 ERA over the course of the regular season, a remarkable number given that most of their starters entered 2026 with fewer than two years of Major League service time. Young arms staying healthy and effective all season long — that is a sign of quality player development and a well-structured rotation plan.

The Brewers also benefit from playing in Milwaukee for the first two games. Home-field advantage matters in a short series, and the Brewers earned theirs by winning more games than anyone else in baseball.

Oddsmakers have installed Milwaukee as favorites, and the rest week does not hurt their case.

Why the Padres Can Win This

San Diego won the regular-season series between these two teams four games to two. Over the course of six head-to-head matchups, the Padres showed they could beat Milwaukee when it mattered.

More importantly, the Padres have the kind of individual talent that changes a series. Fernando Tatis Jr. is as dangerous a hitter as any in the National League when he is locked in. Manny Machado brings postseason experience and the ability to carry a lineup through cold stretches. And closer Mason Miller gives San Diego a shutdown option at the back of the bullpen that not every team can match.

The Padres also have the advantage of fresh legs from a short Wild Card series. They are not exhausted from a deep postseason run — they are ready to play.

The Key Storyline

Youth versus experience. Milwaukee's young rotation is the most interesting variable in this series. The Brewers' starters have been outstanding in the regular season, but they will be pitching in the postseason for the first time in most cases. Facing Tatis, Machado, and a motivated Padres offense in a playoff atmosphere is different from a September game with nothing on the line.

If Milwaukee's young arms hold up under pressure, the Brewers win this series. If the Padres can expose them early — get into the bullpen and create leverage situations — San Diego has the firepower to pull off the upset.

What's Next

NLDS Game 1 is Saturday, October 3 at approximately 8:30 PM ET in Milwaukee. Game 2 is Sunday, October 4, at 3:00 PM ET. The series shifts to San Diego for Game 3 on Tuesday, October 6. For any series where two teams have never met in the postseason before, Game 1 sets a tone that can carry through the entire matchup. Saturday night at American Family Field is going to tell us a lot about which way this series is headed.
