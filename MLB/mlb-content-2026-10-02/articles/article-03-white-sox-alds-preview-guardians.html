White Sox ALDS Preview: Can the South Side's Cinderella Run Continue Against the Guardians?

By Ryan Calloway

October 2, 2026

Ballpark Banter

A Drought Is Over. The Harder Work Begins.

For the first time since 2005, the Chicago White Sox have won a playoff series. Two wins over the Houston Astros in the Wild Card round -- 6-3 in Game 1 and 7-3 in Game 2 -- produced a clean sweep and, more importantly, a reminder that this White Sox team belongs in October. The South Side celebrated. Now comes the hard part.

Chicago advances to face the Cleveland Guardians in the American League Division Series. Game 1 is Saturday, October 3, at 1 p.m. ET on TBS. The venue is Progressive Field in Cleveland, where the Guardians will have home-field advantage for the first two games of the best-of-five series. Games 3, 4, and 5, if necessary, will be played at Guaranteed Rate Field in Chicago.

What the White Sox Showed in the Wild Card

The sweep of Houston was a statement. The Astros were not a pushover, and the White Sox handled them from start to finish. Tim Montgomery set the tone in Game 1 with a 432-foot home run, and Chicago's pitching staff did not allow Houston to string together the sustained rallies that have characterized that franchise for years. Power from unexpected sources and a disciplined bullpen carried the White Sox through, and they will need both attributes again in Cleveland.

The Guardians Are a Different Kind of Challenge

Cleveland finished the regular season on one of the best runs in baseball. From August 20 onward, the Guardians went 24-11, a pace that would translate to more than 90 wins over a full season. They did not back into the Division Series. They pushed their way in, and they enter the series with genuine confidence built over six weeks of winning baseball.

The biggest reason to worry about Cleveland is the pitching staff. Parker Messick, an AL Cy Young Award finalist, is scheduled to start Game 1. Gavin Williams, the league's strikeout leader, gets Game 2. The White Sox will face two of the best pitchers in the American League in the first two games of the series, both at Progressive Field.

Messick has been one of the most dominant starters in baseball this season, and his Cy Young candidacy is well-earned. Williams missing from the award conversation only illustrates how deep Cleveland's starting staff runs. The White Sox lineup that roughed up Houston will need to show it can perform against a different caliber of competition on Saturday and Sunday.

The Regular-Season Edge and What It Means

During the regular season, the White Sox held a 7-6 edge over Cleveland in head-to-head games. It is a narrow advantage, but it tells you something: Chicago did not fold against a Guardians team that was playing excellent baseball in the second half. These teams are familiar with each other's tendencies, and that familiarity cuts both ways heading into a five-game series.

The Cinderella Question

Every postseason produces a team that exceeds expectations, and the 2026 White Sox have positioned themselves as that team in the American League. The 21-year wait for a playoff series victory is over. The narrative has shifted from heartbreak to momentum.

Whether Chicago can extend the run against Cleveland depends on several factors. Can the offense generate enough against Messick and Williams in the first two road games to return to Guaranteed Rate Field with the series tied or better? Can the White Sox bullpen match what Cleveland's relief corps brings over five games? And can the energy that carried them through the Wild Card sustain itself against a Guardians team that has been built specifically to compete deep into October?

The questions are real. So is the momentum.

What's Next

The White Sox and Guardians open their American League Division Series on Saturday, October 3, at 1 p.m. ET on TBS. Game 1 will be played at Progressive Field in Cleveland. Parker Messick starts for the Guardians against a White Sox team playing with nothing to lose and 21 years of anticipation behind them. Games 3 through 5, if necessary, shift to Guaranteed Rate Field in Chicago.
