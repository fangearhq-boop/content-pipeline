### STORY 1: Braves Eliminate Phillies in Game 3

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 8:00 AM ET

The Braves are going to the NLDS — and they made sure everyone knew it from the first inning.

Atlanta entered Game 3 of the NL Wild Card Series on the road in Philadelphia with a shot at ending their six-game losing streak in winner-take-all situations. They handled it emphatically.

Michael Harris II greeted Aaron Nola with a three-run homer in the first inning, giving Atlanta a 3-0 lead before Philly could blink. Ozzie Albies added a two-run shot off Zack Wheeler in the fourth. Matt Olson finished the scoring in the eighth with a solo blast. Final: Braves 6, Phillies 2.

The Phillies, who had battled back from a Game 1 loss to win Game 2 in 10 innings on Kyle Bohm's walk-off, never had a chance in the decisive game. The Braves' home run barrage was too much to overcome.

Atlanta now faces the Los Angeles Dodgers in the NLDS, with Game 1 scheduled for Saturday at 4 PM ET on Fox. The Braves-Dodgers matchup is one of the most anticipated series of the postseason.

### STORY 3: White Sox ALDS Preview — Facing Guardians in an AL Central Showdown

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 10:00 AM ET

The Chicago White Sox are riding something. Let's see if it carries over to Cleveland.

After 20 years without a playoff series win — the last was in 2005, the year they won the World Series — the White Sox just swept the Houston Astros in the Wild Card round. Now they face the Cleveland Guardians in the ALDS, starting Saturday at 1 PM ET at Progressive Field.

Cleveland is the better team on paper. The Guardians went 24-11 from August 20 onward and will throw likely AL Cy Young finalist Parker Messick in Game 1, followed by league strikeout leader Gavin Williams in Game 2. They are deep, they are well-rested, and they have home-field advantage.

But the White Sox went 7-6 against Cleveland in the regular season, so they are not intimidated. And momentum matters in October. After two straight clutch wins over the Astros, the South Side is energized.

Game 1 is on TBS, Saturday at 1 PM ET.

### STORY 4: Aaron Judge Injury Update — Will He Play vs. the Rays?

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 6:00 PM ET

The biggest question in baseball this postseason isn't about a series outcome. It's about one player.

Aaron Judge has been out since September 19 with a moderate-grade strain of the soleus muscle in his right calf. He was not on New York's Wild Card roster for the sweep of the Red Sox — a series in which Ben Rice more than held his own, launching multiple home runs including a grand slam as the Yankees outscored Boston 18-2 across two games.

Now comes the ALDS, which starts Saturday at 6:30 PM ET against the Tampa Bay Rays. Under the roster rules, Judge is eligible to be activated for the Division Series — and everything points to him being ready to try.

Here's the catch: Judge wants to come back as a full outfielder, not just a designated hitter. The calf matters on the bases and tracking fly balls in right field. Testing it in a playoff game is different from a bullpen session, and the Yankees have to decide whether the risk is worth it.

If he's right, the Yankees go from a very good team to the most dangerous team in the American League.

### STORY 5: Padres vs. Brewers NLDS Preview

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 8:00 PM ET

It has never happened before. The San Diego Padres and Milwaukee Brewers have never met in the postseason — not once, in either franchise's history. That changes Saturday night.

This is a fascinating matchup of contrasting styles.

Milwaukee earned the No. 1 seed in the National League with a 103-59 record — the best in all of baseball. The Brewers sat out the Wild Card round entirely, resting their rotation for a full week while the Padres were in a fight with the Cubs. Their young starting rotation — which posted a 3.61 ERA despite a roster full of players with under two years of MLB service — will be fresh.

San Diego is battle-tested. The Padres swept Chicago in the Wild Card, with Gavin Sheets delivering a pinch-hit two-run homer that sealed Game 2. Fernando Tatis Jr., Manny Machado, and closer Mason Miller give them a dangerous lineup and bullpen.

Milwaukee is favored, but the Padres won the regular-season series between these two teams 4-2. This feels like a series that could go the distance.

Game 1 is Saturday night in Milwaukee.
