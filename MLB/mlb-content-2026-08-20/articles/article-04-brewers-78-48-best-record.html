The Brewers Have the Best Record in Baseball — Here's What That Means for October

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | August 20, 2026

Two nights ago, the Milwaukee Brewers beat the Seattle Mariners 22-0 in one of the most lopsided games in baseball history — tying the modern-era record for the most lopsided shutout since 1900. The final score felt like a football result. The Brewers scored nine runs in the eighth inning alone, aided in part by Julio Rodriguez's baserunning gaffe: he caught a flyball with the bases loaded and threw the ball into the stands, believing it was the third out, when it was not.

That kind of win grabs headlines. But the headline that matters most for Milwaukee's October chances is quieter, and more important: the Brewers are now 78-48, and that is the best record in all of baseball.

What 78-48 Actually Means

Best record in baseball is not just a talking point. It has structural implications for the postseason bracket. With 78 wins and 48 losses through Wednesday's games, the Brewers sit two games ahead of the Los Angeles Dodgers (76-51) for the top spot in all of MLB. Ahead of the Atlanta Braves (74-53), ahead of everyone.

If the postseason started today, the Brewers would host every National League series through the NL Championship Series. Home field advantage in October at American Family Field — in a market where the fanbase has grown into one of the most passionate in the league — is a genuine asset, not just a geographic designation.

The path to the World Series runs through Milwaukee right now.

Misiorowski at the Center of Everything

Jacob Misiorowski (12-5, 1.76 ERA, 204+ strikeouts) is the NL Cy Young Award favorite at -550 odds, and he is the player who makes the Brewers' October outlook qualitatively different from any statistical argument you can construct.

A starting pitcher with a sub-2.00 ERA and more than 200 strikeouts is the kind of advantage that can swing a series in October. In a short-series format where matchups are stacked and managers can manipulate rotation order, having Misiorowski available in Game 1 of any series the Brewers play is the equivalent of getting a head start every time.

He is not the only reason the Brewers are 78-48. Their offense has been consistent, their defense has been reliable, and their bullpen has not been a liability. But Misiorowski is the reason the Brewers are not just a good team — they are a team that can seriously contend for a World Series championship.

The 22-0 Game in Context

The blowout of Seattle on August 18 was historic in margin, but it is worth noting what drove it beyond Rodriguez's error. Christian Yelich hit a three-run home run. David Hamilton hit a three-run home run. Luis Lara hit a three-run home run. Jake Bauers hit a two-run homer. Three separate players connecting for three-run shots in a single game is a demonstration of lineup depth, not a fluke of circumstance.

Milwaukee is not a one-trick offensive team. They can beat you in different ways on different nights. That versatility, combined with Misiorowski at the front of the rotation and a bullpen that has supported him effectively, is why the best record in baseball is not a surprise to anyone who has been watching this team since April.

What's Next

The Brewers have 36 games remaining to hold the best record in baseball and prepare for a playoff run. Misiorowski's next start — after some extra rest — is expected in the coming days. The NL bracket will continue to take shape over the final weeks, with the Dodgers and Braves also positioned as legitimate contenders. But for now, and for the foreseeable future, the road to the World Series runs through American Family Field.
