Yordan Alvarez Is Still Chasing the Triple Crown — And the Home Run Race Is Tightening

By Ryan Calloway | Ballpark Banter | August 20, 2026

With 36 games remaining in the 2026 regular season, Yordan Alvarez is still leading all three Triple Crown categories — batting average, home runs, and runs batted in — in the American League. If he holds on, he will be the first player to win the Triple Crown since Miguel Cabrera did it with the Detroit Tigers in 2012.

That would be 14 years without a Triple Crown winner. In a sport that sees its statistical records approached and broken regularly, that particular benchmark has proven uniquely elusive — which is why Alvarez's chase is generating the attention it deserves.

But the home run race has gotten genuinely tense over the last two weeks.

The Three Categories

On batting average, Alvarez's lead is essentially insurmountable over 36 games. He's hitting .329 — nearly 30 points ahead of the next closest AL hitter. That kind of gap does not evaporate in a month and a half of baseball. The batting average title is, barring a dramatic collapse, Alvarez's.

On RBI, his lead is substantial. With 88 RBIs and a lineup around him that continues to put runners on base, Alvarez is well-positioned to maintain the lead through September. RBI totals can shift with lineup construction and opportunities, but Alvarez's number reflects a season's worth of sustained production rather than a hot streak.

On home runs — that is where the drama lives.

Alvarez has 36 home runs. Kyle Schwarber, the Philadelphia Phillies slugger, sits at 37 — one home run ahead of Alvarez. Junior Caminero has been on a tear in August and is closing the gap from the other direction. The four-way race for the HR title is one of the most competitive in recent memory.

The Cabrera Comparison

Miguel Cabrera's 2012 Triple Crown was the last of its kind in MLB. He led the AL in batting average, home runs, and RBI — three categories that almost never align in one player across a full season in the modern game, because the specialization of lineups, the rise of on-base-focused batting theory, and the sheer depth of pitching talent make dominance in all three simultaneously extraordinarily difficult.

Alvarez's profile is different from Cabrera's but similarly complete. He is a switch-hitter with elite bat speed, exceptional pitch recognition, and the kind of production across multiple categories that defines the very best hitters in the game. That he is doing this while the Astros have underperformed as a team (sitting at .500 despite Alvarez's historic season) only underlines how dominant his individual performance has been.

What Has to Happen

For Alvarez to complete the Triple Crown, he needs to either get hot at the plate and reclaim the HR lead from Schwarber, or hold on as Schwarber cools off. Schwarber has been one of the most consistent power hitters in the National League — but he is in the other league, which means the competition between them is statistical rather than head-to-head. The HR race is ultimately about who hits more over the next 36 games.

The RBI and batting average races appear largely settled. The home run title is the one that remains genuinely in play — and watching whether Alvarez or Schwarber (or Caminero) finishes with the most will be one of the storylines of September.

What's Next

The Astros continue their September run-in over 36 remaining games. Alvarez's performance during that stretch will either cement or complicate his Triple Crown case. The HR race will update daily. Miguel Cabrera won the Triple Crown in 2012 — the next one has been 14 years in the making.
