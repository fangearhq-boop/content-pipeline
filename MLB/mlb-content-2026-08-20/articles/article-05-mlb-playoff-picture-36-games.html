36 Games Left: The 2026 MLB Playoff Picture Is Almost Set — Almost

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | August 20, 2026

With 36 games remaining in the 2026 regular season, the playoff picture is clearer than it was in July — but it is not yet completely clear. Some division races are decided. Some wild card spots remain genuinely in play. And at least one division features a surprise contender and a surprise non-contender that nobody expected to be in those positions this late in the year.

Here is where things stand heading into the final stretch of the 2026 season.

National League

The Milwaukee Brewers (78-48) have the best record in all of baseball and are the class of the National League Central. Jacob Misiorowski's Cy Young campaign, the historic 22-0 rout of Seattle, and a lineup that scores in multiple ways have made the Brewers the most complete team in the sport. Their path to the World Series runs through homefield advantage in the NL bracket.

The Atlanta Braves (74-53) lead the NL East, though the Phillies (68-58) are close enough to make the final month interesting. The Phillies are more likely to end up in the wild card, where they currently hold the No. 2 spot.

In the NL West, the Los Angeles Dodgers (76-51) have held the division lead despite an inconsistent season by their historical standards. Shohei Ohtani's two-way production has kept the Dodgers afloat.

The NL Wild Card race is the most settled of the four: the Cubs (73-53, No. 1) and Phillies (68-58, No. 2) look locked in, with the Padres (67-59) holding the third spot and the Diamondbacks one game back. The Cubs' five-game lead at No. 1 gives them the best wild card seeding available and positions them for a home wild card game.

American League

The Tampa Bay Rays (75-50) are the class of the AL East, leading the division by a comfortable margin and positioning themselves as a legitimate AL playoff threat. The Rays' run prevention, which has been elite all season, makes them dangerous against any opponent.

The AL Central features the most surprising leader: the Chicago White Sox (66-60). A .524 winning percentage would not hold the AL Central lead in most years, but the division has played to a lower competitive standard in 2026. The White Sox lead by 3.5 games and appear likely to hold on despite a record that would be a wild card team at best in either coast division.

The biggest surprise in the AL: the Houston Astros sitting at 63-63. Exactly .500, despite Yordan Alvarez's Triple Crown-caliber season. It is one of the starkest examples of individual excellence coexisting with team mediocrity in recent memory. The Astros are out of the playoff picture as currently constituted unless they go on a run in the final 36 games.

The AL Wild Card race — Yankees, Red Sox, and Twins are in the conversation — has more life than the NL equivalent. Any of those three teams could make a move in the final weeks, and the differences between spots are close enough to make late-September Yankee Stadium starts meaningful again.

The Stretch Run to Watch

The most compelling remaining races: Can the Diamondbacks catch the Padres for the third NL Wild Card spot? Can the Astros, despite Alvarez's historic season, get back into the AL picture? And will the White Sox hold the AL Central lead long enough to avoid a collapse?

None of these questions has an obvious answer yet. Thirty-six games is a meaningful amount of baseball — enough for a team to go 25-11 or 11-25 and completely change the picture. What we know today is a reasonable snapshot, not a final answer.

What's Next

The stretch run begins now. Every series in the final six weeks carries postseason implications for at least one team in the field. The wild card races — in both leagues — will be resolved by the final weekend of the regular season. And the Brewers, with the best record in baseball and Misiorowski on the mound, are already looking ahead to October.
