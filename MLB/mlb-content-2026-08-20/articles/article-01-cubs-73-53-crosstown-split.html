Cubs Drop Crosstown Finale 3-0 But Still Own NL Wild Card Lead With 36 Games to Play

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | August 20, 2026

The White Sox took the finale of the Crosstown Series on Wednesday night, beating the Cubs 3-0 behind a sharp outing from Jose Urquidy and Andrew Benintendi's go-ahead RBI single in the sixth inning. It was the kind of clean, well-executed win Chicago's South Side team needed — and it gave them the split in a series the Cubs won two of three.

But let's be clear about what Wednesday's result actually means for the Cubs' October chances: not much, at least not yet.

At 73-53, the Cubs still sit atop the National League Wild Card standings by five games over the Philadelphia Phillies (68-58). The Padres are six back. With 36 games remaining, the Cubs would need to collapse at a historic rate for any of those teams to catch them. History says that won't happen. Numbers say the Cubs are in.

How Wednesday Unfolded

Urquidy was the story on the South Side. He entered the third inning and struck out eight White Sox hitters without issuing a walk, allowing just one run on six hits in 5 1/3 innings of work. For a White Sox rotation that has sometimes leaned on its bullpen more than it has liked, Urquidy's length was a genuine win for the staff.

Benintendi's sixth-inning RBI single broke a scoreless tie and gave Chicago's South Side team the lead it would not relinquish. Montgomery added a two-run double in the eighth — the ball glancing off the glove of Cubs first baseman Michael Busch — and that was all the separation the White Sox needed.

Clay Holmes gave the Cubs a quality outing on the other side, allowing one run on six hits in 5 1/3 innings, but Wednesday was the kind of night where the Cubs' offense simply could not generate anything against a pitcher who was operating on a high level.

PCA's MVP Case Doesn't Live or Die on One Game

Pete Crow-Armstrong's 2026 season remains one of the most compelling individual cases for the National League Most Valuable Player Award in recent memory. A .281 batting average, 30 home runs, 78 RBIs, 31 stolen bases, and a 7.7 WAR — calculated by both Baseball-Reference and FanGraphs as meaningfully ahead of the competition — make him the clear frontrunner despite Wednesday's hitless night.

The two-man MVP conversation between PCA and Shohei Ohtani has been a through-line of the 2026 season, and that conversation has generally favored Crow-Armstrong when advanced metrics enter the picture. Ohtani's 6.4 WAR, while impressive for a two-way player, trails Crow-Armstrong's 7.7 in both calculation systems.

Thirty-six games is plenty of time for Crow-Armstrong to close the case. The Cubs need to stay healthy, stay hot, and get into October — and then the MVP conversation becomes secondary to the actual prize.

Where the NL Wild Card Stands

The Cubs are five games ahead of the Phillies and six ahead of the Padres in the NL Wild Card race. The three wild card spots are the target — two of them appear to be effectively claimed by the Cubs and Phillies, with the Padres and Diamondbacks fighting for the third.

Thirty-six games is a long time. It is also, historically, not nearly enough time to overcome a five-game deficit against a team with the Cubs' pitching depth and lineup balance. Unless something goes very wrong in the next month, the Cubs will be playing in October.

What's Next

The Cubs open a new series Thursday. The Crosstown Schedule is complete; both Chicago teams now turn to the final six weeks of the regular season. For the Cubs, the focus shifts to locking up home-field advantage in the wild card round — and for PCA, to building the kind of final-month stat line that closes the MVP conversation before voting begins.
