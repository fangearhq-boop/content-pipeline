Cardinals Give Joshua Baez First Taste of Leadoff Spot — The Adjustment Period Has Begun

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | August 20, 2026

Joshua Baez got a day off Wednesday against the Reds — a quiet development that might have gone unnoticed if not for what happened earlier in the week. Because earlier in the week, Cardinals manager Oliver Marmol tried something different: he put the 21-year-old rookie in the leadoff spot.

It was a decision that tells you a lot about how the Cardinals are thinking about what they have on their hands. Five days into his major league career, the adjustment period is underway.

What the Debut Established

Baez's major league debut on August 15 against the Cubs at Wrigley Field was unlike anything the sport had seen before. He homered in his first at-bat. Then again in his second. Then again in his third — becoming the first player in MLB history to hit three home runs in his first three career at-bats. His first home run traveled 449 feet. He finished the debut 3-for-4 with five RBIs in a Cardinals victory.

Before the call-up, Baez had hit 34 home runs at the Triple-A level. The power is real and it is generational. That much was established in three at-bats.

Where He Hits Is the Question

But power alone does not answer the lineup construction question. Baez is currently batting .251/.332/.552 with 12 home runs through his first week of MLB games. The on-base percentage is solid, the slugging is exceptional, and the 11 stolen bases suggest a player with genuine speed to complement the power. The issue is a 32.7 percent strikeout rate that, while not unusual for a 21-year-old making their big league debut, raises legitimate questions about how the Cardinals will handle the contact challenge.

Marmol's decision to try him at leadoff was not a random experiment. It was a deliberate test of a specific question: can Baez use his on-base skills and athleticism to function as a table-setter, or is his value maximized in the middle of the order where his raw power can drive in runners?

Neither answer is wrong. Both answers have merit. The Cardinals are in the enviable position of having a player talented enough to make the question worth asking.

The Day Off and What It Means

Baez's absence from Wednesday's lineup was almost certainly precautionary — a rest day for a 21-year-old playing in his first week of major league baseball, managing physical workload and mental adjustment simultaneously. There is no reason to read anything negative into a veteran manager giving a rookie a day to breathe in the middle of his first big league week.

What matters more is what happens over the next several weeks as MLB pitchers continue to make adjustments. Baez's debut was extraordinary, but pitchers are already watching film, identifying approach tendencies, and working to exploit that strikeout rate. How Baez responds to those counter-adjustments will determine whether this debut was the start of a star career or a cautionary tale about small-sample theatrics.

The early signs are encouraging. The athleticism, the raw power, and the organization around him in St. Louis all point toward someone with a real chance to be something special.

What's Next

Baez is expected to return to the Cardinals lineup Thursday. The Cardinals' series against Cincinnati continues. Watch where Marmol slots him in the order — the leadoff experiment is likely ongoing, and the deployment pattern will tell you a lot about how the coaching staff is thinking about their newest addition for the final six weeks of the season and beyond.
