Michael King's Near-No-Hitter Powers Padres Past Cubs, 8-0

By Marcus Cole | September 30, 2026

King carried a no-hitter into the 7th inning. Tatis hit a leadoff homer on the first pitch of the game. Machado homered twice. The Padres sent a message to the rest of October: San Diego is here.

There was a moment in the 7th inning at Petco Park when the crowd fell into the kind of quiet that only happens in baseball. Michael King was on the mound with one out, carrying a no-hitter. Every pitch had become a communal experience. Every Cubs at-bat was existential.

Nico Hoerner broke it with a soft infield single. The Cubs' only hit. King had gone 6 2/3 no-hit innings in a playoff game, and Hoerner had found the one spot on the diamond that didn't quite behave the way it was supposed to.

King finished with 7 innings, 8 strikeouts, and a performance that will be referenced for years. His no-hit bid was the longest individual postseason no-hit attempt since Aníbal Sánchez went 7 2/3 innings for the Washington Nationals in the 2019 NLCS.

The Padres won 8-0.

Tatis Sets the Tone Before the First Inning Ends

Fernando Tatis Jr. did not wait for the moment to come to him. On the very first pitch of the Wild Card series — the very first pitch — he hit it out of the park. A leadoff home run on pitch No. 1 of the 2026 Padres postseason, and according to franchise historians, the first leadoff playoff home run in Padres history.

The sellout crowd at Petco Park erupted and did not fully calm down for eight innings. The tone was set before the game had technically begun to develop any narrative tension. Manny Machado added another home run in the 1st inning and contributed an RBI double in the 3rd. By the time Hoerner recorded that famous infield single, San Diego had already built a 7-0 lead.

What King's Night Means for the Padres

King gave San Diego exactly what a Game 1 starter should: a performance so dominant that it sets the psychological tone for the series and preserves the bullpen for later games. The Cubs were held to one hit. King's command was exceptional. His stuff played up in a way that even the most optimistic Padres fan couldn't have fully anticipated.

This is a San Diego team that has been built for this. Tatis at the top of the order, Machado in the middle of the lineup, and now a starting pitcher who can carry a no-hitter into the 7th inning in a playoff game. These are the ingredients of a World Series team.

What's Next

The Cubs will need a completely different performance in Game 2 on September 30 at 9:00 PM ET. Kevin Gausman starts for Chicago against Nick Pivetta for San Diego. The Cubs have the pitching to compete — Gausman is one of the best starters in the National League — but they need to score some runs. One hit in Game 1 is not a template for winning a playoff series.

The Padres, meanwhile, have demonstrated exactly who they are. Michael King nearly wrote history. Fernando Tatis Jr. started the party on the first pitch. San Diego has made its October statement.
