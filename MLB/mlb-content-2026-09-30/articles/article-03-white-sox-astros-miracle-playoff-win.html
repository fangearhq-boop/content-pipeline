From 121 Losses to a Playoff Win: The White Sox Miracle Run Continues in Houston

By Ryan Calloway | September 30, 2026

Two years after losing an MLB-record 121 games, the Chicago White Sox walked into Minute Maid Park and won a playoff game. Colson Montgomery homered. The South Side is alive.

The number that matters most heading into this series isn't 6-3. It's 121.

In 2024, the Chicago White Sox lost 121 games — the most losses by any team in the modern 162-game era, the worst single-season record since the 1899 Cleveland Spiders played in a different century. They followed that with 60-102 in 2025 and had gone 61-101 in 2023. Three straight 100-loss seasons. No team in baseball history had ever descended that far and then climbed back to the playoffs the following year.

On September 29, 2026, the White Sox made that climb official in the most dramatic way possible. They went to Houston — where the Astros have made the playoffs nine times in ten years — and won Game 1, 6-3.

Montgomery's Statement Home Run

Colson Montgomery was the story of the game. The shortstop hit a 432-foot solo home run to dead center field in the 4th inning — his 31st of the regular season. The irony is thick: Montgomery also set a single-season MLB strikeout record at 227 Ks during the regular season, a symbol of the feast-or-famine volatility that defines his game. Manager Will Venable has batted him 9th in the lineup, trusting him to be an impact bat from the bottom of the order.

On the biggest stage of his career, with the most improbable backdrop in baseball, Montgomery hit it 432 feet. Sometimes the story writes itself.

How the White Sox Won

The game was never close after the early innings. Sam Antonacci led off the Chicago lineup and contributed an RBI single in the 2nd inning to push the lead to 3-0 with bases loaded. Chase Meidroth added a bases-loaded RBI single in the 5th to make it 5-0. Montgomery's homer had already set the tone in the 4th.

The Astros answered when Nelson Velázquez hit a two-run pinch-hit home run to cut it to 5-2 in the 5th. But the White Sox held on, scoring an insurance run to finish at 6-3. The team deployed an opener strategy — consistent with their 31 opener appearances during the regular season — and got multiple arms through Houston's lineup.

The Context That Makes This Special

The White Sox didn't just make the playoffs. They made the playoffs with 1.1 percent Opening Day odds — the third-lowest probability in baseball entering the 2026 season. They built their identity around 11 walk-off wins, 35 come-from-behind victories, and a manager in Will Venable who never wavered publicly about what this team could be.

They clinched a playoff berth on September 24 by beating the Kansas City Royals 9-1. They finished 84-78. Both Chicago teams — the Cubs and White Sox — made the playoffs in the same year for only the fourth time in MLB history.

And now they've won a postseason game for the first time since 2021.

What's Next

Game 2 is September 30. The Astros — a nine-time playoff team in ten years — are not done. They have the talent, the experience, and the motivation of a team that does not lose playoff series easily. Houston will come out with everything they have in a must-win situation.

But the White Sox have already done the impossible once. They're not afraid of this stage. And the 40,000-plus fans on Chicago's South Side who lived through 121 losses in a single season are watching something that sports stories are made of.
