Wild Card Day 1: Home Teams Go 3-1 as Yankees and Padres Dominate

By Marcus Cole | September 30, 2026

Four games. Four compelling stories. The 2026 MLB Wild Card round opened September 29 with blowouts in the Bronx and San Diego, a miracle in Houston, and a late-inning classic in Atlanta.

Wild Card Day 1 delivered everything October baseball promises. Four games. Four storylines. And enough drama to fill a week's worth of highlights. Here's how it went.

Home teams went 3-1 across all four Wild Card series, putting three road teams in immediate must-win situations entering Game 2 on September 30.

Braves 5, Phillies 3: Riley Comes Through

At Truist Park, the game was tight until it wasn't. Atlanta third baseman Austin Riley hit a go-ahead three-run home run off Phillies All-Star closer Jhoan Duran with two outs in the 8th inning to break a tie and give the Braves the series lead. The Phillies — one of the best offensive teams in the National League during the regular season — now face a must-win Game 2 at 1:00 PM ET.

White Sox 6, Astros 3: History on the Road

The White Sox walked into Minute Maid Park and won. That simple sentence carries historic weight. Chicago became the first team in MLB history to make the playoffs after three consecutive 100-loss seasons — 2023 (61-101), 2024 (41-121, an MLB record), and 2025 (60-102). They had 1.1 percent Opening Day playoff odds. They won 84 games in 2026. And shortstop Colson Montgomery hit a 432-foot solo home run in the 4th inning to help Chicago build a 5-0 lead. The Astros cut it to 5-2 but couldn't complete the comeback. White Sox win, 6-3.

Yankees 9, Red Sox 0: Without Their MVP

Aaron Judge did not play. Aaron Judge will not play in this series. And the Yankees won 9-0 anyway. Cam Schlittler delivered 6.1 scoreless innings on 117 pitches, and designated hitter Ben Rice hit two home runs. The difference between these two teams in Game 1 was as large as the final score suggested. Red Sox must respond immediately or face elimination in New York.

Padres 8, Cubs 0: King's Near-Historic Night

Michael King was seven outs away from a postseason no-hitter. He carried the bid into the 7th inning before Nico Hoerner broke it up with a soft infield single — the Cubs' only hit. King finished with 7 innings, 8 strikeouts, and a performance that will be remembered regardless of how the Cubs' hit ended it. Fernando Tatis Jr. hit a leadoff home run on the game's very first pitch — the first leadoff playoff home run in Padres history. The 8-0 final made it official: San Diego is playing its best baseball at the right time.

What's Next

Game 2 of all four series is September 30. The Phillies host the Braves at 1:00 PM ET. The Cubs and Padres go in San Diego at 9:00 PM ET, with Kevin Gausman starting for Chicago against Nick Pivetta. The Yankees and Red Sox, along with the White Sox and Astros, have Game 2 times that are TBD. Three of the four road teams are one loss from elimination. History says they'll fight. Tuesday night's Game 1s showed how strong the home-field advantages really are.
