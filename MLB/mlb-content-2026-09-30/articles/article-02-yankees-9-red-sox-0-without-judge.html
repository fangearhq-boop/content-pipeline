Without Aaron Judge, the Yankees' Depth Devoured the Red Sox, 9-0

By Jake Torres | September 30, 2026

Cam Schlittler threw 6.1 scoreless innings. Ben Rice hit two home runs. Aaron Judge watched from the bench with a calf injury. And the Yankees won by nine. The story of Game 1 isn't that Judge didn't play — it's that the Yankees are so deep, it barely mattered.

There is a version of the 2026 Wild Card story that centers entirely on Aaron Judge's absence. It is not the story the Yankees told on September 29.

The three-time American League Most Valuable Player sat out Game 1 against the Red Sox, officially left off the Wild Card Series roster due to a right calf and soleus strain that has kept him out since September 16. He had arrived at the ballpark, taken live batting practice two days before, and made his case to manager Aaron Boone. Boone's response — "I don't think he's all the way back" — was the final word before the game started.

The Yankees won 9-0.

Schlittler and Rice Take Center Stage

In Judge's absence, two Yankees delivered the kind of performances that build October legends. Cam Schlittler, one of the organization's homegrown young starters, was dominant. He threw 6.1 scoreless innings on a career-high 117 pitches — the kind of start that establishes a pitcher's playoff identity. He attacked the Red Sox lineup with conviction and did not waver under the pressure of a postseason start in one of baseball's most storied rivalries.

Ben Rice provided the offensive firepower. The first baseman and designated hitter hit two home runs, driving in multiple runs and making Boston's task difficult from the early innings. Rice's performance in a high-stakes situation — stepping into a lineup that was already short its best player — was a statement about the Yankees' organizational depth.

The Judge Storyline

Judge's injury situation carries layers beyond a single game. He had already missed significant time earlier in 2026 with a rib stress fracture before returning September 7. The calf/soleus strain came shortly after that return, limiting him to just 66 games on the season — a career low. He received a platelet-poor plasma injection to accelerate healing and made his push to play in Game 1 as aggressive as possible.

A surgeon publicly warned that returning too quickly risks an Achilles rupture — a far more severe injury than the current problem. Boone's caution, while frustrating to watch from Judge's perspective, came from medical guidance that the Yankees organization is taking seriously.

The potential upside: if the Yankees advance from the Wild Card round, Judge may be available for the ALDS. A healthy Judge at full capacity in the Division Series would be a significant addition to a team that just won 9-0 without him.

What's Next

The Red Sox now face a must-win Game 2 in New York. The series is best-of-3. Boston has everything it needs to respond — a quality roster, playoff experience, and the motivation of facing elimination. The Yankees, meanwhile, know that Game 1's dominant performance doesn't end the series. They'll need another quality start, and the Red Sox will bring a different approach in Game 2.

But the takeaway from September 29 is clear: the 2026 Yankees are a complete team. They don't need their MVP to win a playoff game by nine. That depth is what World Series champions are built from.
