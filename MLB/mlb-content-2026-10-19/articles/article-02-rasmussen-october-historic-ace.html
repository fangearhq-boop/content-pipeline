Drew Rasmussen Is the Best Pitcher in the 2026 Playoffs

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | October 19, 2026

Start with the numbers.

In ALDS Game 1 against the New York Yankees, Drew Rasmussen threw eight innings, allowed one hit, walked two, and struck out 10. The Rays won 1-0. The 10 strikeouts tied Tyler Glasnow's franchise record for most punchouts by a Tampa Bay pitcher in a single postseason game. It was one of the most dominant starting performances of any October in recent memory.

In ALCS Game 5 at Progressive Field, Rasmussen threw seven innings and allowed one run. The Rays won 3-1 and took a 3-2 series lead heading into tonight's potential clincher at Tropicana Field.

His 2026 postseason line: 1-0, 1.29 ERA, 15 innings pitched. He has allowed one hit in his best outing and one run in his second. He has been, across both starts, as good as a playoff pitcher can be.

The ALDS Opener Was Special

The conversation about October 2026's best pitching performance has to start with what Rasmussen did against the Yankees in the ALDS. Gerrit Cole was the opposing starter. The Rays won on a Jonathan Aranda home run — their only run. The rest of the game belonged to Rasmussen.

Eight innings. One hit. He came within a single base runner of throwing a postseason no-hitter. His 10 strikeouts tied Glasnow's record for the Tampa Bay franchise in a single postseason game. He threw 101 pitches, 69 for strikes, and never let the Yankees offense breathe.

That kind of start — in a winner-take-the-momentum playoff game on the road against a lineup that includes some of baseball's best hitters — is the stuff that defines postseason legacies.

The Regular Season Set the Stage

None of this came out of nowhere. Rasmussen was one of baseball's best pitchers in 2026 before October arrived. He finished the regular season 16-5 with a 2.67 ERA and earned his second consecutive All-Star selection. He was durable, consistent, and dominant in the ways that matter: he didn't blow leads, didn't fall apart in tough situations, and kept his team in games night after night.

That kind of regular season earns trust. It earned the Rays' trust when they handed him the ball for ALDS Game 1, knowing exactly what they were getting.

The World Series Awaits

If Tampa Bay closes out the ALCS tonight — a win away at Tropicana Field — Rasmussen lines up to start World Series Game 1 on October 23 against the Milwaukee Brewers. He would face a Milwaukee team that swept the Dodgers in the NLCS and has been resting for a week, led by Jacob Misiorowski, one of baseball's best starters in his own right in 2026.

An October Game 1 between Rasmussen and Misiorowski, with the best record in each league's playoffs on the line, would be the kind of pitching matchup that becomes a sporting event conversation all week before it happens.

What's Next

ALCS Game 6 is tonight. A Rays win sends Tampa Bay to the World Series — and Rasmussen to the mound in Game 1 against Milwaukee on October 23. If the Guardians force a Game 7, Rasmussen could be available in relief, though Tampa Bay would likely keep him ready for a potential World Series start. Either way, his October so far has already made the case. When the 2026 postseason is written up, his name belongs near the top.
