Milwaukee Has Been Waiting a Week. The World Series Is Four Days Away.

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | October 19, 2026

The Milwaukee Brewers completed their sweep of the Los Angeles Dodgers in the National League Championship Series on October 16. Three days ago. Since then, the Brewers have been doing what teams that clinch early always do — resting, watching, and waiting for the rest of baseball to sort itself out.

That wait ends Tuesday night.

World Series Game 1 is October 23 at American Family Field in Milwaukee. First pitch at 8 p.m. ET on FOX. It will be the first World Series home game Milwaukee has hosted in 44 years, since the franchise appeared in the 1982 Fall Classic.

The Roster That Got Here

Milwaukee didn't sneak into the World Series. The Brewers finished the regular season 103-59 and earned home-field advantage throughout the National League playoffs. They swept the Dodgers — one of the most decorated franchises in recent postseason history — and did it convincingly.

The lineup behind Jacob Misiorowski is deep and dangerous. Brice Turang drove in 100 runs in the regular season and scored in every game of the NLCS, providing the kind of consistent offense that doesn't disappear in big moments. Jackson Chourio made franchise history in the NLCS by hitting his 6th career postseason home run — the most in Milwaukee Brewers history. William Contreras added three home runs in the 2026 playoffs.

And then there is Jake Bauers — the NLCS Most Valuable Player who nearly retired before this season began. His story is the most Milwaukee story on the most Milwaukee team, a player who almost wasn't here at all turning in the performance of his career when it mattered most.

Misiorowski on the Mound for Game 1

Jacob Misiorowski is lined up to start World Series Game 1. In the 2026 regular season, he posted a 1.80 ERA and struck out 252 batters — numbers that placed him among baseball's most dominant starting pitchers. His NLCS performances only strengthened what was already an October reputation.

The World Series matchup will pit Misiorowski against either the Tampa Bay Rays or the Cleveland Guardians, depending on tonight's ALCS Game 6. If Tampa Bay wins, Misiorowski is expected to face Drew Rasmussen in Game 1 — a matchup of two pitchers having historically great Octobers.

What a World Series Means for Milwaukee

Numbers like "44 years" don't fully capture what a World Series return means to a baseball city. The Brewers last hosted a Fall Classic game in 1982. The franchise has given Milwaukee great teams and individual performances over the years — Ryan Braun, Robin Yount, Cecil Cooper, and generations of ballplayers who wore the uniform. But the World Series stage itself has been absent from County Stadium and now American Family Field for nearly half a century.

Tuesday night changes that.

What's Next

ALCS Game 6 is tonight — Rays vs. Guardians at Tropicana Field. The winner advances to face Milwaukee in the World Series beginning October 23. The Brewers will be watching, rested, and ready. American Family Field will be sold out. For Milwaukee, the wait is almost over.
