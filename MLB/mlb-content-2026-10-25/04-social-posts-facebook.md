#### Long-Form Post — 10:30 AM ET

Tomorrow night might be the most compelling individual storyline of the entire World Series.

Freddy Peralta starts Game 3 for the Tampa Bay Rays. He pitches at Tropicana Field — the first World Series game at that venue since 2008. The opponent? Milwaukee. The franchise where Peralta spent eight full seasons.

This is not a brief stop or a cup-of-coffee situation. Peralta was a Brewer from the time he made his MLB debut through his final season in Milwaukee in 2025, when he went 17-6 with a 2.70 ERA. The Brewers traded him to the Mets in January 2026. Tampa Bay acquired him at the trade deadline. And now he pitches in the World Series against the team that defined his career.

He's also the best pitcher in this postseason. Peralta was named ALCS MVP after going 2-0 with a 0.75 ERA against Cleveland — numbers that don't feel real. He comes into Game 3 having been completely dominant.

Robert Gasser starts for Milwaukee. Gasser has been quiet but effective this postseason — 1.00 ERA, including five scoreless innings against the Dodgers in NLCS Game 3. He doesn't create the headlines that Peralta does, but he's been exactly what the Brewers need.

The Milwaukee Brewers bullpen has a 1.33 ERA over 40⅔ postseason innings — and that number includes the NLCS against the Los Angeles Dodgers, where they posted a 0.38 ERA over 23.2 innings against one of the most dangerous offenses in baseball.

In Game 2, the bullpen was handed a 4-0 deficit after Logan Henderson's difficult start. Six different relievers entered. The final tally from the pen: six innings, zero runs. JoJo Romero, Dustin May, DL Hall, Abner Uribe, and Aaron Ashby combined to completely shut down a Tampa Bay lineup that had erupted for four runs in a matter of minutes.

Ashby closed it out with the save — striking out Yandy Díaz on a full count in the ninth inning with the tying run on base.

This is not a hot streak. This is a full postseason run of historically dominant relief pitching. Manager Pat Murphy has built a system where the starter's job is to give five innings of clean work, hand it over, and trust the pen to do the rest. That system is now two wins from delivering Milwaukee its first World Series championship since 1957.

### STORY 5: Rays Down 2-0 — The Path Back

#### Long-Form Post — 6:00 PM ET

History is against the Rays. Teams down 2-0 in the World Series win the championship roughly 21% of the time. That's not impossible, but it requires something exceptional.

Tampa Bay has reason to believe exceptional is possible.

Freddy Peralta starts tomorrow night — the ALCS MVP with a 0.75 ERA in his last three starts. If there's a pitcher who can stop a hot Brewers team cold, it's him. Drew Rasmussen (1.29 ERA in the postseason, 15 innings) is available for later in the series if Tampa Bay can win two or three straight.

Junior Caminero is 22 years old. He hit 45 home runs and drove in 110 runs in 2025, making two All-Star appearances. This October he's hitting .265/.356/.490 with 13 home runs. If there's a player capable of single-handedly shifting a World Series with his bat, he's on this roster.

And then there's the venue. Tropicana Field is not a building known for its atmosphere, but in October, when the Rays are alive and their fans are in, it becomes something different. Games 3, 4, and 5 are all at home. The Rays went 3-1 in the postseason at Tropicana. That matters.

The path back requires winning three of the next five games, at least two at home. It requires Peralta tomorrow. It requires the offense doing what it couldn't sustain in Games 1 and 2. It's hard. It's not impossible.
