Down 2-0 in the World Series: The Rays' Path Back Starts at Tropicana Field

By Marcus Cole | October 25, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

History is not kind to teams trailing 2-0 in the World Series. Historically, teams in that situation win the championship roughly 21 percent of the time. Most fall 2-0, lose again at home, and it ends there.

Tampa Bay has reasons to believe they are different. The evidence is thin right now. The belief is not.

What Went Wrong in Milwaukee

In Game 1, the Rays lost 3-2 in a close game where Milwaukee's bullpen held them scoreless after an early lead. In Game 2, Tampa Bay scored four runs in the first inning — then scored none for the final eight frames as the Brewers' relievers cycled through and shut down every rally.

The pattern is the problem. Tampa Bay can clearly score early. They cannot sustain it. Junior Caminero, Yandy Díaz, and the rest of the lineup have shown flashes but not consistency against a bullpen that has a 1.33 ERA over 40 postseason innings. The Rays' biggest offensive performance of the series — that four-run first inning — still wasn't enough to win.

The solution is not mysterious. The Rays need to score against the Milwaukee bullpen late in games. That requires getting to the Brewers' starter early and forcing Milwaukee to burn its best relievers in innings three through six. It's a specific strategic objective and it hasn't happened yet.

The Reasons for Optimism

Freddy Peralta starts Game 3 on Monday at Tropicana Field. The ALCS MVP has been the best pitcher in this entire postseason. His 0.75 ERA against Cleveland in the American League Championship Series represents one of the best three-start postseason performances in recent memory. If any pitcher can stop this Brewers offense — which is built on depth rather than firepower — it's Peralta.

Drew Rasmussen is also still available. His regular-season ERA was 2.67 and his postseason ERA entering Game 2 was 1.29. The Brewers tagged him in Game 2 to help fuel their comeback, but Rasmussen remains one of the better starting pitchers in this series. He's available for Game 4 or 5.

Junior Caminero is 22 years old. He hit 45 home runs with 110 RBI in 2025 and made two All-Star appearances. In this year's postseason he has been dangerous — hitting .265/.356/.490 with 13 home runs and 27 RBI in 51 games. His ceiling as an October performer hasn't been reached. If the Rays can survive to the middle of the series, Caminero could be the bat that changes everything.

And Tropicana Field is Tampa Bay's home. The Rays went 3-1 there in the postseason. Games 3, 4, and 5 are all at home. Home games in October create pressure on the opposing team that is difficult to quantify. The Brewers have a comfortable series lead, but being on the road with a desperate opponent is not a comfortable situation.

The Math

The Rays need to win five of the next five remaining games to take the series — or, more practically, they need to win three in a row starting Monday to put themselves back in position. No team has ever come back from 3-0 down in the World Series. 2-0 is survivable. 3-0 is essentially final.

Peralta gives Tampa Bay the best possible chance to stay alive Monday night. If the Rays can win Games 3 and 4, they return to Milwaukee for Game 6 with genuine momentum and a series that is suddenly even. That's the realistic path. It requires winning Monday.

What's Next

Game 3 is Monday, October 26 at 8 PM ET on FOX at Tropicana Field. Peralta starts for Tampa Bay, Gasser for Milwaukee. The first World Series game at Tropicana Field since 2008 will tell us whether this series continues or whether the Brewers are on the verge of something historic.
