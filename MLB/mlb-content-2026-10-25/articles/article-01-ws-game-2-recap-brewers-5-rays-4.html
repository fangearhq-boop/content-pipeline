World Series Game 2 Recap: Brewers 5, Rays 4 — Milwaukee Erases 4-Run Deficit, Leads Series 2-0

By Ryan Calloway | October 25, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The Milwaukee Brewers trailed 4-0 before they came to bat in the first inning. By the time Aaron Ashby struck out Yandy Díaz to end the ninth, they had won Game 2 of the World Series 5-4 and taken a commanding 2-0 series lead over the Tampa Bay Rays.

It was a game that briefly looked like a disaster and turned into a testament to why this Milwaukee team is two wins from its first World Series championship.

Tampa Bay's First-Inning Explosion

Tampa Bay did everything right in the first inning. Liam Hicks hit a two-run home run, giving the Rays an immediate 2-0 lead before the Brewers made a single out. Chandler Simpson followed with an RBI single. Junior Caminero added another RBI single, and just like that the scoreboard read 4-0 and Logan Henderson's night was in serious jeopardy.

Henderson lasted three innings. His final line — 3.0 IP, 3 hits, 3 earned runs, 4 strikeouts — reflected a start that was shaky but not catastrophic. The real damage came in bunches, and by the time Henderson was through, the Brewers had turned it over to a bullpen that had spent the entire postseason proving it could handle exactly this kind of situation.

The Brewers Bullpen Does It Again

JoJo Romero, Dustin May, DL Hall, Abner Uribe, and Aaron Ashby combined for six scoreless innings. From the second inning through the final out, Tampa Bay scored zero runs.

It is a pattern that has defined this Milwaukee postseason. The Brewers enter each late game with a 1.33 ERA from their bullpen over 40⅔ postseason innings. During the National League Championship Series alone, they posted a 0.38 ERA over 23.2 innings against the Los Angeles Dodgers — a lineup that includes Shohei Ohtani, Mookie Betts, and Freddie Freeman. The bullpen did not care who was batting. It went out, threw strikes, got outs.

Ashby received the save. With Yandy Díaz at the plate in the ninth inning, the tying run on base, he threw a pitch that Díaz swung through. Game over.

Frelick and Pratt: The Comeback Makers

The offense that finally broke through did so in the most Milwaukee way possible: not through its stars, but through its depth.

Sal Frelick hit the home run that tied the game at 4-4. He went 2-for-4 on the night. For a player who is not Milwaukee's primary power threat, the timing of that swing was the most important hit of his career.

Cooper Pratt delivered the go-ahead run in the seventh inning with an RBI single that put Milwaukee ahead 5-4. Pratt finished 1-for-3 with a walk. When the Brewers talk about depth as a competitive advantage, these are the names they mean — players who won't be on the highlight reel every night but who show up when the moment demands it.

Drew Rasmussen had started for Tampa Bay and lasted just 3.2 innings, giving up 6 hits and 3 earned runs. His ERA in this postseason had stood at 1.29 entering Game 2. The Brewers' comeback erased that command performance in the same game it was supposed to matter most.

What's Next

The World Series shifts to Tropicana Field in St. Petersburg for Games 3 through 5. Game 3 is Monday, October 26 at 8 PM ET on FOX — the first World Series game at Tropicana Field since 2008.

Freddy Peralta starts for Tampa Bay. Robert Gasser starts for Milwaukee. The Rays need to win to stay relevant. The Brewers need one more road win before returning to American Family Field with a chance to clinch.
