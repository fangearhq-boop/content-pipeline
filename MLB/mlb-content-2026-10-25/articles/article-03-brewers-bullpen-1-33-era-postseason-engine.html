The Brewers Bullpen Has a 1.33 ERA in the Postseason — and It Just Saved Game 2

By Jake Torres | October 25, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Down 4-0. Starter pulled after three innings. Everything that shouldn't happen in a World Series game happening to Milwaukee in Game 2.

The Brewers bullpen walked into that mess and delivered six scoreless innings. It is not a surprise anymore. It is who they are.

The Numbers Are Historic

Through the NLCS and the first two games of the World Series, the Milwaukee Brewers bullpen has a 1.33 ERA over 40⅔ innings pitched. During the National League Championship Series against the Dodgers alone — against Shohei Ohtani, Mookie Betts, Freddie Freeman, and the rest of a lineup that had terrorized the league all season — the bullpen allowed runs at a 0.38 ERA rate over 23.2 innings.

These are not numbers produced by facing weak competition or benefiting from favorable sequencing. The Brewers have faced the best offenses remaining in baseball over six weeks, and their relief corps has been the best-performing unit in the postseason by ERA.

Game 2's Six Scoreless

JoJo Romero was first out of the pen. He held Tampa Bay. Dustin May followed. Then DL Hall. Then Abner Uribe. Finally, Aaron Ashby inherited the save situation in the ninth with the Brewers ahead 5-4 and Yandy Díaz at the plate with a runner on base.

Ashby threw a pitch that Díaz swung through. Save. Win. Series lead extended to 2-0.

What makes this bullpen particularly daunting for Tampa Bay is depth. There is no weak link. There is no matchup the Rays can target to get a bridge to the ninth inning without facing genuine danger. Romero, May, Hall, Uribe, and Ashby are five legitimate relievers, each capable of getting outs against any lineup. Manager Pat Murphy has been able to use them in any order based on matchup and situation — and that flexibility makes the entire group harder to prepare for.

The Formula That's Carrying Milwaukee

The Brewers' postseason formula is now established: get five innings from a starter, hand it to the pen, and let the bullpen lock it down. Jacob Misiorowski is still their ace and still their best starter, but his postseason starts have averaged around four to five innings. The bullpen has absorbed the difference every time and allowed zero runs doing it.

For Tampa Bay's lineup — which scored four runs in the first inning of Game 2 and then went silent — the challenge isn't just facing one or two good arms. It's facing a fresh reliever every two innings for the entire second half of each game. That is an exhausting offensive task that requires patience, discipline, and the kind of quality at-bats that are very hard to sustain over a seven-game series.

Games 3 through 5 are at Tropicana Field. The Rays' offense will need to solve this problem away from Milwaukee, in their home environment, starting Monday night.

What's Next

The Brewers bullpen takes a travel day before Game 3 Monday at Tropicana Field. Most of the arms that worked Game 2 will be available by Monday. Robert Gasser starts and gives the pen the option to enter early if needed.

Tampa Bay's best shot at cracking this group is to get to the Brewers starter quickly and force Milwaukee to burn its best arms early in the game — a scenario that requires the Rays to continue scoring runs at the pace they showed in the first inning of Game 2. That's the formula the Rays will need. It hasn't worked for four consecutive innings yet. Monday tests whether it can.
