Sal Frelick Tied It. Cooper Pratt Won It. The Brewers' Depth Is Their Secret Weapon.

By Ryan Calloway | October 25, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

When Tampa Bay jumped ahead 4-0 in the first inning of World Series Game 2, the natural assumption was that the game would turn on Milwaukee's stars. Would Jackson Chourio step up? Would William Contreras make something happen?

The game turned on Sal Frelick and Cooper Pratt — two players who won't be mentioned in the same breath as those names but who delivered the moments that mattered most.

Frelick's Tying Home Run

Sal Frelick is not the first or second option in a comeback rally. He bats toward the bottom of the lineup, he plays a supporting role, and his value to this team is built on consistency and defensive versatility rather than power. Which makes what happened in the middle innings of Game 2 so compelling.

Frelick homered to tie the game at 4-4. He finished 2-for-4 on the night. The Brewers had been down four runs, had watched their starter pulled early, and had spent several innings inching back through the lineup without breaking through. Then Frelick connected, the score was tied, and the entire tenor of the game changed.

American Family Field went from anxious to electric. The bullpen had been doing its job. Now the offense had answered. The game that Tampa Bay thought it was winning was, suddenly, a tie.

Pratt's Go-Ahead Single

Cooper Pratt came to bat in the seventh inning with the game still tied at 4-4. The situation called for a baserunner to score, and Pratt delivered an RBI single that put Milwaukee ahead 5-4.

Pratt finished 1-for-3 with a walk. He is exactly the kind of player this Brewers team relies on — somebody who won't be in every highlight package but who executes when the game is on the line. His hit was the difference between a tied game and a win. In a World Series, that's not a small thing.

Why Depth Wins Championships

The 2026 Milwaukee Brewers won 103 games in the regular season. They did not do it because they have two or three superstars who are better than everyone else. They did it because every position in the lineup has someone capable of contributing on any given night.

When Frelick is your tying home run in a World Series game, that's not luck — it's roster construction. When Pratt is your go-ahead RBI, that's not randomness — it's the result of building a team where the guys at the bottom of the lineup take quality at-bats and capitalize on opportunities.

Tampa Bay's lineup has genuine stars — Junior Caminero (45 HR in 2025), Yandy Díaz, and others. But in the World Series so far, the Rays' offense has scored four runs in one inning and then produced very little afterward. Milwaukee's offense, meanwhile, hasn't needed its stars to win either game. It has needed its depth to keep executing.

Two wins in a World Series isn't a trend. But through two games, the Brewers' formula is working in the exact way their season was designed to produce.

What's Next

The series shifts to Tropicana Field for Games 3 through 5. Milwaukee has the opportunity to take a 3-0 lead Monday night if Robert Gasser and the bullpen can handle Freddy Peralta's performance for Tampa Bay.

If the Brewers do win Game 3, they return to American Family Field needing just one victory to close out their first championship since 1957. The depth that beat Tampa Bay in Games 1 and 2 will be ready.
