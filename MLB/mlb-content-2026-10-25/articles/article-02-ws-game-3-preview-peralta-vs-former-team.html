World Series Game 3 Preview: Freddy Peralta Returns to Face Milwaukee — First WS Game at Tropicana Since 2008

By Marcus Cole | October 25, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Freddy Peralta spent eight years as a Milwaukee Brewer. He grew into an ace in that uniform, posted a 17-6 record with a 2.70 ERA in his final season there, and became one of the more beloved pitchers in franchise history.

On Monday night, he takes the mound against them.

Peralta starts Game 3 of the World Series for the Tampa Bay Rays at Tropicana Field — the first World Series game played at that venue since 2008. First pitch is 8 PM ET on FOX. The Brewers lead the series 2-0. The Rays' entire season may rest on what Peralta does in the next seven innings.

The Story Behind the Matchup

The mechanics of Peralta's path from Milwaukee to this moment matter for understanding what's at stake. The Brewers traded him to the New York Mets in January 2026 — a decision that freed up payroll and reflected Milwaukee's belief in its pitching depth. Peralta was then acquired by Tampa Bay at the trade deadline.

That chain of events means Peralta doesn't just face a former employer Monday night. He faces the organization that let him go, while pitching in a World Series where his former team is two wins from the title. It is not a neutral situation. Even in a sport where players are accustomed to transitions, pitching in the World Series against the team where you built your career is different from a regular-season start.

Peralta won the American League Championship Series MVP award. He went 2-0 against Cleveland with a 0.75 ERA — a performance that stands as one of the best in recent postseason memory for a starting pitcher. He arrives at Game 3 as the best pitcher left in this series.

Gasser Counters for Milwaukee

Robert Gasser has been the quiet man of the Brewers' postseason rotation. His ERA through the postseason stands at 1.00. In NLCS Game 3 against the Dodgers, he delivered five scoreless innings — holding a Los Angeles lineup that had terrorized opposing starters all season to nothing.

Gasser is not the headline. He is not the ALCS MVP. He is the pitcher who makes the Brewers' rotation functional around Misiorowski and gives manager Pat Murphy confidence that whoever starts is going to give the team a chance. On Monday in Tampa Bay, that might be exactly enough.

The Tropicana Field Factor

Tropicana Field does not have a reputation for atmosphere. The roof, the artificial turf, the quirky angles in the outfield — it is a divisive park. But in October, with the Rays facing elimination, the building can generate something. Tampa Bay went 3-1 at home in the postseason before Monday's game.

The last World Series game at Tropicana Field was in 2008, when the Rays faced the Philadelphia Phillies. That team — featuring Evan Longoria, Carl Crawford, and a rotation anchored by Scott Kazmir — pushed the series to five games before falling. The current Rays group needs to generate something similar.

Down 2-0, facing a team with a 1.33 ERA bullpen and a lineup that proved it can erase four-run deficits, Tampa Bay needs Peralta to be exceptional. Based on what he has shown in this postseason, exceptional is not out of the question.

What's Next

Game 3 is Monday, October 26 at 8 PM ET on FOX from Tropicana Field in St. Petersburg. If Tampa Bay wins, the series continues with Game 4 on Tuesday and Game 5 on Wednesday at the same venue.

If Milwaukee wins, they return to American Family Field for Game 6 — three wins away from ending a 69-year championship drought, dating back to the 1957 Milwaukee Braves.
