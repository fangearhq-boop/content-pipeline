Petey Halpin: The Guardians' Surprise October Hero Who Silenced the Rays

By Marcus Cole | October 16, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Petey Halpin was not supposed to be the ALCS story.

Jose Ramirez, Cleveland's superstar third baseman who played through a hamate bone injury to deliver in the ALDS, was the story. Gavin Williams, the Guardians' young right-hander with a 14-8 record and 3.76 ERA, was the story. Cleveland's improbable comeback from 0-2 down against the Chicago White Sox to win the ALDS — completing the journey as only the 11th team in MLB postseason history to overcome that deficit — was the story.

Petey Halpin wasn't supposed to be anything more than a name on the lineup card.

Then Freddy Peralta struck him out in Game 2, and everything changed.

The Changeup That Started Everything

In ALCS Game 2 at Tropicana Field, the Rays' Freddy Peralta was pitching the game of his life. Six scoreless innings. Six strikeouts. When he got Halpin to chase a 3-2 changeup for the third strike in the sixth inning, Peralta couldn't hide his satisfaction. He pumped his fist walking off the mound — he'd gotten his man, the pitcher had beaten the hitter, and the Rays were on their way to a 3-1 win that tied the series at 1-1.

Baseball keeps score, though. And it kept score on Thursday night in Cleveland.

ALCS Game 3 was tied in the eighth inning when Halpin came to the plate with two runners on. Whatever he was thinking at that moment, whatever adrenaline or motivation was coursing through him, it came out in the swing.

Two-run home run. Left field fence. Progressive Field erupting. Guardians take the lead, 3-2. That's exactly how it ended.

The Bigger Picture

Halpin's emergence in this series is the kind of story that October manufactures. He's a rookie outfielder who hit a key home run in September — a three-run shot against the White Sox that gave Cleveland a meaningful regular season win. He's not a celebrated prospect or a guaranteed starter. He's a player who made the roster and earned his place in the lineup.

That September homer against the White Sox showed Cleveland's coaching staff that Halpin could handle the moment. The Game 3 homer against the Rays proved it in October. There is a difference between performing in a mid-September game with standings implications and delivering in an eighth-inning, postseason, must-win situation. Halpin did both.

What's Next

ALCS Game 4 is tonight at Progressive Field (8 PM ET, TBS). Cleveland leads 2-1 and plays with the home crowd behind them and the momentum of Halpin's swing still in the air. The Rays will counter with a lineup and bullpen looking to extend the series. Halpin will be in the lineup again — he has earned that right.

Baseball is a game of adjustments. The Rays adjusted to their loss in Game 3. Halpin adjusted to getting struck out in Game 2. The player who adjusts faster, hits the bigger homer, and makes the next clutch moment will determine who moves on to the World Series.

For now, the answer to "who is Petey Halpin?" is simple: he's the man who gave Cleveland the lead when they needed it most.
