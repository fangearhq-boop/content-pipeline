NLCS Game 4: Brewers Beat Dodgers 6-4, Mitchell Delivers as Milwaukee Moves One Win From World Series

By Ryan Calloway | October 16, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Garrett Mitchell hit a go-ahead homer in the sixth inning and the Milwaukee Brewers beat the Los Angeles Dodgers 6-4 in NLCS Game 4 Thursday night at Dodger Stadium. The Brewers lead the series 3-1 and are one win away from their first World Series appearance since 1982.

Forty-four years since Milwaukee was last in the Fall Classic. One game separating a city from getting there. The Brewers play at Dodger Stadium again tonight — Game 5, a potential series closer, with the defending 14-time consecutive postseason participants playing for their baseball lives.

The Mitchell Moment

Tanner Scott, Los Angeles's lefthanded reliever, came in to face Mitchell in the sixth. The scoreboard had the game within reach. The crowd was loud. The Brewers needed someone to step up.

Mitchell stepped up.

His go-ahead shot changed the complexion of the game and put the Brewers in the driver's seat for the final three innings. Milwaukee's bullpen, which has been one of the stories of this postseason, held the lead and brought the series to its current position: 3-1 Brewers, one win needed, with Game 5 on the schedule tonight.

The Brewers have now held Los Angeles to four runs or fewer in three of the four NLCS games. Their pitching has been the story. Their defense has been clean. And when they've needed a clutch offensive moment, they've found one.

The Weight of 1982

The 1982 World Series ended with the St. Louis Cardinals defeating Milwaukee in seven games. For an entire generation of Brewers fans — and a second generation that grew up hearing the stories — that remains the franchise's closest encounter with a championship.

The 2026 roster is different. This team won 103 regular season games. They swept into the NLCS as one of the NL's premier clubs and have backed it up against the Dodgers, who entered October as one of the most formidable teams in baseball. Milwaukee is not here by accident. They have earned every game of this series.

The Dodgers, meanwhile, are staring down the end of one of the most remarkable postseason dynasties in recent baseball history. Fourteen consecutive postseason appearances, beginning in 2013. If Los Angeles loses tonight, that streak ends without a 2026 World Series ring.

What's Next

Game 5 is tonight at Dodger Stadium. Tyler Glasnow is expected to start for Los Angeles — the Dodgers' ace, healthy, pitching with everything on the line. The Brewers will counter with their rotation's next option.

If the Dodgers win, the series shifts back to Milwaukee for Games 6 and 7. If the Brewers win, the party starts on the road, in the middle of Dodger blue, 44 years in the making.

Either way, Game 5 tonight is must-watch baseball.
