Ohtani's October: .150, Zero Home Runs, and One Game Left to Change the Story

By Jake Torres | October 16, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Shohei Ohtani is the most fascinating player in baseball. He is also, through NLCS Game 4, the most confounding story of the 2026 postseason.

In the regular season, Ohtani was brilliant. He hit .277 with 30 home runs and 80 RBI before a right biceps injury ended his pitching season — but not his career as a designated hitter. He returned to the lineup and carried the Dodgers down the stretch, the team going 9-3 while he worked through soreness to contribute offensively. As a pitcher, his 1.79 ERA across 14 starts was the best of his career. He was everything the Dodgers paid $700 million for.

Then October arrived. And October has been something else entirely.

The Numbers

Through NLCS Game 4, Ohtani is hitting .150 in the postseason — 3-for-20 with seven strikeouts, zero home runs, and a .411 OPS. He has not homered since August 18 in Colorado. Two months. Thirty-five regular season games and now four postseason games without seeing one leave the yard.

The Dodgers' manager has kept him in the lineup. The reasoning is sound: Ohtani's track record is extraordinary, and the sample size of an NLCS is small. But the numbers are accumulating, and tonight — Game 5 at Dodger Stadium, a potential series finale with the Brewers needing just one more win — may be the last opportunity for the narrative to change.

The Question Without an Easy Answer

Is this a slump, an injury, or just baseball's randomness playing out? The honest answer is that no one fully knows. Ohtani has been managing knee and biceps discomfort for months. Those issues don't get easier in October, when the schedule is compressed and rest is hard to come by.

What is known is that the Brewers' pitching staff has handled him with precision. They have mixed speeds, spotted pitches on the corners, and kept him off-balance in ways that the regular season did not. That speaks to Milwaukee's preparation as much as it speaks to any limitation of Ohtani's.

The talent is unquestioned. The contract is irrelevant in the context of a single postseason series. What matters tonight is whether Shohei Ohtani can deliver in the biggest moment of his Dodgers career so far.

What's Next

Game 5 tonight at Dodger Stadium is the Dodgers' season. If they win, the series goes back to Milwaukee with a 3-2 deficit — hard, but not impossible. If they lose, the 2026 season ends and the questions about Ohtani's October will be the first ones asked in the postmortem.

He has had long slumps before and broken them with a single swing. October has not been kind to him this year. But one swing is all it takes.
