### STORY 2: Matt Olson Surging

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 12:30 PM ET

The home run race just became a two-man race.

Matt Olson has hit seven home runs in his last 11 games. That's the hottest power streak in all of baseball over that span. He sits at 35 HRs for the season — two behind Kyle Schwarber's 37 — and he is closing the gap fast.

This is the context: less than a week ago, four players were tied at 35 home runs. That was the first four-way tie for the MLB HR lead on August 11 or later since 1982. Schwarber's two-homer Field of Dreams game broke it, and now the field is separating.

Yordan Alvarez is at 35. He hasn't homered since July 28. He's batting .297 in August — he's making contact — but the power is missing. Junior Caminero is at 35 too, with an incredible pace for a 23-year-old, but he hasn't closed on Schwarber.

And then there's Olson. Seven home runs. Eleven games. A 423-foot shot earlier this week. He's also playing in his 903rd consecutive game — a streak that started in May 2021 — and he is swinging a scorching bat.

If Olson hits one today, he's at 36. One behind. Get ready. ⚾

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 3:00 PM ET

And he is still the frontrunner for the NL Cy Young Award by a wide margin.

Here's what the loss doesn't change: Misiorowski leads all of Major League Baseball in ERA (1.76), strikeouts (204), and WHIP (0.73) simultaneously. He is the second-fastest pitcher in MLB history to reach 200 strikeouts in a season — the only pitcher who got there faster was Spencer Strider in 2022. His NL Cy Young odds are -550. The Milwaukee Brewers are out of the playoff race, which means he is pitching for history on every five-day cycle.

The Dodgers are the best offensive team he will face all season. Losing 3-1 to them at Dodger Stadium in August with those season-long numbers is not an indictment. It's context.

Misiorowski's Cy Young case doesn't live in win-loss records. It lives in the stat line that no other pitcher in baseball has produced in 2026: 1.76 ERA. 204 strikeouts. 0.73 WHIP. In 133+ innings.

That's the story. ⚾

### STORY 5: Yordan Alvarez — 0 HRs in August

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 10:00 PM ET

Yordan Alvarez entered August tied for the MLB home run lead.

It's August 15. He still hasn't hit one.

That's not a misread. Alvarez — the Houston Astros slugger who has been one of the two or three most dangerous hitters in baseball for the past several years — has hit zero home runs this month. His last one came on July 28. Fifteen days ago.

What makes this strange: he's not struggling to make contact. He's batting .297 in August. He's putting the ball in play. The hits are there. The power — the thing that put him in a four-way tie for the MLB HR lead at 35 as recently as last week — is simply not showing up.

Meanwhile, Matt Olson has hit seven home runs in the past 11 games. Kyle Schwarber already has 37. The field is moving while Alvarez stands still.

This might resolve itself next week when he goes yard twice and shrugs it off. Or this might be the stretch run where a great hitter's power numbers level off at 35. The HR race doesn't wait around for anyone. ⚾
