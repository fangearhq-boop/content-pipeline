White Sox 9, Tigers 5 — Chicago Blows Open AL Central Lead to 3.5 Games in Series Opener

By Marcus Cole

August 15, 2026

Detroit called this the series that could flip the American League Central. Chicago answered with a nine-run, four-homer win in Game 1.

The White Sox beat the Tigers 9-5 at Comerica Park on Friday night, August 14, extending their AL Central division lead from 2.5 games to 3.5 games. Miguel Vargas drove the game's offensive narrative with two home runs. Munetaka Murakami and Tristan Peters each added solo shots. Four Chicago home runs in a visiting ballpark, against a Tigers team that had won 37 of its last 60 games entering the series.

Detroit Had Framed This as a Must-Win

The Detroit News published a series-preview headline that framed Game 1 through Game 3 as Detroit's window to take over first place. With the Tigers at 60-61 and the White Sox at 62-58 entering the series, a sweep would have moved Detroit past Chicago and into first place for the first time in the second half. That narrative collapsed in the first game.

Detroit is now 60-62. The White Sox are 63-58. Games 2 and 3 remain in Detroit on Saturday and Sunday. If the White Sox win the series, their AL Central lead could reach as high as 5.5 games. If Detroit sweeps the final two, they cut the lead to 1.5. The swing in this three-game set is as large as any series left on the schedule for either team.

The Context No One Ignores

In 2024, the Chicago White Sox went 41-121. That was among the worst records in MLB history. They were historically bad. Every outcome that season reinforced the scale of the rebuild ahead of them.

In 2026, with many of the same foundational pieces and an influx of young talent, the White Sox are leading a division with six weeks left and just posted a nine-run road win in their most important series of the year. The franchise's turnaround is not a fluke — it is the outcome of a constructed roster that has coalesced into a genuine AL Central contender. Vargas's two-homer night and the team's four-HR output are the product of a lineup built around exactly this kind of moment.

What's Next

Games 2 and 3 of the White Sox-Tigers series are Saturday and Sunday at Comerica Park. A Chicago series win would push their AL Central lead past 4 games with roughly six weeks remaining — a meaningful buffer entering the final stretch. Minnesota (in third) is further back. The AL Central race belongs to Chicago and Detroit for now, and the White Sox are up 1-0 in the series that matters most.
