Yordan Alvarez Has Hit Zero Home Runs in August — The HR Race May Be Leaving Him Behind

By Jake Torres

August 15, 2026

Yordan Alvarez entered August tied for the MLB home run lead at 35. He's now batting .297 in the month, making consistent contact, and has not hit a single home run since July 28.

Fifteen days. Zero home runs. And a race that is actively moving on without him.

Kyle Schwarber stands at 37 home runs, having hit two in the Field of Dreams game on August 13. Matt Olson sits at 35 and has hit seven home runs in his last 11 games — the hottest power stretch in baseball right now. Junior Caminero is also at 35. Alvarez is at 35 and standing still.

The Strange Part: He's Making Contact

Power droughts for sluggers come in two varieties. The first is a full-scale slump — the hitter is struggling to make contact, the batting average falls, the approach breaks down. The second is the stranger variety: the hitter is doing everything right except hitting the ball over the fence.

Alvarez is in the second category. A .297 batting average in August means he's putting the barrel on the ball regularly. The hits are there. The walks are presumably there. The power — the defining attribute that has made Alvarez one of the two or three most dangerous hitters in baseball over the past several seasons — is simply not producing home runs right now.

There is no confirmed explanation for the outage. No injury disclosure. No mechanical change that's been reported. Just 15 days and zero home runs during which Olson has accelerated and Schwarber has held his lead.

The Race Is Still There to Win

Alvarez's 35 home runs with roughly 40 games remaining is not a season-ending total. If he returns to his normal power pace in the second half of August and through September, he can finish in the 45-50 range — a legitimate MVP-caliber power total that would represent a career-best season in home runs for a player who has consistently produced 30-40 per year.

The question is whether the race runs far enough away from him before he returns to form that the final standings make it irrelevant. Schwarber at 37 with Olson surging at 35 — and now running the table in August — means Alvarez needs his power to return soon if he wants to compete for the title at season's end.

What's Next

Alvarez's next home run will generate significant coverage — any Astros power outage that ends is a story, and his return to the HR race leaderboard will be tracked closely by the NBC Sports "Race to 40" tracker and every baseball outlet following the chase. Watch for the date and the context of his 36th home run. It will tell the story of whether the drought was a blip or a turning point in a race that now belongs to two other players. ⚾
