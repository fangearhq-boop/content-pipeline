Cubs Blank the Cardinals 3-0 — NL Wild Card Lead Is Now 6 Games With 40 to Play

By Marcus Cole

August 15, 2026

The Chicago Cubs shut out the St. Louis Cardinals 3-0 at Wrigley Field on Friday night, August 14, to push their NL Wild Card lead to six games. They are 71-51 with approximately 40 games remaining. The Padres (65-57) are six back. The Phillies (65-58) are six and a half back. The Cubs' grip on a postseason berth is tight and getting tighter.

The Numbers That Matter

Six games with 40 to play is not a mathematical lock, but it's close enough that the question has shifted from whether the Cubs reach the postseason to what kind of postseason positioning they can build. A Wild Card team that wins six more games than the next-closest Wild Card competitor over 40 games would have to win at a .200 pace to get caught — an extreme that requires a historic collapse combined with a historic hot streak from the teams behind them.

The Cubs are not playing like a team preparing to collapse. Their rotation has delivered through August. The offense blanked the Cardinals on Friday, the latest example of a team that plays complementary baseball at the right times. They have two separate 10-game winning streaks on their resume this season after spending the first month and a half of the year looking like a team that might miss the playoffs entirely.

The Road Back

In mid-May, the Cubs were double-digit games below .500. The Cardinals were a plausible NL Central threat. The Padres were ahead of Chicago in the Wild Card standings. None of that is true now. Chicago ran off a 10-game winning streak in late May, rebuilt their position, then ran off another 10-game winning streak later in the summer. Each time they were counted on to produce, they did.

The mid-May version of this team looked like a problem. The August version has a 71-51 record, a 6-game Wild Card lead, and a pitching staff that just threw a shutout against a divisional opponent on a Friday night in August.

What's Next

The Cubs' magic number for clinching a playoff berth continues to drop after every win. The Padres and Phillies would need Chicago to simultaneously lose ground while they each go on extended winning streaks — a low-probability outcome with 40 games left. The more relevant question now is seeding: can the Cubs move up in the NL Wild Card standings to earn a higher seed and better matchup in the first round? The regular season still has value for this team; the conversations just shifted from "will they make it" to "how high can they go." ⚾
