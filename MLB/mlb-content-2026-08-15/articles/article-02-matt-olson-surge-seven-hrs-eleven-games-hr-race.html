Matt Olson Has Hit 7 Home Runs in 11 Games — The HR Race Has a New Threat

By Jake Torres

August 15, 2026

The MLB home run race spent most of the week as a four-way story. It's a two-man race now, and one of those men is getting hotter by the day.

Matt Olson has hit seven home runs in his last 11 games, the hottest power stretch by any player in baseball over that span. The Atlanta Braves first baseman sits at 35 home runs for the season — two behind solo MLB leader Kyle Schwarber — and the gap is closing. His last home run came August 11 in the form of a 423-foot blast. The math is straightforward: at his current pace, Olson matches Schwarber's lead within days.

The Four-Way Tie That Was (And Now Isn't)

As recently as August 11, four players were tied at 35 home runs: Schwarber, Olson, Yordan Alvarez (Houston Astros), and Junior Caminero (Tampa Bay Rays). According to research into historical HR races, it was the first time since 1982 that four players were tied for the MLB home run lead on August 11 or later in a season. The symmetry made for compelling storylines.

Then Schwarber hit two in the Field of Dreams game on August 13 and broke away. Now the symmetry is gone. Schwarber leads at 37. Olson is at 35 and charging. Alvarez is at 35 and has hit zero home runs in August. Caminero is at 35 and has slowed since his extraordinary 11-in-13-game stretch earlier in the month.

The race has a clear leader and a clear chaser. Schwarber and Olson.

Olson's Streak in Context

Olson's 7-in-11 stretch is remarkable not just in quantity but in quality. A 423-foot home run isn't a short-porch shot — it's the kind of contact that reflects a hitter locked in at the plate. For a first baseman playing in his 903rd consecutive game (a streak that began May 2, 2021, currently the longest active iron-man streak in baseball), maintaining that level of output deep into August is physically significant.

The consecutive games streak is its own story. Cal Ripken's 2,632-game record is not in play, but Olson's streak has placed him among the most durable players of his generation. He's in the top 10 of all-time consecutive games leaders for active players, and the combination of streak and power production in August puts him in the national conversation for multiple reasons simultaneously.

What's Next

Olson and Schwarber are the HR race, and neither is slowing down voluntarily. Schwarber leads by two with roughly 40 games remaining. Whether Olson catches him depends on whether his 7-in-11 pace continues, plateaus, or regresses. Either way, the August stretch run is where HR races are won and lost. Olson has entered the fight at exactly the right time.
