Misiorowski Silences Wrigley: 8 Strikeouts, Scoreless Outing, Brewers Take NL Central First

By Ryan Calloway | September 2, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Two weeks ago, Jacob Misiorowski's velocity numbers were a topic of concern. His average fastball had dipped from 100.5 mph to 98.7 mph across his August starts, and pitching analysts flagged it as something to watch. On Wednesday night at Wrigley Field, Misiorowski erased those concerns in the most emphatic way possible.

The Milwaukee Brewers left-hander delivered a scoreless outing against the Chicago Cubs, striking out eight batters and averaging 101.5 mph on his fastball. Milwaukee won the game and took over first place in the NL Central — a standing that puts the Brewers in position to host the first round of the playoffs.

The Dominant Season in Context

Entering Wednesday's start, Misiorowski stood at 14-5 with a 1.73 ERA, 222 strikeouts, and a 0.75 WHIP — all MLB-leading marks among qualified starters. He entered as the NL Cy Young frontrunner by a wide margin.

Against the Cubs specifically, Misiorowski has been untouchable in 2026. In his two previous appearances against Chicago this season, he combined for 12 innings pitched, 1 earned run, and 16 strikeouts. Wednesday's scoreless performance extended that mastery to three starts against the Cubs, now spanning 19-plus innings with 24 strikeouts.

The velocity story is the defining footnote. Misiorowski built his reputation in part on elite fastball velocity — a 100.5 mph average that ranked among the highest for any starter in baseball. The dip to 98.7 mph in August prompted questions about workload, fatigue, or something more structural. His 101.5 mph average at Wrigley suggests the dip was a short-term fluctuation rather than a structural concern.

Standings Implications

The Brewers' win on Wednesday moved Milwaukee into first place in the NL Central. The Cubs — despite holding the No. 1 Wild Card position — now trail the Brewers in the division standings.

That seeding gap matters because NL Central champions receive home-field advantage in the first round, while Wild Card teams play the best-record division winners. For Chicago, falling behind Milwaukee in the standings means October's first-round matchup becomes less favorable. The Cubs have spent September with that calculus in mind.

The four-game Wrigley series between the two teams illustrates the wider narrative: the Brewers were blown out 17-3 on August 31, bounced back to win 9-4 on September 1, and then Misiorowski finished the job on September 2. That swing from blowout loss to taking first place in three days is the Brewers' September identity in a single series.

The Cy Young Case

Misiorowski's Cy Young case needed no further evidence entering Wednesday — his ERA, WHIP, and strikeout totals were each best in the NL. But dominant starts against playoff-caliber opponents in September carry weight in voters' minds, and the Wrigley performance adds to an already-overwhelming file.

Ben Sheets holds the Brewers' franchise strikeout record at 264, set in 2004. Misiorowski entered Wednesday at 222 — 42 strikeouts short of that mark with the remainder of September and potentially October still to come. The franchise record is within reach.

What's Next

Misiorowski's next start is projected for early next week, with Milwaukee's full September schedule still to come. The Brewers hold first place in the NL Central with 27 games remaining. If Misiorowski continues to pitch at his season-long level — ERA at 1.73, velocity at 101-plus mph, strikeout rate elite — Milwaukee's path to the playoffs as division champions runs directly through his starts.
