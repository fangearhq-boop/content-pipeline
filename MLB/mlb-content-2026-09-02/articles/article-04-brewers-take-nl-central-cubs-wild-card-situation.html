Brewers Take First in the NL Central. The Cubs Have a New Problem.

By Ryan Calloway | September 2, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The Chicago Cubs entered September as the No. 1 NL Wild Card team. They still hold that position. But after Jacob Misiorowski's scoreless outing at Wrigley Field on Wednesday night gave the Brewers a second win in the four-game series, Milwaukee moved into first place in the NL Central — and Chicago's playoff picture became more complicated.

The Cubs at 78-61 are in the playoffs. That is not in question. But which kind of playoff team they are — a Wild Card team or an NL Central champion — determines who they play in October's first round. And for a team that has spent the summer trying to avoid a first-round matchup with Milwaukee, falling behind the Brewers in the division standings is not a small development.

The Wild Card Picture

The NL Wild Card race remains a tight four-team conversation for three spots. The Cubs hold the first Wild Card position. The Philadelphia Phillies are in the second spot. The Arizona Diamondbacks occupy the third — though the San Diego Padres are just a half-game behind Arizona for that final berth. The Miami Marlins, 3.5 games back, are on the outer edge of mathematical contention.

The Cubs being in Wild Card position rather than division position matters because the NL bracket structure sends Wild Card teams to face division winners with home-field advantage going to the division champion. If Milwaukee finishes with the NL Central title and Chicago finishes as a Wild Card team, the postseason path may route those two programs toward each other in the first round — with the Brewers hosting.

The Three-Day Swing

The Brewers' rise in this series illustrates the pendulum nature of September baseball. On August 31 at Wrigley Field, the Cubs routed Milwaukee 17-3. It looked like Chicago was asserting its claim to the NL Central and doing so emphatically. Then the Brewers won 9-4 on September 1. Then Misiorowski shut the Cubs out on September 2, and Milwaukee took first place.

Three games. Three days. Both teams went from blowout loser to first place within the same four-game series. That is the volatility of September standings, compressed into one Wrigley weekend.

What's Next

With 27 games remaining, both the Cubs and Brewers have full control of their division fate. Chicago's goal has shifted: winning the NL Central is no longer a secondary preference — it is the primary objective of the final month. The Cubs' pitching staff, their ability to protect Wild Card leads, and the remaining head-to-head schedule with the Brewers will define whether the Cubs enter October as Central champions or Wild Card entrants.
