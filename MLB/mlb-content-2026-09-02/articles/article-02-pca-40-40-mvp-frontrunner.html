Pete Crow-Armstrong Is the NL MVP. Now He's Chasing 40-40.

By Marcus Cole | September 2, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The NL MVP award is not decided in September. But it might as well be. Pete Crow-Armstrong of the Chicago Cubs holds 29 of 37 first-place votes in the official MLB writers' poll and is the -1100 betting favorite — a margin so large that the award is effectively decided before October begins.

What September actually decides, at this point, is whether Crow-Armstrong does something historically rare on top of winning the award.

He enters September hitting .283 with 38 home runs, 91 RBI, and 32 stolen bases. With 27 games remaining, he needs two more home runs and eight more stolen bases to join the 40-40 club — one of the rarest individual accomplishments in baseball history.

The 40-40 List

Only five players in MLB history have ever hit 40 home runs and stolen 40 bases in the same season. José Canseco did it first in 1988 for the Oakland Athletics. Barry Bonds reached it with San Francisco in 1996. Alex Rodriguez accomplished it in 1998 with Seattle. Alfonso Soriano matched it in 2006 with Washington. And Ronald Acuña Jr. joined the list most recently in 2023 with Atlanta.

PCA at 38-32 is closer to the threshold than any player at this point of the season since Acuña. His stolen base pace — 32 in 135 games — projects him to add 6-7 more through the end of the regular season at his current rate. That puts him at 38-39 on pure pace. But pace does not capture who Crow-Armstrong is: a player who runs when the game calls for it, not on a fixed schedule.

The home runs are straightforward math: two more in 27 games. He averaged 1.4 home runs per 10 games in August. He needs two total. That is not a stretch.

The MVP Race

Shohei Ohtani, PCA's nearest competition in the MVP conversation, is listed at +600. Ohtani is having a strong offensive season and cannot pitch until 2027 following surgery. The gap between -1100 and +600 in MVP odds represents a contest that, for practical purposes, is over.

What keeps the award from being officially decided before October is the remaining schedule. Crow-Armstrong is not a passive participant in the vote — each at-bat, each game, each stolen base either adds to or slightly diminishes the case. Wednesday night, Misiorowski shut him down at Wrigley in the Cubs' loss. Those quiet nights are part of September, too.

But 38-32 with 27 games left, on an -1100 MVP favorite, is not a story about doubt. It is a story about how large the individual accomplishment can grow before October starts.

What's Next

The Cubs (78-61) are still the No. 1 NL Wild Card team, though the Brewers have now taken first place in the NL Central. Chicago's team fortunes and Crow-Armstrong's individual milestones are on parallel but separate tracks at this point in September. The MVP is essentially settled. The 40-40 remains the goal.
