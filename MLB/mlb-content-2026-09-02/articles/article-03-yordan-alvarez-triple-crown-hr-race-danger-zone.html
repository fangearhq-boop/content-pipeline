Alvarez's Triple Crown Bid Has a Danger Zone: The Home Run Race

By Jake Torres | September 2, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Yordan Alvarez of the Houston Astros leads all three American League Triple Crown categories with 27 games remaining in the 2026 regular season. He is batting .322. He has 36 home runs. He has 91 RBI.

Each of those leads is real. But they are not equal. The batting average lead is comfortable. The RBI situation is a dead heat. And the home run race could flip to a three-way tie with a single swing.

The batting title may be the most durable: Alvarez's .322 average leads Chandler Simpson by 14 points. Fourteen points over 27 games is meaningful separation. Simpson would need to hit at an elite clip while Alvarez slumped significantly for the BA category to change hands.

The Categories That Could Break Either Way

The RBI race is more volatile. Pete Alonso enters September tied with Alvarez at 91. A single productive series from Alonso — or a quiet week for Alvarez — flips the RBI lead out of Alvarez's hands. RBI depend in part on teammates getting on base in front of you, meaning this category has an element of randomness that batting average and home runs do not.

The home run race is the most dangerous category for Alvarez's Triple Crown bid. Two players sit at 35 home runs — one behind Alvarez. Junior Caminero of the Tampa Bay Rays entered September at 35 HR after a breakout 2026 season. And New York Yankees first baseman Ben Rice — a 27-year-old who debuted in June 2024 — is also at 35 HR, making this a three-way race with 27 games left.

One multi-HR game from either Caminero or Rice gives them the lead. One quiet week for Alvarez and the home run crown slips away. Losing the home run title means losing the Triple Crown.

What a Triple Crown Would Mean

The last American League Triple Crown winner was Miguel Cabrera in 2012, when he hit .330 with 44 home runs and 139 RBI for the Detroit Tigers. No AL hitter has won the award since. Alvarez is 29 years old and in his statistical prime. His career numbers already place him among the elite left-handed hitters of this generation.

Winning the Triple Crown would add a historical footnote that transcends individual season counting stats. It is one of those accomplishments that appears on a player's permanent record — a signature achievement in a sport that does not award them often.

What's Next

Alvarez and the Astros have 27 games remaining. Each of the three Triple Crown categories remains in play. The most urgent question is the home run race — whether Caminero or Rice make a September move that forces Alvarez to respond. The batting average and RBI leads are real but not insurmountable. For now, Alvarez holds all three. The race to hold them through October 1 is the September story. #Astros #TripleCrown
