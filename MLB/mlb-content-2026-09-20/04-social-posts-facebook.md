### STORY 1: Pete Crow-Armstrong — 40-40 Chase

**Long-Form Post**
Seven games. Three stolen bases. And a place in baseball history.

Pete Crow-Armstrong enters the final week of the 2026 regular season at 47 home runs and 37 stolen bases — three steals away from becoming the seventh player in MLB history to hit 40 home runs and steal 40 bases in the same season. The 40-40 club is one of the most exclusive in the sport: Jose Canseco (1988), Barry Bonds (1996), Alex Rodriguez (1998), Alfonso Soriano (2006), Ronald Acuña Jr. (2023), and Shohei Ohtani (2024) are the only members. PCA is on the doorstep.

And there's more. No Cub has ever hit 40 home runs and stolen 40 bases in the same season. He already broke Billy Williams' record for the most home runs by a left-handed hitter in Cubs franchise history — Williams set the mark at 42 in 1970, and PCA has 47. Five more home runs than a Hall of Famer's single-season franchise record.

The context makes the 40-40 bid even more compelling. The Cubs (85-69) are locked in a three-way tie with the Phillies and Padres for the three NL Wild Card spots. Every Cubs win this week matters for October. And Pete Crow-Armstrong is the reason the Cubs are in this position at all.

Three steals. Seven games. No pressure. ⚾

### STORY 3: Jacob Misiorowski — Cy Young

**Long-Form Post**
The award seems locked. But there's still history left to chase.

Jacob Misiorowski's 2026 season has been one of the most dominant pitching performances in recent memory. The 24-year-old Milwaukee Brewers right-hander leads MLB in ERA at 1.89 and strikeouts at 243. His WHIP is 0.81. He became the first pitcher in the Statcast tracking era — which dates to 2015 — to throw 1,000 pitches at 100 miles per hour or faster in a single season. His Cy Young odds have settled at -700, with Chris Sale the only other pitcher drawing serious mention at +500.

But the NL Cy Young is not the only award on the table. Brewers fans have been watching another number closely: 264. That's the franchise single-season strikeout record, set by Ben Sheets in 2004. Misiorowski has 243. He needs 21 more to break Sheets' mark and claim a piece of Brewers history on top of what is already a historically elite season.

The Brewers have already clinched their fourth consecutive NL Central crown — their fourth straight since the division was restructured — making this a celebratory backdrop for the final chapter of Misiorowski's remarkable year. Twenty-one strikeouts and the Sheets record falls. November brings the Cy Young. Year 2 brings the next challenge.

For now, it's one of baseball's best stories of 2026. ⚾

### STORY 4: Aaron Judge — Calf Strain IL

**Long-Form Post**
Twelve days after returning from one injury, Aaron Judge is dealing with another.

The New York Yankees placed their slugger on the 10-day injured list with a right soleus calf strain — retroactive to September 17 — just seven games into his comeback from a 99-day absence for a rib stress fracture. Manager Aaron Boone did not provide an estimate on the severity of the strain or a timeline for Judge's return. The soleus — a deep calf muscle — can range from a minor inconvenience to a weeks-long absence depending on the severity of the strain.

The Yankees have already clinched their 61st postseason berth, so the regular season outcome is settled. The question is entirely about October. Judge is eligible to come off the injured list on the final day of the regular season, which would allow him to be on the postseason roster. He has publicly targeted a return for the postseason.

The calf strain is the second significant injury in a season that began with enormous expectation. With 18 home runs in his limited appearances, Judge showed flashes of his MVP form — but the Yankees will enter October hoping their franchise player is healthy enough to be the version of himself that makes New York genuinely dangerous in the postseason.

No timetable. Targeting October. The baseball world is waiting. ⚾

### STORY 5: Yordan Alvarez — Triple Crown Math

**Long-Form Post**
The numbers are there. The math is harder.

Yordan Alvarez leads the American League in batting average at .311 — a mark that has held through the entire back half of the 2026 season and represents the kind of sustained contact hitting that defines elite major league hitters. But the Triple Crown requires three leads simultaneously, and two of those leads have slipped away.

In the home run race, Tampa Bay's Chandler Caminero holds the AL lead at approximately 40-41 home runs. Alvarez is at 38 — a three-home-run gap with seven games left is not impossible to close, but the power output that defined his first four months of the season has largely gone quiet. Since August 2, Alvarez has hit just three home runs with approximately 13 RBI in 35+ games — a prolonged slump that has opened the door for Caminero to take the lead.

In the RBI race, Baltimore's Pete Alonso holds a similar gap at approximately 102, with Alvarez sitting at 99.

The last American League Triple Crown winner was Miguel Cabrera in 2012 — a feat that went unchallenged for more than a decade before Alvarez put himself in position to make history. Seven games remain. A surge and a Caminero slump could still turn the math — but seven games is not a lot of margin for the kind of swing required.

One lead remains. The batting title is Alvarez's to lose. ⚾
