Three Teams, One Week: The NL Wild Card Race Comes Down to Seven Games

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | September 20, 2026

The Chicago Cubs, Philadelphia Phillies, and San Diego Padres all enter the final week of the 2026 regular season at 85-69, deadlocked in a three-way race for the three National League Wild Card spots.

With seven games left in the 2026 regular season, the National League Wild Card race has produced one of the most compressed finishes in recent memory. Three teams — the Chicago Cubs, Philadelphia Phillies, and San Diego Padres — enter this final week with identical 85-69 records, all fighting for the three Wild Card spots that will send them to October baseball starting September 29.

The Cubs currently own the tiebreaker, which slots them into the top Wild Card seed. But seven games of baseball is a long time in a race this tight, and any of the three clubs could finish anywhere in the bracket — or miss the postseason entirely — depending on how the next week unfolds.

The Division Picture

The NL division races are largely settled. The Milwaukee Brewers have clinched their fourth consecutive NL Central title, the Los Angeles Dodgers have locked up the NL West, and the Atlanta Braves are on the verge of claiming the NL East. Those three division winners will receive byes into the Division Series, which means all three Wild Card spots will be contested in a best-of-three home series beginning September 29.

That context matters for the Cubs, Phillies, and Padres: whoever earns the top Wild Card seed hosts the best-of-three round, a meaningful edge in a format that can end a team's season in two games. Chicago owns that spot right now, but its grip on it is no stronger than the next loss.

What Each Team Is Playing For

For the Cubs, this week carries a dual narrative. Pete Crow-Armstrong's chase for the 40-40 club — currently at 47 home runs and 37 stolen bases, needing three more steals — gives every Chicago win an individual storyline on top of the team stakes. The Cubs need wins, and PCA needs opportunities. The two goals are perfectly aligned.

The Phillies have been a postseason fixture in recent years and enter this stretch as arguably the most experienced club of the three. Philadelphia's lineup carries October pedigree, and the Phillies' rotation has the depth to navigate a seven-game sprint without overextending any single starter.

The Padres have been the most inconsistent of the three clubs over the second half, but their talent level makes them dangerous regardless of seeding. San Diego's offense has the capability to carry stretches, and their bullpen is built for postseason-style leverage situations.

What's Next

Seven games separate all three clubs from either a home Wild Card series in October or a very long offseason. The final day of the regular season is September 27, giving each team one week to sort out a race that has been running on a knife's edge for months.

The Wild Card round begins September 29. Whoever earns the top seed hosts Game 1 immediately. For Cubs fans, the convergence of a team fighting for October and a franchise player on the doorstep of history makes this one of the most compelling final weeks at Wrigley Field in years. Check the standings every morning. Every game counts.
