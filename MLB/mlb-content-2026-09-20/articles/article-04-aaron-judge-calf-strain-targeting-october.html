Aaron Judge Is on the IL Again. The Yankees Are Waiting for October.

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | September 20, 2026

Just seven games into his return from a 99-day IL stint, Aaron Judge is back on the injured list with a right soleus calf strain. The New York Yankees have already clinched a postseason spot. Now they wait.

The New York Yankees placed Aaron Judge on the 10-day injured list on September 17 with a right soleus calf strain, retroactive to that date — just seven games after he had returned from a 99-day absence for a rib stress fracture. Manager Aaron Boone offered no estimate on the severity of the strain or a timeline for Judge's return. The soleus is a deep calf muscle, and strains of that muscle can range from a minor setback measured in days to an absence measured in weeks, depending on severity.

The baseball world is waiting for clarity. So are the Yankees.

The Injury Timeline

Judge's 2026 season has been defined by absence as much as performance. The rib stress fracture that cost him 99 days was the kind of structural injury that typically requires patience and a prolonged recovery, and Judge appeared to be moving well in his seven-game return before the calf strain emerged. He hit two home runs in that span, flashing the power that makes him one of the most feared hitters in the game when healthy.

The calf strain adds a second significant injury to a season that began with enormous expectations. Judge finished with 18 home runs in his limited appearances — a pace that, extrapolated over a full season, would put him in elite company. But the Yankees and their fans have spent most of the summer watching their franchise player from a distance.

What It Means for October

The Yankees have already clinched their 61st postseason berth, so the regular season outcome is settled. The entire conversation around Judge's calf strain is about October. He is eligible to come off the injured list on the final day of the regular season, which would allow him to be on the postseason roster. He has publicly targeted the postseason as his return date.

Whether that timeline holds depends on how the calf responds to treatment. Boone's refusal to set any expectations in either direction is the honest answer from a medical standpoint: a soleus strain doesn't always behave predictably, and committing to a return date before the tissue has had time to respond to treatment would be speculation more than prognosis.

What's Next

Judge will begin treatment and monitoring immediately. If the strain is minor, he has enough time before the postseason roster locks to be ready for the first round. If it is more significant, the Yankees face the prospect of entering October without their best player for a second straight postseason.

New York is good enough to win without him — their roster depth has been a strength all season. But Aaron Judge is the kind of player who changes the shape of a postseason series simply by being in the lineup. The Yankees need him healthy. The baseball world will keep watching for updates between now and September 29.
