Yordan Alvarez Is Leading the AL in Batting Average. The Triple Crown Math Still Doesn't Work.

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | September 20, 2026

Yordan Alvarez has led the American League in batting average all season. But with seven games left and a prolonged slump since August, he trails in both home runs and RBI — and the Triple Crown that looked possible in July now requires a surge that may not come.

Yordan Alvarez enters the final week of the 2026 regular season leading the American League in batting average at .311 — a mark he has held through the entire back half of the season. In any other year, that kind of sustained contact hitting from one of the game's most feared sluggers would be the central storyline of an individual award chase. In 2026, it is the one lead he has left.

The Triple Crown requires leading the league in batting average, home runs, and RBI simultaneously. Alvarez has the first. The other two slipped away during a two-month slump that began in early August and has not fully resolved.

The Numbers That Don't Add Up

In the home run race, Tampa Bay's Chandler Caminero holds the American League lead at approximately 40 to 41 home runs. Alvarez sits at 38 — a three-homer deficit with seven games remaining. Closing a gap that size is not mathematically impossible, but it requires Alvarez to heat up at exactly the moment he has been cooling off.

The RBI race tells a similar story. Baltimore's Pete Alonso leads with approximately 102 RBI, while Alvarez sits at 99 — another three-unit deficit. Like the home run gap, it is theoretically closeable but practically difficult given Alvarez's recent production.

Since August 2, Alvarez has hit just three home runs with approximately 13 RBI in more than 35 games. A slump of that length and depth is not a cold week — it is a sustained shift in how opposing pitchers are attacking him, or in how his mechanics have responded to accumulated wear. Neither explanation makes for an easy fix in the final seven games of the regular season.

The Historical Stakes

The last American League Triple Crown winner was Miguel Cabrera in 2012 — a feat that went unchallenged for more than a decade before Alvarez put himself in position to make history. The rarity of the accomplishment is part of what made the Alvarez chase so compelling when it was at its peak in July. He was tracking all three categories simultaneously, and the combination of his power, contact ability, and lineup protection gave the chase a legitimacy that is rare even among elite hitters.

The slump is what makes the current math so frustrating for Houston fans. The July version of Alvarez could have closed a three-home-run gap. Whether the September version can is the question the remaining seven games will answer.

What's Next

Seven games remain in the regular season. The batting title is Alvarez's to lose — his .311 average is a comfortable lead, and a batting title in its own right is a meaningful accomplishment. But the Triple Crown requires all three, and the home run and RBI races are running away from him.

If Caminero and Alonso maintain their leads, Alvarez finishes with the batting title and a season that fell just short of one of baseball's most storied individual accomplishments. That is still a remarkable year. It is not the year it could have been.
