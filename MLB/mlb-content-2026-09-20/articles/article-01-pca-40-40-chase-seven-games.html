Three Steals, Seven Games: Pete Crow-Armstrong on the Doorstep of the 40-40 Club

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | September 20, 2026

Pete Crow-Armstrong is 47 home runs, 37 stolen bases, and three steals away from becoming the seventh player in MLB history to hit 40 HR and steal 40 bases in the same season.

With seven games left in the 2026 regular season, Pete Crow-Armstrong stands three stolen bases away from one of the most exclusive accomplishments in baseball history.

The Chicago Cubs outfielder sits at 47 home runs and 37 stolen bases — marks that have already broken the Cubs' franchise record for home runs by a left-handed hitter in a single season (Billy Williams hit 42 in 1970) and positioned PCA on the edge of baseball's 40-40 club. Six players have ever reached 40 home runs and 40 stolen bases in the same season. Crow-Armstrong needs three more steals to join them.

The Company He Would Keep

The 40-40 club is one of the rarest in professional sports. The six members: Jose Canseco, who started it in 1988 with 42 home runs and 40 stolen bases. Barry Bonds in 1996, with 42 and 40. Alex Rodriguez in 1998, with 42 and 46. Alfonso Soriano in 2006, with 46 and 41. Ronald Acuña Jr. in 2023, the first to do it in the modern era, with 41 and 73. And Shohei Ohtani in 2024, with 54 and 59 in a season that felt like it belonged in a different dimension of baseball.

If Crow-Armstrong closes the deal, he will be the seventh member — and the first Cub ever. In a franchise that has produced Hall of Fame hitters across more than a century of baseball, no one has done what Pete Crow-Armstrong is about to do.

The Broader Context

Crow-Armstrong's bid for history doesn't exist in a vacuum. The Cubs (85-69) are locked in a three-way tie with the Philadelphia Phillies and San Diego Padres for the three NL Wild Card spots. Chicago owns the tiebreaker, which currently gives them the top Wild Card seed — but seven games of baseball can change everything, and every Cubs win this week serves a dual purpose: keeping the team in October and giving PCA the games he needs to write his name in the record books.

The 40-47 home run total alone would have made this a historically remarkable season. He broke Billy Williams' record and sits five home runs above a mark Williams set in 1970 — a record that stood for more than 55 years. The stolen base chase adds the dimension that takes it from a great season into a transcendent one.

What's Next

The Cubs play seven games between now and the end of the regular season on September 27. Three stolen bases in seven games is eminently achievable for a player who has stolen 37 bases across the season. But Crow-Armstrong's steal totals have fluctuated game to game, and there are no guarantees in a race this close.

The Wild Card round begins September 29, which means the Cubs have very little margin for error in the regular season and essentially no time to recover if the wild card standing shifts. Three steals. Seven games. The biggest individual stat chase of the 2026 baseball season comes down to this week.
