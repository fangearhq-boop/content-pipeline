Misiorowski Survives Efficiency Crisis in NLCS Game 1 — Can He Sharpen Up for Game 2?

By Jake Torres

October 12, 2026

Jacob Misiorowski pitched Sunday night the way you hope your ace doesn't have to — battling through traffic, making critical pitches when everything mattered, and leaving after 4⅓ innings having thrown 94 pitches.

The Brewers won, 2-1. But the story of Misiorowski's NLCS start deserves a longer look, because Milwaukee's run in this series may depend on whether he can be more efficient than he was in Game 1.

The Numbers Tell the Story

Misiorowski threw 94 pitches to get 13 outs — an average of over 21 pitches per inning. He threw just 60 strikes. He walked three batters. His whiff rate on his primary pitch (18.5%) was the second-lowest rate of any start he made in 2026.

This was not an aberration. Misiorowski also had a short, high-pitch outing in the NLDS against San Diego. Two consecutive postseason starts where he could not get deep into the game is a pattern that the Brewers' front office and coaching staff cannot ignore.

He Made the Pitches That Counted

To his credit, Misiorowski made two of the biggest pitches of the game when Milwaukee needed them most. In the second inning, with the bases loaded and one out, he induced Kiké Hernández into an inning-ending ground ball double play. In the third, he struck out Shohei Ohtani after Mookie Betts had reached with a leadoff single.

A different pitcher might have allowed four runs in those two innings and the game would have been over. Misiorowski's ability to bear down in crisis situations is a genuine strength. But getting into those crises in the first place because of poor pitch efficiency is a liability Milwaukee cannot afford against a Dodgers team that will make adjustments.

What's Next

Milwaukee's bullpen bailed Misiorowski out on Sunday, retiring 14 straight Dodgers batters over 4⅔ scoreless innings. That kept the lead intact and sealed the win. But the Brewers need their ace to go deeper into games as the series extends. The bullpen is not infinite. Misiorowski's next start in the series — likely Game 3 or Game 4 after the teams travel to Los Angeles — will tell Milwaukee whether they have an ace who can close out a series or a pitcher who needs significant bullpen support every single outing.
