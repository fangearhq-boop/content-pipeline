Brewers 2, Dodgers 1 — Milwaukee Steals NLCS Game 1 Behind Turang Homer, Bullpen Brilliance

By Ryan Calloway

October 12, 2026

The Milwaukee Brewers are one win away from seizing control of the National League Championship Series.

Milwaukee beat the Los Angeles Dodgers 2-1 at American Family Field on Sunday night, taking a 1-0 lead in the NLCS behind a Brice Turang home run, a clutch Luis Lara single, and one of the most dominant bullpen performances of this postseason. The two-time defending World Series champions came to Milwaukee and left with a loss.

How It Happened

The Dodgers took a 1-0 lead in the early innings. The Brewers answered in the fourth when Turang drove a solo home run off Tarik Skubal to tie the game at one.

The go-ahead run came in the fifth. Skubal left a changeup over the plate, and Lara hit a single that scored Garrett Mitchell from second base. That 2-1 lead stood for the rest of the game.

Jacob Misiorowski started for Milwaukee and was anything but clean. He needed 94 pitches to get through 4⅓ innings, allowing multiple baserunners and walking three. He escaped a second-inning bases-loaded jam when Kiké Hernández grounded into a double play. He struck out Shohei Ohtani in the third. But the efficiency was poor — more than 21 pitches per inning — and manager Pat Murphy pulled him after the fifth started.

That is when the bullpen took over. And it was masterful.

The Bullpen Sealed It

Milwaukee's relievers — including DL Hall and closer Trevor Megill — pitched 4⅔ scoreless innings and retired 14 consecutive Dodgers batters. Against a lineup featuring Ohtani, Mookie Betts, Freddie Freeman, and Hernández, that kind of shutdown performance is remarkable. The Dodgers did not threaten seriously after the fifth inning.

It is the clearest indication yet of how the Brewers plan to win this series: imperfect starting pitching, a dominant bullpen, and just enough offense from an underrated lineup. In Game 1, that formula worked exactly as designed.

What's Next

NLCS Game 2 remains at American Family Field, giving Milwaukee the chance to go up 2-0 in the series before the games shift to Los Angeles. The Dodgers, despite their two-year dynasty and 14 consecutive postseason appearances, must now win on the road to stay alive in the series opener. Game 2 schedule and pitching matchups will be announced ahead of the next game.
