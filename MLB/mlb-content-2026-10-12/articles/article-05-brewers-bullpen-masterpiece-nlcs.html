Milwaukee's Bullpen Retired 14 Straight Dodgers — It Won NLCS Game 1

By Marcus Cole

October 12, 2026

When Jacob Misiorowski walked off the mound with 4⅓ innings done and 94 pitches thrown, the Milwaukee Brewers needed their bullpen to stop one of the most dangerous lineups in baseball for 4⅔ innings.

They did. Fourteen consecutive batters retired. Not a single run. Final score: Brewers 2, Dodgers 1.

It was the defining performance of NLCS Game 1 — and it tells you everything about how Milwaukee is built to win in October.

Who They Faced

The importance of this performance cannot be overstated without considering the opposition. The Brewers' bullpen was not retiring weak spots at the bottom of the order. They were shutting down Shohei Ohtani. Mookie Betts. Freddie Freeman. Kiké Hernández. Four players who collectively had historic 2026 seasons and who were brought to Los Angeles specifically to win in October.

Not one of them reached base in the final 4⅔ innings. The Dodgers' offense, which had produced enough runs throughout the season to make them the most feared lineup in the National League, went completely quiet once Misiorowski left. DL Hall and closer Trevor Megill were the headliners among the relievers who combined for the perfect finish.

The Brewers' Formula

Milwaukee's approach in this postseason has been clear: use starting pitching to get deep enough that the bullpen can close it out, score just enough to hold the lead, and let the back of the roster finish the job. In Game 1, that plan was executed exactly as designed.

The concern heading into the series was whether Misiorowski could anchor a rotation that might face seven games. Sunday showed that Milwaukee can win even when their ace is not sharp — as long as the bullpen is. And on Sunday night in front of a hometown crowd, the bullpen was as good as any group of relievers gets in the postseason.

What's Next

Milwaukee leads the NLCS 1-0 heading into Game 2 at American Family Field. The Brewers' next pitching decision — and how the bullpen is managed against the Dodgers' adjustments — will shape whether Sunday's dominant relief performance was a one-time highlight or a sustainable pattern. Los Angeles, the two-time defending champion, will not let the bullpen keep shutting them down without making changes at the plate. How Milwaukee's relievers respond to those adjustments is the biggest subplot of the series.
