### STORY 1: NLCS Game 1 — Brewers 2, Dodgers 1

**Long-Form Post (9:00 AM ET)**
Milwaukee just sent a message to the defending champions.

The Brewers beat the Los Angeles Dodgers 2-1 in NLCS Game 1 Sunday night at American Family Field, taking a 1-0 series lead against the two-time reigning World Series champions.

The key plays: Brice Turang hit a solo home run off Tarik Skubal in the fourth inning to tie the game at 1-1. Then in the fifth, Luis Lara singled on a first-pitch Skubal changeup to score Garrett Mitchell and put Milwaukee ahead 2-1. That's all the Brewers needed.

Jacob Misiorowski started but labored — 94 pitches to get through 4⅓ innings. He escaped a bases-loaded jam in the second when Kiké Hernández grounded into a double play. He struck out Shohei Ohtani in the third. But he was far from dominant, and the Brewers' bullpen had to finish the job.

And it did. In emphatic fashion. DL Hall and closer Trevor Megill led a group of relievers who retired 14 consecutive Dodgers batters over 4⅔ scoreless innings — shutting down a lineup that features Ohtani, Mookie Betts, Freddie Freeman, and Hernández.

Game 2 heads back to American Family Field as the Brewers look to go up 2-0.

### STORY 3: Misiorowski Efficiency Issues

**Long-Form Post (3:00 PM ET)**
Milwaukee won NLCS Game 1. But Jacob Misiorowski left a few things on the table.

The Brewers' ace needed 94 pitches to get through just 4⅓ innings — averaging more than 21 pitches per inning. He threw only 60 strikes. He walked three batters. His whiff rate on his primary pitch (18.5%) was the second-lowest of any start all season.

He did make some critical pitches when it mattered. He escaped a bases-loaded, one-out jam in the second inning when Kiké Hernández grounded into an inning-ending double play. He struck out Shohei Ohtani in the third inning. But the overall efficiency was a problem — and this wasn't an isolated incident. Misiorowski also had a short, high-pitch outing in the NLDS against the Padres.

Here's the question heading into Game 2: can the Brewers keep relying on their bullpen to carry 4⅔ innings every time Misiorowski starts? Sunday they did it flawlessly. Over a seven-game series, that's a lot to ask.

Milwaukee won. But if Misiorowski keeps starting games like this, the Brewers will need their bullpen to be perfect again. That's a high bar to clear twice.

### STORY 4: Dodgers 14 Straight Postseasons

**Long-Form Post (10:30 AM ET)**
They lost Game 1. But they made history just by showing up.

The Los Angeles Dodgers' appearance in the 2026 NLCS represents their 14th consecutive postseason berth — a streak that started back in 2013. With that, they have tied the Atlanta Braves for the longest continuous postseason streak in Major League Baseball history.

Think about what it takes to sustain that. Fourteen years of making the playoffs requires navigating roster turnover, injuries, contract negotiations, and the ups and downs of a 162-game season — year after year after year. From Clayton Kershaw's prime to the Corey Seager era to Shohei Ohtani's first postseason, through World Series titles and early exits, the Dodgers have been there every single October.

The Braves set their record across a different era. The Dodgers are matching it in the modern game, with expanded wild-card formats and more competition than ever.

They're down 0-1 in the NLCS to Milwaukee. They may not extend this streak to a championship. But Game 1's result doesn't diminish what they've built over 14 years.

### STORY 5: Milwaukee Bullpen

**Long-Form Post (5:00 PM ET)**
When Jacob Misiorowski walked off the mound in the fifth inning Sunday night, the Brewers' bullpen had a job to do.

They delivered one of the best relief performances in recent postseason memory.

The Milwaukee relievers — led by DL Hall and closer Trevor Megill — shut down the Los Angeles Dodgers for the final 4⅔ innings. Not one Dodger reached base. Fourteen consecutive batters retired. Final score: Brewers 2, Dodgers 1.

Consider who those 14 batters were. This isn't a weak lineup. The Dodgers have Shohei Ohtani, Mookie Betts, Freddie Freeman, and Kiké Hernández at the heart of their order — four players who collectively had historic regular seasons. Against all of them, the Brewers' bullpen was perfect.

This is what makes Milwaukee dangerous in October. They don't have to win the old way — overpowering lineups with a dominant starter who goes eight innings. They can piece it together. Five innings from the starter, four innings from the bullpen, two runs on the board. That is a formula that wins playoff series.

If that bullpen keeps pitching like this, the Dodgers are going to be in serious trouble.
