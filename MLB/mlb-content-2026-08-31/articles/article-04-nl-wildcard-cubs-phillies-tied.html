Cubs and Phillies Enter September Tied at 77-60 — Seeding Determines Everything

By Ryan Calloway

August 31, 2026

The two teams fighting for the top two NL Wild Card seeds enter September with identical records. The Chicago Cubs and Philadelphia Phillies are both 77-60 — tied for the first and second Wild Card spots as 29 games remain in the regular season.

For both teams, the difference between first and second seed is not minor. The NL's best record belongs to the Milwaukee Brewers, who have posted the most dominant first-half run in baseball and are comfortably ahead in the NL Central. A Wild Card team finishing second instead of first in its bracket could find itself in a first-round series against Milwaukee — meaning facing Jacob Misiorowski in a best-of-three. The seeding question is functionally a matchup question.

The Current Picture

The NL Wild Card standings entering September: Cubs (77-60), Phillies (77-60), and Arizona Diamondbacks (73-65) in the third spot. The gap between second and third is four games — a meaningful cushion — while the gap between first and second is zero.

The Brewers have all but wrapped up the NL Central and the No. 1 overall seed in the National League. Their primary concern is the same as the Cubs' and Phillies' from the opposite direction: who they face in the first round. From Milwaukee's perspective, the bracket fills itself. From Chicago's and Philadelphia's perspective, the final 29 games will determine how difficult the path to the NLCS is.

The Cubs' September Calculus

Chicago's goal entering September is clear: avoid the Brewers in Round 1. That requires finishing ahead of Philadelphia for the NL Wild Card 1 seed. It requires holding off Arizona from closing the gap. And it requires PCA — who enters September as the NL MVP frontrunner — to continue producing at a level that keeps the lineup dangerous through October.

The Cubs have the talent to win this race. The Phillies do too. Twenty-nine games will sort it out.

What's Next

The NL Wild Card seeding race will be one of September's defining stories. Direct Cubs-Phillies matchups later in the month will carry significant weight. The team that finishes first earns a path through the Wild Card round that avoids Milwaukee — potentially the difference between a first-round exit and a deep postseason run. The Cubs' next major test is Wednesday's home game against Misiorowski and the Brewers, which will give them an early look at what the potential postseason matchup could look like.
