Yordan Alvarez Leads All Three AL Triple Crown Categories Entering September's Final 29 Games

By Jake Torres

August 31, 2026

Twenty-nine games. Three category leads. One shot at something baseball has not seen in 14 years.

Yordan Alvarez of the Houston Astros enters September leading the American League in batting average (.322), home runs (36), and RBI (91) — the three categories that together constitute the Triple Crown. If he can hold all three leads through the end of the regular season, he would become the first AL Triple Crown winner since Miguel Cabrera won it for the Detroit Tigers in 2012.

Where Each Race Stands

The batting average lead is comfortable. Alvarez's .322 average provides a 14-point cushion over Chandler Simpson, who is second at .308. Closing a gap that large over 29 games is possible but would require sustained contact from Simpson and a significant cold stretch from Alvarez.

The home run race is not comfortable. Alvarez has 36 home runs, with Junior Caminero and Ben Rice both at 35 — one back. August was a slow month for Alvarez in the power department, while Caminero was active. With 29 games remaining, the home run category is a one-swing swing at any time.

The RBI lead — 91 — is the steadiest of the three, as Alvarez's run production has been consistent all season.

The Weight of History

The last player to win the AL Triple Crown before Cabrera's 2012 performance was Carl Yastrzemski, who accomplished the feat in 1967. Winning the Triple Crown — holding the lead in all three major offensive categories simultaneously across a full season — is one of the rarest individual accomplishments in professional baseball. It requires not just individual excellence but sustaining that excellence across every aspect of hitting while competitors remain close in each category throughout September.

Alvarez has done that through 133 games. He needs to do it for 29 more.

What's Next

The Triple Crown race will be one of the defining storylines of the final month of the 2026 AL season. Alvarez needs to hold his leads in all three categories simultaneously, with the home run gap representing the most vulnerable category entering September. Each at-bat from Caminero or Rice in the HR column changes the dynamic; each Alvarez long ball restores it. The race goes to the final weekend of the regular season.
