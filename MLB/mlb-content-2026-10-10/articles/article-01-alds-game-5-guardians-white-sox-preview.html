Winner-Take-All: Guardians vs. White Sox in ALDS Game 5 at Progressive Field

By Jake Torres | October 10, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

One game. That is all that stands between the Chicago White Sox and the American League Championship Series for the first time since 2005. The Cleveland Guardians, who erased a 0-2 series deficit to force tonight's winner-take-all finale, will put every ounce of Progressive Field's electricity behind their bid to keep Chicago out. Game 5 starts at 8 PM ET on TBS.

Gavin Williams gets the ball for Cleveland. Sean Burke starts for Chicago. The series has been one of October's better storylines: a White Sox team that went 81-81 in the regular season — the first in MLB history to clinch a playoff berth with a non-winning record — somehow finds itself one win from the ALCS in a city that is desperate to stop them.

How the Series Got Here

Chicago took Games 1 and 2 on the road, giving themselves the series lead and a cushion. Cleveland answered, winning Game 3 in Chicago and then coming home to take Game 4, 9-5. In that game, José Ramírez did what Ramírez does: hit a home run, drove in three runs, and reminded everyone why Progressive Field becomes a different place in October.

The White Sox, built from nearly nothing over two seasons of heavy rebuild, are running on momentum and the kind of loose-nothing-to-lose energy that makes a 81-81 team dangerous. Catcher Kyle Teel, a young starter, has been solid behind the plate. Joey Bart missed the postseason with a fractured hand suffered in August, leaving Teel to carry the load with veteran Jake Rogers backing him up.

What's at Stake

The winner faces the Tampa Bay Rays in the ALCS beginning Monday, October 12. The Rays swept the Yankees 3-0 in the other ALDS and are waiting in Tampa Bay for whoever emerges from tonight's game.

For Chicago, winning tonight would mean the franchise's first ALCS appearance in 21 years — since their 2005 World Series championship run. For Cleveland, it would mean defending home turf and using the season's most hostile crowd noise to close out a team that was never supposed to be here. Both narratives are real. Only one gets to play out.

What's Next

Game 5 is tonight at 8 PM ET from Progressive Field in Cleveland, televised on TBS, truTV, and HBO Max. The winner opens the ALCS on Monday, October 12, in Tampa Bay against the Rays. Every run tonight carries ALCS implications — this is October baseball at its most compressed and most consequential.
