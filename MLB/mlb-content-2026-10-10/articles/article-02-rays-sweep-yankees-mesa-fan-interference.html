Rays Sweep Yankees in ALDS: Mesa Jr. Homer, Fan Interference Drama, and Tampa Bay's Third ALCS

By Ryan Calloway | October 10, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The Tampa Bay Rays are going to the American League Championship Series, and the road they took to get there was everything October baseball promises to be. The Rays swept the New York Yankees 3-0 in the ALDS, finishing with a 4-3 Game 3 victory on October 7 in a game that featured a go-ahead home run, a fan interference controversy, and a review that went Tampa Bay's way.

It is only the third ALCS appearance in Rays franchise history. They allowed five runs total across the entire three-game series — a display of pitching depth and defensive execution that is easy to overlook in a lineup-heavy postseason conversation.

The Decisive Moment: Mesa Jr. and the Review

In the sixth inning of Game 3, with Tampa Bay trailing 3-2, Victor Mesa Jr. stepped in against Max Fried and hit a two-run home run to give the Rays a 4-3 lead. It was the kind of moment that swings a series — a young player with something to prove delivering when the margin was thinnest.

But the real drama came later. A Yankees batter hit a ball toward the wall, and a fan reached out from the stands. For a moment, the question was whether it would count as a home run by fan interference — which would have tied the game. Video review answered it definitively: the ball would not have cleared the wall regardless of the fan's contact. The interference ruling stood. The Yankees' rally died. Tampa Bay closed it out.

The Yankees had not been swept in a best-of-five postseason series since the 1980 American League Championship Series, when Kansas City dispatched them in three games. Forty-six years between sweeps is a long time to keep a streak alive. October 7 ended it.

What Five Runs in Three Games Means

The Rays' pitching staff allowed five runs in the entire ALDS. That is the story of this team and has been for years: manufacture offense, control the strike zone, limit damage. It does not always play well in highlights, but it plays well in playoff standings.

Tampa Bay now waits for the winner of the Guardians-White Sox Game 5 tonight. They will host ALCS Game 1 on Monday, October 12, at Tropicana Field. After a 2026 regular season that justified every bit of its optimism, the Rays are two series wins from the World Series.

What's Next

The Rays open ALCS play Monday, October 12, at Tropicana Field in St. Petersburg, on TBS. Their opponent will be determined by tonight's Guardians-White Sox Game 5. For a franchise that has been to the World Series once (2020) and the ALCS twice before this run, a third ALCS is a significant milestone — and a series that starts with Tampa Bay pitching at home is one worth watching.
