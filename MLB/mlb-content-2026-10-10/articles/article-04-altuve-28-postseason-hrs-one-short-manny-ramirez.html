Altuve Ends 2026 at 28 Postseason Home Runs — One Short of Manny Ramírez's All-Time Record

By Jake Torres | October 10, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

José Altuve's 2026 postseason is over. The Houston Astros were eliminated by the Chicago White Sox in the ALDS. And Altuve will spend the offseason knowing he is one home run away from the most celebrated individual record in postseason history.

Altuve hit his 28th career postseason home run in Game 2 of the ALDS against Chicago. Manny Ramírez holds the all-time record with 29. The gap between Altuve and history is now a single swing.

What the Record Would Mean

Manny Ramírez's 29 postseason home runs span a career that included World Series titles with Cleveland and Boston. Ramírez was one of the most feared hitters in baseball history, and his postseason numbers remain a benchmark that very few players have come close to approaching.

Altuve has built his postseason legacy through longevity and sustained excellence in Houston's decade-long dynasty. He already holds the record for the most career postseason home runs by an infielder. He holds the record for the most first-inning postseason home runs, with eight. Home run No. 29 would simply be the one that puts him at the top of a list he has been climbing for years.

The 81-81 Astros

It is worth pausing on what the Astros accomplished just to get to this conversation. Houston went 81-81 in the 2026 regular season — the first team in MLB history to qualify for the playoffs with a non-winning record in a full 162-game season. They won the AL West title for the eighth time in 10 years, and they extended the White Sox to two ALDS games before being eliminated.

Jeremy Peña led the team with a .375 batting average in the Wild Card Series. Altuve hit the home run. For a .500 team, the Astros made more noise than anyone expected. But the ALCS is not theirs this year, and neither is the record.

What's Next

The Astros' offseason begins immediately. Altuve will return in 2027 carrying 28 career postseason home runs and the expectation that if Houston gets back to October, one more might finally put him in sole possession of history. At 36 years old, he is not out of runway. He is one good October from all of it.
