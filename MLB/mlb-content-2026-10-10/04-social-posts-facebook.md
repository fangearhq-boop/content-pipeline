### STORY 2: Rays Sweep Yankees ALDS

**Long-Form Post:**
Tampa Bay is going to the ALCS, and the story of how they got there is one of the better October narratives in recent memory.

The Rays swept the New York Yankees 3-0 in the ALDS, capping the series with a 4-3 Game 3 win on October 7. The decisive moment: Victor Mesa Jr. hit a two-run homer off Max Fried in the sixth inning to put Tampa Bay ahead. Then came the fan interference controversy.

A Yankees hitter appeared to hit a ball toward the wall — a fan reached out, and the Yankees briefly thought it might count as a home run. Video review quickly determined the ball would not have cleared the wall regardless of the interference. The call stood. The Rays held on.

It's only the third time Tampa Bay has ever reached the ALCS. They allowed just five runs across the entire three-game series. And they did it against the Yankees — who hadn't been swept in a best-of-five postseason since losing to Kansas City in 1980.

ALCS Game 1 is Monday. The Rays wait on the winner of tonight's Guardians-White Sox Game 5.

### STORY 3: NLCS Preview — Dodgers vs. Brewers

**Long-Form Post:**
The NLCS starts tomorrow night, and it is a matchup worth getting excited about.

The No. 2 Los Angeles Dodgers meet the No. 1 Milwaukee Brewers at American Family Field for Game 1, Saturday, October 11, at 8 PM ET on FOX. The Dodgers are going for a third consecutive World Series title. The Brewers are playing in their first NLCS since 2025, when they lost to these same Dodgers.

Los Angeles is bringing Blake Snell — healthy, confident, and back in a rotation after undergoing elbow surgery. Yoshinobu Yamamoto is also projected to start. And Shohei Ohtani's pitching status remains unresolved heading into the series, with Dave Roberts saying only that there are "no limitations" without giving a specific assignment.

For Milwaukee, the story is Jacob Misiorowski — who posted an 11-5 record and a 1.76 ERA during the regular season. Trevor Megill is their elite closer. The Brewers have pitching, and on a neutral site, this is closer than it looks.

One lingering question: Miguel Rojas aggravated a back and hip issue for the Dodgers in the NLDS and was unavailable for Game 4, but remains on the active roster.

### STORY 4: Altuve Falls One Homer Short of All-Time Postseason HR Record

**Long-Form Post:**
José Altuve's 2026 postseason is over, and he leaves it one home run short of history.

The Houston Astros were eliminated in the ALDS by the Chicago White Sox. Altuve, who hit his 28th career postseason home run in Game 2 of that series, finished the year at 28 — one behind Manny Ramírez's all-time postseason record of 29.

It is an extraordinary number in its own right. Altuve already holds the record for the most career postseason home runs by an infielder. He holds the record for the most first-inning postseason home runs (eight). And now he holds the record for "most times we've talked about whether he's going to break Manny's record."

The Astros won the AL West at 81-81 — the first team in MLB history to make the playoffs with a non-winning record in a full 162-game season. They pushed the White Sox to two games before being eliminated. It was a surprisingly competitive postseason exit for a .500 team.

Altuve will get another shot in 2027. He's one swing away.

### STORY 5: Jacob Misiorowski — The Ace Nobody Saw Coming

**Long-Form Post:**
Before you watch the NLCS, you should know the name Jacob Misiorowski.

The Milwaukee Brewers' ace went 11-5 with a 1.76 ERA in the 2026 regular season. That 1.76 ERA would rank among the best single-season marks in recent National League history. He helped carry the Brewers to the No. 1 seed in the NL and through an NLDS against a quality opponent. Now he is preparing to start a potential NLCS Game 1 against the Los Angeles Dodgers — a team chasing a third consecutive World Series title.

The Brewers have not been a major market darling. They play in Milwaukee, they don't have Ohtani, and their national television presence is limited. But Misiorowski has been building a case all season that he deserves to be in the conversation with the best pitchers in baseball.

The NLCS gives him that platform. The Dodgers give him the opponent. Game 1 tomorrow is his moment.
