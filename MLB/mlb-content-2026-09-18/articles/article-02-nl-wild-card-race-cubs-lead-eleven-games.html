NL Wild Card Race: Cubs Lead by 1.0, Rivals Keep Missing Their Chances With 11 Games Left

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | September 18, 2026

The Cubs are 85-68 and hold NL Wild Card 1. Their competition keeps losing when it matters. With 11 games left, Chicago is running out the clock.

The National League Wild Card race has settled into a familiar rhythm: the Cubs keep winning, or at least holding position, while the teams chasing them keep stumbling when the games matter most.

After Thursday's results, Chicago sits at 85-68, holding the No. 1 Wild Card seed — and with it, home field for the Wild Card Series. The Philadelphia Phillies are 1.0 game back. The San Diego Padres are 1.5 back. The Arizona Diamondbacks, at roughly 4.0 games out, are effectively eliminated from contention. Eleven games remain in the regular season. The Wild Card Series begins September 29.

Home Field Is the Prize

The Cubs' grip on WC1 is meaningful for reasons beyond the seeding. In a Wild Card Series format where the higher seed hosts all three games, playing at Wrigley Field represents a tangible advantage — the ivy, the crowd, the history all working in Chicago's favor. Losing that home field and traveling to Philadelphia or San Diego for a best-of-three elimination round is a different proposition entirely.

The Phillies, chasing that home-field advantage themselves, have had their chances this week. The Padres, battling for any Wild Card seed, have not been able to string together the wins that would tighten the race. Every night the standings hold where they are is another night the Cubs extend the runway toward October.

The Postseason Bracket Above Them

Above the Wild Card line, the NL is already sorted. The Milwaukee Brewers hold the No. 1 seed with a first-round bye after clinching their fourth consecutive NL Central title. The Los Angeles Dodgers hold the No. 2 seed and a bye. The Atlanta Braves have locked up the No. 3 seed.

The Cubs, Phillies and Padres are playing for seeds four, five and six — and the difference between four and six is home field versus road for the Wild Card Series. Cubs hold four right now. That is what 11 games of play is protecting.

What's Next

The final 11 games of the Cubs' regular season will determine where this Wild Card Series is played. If Chicago holds WC1, Wrigley Field hosts. If they slip, the road awaits. The Phillies and Padres each have the schedule and talent to close the gap — but they have been unable to do it consistently, which is precisely why the Cubs still lead. September 29 is 11 days away. The race goes down to the wire.
