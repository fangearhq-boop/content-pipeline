Aaron Judge's Lower-Leg Scare Raises Another Round of October Questions for the Yankees

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | September 18, 2026

Judge exited Wednesday's game against the Twins with right lower-leg tightness — just 10 days after returning from a 99-day IL stint. No timetable. Postseason is 11 days away.

The pattern is becoming harder to ignore for Yankees fans.

Aaron Judge left Wednesday's game against the Minnesota Twins in the third inning with right lower-leg tightness. He had started, gone 0-for-2, and was replaced by Heliot Ramos. Manager Aaron Boone said that Judge "felt something" in the first inning and that the team made the call to remove him rather than push through it.

"Hopefully it's something we were able to catch," Boone said after the game.

There is no timetable for his return. The postseason is 11 days away.

The Second Scare in Ten Days

Judge returned from the injured list on September 8 after a 99-day absence caused by a stress fracture in his first right rib. That injury cost him the bulk of the summer. His return was cautious — the Yankees were careful about workload, deliberate about not rushing him back into a full nine-inning load before he was ready.

Ten days later, a different issue in a different location. The right lower leg. No diagnosis beyond "tightness" as of Thursday morning, but the timing — with the Wild Card Series beginning in less than two weeks — ensures that even a minor injury carries outsized concern.

What the Yankees Have Clinched

New York has already secured their 61st postseason berth — a franchise record. They hold a spot in the American League Wild Card picture. The structure is in place. What the Yankees need from here is Judge healthy and available when the games start counting.

A team can win a Wild Card Series without a fully healthy MVP. Rosters are deep enough in October to absorb a star operating at 80 percent. But the Yankees' margin for error in the postseason depends heavily on what Judge brings as a bat in the middle of the lineup and a presence on the basepaths. Managing him carefully for 11 days — limiting starts, monitoring the leg, trusting the medical staff — is the only viable approach.

What's Next

The Yankees have said they are monitoring Judge's condition daily. An update on his availability is expected before New York's next scheduled game series. For now, the posture is the same as it has been since September 8: protect the asset, build toward October. The latest setback adds urgency to a timeline that was already tight. The Wild Card is 11 days away, and the Yankees are navigating this the only way they can — one day at a time.
