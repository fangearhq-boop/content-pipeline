Pete Crow-Armstrong Hits Three More: 47 Home Runs, Three Steals from 40-40 History

By Ryan Calloway | Ballpark Banter | September 18, 2026

In a 17-5 blowout of the Reds, PCA launched three more home runs to reach 47 on the season — leaving him three stolen bases away from one of baseball's most exclusive clubs.

On Thursday night at Wrigley Field, Pete Crow-Armstrong did what he has been doing all season: hit the ball very hard, very far, and very often. Three home runs in a 17-5 rout of the Cincinnati Reds. Six RBIs. His season total now stands at 47 home runs and 37 stolen bases.

He needs three steals.

That is the only remaining arithmetic separating Pete Crow-Armstrong from the 40-40 club — an achievement only six players in the 150-year history of Major League Baseball have ever reached. With approximately 11 games remaining in the 2026 regular season, the question is no longer whether PCA can do it. The question is when.

The Six Who Did It

The 40-40 club is not a marketing construct. It is a genuine measure of total offensive dominance — the rare combination of elite power and elite speed that has, in the entire history of the sport, produced exactly six members. José Canseco got there in 1988 with Oakland. Barry Bonds did it in 1996. Alex Rodriguez joined in 1998. Alfonso Soriano, in 2006. Ronald Acuña Jr. in 2023. Shohei Ohtani, also in 2023.

No Chicago Cub has ever been a member. No player has ever done it at Wrigley Field, under that ivy, in a Cubs uniform. If PCA gets three more steals in the next 11 games, he changes that.

The Season in Context

Crow-Armstrong is 22 years old. He broke Billy Williams' Cubs record for home runs by a left-handed hitter — Williams had 42, set in 1970 — earlier this month. He is the frontrunner for the National League Most Valuable Player award. He has done all of this in his age-22 season, for a team that is currently seeded first in the NL Wild Card race with 11 games to play.

The three-homer game Thursday — the most recent in what has been a historically productive September — showed exactly why the comparisons to the game's all-time greats keep circulating. The combination of a plus-plus power tool and the kind of speed that keeps opposing catchers on edge is not something teams can game-plan away. It is the product of elite athleticism playing at its peak.

What's Next

The Cubs have approximately 11 games remaining in the regular season. Three stolen bases from a player averaging more than one per week is an attainable target — especially against September pitching staffs protecting nothing more consequential than individual stats. Every at-bat for PCA now carries the full weight of that number: three. One game. Two games. Maybe in the next three appearances.

47 home runs. 37 stolen bases. The 40-40 club has never had a Cub. It will soon.
