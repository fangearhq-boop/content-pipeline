Jacob Misiorowski Becomes First Pitcher in Statcast Era to Throw 1,000 Pitches at 100 MPH — And the NL Cy Young Is His

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | September 18, 2026

The 24-year-old Brewers ace crossed a threshold no pitcher has reached in the Statcast tracking era — while leading all of baseball with a 1.95 ERA and 236 strikeouts. The award vote is a formality at this point.

The Statcast era of pitch tracking has produced more data about pitcher performance than any previous generation could have imagined. Velocity, spin rate, movement — every pitch catalogued, every delivery documented. In that entire tracking history, no starting pitcher had ever thrown 1,000 pitches in a single season at 100 miles per hour or faster.

Jacob Misiorowski did it this week.

The 24-year-old Milwaukee Brewers starter reached the milestone and kept going, adding it to a season that already sat at the top of every relevant pitching leaderboard. A 1.95 ERA, best in all of baseball. Two hundred and thirty-six strikeouts, also best in baseball. A 0.80 WHIP that speaks to the combination of strikeout ability and command that makes Misiorowski a fundamentally different kind of problem for opposing lineups than anyone else in the NL.

The Award Picture

The NL Cy Young Award is not a race at this point. Misiorowski is priced at approximately -700 by most sportsbooks. Atlanta's Chris Sale, the only other pitcher mentioned in serious discussions, sits at around +500. In terms of the underlying numbers, the distance between the two is significant — Sale has had a strong season, but Misiorowski's ERA and strikeout totals are in a different tier.

The 1,000-pitch milestone adds a dimension to the Misiorowski case that extends beyond the standard award metrics. It is a statement about how he generates his dominance — through a fastball that sits and peaks in a range that stresses hitters and challenges bat speed in ways that most modern starters cannot replicate. Pitchers who throw 100 mph routinely exist in modern baseball. Pitchers who do it 1,000 times in a single season have not existed, until now.

The Brewers' Historic Run

Misiorowski's individual excellence is the headliner, but the team context adds another layer. The Milwaukee Brewers clinched the NL Central on September 15, their fourth consecutive division title — a streak that makes them the first team to win four consecutive NL Central championships since the division was created in 1994. The Brewers hold the NL No. 1 seed and a first-round bye.

For a franchise that has operated with a smaller market footprint than most of its NL competitors, four straight NL Central championships represents a sustained run of organizational excellence that deserves its own recognition alongside Misiorowski's individual achievement. The Brewers built something durable in Milwaukee, and Misiorowski is its best expression.

What's Next

The Cy Young vote will be finalized after the season. Misiorowski will add a few more starts to his ledger, and he will be watching Ben Sheets' franchise record of 264 strikeouts in a season (set in 2004) as a secondary target — he currently has 236 and would need 29 more in his remaining starts. The Brewers enter the postseason as the NL's top seed. Their rotation, led by Misiorowski, is the reason the conversation about a deep October run in Milwaukee is not wishful thinking. It is well-founded expectation.
