Yordan Alvarez's Triple Crown Bid Is Essentially Over. Here's How September Closed the Door.

By Ryan Calloway | Ballpark Banter | September 18, 2026

With approximately 8 games left, Alvarez leads the AL in batting average but trails in home runs and RBI. The September slump cost him a shot at history.

In July, Yordan Alvarez looked like a man writing himself into the record books. He led the American League in batting average, home runs and RBIs — a Triple Crown in progress, a piece of history that had not been accomplished in the AL since Miguel Cabrera did it with Detroit in 2012.

In September, the record books closed the door.

With approximately eight games remaining in the 2026 regular season, Alvarez leads the AL in batting average at .310. He does not lead in home runs — Tampa Bay's Junior Caminero has 40 to Alvarez's 38. He does not lead in RBIs — Baltimore's Pete Alonso has approximately 100 to Alvarez's approximately 96. The path to history required leading in all three simultaneously at season's end. That path is now blocked.

What September Did to the Numbers

The culprit is a September slump that arrived at the worst possible moment. Alvarez has been hitting around .206 over his last 10 games of the month, with just three home runs in that span. The power that defined his peak — the Alvarez of summer who was putting up numbers that recalled the greatest offensive seasons in recent memory — has been absent when the calendar turned.

The reasons can be debated: fatigue from a long season, pitchers adjusting, the random variation that makes baseball what it is. Whatever the cause, the effect is the same. Two home runs behind with eight games left, against a young hitter in Caminero who is playing some of the best ball of his career. Four RBIs behind Alonso, whose Baltimore lineup keeps providing the opportunities he needs.

The Historical Context

The Triple Crown — leading your league in batting average, home runs and RBIs at season's end — is one of baseball's rarest achievements. Only three players have done it since 1970: Carl Yastrzemski (AL, 1967), Frank Robinson (AL, 1966) and, most recently, Cabrera (AL, 2012). No National League player has won it in that span.

Alvarez's bid was the closest anyone has come to the Triple Crown in years. He led all three categories simultaneously through much of July and August — and the fact that the conversation was happening at all reflects the extraordinary season he has put together. A .310 batting average leading the AL is elite production. Thirty-eight home runs is an excellent power season by any standard.

What's Next

The batting title is still Alvarez's to lose. His .310 average leads the nearest competition by roughly five points, and eight games is not typically enough time for that lead to evaporate. He will likely end 2026 as the American League batting champion — a legitimate individual honor even in the absence of the Triple Crown. The RBI and home run races will play out without him in serious contention. History waited this long for a Triple Crown winner. It will keep waiting.
