### STORY 1: PCA Three-Homer Game

**Long-form Post:**
Pete Crow-Armstrong did it again.

Three home runs. Six RBIs. A 17-5 demolition of the Cincinnati Reds on Thursday night at Wrigley Field.

He's at 47 home runs and 37 stolen bases on the season. Three stolen bases from 40-40. Eleven games left.

The 40-40 club is one of baseball's most exclusive societies. Only six players have ever done it: José Canseco (1988), Barry Bonds (1996), Alex Rodriguez (1998), Alfonso Soriano (2006), Ronald Acuña Jr. (2023) and Shohei Ohtani (2023). No Cub has ever been a member.

PCA is 22 years old, has already broken the Cubs' all-time left-handed home run record, and is the frontrunner for the NL MVP award. Three steals in 11 games is well within reach for one of the game's most complete players.

The question is no longer whether he can do it. The question is when.

### STORY 3: Aaron Judge Injury

**Long-form Post:**
The Yankees held their breath again Wednesday.

Aaron Judge left the game against the Twins in the third inning with right lower-leg tightness. He had started, gone 0-for-2, and was replaced by Heliot Ramos. Manager Aaron Boone said Judge "felt something" in the first inning and that the team wanted to make sure they didn't push through anything.

There is no timetable. That phrase — "no timetable" — is familiar territory for Yankees fans who watched Judge miss 99 days earlier this season with a stress fracture in his right rib. He returned September 8.

Now, 10 days later, another injury and another round of uncertainty. The postseason is 11 days away.

The Yankees have their 61st postseason berth clinched. What they do not have is certainty that their best player will be healthy when it matters.

### STORY 4: Yordan Alvarez Triple Crown Fading

**Long-form Post:**
It looked like a formality in July. It looks like a missed opportunity in September.

Yordan Alvarez is leading the American League in batting average at .310 — but he's two home runs behind Junior Caminero of the Tampa Bay Rays (40 vs. 38) and roughly four RBIs behind Pete Alonso of the Baltimore Orioles (~100 vs. ~96). With approximately eight games remaining in the regular season, the math is brutal.

The last AL Triple Crown winner was Miguel Cabrera in 2012. Nobody has won it in the AL since, and the NL hasn't produced one at all. For most of 2026, Alvarez seemed to be on a collision course with history.

Then September happened. He's hitting around .206 in his last 10 games. Three home runs. A power drought that arrived at the worst possible moment.

The batting title is still his to lose. The Triple Crown is essentially gone.

### STORY 5: Misiorowski Cy Young

**Long-form Post:**
No pitcher in the history of Statcast tracking has ever thrown 1,000 pitches at 100 miles per hour or faster in a single season.

Jacob Misiorowski just did it.

The 24-year-old Milwaukee Brewers ace reached the milestone this week, adding another chapter to what has already been one of the most dominant pitching seasons in years. He leads all of baseball with a 1.95 ERA and 236 strikeouts. His WHIP is 0.80. He's a -700 favorite to win the NL Cy Young Award, with Atlanta's Chris Sale the only other name in the conversation at +500.

The Brewers, meanwhile, have clinched their fourth consecutive NL Central championship — the first team to accomplish four consecutive NL Central titles since the division was created in 1994. Milwaukee is the NL No. 1 seed with a first-round bye.

Misiorowski is the reason both things are true. He didn't just carry the Brewers this year. He redefined what a dominant starter looks like in 2026.
