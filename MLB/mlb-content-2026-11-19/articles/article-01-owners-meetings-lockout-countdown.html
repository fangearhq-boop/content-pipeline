Owners Meetings Finale: Manfred Says He "Can't Imagine" a Deal Without a Cap — 12 Days Remain

By Ryan Calloway

November 19, 2026

The three-day MLB owners meetings in New York concluded Thursday with no progress toward a new Collective Bargaining Agreement — and Commissioner Rob Manfred delivered the clearest signal yet that the owners' position is not moving.

"I can't imagine the owners ratifying a deal that does not include a cap and floor," Manfred told reporters, noting that all 30 clubs are unanimous in their support of the proposals that have been made.

MLBPA interim executive director Bruce Meyer responded by accusing owners of seeking to suppress player salaries and stating that a lockout appears virtually certain. The two sides have never been further apart on the fundamental structure of baseball's economic system.

The Numbers That Cannot Be Bridged

MLB's most recent offer, presented on November 3, proposed a salary cap of $258.2 million and a floor of $180.2 million. The implementation would be delayed to 2028 as a transition. The union rejected it. The MLBPA has repeatedly stated that any cap proposal is a nonstarter — their position is that salary caps suppress player earnings, and they will not accept one regardless of the number attached.

Manfred said there is "plenty of room for compromise" on other issues, but the cap itself is not negotiable from the owners' perspective. That is not a gap that closes in 12 days. It is a philosophical divide that would require one side to fundamentally change its position.

What December 1 Means

The current CBA expires at 11:59 p.m. ET on December 1. Teams are expected to lock players out of facilities on December 2. That means no free agent signings, no trades involving 40-man roster players, no arbitration hearings, and no team-player communication.

The last time owners imposed a lockout on players was December 2, 2021. It lasted 99 days, ending March 10, 2022. The first two series of the 2022 season were canceled. If this stoppage runs the same length — or longer — the 2027 season is at risk.

The Next 12 Days

Free agents, teams, and agents will spend the next 12 days trying to close deals before the market freezes. Tarik Skubal's negotiations are the most prominent example. Jazz Chisholm, Randy Arozarena, Seiya Suzuki, Brandon Lowe — all of them need to sign or face months of waiting.

Manfred indicated he would continue working toward a deal and would work through Thanksgiving. No one in the industry believes him — the coverage of Wednesday's and Thursday's meetings was uniform in characterizing a lockout as nearly inevitable.

What's Next

Watch for any late CBA developments in the days leading up to December 1. For fans: enjoy the accelerating transaction market over the next 12 days. The blockbusters will keep coming. After December 1, the calendar goes quiet until a new deal is reached.
