Pre-Lockout Sprint: Mariners Land Maikel Garcia, Twins Sign Sonny Gray as Market Accelerates

By Marcus Cole

November 19, 2026

With 12 days until the CBA deadline freezes all transactions, the MLB market is running at a pace the sport has rarely seen this early in an offseason. Teams know the window, and they are using it.

The biggest deal of the winter so far landed Wednesday: the Seattle Mariners acquired All-Star third baseman Maikel Garcia from the Kansas City Royals in a six-player blockbuster. It is the kind of move that reshapes a franchise — and it happened in the middle of November because both sides understood the urgency of the December 1 deadline.

Mariners-Royals: The Winter's First Blockbuster

Garcia made the All-Star Game this past season and is one of the best defensive third basemen in the American League. The Mariners prioritized defense in their lineup rebuild and had starting pitching depth to offer as trade capital. The result was a six-player deal that sent Garcia from Kansas City to Seattle.

For the Royals, the trade is a rebuild play — loading up on young players from an organization with deep pitching. For the Mariners, it is a statement that the front office is serious about competing in 2027 regardless of what the lockout does to the offseason calendar.

Pitching Market Moves: Gray to Minnesota

The Minnesota Twins addressed their rotation Thursday, signing free agent starter Sonny Gray to a two-year, $55 million deal. Gray provides the Twins with proven, reliable innings — the kind of anchor that makes a rotation work in the postseason.

At $27.5 million per year, Gray's deal reflects the going rate for a mid-tier starter in a market that has been inflated by the looming freeze. Teams that need pitching know that signing after a lockout means competing for fewer available arms at higher prices. The Twins chose to act now.

Relief and Catching Market Moving Too

The Atlanta Braves signed reliever Evan Phillips to a three-year, $35 million deal. Phillips was among the top bullpen arms available, and the Braves secured him before he could become a lockout casualty on the unsigned list.

The Boston Red Sox acquired catcher Nick Fortes from the Tampa Bay Rays, sending 17-year-old right-hander Angel Roa to Tampa in return. It is a low-risk move for Boston to add depth behind the plate.

What's Next

Expect this pace to continue through December 1. Teams with payroll flexibility and roster needs will keep transacting for the next 12 days. The Skubal market, the Chisholm market, and the Arozarena market are all still active. The bigger the deal, the more urgency there is to close it before the freeze. Watch the transaction wire daily — the sprint is on.
