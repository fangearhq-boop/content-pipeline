The Blue Jays Are the Most Aggressive Team in the Pre-Lockout Market

By Ryan Calloway

November 19, 2026

If you want to track which organization is using the 12-day pre-lockout window most aggressively, follow the Toronto Blue Jays. They are pursuing a remarkable portfolio of targets, and landing even two of them would transform their roster.

Jazz Chisholm: The Frontrunner

Shi Davidi of Sportsnet has identified the Blue Jays as the frontrunner in the Jazz Chisholm sweepstakes. Toronto's vision for Chisholm: second base, with Ernie Clement moving to a super-utility role. Jon Morosi reports that 11 teams have engaged with Chisholm's representatives, and personal meetings are beginning this week.

The Yankees declined to extend Chisholm a qualifying offer, so there is no draft-pick compensation attached to signing him. That removes a significant barrier for teams on the fence. At second base in Toronto, Chisholm — 19 home runs and 41 stolen bases in 2026 despite a down offensive year — gives the Blue Jays an elite defender and elite speed threat.

Seiya Suzuki: The Outfield Target

Suzuki declined the Cubs' $23.15 million qualifying offer and hit the open market. Spotrac projects his contract at approximately four years and $109.9 million. The Blue Jays are among the teams in pursuit, per reports, and Toronto's history of signing Japanese players — combined with their market size and fan base — makes them a compelling destination.

Signing Suzuki would address Toronto's outfield needs and add a proven offensive producer to a lineup that has been average in recent seasons.

Brandon Lowe and the Walker Trade Possibility

The Blue Jays have also shown interest in Brandon Lowe, the second baseman who declined the Rays' qualifying offer. Lowe adds another infield option that Toronto may be exploring as a backup plan if the Chisholm pursuit falls through.

The Houston Astros are gauging trade interest in first baseman Christian Walker. Toronto is reportedly monitoring the situation. Walker would provide a right-handed power bat at first base — a need the Blue Jays have carried for several seasons.

What's Next

The Blue Jays' ability to close multiple deals in the next 12 days depends on how quickly Chisholm's market moves and whether Suzuki picks a preferred destination. Personal meetings are the next step in both pursuits — the results of those conversations will determine whether Toronto becomes the offseason's biggest winner before December 1.
