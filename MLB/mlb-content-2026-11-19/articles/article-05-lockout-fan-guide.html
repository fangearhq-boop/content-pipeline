What the MLB Lockout Actually Means: A Fan's Guide to the December Freeze

By Marcus Cole

November 19, 2026

Most baseball fans know an MLB lockout is probably coming on December 2. Far fewer know what that actually means, day by day. Here is the practical guide to what stops, what continues, and what it means for the 2027 season.

What Stops the Moment the CBA Expires

When the Collective Bargaining Agreement expires at 11:59 p.m. ET on December 1, the freeze begins immediately. Teams can no longer sign free agents who are on the 40-man roster market. Any player not under contract before midnight is frozen in place until a new deal is reached.

Trades involving 40-man roster players also stop. Teams cannot swap major-league players — either for cash or for other players — until a new CBA is in effect. The Rule 5 Draft, which allows teams to claim minor leaguers left off 40-man rosters, is expected to be canceled entirely. The MLB draft lottery is also reportedly expected to be scrapped.

Arbitration hearings are suspended. Players eligible for salary arbitration cannot exchange numbers, have hearings, or settle disputes. All of that waits for a new agreement.

The Most Unusual Provision: No Communication

Perhaps the strangest aspect of a lockout for fans to understand is the communication blackout. During a lockout, teams cannot discuss baseball business with their own players. Players cannot access team facilities for workouts, medical treatment, or coaching. The relationship between employer and employee is legally severed until a new CBA restores it.

Players work out on their own. Some travel abroad. Agents communicate with other agents, but nothing official happens between clubs and players. The sport goes dark.

What Continues

Not everything stops. Teams can sign international amateur free agents, whose status is governed separately. Minor-league operations continue under different rules. Some front-office work — scouting, analytics, draft preparation — goes on internally. But anything that touches a 40-man-eligible player or free agent stops.

What's Next

The last lockout ran 99 days, from December 2, 2021 through March 10, 2022. The first two series of the 2022 regular season were canceled, and spring training started late. If this lockout runs the same length, the 2027 season is threatened. If it runs longer — and there is no mechanism to prevent that — the damage could be worse. The CBA negotiations between owners and players are further apart than they were in 2021. Watch for any late-breaking news in the next 12 days. If a deal is reached, it will be announced quickly. If not, December 2 is the new reality of baseball.
