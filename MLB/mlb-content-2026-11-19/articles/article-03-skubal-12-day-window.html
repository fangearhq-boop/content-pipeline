Skubal's 12-Day Window: 78 Percent of Experts Say He Signs Before the Lockout

By Jake Torres

November 19, 2026

The consensus among baseball insiders is as close to unanimous as it gets: Tarik Skubal will sign a contract before the December 1 CBA deadline. ESPN surveyed its panel, and 78 percent believe Skubal closes a deal in the next 12 days.

The logic is straightforward. Skubal has said he wants to sign before the freeze. The teams pursuing him want certainty before their transaction power evaporates. And a contract this large — projections sit in the $325 million to $400 million range — is too risky to leave exposed to a lockout that could last months.

The Mets Are Still the Frontrunner

Jon Heyman reported that the New York Mets are "probably the favorite" after their in-person pitch in Orange County. The Mets made their case directly to Skubal and his representatives, with Steve Cohen and Billy Eppler believed to be in the room for the meeting.

The Mets have the payroll to absorb a $400 million commitment. They have the rotation need — New York is building around Skubal as a true ace. And Steve Cohen's ownership has demonstrated a willingness to spend at levels that other franchises simply cannot match.

The Padres Are Emerging as a Sleeper

Recent reporting has identified the San Diego Padres as a potential surprise destination. The Padres' front office has been aggressive in this market, and they have made a compelling case for why a pitcher of Skubal's caliber would thrive in Petco Park — a pitcher-friendly environment that could reduce his ERA even further.

San Diego has the financial resources to compete for Skubal. Whether they will go to the full $400 million ceiling remains unclear, but they are a legitimate alternative to the Mets.

The Contract That Rewrites History

Yoshinobu Yamamoto set the record for a pitcher with his $325 million, 12-year deal with the Dodgers. His average annual value of $27 million per year was considered staggering at the time.

Skubal is projected to exceed $44 million per year — $17 million more annually than what was considered a historic record two years ago. The market has moved that dramatically. At $44 million per year for eight years, Skubal would earn more in salary than most entire team payrolls from a decade ago.

What's Next

The 22 percent of insiders who think Skubal misses the December 1 window are not wrong to hedge. These negotiations can stall over contract length, opt-out clauses, medical requirements, or simple timing. If Skubal does not sign by November 30, he faces the prospect of waiting until the lockout ends — potentially March or later. No team will sign a pitcher to a $400 million deal in spring training. Watch for a signing announcement before the Thanksgiving holiday weekend.
