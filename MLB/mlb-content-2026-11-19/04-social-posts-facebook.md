### STORY 1: Owners Meetings Finale — Manfred "Can't Imagine" Deal Without Cap

**Long-Form Post (8:00 AM ET)**

The three-day MLB owners meetings in New York concluded Thursday, and Commissioner Rob Manfred delivered the clearest message yet about where this CBA stands.

"I can't imagine the owners ratifying a deal that does not include a cap and floor," Manfred told reporters.

All 30 MLB team owners are unanimously in support of the salary cap and floor proposals. MLBPA interim executive director Bruce Meyer responded by stating that a lockout appears virtually guaranteed — the union sees any cap proposal as a tool to suppress player salaries, and that position has not changed.

Where things stand: MLB's most recent offer (November 3) proposed a $258.2 million salary cap and $180.2 million floor, with implementation delayed to 2028. The union rejected it. No new proposal has been made public.

The CBA expires at 11:59 p.m. ET on December 1 — 12 days from now. When it expires, teams are expected to lock players out of facilities on December 2. That means no free agent signings, no trades, no arbitration hearings.

The last lockout ran from December 2, 2021 through March 10, 2022. It lasted 99 days. The 2027 season could be in jeopardy if this one runs even longer.

For fans: enjoy the next 12 days of hot stove activity. After December 1, baseball goes quiet.

The Mets remain the frontrunner. Jon Heyman reported that New York is "probably the favorite" after their in-person pitch in Orange County. Skubal met with six finalists: the Mets, Dodgers, Padres, Blue Jays, and two unnamed clubs.

The alternative: if Skubal doesn't sign by December 1, he is frozen in the market until the lockout ends. If the lockout mirrors 2021-22's 99-day stoppage, that could mean March or later. No team will commit $400 million to a pitcher in spring training. The 12-day window is the window that matters.

### STORY 5: What the MLB Lockout Actually Means — A Fan's Guide to the Freeze

**Long-Form Post (6:00 PM ET)**

A lot of fans know an MLB lockout is probably coming on December 2. Far fewer know what that actually means, day by day. Here's the practical guide.

What stops on December 2:

All free agent signings freeze. Any player not under contract before midnight on December 1 cannot sign until a new CBA is reached. That includes Tarik Skubal, Jazz Chisholm, Randy Arozarena, and everyone else still on the market.

All 40-man roster trades freeze. Teams cannot trade players on the 40-man roster. Rule 5 draft players can still be moved in some scenarios, but major trades are off the table.

Arbitration hearings are suspended. Players eligible for arbitration cannot exchange numbers or have hearings.

The Rule 5 draft is expected to be canceled. The draft lottery for the amateur draft is also reportedly expected to be scrapped.

No communication between teams and players. This is the most unusual provision — teams cannot discuss baseball business with their own players. Players cannot access team facilities.

What continues:

Teams can sign amateur free agents (international players). Some minor-league operations continue. Players can work out independently.

What it means for the 2027 season:

The last lockout ran 99 days, from December 2, 2021 through March 10, 2022. During that lockout, spring training was delayed and the first two series of the regular season were canceled. If this lockout runs the same length, the 2027 season is threatened. If it runs longer — and there is no guarantee it won't — the damage could be more severe.

The next 12 days matter. After December 1, we all wait.
