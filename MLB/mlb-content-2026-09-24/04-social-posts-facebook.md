### STORY 1: Pete Crow-Armstrong Is ONE Stolen Base From the 40-40 Club

**FB Long-Form** | 9:00 AM ET

One. Stolen. Base.

That is the margin between Pete Crow-Armstrong and one of the most exclusive clubs in baseball history. He enters Thursday's game with 39 stolen bases. He needs one more to become the seventh player ever to post a 40-40 season — joining Jose Canseco, Barry Bonds, Alex Rodriguez, Alfonso Soriano, Ronald Acuña Jr., and Shohei Ohtani.

What makes this remarkable isn't just the number. It's the context. Crow-Armstrong is 24 years old. He's in the middle of a three-team NL Wild Card race. He's already holding franchise records — the first Cubs player to reach 40 homers since Derrek Lee hit 46 in 2005, and the first left-handed Cubs batter to reach that mark since Billy Williams did it in 1970. His 24-year-old Cubs home run total is second only to Ernie Banks.

And through all of it — the records, the MVP chase, the playoff pressure — he needs one stolen base. Every game is a potential history-making moment. Watch the basepaths.

### STORY 4: Aaron Judge — Return Timeline

**FB Long-Form** | 5:30 PM ET

Aaron Judge's 10-day injured list stint — placed retroactive to September 17 for a right soleus calf strain — means his earliest possible return is September 27, the final day of the regular season.

The question isn't whether that's technically possible. It's whether it's wise.

The soleus is the deeper calf muscle, and it's more critical to explosive athletic movement — running, pivoting, and generating torque in the batter's box — than the more superficial gastrocnemius. Players who push back too quickly from soleus strains often re-aggravate the injury and miss more time. Judge, who already missed 99 days earlier this season with a stress fracture in his ribs, has been through this kind of calendar management before.

Manager Aaron Boone said he wouldn't place a timeline on Judge's return. Judge himself said he'd like to be back by the postseason. The Yankees have already clinched. There's no reason to risk him in a regular season game when the Wild Card round begins September 29 and the real games start then.

For Yankees fans, the worst case scenario is Judge missing the first round entirely. The best case is he walks back onto the field in October healthy and locked in. The Yankees have enough to survive without him — but with him, they're a different team.

### STORY 5: Yordan Alvarez Triple Crown

**FB Long-Form** | 8:00 PM ET

The math on Yordan Alvarez's Triple Crown chase is getting harder to ignore.

Alvarez leads the American League in batting average at .311 — his lead over Tampa Bay's Chandler Simpson is about five points, manageable but not safe. The bigger problems are the other two legs.

In home runs, Alvarez sits at roughly 38, trailing Simpson's Rays teammate Junior Caminero by approximately two. In RBIs, Alvarez is at roughly 96, trailing Baltimore's Pete Alonso by about four. With three or four games left, he needs to produce while both Caminero and Alonso cool off.

That's a lot to ask. But Alvarez is one of the most dangerous hitters in baseball, and the Astros still have meaningful games this week. The last American League Triple Crown winner was Miguel Cabrera in 2012. Before Cabrera, you have to go back to Carl Yastrzemski in 1967. This is genuinely historic territory, and Alvarez hasn't been eliminated yet.

He's fading. He's not done. Watch the stat line over the next 72 hours.
