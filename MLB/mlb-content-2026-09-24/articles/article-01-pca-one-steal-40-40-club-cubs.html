Pete Crow-Armstrong Is ONE Stolen Base From the 40-40 Club

By Ryan Calloway | September 24, 2026

The number is no longer 2. It's 1.

Pete Crow-Armstrong enters Thursday's game with 39 stolen bases. One more puts him in the 40-40 club — one of the most exclusive individual achievements in the history of professional baseball. Only six players have ever done it: Jose Canseco, Barry Bonds, Alex Rodriguez, Alfonso Soriano, Ronald Acuña Jr., and Shohei Ohtani. Crow-Armstrong would be No. 7.

He has three regular season games to get there.

From 2 Away to 1 Away — The Chase Intensifies

Twenty-four hours ago, PCA needed two stolen bases. The Cubs lost to the Marlins 3-2 Wednesday night — a game in which they couldn't clinch their playoff spot and Crow-Armstrong finished the night still one steal short of the mark. Now, the final weekend of the regular season stretches out in front of him, and every game he plays carries potential history-making weight.

The home run side of the equation was settled weeks ago. Crow-Armstrong already broke the Cubs franchise record for home runs by a left-handed batter in a single season — surpassing Billy Williams' mark of 42 set in 1970. He also became the first Cub to reach 40 home runs in a season since Derrek Lee hit 46 in 2005. His current total of 45-plus puts him second only to Ernie Banks in Cubs history for home runs by a player age 24 or younger.

The stolen base is the one outstanding variable. And the whole baseball world is watching the basepaths every time he reaches first base.

The Club He's Joining

The 40-40 club is not a list that gets many new members. Canseco opened it in 1988, delivering 42 home runs and 40 stolen bases for the Oakland A's. Barry Bonds joined in 1996. Alex Rodriguez in 1998. Alfonso Soriano made it in 2006. Then there was a 17-year gap before Ronald Acuña Jr. reached the milestone for the Atlanta Braves in 2023. Shohei Ohtani joined the following year.

Each of those seasons was a milestone in its own right. Crow-Armstrong would be reaching the milestone at 24 — younger than Bonds (31) and significantly younger than Rodriguez (22 — actually the youngest), and doing it in the final days of a meaningful pennant race. The combination of individual achievement and team stakes makes this one of the most compelling storylines of the 2026 season.

What's Next

The Cubs play the Reds, Marlins, and Red Sox in their final three regular season games. Crow-Armstrong will play in all of them barring injury, and each game is a new chance to complete the milestone. One stolen base. Three games. The postseason starts September 29 — and if he gets that steal, he'll be entering October as a member of one of baseball's most exclusive clubs. Watch closely.
