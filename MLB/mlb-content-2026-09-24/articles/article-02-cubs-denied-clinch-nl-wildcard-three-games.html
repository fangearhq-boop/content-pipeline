Cubs Denied Again: Two Marlins Losses Leave NL Wild Card Race Alive With 3 Games to Go

By Marcus Cole | September 24, 2026

For the second consecutive night, the Miami Marlins played the role of spoiler.

Wednesday at Wrigley Field, Kyle Stowers hit his 21st home run of the season off Cubs starter Kevin Gausman in the sixth inning, breaking a scoreless tie. Joe Mack's groundout in the seventh added an insurance run. Dansby Swanson drove in both Chicago runs with a broken-bat single to left, but the Cubs' comeback fell short. Final score: Marlins 3, Cubs 2.

Chicago needed either a win or an Arizona Diamondbacks loss to clinch an NL Wild Card berth Wednesday night. The Diamondbacks beat the Rockies 5-3. Neither path worked. The Cubs go into Thursday still waiting — and with only three games left on the schedule.

Still Leading, But Not Safe

The Cubs maintain the lead in the NL Wild Card standings. But the Padres and Phillies are both within striking distance, and the final three days of the regular season will determine not just whether Chicago makes the playoffs — a result that still seems likely — but where they're seeded when the Wild Card round begins September 29.

The Cubs' remaining schedule: Cincinnati Reds, Miami Marlins, Boston Red Sox. A team they've already had trouble with this week, and opponents with varying motivation going into the season's final week.

The Padres close with the Marlins, Dodgers, and Diamondbacks — the most brutal remaining schedule of the three Wild Card contenders. If San Diego struggles to close, it opens the door for the Cubs and Phillies to separate themselves. The Phillies face the Mets, Brewers, and Rays — with the Brewers (NL Central clinched, Misiorowski likely pitching) a formidable late-schedule opponent.

The Stakes Are Real

Wild Card seeding matters in baseball's bracket system. The No. 1 Wild Card seed hosts the three-game series and controls home-field advantage for the round. The Cubs' two consecutive losses have tightened the race to the point where that seeding is no longer certain.

Two consecutive losses to a non-competitive Marlins team — while the Cubs were trying to clinch — is the kind of thing that gets circled in an offseason review if Chicago doesn't hold on. But they still control their own destiny. Win the next game, and the math simplifies dramatically.

What's Next

The Cubs play Thursday night with a chance to finally close out a postseason berth. A win eliminates the need for scoreboard watching. With Pete Crow-Armstrong one stolen base from a 40-40 season and the Wild Card on the line, every inning between now and Sunday matters. The Cubs still have the lead. They just need to close it.
