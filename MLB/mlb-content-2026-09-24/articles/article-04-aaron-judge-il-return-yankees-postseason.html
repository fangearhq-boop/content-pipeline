Judge's 10-Day IL Expires by Sept. 27 — Yankees Managing Him Carefully for October

By Ryan Calloway | September 24, 2026

Aaron Judge's 10-day injured list stint, placed retroactive to September 17, means the New York Yankees slugger could technically return as early as September 27 — the final day of the regular season.

The operative word is "technically."

Manager Aaron Boone hasn't put a timeline on Judge's return, and for good reason. The injury in question is a right soleus calf strain — and the soleus is the muscle you don't want to rush. It sits beneath the more visible gastrocnemius, deeper in the calf, and it's critical for explosive athletic movements: the push-off on a stolen base attempt, the rotation in the batter's box, the burst required to chase down a fly ball. Players who return too quickly from soleus strains often end up back on the injured list within days.

A Difficult Year, Carefully Managed

Judge has already been through one lengthy absence in 2026. He spent 99 days on the injured list with a stress fracture in his ribs, returning to the Yankees on September 8. He played seven games before the soleus strain put him back on the shelf.

That's the uncomfortable reality for New York: their franchise player has now had two significant injuries in one season. The first was the ribs. The second is the calf. Neither is the kind of thing that simply heals and goes away — particularly for a player of Judge's size and athleticism, where the physical demands on every body part are amplified by frame and usage.

The Yankees clinched their playoff spot before Judge's second injury. They've been managing their roster with postseason health in mind, and that calculation hasn't changed. Getting Judge back for three final regular season games isn't worth the risk if it costs him games in October.

What Postseason Looks Like

Judge himself said he'd like to be back for the postseason, and the Yankees are cautiously optimistic that timeline is possible. The Wild Card round begins September 29, giving him roughly two weeks from the date of the injury to get healthy enough to participate.

Two weeks for a soleus strain is tight, but not unrealistic if the injury is at the mild-to-moderate end of the spectrum. The Yankees will watch his progress through the final days of the regular season and make the postseason roster decision accordingly. An elongated playoff run also helps — if the Yankees advance past the Wild Card round, Judge could be back at some point in the Division or Championship Series even if he's not ready for the opening round.

What's Next

The Yankees will evaluate Judge daily through the weekend. September 27 is the target eligibility date, but the real target is Game 1 of the postseason on September 29. The Yankees are a playoff team regardless of his availability, but the gap between their ceiling with Judge and without him is significant enough that this injury watch will be the dominant storyline of the New York offseason if his recovery runs long.
