Misiorowski Gets His Second Shot at Wrigley — Cy Young Front-Runner Seeks Redemption

By Jake Torres | September 24, 2026

Jacob Misiorowski is going back to Wrigley Field.

The Milwaukee Brewers ace starts Thursday night against the Chicago Cubs at Wrigley — the same ballpark where he posted the worst start of his career on September 2: five runs allowed in four innings, four walks, five strikeouts. The Brewers rallied to win 9-5, but the outing was a rare rough patch in one of the best pitching seasons in baseball.

That was then. Tonight is different.

The Case for Misiorowski This Season

The numbers tell a story that doesn't leave much room for argument. A 1.95 ERA. A 20-8 record. 247 strikeouts, which lead the major leagues. On September 8, he became the first pitcher in the history of tracking to throw 1,000 pitches at 100 miles per hour or faster in a single season.

He's the Cy Young Award front-runner in the National League — and he deserves the award. The Brewers have clinched the NL Central under his leadership, and Misiorowski has been the foundation of everything that's gone right in Milwaukee this year.

The September 2 start at Wrigley was the outlier. Five runs, four walks — that's not who he is. The ballpark didn't help. The conditions weren't ideal. And the Cubs had a specific approach against him that day that worked. Tonight, he's had 22 days to think about it.

A Record Within Reach

There's a milestone attached to tonight's start. Ben Sheets struck out 264 batters in 2004 — the Milwaukee Brewers franchise record for strikeouts in a single season. Misiorowski sits at 247. He needs 17 more strikeouts in his remaining starts to break it.

It's a tight timeline, but not impossible. If he deals tonight against the Cubs and gets double-digit strikeouts, the math starts looking more favorable for the regular season. Two strong remaining starts could get him there — or he could return to that chase in the postseason, although strikeouts in playoff starts don't count toward regular-season records.

For Misiorowski, the Sheets record is a secondary story. The primary one is finishing the year with the statement start that puts an exclamation point on the best pitching campaign Milwaukee has seen in two decades.

What's Next

Tonight's start will be one of the most watched pitching matchups of the final regular season week. A dominant Misiorowski outing — deep innings, double-digit strikeouts — would advance his Cy Young case to a nearly unanimous conclusion and push his single-season K total toward the Brewers franchise record. A rough outing would mean the September 2 anomaly wasn't entirely an anomaly. One start, two storylines, and a lot of baseball left to play. Watch closely.
