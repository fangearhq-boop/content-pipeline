Yordan Alvarez's Triple Crown Chase: The Math Is Hard, But He's Not Done

By Marcus Cole | September 24, 2026

Yordan Alvarez is still alive in the American League Triple Crown chase. But the window is closing fast.

Entering the final series of the regular season, the Houston Astros slugger leads the AL in batting average at .311 — his five-point edge over Tampa Bay's Chandler Simpson is the most secure of his three Triple Crown categories. The other two are a different story.

In home runs, Alvarez sits at roughly 38, trailing Simpson's teammate Junior Caminero of the Tampa Bay Rays by approximately two. In RBI, he's at roughly 96, trailing Baltimore's Pete Alonso by about four. With three or four games left, the path to all three titles runs through a hot finish from Alvarez and a cold finish from two separate opponents.

Why It's So Rare

The Triple Crown is one of the rarest achievements in baseball precisely because it requires dominating three entirely different metrics simultaneously against an entire league's worth of competition. Every player trying to lead in batting average is trying not to swing at bad pitches. Every player trying to lead in home runs is swinging for power. Every player trying to lead in RBI needs teammates on base. The three demands pull in different directions.

The last AL Triple Crown winner was Miguel Cabrera with the Detroit Tigers in 2012. Before Cabrera, the last American League winner was Carl Yastrzemski in 1967 — a gap of 45 years. In the National League, the story is even more sparse.

When Alvarez was leading all three categories in August, the chase felt genuinely real. Production has cooled somewhat since then — as it often does for anyone chasing historic numbers as the pressure of late September builds — and now he's in the uncomfortable position of needing outside help.

The Math

To win the Triple Crown, Alvarez needs to finish ahead of Caminero in home runs and ahead of Alonso in RBI. In a three-game window, gaining two home runs on a player who isn't slumping is possible but unlikely. Gaining four RBI on a player in four games is even harder, particularly when RBI is a counting stat that depends as much on teammates as on the individual.

It's not impossible. Baseball has seen stranger things in the final series of a season. But Alvarez is betting on a combination of his own dominance and his rivals' slumps — and that combination hasn't fully materialized over the last few weeks.

What's Next

The Astros play their final three games of the regular season beginning Thursday. Alvarez will play in all of them, and his stat line will be one of the most closely watched in baseball. If Caminero goes cold and Alonso stops driving in runs, the door cracks open. If neither happens, Alvarez finishes with a brilliant season that came one category short of historic. Either way, his 2026 season has been one of the best in recent AL history. The Triple Crown would make it legendary. Watch the numbers this weekend.
