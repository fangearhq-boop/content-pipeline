From 121 Losses to First Place: The White Sox Are Writing One of Baseball's Great Rebuild Stories

By Jake Torres

July 27, 2026

Two years ago, the Chicago White Sox lost 121 games. That is not a typo. It was the most losses in a single season by any team in baseball history — a record that arrived at the lowest point of a rebuild that had tested the patience of an entire fanbase.

Today, the White Sox are in first place in the AL Central at 54-48.

What they are doing right now is one of the most remarkable franchise turnarounds in the history of professional baseball.

The Numbers Tell the Arc

2024: 41-121. An MLB record for losses in a single season. The bottom of the pit. 2025: 60-102. Still losing, still painful, but the direction was beginning to change. 2026: 54-48, in first place, acting as buyers at the trade deadline.

A 30-win improvement from worst-ever to first place in fewer than two full seasons. Only seven teams in baseball history have matched that kind of single-season improvement. The White Sox are doing something that scouts, analysts, and fans said could not happen this fast.

Who Is Doing It

Colson Montgomery has emerged as one of the most productive power bats in the American League this season. His 21-plus home runs put him on one of the most impressive paces for a player early in his career — a first-round pick delivering on his promise ahead of schedule.

The team recently absorbed a significant blow when catcher Kyle Teel went on the 10-day IL with a high ankle sprain — an injury that is expected to keep him out for the rest of the season, somewhere in the six-to-twelve-week range. In response, the White Sox called up 22-year-old Edgar Quero from Triple-A Charlotte. That is not a panic move — it is a franchise moving its top organizational depth through the pipeline when the opportunity arrives.

Buyers at the Deadline

The most telling signal about where the White Sox believe they are came last week, when it became clear the organization is approaching the August 3 trade deadline as buyers. Not sellers. Not bystanders. Buyers.

Two years removed from 121 losses, the Chicago White Sox are making moves to improve a first-place team. That sentence would have been difficult to write with a straight face in April 2025. It is simply true now.

What's Next

The AL Central race will intensify through the final two months of the season. The White Sox have proven they belong in it — but maintaining a division lead requires consistency across 162 games, and the competition in that division has not gone away. The deadline moves they make in the next seven days could shape whether this becomes a story that ends in October.

For now, though, they are in first place. And in Chicago, after what the last two years have asked of this fanbase, that is worth appreciating on its own terms.
