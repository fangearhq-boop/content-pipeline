Mike Trout Goes 4-for-4, Passes Tim Salmon for Second on Angels All-Time Doubles List

By Marcus Cole

July 27, 2026

Mike Trout had a night on Saturday that reminded everyone why he spent years as the consensus best player in baseball.

In the Angels' 4-3 victory over the San Francisco Giants on July 26, Trout went 4-for-4 — a double and three singles — snapping a three-game Angels losing streak and delivering the kind of performance that punctuates a career. It was his first four-hit game since April 2021, and the 21st time he has reached that milestone over the course of his career.

The Doubles Record

The moment that will hold its place in the franchise history books came via Trout's double — his 340th career two-base hit. That single extra-base hit moved him past Angels legend Tim Salmon and into second place on the Los Angeles Angels all-time franchise doubles list.

The only name ahead of Trout now is Garrett Anderson, who accumulated 489 doubles over a career spent entirely in Anaheim. Anderson's mark represents a significant gap — and whether Trout can reach it depends on the seasons ahead of him, his health, and how long he continues to play in an Angels uniform.

But second on the franchise list, ahead of Salmon, is not a small thing. Tim Salmon is one of the most beloved players in franchise history. Trout moving past him in doubles tells you something about the scope of what he has built in Anaheim.

The Rest of the Game

Jorge Soler and Jo Adell both hit home runs in the fourth inning to provide the power foundation for the Angels' win. Jose Soriano pitched five sharp innings as the starter, giving Los Angeles a quality start that set up the bullpen to close out the victory.

It was the kind of complete team performance the Angels have needed more of, and Trout's 4-for-4 line at the top of the order set the tone from the first pitch.

Trout's 2026 Season

Trout has had a quieter injury-interrupted career in recent seasons — the kind of stretch that occasionally makes fans and analysts wonder whether the peak has passed. But nights like Saturday are a reminder that the talent never went anywhere. When healthy and locked in, he remains one of the most dangerous hitters in the game.

What's Next

The Angels continue their series Sunday. Trout will likely spend the next several years within striking distance of Garrett Anderson's doubles record — and every two-base hit from here on out moves him closer to a place no Angel has been able to reach since Anderson retired. For a player who has already set franchise records in nearly every meaningful category, the doubles list may be the last major franchise milestone still ahead of him.
