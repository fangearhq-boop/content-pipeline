Beltran, Jones, and Kent Enter Cooperstown in an Emotional Hall of Fame Ceremony

By Marcus Cole

July 27, 2026

Three men walked into the Clark Sports Center in Cooperstown, New York, on Sunday afternoon and walked out Hall of Famers.

Carlos Beltran. Andruw Jones. Jeff Kent. The Baseball Hall of Fame Class of 2026 was officially inducted July 26 before an estimated crowd of 12,000 fans, in a ceremony marked by emotion, cultural pride, and moments that will not be forgotten easily. Sixty Hall of Famers were in Cooperstown for the weekend — and 57 of them stood on stage simultaneously, the largest gathering of Hall of Famers in the history of the institution.

Andruw Jones: A Love Letter to Curacao

Andruw Jones became the first player from Curacao ever inducted into the Baseball Hall of Fame — and his speech reflected the magnitude of that moment. Entering his ninth year of BBWAA eligibility with 78.4 percent of the vote, Jones spoke at length about his home island, with his address described in attendance as "almost like a love letter" to the place that made him.

"There's no easy way to get here from the small island of Curacao, more than 2,000 miles away," Jones said from the podium.

His career numbers — ten consecutive Gold Gloves, a resume that many analysts consider the finest defensive center field career in baseball history — have always made his Hall case. But on Sunday, it was the story behind those numbers that stood out.

Carlos Beltran: For Puerto Rico

Carlos Beltran entered in his fourth year of BBWAA eligibility with 84.2 percent of the vote — a reflection of how thoroughly the baseball world has come to appreciate a player who combined elite hitting, elite defense, and elite baserunning across 20 seasons.

When Beltran stepped to the podium, he acknowledged feeling the same nerves he had felt at his first major league at-bat. But it was what he said next that defined his speech. He dedicated a significant portion of his remarks to the island of Puerto Rico, where he grew up and where he has always returned.

"Everywhere I play baseball, I always try to represent my island," Beltran said.

Jeff Kent: The Gruff Man Who Cried

Jeff Kent was elected by the Contemporary Baseball Era Committee at the December 2025 Winter Meetings — a route to Cooperstown that arrived after his BBWAA eligibility expired in 2023. He is the first second baseman inducted into the Hall since Craig Biggio in 2015, and he wore a San Francisco Giants cap on the most important day of his professional life.

Kent is known publicly as a hard, private man — one who did not always cultivate warmth with the media or, at times, his own teammates. Which is why it stopped the room when, during his speech, he looked at his parents in the crowd and reached for tissues.

It was not the Jeff Kent anyone expected to see. It was the Jeff Kent who had been there all along.

What's Next

The Class of 2026 joins one of the most celebrated institutions in American sports. Beltran, Jones, and Kent all wore their game hats — Mets, Braves, and Giants — and each told the story of their journey from their hometowns to the most exclusive club in baseball.

Sixty Hall of Famers were in Cooperstown this weekend. Fifty-seven stood on stage at once. Records were set. Speeches were given. And three more names are now on the plaques in the gallery at Cooperstown, New York — permanent, alongside the greatest the game has ever known.
