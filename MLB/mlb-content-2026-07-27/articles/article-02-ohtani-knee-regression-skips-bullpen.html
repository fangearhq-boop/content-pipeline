Ohtani Skips Scheduled Bullpen After Knee Shows "Regression" — Return Timeline Pushed Back Again

By Jake Torres

July 27, 2026

Shohei Ohtani's path back to the mound took another step backward on Saturday.

The Los Angeles Dodgers superstar skipped his scheduled bullpen session July 26 after manager Dave Roberts reported "a little bit of regression" in the left knee that has kept Ohtani off the pitching mound since July 3. The setback appears to reset what was already an open-ended recovery timeline — and Roberts confirmed over the weekend that Ohtani will not return to pitching "next week."

The Sequence of Events

Ohtani threw a bullpen session of approximately 30 to 31 pitches around July 22. Initial reports from the Dodgers were cautiously encouraging — Roberts said it looked good, and pitching coach Mark Prior described it as a "normal side session." But in the days that followed, the knee responded poorly.

"We're not going to move forward until he feels 100% confident, we feel 100% confident that he should do it," Roberts said after Saturday's canceled session.

The medical specifics make this a particularly complex situation. The torque generated by landing on the left leg during the pitching delivery is the precise mechanical action that aggravates the knee — meaning Ohtani can continue to serve as the designated hitter without issue, but the act of pitching places a different physiological demand on the joint entirely.

The Numbers He Left Behind

When Ohtani last took the mound on July 3, he was one of the most dominant pitchers in baseball. His season line at that point: 8-2, 1.79 ERA, 95 strikeouts, 85.2 innings pitched. A Cy Young Award in any previous season. A line that his competitors were watching with genuine concern.

Since then, he has continued to appear in the Dodgers lineup as the DH, currently slashing .288/.397/.536 — production that would make him one of the most valuable designated hitters in the National League even in a season where he never takes the mound again.

But the Dodgers did not sign Ohtani for his DH production alone. They signed him for the version of Shohei Ohtani who changes the rotation, elevates the pitching staff, and turns the Dodgers from a very good team into the most dangerous team in baseball. That version currently has no timetable.

What's Next

Roberts did not provide a specific window for Ohtani's next bullpen attempt after Saturday's cancellation. The message from the Dodgers is clear: they will not rush this, and they will not commit to a return date until they are fully confident the knee can handle the demands of pitching in game conditions.

For a Dodgers team chasing a third consecutive World Series title, Ohtani's health — and his eventual return to the mound — remains the most consequential story of the second half. For now, they wait.
