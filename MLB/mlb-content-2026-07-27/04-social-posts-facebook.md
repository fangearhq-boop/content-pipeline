### STORY 1: HOF Induction Ceremony

**POST S1-FB-LONGFORM** | 8:00 AM ET
Three men walked into Cooperstown on Sunday and walked out Hall of Famers.

Carlos Beltran. Andruw Jones. Jeff Kent. The Baseball Hall of Fame Class of 2026 was officially inducted yesterday at the Clark Sports Center in front of an estimated crowd of 12,000 fans — and the ceremony will be remembered for years.

**Andruw Jones** made history as the first player from Curacao ever inducted into the Hall. His speech was described as "almost like a love letter" to his home island, where he said: "There's no easy way to get here from the small island of Curacao, more than 2,000 miles away." He entered his 9th year of BBWAA eligibility with 78.4% of the vote. Ten Gold Gloves. The defensive standard for center field.

**Carlos Beltran** was one of the most complete players of his generation — a switch hitter who could do everything. He entered in his 4th year with 84.2% of the vote. And when he stepped to the podium, he compared the moment to the nerves of his first major league at-bat, then dedicated his speech to the island of Puerto Rico: "Everywhere I play baseball, I always try to represent my island."

**Jeff Kent** was elected by the Contemporary Baseball Era Committee this past December, becoming the first second baseman inducted since Craig Biggio in 2015. Kent is known for being gruff and private — so when he needed tissues to wipe away tears after looking at his parents in the crowd, it was a moment that stopped everyone in the building.

The record: 60 Hall of Famers were in Cooperstown for the weekend. 57 took the stage simultaneously — the largest gathering of Hall of Famers in history.

Baseball is a game of moments. Yesterday was full of them. 🏟️ #BallparkBanter #HallOfFame #MLB

### STORY 2: Ohtani Knee Regression

**POST S2-FB-LONGFORM** | 10:30 AM ET
Shohei Ohtani's return to the mound just got pushed back — again.

The Dodgers superstar skipped his scheduled bullpen session on Saturday after manager Dave Roberts reported "a little bit of regression" in Ohtani's troublesome left knee. Ohtani had thrown approximately 30-31 pitches in a bullpen session around July 22 that initially looked encouraging. But the knee responded poorly in the days that followed.

Roberts was direct about the situation: "We're not going to move forward until he feels 100% confident, we feel 100% confident that he should do it." When asked about a return timeline, Roberts said flatly: it "won't be next week" when Ohtani pitches in a game.

The last time Ohtani pitched in a game: July 3, 2026. At that point he was 8-2 with a 1.79 ERA, 95 strikeouts, and 85.2 innings pitched — Cy Young numbers by any measure. Since then, he has continued to serve as the Dodgers' DH, currently slashing .288/.397/.536.

What makes this particularly tricky is the medical situation. The torque of landing on the left leg during the pitching delivery is precisely what aggravates the knee — meaning Ohtani can continue to hit without issue, but throwing a pitch is a different physiological demand entirely.

For the Dodgers, who are chasing a third consecutive World Series title, Ohtani as a DH is still a force. But Ohtani as a two-way ace is the version that changes the calculus. Right now, that version has no timetable. #BallparkBanter #Dodgers #Ohtani #MLB

### STORY 3: Trade Deadline

**POST S3-FB-LONGFORM** | 12:30 PM ET
The MLB Trade Deadline is one week away — August 3, 2026 — and the market is heating up.

Here's a snapshot of where things stand on the major names:

**Tarik Skubal (Tigers):** The Tigers are "at least mulling" a deal, per Bleacher Report, but the growing consensus among rival executives is that Detroit will keep him. Skubal himself says he's "never been more motivated" and wants to stay. He's been excellent in 2026 with a 2.83 ERA across 14 starts. If he goes, the Dodgers are the most frequently mentioned destination.

**Mason Miller (Padres):** The San Diego closer has been historically good this season — 0.89 ERA with 40.2 innings pitched and 25-for-25 in saves. Rival executives are "convinced" the Padres will move him, per SI.com. The asking price will be steep.

**First major deal of the summer:** The Red Sox sent SP Connelly Early to the Nationals in exchange for 3B Curtis Mead (25). A sign that things are moving.

### STORY 4: Mike Trout 4-for-4

**POST S4-FB-LONGFORM** | 3:00 PM ET
Mike Trout reminded everyone Saturday why he's still one of the most dangerous hitters in baseball.

In the Angels' 4-3 win over the Giants, Trout went 4-for-4 — his first four-hit game since April 2021 and the 21st four-hit game of his career.

But it was his double — the 340th of his career — that carried a franchise milestone. That hit moved Trout past Tim Salmon and into second place all-time on the Los Angeles Angels' franchise doubles list. The only name ahead of him is Garrett Anderson, who accumulated 489 doubles over a career spent entirely in Anaheim.

Two other Angels contributed to the victory: Jorge Soler and Jo Adell both went deep in the fourth inning, providing the power surge the team needed. Jose Soriano was effective in five innings of work as the Angels snapped a three-game losing streak.

Mike Trout has been through a lot in recent seasons — injuries, a rebuilding roster around him, and the long wait for a contender in Anaheim. But when he is right, he is still one of the most complete hitters in the game. Saturday was a reminder of that.

### STORY 5: White Sox Leading AL Central

**POST S5-FB-LONGFORM** | 6:00 PM ET
Let's take a moment to appreciate what the Chicago White Sox are doing right now.

In 2024, the White Sox lost 121 games — an MLB record. The season was a historic low point for the franchise, a painful marker of a rebuild that had gone sideways.

In 2025, they lost 102 more — still bad, still losing, but with signs of a foundation forming.

In 2026? They're 54-48 and leading the AL Central.

That's a 30-win swing from the worst season in baseball history to a first-place team in fewer than two full years. To put that in context: only seven teams in baseball history have matched a single-season improvement of that magnitude.

The engine driving this run includes Colson Montgomery, who has been one of the best power bats in the AL this season. The team recently called up Edgar Quero (22) from Triple-A Charlotte to replace the injured Kyle Teel, who is expected to miss the rest of the season with a high ankle sprain.

With the August 3 trade deadline approaching, the White Sox find themselves in a position no one predicted: acting as buyers. That's not just a transaction — it's a statement about where this organization believes it is heading.

Three years ago, losing 121 games, no one would have believed it. Now here they are. 🙌 #BallparkBanter #WhiteSox #MLB
