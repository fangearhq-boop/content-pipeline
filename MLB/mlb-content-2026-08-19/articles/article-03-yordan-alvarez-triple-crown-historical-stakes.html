Yordan Alvarez and the Triple Crown: What It Takes, What It Means, What's Left

By Ryan Calloway

August 19, 2026

Yordan Alvarez is leading the American League in batting average (.329), home runs (36), and runs batted in (88). He is doing it simultaneously, which is what makes this different from just having a great season. Any one of those categories would be noteworthy. Leading all three at the same time, with 44 games left, is historically rare.

The last player to lead his league in all three Triple Crown categories at the end of a season was Miguel Cabrera, who accomplished the feat in 2012. Before the final games are played in September 2026, Alvarez needs to hold all three leads. That is harder than it sounds.

The Three Categories

Each of the three Triple Crown categories presents a different challenge for the final 44 games.

Batting average is Alvarez's most secure position. He leads the AL's second-place hitter by .029 points. Batting average leads of that size are extremely difficult to eliminate late in a season — a hitter trying to catch up would have to sustain a pace well above their normal output while Alvarez falls into a slump at the same time. This category should hold.

RBI is the second-most secure. Alvarez leads the next closest competitor by seven runs batted in. RBI totals can shift with hot stretches by rivals, but a seven-run lead this late in the season requires specific circumstances — sustained production from one hitter and a cold stretch from Alvarez — to close meaningfully.

Home runs are the most volatile category. Kyle Schwarber sits at 37 home runs, one ahead of Alvarez. That one-homer gap can change in a single game. Schwarber is a legitimate threat. Junior Caminero, at 33, would need to be exceptional over the final six weeks to close the gap, but that possibility is not zero.

The Home Run Race Is the Key

For Alvarez to win the Triple Crown, he needs to finish the season leading the AL in home runs. That means either catching Schwarber or maintaining the current gap while Schwarber cools off. Neither outcome is guaranteed.

What Alvarez has in his favor is power consistency. His slash line of .329/.442/.640 reflects both on-base discipline and slugging ability — he is not in a stretch where the power has left him. If he continues producing at his current rate, the home run total will climb and the gap with Schwarber will become the daily storyline through September.

Why It Matters

Miguel Cabrera won the Triple Crown in 2012 and it was celebrated as one of the great individual achievements in baseball history — because it had not been done in 45 years at that point. Cabrera's achievement came after a long drought. Alvarez would be completing one 14 years after that.

The difficulty of winning the Triple Crown is not just the quantity of production required but the simultaneity of it. A player has to sustain elite performance in three completely different skills — contact, power, and run production — across an entire 162-game season without a prolonged slump in any one category. The categories work against each other statistically: protecting the batting average sometimes means being patient in counts that reduce home run opportunities; swinging for power sometimes reduces contact rate.

Alvarez has balanced all three for most of the 2026 season. 44 games is enough time to close any of these gaps. It is also enough time to run away with them entirely.

What's Next

Watch Alvarez's daily stats from now through the final week of September. The home run race with Schwarber is the number to track every morning. If Alvarez builds a three-or-more homer lead in that category, the Triple Crown becomes a realistic probability. Check the AL leaderboard daily — this is one of the most compelling individual storylines of the 2026 season.
