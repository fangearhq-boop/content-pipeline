Jacob Misiorowski's NL Cy Young Case: The Numbers Make the Argument

By Jake Torres

August 19, 2026

The NL Cy Young Award race is not a race. It is a destination, and Jacob Misiorowski is already there.

The Milwaukee Brewers right-hander enters the final 44 games of the season with an 11-5 record, a 1.76 ERA, and 204 strikeouts in 133 and two-thirds innings pitched. He has set the Brewers' franchise single-season strikeout record. He is the second-fastest pitcher in major league history to reach 200 strikeouts in a season, trailing only Spencer Strider's 2022 pace. His 40.6% strikeout rate leads all of baseball.

The odds makers have seen enough. Misiorowski is listed at -550 to win the NL Cy Young Award.

The Season in Numbers

A 1.76 ERA across this many innings is not a product of luck or soft competition. It reflects consistent execution at an elite level across months of major league baseball. Misiorowski has been genuinely dominant — not just statistically successful, but the kind of dominant that opposing hitters discuss during batting practice.

His strikeout rate of 40.6% means that roughly four in ten at-bats against him end in a strikeout. No pitcher in baseball is doing that more frequently. The Cy Young Award is, at its core, a recognition of pitching dominance, and no pitcher in the National League has been more dominant in 2026.

The wins-and-losses record — 11-5 — is secondary to the peripheral numbers, but it reinforces the case. Misiorowski is winning games while the broader run environment in baseball makes wins increasingly difficult to accumulate. The Brewers are 77-48, the best record in major league baseball, and he has been a central reason why.

The Moment That Defined the Season

On July 26, Misiorowski faced the Colorado Rockies and struck out 7 consecutive batters to open the game — a Brewers franchise record. He struck out 12 of the first 13 batters he faced in that game. In the process, he threw 33 fastballs at 103 miles per hour or faster — the most in a single game since pitch tracking began.

Moments like that do not happen by accident. They happen because a pitcher's stuff is so far above what hitters expect that the normal adjustment process does not apply. Misiorowski, on that night, was not pitchable. He was the best pitcher in baseball in one complete performance.

That game also put him in historical company: he reached 200 strikeouts faster than any pitcher in history except Strider. The Brewers delayed his next scheduled start to give him extra rest, protecting their investment in a staff ace during a pennant race.

The Only Counter-Argument

The only scenario in which Misiorowski does not win the NL Cy Young Award is a dominant final six weeks by another pitcher combined with a significant Misiorowski decline. There is no NL pitcher currently within statistical shouting distance of his ERA or strikeout rate. He would have to stop being himself for the award to go elsewhere.

Health is the variable. A pitcher who misses time with injury loses Cy Young votes regardless of the numbers that came before. If Misiorowski takes the ball every fifth day through the end of September, this award is settled.

What's Next

Misiorowski's next start is expected around August 23. Each outing from here until the Brewers' final game adds to the case — or creates an opening for an opponent to close the gap. Track his starts, his strikeout totals, and his ERA through September. The Cy Young Award ceremony is in November. The decision, based on the 2026 numbers, looks like it has already been made.
