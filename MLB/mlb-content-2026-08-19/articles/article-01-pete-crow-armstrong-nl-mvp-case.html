Pete Crow-Armstrong's NL MVP Case Is Real — And It's Getting Stronger

By Marcus Cole

August 19, 2026

Monday night at Wrigley Field, Pete Crow-Armstrong hit a walk-off home run in the 10th inning to give the Chicago Cubs a 7-5 win over the White Sox. It was his 30th home run of the season. It was his first career walk-off. And it made him the sixth player in MLB history to hit both a leadoff home run and a walk-off home run in the same game.

He went 4-for-5 with a double, two home runs, and 3 RBI. When the ball left the park in the 10th, he was being chased around the bases by his teammates in one of the most electric moments of the Cubs' season.

The game recap wrote itself. Now comes the bigger question: is Pete Crow-Armstrong the National League Most Valuable Player?

The Numbers

Crow-Armstrong now has 30 home runs and 31 stolen bases on the 2026 season. He is the first player in Cubs history to record back-to-back 30-30 seasons, and the 19th player in major league history to achieve multiple 30-30 campaigns. These are not merely impressive numbers — they are genuinely rare ones.

Power-speed combination at the level PCA is producing it is one of the most valuable skillsets in the sport. Home runs change scores. Stolen bases change game states. A player who does both at this volume is operating as a constant offensive threat in a way that accumulates differently than a slugger or a burner alone.

The Cubs are 72-52. They hold the No. 1 NL Wild Card seed heading into the final 44 games of the regular season. PCA is the engine of that offense.

The MVP Competition

NL MVP races in recent years have rewarded players who combine statistical volume with winning context — which makes the Cubs' position in the Wild Card race meaningful for PCA's case. He is not putting up big numbers on a losing team. He is at the center of one of the best offenses in the National League on a team that has a legitimate postseason path.

His competition will emerge from the final six weeks of the season. The award goes to voters in November, and what happens over the final 44 games carries significant weight. PCA needs a strong finish to make the case undeniable.

The walk-off moment on Monday is the kind of highlight that voters remember. Production and winning context are the arguments. The walk-off was the reminder.

The Historical Frame

Back-to-back 30-30 seasons represent a level of sustained elite performance that very few players achieve. The list of players with multiple 30-30 seasons reads like a Hall of Fame discussion: speed and power combined at that volume requires a player to stay healthy, stay in the lineup, and make consistent hard contact all season long.

PCA doing it in consecutive years, at his age, for a contending franchise, is the argument in one sentence. The NL MVP conversation needs to include him.

What's Next

The Cubs close out the Crosstown Classic series today at Wrigley before heading into a full September slate. Watch PCA's production over the next 44 games — if the home runs and stolen bases keep accumulating alongside winning, the MVP case will write itself. Final ballots come due in November, and the voters will look at the full picture.
