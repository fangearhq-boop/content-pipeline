### STORY 1: Pete Crow-Armstrong — 3 SB Away From 40-40 With 6 Games Left

**Long-Form Post**
Three stolen bases. Six games. That's the entire equation standing between Pete Crow-Armstrong and one of the rarest achievements in baseball history.

PCA enters the final week of the regular season with 47 home runs and 37 stolen bases — putting the 40-40 club squarely in his sights. Only six players have ever done it: Jose Canseco (1988), Barry Bonds (1996), Alex Rodriguez (1998), Alfonso Soriano (2006), Ronald Acuña Jr. (2023), and Shohei Ohtani (2024). PCA would become No. 7 — and the first Cub in franchise history to accomplish the feat.

He already rewrote the record books this season by breaking Billy Williams' left-handed Cubs home run record of 42, set back in 1970. Now he's chasing something even bigger.

The Cubs sit at 87-69 with a magic number of 1 to clinch their postseason spot. PCA could be stealing his way into history in the middle of a Wild Card clinching celebration. The stars are aligning.

Three steals. Six games. Don't look away.

### STORY 3: Misiorowski's Cy Young Case — 243 K, 21 from the Brewers Record

**Long-Form Post**
Jacob Misiorowski isn't just winning the Cy Young Award — he's rewriting what a pitching season can look like.

At 24 years old, the Brewers ace leads all of baseball with a 1.89 ERA, 243 strikeouts, and a 0.81 WHIP. He's the -700 favorite in NL Cy Young odds — essentially a lock. And his nearest challenger, Chris Sale at +500, isn't really close.

But the individual award is almost a footnote now. Misiorowski is 21 strikeouts away from breaking Ben Sheets' Brewers franchise record of 264, set in 2004. He also became the first pitcher in the tracking era to record 1,000 pitches at 100-plus mph in a single season.

The Brewers have clinched their fourth consecutive NL Central title. Their ace is putting together a historically elite season. One more start could crack the franchise record.

Yordan Alvarez entered the final week of the regular season trailing in two of three Triple Crown categories — and his September batting average has been ice cold at .206. The window isn't just closing. It's nearly shut.

Alvarez leads the AL with a .310 batting average (Chandler Simpson of the Rays is five points back at .305). But here's the thing: it doesn't need to. Alvarez's 2026 stands as one of the great AL offensive seasons of the decade regardless. His MVP case remains as strong as anyone's in the league.

The Triple Crown is gone. The MVP is his to lose.
