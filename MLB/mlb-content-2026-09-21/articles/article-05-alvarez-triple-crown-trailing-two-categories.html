Alvarez's Triple Crown Bid Is Nearly Over — His MVP Case Isn't

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | September 21, 2026

Yordan Alvarez is having one of the great American League offensive seasons in years. He is also going to fall short of the Triple Crown.

Those two things coexist without contradiction, and understanding both is the key to reading the end of his 2026 season clearly. Alvarez enters the final week trailing in two of three categories — home runs and RBI — and his September batting average of .206 has been the coldest stretch of his year. The math that looked possible in August has become nearly impossible now.

The last American League Triple Crown winner was Miguel Cabrera with the Detroit Tigers in 2012. That title will not belong to Alvarez in 2026.

Where the Numbers Stand

Alvarez leads the AL in batting average at .310, holding a five-point edge over Chandler Simpson of the Rays at .305. That lead has shrunk throughout September as Alvarez's cold stretch has coincided with Simpson playing well.

In home runs, Alvarez sits at 38 — two behind Yandy Caminero of the Rays, who has reached 40. Closing that gap would require Alvarez to hit home runs at a rate he simply hasn't been matching in September while Caminero stays cold or goes cold. It is possible but not probable.

In RBI, Alvarez is at roughly 96-99, trailing Pete Alonso of the Orioles, who is near 100. Again — possible to close, but requiring a combination of Alvarez performance and Alonso underperformance that the final six games may not provide.

The Season That Stands Regardless

Here is what does not change based on the Triple Crown outcome: Yordan Alvarez's 2026 is one of the premier offensive seasons of his career and one of the best in the AL this year. A .310 average, 38 home runs, and approximately 100 RBI represents elite production by any standard. His OPS and offensive WAR remain near the top of the league.

The AL MVP race is effectively his to lose. The Triple Crown was always a bonus possibility, an asterisk on an already historic season. Its absence does not diminish what he built over 156 games.

What it does is serve as a reminder of how difficult the Triple Crown actually is. Even players having one of the great offensive seasons of their generation — and Alvarez qualifies — can fall short when three separate categories all need to align simultaneously across a full season of 162 games.

What's Next

Watch Alvarez's final six games with appreciation rather than frustration. He has a batting title to defend and a shot at making the numbers look a little better before the regular season ends.

The real story begins when the postseason opens. The Astros are in, and Alvarez in October is a different conversation entirely — one that does not require a Triple Crown to be compelling.

The window for history closed. The window for October just opened.
