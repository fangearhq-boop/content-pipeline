Cubs Take the Wild Card Lead — Magic Number Down to 1

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | September 21, 2026

Twenty-four hours changed the entire Wild Card picture.

The Cubs entered September 20 stuck in a three-way tie with the Phillies and Padres, every team at 85-69, every team with identical postseason odds, every team watching the others for a slip. By end of day, only one team had avoided a slip — and it was Chicago.

The Phillies lost. The Padres lost. The Cubs won. Simple math produced a dramatic result: the Cubs now own a one-game lead atop the National League Wild Card standings, with a magic number of 1 to clinch their postseason berth. One win. One Padres or Phillies loss. That's it.

How the Standings Stack Up

As of September 21, the NL Wild Card race looks like this: Cubs 87-69, Padres 86-69, Phillies 86-70. Chicago holds the tiebreaker advantage over both clubs, meaning even a three-way tie at the end of the regular season goes in their favor.

The Wild Card round begins September 29. The Cubs have six games remaining to finish the job — a job that felt far less certain even 48 hours ago.

What changed? The Cubs stopped treating the Wild Card like an opponent and started playing like they owned it. The results followed.

From Survival Mode to Driver's Seat

September has been a grind for the NL Wild Card field. At various points, five or six teams had a legitimate case for the three available spots. The Cubs spent stretches of the month looking up rather than down — struggling through a stretch where every loss felt potentially fatal.

The turnaround to this moment — a 1-game lead, a favorable tiebreaker, and a magic number that could disappear with a single result — represents a genuine shift in how this team is playing and how the race has broken in their direction.

The Diamondbacks, who also lost on September 20, continue to lurk. But the pressure is now on San Diego and Philadelphia to chase rather than Chicago to hold on.

What's Next

The Cubs can clinch their postseason spot as early as Monday. If they win and either the Padres or Phillies lose, the math resolves and Chicago is in.

Then the focus shifts entirely to seeding and matchups within the Wild Card bracket — and to Pete Crow-Armstrong's 40-40 chase, which would give the Cubs something to celebrate beyond the clinch itself.

One win away. This is what the Cubs have been building toward all September.
