Aaron Judge Is Back on the IL — Yankees Hold Their Breath

By Ryan Calloway | Ballpark Banter | September 21, 2026

The New York Yankees clinched their 61st postseason berth. Their captain is back on the injured list. Neither fact fully cancels the other.

Aaron Judge was placed on the 10-day IL on September 17 — retroactive to that date — with a right soleus strain, a calf injury that came just nine days after he had returned from a 99-day IL stint for a rib fracture. He played seven games after that return. Then he went down again.

With six games left in the regular season, the Yankees find themselves in familiar October territory — in the playoffs, but uncertain about their best player.

A Season of Interruptions

Judge has totaled 18 home runs in 2026, a figure that reflects how many games he missed rather than what he is capable of. He returned from the rib injury on September 8, immediately reminded everyone what elite looks like, and then had the calf give out beneath him.

An MRI taken September 17 in Phoenix confirmed the soleus strain. The 10-day IL designation, with its retroactive start date, leaves a theoretical window for him to be activated before the postseason begins — but only barely, depending on how quickly the injury responds to treatment.

Manager Aaron Boone, when pressed about the postseason, gave reporters exactly the level of certainty they probably expected: "I don't know." Judge himself was more direct, saying the plan is to be active in October.

Can the Yankees Win Without Him?

The Yankees have enough to be a legitimate postseason threat regardless of whether Judge suits up in the Wild Card round. Their pitching has been strong, and the supporting cast in the lineup is capable. But Judge is not a piece of the puzzle — he is the frame.

His presence changes pitching decisions, lineup construction, and the psychological weight of any at-bat in a close game. His absence, particularly in October when margins shrink to their minimum, matters.

The organization is treating this like a wait-and-see situation. Six games of rest with medical treatment is not nothing. Players have returned from similar injuries on shorter timelines. The question is whether Judge can not just return, but return at a level where he helps rather than risks further damage.

What's Next

The Yankees will monitor Judge's progress through the final week of the regular season. If he is cleared to return before the regular season ends, that would be a significant development worth watching. More likely, the team manages him conservatively and makes the activation call when the postseason begins.

Watch for updates from Boone's pregame press conferences this week. Any change in the prognosis — better or worse — will come from there first.

The Bronx is holding its breath. October is coming.
