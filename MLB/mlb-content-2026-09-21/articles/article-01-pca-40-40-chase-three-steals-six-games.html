Pete Crow-Armstrong Is Three Steals Away From Baseball History

By Ryan Calloway | Ballpark Banter | September 21, 2026

Three stolen bases. Six games. Those are the only numbers that matter for Pete Crow-Armstrong right now.

PCA enters the final week of the 2026 regular season with 47 home runs and 37 stolen bases, standing on the edge of one of the most exclusive clubs in baseball history. A 40-40 season — 40 home runs and 40 stolen bases — has been accomplished just six times since the modern era began. Only Jose Canseco, Barry Bonds, Alex Rodriguez, Alfonso Soriano, Ronald Acuña Jr., and Shohei Ohtani have pulled it off. Crow-Armstrong needs three steals in six games to become No. 7.

He would also become the first player in Cubs franchise history to achieve the feat.

A Season Already in the Record Books

Even before the final week's drama, Crow-Armstrong had already rewritten Cubs history. Earlier this season he surpassed Billy Williams' left-handed home run record of 42, a mark that had stood since 1970. The record fell quietly in the middle of a season that has been anything but quiet.

The numbers tell the story: .282 batting average, .375 on-base percentage, .956 OPS, 105 RBI. He has been the engine of a Cubs offense that climbed from a three-way Wild Card logjam to outright pole position in a single afternoon. Chicago entered September 21 with an 87-69 record and a magic number of 1 to clinch their postseason spot.

That context matters. Crow-Armstrong isn't chasing history in a lost season. He's doing it with everything on the line.

The Math and What It Takes

Three stolen bases in six games is not an outrageous ask on paper — Crow-Armstrong has been one of the most aggressive and successful baserunners in the National League all season. But the final week of a pennant race brings its own complications: reduced running opportunities, pitchers holding runners close, managers protecting leads late.

The last member of the club to join was Shohei Ohtani in 2024. Before him, Acuña reached the mark in 2023. The 40-40 had gone unachieved for 17 years between Soriano in 2006 and Acuña's breakthrough. Now it has happened back-to-back — and potentially three times in four seasons.

The threshold has not changed, but the context around it has. What was once the rarest individual achievement in baseball has shown it remains extraordinarily rare, just not impossible. PCA is close enough to touch it.

What's Next

The Cubs close the regular season with six games remaining, and every one of them carries double meaning: postseason clinch implications and the 40-40 countdown. If Chicago gets to one win or one rival loss first, the clinching celebration will arrive — and somewhere in the middle of it, Crow-Armstrong will still need three steals.

Follow his stolen base total through the week. If he's at 39 heading into the final weekend, the pressure will be something this franchise hasn't felt since the drought-breaking years. If he reaches 40 before the calendar runs out, he joins the rarest company in the sport.

Three steals. Six games. The clock is running.
