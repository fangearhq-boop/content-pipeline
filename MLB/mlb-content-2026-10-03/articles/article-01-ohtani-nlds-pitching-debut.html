Shohei Ohtani Makes His First Career Postseason Pitching Start in NLDS Game 1

By Marcus Cole | October 3, 2026

The Moment Baseball Has Been Waiting For

When Shohei Ohtani walks to the mound at Dodger Stadium today for the first pitch of NLDS Game 1, something that has never happened before in baseball will become a reality. At 4 PM ET on Fox, he will make his first career postseason start as a pitcher -- the most anticipated individual moment in the 2026 October baseball calendar.

The circumstances that made this moment so long in coming are well known. Ohtani underwent Tommy John surgery in September 2023 after his Angels tenure ended, costing him all of 2024 as a pitcher while he completed his recovery. He signed with the Dodgers before the 2024 season and spent that year contributing exclusively as a hitter. In 2025, his first full season in Los Angeles, he returned to pitching in the regular season but the Dodgers navigated their postseason without leaning on him on the mound.

In 2026, the two-way player is back in full. The version of Ohtani the baseball world signed up for -- the guy who bats third and then jogs to the bullpen mound -- arrived this season and never looked better. And today he takes that version into October.

What He's Done in 2026

Ohtani's 2026 season as a fully restored two-way player has validated every dollar of the contract and every patient day of recovery. He posted a 2.14 ERA with 23 wins as a starter while continuing to hit at an elite level alongside Freddie Freeman in the Dodgers' lineup. No player in modern baseball history has done what Ohtani has done this season -- and that is the player starting NLDS Game 1 for Los Angeles.

The matchup he draws is a worthy one. Atlanta is sending Chris Sale to the mound -- the 36-year-old left-hander who struck out nine Philadelphia batters in 6.1 innings in Wild Card Game 1 and has been arguably the best pitcher in the National League not named Ohtani this season. A pair of aces. A sold-out Dodger Stadium. The first game of the most anticipated series of the first round.

What the Braves Are Walking Into

The Atlanta Braves won their NL Wild Card Series over the Phillies the hard way. They won Game 1 on an eighth-inning Austin Riley three-run home run, dropped Game 2 in ten innings, then dismantled the Phillies in Game 3 behind Michael Harris II, Ozzie Albies, and Matt Olson. They snapped a six-game losing streak in winner-take-all games in doing so.

That kind of resilience matters. But they have never faced a team with both the pitching ace and lineup that the Dodgers have assembled. The Dodgers have the luxury of Ohtani batting in addition to pitching, and Freeman behind him. If Ohtani is throwing like he has all season -- working quickly, generating swings and misses with his splitter -- Dodger Stadium will be a hostile environment in every sense from the first inning forward.

The Braves' best hope is Sale matching Ohtani, keeping it close, and making noise with their lineup late. They have the pieces to do it. Whether they can execute against the best pitcher in baseball on his personal debut October stage is the defining question of Game 1.

The Weight of the Moment

There are starts that matter because of the standings. And then there are starts that matter because of what they mean -- for a player, for a franchise, for the game itself. This is the second kind.

Ohtani spent three years building toward today. He recovered from surgery. He came back and proved he could still hit. Then he proved he could still pitch. And now he takes the hill in the only context in baseball that matters in October.

When the first pitch crosses the plate at 4 PM ET today, every fan of baseball -- regardless of team -- is going to feel the weight of that moment.
