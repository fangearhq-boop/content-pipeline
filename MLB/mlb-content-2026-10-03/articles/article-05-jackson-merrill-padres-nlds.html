Jackson Merrill's Biggest Moment: Padres Open NLDS Against the Brewers Tonight

By Jake Torres | October 3, 2026

A New Face for the Padres in October

The San Diego Padres have been to the postseason with big stars and big expectations before. This year the identity of their run is younger and less scripted -- and at the center of it is Jackson Merrill.

Merrill emerged in 2026 as one of the National League's best young players and a legitimate Rookie of the Year candidate. His combination of bat, instincts, and energy has energized a San Diego roster that also features Manny Machado and Fernando Tatis Jr. -- established stars who now have a new kind of impact presence alongside them. The Padres swept the Chicago Cubs 2-0 in the NL Wild Card, including a Game 2 win anchored by Gavin Sheets' clutch pinch-hit two-run home run, and advanced to their second NLDS appearance in the last four years.

Tonight at 8:30 PM ET at American Family Field in Milwaukee, Merrill takes the field for the biggest game of his career.

The Opponent Is the Best Team in Baseball

The Milwaukee Brewers went 103-59 in 2026 -- the best record in all of baseball, not just the National League. They earned the No. 1 NL seed, bypassed the Wild Card round entirely, and had a full week of rest while the Padres ground through two games in Chicago.

Milwaukee's rotation posted a 3.61 ERA from their starters all season, one of the best marks in the league. Their lineup features Jackson Chourio, another NL Rookie of the Year candidate, who gives the Brewers a comparable young star to match San Diego's Merrill. The two are measuring their seasons against each other all year -- tonight they are on opposite sides of an NLDS.

The concern for the Padres is a familiarity gap. The Brewers were the best team in baseball for six months. They are well-rested, playing at home, and deeply motivated after a dominant regular season. The layoff can cut both ways -- rust is a real risk for any team -- but Milwaukee has the talent to shake it off quickly.

Why San Diego Can Win

The Padres beat the Brewers 4-2 in their regular-season series. They know how to beat this team. They have Machado's postseason experience, Tatis Jr.'s electric upside, and Mason Miller as one of the most dominant closers in the game.

Miller's save numbers this season represent one of the most effective individual closer performances in the NL. If the Padres can get to the eighth inning with a lead, they have the weapon to close it. The challenge is getting there against a starting rotation that has been elite all season.

Merrill gives San Diego something that matters in a short series: unpredictability. He does not have a deep postseason profile for opponents to study. He is a player at the beginning of his career making his first NLDS appearance, and that freshness can be an asset. The biggest moments in baseball sometimes belong to the player who has not yet been taught to be afraid of them.

What Merrill Represents

Jackson Merrill is not just a lineup piece. He is a statement about where the Padres are going. San Diego has spent years building around marquee free agents and trades. Merrill represents something different: organic growth, a homegrown talent who arrived and immediately performed.

If the Padres are going to make a deep run in 2026, it will almost certainly have Merrill's fingerprints on it. He is the player the fanbase rallied behind this season, and tonight at 8:30 PM ET in Milwaukee, he gets his first chance to prove what he can do when it matters most.

What's Next
