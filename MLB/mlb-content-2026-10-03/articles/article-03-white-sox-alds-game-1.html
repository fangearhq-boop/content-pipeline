From 121 Losses to the ALDS: White Sox Open at Cleveland Today

By Ryan Calloway | October 3, 2026

How Did This Happen?

In 2024, the Chicago White Sox lost 121 games. It was the worst record by any team since 1900. The franchise was historically bad, its fan base demoralized, its stadium quiet on summer nights when every game felt futile. The end of that season was not a conclusion -- it was a rock bottom.

Today -- 23 months later -- the White Sox play ALDS Game 1 at Progressive Field in Cleveland at 1 PM ET on TBS. This is not the product of luck or a soft schedule or one good month. It is the result of deliberate organizational choices, a manager who believed in young players, and a roster that learned to win together.

It is the most remarkable two-year turnaround in modern baseball history.

The Players Who Built It

General manager Chris Getz and manager Will Venable identified a path forward through international talent and patient player development. The two most visible results of that approach are now center stage in the American League playoffs.

Munetaka Murakami made his MLB debut on March 26, 2026. By mid-May he was named AL Rookie of the Month. By July he was in the All-Star Game. By October he is playing in the ALDS with a .244 batting average, 24 home runs, and a .929 OPS in his first major league season. He is one of the most successful international position player debuts in baseball history, and he is doing it for a team that people said could not compete.

Colson Montgomery arrived as a 20-homer third baseman who hit a 432-foot shot off the Houston Astros in the Wild Card -- the kind of home run that makes an entire team believe they belong. Miguel Vargas provides steady production alongside them. The lineup is not overwhelming, but it is deep enough and young enough to surprise.

The Wild Card Win That Changed Everything

The White Sox did not just sneak into the ALDS. They swept the Houston Astros -- who won the AL West despite an 81-81 record -- in two Wild Card games at Daikin Park in Houston. Games won 6-3 and 7-3. Their first playoff series win since the 2005 World Series.

That series did something for the White Sox beyond the standings. It proved to them that they could perform under pressure against a playoff opponent with a real crowd against them. The Guardians will be a more difficult challenge -- Cleveland went 24-11 from August 20 through the end of the regular season and enters as the No. 2 AL seed. Parker Messick, an AL Cy Young candidate, is expected to start Game 1 for Cleveland.

But the White Sox beat their regular-season series against the Guardians 7-6 in head-to-head meetings. They are not overmatched. They are exactly where the building process was designed to put them.

The First ALDS Since 2008

The last time the White Sox played in an ALDS was in 2008, when they lost to the Tampa Bay Rays in four games. The franchise that won the World Series in 2005 spent nearly two decades trying to get back to this stage.

The version that gets here in 2026 is unrecognizable from the one that walked away in shame in 2024. It is younger, faster, and more interesting than anything Chicago has put on the field in years. Murakami is appointment television. Montgomery is a star being born in real time. Venable has this team believing -- and playing like -- they deserve to be here.

Whether they advance past Cleveland or not, this is baseball that matters. After 2024, that alone feels like a miracle.
