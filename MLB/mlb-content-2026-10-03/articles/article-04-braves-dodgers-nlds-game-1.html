Sale vs. Ohtani: The Braves Survived Philly and Now Face the Dodgers in NLDS Game 1

By Marcus Cole | October 3, 2026

The Hard Way

The Atlanta Braves won their NL Wild Card Series against the Philadelphia Phillies by winning the last game. That sounds unremarkable -- all series come down to a decisive moment eventually. But after losing Game 2 in ten innings and entering Game 3 carrying a six-game losing streak in winner-take-all situations, it felt like exactly the kind of outcome Atlanta was destined to avoid.

Instead, the Braves came home to Truist Park on October 1 and ended the Phillies' October. Michael Harris II crushed a three-run home run off Aaron Nola in the first inning. Ozzie Albies followed with a two-run blast off Zack Wheeler in the fourth. Matt Olson added a solo shot in the eighth. Atlanta won 6-2, advanced 2-1 in the series, and snapped the streak. They are going to Los Angeles.

The Pitcher Who Got Them Here

Chris Sale is 36 years old and still one of the best pitchers in the National League. In Wild Card Game 1 against the Phillies, he struck out nine hitters in 6.1 innings to set the tone for the entire series. His combination of velocity, deception, and command has not diminished -- if anything, his October focus gives him a level of sharpness that makes him uniquely valuable when the margin for error is smallest.

Today Sale gets the ball for NLDS Game 1 against the Dodgers at 4 PM ET. His opponent is Shohei Ohtani, making the first start of his postseason pitching career. Two elite left-handed starters. One game that the entire National League has been building toward.

Ohtani was electric all season -- a 2.14 ERA and 23 wins as a fully restored two-way player. Sale's ERA across 29 starts in 2026 ranks among the NL's top five. Both pitchers will have to execute at a high level on the biggest stage of the year.

Atlanta's Lineup in Los Angeles

The Braves' offensive production in the Wild Card was not generated by one or two names -- it was distributed. Austin Riley's go-ahead homer in Game 1 off Duran. Harris II's first-inning statement in Game 3. Albies' run-scoring support. Olson's insurance shot. That collective offensive approach is what gives Atlanta a chance to compete in Dodger Stadium even with Ohtani pitching.

Freddie Freeman anchors the Dodgers' lineup alongside Ohtani in the batting order. If Sale can neutralize Freeman and force the Dodgers to score through their bench contributors, Atlanta's game plan remains viable. The Braves are a patient lineup that has experience against premium pitching. They need Sale to give them five to seven innings, their bullpen to hold the lead or a tie, and their offense to generate enough to match or exceed what the Dodgers score.

That is a lot of things that need to go right. But the Braves have spent the last two weeks proving they can get the right things to happen when it matters most.

Can Anyone Beat the Dodgers?

The honest question hanging over the entire National League postseason is whether any team is capable of beating Los Angeles in a five-game series. The Dodgers have Ohtani as a pitcher and hitter, Freeman as an anchor, and a rotation that does not have obvious weak spots. They earned the No. 2 NL seed behind the Brewers' historically dominant regular season, and they played their best baseball from August through the end of September.

Atlanta is the first team that gets to find out the answer. They will need Chris Sale to be great. They will need their lineup to find opportunities against Ohtani. They will need their bullpen to be clean.

They have survived before when it seemed unlikely. Game 3 vs. the Phillies is the evidence. Whether that resilience translates to beating the Dodgers over five games in October is the most interesting storyline in the National League.
