Caminero's 112.7 MPH Double: The Hardest Hit Ball in Game 1 Went Nowhere

By Marcus Cole | October 24, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Junior Caminero hit the ball harder than anyone in Game 1 of the World Series. His double off Jacob Misiorowski registered 112.7 mph exit velocity — the hardest-struck ball of the entire game. He was stranded. The Brewers won.

That sequence tells you more about how dominant Misiorowski was on Friday night than any box score can.

Caminero's Season and Background

Caminero is 22 years old and already one of the most fearsome power hitters in the American League. In 2025, he hit 45 home runs and drove in 110 runs, earning his second consecutive All-Star selection. He is a player the baseball world had been watching since his debut with Tampa Bay, and the Rays built their offensive identity around his bat.

This season was difficult. He appeared in only 51 games due to injury, posting a .265/.356/.490 slash line with 13 home runs and 27 RBI. The abbreviated campaign left questions about his health and rhythm heading into the postseason. He answered some of those questions in the ALCS when he hit a foul-pole two-run home run in the series clincher against the Cleveland Guardians.

In Game 1 of the World Series, he was back in the middle of Tampa Bay's lineup with eyes on him from the first inning.

The Double That Didn't Hurt

Caminero's 112.7 mph double came off Misiorowski and dropped into the gap — a legitimate piece of hard contact against one of the best pitchers in baseball. By the measure of how hard the ball was hit, it was the most dangerous swing Tampa Bay produced all game. The exit velocity put it above the threshold of what players typically drive for extra bases.

And yet the runner was stranded. Misiorowski worked around it. The inning ended without additional damage.

That outcome — dominant contact, no result — defined Tampa Bay's night. The Rays' bottom of the lineup, spots 5 through 9, went a combined 1-for-17 with six strikeouts. The top of the order had moments but could not convert when it mattered. Only Caminero made contact that truly tested Misiorowski.

What It Means for the Series

The Rays cannot afford for only Caminero to be dangerous. If Tampa Bay is going to even the series in Game 2 and find a path to winning this World Series, they need more of their lineup to put together competitive at-bats against Milwaukee's pitching.

The good news for the Rays is that Drew Rasmussen starts tonight with a 1.29 ERA in the postseason, giving Tampa Bay a real chance to hold the Brewers down while the offense recalibrates. Game 2 is tonight at 8 PM ET on FOX.

Caminero's 112.7 mph double is a reminder that the Rays' best threat is locked in. Whether the rest of the lineup joins him in Game 2 may determine whether this series goes back to Tampa Bay tied or with Milwaukee firmly in control.
