Uribe, Ashby, Megill: Milwaukee's Bullpen Bent but Held in Game 1

By Jake Torres | October 24, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The Milwaukee Brewers' bullpen had one of the most dominant postseason stretches in recent memory during the National League Championship Series — a 0.38 ERA across 23.2 innings in a four-game sweep of the Los Angeles Dodgers. On Friday night in Game 1 of the World Series, they mostly kept that going. Then came the ninth inning.

Abner Uribe, Aaron Ashby, and Trevor Megill combined for six strikeouts over four innings of work, protecting a 3-1 lead through the middle frames of a taut, tense Game 1. It was the continuation of an October story that has defined Milwaukee's run to the World Series. Then Megill walked into the fire in the ninth and came out the other side with a win.

How the Pen Took Over

Jacob Misiorowski pitched five innings — making World Series history with a 103.5 mph first pitch and earning the first win by a Milwaukee starter this October — before handing things off to the Brewers' deep relief corps. The handoff was smooth for the first four innings of bullpen work.

Uribe, who has been one of the Brewers' most reliable late-inning options throughout the postseason, began the bridge. Ashby, who threw 12.1 shutout innings during the NLCS, continued it. The tandem combined for the bulk of those six strikeouts and kept Tampa Bay from threading any rallies together until the very end.

Through eight innings, the Brewers led 3-1. The game appeared under control.

The Ninth Inning

Megill came on to close it out and discovered that October games rarely end quietly.

He allowed two hits and a walk, loading the situation for Tampa Bay's bottom of the order — a group that had gone 1-for-17 with six strikeouts through eight innings. Then a replay review changed everything.

With two outs, a replay challenge by the Rays' dugout overturned a potential double-play call at second base. Officials determined that Brewers shortstop Pratt had lifted his foot off the bag too early during the transfer, nullifying what would have been the final out of the game. Under the corrected ruling, a Tampa Bay run scored, cutting the lead to 3-2, and the tying run stood on base.

American Family Field went quiet. The series lead suddenly felt less secure.

Megill got the next batter out. Brewers win, 3-2. Series lead secured.

The Bigger Picture

Milwaukee's bullpen success in October is not an accident. Manager Craig Counsell — who rebuilt the Brewers into a 103-win juggernaut over multiple years — has deployed his relief corps in a way that maximizes their collective depth rather than leaning on a single dominant closer. The NLCS was the ultimate expression of that philosophy: four games, four wins, 0.38 ERA.

The ninth inning against the Rays was the first real vulnerability the pen has shown all October. They allowed it because of a defensive miscue and a replay call, not because Tampa Bay was overwhelming them. That distinction matters. The process was sound. The result was what mattered.

The Brewers lead the World Series 1-0 and are at home tonight for Game 2.

What's Next

Game 2 of the World Series is tonight at American Family Field. First pitch: 8 PM ET on FOX. Milwaukee rookie Logan Henderson starts opposite Drew Rasmussen of the Rays. The Brewers bullpen will again be available to close things out if Henderson gives them length. The series continues in Tampa Bay for Games 3-5 starting Oct. 26.
