Misiorowski Throws 103.5 MPH in Game 1: A New World Series Record

By Ryan Calloway | October 24, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The record had stood for a decade. Aroldis Chapman set it in Game 2 of the 2016 World Series, throwing a 103.2 mph fastball for the Chicago Cubs on the sport's biggest stage. No pitcher in nine subsequent World Series had come close to matching it.

Then Jacob Misiorowski stepped on the mound Friday night at American Family Field, looked in for the sign, and threw his very first pitch of the 2026 Fall Classic at 103.5 mph.

Record broken. History made. The first pitch of the game.

The Context Behind the Velocity

Misiorowski is not a reliever throwing one inning of maximum effort. He is a starter — the best starter in the National League in 2026, leading the league in both ERA and strikeouts. He had come into Game 1 off 12 days of rest following the NLCS sweep of the Dodgers, where he averaged between four and four-and-a-half innings per start while the Brewers' bullpen handled the back end.

His first pitch in the World Series arrived at the velocity of a closer maxing out in a tied game in October. At 103.5 mph, it was the hardest first pitch any starting pitcher has thrown in a Fall Classic.

Chapman's record in 2016 was notable because it came from a reliever in a dominant closer role — not a starter settling in during the first frame. Misiorowski delivered that velocity as the opening salvo of a planned five-inning outing, which made it even more remarkable.

What Followed the Record

Misiorowski did not let the moment get bigger than his game plan. He proceeded to throw five innings of one-run baseball, allowing four hits, issuing one walk, and striking out three. His final pitch count was 73. He was efficient, he was commanding, and the Brewers won 3-2.

The one run he allowed came in the fourth inning on a groundout by Rays catcher Liam Hicks — a balls-in-play result rather than anything Misiorowski was particularly hittable on. Tampa Bay made hard contact only once: Junior Caminero's double, which Statcast measured at 112.7 mph exit velocity. The rest of the Rays' lineup managed little against him.

It was also the first postseason win of Misiorowski's career, and the first by a Milwaukee starter this October. The Brewers had relied on their historically dominant bullpen through the entire NLCS, meaning Misiorowski had not yet taken a win in the 2026 playoffs. He fixed that Friday night.

Speed in Context

The 0.3 mph difference between Misiorowski's opener and Chapman's 2016 record may seem small. In baseball, it is significant. Every tenth of a mile per hour at the top of the velocity scale represents a meaningful shift in the physics of what a hitter can do with the pitch. Chapman's fastball was already borderline impossible to time. Misiorowski's opening delivery was harder.

Whether it becomes a footnote or a defining moment depends on how the rest of this World Series unfolds. But on Friday night, the youngest Milwaukee starter to win a postseason game did it while rewriting the record books before the first inning was over.

What's Next

Misiorowski's next potential start would be Game 4 of the World Series on Oct. 27 at Tropicana Field in Tampa Bay. Between now and then, the Brewers send rookie Logan Henderson in Game 2 tonight (8 PM ET, FOX), while the Rays counter with their own October ace, Drew Rasmussen. Milwaukee leads the series 1-0.
