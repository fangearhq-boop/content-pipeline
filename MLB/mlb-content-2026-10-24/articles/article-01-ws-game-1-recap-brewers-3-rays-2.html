Brewers 3, Rays 2: Milwaukee Takes Game 1 of the World Series

By Marcus Cole | October 24, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Jacob Misiorowski made World Series history on the very first pitch of his very first Fall Classic start — and the Milwaukee Brewers made the most of it, beating the Tampa Bay Rays 3-2 on Friday night at American Family Field to take a 1-0 lead in the 2026 World Series.

Misiorowski, the 24-year-old who led the National League in both ERA and strikeouts this season, threw a 103.5 mph fastball on his opening delivery — the fastest first pitch ever recorded in a World Series, breaking the record of 103.2 mph set by Aroldis Chapman when he was pitching for the Chicago Cubs in Game 2 of the 2016 Fall Classic.

That moment set the tone for everything that followed.

How Milwaukee Scored

The Brewers struck quickly. They pushed across two runs in the first inning against Tampa Bay starter Nick Martinez, building an early cushion that would prove decisive. Milwaukee added an insurance run in the seventh inning to extend the lead to 3-1.

Jackson Chourio, the Brewers' 22-year-old outfielder who set a franchise postseason home run record during the NLCS, went 2-for-4 with a run scored and an RBI, continuing his breakthrough October.

Martinez pitched five innings for Tampa Bay, allowing five hits and two earned runs without issuing a walk. He struck out five. It was a solid outing that simply wasn't enough against a Milwaukee lineup that found early gaps and applied pressure from the opening inning.

Misiorowski Delivers Five Dominant Innings

The starting pitching matchup — Misiorowski versus Martinez — delivered. Misiorowski's final line: five innings, four hits, one run, one walk, three strikeouts, 73 pitches. The one run he allowed came in the fourth inning when Rays catcher Liam Hicks grounded into a run-scoring play to cut the deficit to 2-1.

That was as close as Tampa Bay would get while Misiorowski was on the mound. He was efficient, commanding, and backed by a defense that gave him nothing to worry about through the middle innings. It was the first postseason win of his career and the first by a Milwaukee starter in this October's run — the Brewers had leaned heavily on their bullpen throughout the NLCS sweep of the Dodgers.

The Bullpen, a Disputed Replay, and a Tense Ninth

After Misiorowski's exit, the Milwaukee bullpen took over and mostly kept things clean. Abner Uribe, Aaron Ashby, and Trevor Megill combined for six strikeouts over four innings, extending what had been one of the most dominant postseason bullpen runs in recent memory — the Brewers' relief corps posted a 0.38 ERA over 23.2 innings in the NLCS.

Megill closed out the ninth, but the final frame got complicated. With the Brewers leading 3-1 and two outs on the board, a replay review overturned a double-play call at second base. Officials determined that Milwaukee shortstop Pratt had lifted his foot off the bag too early on the transfer. The ruling allowed a Tampa Bay run to score, cutting the lead to 3-2 and putting the tying run on base.

Megill got the next batter out to end it. Brewers win, 3-2.

Tampa Bay's Bottom Order Struggled

The Rays' biggest offensive issue in Game 1 was production from the bottom of their lineup. Spots 5 through 9 combined to go just 1-for-17 with six strikeouts. Junior Caminero, the 22-year-old who has been Tampa Bay's most dangerous hitter when healthy, made the hardest contact of the night — a double hit at 112.7 mph exit velocity — but was stranded and couldn't turn it into runs.

Drew Rasmussen starts for Tampa Bay in Game 2 with the Rays needing a win to avoid going back to Florida in a 0-2 hole.

What's Next

World Series Game 2 is tonight at American Family Field in Milwaukee. First pitch is scheduled for 8 PM ET on FOX. Drew Rasmussen, who owns a 1.29 ERA in this postseason, starts for Tampa Bay. Logan Henderson starts for Milwaukee.

The series continues with Games 3 through 5 at Tropicana Field in Tampa Bay (Oct. 26-28), then returns to Milwaukee for Games 6 and 7 (Oct. 30-31) if necessary. Neither franchise has ever won a World Series. One of them will end that drought in October 2026.
