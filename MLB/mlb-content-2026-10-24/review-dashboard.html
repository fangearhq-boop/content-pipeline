Brewers 3, Rays 2 — Milwaukee Takes Game 1
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post **Window:** 8:00 AM ET Milwaukee drew first blood. The Brewers beat the Tampa Bay Rays 3-2 in World Series Game 1 on Friday night at American Family Field, taking a 1-0 series lead in the Fall Classic. It was a tense, dramatic game that ended with a disputed replay in the 9th inning — but Milwaukee held on. Jacob Misiorowski was sensational. The young right-hander threw five innings of one-run ball, striking out three and limiting the Rays to 4 hits. He started the night by throwing a 103.5 mph fastball — the fastest first pitch in World Series history, breaking Aroldis Chapman's record from the 2016 Fall Classic. Milwaukee scored twice in the first inning and added an insurance run in the 7th to take a 3-1 lead. The Rays got on the board in the 4th when catcher Liam Hicks grounded into a run-scoring play, then nearly pulled within one in the 9th when a replay review overturned a double play call at second base — SS Pratt's foot came off the bag — allowing a run to score and making it 3-2. But closer Trevor Megill got the final out and the Brewers survived. Jackson Chourio went 2-for-4, Nick Martinez took the loss for Tampa Bay after five solid innings, and the Rays' bottom of the order went just 1-for-17 with six strikeouts. Game 2 is tonight. Drew Rasmussen starts for Tampa Bay. First pitch: 8 PM ET on FOX. **Caption (Image Post)** Brewers 3, Rays 2. Milwaukee takes Game 1. Misiorowski threw 5 innings and made World Series history with a 103.5 mph first pitch. The Fall Classic is underway. #WorldSeries #Brewers ---

### Long-Form Post **Window:** 10:30 AM ET 103.5 mph. The first pitch of Game 1 of the World Series. Jacob Misiorowski didn't just start Friday night — he made history before the first hitter even swung. His opening pitch registered 103.5 mph on Statcast, breaking the World Series pitch speed record of 103.2 mph set by Aroldis Chapman during the 2016 Fall Classic when he was pitching for the Chicago Cubs. That record had stood for a decade. It fell on the first pitch of the very first World Series game these two franchises have ever played in. Misiorowski backed up the velocity with five dominant innings — allowing just one run on four hits, walking one, and striking out three. His final line: 5 IP, 4 H, 1 R, 1 BB, 3 K, 73 pitches. He became the first Milwaukee starter to earn a postseason win this October, as the Brewers had relied heavily on their bullpen throughout the NLCS sweep of the Dodgers. The young right-hander now has one of the most memorable debuts in World Series pitching history — and the Brewers have a 1-0 series lead. **Caption (Image Post)** 103.5 mph. Jacob Misiorowski's first pitch of Game 1 broke Aroldis Chapman's World Series pitch speed record. The Brewers are winning. #WorldSeries #Brewers ---

### Long-Form Post **Window:** 12:30 PM ET The Brewers' bullpen had one of the most dominant postseason stretches in recent memory during the NLCS — a 0.38 ERA over 23.2 innings in the four-game sweep of the Dodgers. On Friday night in Game 1, they mostly kept that going. After Jacob Misiorowski exited following five innings, Abner Uribe, Aaron Ashby, and Trevor Megill combined for six strikeouts over four innings of work. They held a 3-1 lead deep into the game. Then the 9th inning happened. Megill came on to close things out and walked into some trouble — two hits, a walk, and a disputed replay changed the score. With two outs, a replay review overturned a double-play call at second base when it was determined that Brewers shortstop Pratt had lifted his foot off the bag too early. The run scored, cutting the lead to 3-2. But Megill got the next out. Milwaukee survived. The bullpen's October dominance continues — battered slightly, but undefeated in the World Series. **Caption (Image Post)** Uribe. Ashby. Megill. The Brewers bullpen bent but didn't break in the 9th.
