Olson Reaches 897 Consecutive Games; Acuña Hits Two as Braves Make It Eight Straight

By Marcus Cole

August 7, 2026

Matt Olson launched a 422-foot solo home run off John King in the sixth inning on Thursday, his 32nd of the season, that kicked off a four-run frame and helped turn a manageable Braves lead into a comfortable margin. He also doubled and walked twice. More than the numbers from one game, the performance added another entry to the streak: Olson's consecutive games total now stands at 897.

Atlanta beat Miami 11-3 to complete a three-game sweep of the Marlins and extend their winning streak to eight — the longest of their 2026 season. Ronald Acuña Jr. contributed home runs No. 10 and No. 11, including one that found its way over the wall after deflecting off a Marlins outfielder's glove on a defensive collision in the outfield.

897: What the Number Means

Matt Olson has been in the Atlanta Braves lineup every single game since May 2, 2021. On that date, he was still a member of the Oakland Athletics. He has since been traded, signed a nine-year contract extension with Atlanta, and become one of the most consistent power hitters in the National League — all without missing a game.

The streak passed Stan Musial's 895-game mark on August 5, moving Olson into eighth place on the all-time consecutive games list in MLB history. Thursday's game pushed the streak to 897. The milestone is the kind that accumulates quietly — each game simply adding one more day to an uninterrupted run that is increasingly rare in modern baseball, where load management and minor injuries regularly keep players out of lineups.

Olson's production during the streak has not been ceremonial. He hit his 32nd home run Thursday. He has been a key run-producer in an Atlanta lineup that can generate offense at nearly any spot. The streak is notable not just for the number, but for the consistency of the player sustaining it.

Acuña and the Deflection

Ronald Acuña Jr.'s first home run Thursday was straightforward — a ball he hit well enough to clear the fence. His second was more unusual. The ball left his bat and headed toward the outfield, where a Marlins outfielder converged on it and made contact, but the collision with a teammate knocked the ball loose and sent it over the wall. The umpires awarded a home run. Acuña's total moved to 11 on the season.

The result was part of an 11-run night for Atlanta, one that left no doubt about the outcome and completed the Marlins sweep by a margin that reflected the run differential between two teams heading in very different directions.

Eight in a Row for the Braves

Atlanta's eight-game winning streak is the longest of their 2026 season. In a division and league where consistency matters for playoff seeding, the Braves have found a stretch of baseball that has put them in a strong position heading into mid-August. The series win over Miami gave them additional ground in the standings before Friday's action begins.

What's Next

Matt Olson will look to extend his consecutive games streak to 898 in Atlanta's next game. He remains in eighth place all-time and will continue moving up the list with every game he appears in.
