The Dodgers Have the Most Star Power in Baseball. They've Also Lost Six in a Row.

By Jake Torres

August 7, 2026

On July 28, the Los Angeles Dodgers made one of the boldest moves at the trade deadline in recent memory. They acquired Tarik Skubal — the reigning back-to-back American League Cy Young Award winner — from the Detroit Tigers, in exchange for outfielder Zyhir Hope, right-hander River Ryan, and right-hander Brady Smith.

The move created something that has never existed in MLB history: a team with a reigning two-time Most Valuable Player and a reigning two-time Cy Young Award winner on the same roster at the same time. Shohei Ohtani had won consecutive unanimous MVPs. Skubal had won back-to-back Cy Youngs in the American League. Together, under one set of uniforms in Dodger blue, they are unprecedented.

Since the trade, the Dodgers have lost six consecutive games.

A Sweep, Then Another

The Dodgers were swept in three games by the Boston Red Sox. Then they were swept in three games by the Chicago Cubs. The six-game skid is the longest of their 2026 season. They enter Friday night's opener at Chase Field in Arizona having not won since before the Skubal deal was finalized.

The irony is not subtle. The Dodgers spent prospect capital to add a dominant pitcher to a rotation that was already strong. The move was designed to remove any remaining doubt about their World Series credentials. Instead, the offense that drove their record to 69-45 before the losing streak began has stalled. They left eight runners on base in the Cubs series finale. At the trade deadline and in the weeks that follow, outcomes rarely move in neat narratives — a roster addition does not guarantee immediate results, and the Dodgers are in the middle of a demonstration of that reality.

Tonight in Arizona

Roki Sasaki starts for Los Angeles on Friday night against Merrill Kelly and the Diamondbacks. Arizona is eight games behind the Dodgers in the NL West standings, meaning the Dodgers' lead in the division has not been threatened by the losing streak — yet. A win Friday stops the bleeding at six. A loss extends a skid that is already drawing comparisons, in one headline, to the longest such stretch for the franchise since 2017.

Ohtani was in the middle of a nine-game hitting streak during parts of this skid and hit his 29th home run as a Dodger — a leadoff shot that passed Davey Lopes for second on the franchise's all-time leadoff home run list. Individual production has not gone away. The team outcomes have simply not followed.

What's Next

The Dodgers play Friday night at Chase Field in Tempe, Arizona. A win would end the six-game losing streak and refocus attention on the broader picture of their trade deadline additions. A loss would raise legitimate questions about what has gone wrong in a short stretch for the best team in the NL West. Follow tonight's result at ESPN.com or MLB.com.
