Durbin's Walkoff in the 13th Extends Boston's Win Streak to Eight

By Ryan Calloway

August 7, 2026

It took four hours and thirteen innings, but the Red Sox found a way to win again.

Caleb Durbin lined a single to center field off Erick Fedde in the bottom of the 13th inning on Thursday, scoring Nick Sogard from second to give Boston a 12-11 win over the Chicago White Sox. The victory extended the Red Sox winning streak to eight games — one more than the streak that Sonny Gray's August 5 shutout had pushed to seven.

The Win Streak in Context

Since July 3, the Boston Red Sox have gone 25-3. That number has been described as a franchise-best stretch over a comparable span, though the winning streak itself tells a sharper version of the same story: eight consecutive wins, with the latest coming in a comeback that required extra innings, a back-and-forth game that swung multiple times, and a late decision in the 13th to close it out.

The Red Sox are not just winning — they are winning in a variety of ways. Sonny Gray's August 5 shutout (six innings, three hits, two walks, eight strikeouts, win No. 14) is the dominant-performance version. Thursday's 13-inning walkoff is the grinding, never-quit version. Both count the same in the standings.

Gray's record stands at 14-2, the most wins among American League pitchers this season. His start on August 5 was the kind of outing that wins Cy Young votes in October. The Aug 6 game, which he did not start, showed that the team does not need his exact performance to find a win.

The Durbin Moment

Caleb Durbin's walkoff was the kind of role-player moment that distinguishes teams in long winning streaks. The Red Sox needed a hit in an extra-inning situation, with Nick Sogard representing the winning run, and Durbin delivered against Fedde. The lineup depth that allows Boston to produce in those situations — not just from their stars but from contributors throughout the order — is part of what has made this run possible.

The White Sox, meanwhile, remain in a competitive position in the American League Central despite the loss. They still hold a lead in the division over Cleveland and Minnesota, both of whom also lost on August 5. A one-game setback in extra innings to a team as hot as Boston is not a fatal outcome for a division leader.

What's Next

The Red Sox carry their eight-game winning streak and 25-3 record since July 3 into their next series. Full standings and schedule are available at MLB.com. Boston's next opponents will face one of the hottest teams in baseball at one of its most dangerous stretches of the season.
