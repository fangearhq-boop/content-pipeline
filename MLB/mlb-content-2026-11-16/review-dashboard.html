QO Deadline Tomorrow — Seven Players, $23 Million, One Day Left
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post The qualifying offer deadline arrives tomorrow at 4:00 PM ET — and the baseball world is watching. Here's the situation. Eight players received qualifying offers this offseason worth $23.15M each. Jake Bauers is already off the board — he signed a 4-year, $58 million deal with the Boston Red Sox and therefore declined. That leaves seven players still undecided. The names: Seiya Suzuki (Cubs), Randy Arozarena (Mariners), David Bednar (Yankees), Ryan Jeffers (Twins), Adrian Morejon (Reds), and two others. Each has until 4 PM ET on November 17 to accept or decline. Any player who accepts gets guaranteed $23.15 million on a one-year contract from their current team. Any player who declines hits the open market — but their new team surrenders a draft pick if the original club offered arbitration. History says almost none of them will accept. All-time, 18 of 157 qualifying offer recipients have said yes — that's roughly 11.5 percent. The incentive to test free agency is overwhelmingly strong for established players. Adrian Morejon is reportedly "almost certainly" declining, per Yahoo Sports. The others have been quieter, but expect most or all to decline. This may also be the last QO cycle ever. The MLB CBA expires December 1, and the new deal — if one ever gets done — could eliminate the qualifying offer system entirely. One day left. Watch the wire at 4 PM ET tomorrow. ### Image Post Caption One day left for seven players to decide whether $23.15 million guaranteed is worth giving up free agency. History says almost all will say no. Tomorrow we find out. #MLB

CBA Countdown — 15 Days Until Expiration
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Fifteen days from tonight, the MLB collective bargaining agreement expires — and no one is predicting a last-minute deal. The core dispute is familiar and unresolved. MLB owners want a salary cap, arguing it would create competitive balance and predictability. The MLBPA has refused a cap in every negotiation cycle and shows no sign of changing its position. Commissioner Rob Manfred has signaled that if no agreement is reached by December 1, baseball will implement a work stoppage. The last lockout — which began December 2, 2021 — lasted 99 days and pushed spring training back by weeks, with the regular season losing a handful of games at the start. That was widely viewed as a contained outcome. If the upcoming negotiations drag into spring, the 2027 season itself could be in jeopardy. What does this mean for the offseason right now? Any player who hasn't signed before December 1 joins a frozen market. That includes Tarik Skubal, whose California meetings are happening this week under exactly this pressure. It includes Jazz Chisholm, with 11 teams engaged and meetings starting next week. The CBA countdown isn't background noise — it's the defining pressure of this entire offseason. Fifteen days. No deal in sight. Watch closely. ### Image Post Caption 15 days. No deal in sight. Commissioner Manfred has pledged a lockout. The 2027 season is officially at risk. #MLB
