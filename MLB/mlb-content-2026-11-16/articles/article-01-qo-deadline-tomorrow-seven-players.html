The QO Deadline Is Tomorrow: Seven Players, $23 Million, and One Day Left to Decide

By Ryan Calloway | November 16, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Tomorrow at 4:00 PM ET, one of the annual rituals of the MLB offseason plays out in full: the qualifying offer deadline. Seven players still have not publicly declared whether they will accept or decline the $23.15 million guarantee sitting on the table. By the time business hours close on November 17, every answer will be in.

Jake Bauers is already off the board. The first baseman, who received a qualifying offer from the Milwaukee Brewers, signed a 4-year, $58 million deal with the Boston Red Sox and therefore declined. That means the QO sits at seven remaining decisions — all expected to come tomorrow.

Who's Still Deciding

The players who still have outstanding qualifying offers: outfielder Seiya Suzuki of the Cubs, outfielder Randy Arozarena of the Mariners, reliever David Bednar of the Yankees, catcher Ryan Jeffers of the Twins, and starter Adrian Morejon of the Reds, among two others.

Each of those players has until 4:00 PM ET to call their team and say yes or no. A yes means a guaranteed $23.15 million on a one-year deal with the club that tendered the offer. A no means hitting free agency — but with a condition: any team that then signs a player who declined a QO must surrender a draft pick as compensation to the original club, unless that club is in the bottom 15 of revenue, which carries a slot instead of a pick.

History Says No

The numbers on QO acceptances are stark. All-time, 18 of 157 qualifying offer recipients have said yes — roughly 11.5 percent. The incentive to test the open market is almost always stronger than the security of a one-year guarantee, particularly for established players who can reasonably expect to earn more in multi-year deals.

Yahoo Sports reports that Adrian Morejon is "almost certainly" declining in pursuit of a multi-year deal. No other player has broken publicly, but the expectation around the league is that the overwhelming majority of tomorrow's decisions will be no.

The Last QO Cycle?

There's an additional layer of significance this year. The MLB collective bargaining agreement expires December 1 at 11:59 PM ET — 15 days from now. If a lockout follows, as most expect, it's possible that the qualifying offer system gets restructured or eliminated entirely in the next CBA. That means the seven players deciding tomorrow may be doing so in the final QO cycle in baseball history.

That framing doesn't change the math for any individual player. For Suzuki, Arozarena, Bednar, Jeffers, and the others, the decision comes down to the same calculation it always does: is free agency worth the uncertainty? For almost all of them, the answer will be yes.

What's Next

Tomorrow's decisions open the flood gates for November free agency. Players who decline their QOs become unrestricted free agents immediately, and teams can begin negotiating or signing them right away — provided the CBA remains in effect. With Dec. 1 fifteen days away, any player who doesn't sign quickly risks having the market freeze beneath them. That urgency applies to everyone on this list. By this time tomorrow, we'll know who's available. The offseason sprint begins in earnest on November 18.
