The Pre-Lockout Sprint Is On: Teams Are Moving Before the Dec. 1 Deadline

By Ryan Calloway | November 16, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The collective bargaining agreement expires in 15 days. The qualifying offer deadline is tomorrow. The free-agent market is about to open wide — and teams are already moving, trading pieces and locking up players they don't want to leave unresolved when December 1 arrives.

Today brought the most direct signal yet that the pre-lockout transaction sprint is fully underway: the San Francisco Giants traded reliever Ryan Walker to the Milwaukee Brewers in exchange for prospects Blake Holub and Gavin Perry, per Yahoo Sports. It's a roster-clearing move that reflects a franchise taking stock of its bullpen assets before the market freezes.

What's Already Happened

The Walker trade is the latest in a string of moves that have accelerated over the past week:

On November 12, reliever Evan Phillips signed a three-year deal with the Atlanta Braves. The Braves, building around their core position players, identified bullpen depth as a priority and moved before the QO deadline and CBA clock put further pressure on the market. On November 10, Emilio Pagán signed a one-year deal with the Detroit Tigers. And on November 13, Jake Bauers signed the most significant deal yet — a 4-year, $58 million contract with the Boston Red Sox, becoming the first player to formally decline his qualifying offer this cycle.

These moves share a common thread: teams finishing their business while they still can.

What's Coming

MLB owners meetings run November 17 through 19. That gathering has historically been one of the most active stretches of the offseason, with general managers in the same rooms accelerating trade and signing talks. With the CBA expiring on December 1, this year's owners meetings carry a different weight — anything that doesn't get done in the next two weeks may not get done until the spring, or later.

The major free agents — Tarik Skubal, Jazz Chisholm, and others — are conducting their own parallel process this week in California meetings. But beneath the headline deals, dozens of secondary moves are being accelerated. Teams are trading depth pieces they'd normally hold through winter. Relievers, bench bats, and depth starters are changing hands at a faster rate than a typical early November.

What's Next

Expect the pace to intensify through the weekend and into next week. The qualifying offer deadline tomorrow will release seven players into the open market simultaneously, creating an immediate surge of signings for mid-tier free agents. Meanwhile, the owners meetings Nov. 17–19 will accelerate trade conversations that have been in preliminary stages. For teams with significant holes — rotation depth, bullpen arms, lineup upgrades — this is the two-week window. After December 1, the only certainty is uncertainty.
