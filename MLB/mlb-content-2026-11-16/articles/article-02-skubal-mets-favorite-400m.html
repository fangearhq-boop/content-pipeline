Tarik Skubal's Market Is Wide Open — and the Mets Are Ahead

By Marcus Cole | November 16, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The best starting pitcher in baseball is in California this week — and six teams are flying to meet him. Tarik Skubal's free-agent meetings have begun, and by most accounts, the New York Mets are the team to beat.

Jon Heyman, who has covered this process closely since the GM Meetings in Las Vegas, characterizes the Mets as "probably the favorite" to land Skubal. That assessment carries weight in a market where there's no shortage of interested parties — but where the Mets, under owner Steve Cohen and president of baseball operations David Stearns, have the resources and the documented appetite to make a transformational pitching acquisition.

What Teams Are Saying

The Dodgers, as expected, are in. GM Brandon Gomes addressed the Skubal process at the GM Meetings: "We've been in touch with his camp, and our expectation is we'll meet at some point." Los Angeles has won three straight World Series titles and has shown no reluctance to add high-end starting pitching — but there are reports that the Dodgers may not pursue the deal at the highest contract levels that other teams could offer. The Padres are also confirmed in the process, along with three additional clubs.

No team appears to have broken from the pack with a formal offer yet. This is the listening and pitching phase — both sides are figuring out fit, structure, and urgency.

The Contract

The numbers being discussed are historic. ESPN projects the total value of Skubal's deal could reach $400 million. The floor on average annual value is estimated at $44 million per season — which would shatter the existing pitcher AAV record of $27 million set by Yoshinobu Yamamoto when he signed with the Dodgers. Skubal isn't just the most coveted starting pitcher on the market. He may be the most coveted pitcher who has ever hit free agency.

At 29 years old — he turns 30 on November 20 — Skubal represents a rare combination of elite stuff, postseason durability, and the kind of frontline ace identity that championship rosters are built around. His 2026 season was among the best by any starting pitcher in the American League.

The Clock

The defining pressure in this negotiation is time. The MLB collective bargaining agreement expires December 1. Any player who hasn't signed before that date watches the market freeze until a new CBA is in place — which, based on the current state of labor negotiations, could mean months. Every team meeting with Skubal this week in California is operating under that constraint. Fifteen days is not a long time to agree on $350 to $400 million.

The Mets have been here before. They spent aggressively on pitching when it mattered. The question now is whether the Dodgers or another team can break their lead.

What's Next

Skubal's California meetings continue through the week. If the pattern from previous high-end pitching markets holds, an agreement — if one comes — will likely emerge rapidly once the top bidder steps forward with a formal number. The Mets are positioned to do that. With 15 days left before the CBA expires, every day without a deal makes the eventual outcome less certain. Watch the wire through the weekend. This one is moving fast.
