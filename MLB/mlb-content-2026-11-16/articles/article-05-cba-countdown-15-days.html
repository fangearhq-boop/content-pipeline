The CBA Is 15 Days from Expiring — and a Lockout Is Coming

By Marcus Cole | November 16, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Fifteen days from tonight, the MLB collective bargaining agreement expires at 11:59 PM ET on December 1. No one in baseball expects a last-minute deal. Commissioner Rob Manfred has signaled that if no agreement is reached, baseball will implement a work stoppage. The question isn't really whether there will be a lockout. The question is how long it lasts.

What They're Fighting About

The core dispute is the same one that has defined labor relations in baseball for a generation: the salary cap. MLB ownership has pushed for a cap system that would limit how much teams can spend on player salaries, arguing it promotes competitive balance. The MLB Players Association has refused to accept any form of a salary cap in every collective bargaining negotiation and shows no sign of changing that position.

Beyond the cap, the two sides have disagreed on luxury tax thresholds, the minimum salary floor, revenue sharing formulas, and the structure of the free-agent system itself — including, potentially, the qualifying offer mechanism that seven players are deciding on tomorrow. No outlet has reported meaningful progress toward a deal. The industry consensus puts the odds of avoiding a lockout in the low single digits.

The Precedent

The last MLB lockout began on December 2, 2021 — the day after the previous CBA expired. It lasted 99 days, ending on March 10, 2022. Spring training was delayed by weeks, and the regular season lost a handful of games at the start. That outcome was considered relatively contained: the work stoppage resolved before the season was materially impacted.

A longer work stoppage this time could mean a delayed or shortened 2027 regular season. If talks drag into spring training — and there is real precedent for extended labor disputes in baseball — the fan experience, broadcast revenues, and player earnings all take significant hits.

What's Next

The free-agent market will effectively freeze on December 1 if a lockout takes effect. Any player who hasn't signed — including Tarik Skubal, Jazz Chisholm, and dozens of others still on the market — will wait for a resolution. That's the most immediate consequence for fans tracking this offseason. Beyond that, the timeline for a new deal depends entirely on how far apart the two sides remain and how much pressure a stalled season puts on each side to compromise. Fifteen days is not enough time to expect a deal. Watch for it to get worse before it gets better.
