Jazz Chisholm Has 11 Teams Chasing Him — and No Qualifying Offer to Worry About

By Jake Torres | November 16, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Jazz Chisholm is the most dynamic free agent at second base in years — and right now, he has the entire market in front of him.

MLB Network's Jon Morosi reports that 11 teams have engaged Chisholm's representatives. In-person meetings are scheduled to begin next week. The Blue Jays have emerged as the early frontrunner, per Sportsnet's Shi Davidi, who reports that Toronto met with Chisholm's agents at the GM Meetings in Las Vegas and envisions him as the starting second baseman, with Ernie Clement moving to a super-utility role. The Yankees, who had Chisholm last season, are not expected to re-engage, per Morosi.

No QO, No Problem

What makes Chisholm especially attractive to a wide field of teams is the absence of a qualifying offer. No QO was tendered to Chisholm, which means any team that signs him pays no draft pick penalty. That removes one of the key barriers that often narrows a player's market to larger-market clubs. The result: 11 teams are actively engaged, and that number may understate the total interest.

For comparison, players who decline qualifying offers typically see their markets compressed by the draft pick cost. Chisholm has no such ceiling on demand.

What He Brings

In 2026, Chisholm hit .231 with a .312 on-base percentage and .399 slugging, with 19 home runs and 41 stolen bases. That stolen base total puts him among the most dangerous baserunners in the sport. The 19 home runs give him above-average pop for a middle infielder. The combination of speed and power at second base is rare enough that teams are willing to look past a batting average that falls below .240.

At second base specifically, the free-agent market is thin. Chisholm is the best option available, and 11 teams have already recognized that.

The CBA Wrinkle

Like every free agent this offseason, Chisholm is operating against the Dec. 1 CBA expiration. In-person meetings next week means negotiations would need to accelerate quickly if any team wants to close before the market freezes. The Blue Jays' frontrunner status is meaningful, but it's early — 11 teams engaged means 10 others are prepared to push the price.

What's Next

In-person meetings begin next week. Expect the Blue Jays to make a formal play early given their established position. The Padres, Angels, and several other clubs are rumored fits, though no deal is imminent. The 15-day window before the CBA expires puts real pressure on both sides to reach a conclusion. Chisholm has the leverage — 11 teams is a seller's market. The question is which team blinks first with an offer he can't refuse.
