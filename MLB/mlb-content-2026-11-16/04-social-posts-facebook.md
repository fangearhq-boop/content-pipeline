### STORY 1: QO Deadline Tomorrow

**Long-Form Post (8:00 AM ET)**

The qualifying offer deadline arrives tomorrow at 4:00 PM ET — and the baseball world is watching.

Here's the situation. Eight players received qualifying offers this offseason worth $23.15M each. Jake Bauers is already off the board — he signed a 4-year, $58 million deal with the Boston Red Sox and therefore declined. That leaves seven players still undecided.

The names: Seiya Suzuki (Cubs), Randy Arozarena (Mariners), David Bednar (Yankees), Ryan Jeffers (Twins), Adrian Morejon (Reds), and two others. Each has until 4 PM ET on November 17 to accept or decline. Any player who accepts gets guaranteed $23.15 million on a one-year contract from their current team. Any player who declines hits the open market — but their new team surrenders a draft pick if the original club offered arbitration.

History says almost none of them will accept. All-time, 18 of 157 qualifying offer recipients have said yes — that's roughly 11.5 percent. The incentive to test free agency is overwhelmingly strong for established players.

Adrian Morejon is reportedly "almost certainly" declining, per Yahoo Sports. The others have been quieter, but expect most or all to decline.

This may also be the last QO cycle ever. The MLB CBA expires December 1, and the new deal — if one ever gets done — could eliminate the qualifying offer system entirely.

One day left. Watch the wire at 4 PM ET tomorrow.

### STORY 2: Tarik Skubal West Coast Meetings

**Long-Form Post (10:30 AM ET)**

The most important free-agent pitcher in a generation is in California this week — and teams are lining up to make their case.

Tarik Skubal is meeting with six teams, including the Mets, Dodgers, and Padres. Jon Heyman reports the Mets are the industry favorite to land him — "probably the favorite" in his words. Dodgers GM Brandon Gomes addressed the meetings at the GM Meetings earlier this month: "We've been in touch with his camp, and our expectation is we'll meet at some point." The Padres are also confirmed.

What's Skubal worth? According to ESPN, the ceiling on this deal is $400 million. The floor on his average annual value is estimated at $44 million per season — which would shatter the existing pitcher AAV record set by Yoshinobu Yamamoto at $27 million. Skubal isn't just the top arm on the market. He's in a category of one.

There's a hard clock on this negotiation. The MLB CBA expires December 1. If Skubal hasn't signed by then, the market freezes until a new agreement is reached — which could mean months. Every team in those California meetings knows that the window to sign the best pitcher in baseball is 15 days long.

The Mets are ahead. The Dodgers are in. The question is how much either side is willing to pay — and whether the Mets can close before the lockout.

### STORY 3: Jazz Chisholm — 11 Teams, No Qualifying Offer

**Long-Form Post (12:30 PM ET)**

Jazz Chisholm is the hottest position player on the free-agent market — and there's no shortage of competition to sign him.

MLB Network's Jon Morosi reports that 11 teams have engaged Chisholm's representatives. In-person meetings are scheduled to begin next week. The Blue Jays are emerging as the frontrunner, per Sportsnet's Shi Davidi, who reports that Toronto met with Chisholm's agents at the GM Meetings and envisions him as their starting second baseman, with Ernie Clement moving into a super-utility role.

The Yankees, who had Chisholm last season, are not expected to re-engage, per Morosi.

What makes Chisholm especially attractive to teams: no qualifying offer was attached to him, meaning any team that signs him pays no draft pick penalty. That significantly expands the market. Eleven teams engaged isn't surprising — it may actually be an undercount.

In 2026, Chisholm hit .231/.312/.399 with 19 home runs and 41 stolen bases. He's one of the fastest players in baseball, he has above-average power for a middle infielder, and he's not yet 30 years old.

Meetings start next week. The Blue Jays have momentum. But 11 teams is a crowded field — expect a bidding war.

### STORY 4: The Pre-Lockout Transaction Sprint Is On

**Long-Form Post (3:00 PM ET)**

Teams aren't waiting for free agency to heat up on its own. With the CBA expiring in 15 days, the pre-lockout transaction sprint is officially underway.

Today, the Giants traded reliever Ryan Walker to the Brewers in exchange for prospects Blake Holub and Gavin Perry. It's a relatively modest move in isolation — but it's part of a broader pattern of teams clearing cap space, moving pieces, and positioning themselves before December 1.

Here's what's happened in just the past week:

— Evan Phillips: 3-year deal with the Atlanta Braves (November 12)
— Emilio Pagán: 1-year deal with the Detroit Tigers (November 10)
— Jake Bauers: 4-year, $58 million with the Boston Red Sox (November 13) — the first major qualifying offer decision of the cycle

MLB owners meetings run November 17–19. That gathering historically produces a surge of signings and trade talks. With the Dec. 1 hard stop looming, the next two weeks will be the most active stretch of the offseason — perhaps the most consequential offseason sprint in years, given that whatever doesn't get done may not get done until a new CBA is in place.

If you're a baseball fan: set your alerts. This window is closing fast.

### STORY 5: The CBA Is 15 Days from Expiring

**Long-Form Post (6:00 PM ET)**

Fifteen days from tonight, the MLB collective bargaining agreement expires — and no one is predicting a last-minute deal.

The core dispute is familiar and unresolved. MLB owners want a salary cap, arguing it would create competitive balance and predictability. The MLBPA has refused a cap in every negotiation cycle and shows no sign of changing its position. Commissioner Rob Manfred has signaled that if no agreement is reached by December 1, baseball will implement a work stoppage.

The last lockout — which began December 2, 2021 — lasted 99 days and pushed spring training back by weeks, with the regular season losing a handful of games at the start. That was widely viewed as a contained outcome. If the upcoming negotiations drag into spring, the 2027 season itself could be in jeopardy.

What does this mean for the offseason right now? Any player who hasn't signed before December 1 joins a frozen market. That includes Tarik Skubal, whose California meetings are happening this week under exactly this pressure. It includes Jazz Chisholm, with 11 teams engaged and meetings starting next week. The CBA countdown isn't background noise — it's the defining pressure of this entire offseason.

Fifteen days. No deal in sight. Watch closely.
