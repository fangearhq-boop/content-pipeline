### STORY 1: CBA Countdown

#### Long-Form Post

Four days. That's how long Major League Baseball and the Players' Association have before the current collective bargaining agreement expires.

The CBA runs out at 11:59 PM ET on December 1. Commissioner Rob Manfred has said he "can't imagine" the owners ratifying a new deal without a salary cap and floor — a position the union has categorically rejected. On November 25, MLB filed a preemptive federal lawsuit in New York seeking legal protection for the lockout, trying to block any potential antitrust challenge from the union. The MLBPA called it "completely frivolous."

The 2021-22 lockout lasted 99 days. This dispute involves the same fundamental disagreement about player compensation, with MLB proposing a $258 million salary cap and $180 million floor to start in 2028. The union has not moved.

The sprint to sign players before December 1 is already underway — and that sprint ends in 96 hours.

### STORY 2: Pre-Lockout Signing Blitz

#### Long-Form Post

The MLB free agent market is moving at a remarkable pace — because it has to.

Before December 1, teams cannot sign or trade players once the CBA expires and the lockout begins. So the offseason's normal deliberate pace has turned into a sprint.

In the last 48 hours: Freddy Peralta — the reigning ALCS MVP — agreed to a five-year, $125 million deal with the Baltimore Orioles. Kevin Gausman signed a one-year, $25 million deal with the Detroit Tigers. Kris Bubic agreed to a one-year, $17.5 million deal with the Cleveland Guardians. Kevin Ginkel is headed to the Twins on a two-year, $18 million contract. Gary Sanchez signed a one-year, $6.5 million deal with the Miami Marlins.

Six starting pitchers have now signed guaranteed deals worth $50 million or more this offseason. The class outside Tarik Skubal ($450M, Mets) has been headlined by mid-tier contracts — but the volume is remarkable.

Who still needs to sign before the freeze? Jazz Chisholm. Brandon Lowe. And a few names who haven't moved yet.

### STORY 3: Skubal $450M

#### Long-Form Post

Tarik Skubal is about to become the highest-paid pitcher in baseball history. And he's going to do it on a Mets team that went 74-88 last season.

The nine-year, $450 million deal the Mets and Skubal agreed to is not just a record. It is a statement about how the game values dominant starting pitching. At $50 million per year in average annual value, Skubal shatters the previous record for a pitcher — Yoshinobu Yamamoto's $27 million AAV — by nearly double.

Skubal was the defining pitcher in the American League last year. The question was always whether a team was willing to commit nine years and $450 million to someone who, however good, is still one injury away from becoming a very expensive DL entry. The Mets were willing. They beat the Dodgers and Padres in the bidding.

What does this mean for the rest of the pitching market? Simply: the bar for elite pitching has moved up. Every agent representing a frontline starter now has a new data point. The pitchers who sign after the lockout — whenever that is — will be negotiating in a world where $50M AAV is the established ceiling.

### STORY 4: Jazz Chisholm

#### Long-Form Post

Jazz Chisholm Jr. is spending Thanksgiving without a contract.

The former Yankee outfielder — 41 stolen bases, 19 home runs in 2026, no qualifying offer attached — opted for free agency in October and has been fielding interest from more than 11 teams since. The Washington Nationals and Toronto Blue Jays are considered the most actively engaged suitors, with the Blue Jays having already held what one insider described as a "productive meeting" with Chisholm's representatives.

Here's the problem: the market freezes in four days.

When the CBA expires December 1 and the lockout begins December 2, every transaction stops. No signings. No trades. No minor league contracts. Chisholm will not be able to sign with any team until the lockout ends — which, based on the 2021 precedent, could be months.

For a player who turned down the safety of a qualifying offer to bet on himself in free agency, an extended lockout is the worst possible outcome. The window to get a deal done is exactly four days.

### STORY 5: Lockout FAQ

#### Long-Form Post

If you weren't paying close attention to baseball in the winter of 2021-22, here's what you missed — and what you're about to see again.

A lockout is an owner-imposed work stoppage. Unlike a strike, players don't walk out — owners lock them out. During a lockout, all 30 teams cannot sign, trade, or even contact any player under contract or on their 40-man roster. Free agents cannot sign. Minor league players cannot be called up. The Hot Stove goes completely cold.

The 2021-22 lockout started December 2, 2021 and lasted until March 10, 2022 — 99 days. Spring training games were canceled. Opening Day was pushed back by one week. It was the longest MLB work stoppage since 1994-95. 

This one starts December 2, 2026. The 2027 schedule puts Opening Day around March 25. That gives both sides roughly 115 days before the first game is threatened. But spring training would be in jeopardy weeks before that.

The core dispute hasn't changed in five years: owners want a salary cap and revenue-sharing floor. The union says a cap is a nonstarter. Something has to give — and historically, something always does. The question is how long it takes.
