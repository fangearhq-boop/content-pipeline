Pre-Lockout Sprint: Six Pitchers Sign as the Free Agent Clock Winds Down

By Marcus Cole | November 27, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The MLB free agent market does not typically move this fast. But with the collective bargaining agreement expiring December 1 and a lockout expected to begin December 2, every team with a roster need and a checkbook has been forced to compress a normal offseason into a matter of days.

In the last 48 hours alone, five pitchers agreed to deals totaling more than $192 million. The Hot Stove is not warming up — it is running at full temperature before it goes cold entirely.

Freddy Peralta to Baltimore: $125 Million

The biggest deal of the sprint belongs to Freddy Peralta, who agreed to a five-year, $125 million contract with the Baltimore Orioles. Peralta, the reigning ALCS MVP, was one of the most coveted starters on the market after a dominant postseason run. The Orioles, who have been building toward a championship window, locked up a frontline arm before the freeze.

At $25 million AAV, Peralta's deal reflects a market that has recalibrated upward in the wake of Tarik Skubal's nine-year, $450 million agreement with the Mets. What would have looked like an overpay in prior offseasons now represents reasonable value for a proven playoff performer.

Gausman, Bubic, Ginkel, Sanchez

Behind the Peralta deal, the signings have kept coming. Kevin Gausman agreed to a one-year, $25 million deal with the Detroit Tigers — a short-term bet from a veteran who may be positioning himself for a larger contract after the lockout resolves. Kris Bubic signed a one-year, $17.5 million deal with the Cleveland Guardians. Kevin Ginkel agreed to a two-year, $18 million contract with the Minnesota Twins. Gary Sanchez signed a one-year, $6.5 million deal with the Miami Marlins.

The common thread: short-term. Teams are not making long-term commitments under a 96-hour clock. The deals getting done are either elite players worth locking up regardless of timing (Peralta), or one-year arrangements that give both sides flexibility once the new CBA is eventually negotiated.

What Teams Are Trying to Avoid

The calculus driving this sprint is straightforward. Once the lockout begins, teams cannot sign free agents, complete trades, or negotiate extensions. Any player left unsigned on December 2 enters a freeze of indeterminate length. The 2021 lockout lasted 99 days. This one has no defined end date — and the fundamental disagreement between owners and the MLBPA over a salary cap suggests it may last longer.

Teams with rotation needs or lineup gaps face a choice: sign someone now at a slight premium, or wait out the lockout and compete for the same players in a compressed market once negotiations conclude. Most front offices have concluded that certainty is worth paying for.

Who Is Still Out There

The names who have not yet signed are notable. Jazz Chisholm remains unsigned, with the Washington Nationals and Toronto Blue Jays considered the most engaged suitors. Brandon Lowe has not yet found a landing spot. Several relief arms and depth starters are still on the market, fielding calls from teams trying to solve a problem before the window closes.

The next 96 hours will determine whether those players sign before the freeze or spend an indefinite stretch waiting for a lockout to end. For some of them, the difference may be tens of millions of dollars — deals that get made in a full market versus deals that get made once owners and players are legally permitted to talk again.

What's Next

The free agent window closes when the CBA expires at 11:59 PM ET on December 1. Any player without a signed contract at that moment enters the lockout as a free agent, unable to negotiate or sign until an agreement is reached. Watch for a final burst of signings in the next 48-72 hours as teams attempt to close out their offseason business before the freeze begins.
