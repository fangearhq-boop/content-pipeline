Jazz Chisholm Has 4 Days to Sign Before the Market Freezes

By Ryan Calloway | November 27, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Jazz Chisholm Jr. is spending Thanksgiving without a contract. The former Yankees outfielder — 41 stolen bases, 19 home runs in 2026 — opted for free agency in October, turned down a qualifying offer, and has been fielding interest from more than 11 teams since. None of those conversations has produced a deal.

That is about to become a problem. The MLB free agent market closes in four days.

What He Brings

Chisholm is one of the most dynamic position players on the market. His 41 stolen bases rank among the best in the American League last season, and his combination of speed and power — 19 home runs alongside the base-stealing production — is rare in a market that consistently pays a premium for it.

He did not receive a qualifying offer from the Yankees, which removes one of the typical complications in negotiations with top free agents. Teams pursuing Chisholm do not need to surrender a draft pick, which widens the pool of interested suitors and, in theory, should accelerate a deal.

That it has not accelerated to a completed contract by Thanksgiving is the question the market is trying to answer.

The Frontrunners

The Washington Nationals and Toronto Blue Jays are considered the most actively engaged suitors. The Blue Jays have already held what one insider described as a "productive meeting" with Chisholm's representatives. Washington has been aggressive in reshaping its roster this offseason, and an outfielder of Chisholm's caliber would fit the Nationals' timeline as they build toward contention.

Neither team has closed the deal. With more than 11 organizations reportedly involved in the process, Chisholm's camp is clearly waiting for the right offer rather than accepting the first competitive one.

The Lockout Clock

The collective bargaining agreement expires December 1, 2026 at 11:59 PM Eastern Time. A lockout is expected to begin December 2. When that happens, every transaction in baseball stops. No free agent signings. No trades. No negotiations.

For Chisholm, that means the window to get a deal done is exactly four days. Any player who remains unsigned when the lockout begins cannot sign until the dispute between owners and the MLBPA is resolved — and based on the 2021 precedent, that could take months. The last lockout lasted 99 days. This one, with a harder fundamental disagreement at its core, may last longer.

Chisholm opted for free agency to bet on himself and his market value. An extended lockout is the worst possible outcome for that bet. The longer the freeze extends, the more his market is determined by whatever new CBA framework emerges rather than the open competition his camp was counting on.

The Decision

At some point in the next 96 hours, Chisholm and his representatives will face a choice: accept the best available offer before December 1 and guarantee a deal under the current market conditions, or hold out for more and risk entering the lockout without a contract.

Teams know this dynamic. Any front office engaged in talks with Chisholm right now understands that the freeze creates negotiating leverage they would not have in a normal market. Some will use it. Others may move before Chisholm's camp expects the pressure to arrive.

Four days. Then the market stops.

What's Next

The next 96 hours will determine whether Chisholm is part of the pre-lockout signing blitz or one of the marquee names still unsigned when the freeze begins. Watch for activity from the Blue Jays and Nationals over the holiday weekend. If neither team moves before December 1, Chisholm's market will be on hold until the lockout resolves — however long that takes.
