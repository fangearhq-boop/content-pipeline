### STORY 1: Misiorowski First Loss vs. Mets

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 9:00 AM ET

The Milwaukee Brewers' phenom got tagged for the first time in 2026 on Thursday — and the fact that it happened matters more for the narrative than the box score.

Jakob Misiorowski walked into Thursday's start against the New York Mets with a 13-5 record and a 1.68 ERA. The NL Cy Young market had him at -550. His previous start, August 22 against the Braves, was six innings, zero earned runs, six strikeouts. The Brewers were winning games when Misiorowski pitched, and he was producing some of the best pitching numbers in baseball over an extended stretch.

Then the Mets happened.

Per NBC Sports, Misiorowski "gets tagged for the first time in a loss to the Mets" — a headline that underscores just how unusual a bad outing has been for him in 2026. Over 24 starts entering Thursday, the number of times he had been "tagged" in any meaningful sense was essentially zero. The Mets broke that streak.

Here is what one loss doesn't change: Misiorowski is still 13-6 with a 1.68 ERA and 216 strikeouts. In any reasonable Cy Young framework, those numbers lead the conversation. The ERA is historic. The strikeout total is dominant. The win total, while a team-dependent stat, reflects a pitcher who gives the Brewers consistent length and quality.

What the loss does is open the door for the "is he mortal?" conversation — the one that every front-runner eventually faces. One bad start in August is a data point. Two would be a trend. What Misiorowski does in his next start will be worth watching more closely than almost any outing the Brewers have remaining.

The Cy Young is still his to lose.

### STORY 2: PCA NL MVP — Cubs Drop Back-to-Back to D-backs

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 12:30 PM ET

The Chicago Cubs just lost back-to-back games to the Arizona Diamondbacks. Ryan Waldschmidt's walk-off put the Diamondbacks on top Tuesday night (5-4). Arizona made it two in a row Wednesday (2-0). Chicago sits at 76-57 with 32 games left — still the NL Wild Card No. 1 seed, but absorbing losses at a bad time in the calendar.

None of that changes Pete Crow-Armstrong's MVP case. Yet.

PCA enters Thursday with a .281 batting average, 33 home runs, 83 RBI, 32 stolen bases, and a 7.8 fWAR. He leads the NL MVP poll in MLB.com's 5th ballot of the season with 29 of 37 first-place votes. Shohei Ohtani is second with 8 first-place votes and trails PCA in fWAR 7.8 to 6.8.

The numbers support PCA at the top. Since July 3, he has posted a 171 wRC+ and a 1.014 OPS with 14 home runs. He leads all major league outfielders in CF Fielding Run Value with 24 — elite defensive production on top of the offensive numbers. And he is on pace for back-to-back 30-30 seasons, which no Cubs player has ever accomplished.

The complication is the team-success question. MVP voters have historically factored in a player's team performance, especially in close races. PCA's Cubs are 76-57, which is not a bad record — they hold a postseason spot. But if the D-backs (70-63) or the Phillies (73-60) continue to push, and the Cubs' lead narrows or disappears, the narrative around PCA's supporting cast becomes a real factor.

The MVP is PCA's right now. What the Cubs do in 32 games will determine if it stays that way.

### STORY 3: Yordan Alvarez Triple Crown Chase

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 10:30 AM ET

Yordan Alvarez leads the American League in batting average, home runs, and RBI. That is the Triple Crown. Nobody has won it since Miguel Cabrera in 2012. And ZiPS now gives Alvarez nearly a two-in-three chance of becoming the first player in 14 years to do it.

The numbers: approximately .329-.330 batting average, 35 home runs, 84-85 RBI — and leading all three categories. His batting average lead over the field is described as "essentially insurmountable," sitting roughly 29 points above the next closest AL hitter. His RBI lead is seven. The home run race is tighter, but Alvarez has been the most consistent producer in the AL over the past several months.

The recent context makes the case even stronger. Over a seven-game hitting streak entering this week, Alvarez went 13-for-25 (.520) with 4 home runs, 4 doubles, and 8 RBI. He has reached base safely in 18 consecutive games. These are not the numbers of a player fading down the stretch — they are the numbers of a player accelerating into the final month.

For historical perspective: the Triple Crown is the rarest individual achievement in baseball. It requires leading the entire league in three different categories simultaneously while surviving roster moves, schedule quirks, and the inevitable variation that 162 games produces. Cabrera did it in 2012. Before him, it was Carl Yastrzemski in 1967. The list is that short.

ZiPS says 66.5%. That means in roughly one-in-three projections, someone catches Alvarez in the next 30 games. That is not impossible. But Yordan Alvarez right now is the best hitter in the American League, and he is building a lead that will take a historic September to close.

### STORY 5: Dustin May — 106 MPH Comebacker

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 6:00 PM ET

It happened on the very first pitch.

Tuesday night in Milwaukee, Dustin May was on the mound for the Brewers against the New York Mets. Before a single ball had crossed the plate, A.J. Ewing sent a line drive back up the middle at 106 miles per hour — right into May's back. The ball deflected off May to third baseman David Hamilton, who threw to first for the out. May was removed from the game immediately.

The diagnosis: right scapula contusion — a bruised shoulder blade. X-rays were negative. Which is, medically speaking, the best possible outcome from a 106 mph comebacker off a pitcher's back on the first pitch of the game.

The Brewers managed. Robert Gasser stepped in and provided approximately six innings of relief in an emergency start capacity — the kind of performance that veteran pitching staffs can produce but that is genuinely difficult to plan for. Milwaukee won 8-1. The Mets' lineup, which seemed ready to do damage, was held in check for the rest of the night.

The question for the Brewers going forward is what May's availability looks like for the remainder of the season. A scapula contusion with negative X-rays is meaningfully different from a fracture — there is no structural damage — but bruised tissue around the shoulder blade affects throwing mechanics, and pitching through discomfort in that area is not something teams push. The Brewers have not provided a return timeline.

Milwaukee is positioned well enough in the NL standings that they have some flexibility. But Dustin May was a valuable piece of their rotation before Tuesday's first pitch. His status in the next two to three weeks matters.
