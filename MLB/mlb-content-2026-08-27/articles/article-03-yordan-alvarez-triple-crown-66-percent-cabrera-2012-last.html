Yordan Alvarez Is Chasing the Triple Crown. ZiPS Says He Has a 2-in-3 Shot.

By Ryan Calloway | Ballpark Banter | August 27, 2026

Yordan Alvarez leads the American League in batting average, home runs, and RBI. He has done it for long enough, and built enough of a lead in each category, that ZiPS — the projection system that runs millions of simulations of the remaining schedule — now gives him approximately a 66.5 percent chance of completing the Triple Crown. The last player to win it was Miguel Cabrera in 2012. Before Cabrera, it was Carl Yastrzemski in 1967. The list is short by design.

The Numbers He Is Protecting

Alvarez's current stat line places him at approximately .329 to .330 in batting average, 35 home runs, and 84 to 85 RBI — all leading their respective AL categories. His batting average lead over the next closest AL hitter has been described by analysts as "essentially insurmountable," sitting roughly 29 points clear of the field. His RBI lead is seven. The home run lead, while smaller, has been consistent through a late-season stretch where other AL power hitters have tried to close the gap.

The recent performance reinforces the projection. Over a seven-game hitting streak entering this week, Alvarez went 13-for-25 — a .520 batting average over that stretch — with four home runs, four doubles, eight RBI, and five runs scored. He has reached base safely in 18 consecutive games. These are not the numbers of a player fading in August. They are the numbers of a player accelerating as other teams adjust and the games matter more.

Why the Triple Crown Is So Rare

The batting average, home run, and RBI categories each attract elite hitters from different parts of the offensive spectrum. Contact hitters lead batting average categories. Power hitters lead home run and RBI races. A player who leads all three simultaneously is, by definition, doing something that neither contact specialists nor pure sluggers can do in isolation — performing at the elite level in every dimension of offense for 162 games without a category falling off.

Yastrzemski did it in 1967 in one of the all-time AL pennant races. Cabrera did it in 2012, winning the AL MVP and helping the Tigers reach the postseason in the process. Between those two, a span of 45 years, nobody else accomplished it. Alvarez is now positioned to end a 14-year drought.

The 33.5 percent of ZiPS projections where Alvarez does not win the Triple Crown account for the realistic scenarios where one category slips: a batting average cold streak, a hot month from a competing home run hitter, or a September run by another AL first baseman or DH in the RBI column. These are not small risks — 30 games of baseball can produce significant statistical movement. But they are less likely than the alternative, and Alvarez's recent form suggests he is aware of what is in reach.

The Historical Weight

If Alvarez wins the Triple Crown, it would be the first time since 2012 — Cabrera's historic season that also produced one of the finest MVP arguments in AL history. Cabrera hit .330 with 44 home runs and 139 RBI that year. The categories Alvarez is currently leading are not at Cabrera's levels numerically, but Triple Crown success is measured against the league in a given year, not against history.

In the 2026 AL context, Alvarez's numbers lead. ZiPS says he is the most likely winner. With 30 games remaining, the math is on his side.
