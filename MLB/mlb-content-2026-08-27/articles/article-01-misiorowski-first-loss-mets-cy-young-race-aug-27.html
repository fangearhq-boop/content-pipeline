Misiorowski Gets Tagged for the First Time. The Mets End His Flawless 2026 Run.

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | August 27, 2026

The Milwaukee Brewers' NL Cy Young favorite took a loss Thursday against the New York Mets — and for the first time in 2026, Jakob Misiorowski was not the best pitcher on the field. Per NBC Sports, Misiorowski "gets tagged for the first time in a loss to the Mets." The headline captures what made this start unusual: across 24 starts and 145 innings before Thursday, the number of times Misiorowski was genuinely "tagged" by a major league lineup had been essentially zero.

Now that streak is over. And the conversation around the NL Cy Young Award just got a little more interesting.

What He Carried Into Thursday

Misiorowski entered the Mets start with a 13-5 record, a 1.68 ERA, a 2.14 FIP, and 216 strikeouts across 145 innings pitched. The market had him at -550 to win the NL Cy Young — a price that reflects the near-consensus view among bettors and analysts that no other NL pitcher had made a compelling case to take the award from him. That ERA is historically strong. That strikeout total, in 24 starts, is dominant.

His previous outing (August 22 against the Braves) was six innings, zero earned runs, four hits, two walks, six strikeouts — a win. He had been the kind of reliable, elite pitcher that Cy Young voters reward: not flashy, not variable, just consistently excellent across a full first half and into late August.

The Mets ended that run on Thursday.

What One Bad Start Does — and Doesn't — Mean

One loss in August does not dismantle a Cy Young case. Misiorowski is now 13-6, and his ERA will absorb whatever earned runs the Mets put up — but the scale of a single start relative to 145 innings of previous work is small. A pitcher can allow four earned runs in five innings and watch their ERA move by a fraction of a point when the denominator is that large. The math insulates him from one bad day.

What does change is the narrative. Before Thursday, Misiorowski's story was about sustaining perfection — each start adding to a record that made the Cy Young feel like a formality. After Thursday, the story shifts slightly: Can he bounce back? Is this a warning sign about fatigue entering September? Does the market recalibrate?

In a normal year, these questions would barely register. But the NL Cy Young race has been one-sided enough that any crack in Misiorowski's record invites scrutiny. Other pitchers have been watching from behind, waiting for exactly this kind of stumble.

MLB.com published post-game video of Misiorowski addressing the loss — a pitcher aware of what the outing means and, presumably, already thinking about the next start. That response — not the box score — is what will define how this loss reads in October when voters mark their ballots.

The Cy Young Race: Still His

At 13-6, 1.68 ERA, 216 strikeouts, Misiorowski still leads every meaningful Cy Young metric available. His FIP of 2.14 indicates the ERA is not a product of luck — he has been genuinely that good. The next pitcher behind him in the NL Cy Young conversation would need to close a significant gap in ERA and innings quality to make the award competitive. One Misiorowski loss, by itself, does not open that gap.

His next start will matter. September starts always do when an award is in play. But the Brewers' ace enters that start with a body of work that remains the strongest in the National League — and the vote, if it were held today, would not be close.
