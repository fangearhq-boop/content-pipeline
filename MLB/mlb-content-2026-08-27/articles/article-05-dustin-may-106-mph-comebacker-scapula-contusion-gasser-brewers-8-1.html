A 106 MPH Line Drive Off His Back on the First Pitch. Then the Brewers Won 8-1 Without Dustin May.

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | August 27, 2026

It happened before a single pitch had crossed the plate. On Tuesday night in Milwaukee, Dustin May stood on the mound against the New York Mets — and A.J. Ewing sent a comebacker back up the middle at 106 miles per hour. The ball struck May directly in the back. He was removed from the game. The Brewers, without their scheduled starter after one pitch, went on to win 8-1.

The Play and the Diagnosis

The comebacker deflected off May's back to third baseman David Hamilton, who threw to first base in time for the out. The play was executed cleanly despite the violence of the contact — but May was not able to continue. He was evaluated immediately and left the field under his own power.

The diagnosis: right scapula contusion, also described as a bruised shoulder blade. X-rays were negative. In the immediate aftermath, negative X-rays represent the best possible medical outcome from a 106 mph shot to the back — there is no structural fracture, no broken bone to complicate the recovery timeline. Soft tissue damage and bruising around the shoulder blade are real and painful, but they are recoverable injuries.

Milwaukee has not announced a specific return timeline for May. The Brewers described the injury as a right scapula contusion and noted the negative X-rays without committing to when he would be available to pitch again.

Robert Gasser Keeps the Game

What happened next illustrates why depth matters in a pennant race. Robert Gasser stepped into an emergency start capacity and delivered approximately six innings of relief — keeping the Mets' lineup from making the game competitive after May's departure. The Brewers scored eight runs. The Mets scored one. The win kept Milwaukee in strong position in the NL.

Gasser's performance was the kind of outing that rarely gets the attention it deserves: a pitcher called into an unexpected situation, with no opportunity to prepare mentally or physically the way a typical starter does, producing quality innings in a game with postseason implications. Milwaukee's pitching depth has been a quiet asset all season, and Tuesday night tested it in the most abrupt way possible.

May's Status and What It Means for the Brewers

A scapula contusion affects pitching mechanics because the shoulder girdle — the system of muscles and bones that allows the arm to operate — connects directly to the scapula. Bruised tissue in that area can create discomfort in the throwing motion at angles that are invisible from the outside but real to the pitcher. Teams are careful about pushing pitchers through that kind of injury, particularly in late August when adding weeks to a recovery timeline has postseason consequences.

The Brewers entered the week in a strong position in the NL standings. May's absence for whatever recovery period is necessary is manageable if Gasser and the rest of the rotation can absorb the extra starts. But rotation disruption in late August, when every game tightens playoff seeding, is not trivial. Milwaukee will monitor May's progress and make decisions about his availability in the coming days.

Tuesday night, one pitch was enough to test the Brewers' resilience. They passed that test 8-1.
