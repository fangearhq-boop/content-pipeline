The Cubs Lost Two Straight to the D-backs. PCA's MVP Lead Still Holds.

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | August 27, 2026

The Chicago Cubs dropped back-to-back games to the Arizona Diamondbacks this week — Ryan Waldschmidt's walk-off home run Tuesday (5-4) and a 2-0 shutout Wednesday. The Cubs sit at 76-57 with 32 games remaining, still holding the NL Wild Card No. 1 seed, but absorbing losses at an uncomfortable point in the calendar. None of that has moved Pete Crow-Armstrong off the top of the NL MVP ballot.

Not yet.

The Numbers That Hold Up

As of August 24, Pete Crow-Armstrong's 2026 numbers are: .281 batting average, .379 on-base percentage, .559 slugging, 33 home runs, 83 RBI, and 32 stolen bases — alongside a 7.8 fWAR. In MLB.com's fifth MVP ballot of the season, PCA received 29 of 37 first-place votes and 177 total points. Shohei Ohtani, the nearest challenger, earned 8 first-place votes and 154 points.

PCA leads Ohtani in fWAR by a full win — 7.8 to 6.8. That margin, in a statistically rigorous framework, represents the gap between the best player in the NL and the next closest candidate. fWAR accounts for offense, defense, and baserunning. PCA leads all major league outfielders in CF Fielding Run Value with 24, which means his defensive contributions alone are among the most valuable in the sport.

Since July 3, PCA has posted a 171 wRC+ and a 1.014 OPS with 14 home runs. He is not in a late-season slump. He is performing at an elite level and has been for two months.

The Team-Success Question

MVP voters have historically weighted team performance in close races. This race is not yet close enough for that factor to flip the outcome — but the Cubs' recent struggles introduce the question that voters will eventually ask. If Chicago loses a Wild Card spot before September ends, the "team success" argument loses some of its support for PCA's case. If the Cubs hold or improve their position, it remains a non-factor.

PCA is also completing back-to-back 30-30 seasons. No Cubs player has ever done that in consecutive years. The milestone represents sustained production of the highest order — power, speed, and the consistency to maintain both across a 162-game schedule. In the era of modern defensive metrics, adding 24 fielding run value on top of those offensive numbers gives PCA a case that is difficult to construct a statistical argument against.

Ohtani's counter is that two-way value — pitching and hitting simultaneously — creates a production floor that position players cannot match. But PCA's fWAR advantage, as of this week, reflects a player who is outproducing Ohtani in the NL's most sophisticated evaluation framework. The argument is with the numbers, not the narrative.

32 Games Left

The Cubs have 32 games remaining. PCA has time to add to his numbers, and the Cubs have time to recover. The Wild Card race is not lost — Chicago's buffer over the Phillies (3 games) and Padres (5 games) provides room. The Diamondbacks (6 games back) need to run a table that includes passing two other teams first. The Cubs are in a defensible position.

But the two-game losing streak to Arizona is the kind of development that voters notice and fans track. PCA needs the Cubs to hold, and the Cubs need PCA to keep performing at this level. Those two things are not in tension right now — but 32 games can produce significant movement in both directions.

The MVP is PCA's entering Thursday. Whether it stays his depends on what happens next.
