The D-backs Won Two From the Cubs. The NL Wild Card Cushion Is Shrinking.

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | August 27, 2026

The NL Wild Card picture has a new energy this week, and it comes from Arizona. The Diamondbacks won back-to-back games from the Chicago Cubs — a walk-off on Tuesday (5-4) and a shutout on Wednesday (2-0) — and with 32 games left, the race to hold three postseason spots in the NL has tightened. The Cubs (76-57) still lead the Wild Card field. But the teams below them are not conceding anything.

The Current Standings

As of Thursday, the NL Wild Card picture: Cubs at 76-57 (No. 1 seed), Phillies at 73-60 (No. 2 seed, approximately 3 games back), Padres at 71-62 (No. 3 seed, approximately 5 games back), and the Diamondbacks at 70-63 — 6 games out of a Wild Card spot, chasing the Padres for the third and final position.

That last number is the most important one in Arizona's math. The Diamondbacks do not need to catch the Cubs to get in — they need to pass the Padres first. And San Diego, sitting at 71-62, has two games of separation between them and Arizona. A two-game swing in a 32-game span is entirely achievable, which is why the D-backs' momentum matters.

What the D-backs Win Means

Ryan Waldschmidt has emerged as one of the most clutch rookie bats in the NL this season. His walk-off Tuesday night — two out, ninth inning, two-run home run to left field — pushed the D-backs past a Cubs team that had led 4-1 before the ninth. It was the kind of victory that changes a locker room's belief about what is possible in the final month. Arizona's 40th comeback win of the season tied them with the Cubs for the most in the majors.

The follow-up shutout Wednesday reinforced that Tuesday was not a fluke. Two quality games against a 76-57 team in a Wild Card race is a meaningful statement from a team on the outside of the postseason picture.

What the Cubs Need to Do

Chicago's primary goal is not to catch anyone — it is to protect what they have. At 76-57, a six-game lead over the Padres means the Cubs can afford to go .500 the rest of the way and likely still be in the postseason. But the back-to-back losses to Arizona serve as a reminder that the cushion is not infinite. A 7- or 8-game losing streak in September, paired with a hot Padres or D-backs run, could flip the picture dramatically.

The Phillies (73-60) are the more immediate threat to seeding. Philadelphia is 3 games back of the Cubs for the No. 1 seed, and the seeding matters. In the Wild Card format, avoiding the No. 1 division winner in round one is a meaningful competitive advantage — and the Cubs have been gunning for that position all summer. Giving it up to the Phillies in the final month would not change whether Chicago makes the postseason, but it would change who they face first.
