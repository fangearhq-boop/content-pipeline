### STORY 3: Dylan Cease — AL Cy Young Frontrunner

**FB Long-Form:**
With Tarik Skubal now in Los Angeles, there's a new frontrunner for the AL Cy Young Award — and his case is about as strong as it gets.

Dylan Cease signed with the Blue Jays last December for seven years and $210 million, and he has delivered. His 2026 numbers: 7-5, 2.46 ERA, 167 strikeouts in 113.1 innings. Cease missed time in May with a hamstring issue, but since returning in late June, he has been dominant.

His July ERA was 1.00 — the fourth-lowest single-month ERA in Blue Jays franchise history among pitchers who threw at least 30 innings. He also threw a one-hit complete game against the Red Sox, striking out 12. It was the first Blue Jays one-hit complete game in 16 years.

Toronto is not trading Cease. With Skubal now splitting his season between Detroit and Los Angeles, Cease's full-season consistency with one franchise gives him a compelling story for Cy Young voters. The Blue Jays held their ace — and it's paying off.

### STORY 4: Shohei Ohtani Pitching Return — Still Weeks Away

**FB Long-Form:**

Shohei Ohtani completed a 31-pitch bullpen session over the past few days — a positive step after a July 25 setback that forced him to cancel a scheduled session due to left knee regression. Dodgers pitching coach Mark Prior described the session as proceeding "as usual."

His numbers before the shutdown were remarkable — 1.79 ERA in 14 starts across 85.2 innings, with 95 strikeouts. The Dodgers have not stopped believing in him. ### STORY 5: Chris Sale — Historic Season at 37

**FB Long-Form:**
In a deadline week full of blockbuster trades, Chris Sale's 2026 season keeps getting overlooked — and it deserves your full attention.

At 37 years old, the Atlanta Braves ace is posting one of the most historically significant pitching seasons in a generation. His current line: 12-6, 2.08 ERA, 143 strikeouts, 1.02 WHIP, 117 innings pitched in 20 starts. That ERA is the third-lowest posted by any pitcher age 37 or older since 1950 — behind only Roger Clemens' 1.40 in 2005 and Justin Verlander's 1.73 in 2022.

His last start on July 23 against the Padres: six innings, five hits, three earned runs, one walk, and 11 strikeouts — matching his season high. He is also the fastest pitcher in MLB history to reach 2,500 career strikeouts by innings pitched.

The Braves were buyers at the trade deadline, and Sale is the reason why. At an age when most pitchers are long retired, he remains one of the best starters in the sport. Are you paying enough attention to what Chris Sale is doing in 2026?
