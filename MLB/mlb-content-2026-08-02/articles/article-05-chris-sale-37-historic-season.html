Chris Sale at 37 Is Having One of the Most Historic Pitching Seasons in a Generation

By Jake Torres | August 2, 2026

The 2026 MLB Trade Deadline has consumed most of this week's attention — Skubal to LA, Kirby rumors, the Rutschman question. Meanwhile, in Atlanta, a 37-year-old left-hander has been quietly building one of the most historically significant pitching seasons in a generation.

Chris Sale's 2026 numbers: 12-6, 2.08 ERA, 143 strikeouts, 1.02 WHIP, 117 innings pitched across 20 starts. They are excellent numbers for a pitcher of any age. At 37, they are extraordinary.

The Historical Context

Sale's 2.08 ERA is the third-lowest posted by any pitcher age 37 or older since 1950. The only two performances ahead of him: Roger Clemens' 1.40 ERA in 2005 (at age 42) and Justin Verlander's 1.73 ERA in 2022 (at age 39, his Cy Young season with Houston). In nearly 75 years of modern baseball, only two pitchers who were at least 37 years old have posted a lower ERA over a full season's workload. Sale is now in that conversation.

He is also the fastest pitcher in MLB history to reach 2,500 career strikeouts measured by innings pitched — a mark of pure efficiency that reflects how often he has dominated hitters across a career that has been interrupted but never diminished by injuries.

July and the Run to the Deadline

Sale's most recent start, July 23 against the San Diego Padres, offered a sharp reminder of what he still is. He went six innings, allowing five hits and three earned runs, but struck out 11 — tying his season high. It was a start that mixed imperfection with dominance, which has characterized his 2026 at its most honest: not every outing is pristine, but the strikeouts keep coming and the ERA has held through 20 turns.

The Atlanta Braves were buyers at the trade deadline. Sale was the primary reason. When a front office can point to a 37-year-old ace posting a sub-2.10 ERA as its rotation anchor, it does not sell at the deadline — it adds. The Braves acted accordingly.

The Larger Story

Sale's career has not been a straight line. He has battled injuries across multiple seasons and required Tommy John surgery earlier in his career. The fact that he is performing at this level at 37 is not just impressive — it is the kind of outcome that challenges assumptions about aging curves and pitcher longevity.

His 10th career All-Star selection earlier this season was recognition from his peers and the fans. The historical ERA data puts him in a different category entirely. Whatever Sale does in the remaining weeks of this regular season and in October, 2026 is already one of the more remarkable chapters of his career.

What's Next

Sale will continue to take his regular rotation turn for the Braves through the postseason chase. Atlanta's fortunes in the National League will depend in large part on whether Sale continues to pitch the way he has all summer. If he does, the Braves are a genuine threat — anchored by a pitcher old enough to know exactly how rare what he is doing really is.
