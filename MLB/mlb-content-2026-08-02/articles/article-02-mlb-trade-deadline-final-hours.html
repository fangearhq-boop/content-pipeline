MLB Trade Deadline Final Hours: What's Done, What's Still Moving, and What to Watch

By Jake Torres | August 2, 2026

The 2026 MLB Trade Deadline closes Sunday, August 3 at 6 PM ET — and the market has been anything but quiet. With 24 hours remaining, here is a full accounting of what has been confirmed and what is still in play.

The Confirmed Deals

The headliner is Tarik Skubal, who was officially acquired by the Los Angeles Dodgers from the Detroit Tigers on Saturday. The two-time AL Cy Young winner joins LA in exchange for outfield prospect Zyhir Hope (MLB Pipeline Top 25 overall), right-hander River Ryan, and right-hander Brady Smith. It is the largest deal of the 2026 deadline.

Beyond Skubal, the moves have come in waves. Luis Castillo was dealt from the Seattle Mariners to the Chicago White Sox — a notable swap given that Castillo posted a 3-9 record and 5.06 ERA in 2026, and Chicago absorbed the remainder of his $24.2 million annual salary. The Twins added two arms: right-hander Dean Kremer from the Orioles and left-hander AJ Minter from the Mets. The Pittsburgh Pirates landed closer Camilo Doval from the Yankees. The Milwaukee Brewers picked up catcher Bo Naylor and right-hander Codi Heuer from the Guardians. The Texas Rangers grabbed catcher Logan O'Hoppe and right-hander Chase Silseth from the Angels.

The Names Still Moving

Three significant players remain in active trade discussions heading into the final 24 hours.

George Kirby, Seattle's right-hander, was described as untouchable as recently as early July. That designation changed almost overnight as the Mariners faded out of the Wild Card race. Multiple reports confirmed Kirby is now available, and the market for him figures to be substantial — he's a proven starting pitcher with team control extending beyond this season.

Adley Rutschman in Baltimore is the most intriguing name. The Orioles have described themselves as open-minded on buying or selling, leaving the door open for a surprising move. Rutschman, considered one of the game's premier catchers, would reshape any contender's lineup.

Freddy Peralta of the Mets is also in play. The right-hander is scheduled for a pre-deadline start with scouts in attendance — teams are expected to make decisions based on what they see. Clay Holmes, also a Met, returned from injury and threw four scoreless rehab innings with scouts watching, giving New York another potential chip to move.

What to Watch

The Kirby situation is the one most likely to produce a headline move in the remaining hours. A controllable, premium starting pitcher becoming available this late is unusual, and the teams with rotation needs — several playoff contenders — know this window is narrow. Rutschman is the wildcard; an Orioles sell in the final hours would be one of the more surprising deadline outcomes in recent years.

What's Next

The clock runs out at 6 PM ET on Sunday, August 3. Whatever deals are not completed by then are done for the season — no post-deadline trades are permitted. The teams that moved aggressively before the bell will spend the next two months finding out if their moves hold up in October.
