Shohei Ohtani's Return to the Mound Is Still Weeks Away

By Marcus Cole | August 2, 2026

Shohei Ohtani took a step forward this week, but his return to game action on the mound remains a minimum of two to three weeks away — and possibly longer.

Ohtani completed a 31-pitch bullpen session in recent days, his most significant pitching activity since a July 25 setback forced him to cancel a scheduled session due to regression in his left knee. Dodgers pitching coach Mark Prior described the session as proceeding "as usual" — measured progress, but progress nonetheless.

Manager Dave Roberts, however, was direct about what comes next. He told reporters it will be "at least a week to 10 days" from the bullpen session before Ohtani would be eligible to pitch in a game. He also confirmed Ohtani will not take the mound on the Dodgers' current road trip. Given that timeline, mid-August is the earliest realistic target for his return.

The Injury Picture

Ohtani has been managing two concurrent issues since he last pitched in a game on July 3: soreness in his left knee and irritation in his right biceps. Neither has been described as structurally severe, but the combination has slowed his rehabilitation timeline significantly. The July 25 setback — when knee regression forced the cancellation of what was supposed to be a major bullpen milestone — pushed his expected return back by at least two weeks from where the Dodgers had hoped to be.

As a hitter, Ohtani has continued to produce. He hit his 23rd home run of the season on July 31, maintaining his presence in the lineup while his pitching status remains unresolved. But the Dodgers need the full Ohtani — the two-way player who posted a 1.79 ERA in 14 pitching starts before the shutdown — to truly realize their October ceiling.

The Skubal Connection

The Dodgers' acquisition of Tarik Skubal earlier Saturday exists in direct response to this uncertainty. LA could not enter the postseason relying on Ohtani's arm being available without a contingency. Skubal provides a proven rotation anchor regardless of what Ohtani's arm allows over the next eight weeks.

That is not a concession that Ohtani is done for the year — it is responsible planning for a franchise with championship expectations. If Ohtani returns healthy and pitches in October, the Dodgers become even more dangerous. If he doesn't, Skubal gives them a legitimate path anyway.

What's Next

The Dodgers will continue monitoring Ohtani's progress on a day-by-day basis. The next significant checkpoint will come at the end of the "week to 10 days" window Roberts referenced, when the medical staff will assess whether a game appearance is realistic. Until then, the most valuable thing Ohtani can do for his team is hit — and on that front, he has not missed a beat.
