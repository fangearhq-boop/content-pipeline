Dylan Cease Is the AL Cy Young Frontrunner — and the Numbers Back It Up

By Ryan Calloway | August 2, 2026

With Tarik Skubal now in Los Angeles, the AL Cy Young race has a new frontrunner — and his name has been on the leaderboard all season.

Dylan Cease, the Blue Jays' left-hander who signed the largest free agent contract in franchise history last December (seven years, $210 million), has been one of the best pitchers in baseball in 2026. His current line: 7-5, 2.46 ERA, 167 strikeouts in 113.1 innings pitched. Toronto has made clear it is not trading him. Cease will finish the season in a Blue Jays uniform, and right now, that is very good news for his Cy Young case.

The July That Changed Everything

Cease missed time in May with a hamstring injury and was placed on the 15-day IL before returning in late June. What followed was one of the most dominant stretches of pitching in the American League this season.

His July ERA was 1.00 — the fourth-lowest single-month ERA in Blue Jays franchise history among pitchers who threw at least 30 innings in a single calendar month. The three names ahead of him on that list include Roger Clemens twice (1997 and 1998) and Alex Manoah in September 2022.

The headline game of the month was a one-hit complete game against the Red Sox: nine innings, one hit, zero earned runs, 12 strikeouts. It was the first one-hit complete game by a Blue Jays pitcher in 16 years. On a staff that has seen generational performances, that game stood out.

The Cy Young Case

The argument for Cease comes down to volume, dominance, and continuity. His ERA sits at 2.46 on a full season's worth of work. His 167 strikeouts through 113.1 innings represent a 31 percent-plus strikeout rate in the American League. And unlike Skubal, who will have spent roughly the first half of the season in Detroit and the second half in Los Angeles, Cease will log every start for Toronto.

Cy Young voters have historically rewarded pitchers who spent the full season with one team when the numbers are close. With Skubal changing organizations mid-year and Cease matching or exceeding him statistically, that continuity argument carries real weight. The Blue Jays made the right call in holding their ace, and Cease is repaying them with the best sustained run of his career.

What's Next

Cease will continue his rotation turn for the Blue Jays through the final two months of the regular season. If he maintains anything close to his current pace — and his July suggests he is pitching as well as ever — he will enter the Cy Young voting as the clear frontrunner among AL starters. The award will be decided on the final body of work, and right now, Cease's body of work is the best in the league.
