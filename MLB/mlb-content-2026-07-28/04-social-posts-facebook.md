### STORY 1: Salvador Perez — New Royals All-Time HR Leader

**POST S1-FB-LONGFORM** | 8:00 AM ET
History was made in Kansas City on July 25.

Salvador Perez hit his 318th career home run as a Royal — a solo shot in a win over the Detroit Tigers — and in doing so, passed Hall of Famer George Brett to become the all-time home run leader in Royals franchise history.

Brett's 317 home runs were built over 21 seasons, from 1973 to 1993. He is one of the greatest players in baseball history. And Perez — at 36 years old, in his 14th season, all spent in Kansas City — has now passed him.

Let that sink in for a moment.

Perez has never played for another team. He has spent his entire career in a Royals uniform, battling through injuries, playing in small markets, catching some of the most talented pitching staffs Kansas City has put together. He has never chased a ring somewhere else. He has been, and continues to be, a Royal.

And now he holds the franchise's all-time home run record.

The rest of baseball can take note: Kyle Schwarber leads the majors in 2026 with 32 HR, Yordan Alvarez is second at 31, and Ben Rice is third at 29. But the most meaningful home run story of the 2026 season may have happened quietly on a Saturday in Kansas City.

315 home runs of history. 318 to surpass it. One man, one city.

Congratulations, Salvy. 🎊 #BallparkBanter #Royals #MLB

### STORY 2: Trade Deadline Countdown — 6 Days, Skubal Decision This Week

**POST S2-FB-LONGFORM** | 10:30 AM ET
The August 3 MLB trade deadline is six days away, and the biggest name on the board is about to be decided.

Tarik Skubal — the Detroit Tigers' two-time AL Cy Young winner — has a decision timeline. Per league executives, Detroit will make up their mind by end of the week, Friday at the latest. The Dodgers remain the primary suitor, and if a deal happens, it will happen fast.

But the Tigers might keep him. Detroit is 25-15 since June 1, the fourth-best record in baseball over that stretch. They believe they can win the AL Central. And Skubal — who was openly reluctant to leave last year — hasn't changed his feelings about wanting to finish the job in Detroit. This is a genuine two-way tension, not a foregone conclusion.

Meanwhile, two other names deserve your attention entering decision week:

Foster Griffin (Nationals LHP, 2.68 ERA, 1.03 WHIP, NL All-Star): Multiple teams view his departure as "inevitable." He may be the more likely Nationals move than Abrams.

Mitch Miller (Padres closer, 0.86 ERA, 78 strikeouts in 41.2 innings, club control through 2029): This is the sleeper name. He is not a rental — he is a controllable elite reliever for the next three years. Whoever acquires him is not just adding for August; they're adding for a championship window.

CJ Abrams (Nationals SS, .293/25 HR/79 RBI): The Nationals are listening, but a deal is "not particularly likely."

One deal is already done: the Red Sox acquired infielder Curtis Mead from Washington for pitcher Connelly Early. It was a small move, but it signaled that the Nationals are open to business — and that intensified all the Abrams speculation.

Six days. Watch the Tigers and watch the Nationals. #BallparkBanter #MLB #TradeDeadline

### STORY 3: Ohtani Misses Next Start — Dodgers' Deadline Urgency

**POST S3-FB-LONGFORM** | 12:30 PM ET
Shohei Ohtani will miss his next scheduled start.

It's been seven weeks since he last took the mound — July 3 — and the left knee that has kept him off it isn't ready to go. Dave Roberts confirmed last weekend that Ohtani's knee experienced "regression" when he skipped a scheduled bullpen session on July 26. His assessment: "won't be next week."

What Ohtani won't miss is hitting. He's batting .288/.397/.536 as the Dodgers' DH, and he'll stay in the lineup in that role indefinitely. But the two-way ace who posted an 8-2 record with a 1.79 ERA, 95 strikeouts, and 85.2 innings pitched before his knee flared up is now an unknown quantity as a pitcher.

The Dodgers are choosing not to place him on the injured list — likely to preserve options and avoid a longer shutdown clock — but there is no timetable and no clear path back to the rotation.

Here's the ripple effect: Ohtani's timeline directly shapes how aggressively the Dodgers pursue Tarik Skubal at the deadline. If Ohtani were healthy, the Dodgers' rotation would be fine. With him gone indefinitely, Skubal goes from "nice addition" to "necessary one."

The Cy Young race is increasingly a two-man battle between whoever is healthy and pitching well in September. Ohtani, sitting at 8-2 with a 1.79 ERA before this injury, still leads on numbers — but games are passing him by.

### STORY 4: Detroit Tigers — First Combined No-Hitter in Franchise History

**POST S4-FB-LONGFORM** | 3:00 PM ET
The Detroit Tigers have been playing baseball since 1901. In more than 125 years of franchise history, their pitchers had never — not once — combined to throw a no-hitter.

Until this season.

Three Tigers pitchers teamed up to blank the Toronto Blue Jays, combining for the first combined no-hitter in franchise history. The Blue Jays had zero hits. The Tigers won. And a piece of history that had waited longer than most fans have been alive was finally written.

The Houston Astros also threw a combined no-hitter this season — on May 25, when Tatsuya Imai, Steven Okert, and Alimber Santa combined for a 9-0 win over the Texas Rangers. No-hitters, whether solo or combined, are increasingly rare but still among the most electric moments in baseball.

For the Tigers — a storied franchise with Hall of Famers, World Series championships, and a century of memories — this was a gap in their history that is now permanently closed.

Detroit fans have been waiting a long time for this one. Worth celebrating. ⚾ #BallparkBanter #Tigers #MLB #NoHitter
