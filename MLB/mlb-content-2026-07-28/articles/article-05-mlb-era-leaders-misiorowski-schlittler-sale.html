MLB's Three Best ERAs: Misiorowski, Schlittler, and Sale — The Names Leading the Pitching Race

By Jake Torres

July 28, 2026

Ask a casual baseball fan to name the MLB ERA leader entering the final stretch of July and most of them will give you a wrong answer. The man at the top is not a perennial contender, a household name, or anyone who appeared in last year's postseason spotlight.

The top three ERA leaders in baseball right now are Jacob Misiorowski (1.58), Cam Schlittler (2.07), and Chris Sale (2.19). Two of those three names may not be instantly familiar — and that is exactly what makes this one of the more interesting stories in baseball right now.

No. 1: Jacob Misiorowski, 1.58 ERA

Misiorowski tops the list with the best ERA of any starting pitcher in the majors at 1.58. His fastball has been clocked at 105.5 mph — one of the fastest ever recorded from a starting pitcher, tying Ben Joyce for third all-time. The combination of elite velocity and an ability to miss bats has translated into a statistical profile that very few pitchers in the game can match.

He is not a name that most fans encountered during the winter. He will be a name they know well by the playoffs.

No. 2: Cam Schlittler, 2.07 ERA

Schlittler sits in second place at 2.07, and his profile mirrors the trajectory of the pitching analytics era: developing under the radar, refining command and pitch mix, and arriving as a finished product that is harder to hit than most batters expect. He is consistent, durable, and efficient — the kinds of qualities that produce a 2.07 ERA over a full season.

Like Misiorowski, he is not yet a nationally famous name. Like Misiorowski, that will change if this continues.

No. 3: Chris Sale, 2.19 ERA

Sale is the name most fans will recognize — the Atlanta Braves left-hander who has been one of the most talented pitchers of his generation when healthy. He is starting tonight against the New York Mets and arrives with his best statistics in recent memory: a 2.19 ERA that ranks third in the majors and a continued ability to generate swing-and-miss with a delivery that still features one of the most unusual arm angles in the game.

Sale has always been in this conversation when he has stayed on the mound. The 2026 version of him is exactly that.

Around the Majors — July 27

The Boston Red Sox defeated the Oakland Athletics 4-2 in Sacramento on Monday, continuing Boston's strong second-half pace. The Houston Astros beat the Los Angeles Angels 6-4 at Angel Stadium, keeping pace in the AL West. And in South Florida, the Miami Marlins defeated the Philadelphia Phillies at loanDepot Park, with Miami's outfielder Lopez going 3-for-5 in a strong individual performance.

One player to watch this week: Julio Rodriguez of the Seattle Mariners. The center fielder has been out since July 3 with a concussion, and he is projected to return to the lineup soon. Rodriguez is one of the most dynamic players in the American League, and his return adds significant lineup depth to a Mariners team still chasing a Wild Card spot.

What's Next

Sale's start tonight is one of the better pitching matchups of the week — a veteran ace with a 2.19 ERA against a Mets lineup that has been productive in 2026. The ERA leaderboard may shift slightly depending on how he fares, but the conversation around Misiorowski and Schlittler will only grow louder as the season heads into its final two months.

Baseball has a way of surfacing pitching stories that no one saw coming. This is one of them, and it is worth paying attention to.
