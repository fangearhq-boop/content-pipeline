Ohtani to Miss Next Start: Left Knee Regression Raises Dodgers' Deadline Stakes

By Ryan Calloway

July 28, 2026

Shohei Ohtani will miss his next scheduled start, and the Los Angeles Dodgers are entering the August 3 trade deadline without a clear timetable for getting him back on the mound.

Ohtani's left knee — which prompted him to skip a scheduled bullpen session on July 26 — has not responded as the organization had hoped. Manager Dave Roberts described the development as "regression," and his assessment of the timeline is simple: "won't be next week." The two-way ace last pitched on July 3, when he carried a season record of 8-2, a 1.79 ERA, 95 strikeouts, and 85.2 innings pitched into what appeared to be a brief pause.

That pause has now stretched to 25 days with no end in sight.

What the Injury Means

Ohtani is not on the injured list, and the Dodgers have not placed him there. The likely reason: keeping him active preserves roster flexibility, avoids triggering a mandatory 10- or 15-day clock, and allows the organization to return him as soon as the knee is ready without bureaucratic constraint. The Dodgers say they expect him to pitch again in 2026 — they are not rushing it.

What he can do is hit. Ohtani continues to serve as the Dodgers' designated hitter, and he is doing that extremely well: .288 batting average, .397 on-base percentage, .536 slugging percentage. The offensive version of Ohtani remains one of the most dangerous hitters in the National League. The loss is the version that takes the ball every fifth day.

For the Cy Young race, the injury is a slow-motion problem. Ohtani's numbers before the knee flared — 8-2, 1.79 ERA, 95 strikeouts — were the kind that win awards. But the award is given based on a full season, and he is now watching from the DH spot as other starters accumulate wins, innings, and accolades he cannot add to his total.

The Deadline Consequence

The most immediate consequence of Ohtani's injury is not statistical — it is strategic. The Dodgers are one of baseball's best teams with a healthy Ohtani headlining the rotation. Without him throwing, they are missing their best pitcher, and the gap between their rotation depth and that of the other championship-caliber teams grows with each missed start.

That gap makes Tarik Skubal more necessary. The Detroit Tigers' two-time Cy Young winner is the best available starter on the trade market, and executives say the Tigers will make a decision on him by the end of this week. With Ohtani missing indefinitely, the Dodgers have gone from interested suitor to urgent one. The market for Skubal is about to intensify, and Los Angeles is positioned to push harder than perhaps any other team.

What's Next

Ohtani will continue hitting and remain in the Dodgers' lineup every day the knee allows. When and whether he returns to the mound in 2026 depends on the left knee's response to treatment — and so far, that response has been slower than anyone hoped.

The next meaningful marker will be a bullpen session. When Roberts announces that Ohtani is throwing off a mound again, that will signal progress. Until then, he remains a DH with no pitching timeline — and the Dodgers remain in the market for the rotation piece they suddenly need more than ever.
