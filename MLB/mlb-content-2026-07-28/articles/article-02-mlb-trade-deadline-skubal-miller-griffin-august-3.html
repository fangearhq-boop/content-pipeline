MLB Trade Deadline: Six Days Out, Skubal Decision Week, and the Sleeper Name Nobody Is Talking About

By Jake Torres

July 28, 2026

The August 3 MLB trade deadline is six days away, and the shape of this market is coming into focus. The biggest name — Tarik Skubal — has a decision timeline. The most interesting name — Mitch Miller — is flying under the radar. And the most compelling subplot involves a team that may need to buy harder than anyone expected.

Skubal: Decision by Friday

For weeks, the Skubal question has dominated deadline coverage: will the Detroit Tigers trade their two-time AL Cy Young winner, or will they hold? Executives around the league now say that question will be answered by the end of this week — Friday at the latest.

The Los Angeles Dodgers remain the primary suitor. They have the prospect capital to meet Detroit's asking price, and with Shohei Ohtani's return to the mound in question following a left knee setback, they have a pressing reason to add a frontline starter.

The case for keeping him is equally real. Detroit is 25-15 since June 1 — the fourth-best record in baseball over that stretch. The Tigers believe they can win the AL Central, and their players have not been shy about preferring to stay together. Skubal himself has made no secret of his desire to remain in Detroit. If the front office decides the window is real, he stays.

By Friday, we will know the answer.

The Nationals: Griffin Over Abrams?

Washington has already made one move — dealing pitcher Connelly Early to the Red Sox for infielder Curtis Mead — and that transaction triggered immediate speculation about a larger deal. CJ Abrams, the Nationals' shortstop, has emerged as the most-discussed name: .293 batting average, 25 home runs, 79 RBI, 111 hits, and a second All-Star appearance this season.

But people close to the situation describe an Abrams trade as "not particularly likely." The Nationals, exceeding expectations at 52-52, are not yet convinced they should blow up their foundation. The Yankees and Brewers have made inquiries; nothing has materialized.

Foster Griffin, Washington's NL All-Star left-hander, is a different story. At 2.68 ERA with a 1.03 WHIP, he is one of the best available starting pitchers on the market — and multiple teams view his departure as "inevitable." If the Nationals make a second significant deal before Monday, Griffin may be the name attached to it.

The Name Nobody Is Talking About: Mitch Miller

While Skubal dominates the headlines, the most interesting potential deal of this deadline involves San Diego Padres closer Mitch Miller. The numbers are extraordinary: 0.86 ERA, 78 strikeouts in 41.2 innings. He is not a setup man — he is a dominant closer who has been essentially untouchable in 2026.

What makes Miller different from a typical deadline acquisition is what he is not: a rental. Miller has club control through 2029. Whoever acquires him is not paying for eight to ten appearances in August and a September run. They are adding a cornerstone closer for the next three years of a championship window. That dynamic makes him a fundamentally different kind of deadline piece.

Miller's asking price from San Diego will be steep precisely because of that value. But for a team with the prospects and the window to support it — the Yankees, the Dodgers, the Braves — Miller may be the best return on investment available at this deadline.

What's Next

Six days is a long time in baseball terms and a very short time when franchises are deciding to trade cornerstone players. The Skubal situation should clarify by Friday. The Griffin situation could move at any moment. And the Miller situation may arrive quietly and change everything for the team bold enough to pay the price.

The deadline is Monday, August 3, at 6 PM ET. Between now and then, this is the most interesting week of the baseball calendar — and this market is about to get very interesting very fast.
