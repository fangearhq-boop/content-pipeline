Detroit Tigers Throw First Combined No-Hitter in Franchise History Against Blue Jays

By Marcus Cole

July 28, 2026

The Detroit Tigers have been playing professional baseball since 1901. Through 125 years of franchise history, multiple World Series championships, Hall of Famers on both sides of the ball, and more than 19,000 games played, their pitchers had never combined to throw a no-hitter.

In 2026, that changed. Three Tigers pitchers combined to blank the Toronto Blue Jays, delivering the first combined no-hitter in franchise history and marking one of the most significant individual milestones the storied franchise had not yet checked off its list.

The Weight of Franchise Firsts

A combined no-hitter requires not just one pitcher to be dominant — it requires multiple pitchers to carry forward something that began before they took the ball. The baton passes from starter to reliever to closer with the zero on the scoreboard as the only thing that must not change. When the final out is recorded and the no-hitter is preserved, every pitcher who touched it shares in the moment.

For the Tigers, that moment arrived against the Blue Jays. Three pitchers combined to retire 27 batters without allowing a hit — a feat their predecessors across 125 seasons had never managed to accomplish together. The franchise that once featured Hall of Famers like Hal Newhouser, Mickey Lolich, Jack Morris, and Justin Verlander — pitchers who threw plenty of near-misses and brilliant performances — had never put this specific combination of circumstances together for a full game.

Until now.

No-Hitters in 2026

The Tigers' gem is the second combined no-hitter in the 2026 MLB season. On May 25, the Houston Astros' Tatsuya Imai, Steven Okert, and Alimber Santa combined to no-hit the Texas Rangers, 9-0 — a dominant, clean performance against a division rival. The Astros' no-hitter was built on a strong start from Imai and supported by two efficient relievers who preserved the gem through the late innings.

No perfect game has been thrown in 2026 — the most recent was Domingo Germán's in 2023. But combined no-hitters have become an increasingly common form of the milestone in the modern era, as specialization in bullpen usage has created new pathways to history for teams willing to manage aggressively from the first inning.

The Tigers' 2026 Context

Detroit enters the stretch run in a genuinely competitive position. The Tigers are 25-15 since June 1, one of the best records in baseball over that stretch, and they are positioned as buyers rather than sellers at the August 3 trade deadline. Management is deciding whether to add to the roster or hold what they have — but the no-hitter is a reminder of what this pitching staff is capable of when everything clicks.

Tarik Skubal remains the anchor of the rotation and the most discussed name at the trade deadline. His potential departure — or staying — defines Detroit's ambitions for the second half. But the combined no-hitter belongs to the whole staff, a monument to what the Tigers can do when the right pitchers step up on the right day.

What's Next

The no-hitter will not change the trajectory of the 2026 season for either team. The Blue Jays will come back tomorrow and try again. The Tigers will carry on, with the deadline looming and the AL Central within reach.

But franchise firsts do not come around often. In 125 years, the Tigers had never done this. Now they have. That is the kind of thing that gets framed on a wall — and remembered long after the season is over.
