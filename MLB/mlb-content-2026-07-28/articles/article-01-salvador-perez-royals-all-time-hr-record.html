Salvador Perez Passes George Brett to Become the Royals' All-Time Home Run Leader

By Marcus Cole

July 28, 2026

George Brett's home run record as a Kansas City Royal — 317, accumulated over 21 seasons and one of the most celebrated careers in franchise history — stood for more than three decades. On July 25, 2026, it fell.

Salvador Perez hit his 318th career home run as a Royal that day — a solo shot in a win over the Detroit Tigers — to pass Brett and claim sole possession of the franchise's all-time home run record. At 36 years old and in his 14th MLB season — all of them spent in Kansas City — Perez has now written his name at the top of the franchise's most prestigious offensive list.

What Brett Built

George Brett needs no lengthy introduction in Kansas City. One of the greatest players in baseball history, Brett played 21 seasons for the Royals — from 1973 to 1993 — and compiled a career that included a .305 batting average, 3,154 hits, 317 home runs, and a Hall of Fame plaque in Cooperstown. His 1980 season, in which he hit .390 and nearly became the first .400 hitter since Ted Williams, remains one of the most iconic individual campaigns in modern baseball history.

The 317 home runs he hit as a Royal were never the defining number of his legacy — Brett was a line drive hitter, a contact machine, not a power hitter in the traditional sense. But that record stood for over 30 years, and the players who came after him simply could not surpass it.

What Perez Has Done

Perez arrived in Kansas City as a wide-eyed catcher from Venezuela and became something the sport rarely produces: a franchise player who never left. In 14 seasons — through rebuilds and contention windows, through All-Star seasons and injured ones — he stayed. He caught two Cy Young winners. He helped deliver a World Series championship in 2015. He was named World Series MVP that year. And he kept hitting.

Perez has never been primarily a power hitter either, but he hits the ball hard, and when he catches one cleanly, it travels. His 318 home runs as a Royal came in 14 seasons — seven fewer than it took Brett to reach 317. At 36, with no indication he plans to stop, the record he now holds may grow for years to come.

MLB's 2026 Home Run Context

The broader 2026 home run leaderboard provides some scale for Perez's achievement. Kyle Schwarber leads all of baseball with 32 home runs in 2026, with Yordan Alvarez second at 31 and Ben Rice third at 29. These are the men chasing the season's big power numbers.

Perez's record is not a 2026 story — it is a career story. It is the story of what 14 seasons of consistency, loyalty, and quiet production looks like when you add it all up.

What's Next

Perez will keep playing, keep catching, and almost certainly keep hitting home runs. His chase now is against himself — and against the history he is still writing. George Brett's overall franchise legacy is undimmed by this milestone; his career achievements extend far beyond any single statistic.

But for Royals fans who have watched Perez grow from prospect to franchise cornerstone to living legend, July 25 was the kind of moment that doesn't come often. The man who has given everything to Kansas City now holds one of its most meaningful records. No Royal has ever hit more home runs in a Royals uniform. For now, no one else is close.
