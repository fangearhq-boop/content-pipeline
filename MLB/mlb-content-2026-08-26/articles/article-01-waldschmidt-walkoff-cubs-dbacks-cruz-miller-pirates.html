Waldschmidt's Walkoff and Cruz's Gem: Two Stunners From Tuesday Night

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | August 26, 2026

Tuesday night produced two of the more memorable moments of the 2026 season in back-to-back fashion — a pinch-hit walkoff home run that extended Arizona's already remarkable comeback tradition, and a solo shot in Pittsburgh that ended one of the more quietly impressive streaks a relief pitcher has put together this year. Both results carry postseason implications. Both were worth watching twice.

Waldschmidt Delivers Arizona's 40th Comeback Win

The Diamondbacks entered the ninth inning in Chicago trailing the Cubs by one run. Ryan Waldschmidt came off the bench with two outs, worked a count, and hit a two-run home run that put Arizona ahead for good. Diamondbacks 5, Cubs 4. Walk off. It was Arizona's 40th comeback victory of the 2026 season — the most in Major League Baseball.

Forty comeback wins in one season is a remarkable figure. It means that nearly one-quarter of Arizona's wins this year have come when the team was losing and found a way back. The Diamondbacks have built a culture of late-game resilience that shows no sign of abating as the season enters its final month. Whether that translates to postseason success remains to be seen — but it makes them one of the most dangerous teams in the NL to face with the game on the line.

For the Cubs, the loss was a setback in a week they needed to be clean. Chicago dropped to 76-57, and the NL Wild Card race behind them — particularly a surging Philadelphia Phillies team on a four-game winning streak — is not giving them room to breathe. Waldschmidt's ball left the park at exactly the wrong time.

Cruz Ends Miller's HR-Free Streak in Pittsburgh

Across the country, Pittsburgh was hosting San Diego in a matchup that had Wild Card implications for the Padres. Paul Skenes was sharp on the mound in what was described as his best outing in weeks. And then Oneil Cruz stepped in against Mason Miller in the ninth and ended something that had been building all season.

Cruz hit a solo home run off Miller — the first home run the Padres closer had allowed in more than a year. Miller had been essentially untouchable out of San Diego's bullpen for an extended stretch, building a reputation as one of the most reliable late-game options in the National League. Cruz's drive changed that narrative in a single swing. The Pirates won 1-0. Skenes got the credit. Cruz got the headlines.

For San Diego, the result was doubly painful. Not only did they lose a game they needed in the Wild Card race, but their most reliable reliever gave up a run. The Padres sit at 71-61 — still in the third Wild Card spot, but the Diamondbacks are right behind them and gaining ground.

The Postseason Implications

Both results matter beyond individual box scores. The Diamondbacks are now threatening to knock the Padres out of the Wild Card picture entirely while simultaneously damaging the Cubs' position at the top of the bracket. The Pirates, nominally out of playoff contention, just beat the team in front of them and handed the Padres a loss they cannot easily absorb.

August in baseball operates like this. Teams that are not going anywhere play spoiler. Backups like Waldschmidt come off the bench and become the story. Closers with long scoreless streaks give up the pivotal home run. The standings are shaped by the unexpected as much as by the expected.
