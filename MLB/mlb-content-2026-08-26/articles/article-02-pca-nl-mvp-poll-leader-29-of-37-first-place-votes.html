Pete Crow-Armstrong Now Leads the NL MVP Poll — And It's Not Close

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | August 26, 2026

The fifth 2026 MLB.com NL MVP poll is out, and for the first time all season, Pete Crow-Armstrong holds the top spot. He received 29 of 37 first-place votes and 177 total points, pulling ahead of Shohei Ohtani in the balloting. This is not a narrow lead built on tiebreakers. Twenty-nine of thirty-seven voters put him first. The question is no longer whether Crow-Armstrong is in the conversation. It is whether Ohtani can catch him.

What the Numbers Say

Pete Crow-Armstrong leads all players in baseball with 7.7 fWAR. His season line reads .281/.379/.550 with 30 home runs, 78 RBIs, and 31 stolen bases. Since July 3, he has posted a 171 wRC+, a 1.014 OPS, and 14 home runs — a pace that would rank among the best second-half stretches any hitter has produced this decade.

That is the offensive case. The defensive case is equally strong. Crow-Armstrong leads the majors with 22-plus Outs Above Average in center field. He is not just an above-average outfielder — he is the best defensive center fielder in baseball this season, and it is not particularly close. The combination of elite bat and elite glove is why 7.7 fWAR looks the way it does. Value accumulates when you are exceptional in multiple areas simultaneously.

Back-to-Back 30-30 — First in Cubs History

Crow-Armstrong's 30th stolen base earlier this summer made him the first Cub in franchise history to record back-to-back 30 home run, 30 stolen base seasons. That accomplishment existed quietly amid the volume of what he does — but it contextualizes just how complete a player he has become. Power, speed, defense, on-base ability. Every tool, deployed consistently, across a full season.

The back-to-back 30-30 also places him in rare company historically. Multiple seasons of elite two-way production separate the legitimately great from the statistically fortunate. Crow-Armstrong, through the end of August, belongs in the former category.

The Ohtani Comparison

Shohei Ohtani has been one of the best players in baseball in 2026. That is the baseline. But the MVP race asks which player has been the most valuable — and the gap in fWAR between Crow-Armstrong (7.7) and the field is meaningful. Ohtani's value comes through both pitching and hitting in his two-way role. Crow-Armstrong's comes from being elite in every phase of his position. The voters, in the fifth poll, found Crow-Armstrong's argument more compelling by a 29-to-8 margin.

The Cubs fell to 76-57 Tuesday night after the Diamondbacks' walkoff win. Approximately 29 games remain. The MVP award will be voted on after the regular season concludes. What Crow-Armstrong does between now and the end of September will determine whether he holds this position or whether Ohtani — or someone else — closes the gap.
