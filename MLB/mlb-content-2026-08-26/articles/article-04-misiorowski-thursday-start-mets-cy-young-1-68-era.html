Misiorowski Faces the Mets Thursday. His Cy Young Lead Is Already Historic.

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | August 26, 2026

Jacob Misiorowski will take the mound Thursday against the New York Mets with a 1.68 ERA, a 13-5 record, 216 strikeouts, and a 0.76 WHIP through 24 starts. He is 24 years old, is the -550 favorite for the National League Cy Young Award, and became earlier this season only the second-fastest pitcher in Major League Baseball history to reach 200 strikeouts in a single campaign. Thursday's matchup is the next chapter in what is already one of the most remarkable pitching seasons anyone has produced in recent years.

What Makes the 1.68 ERA Sustainable

A 1.68 ERA in the modern run environment is legitimately unusual. At that ERA, a starter is allowing fewer than two earned runs every nine innings across a significant number of starts. The expected question — is this sustainable? — has been answered by the calendar. Misiorowski is through 24 starts. He has pitched through opposing lineups a second and third time. He has pitched in the heat of the summer stretch when fatigue and familiarity typically cause ERAs to climb. The number has not moved.

Part of the answer is the strikeout rate. With 216 strikeouts in 24 starts, Misiorowski is averaging more than nine per outing. Strikeouts eliminate the variance that puts balls in play — the bloop single, the ground ball that finds a hole, the routine fly ball that a gusty day turns into a double. At his strikeout rate, Misiorowski is reducing his exposure to the noise of the game in a way that helps the ERA stay clean.

The second part is the WHIP. A 0.76 WHIP means Misiorowski is allowing less than one baserunner per inning. Baserunners eventually score. If you limit baserunners, you limit runs. The WHIP and the ERA are telling the same story from different angles: this is a pitcher who is not giving anything away.

The Historical Company He's Keeping

Becoming the second-fastest pitcher in MLB history to 200 strikeouts in a season puts Misiorowski in the kind of company where the names are recognizable even to casual fans. The fastest pitchers to 200 strikeouts in a season — the names at the top of that list — are players who defined their era on the mound. Misiorowski joined that list at 24, in his first full season as a rotation anchor, on a Brewers team that has the best record in the National League at 81-49.

The Cy Young odds (-550) reflect the consensus across the betting market that the award is essentially decided. No other NL pitcher is within a standard deviation of his ERA. The question for Thursday's start against the Mets is not whether Misiorowski wins the Cy Young — it is whether he can keep pushing the statistical ceiling before the regular season ends.
