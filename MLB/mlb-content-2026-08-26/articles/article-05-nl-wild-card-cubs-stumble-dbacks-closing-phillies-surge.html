The D-backs Are Closing In. The NL Wild Card Race Is Getting Interesting.

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | August 26, 2026

The National League Wild Card race entered Wednesday morning in a different shape than it was in 24 hours earlier. The Cubs lost to the Diamondbacks Tuesday night — Waldschmidt's walkoff homer, Arizona's 40th comeback win — and dropped to 76-57. The Phillies are surging on a four-game winning streak. The Padres are holding the third spot at 71-61, but Arizona is right outside the cutoff and has been the hottest team in baseball when it comes to finding ways to win late. Twenty-nine games remain. Nothing is settled.

The Current Picture

The three NL Wild Card spots, entering Wednesday, belong to the Cubs (76-57, No. 1), the Phillies (73-59, No. 2), and the Padres (71-61, No. 3). The Diamondbacks are approximately one game behind the Padres for the third spot. With 29 games remaining, a one-game deficit for a team that has won 40 comeback games is not a deficit that generates comfort among the teams ahead of it.

The Phillies' four-game winning streak has moved them within three games of the Cubs in the Wild Card standings. That matters for seeding — which is the actual prize being contested among the NL Wild Card teams at this point. All three teams are almost certainly going to the playoffs. The question is who gets the best draw.

Why Seeding Matters for the Cubs

The Chicago Cubs' primary goal in the Wild Card race is not making the postseason — they are 76-57, and barring a historic collapse, they will be there. The goal is finishing as the top NL Wild Card seed and avoiding a Round 1 matchup against the Milwaukee Brewers, who have the best record in the National League at 81-49.

The Wild Card bracket in its current format pairs the top seeds against the bottom seeds in the first round. A Cubs team that enters the postseason as the No. 1 Wild Card avoids the Brewers until at least the Division Series. That is a meaningful strategic advantage given how Milwaukee has pitched this year and what Misiorowski specifically does to opposing lineups. No Wild Card team wants to see him in a short series in October.

Tuesday's loss does not cost Chicago that seeding — not yet. But the cushion between the Cubs and the Phillies has compressed. Every game the Phillies win and Chicago loses is a step toward a potential bracket reshuffle.

The Diamondbacks Factor

Arizona's 40 comeback wins are not a fluke. They reflect something about how the Diamondbacks play late-game situations — willingness to turn over roster flexibility to position players who can impact a single game, strong late-inning usage patterns, and a culture of competing in moments when other teams fold. Whether that translates into an actual playoff berth depends on whether they can get past San Diego over the next 29 games. They have the momentum and the formula. San Diego has the current record.

What's Next

The Cubs and Diamondbacks continue their series into midweek. The Padres face their next series against a team competing for their own positioning. Updated Wild Card standings available daily at mlb.com and baseball-reference.com. With 29 games left, no outcome from Tuesday's night is permanent.
