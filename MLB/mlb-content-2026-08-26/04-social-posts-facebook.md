One night. Two wild finishes. That's August baseball.

### STORY 3: Yordan Alvarez Triple Crown — .320/36/90 with Caminero Closing

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 12:30 PM ET

The last player to win the Triple Crown in Major League Baseball was Miguel Cabrera in 2012. Yordan Alvarez is trying to become the next.

Alvarez leads the American League in batting average (.320), home runs (36), and RBIs (90). The batting average lead is comfortable — Chandler Simpson (TB, .310) and Yandy Díaz (TB, .306) are the next-closest in the AL. The RBI lead is safe — Pete Alonso (BAL) has 85, five behind. The home run lead is the problem.

Tampa Bay's Junior Caminero has 35 home runs. Ben Rice of the Yankees has 34. The margin between Alvarez and Caminero is one home run. With approximately 29 games left in the regular season, that lead can evaporate in a single night.

The Triple Crown is won or lost in the final month. Everything else — the batting average, the RBIs — tends to be decided by how a hitter performs in September. Alvarez is one of the best September performers in the game. He's also on a team, the Houston Astros, that is managing his workload carefully. The race is real. The historical stakes are real. Cabrera was the last one to do it, and that was 14 years ago.

#### Long-Form Post

Jacob Misiorowski is 13-5 with a 1.68 ERA, 216 strikeouts, and a 0.76 WHIP through 24 starts. He is 24 years old. He is the shortest-priced Cy Young favorite in the National League at -550. And on Thursday, he takes the mound against the New York Mets.

Every Misiorowski start is worth watching at this point. He became only the second-fastest pitcher in Major League Baseball history to reach 200 strikeouts in a single season. His ERA has been under 2.00 for most of the second half. His WHIP would be historic at any point in the modern era.

What makes him particularly difficult for hitters is the combination of raw stuff — a fastball that generates elite vertical movement — and improved command of his secondary pitches. Early in his career, Misiorowski was a pitcher who overpowered hitters. Now he is a pitcher who can also outwit them. That development is what makes the 1.68 ERA sustainable rather than fluky.

Thursday's start against the Mets will be another opportunity for Misiorowski to add to an already historic statistical line. Whether he wins or loses, the innings and strikeout totals will tell a story. The Brewers are 81-49. Their ace is the best pitcher in the National League. That is not a coincidence.

Twenty-nine games remain. None of them are exhibitions.
