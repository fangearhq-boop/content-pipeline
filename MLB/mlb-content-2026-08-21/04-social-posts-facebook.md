### STORY 2: Baez Returns, Cardinals Decision Looms

**Long-Form Post (10:30 AM ET)**
Joshua Baez is back in the Cardinals lineup after Thursday's rest — and Oliver Marmol still has a decision to make.

What's the right role for a 21-year-old who hit three home runs in his first three career at-bats? Who posted a 449-foot blast on his first MLB swing? Who is batting .251/.332/.552 with 12 home runs in his young career?

Here's the tension:

The .552 slugging percentage says cleanup hitter. Put this bat in the middle of the order where it can do the most damage. Don't waste a plus-plus power threat at leadoff.

But Marmol has already tried him there. He moved Baez to leadoff while resting JJ Wetherholt, giving the young outfielder a look at the top of the lineup. It didn't stick — but the experiment told us something. Marmol is actively figuring this out.

The 32.7% strikeout rate is the number that makes the leadoff case harder. Elite leadoff hitters take pitches. They work counts. They get on base above their batting average. Baez is capable of all of that — but not consistently yet, not with 12 career homers under his belt.

The Cardinals have 35 games left to get this right. With Baez, getting it right could change everything. 🔴

The last Triple Crown winner was Miguel Cabrera in 2012 — 14 years ago. No one has led the league in batting average, home runs, and RBI simultaneously since then.

Here's where Alvarez stands:

Batting Average: .329. He leads the AL by approximately 29 points. This is the most comfortable of the three categories — a lead that would require a significant cold stretch to lose in 35 games.

Home Runs: 36. Kyle Schwarber has 37 — one ahead. This is the tightest category and the hardest to project. Both hitters can go on a streak. Both can go cold. Alvarez needs to stay hot while Schwarber cools.

RBI: 88. Alvarez leads by a comfortable margin. The Astros are still playing meaningful games, and Alvarez will see RBI opportunities. This category is his to keep.

The math says: keep the BA where it is, hit 5-8 more home runs, and he's there. That's not a certainty — but it's not a fantasy either. The player who last won a Triple Crown did it in the modern era. Alvarez is right there. ⚾
