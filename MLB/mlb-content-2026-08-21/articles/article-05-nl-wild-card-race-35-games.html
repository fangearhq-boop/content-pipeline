The NL Wild Card Race With 35 Games Left: Cubs Lead, Phillies and Padres in Pursuit

By Ryan Calloway | Ballpark Banter | August 21, 2026

The National League Wild Card race has 35 games left and a clear top team — but the gap between the Cubs and the teams chasing them is small enough to keep the stretch run genuinely interesting. What is most compelling about this race is not whether anyone gets in, but who gets in where — because seeding determines first-round opponents, and the first-round opponent that everyone wants to avoid is Milwaukee.

The Current Standings

The Chicago Cubs sit at 73-53, holding the No. 1 Wild Card spot in the National League. They lead the Philadelphia Phillies (68-58) by five games and the San Diego Padres (67-59) by six.

Three Wild Card spots are available in the current playoff format. By the raw standings, all three of these teams could theoretically end the season inside the playoff field — though the math gets tighter by September. Neither the Phillies nor the Padres are playing for their lives yet, but neither can afford a long losing streak.

Below those three teams, other clubs are watching. The NL race has a realistic cap: if the Cubs, Phillies, and Padres all stay healthy and produce at their current rates, the three Wild Card spots fill themselves. But "if" is doing a lot of work in October baseball.

Why Seeding Matters More Than Getting In

The Milwaukee Brewers have the best record in baseball at 78-48. They won one game 22-0. They have the NL Cy Young frontrunner in Jacob Misiorowski. They are playing at American Family Field, where they will have homefield advantage in the NL bracket.

No Wild Card team wants to face the Brewers in a first-round series. The Wild Card round is a best-of-three — meaning two wins against the best team in baseball, on the road, to advance. The math does not favor that outcome.

The No. 1 Wild Card seed avoids the top NL division winner in the first round. If the Brewers hold the NL's best record — and at 78-48, they currently do — the No. 1 WC team plays somebody else in Round 1. The Cubs, sitting at No. 1, have everything to fight for in the seeding battle.

This is why every Cubs game over the next 35 days matters more than just staying in. It matters specifically because of who is waiting at the top of the bracket.

The NL Division Picture

For context on the full NL landscape: the Brewers (78-48) lead the NL Central by a substantial margin. The Braves (74-53) hold the NL East. The Dodgers (76-51) lead the NL West. All three have their division titles largely in hand. The NL wild card bracket shapes up around those three teams at the top of each division.

In the American League, the Rays (75-50) lead the East, the White Sox (66-60) hold the Central lead, and the Astros (63-63) have had a disappointing season despite Alvarez's historic individual performance. The AL playoff picture has its own drama, but the NL Wild Card race is where the daily tension is sharpest right now.

What's Next

The Cubs need to protect their lead over the next 35 games while the Phillies and Padres push. Every three-game series swing matters — a Cubs series loss combined with a Phillies series win closes the gap by potentially three games in three days. The seeding conversation intensifies as September arrives. For now, the Cubs hold the position they want. They need to keep it.
