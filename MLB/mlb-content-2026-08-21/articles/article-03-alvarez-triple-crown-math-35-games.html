Yordan Alvarez and the Triple Crown Math: What He Needs in 35 Games to Make History

By Ryan Calloway | Ballpark Banter | August 21, 2026

The last time a player won the Triple Crown in Major League Baseball, Barack Obama was in the White House and the iPhone had been around for only five years. Miguel Cabrera's 2012 season stands as the most recent example of what Yordan Alvarez is now attempting — leading the league simultaneously in batting average, home runs, and RBI.

With 35 games remaining, Alvarez's grip on each category is different. One is commanding. One is comfortable but fragile. And one is a genuine battle against a competitor who has been the hottest power hitter in baseball.

Batting Average: .329 and Comfortable

Alvarez leads the American League in batting average at .329 — approximately 29 points ahead of his nearest competition. This is the most secure of his three leads. To lose this category with 35 games remaining would require a cold streak of historic proportions. Alvarez has shown no signs of one. He has been one of the most consistent hitters in baseball this season, and his approach at the plate — aggressive against hittable pitches, disciplined against waste pitches — has not wavered.

The batting average is essentially his. Barring something unexpected, he finishes with the Crown's first jewel.

RBI: 88 and Leading Comfortably

The RBI category is also in solid position. At 88, Alvarez leads the AL by a substantial margin. The Houston Astros continue to play meaningful games, which means Alvarez will have runners on base and RBI opportunities through September. He does not need a historic final stretch — just consistency.

The RBI title belongs to Alvarez unless he suffers a significant injury or endures a month-long drought at the plate. Neither scenario has any current precedent in his 2026 performance.

Home Runs: 36, Trailing Schwarber by One

This is the category that determines everything. Alvarez has 36 home runs. Kyle Schwarber sits at 37 — one ahead. Schwarber has been on a power surge that Alvarez has not matched in August. Also in the picture: Caminero, who has been hitting home runs at a pace that keeps him relevant in the low-to-mid 30s.

The math is straightforward but unforgiving. Alvarez needs to outpace Schwarber over the final 35 games. That means hitting home runs when Schwarber does not, or hitting more home runs when both are producing. A five-game home run streak from Alvarez while Schwarber goes quiet would flip the category back. A five-game surge from Schwarber while Alvarez is cold would widen the gap to insurmountable territory.

There is no passive path here. The HR title requires active performance — games where the ball is getting to the seats.

The Historical Weight of the Category

Cabrera's 2012 Triple Crown came after 45 years of near-misses and close calls. The category requires three simultaneous peaks across three different statistical dimensions, each of which can deteriorate independently. Being close in all three — which Alvarez clearly is — does not guarantee finishing first in all three. The history of near-Triple Crowns is longer than the history of actual Triple Crowns.

What Alvarez has in his favor is the strength of two categories and a very manageable gap in the third. This is a realistic bid. It is not a guarantee. The final five weeks of this season will decide something that has not been decided since 2012.

What's Next

The next 35 games are the Triple Crown race. Alvarez holds two categories with commanding leads and trails in one by a single home run. Watch his home run production against Schwarber's daily. That gap — one home run — is the number that matters most between now and the end of September.
