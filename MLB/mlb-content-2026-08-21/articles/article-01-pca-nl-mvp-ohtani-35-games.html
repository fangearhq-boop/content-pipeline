PCA vs. Ohtani: The NL MVP Race Has 35 Games Left and One Clear Frontrunner

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | August 21, 2026

With 35 games remaining in the 2026 regular season, Pete Crow-Armstrong's case for the National League Most Valuable Player award has crystallized into something that the numbers strongly support. His closest competitor is Shohei Ohtani, and by the metrics that matter most in MVP voting, Crow-Armstrong has a lead that will be difficult to overcome.

The season Crow-Armstrong is having is not just good. It is historic by Cubs standards, and it is happening at the right moment — when his team is in first place in the NL Wild Card race and every performance carries playoff implications.

The Numbers That Matter

Crow-Armstrong's current line: .281 batting average, 30 home runs, 78 RBI, 31 stolen bases, 7.7 Wins Above Replacement. His WAR total leads Ohtani's estimated 6.4 by more than a full win — a gap that, at this point in the season, reflects a genuine performance advantage rather than a small-sample variance.

The stolen base number adds a dimension that Ohtani does not match. Crow-Armstrong's combination of speed and power — already on pace for a second consecutive 30-30 season — represents exactly the kind of two-way offensive threat that tends to impress voters looking for complete impact.

Most importantly: no Chicago Cubs player has ever posted back-to-back 30-30 seasons. If Crow-Armstrong finishes this year with 30-plus home runs and 30-plus stolen bases, he will have done something that has not been done in over a century of Cubs baseball. That is a historical footnote that does not go unnoticed during ballot-marking season.

The Ohtani Counter-Argument

Shohei Ohtani's case is not without merit, and it would be a mistake to dismiss it. His 2023 MVP win — earned as a legitimate two-way player in a year when no one came close — demonstrated that voters are capable of recognizing performance that defies traditional categories.

In 2026, Ohtani is hitting at an elite level and his all-around impact on the field is real. At 6.4 WAR, he is not a fringe candidate. He is a genuine contender, and his name recognition and narrative appeal — the best player in the sport, the only player in history to perform at his level on both sides of the ball — means his vote total will always be meaningful.

The question is whether his 2026 season is better than Crow-Armstrong's. By WAR, the answer is no. By batting average, home runs, stolen bases, and team standing, the answer is also no.

Why Team Success Matters

The Cubs sit at NL Wild Card No. 1, holding a 5-game lead over the Phillies and a 6-game lead over the Padres. Crow-Armstrong's performance has been central to that standing. His stolen bases create runs on their own — not just by getting on base, but by forcing pitchers and catchers out of their comfort zones, disrupting pitch sequences, and manufacturing pressure that translates into runs.

A player of Crow-Armstrong's WAR on a team in first place historically fares well in MVP voting. Voters like to reward players whose seasons have meaning — where the performance is not happening in a vacuum, but is moving a team toward October. The Cubs are in that position because Crow-Armstrong is on that team.

What's Next

With 35 games remaining, Crow-Armstrong needs to maintain his current production level to stay ahead. A strong September — particularly any milestone moments like the back-to-back 30-30 being officially confirmed — would add to an already compelling case. The NL MVP is not decided in August, but it is often shaped by August. For Crow-Armstrong, this month is going exactly as hoped.
