Misiorowski's Cy Young Is His to Lose — And He Knows It

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | August 21, 2026

Jacob Misiorowski is pitching the best season in baseball. At 12-5 with a 1.76 ERA, 204-plus strikeouts, and a 0.73 WHIP, he has built a Cy Young case that the betting markets have already settled: -550 odds in favor of Misiorowski, a number that reflects near-certainty rather than competition.

His next start arrives around August 23 after the Brewers gave him an extra day of rest following the historic 22-0 win over the Seattle Mariners on August 18. He is healthy. He is sharp. He is the ace of a team with the best record in baseball. What remains between him and the award is six to eight more starts, 40 to 50 more innings, and the maintenance of what has been a historic-quality season.

What 1.76 ERA Means in Historical Terms

An earned run average of 1.76 over a full starting pitcher's season is extraordinarily rare in the modern era. The game has changed — batters are bigger, stronger, more analytically prepared. Exit velocities are up. Ballparks have evolved. The conditions that produced ERAs well below 2.00 in the dead-ball era do not exist anymore.

In the current era, finishing a season with an ERA below 2.00 places a pitcher in rarefied company. Clayton Kershaw had a 1.77 ERA in his historic 2014 Cy Young season. Jacob deGrom's 2018 and 2019 seasons — both Cy Young winning campaigns — came in under 2.00. Misiorowski is in that conversation. Not just this year's conversation — the all-time quality conversation for modern starting pitching.

His 204-plus strikeouts through this point in the season, combined with his WHIP of 0.73, suggest a pitcher who is getting ahead in counts consistently, missing bats when he needs to, and not giving walks away. Every quality start input is where it needs to be.

What the Brewers Need From Him

Milwaukee enters the final stretch at 78-48 — the best record in all of baseball, ahead of both the Los Angeles Dodgers (76-51) and the Atlanta Braves (74-53) in the National League. That record translates into home-field advantage throughout the NL playoffs, potentially including a first-round bye or home-field in the NL Championship Series.

Misiorowski does not need to be perfect to maintain that advantage — the Brewers have enough depth and run support to carry the workload — but every dominant start he delivers in September tightens the grip on the NL's best record. A September collapse is not expected, but it is the only scenario in which the playoff positioning gets complicated.

For a team with genuine World Series aspirations, keeping Misiorowski healthy and effective through the regular season is the highest organizational priority in September. Everything else is secondary.

The Final Six to Eight Starts

In a typical rotation, a starting pitcher will make six or seven more starts after August 21 before the regular season ends. For Misiorowski, that means roughly 40 to 48 innings of regular-season baseball remaining. If he maintains his current ERA across those innings — and there is no reason based on his 2026 performance to expect a significant deviation — he finishes with one of the best pitching seasons in recent memory.

The Cy Young Award is not formally voted on until after the season ends, but the practical reality is that Misiorowski's case is already made. He leads the NL in ERA. He leads in strikeouts. His WHIP is among the best in baseball. The award is his to lose — and losing it would require an implosion that this season has given no indication is coming.

What's Next

Misiorowski's next start is scheduled for approximately August 23. Watch for the Brewers' rotation to manage his workload carefully as September approaches — the organization will prioritize health over regular-season records. Six to eight starts remain. The Cy Young Award, pending a dramatic reversal, goes to Milwaukee.
