## Story 1: PCA 40-40 Watch

**Long-Form Post:**
Pete Crow-Armstrong is two stolen bases away from one of the rarest achievements in baseball history.

He's already cleared 40 home runs. He has 38 stolen bases. With five games left in the regular season, the 40-40 club is within reach — and the company he'd be joining tells you everything about what that means.

The list: Jose Canseco (1988), Barry Bonds (1996), Alex Rodriguez (1998), Alfonso Soriano (2006), Ronald Acuña Jr. (2023), Shohei Ohtani (2024). Seven members. That's it. PCA would be the eighth.

He'd also be the first Cub ever to do it — breaking new ground in Cubs franchise history at the same time the team is fighting for a Wild Card berth. The Cubs are 87-69 and hold the NL Wild Card lead. Five games to close it out, both personally and collectively.

Two stolen bases. Five games. The window is open.

## Story 3: Aaron Judge Injury

**Long-Form Post:**
Aaron Judge is targeting the postseason. He just has no confirmed timeline for getting there.

The Yankees slugger went back on the injured list on September 17 (retroactive) with a right soleus strain — a calf injury that has no publicly disclosed MRI grade and no firm return date. Manager Aaron Boone said he "wouldn't place a timeline" on it. Judge himself said he wants to be back for October but acknowledged he has "no answers" on when that actually happens.

This is Judge's second IL stint of 2026. He missed more than three months earlier in the year with a rib fracture, returned on September 8, played seven games, then went right back on the IL with the calf. It's been a season of interruptions for a player who was producing at one of the best rates of his career before the injuries.

The Yankees have clinched their postseason berth and are not in a position where they need Judge to get through the final week of the regular season. They're waiting. The question is whether the body cooperates in time for October.

## Story 4: Misiorowski at Wrigley

**Long-Form Post:**
Jakob Misiorowski is pitching tonight at Wrigley Field. He has 247 strikeouts. He needs 17 more to break Ben Sheets' all-time Brewers strikeout record of 264, set in 2004.

The stage couldn't be more complicated. Misiorowski is facing the Chicago Cubs — the same team currently holding the NL Wild Card lead by half a game over his Brewers' division rival Padres. The Brewers clinched the NL Central and have their own playoff positioning to think about. But none of that changes what Misiorowski is chasing individually.

The 1.95 ERA is the best in baseball. His 20-8 record is the foundation of a unanimous Cy Young Award — MLB's own panel gave him all 32 first-place votes and 160 points in their annual poll. He's thrown more than 1,000 pitches at 100 mph or faster, the first pitcher in the tracking era to reach that threshold.

But the record matters because it's Milwaukee's record — Sheets set it in 2004, a career-best season for one of the best pitchers in Brewers history. Misiorowski breaking it would put him permanently in the franchise's elite tier, not just this year's, and facing the Cubs in Wrigley is exactly the kind of moment that makes history feel earned.

## Story 5: Yordan Alvarez Triple Crown

**Long-Form Post:**
The math is hard. It's not dead yet.

Yordan Alvarez leads the American League in batting average at .310. That's the one he owns. The other two categories — home runs and RBI — are where the math gets complicated.

He has 39 home runs, two behind Junior Caminero of the Rays (41). He has approximately 98 RBI, roughly three behind Pete Alonso of Baltimore (~101). To win the Triple Crown, he'd need to overtake both in the final five games of the regular season, which would require a strong personal finish and some combination of Caminero and Alonso going cold.

Alvarez was one of the best hitters in baseball through much of this summer. But his production has declined since approximately August, and the gap he'd need to close in five games is real. The last player to win the American League Triple Crown was Miguel Cabrera in 2012 — 14 years ago. It's a genuinely rare achievement.

The batting average isn't going anywhere. The HR and RBI gaps are climbable on paper. Whether it actually happens comes down to five games.
