Aaron Judge Is Targeting October. The Calendar Isn't Cooperating.

By Jake Torres September 22, 2026

Where Things Stand

Aaron Judge went back on the injured list on September 17 with a right soleus strain. The IL stint is retroactive to that date. His manager, Aaron Boone, said he would not place a timeline on the injury. Judge himself said he wants to return for the postseason but has "no answers" on when that actually happens.

"I'd like to be back by the postseason," Judge told reporters. "That's definitely the plan." Then he added: "I've got really no answers for you on any timeline."

The Season That Keeps Getting Interrupted

This is Judge's second IL stint of 2026, and the context around the first one matters. He was placed on the injured list in late May with a rib fracture — a significant injury that kept him out for 99 days. He returned on September 8 and played seven games before the calf strain ended his regular season again.

The result is a 2026 season that looks nothing like what was projected. The talent has been there when he has been on the field. The production rate in limited sample was consistent with what Judge has shown in his best seasons. But the availability has not been there, and two IL stints in a single year — one of them lasting nearly the entire first half — have defined what this season became.

What the Yankees Are Doing

The Yankees clinched their postseason berth and do not need Judge to perform at a high level in the final week of the regular season. They are managing him deliberately. The soleus strain, which affects the lower calf, has no publicly disclosed MRI grade, which means the public timeline is genuinely uncertain — not just managed messaging from a team trying to hide something from opponents.

The math on preparation time is straightforward. The Wild Card begins September 29. If Judge is activated in the final days of the regular season, he will have a handful of games to find his timing before the bracket starts. If his return is delayed further, he goes into October without having faced live pitching in weeks. Neither scenario is ideal for a player whose value is tied to his output in the lineup.

What's Next

Judge is targeting a postseason return, and the Yankees have every reason to be patient with that timeline. The regular season ends approximately September 28. The postseason begins September 29. Whether he is available for the Wild Card round, the Division Series, or not at all remains unknown as of September 22. The Yankees will monitor the injury day to day and make decisions closer to the playoff schedule. The goal is October.
