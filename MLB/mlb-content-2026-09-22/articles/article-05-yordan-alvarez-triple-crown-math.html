Yordan Alvarez's Triple Crown Math: Five Games, Two Homers, Three RBI

By Marcus Cole September 22, 2026

The Numbers and What They Mean

Yordan Alvarez is leading the American League in batting average at .310. That category is his. The other two legs of the Triple Crown require more math. He has 39 home runs, two behind Junior Caminero of the Tampa Bay Rays (41). He has approximately 98 RBI, roughly three behind Pete Alonso of Baltimore (~101). With five games left, the Triple Crown is not impossible. It is not likely. Here is the honest case for both.

What Would Need to Happen

For Alvarez to win the Triple Crown, he would need to hit at least two home runs in the final five games and drive in at least three more runs than Alonso does over that same stretch. That combination is achievable for a hitter of his quality. He has been one of the most productive left-handed hitters in the American League for multiple seasons, and a five-game hot streak is well within his capability.

The complication is that Caminero and Alonso would need to go cold or simply not produce at their recent rates. Caminero leads the AL in home runs with 41. Alonso has been one of the most productive run producers in the league all season. Both are in their own playoff races or fighting for individual accolades, which means they are highly motivated to play well in the final week.

Alvarez's batting average lead also carries some risk. Chandler Simpson is hitting .307 and Yandy Díaz is at .306 — both within range if Alvarez goes cold in the final games. Protecting the BA lead while also trying to close the HR and RBI gaps creates a difficult balance.

Why It Would Matter

The last American League Triple Crown winner was Miguel Cabrera in 2012. That was 14 years ago. In the 14 seasons since, there have been players who led in one or two categories at various points, but no one has held all three simultaneously at the end of a season. The Triple Crown is genuinely rare, not just in baseball history broadly but in recent baseball history.

Alvarez winning it would represent one of the signature individual achievements of the 2026 season. He already has the batting average title locked up if he avoids a dramatic collapse. Two more home runs and a few RBI separating him from Caminero and Alonso would complete one of the most difficult individual accomplishments in the sport.

What's Next

The Astros have five regular-season games remaining. Alvarez will play in all of them barring injury or a decision to rest him with the postseason approaching. The Triple Crown math comes down to his performance relative to Caminero and Alonso over those final games. The BA lead is likely to hold. The HR and RBI gaps are narrow enough to close. Whether they actually close is what the final week is for.
