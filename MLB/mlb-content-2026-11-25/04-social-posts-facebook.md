### STORY 1: Cubs Sign Sato + Rogers

#### Long-Form Post
The Chicago Cubs just spent $240 million in roughly 24 hours. If you weren't watching last night, here's what happened.

First, the Cubs agreed to a six-year, $120 million deal with Japanese slugger Teruaki Sato. He won the Central League Triple Crown with Hanshin — batting .306 with 39 home runs and 106 RBI — and he's 27 years old. He'll play corner outfield, the spot vacated when Seiya Suzuki departed in free agency.

Then, before the Sato deal was even cold, Chicago turned around and signed left-handed starter Trevor Rogers to a five-year, $120 million contract. Rogers went 10-11 with a 4.03 ERA for Baltimore this year. He declined a qualifying offer from the Orioles to become a free agent.

Two deals. $240 million. One day.

The Cubs have been in a complicated position: they lost Suzuki, Ian Happ, and others from their 2026 lineup, leaving significant holes. These signings don't erase all of them, but they represent a clear statement of intent. The team is not in a rebuild — they're spending to compete.

Both deals are pending physicals. With the December 1 CBA deadline creating a hard stop on transactions, the Cubs moved fast. They may not be done.

### STORY 2: Orioles Sign Freddy Peralta

#### Long-Form Post
Baltimore finally did it.

The Orioles agreed to a five-year, $125 million deal with Freddy Peralta — their first multiyear free-agent starting pitcher deal under president of baseball operations Mike Elias, and the largest pitching commitment in franchise history.

Peralta, 30, earned ALCS MVP honors this past October after pitching the Tampa Bay Rays into the World Series, where they lost to Milwaukee in five games. In the regular season he posted a 4.42 ERA over 169 innings, splitting 2026 between the Mets and the Rays.

The deal includes a $10 million signing bonus at $23 million per year — well ahead of the $90 million extension Orioles pitcher Kyle Bradish signed in July. It's the kind of commitment Orioles fans have been pushing for since the team's young core started winning.

The Orioles get an experienced playoff performer, an ALCS MVP arm, and a pitcher with upside at 30. The timing matters: Peralta was not subject to a qualifying offer because he was traded midseason, so Baltimore takes him without draft pick compensation attached.

This is what a franchise that believes in itself does six days before a potential lockout.

### STORY 3: MLBPA Rejects CBA Proposal / Lockout Countdown

#### Long-Form Post
Six days. That's all that stands between baseball and its next work stoppage.

The MLB Players Association called the league's latest CBA proposal "anti-player restrictions" and fired back an immediate rejection. Commissioner Rob Manfred responded by reiterating that the owners will not approve any deal without a salary cap.

Here's where things stand:

**MLB's latest offer:** Delay a salary cap until 2028 with a $258.3 million cap and a $180.2 million floor. Raise the minimum player salary from $780,000 to $1 million. Drop the escrow proposal. Agree to equally share media revenue.

**What the players want:** No salary cap — ever. The union's interim executive director Bruce Meyer called it a "nonstarter."

**The countdown:** The current CBA expires at 11:59 p.m. ET on December 1. Under the 2021 precedent, owners locked out players at 12:01 a.m. on December 2. That lockout lasted 99 days. If the same thing happens now, the 2027 season is in jeopardy.

Teams have been spending frantically in anticipation. More than $1.2 billion has been committed in free agency, with the Skubal deal ($450 million), Peralta ($125 million), Sato ($120 million), and Rogers ($120 million) driving the total.

Once the clock hits midnight December 1, all player transactions — signings, trades, arbitration negotiations — freeze until a new deal is reached.

### STORY 4: Jazz Chisholm 6-Day Countdown

#### Long-Form Post
Jazz Chisholm is still a free agent. With six days until the December 1 lockout, that is not a comfortable place to be.

The Toronto Blue Jays remain the frontrunner, having met with his representatives at the GM meetings in Las Vegas. The Blue Jays see Chisholm as a centerpiece of their rebuild — high-energy, plus speed (41 stolen bases in 2026), no qualifying offer cost attached.

The question is whether his asking price matches reality. Chisholm hit .231 in 2026 — below his 2024 peak — and his defensive position fit is a question mark. More than 10 teams have been reported as interested, but how many will commit big money to a player coming off a down year?

One rumor: a short-term deal with an opt-out could be the structure that gets it done, allowing Chisholm to reset his market value if he bounces back.

The complicating factor is the December 1 CBA deadline. If Chisholm doesn't sign by then, his market freezes with everything else. Teams have no incentive to rush the offer — they can wait until spring and potentially get him cheaper on a shorter deal. But Chisholm has no incentive to wait either.

Six days. Something needs to give.

### STORY 5: Corey Seager / Phillies

#### Long-Form Post
Here's a name that could blow up the trade market in the next six days: Corey Seager.

Phillies insider Jim Salisbury reported this week that the Texas Rangers shortstop — a former World Series MVP — would likely be willing to waive his full 10-and-5 no-trade clause to go to Philadelphia. ESPN's Alden Gonzalez confirmed that the Rangers are open to dealing him.

Seager is owed $155 million over the next five years. He hit .227/.312/.424 in an injury-interrupted 2026, below his career norms. He turned 32 in October. The obvious concern: you're buying the upside he showed when healthy, but paying for the injury risk that derailed his last season.

A Seager-for-Trea Turner swap has circulated, but the salary structures are complicated, and multiple reports say the Phillies are hesitant to give up Turner.

Here's the brutal reality of the timeline: getting a deal this significant done in six days — before the December 1 lockout — would be extraordinary. These blockbusters typically take weeks of negotiation. The more likely scenario is that Seager trade talks resume when the lockout ends, with a fresh set of complications about which teams are still interested.

But in a week when the Cubs just spent $240 million in 24 hours, nothing should be ruled out.
