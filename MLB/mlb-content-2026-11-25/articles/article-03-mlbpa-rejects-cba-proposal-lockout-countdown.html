MLBPA Calls Latest CBA Proposal "Anti-Player Restrictions" — Lockout Dec. 2 Now Near-Certain With 6 Days Left

By Ryan Calloway | November 25, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The gap between Major League Baseball and its players' union narrowed slightly last week when the league put a new proposal on the table. It snapped shut just as fast.

The MLB Players Association called the league's latest CBA offer "anti-player restrictions" and fired back an immediate rejection. Commissioner Rob Manfred responded by reiterating that owners will not approve any deal without a salary cap. With six days until the December 1 deadline, a lockout starting December 2 now appears near-certain.

What MLB Offered

MLB's latest proposal included several concessions designed to give the union something to work with. The league agreed to delay any salary cap until 2028 — the cap would be set at $258.3 million with a $180.2 million floor for that season, with 2027 left uncapped. The minimum player salary was raised from $780,000 to $1 million. The league dropped an earlier escrow proposal and agreed to equally share all media revenue.

On paper, these are real movements. The uncapped 2027 season gives the union an entire year without the structure it opposes.

What the Players Said

None of it was enough. MLBPA interim executive director Bruce Meyer called the cap a "nonstarter" and characterized the full proposal as containing "anti-player restrictions." The union fired back an immediate rejection, characterizing the revenue-sharing concession as offering "no real benefit" under the cap system the league is still insisting on.

The union's position has not moved: a salary cap is unacceptable in any form, delayed or not.

The Commissioner's Line in the Sand

Manfred was equally unmoved. He said publicly that MLB's owners are unanimous: they will not ratify any collective bargaining agreement that does not include a salary cap. All 30 clubs have reportedly agreed on this position.

That leaves no apparent path to a deal before December 1. Unless one side blinks dramatically in the next 144 hours, the sport will enter a lockout on December 2 for the first time since 2021.

What a Lockout Means

When the CBA expires at 11:59 p.m. ET on December 1, all MLB transactions freeze. No signings. No trades. No arbitration negotiations. No communication between teams and players. Everything stops.

The 2021 lockout lasted 99 days, ending March 10 of that year. Had it extended another three weeks, the regular season would have been shortened. The 2027 season's Opening Day is not in immediate jeopardy from a December 2 lockout, but a prolonged dispute could get there quickly.

Several high-profile free agents remain unsigned: Jazz Chisholm, Randy Arozarena, Ketel Marte, and Seiya Suzuki are all still on the market. Any player who does not have a deal by midnight December 1 faces a potentially lengthy wait.

The Pre-Lockout Spending Frenzy

Teams have responded to the deadline with an extraordinary spending spree. More than $1.2 billion has been committed in free agency contracts this offseason — with the Mets' $450 million deal for Tarik Skubal (37.5 percent of the total by itself), the Orioles' $125 million Freddy Peralta signing, and the Cubs' $240 million double-signing of Teruaki Sato and Trevor Rogers combining for the majority of that figure.

The irony is that this pre-lockout rush is exactly the kind of spending the MLBPA has been pushing for — money flowing to players before the market freezes. But it is happening because of the deadline, not despite it.

What's Next

Both sides continue negotiating through December 1. Unofficial reports suggest there is no progress. The CBA expires Saturday at 11:59 p.m. ET. Unless something dramatic changes, baseball wakes up Sunday morning in a lockout. The last time that happened, fans waited more than three months to hear Opening Day is saved. Plan accordingly.
