Cubs Spend $240M in 24 Hours: Sato (6yr/$120M) and Rogers (5yr/$120M) Address Rotation and OF Gaps

By Marcus Cole | November 25, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The Chicago Cubs needed answers. They found two of them in roughly 24 hours, spending $240 million in a pre-lockout signing spree that signals the franchise is not in a rebuilding mood.

On November 24-25, the Cubs agreed to terms with Japanese slugger Teruaki Sato (six years, $120 million) and left-handed pitcher Trevor Rogers (five years, $120 million). Both deals are pending physicals. With the December 1 CBA deadline creating a hard stop on transactions, Chicago moved before the window closed.

Teruaki Sato: Triple Crown Winner, Corner Outfield Answer

Sato, 27, won the Central League Triple Crown with Hanshin, batting .306 with 39 home runs and 106 RBI in 2026. He will play corner outfield — the spot vacated when Seiya Suzuki departed in free agency, one of three significant lineup pieces the Cubs lost this offseason alongside Ian Happ.

At six years and $120 million, Sato is a commitment to the Cubs' window over the medium term. His age profile (27 years old, entering his prime) makes the deal palatable. Lefty bats with this kind of power-contact combination do not hit the market often.

The Phillies were also reported to have interest in Sato, making the Cubs' ability to close quickly particularly noteworthy.

Trevor Rogers: Five-Year Solution for a Rotation That Needed Help

Rogers, 29, went 10-11 with a 4.03 ERA for Baltimore in 2026. He declined a qualifying offer from the Orioles, triggering draft-pick compensation obligations for Chicago — the Cubs, who exceeded the luxury tax in 2026, will forfeit their second- and fifth-highest selections in the 2027 draft and $1 million from their international bonus pool.

The tradeoff is a left-handed starter entering his prime at a rotation-slot cost the Cubs clearly deemed acceptable. With all five of their starting pitchers from 2026 having been free agents, adding a durable arm on a five-year deal stabilizes the front of the rotation.

The CBA Context

Both signings come against the backdrop of an impending labor stoppage. The current collective bargaining agreement expires at 11:59 p.m. ET on December 1, and a lockout starting December 2 appears near-certain after the MLBPA called the league's latest proposal "anti-player restrictions" and fired back an immediate rejection.

Teams across baseball have been accelerating their free agency activity in anticipation. More than $1.2 billion has been committed in this offseason, with the Mets' $450 million deal for Tarik Skubal, the Orioles' $125 million Freddy Peralta signing, and now the Cubs' two-deal $240 million commitment driving the total.

Any deal not completed by December 1 freezes until a new CBA is reached — a potentially lengthy wait given how far apart the two sides remain on a salary cap.

What's Next

Both Sato and Rogers will undergo physicals before their deals are made official. If they clear, Chicago will add both players to their 40-man roster immediately. The Cubs could still have moves left before the December 1 deadline — Jazz Chisholm remains available and has been linked to multiple teams. Spring training for 2027 is at risk if the lockout extends, though the December 1 deadline itself triggers no immediate schedule disruption.
