Jazz Chisholm Countdown: Blue Jays Have 6 Days to Sign Their Top Target Before the Lockout Freeze

By Marcus Cole | November 25, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Jazz Chisholm is still on the market. With six days until the December 1 lockout deadline, that is a situation that needs to resolve itself quickly — for his sake and for the Blue Jays'.

Toronto has emerged as the clear frontrunner in the Chisholm sweepstakes after Shi Davidi of Sportsnet reported that Blue Jays representatives met with Chisholm's agents at the GM meetings in Las Vegas. The meeting was described as productive. The question is whether productive translates to a deal before December 1.

What Toronto Gets

Chisholm brings a package no other available free agent offers: legitimate plus speed (41 stolen bases in 2026), versatility between infield and outfield, and a track record of above-average offense in his best seasons. He received no qualifying offer from the Yankees, meaning the Blue Jays would take him without any draft-pick compensation cost — an important consideration for a Toronto franchise still building its prospect pipeline.

At his best, Chisholm is a game-changer. At his 2026 version (.231 BA, 19 HR), he is a solid contributor who may have been the victim of an unlucky stretch. Jon Morosi of MLB Network has reported more than 10 teams engaged in his market, suggesting the league believes in the upside version.

The Complication: His 2026 Numbers

Coverage has flagged Chisholm's 2026 season as the first red flag in his free agency. He simply was not a good hitter last year. The .231 average represents a significant drop from his career norms, and while his stolen base total remained elite, the offensive production teams pay for in a free-agent contract was largely absent.

A short-term deal with an opt-out has been discussed as a structure that could bridge the gap: Chisholm accepts less guaranteed money for the chance to reset his market value if he returns to form, while the Blue Jays limit their downside on a player coming off a down year.

The Lockout Stakes

Here is the scenario everyone in Chisholm's market is trying to avoid: he doesn't sign before December 1, the lockout hits, and his market sits frozen until a new CBA is reached. If the dispute drags on the way 2021's did — 99 days — Chisholm is not signed until March. Teams may reassess their needs by then, potentially at a lower price point.

That dynamic gives both sides incentive to close a deal this week. The Blue Jays don't want to lose their primary target to a team willing to pay more during the final 144 hours. Chisholm doesn't want to be the cautionary tale of an unsigned free agent stuck in a lockout.

What's Next

Reports through the weekend will reveal whether the Blue Jays and Chisholm are genuinely close or still negotiating structure. If Toronto closes this deal before December 1, it would be one of the defining free-agent moves of this offseason — a speedster and versatility piece for a team trying to build toward playoff contention. The countdown is six days. Something needs to give.
