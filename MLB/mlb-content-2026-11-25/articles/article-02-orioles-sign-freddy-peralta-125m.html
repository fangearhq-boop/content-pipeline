Orioles Sign ALCS MVP Freddy Peralta to 5yr/$125M Deal — Franchise's Biggest Pitching Commitment Ever

By Jake Torres | November 25, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Baltimore Orioles fans have been waiting for Mike Elias to spend big on a free-agent starting pitcher. The wait is over.

The Orioles agreed to a five-year, $125 million deal with Freddy Peralta, the most money the franchise has ever committed to a pitcher. It is also the first multiyear free-agent starting pitcher deal Elias has handed out since taking over as president of baseball operations — a milestone that underscores how seriously Baltimore is approaching 2027.

The deal includes a $10 million signing bonus and an average annual value of $23 million, well ahead of the $90 million extension Baltimore pitcher Kyle Bradish signed in July. Peralta was not subject to a qualifying offer because he was traded midseason, so the Orioles acquire him without draft-pick compensation attached.

What Baltimore Is Getting

Peralta, 30, posted a 4.42 ERA over 169 innings in 2026, splitting the season between the Mets and Rays. The numbers are not flashy, but his October performance told a different story.

After the Rays acquired him at the trade deadline, Peralta dominated in the postseason. He earned ALCS MVP honors after pitching Tampa Bay to the World Series — a run the Rays ultimately lost to Milwaukee in five games. When the pressure was highest, Peralta delivered.

That is the version of Peralta the Orioles are betting $125 million on: a postseason performer, 30 years old, with upside remaining in a rotation that Baltimore is building around young talent.

The Orioles' Pitching Need

Baltimore's young core has been one of baseball's best stories over the past three seasons. The offense features several homegrown stars, but the rotation has been the question mark preventing a deep October run. Bradish anchors it, but beyond him, the depth was a concern entering this offseason.

Peralta gives Baltimore a credible No. 2 starter — an ALCS MVP who has shown he can handle big games. The question is whether he can sustain regular-season consistency. His 4.42 ERA in 2026 is not a number that inspires certainty.

At five years and $125 million, the Orioles are paying for the ceiling, not the floor. It is the kind of bet a franchise makes when it believes its window is opening.

The Deadline Pressure

Like every signing this week, Peralta's deal carries the shadow of the December 1 CBA deadline. With the MLBPA having rejected the league's latest proposal and a lockout appearing near-certain, teams are rushing to close deals. Baltimore moved quickly — and they may not be finished.

What's Next

Peralta will undergo a physical before the deal is officially announced. If he clears, he joins a Baltimore rotation that features Bradish at the front. The Orioles have six days to complete any additional moves before the December 1 CBA expiration freezes all transactions. Whether Baltimore pursues further additions in the remaining window will depend on how their rotation math looks after Peralta officially joins the roster.
