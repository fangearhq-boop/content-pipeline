Corey Seager Would Waive NTC for Phillies — Rangers Open to Deal, But 6-Day Clock Makes Blockbuster a Long Shot

By Jake Torres | November 25, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Corey Seager, the Texas Rangers shortstop and former World Series MVP, would likely waive his full no-trade clause to go to Philadelphia — that is the report from Phillies insider Jim Salisbury.

ESPN's Alden Gonzalez confirmed that the Rangers are open to dealing him. The pieces for a blockbuster are in place. There is just one problem: the December 1 CBA deadline is six days away, and trade negotiations of this complexity do not typically resolve in six days.

Seager's Position and His 2026 Season

As a 10-and-5 player — 10 years in the majors and five years with his current team — Seager has a full no-trade clause that gives him veto power over any deal. That protection is now his leverage to pick his destination. According to Salisbury, he would be comfortable exercising it in the Phillies' direction.

That said, Seager is coming off a difficult 2026. He hit .227/.312/.424 in an injury-interrupted season, well below the career norms that made him one of the game's premier offensive shortstops in his peak years. He turned 32 this past October. He is owed $155 million over the next five years — money the Rangers are reportedly motivated to move off their books.

The Phillies would be taking on that full commitment, plus whatever they give up in return.

The Trade Complexity: Turner and Salary

A Corey Seager trade is not simple. The most discussed framework has been a Seager-for-Trea Turner swap — an exchange of similarly expensive shortstops that would address roster needs on both sides. The Rangers get a premium infielder. The Phillies get a player whose NTC they can use.

But multiple reports flag why this is easier to propose than execute. The salary structures do not align cleanly. The Phillies have been hesitant to give up Turner. And Seager's 2026 production — relative to his contract — creates genuine uncertainty about whether the return value matches the ask.

The Lockout Timeline Problem

Here is what history tells us about blockbuster trades: they take time. Medical reviews, salary negotiations, player approvals, and final term-sheet details typically require weeks of back-and-forth. Getting all of that done by December 1 — in six days — would require exceptional urgency from both front offices.

Several analysts covering the market have assessed a Seager trade before the lockout as unlikely. The more probable scenario: talks surface publicly now (which they have), proceed through the weekend, and then pause when the lockout hits December 2. If the work stoppage resolves quickly, Seager is a priority move in January or February. If it drags, the landscape shifts.

What the Phillies Are Weighing

Philadelphia's front office faces a genuine decision: pursue an aging, recently injured shortstop on a $155 million commitment, or stay patient and see what the post-lockout market brings. The fact that Seager has indicated he would waive his NTC for them adds clarity — he wants to be there. Whether the production and the price justify the move is a different question.

In a week when the Cubs committed $240 million in 24 hours and the Orioles handed out $125 million more, the threshold for "going big" has reset significantly. Even in that context, Seager represents a complicated calculus.

What's Next

Phillies and Rangers officials will likely continue conversations through the weekend. If they can align on structure — particularly around Turner's inclusion or exclusion — a deal before December 1 is at least theoretically possible. More likely, this story continues after the lockout, whenever that ends. Seager's NTC willingness is not time-limited; he is not going anywhere on his own. But a team that waits for spring might find competition has changed and Philadelphia's urgency may have too.
