### STORY 5: Ohtani Rest Question

**Long-Form Post**
Window: 10:30 PM ET

Dave Roberts said it plainly: Shohei Ohtani is "not close to optimal."

For 17 days, Ohtani has been one of baseball's biggest stories for all the wrong reasons. Since August 18, he's gone 7-for-51 (.137) with 20 strikeouts. On September 3 against the Cardinals — in a game the Dodgers lost 8-6 — he went 0-for-4 with four more strikeouts.

The left knee is the issue. Rest discussions are happening. And the Dodgers, at 82-57 with a playoff spot locked up, now face the central question of their October preparation: do you keep running Ohtani out there through September, or do you sit him down and let him get right?

The argument for resting him is obvious. A healthy Ohtani in October is worth far more than a compromised Ohtani in late September regular season games that don't affect the standings. The argument against is harder to quantify — rust, rhythm, the difficulty of just switching a player of his caliber back on after extended rest.

Roberts hasn't announced a decision. But "not close to optimal" isn't nothing. It's a signal that the Dodgers are thinking seriously about what October needs from their franchise player.

Whatever they decide, Ohtani's health is now the biggest storyline in Los Angeles baseball.
