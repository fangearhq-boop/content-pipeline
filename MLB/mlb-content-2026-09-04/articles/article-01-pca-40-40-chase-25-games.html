Pete Crow-Armstrong Is Two Home Runs From History — and the Cubs Need Him to Get There

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | September 4, 2026

The 40-40 club has five members. In the 138-year history of Major League Baseball, only five players have ever hit 40 home runs and stolen 40 bases in the same season. Jose Canseco did it first in 1988. Barry Bonds followed in 1996. Alex Rodriguez in 1998. Alfonso Soriano in 2006. Ronald Acuña Jr. in 2023. Three years passed between each of the last two. Now, in September 2026, the baseball world is watching Pete Crow-Armstrong of the Chicago Cubs creep toward that door.

Crow-Armstrong enters September with a .283 batting average, 38 home runs, and 32 stolen bases. He needs two more home runs and eight more stolen bases in 25 remaining games to join that list. The math is almost done. The question is everything else around it.

The Numbers That Matter

Two home runs in 25 games is not an ask. Crow-Armstrong has been averaging better than one home run every four games this season. Even accounting for September fatigue, pitcher adjustments, and the Cubs managing him carefully, two home runs feels like the easier half of the equation.

Eight stolen bases in 25 games is where it gets interesting. He's averaged a stolen base roughly every three to four games this season, which means the pace supports the target. But stolen base attempts are not made in a vacuum. They require the green light from the dugout. They require the right game situations — close games, right-handed pitchers, lead runners in position to go. On a team competing for a Wild Card spot, those decisions carry more weight than they do in August when the standings are settled.

The Cubs are 82-57 and hold the No. 1 NL Wild Card position. Every game matters. Every run differential matters. The question being asked in the Cubs' dugout this month isn't just "what does PCA need for 40-40?" It's "what does this team need to win today, and does that include sending our best player?"

What the 40-40 Club Actually Means

The scarcity of the 40-40 milestone is worth examining. It requires a player who is both an elite power hitter and an elite base stealer — two skills that don't often coexist at the highest level. Power hitters tend to be slower. Speed threats tend to hit for less power. The players who can do both at 40-level production in both categories represent a rare intersection of athleticism, skill, and durability.

Canseco hit 42 home runs and stole 40 bases in 1988, becoming the first player in history to reach both thresholds. He finished that season as the AL MVP. Bonds did it in 1996 with 42 home runs and 40 steals. Rodriguez hit 42 and stole 46. Soriano hit 46 and stole 41. Acuña's 2023 season was historic — 41 home runs and 73 stolen bases — and he won the NL MVP unanimously.

Crow-Armstrong is the -1100 NL MVP favorite. He doesn't just have a chance to join this list. He has a chance to join it in his age-23 season, making him one of the youngest players ever to reach 40-40.

The Cubs' Situation

The Wild Card race gives this chase a secondary storyline that makes it richer than a simple milestone countdown. The Cubs are good — 82-57 with 25 games left is a strong position — but they're not insulated. The NL Wild Card picture behind them includes the Phillies at WC2 and the Diamondbacks at WC3, with San Diego within striking distance of WC3.

That means September matters. The Cubs need wins, not just milestones. Manager David Ross and his staff will make lineup decisions, baserunning decisions, and game management decisions based on what gives the team the best chance to win that night. Sometimes that aligns perfectly with what PCA needs for 40-40. Sometimes it might not.

That tension — between individual history and team need — is part of what makes this month worth watching. The best outcome is one where both happen simultaneously: the Cubs win, PCA gets there, and the question becomes moot because they needed him either way.

What's Next

Every Crow-Armstrong at-bat for the rest of September will be tracked. Every stolen base attempt will be counted. The 40-40 club is a rare enough distinction that national attention follows players chasing it, and PCA has both the ability and the opportunity to get there before the regular season ends.

Two home runs. Eight stolen bases. Twenty-five games. The Cubs need him and history needs him, and right now, those two things point in the same direction.
