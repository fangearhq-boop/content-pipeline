Cardinals at 70-70: What It Takes to Turn a Stunning Win Over the Dodgers Into an October Run

By Ryan Calloway | Ballpark Banter | September 4, 2026

The St. Louis Cardinals are not supposed to still be in this conversation. At 70-70 with 22 games remaining, sitting 3.5 games outside the NL Wild Card race, the numbers make the road narrow. But on the night of September 3, the Cardinals went to Los Angeles and beat the Dodgers 8-6 in 10 innings — and Thomas Saggese made sure it was memorable.

Saggese broke up Yoshinobu Yamamoto's no-hit bid in the sixth inning with a two-run home run. Then, in the 10th, he came to the plate with the go-ahead run on base and delivered a single that gave St. Louis the lead for good. That's not just a big game. That's a signature performance from a 23-year-old who has emerged as one of the most interesting stories in baseball's final month.

The Wild Card Math

Let's be direct about what 70-70 means. The Cardinals need to go approximately 14-8 over their final 22 games to have a realistic shot at a Wild Card position. That's a .636 winning percentage over the remaining schedule — strong but not impossible. The team that's averaged .500 over 140 games needs to play like a playoff team over 22.

They also need help. The teams ahead of them in the Wild Card standings have to stumble. In September, that's not impossible — good teams lose games they shouldn't, and the schedule tends to feature more meaningful games for teams chasing than for teams sitting safely in playoff positions. But it requires multiple things to break correctly in a short window.

The path is narrow. It is not closed. Those are different things, and the Cardinals earned the right to say so with the September 3 win.

Thomas Saggese and the Emergence of a September Story

Saggese has become the Cardinals' most compelling narrative. The 23-year-old third baseman was not the name most Cardinals fans would have predicted to be at the center of their Wild Card chase heading into September. But baseball has a way of identifying players who can handle the moment, and Saggese has been raising his hand repeatedly.

What happened on September 3 wasn't a fluke. Breaking up a no-hitter requires skill — you still have to hit the baseball. A walk-off in extras requires composure — the moment is different from a routine at-bat. Saggese did both in the same game, against one of the best pitchers in the National League. That profile — young, producing in big moments, improving at the right time — is exactly what a Wild Card chase needs.

The Cardinals' ability to get to October runs directly through how well Saggese continues to play. He is the most important variable in their stretch run.

Leo Bernal and the Roster's Supporting Cast

One element of September 3 that deserves recognition is Leo Bernal's first career MLB home run. Catcher debut moments are often overshadowed by the larger narrative of a game, but Bernal's contribution reminded everyone watching that the Cardinals are not a one-player team. Their path to October requires multiple contributors to perform, and Bernal adding to the offensive output in a meaningful game is the kind of subplot that keeps a team loose and confident.

Báez contributed a pinch-hit double that added insurance runs. The Cardinals won a game they had to win with multiple contributors stepping up on the same night. That's a winning team's pattern — different players in different moments, none of them waiting for one person to save the game.

What's Next

The Cardinals' schedule in the next three weeks will determine everything. Each game is a referendum on whether 70-70 with 22 left can become October baseball. The math requires a run they haven't sustained consistently over the full season, but the win over the Dodgers provides evidence that they're capable of it against the best competition.

Thomas Saggese. Leo Bernal. A Báez pinch-hit double. The Cardinals took everything the Dodgers had on September 3 and still walked out with the win. That's a team that knows how to compete in close games. Whether it's enough to get to October is still an open question — but after this win, the question is at least still worth asking.
