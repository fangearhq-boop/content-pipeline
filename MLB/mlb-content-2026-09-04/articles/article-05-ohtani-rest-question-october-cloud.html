The Dodgers' October Question: Do They Rest Shohei Ohtani, and What Happens Either Way?

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | September 4, 2026

Dave Roberts said it plainly after September 3: Shohei Ohtani is "not close to optimal." Those four words are now the central question surrounding the Los Angeles Dodgers as they move through a September that no longer affects their playoff position — but may determine everything about their postseason ceiling.

Since August 18, Ohtani has gone 7-for-51 at the plate. That's a .137 batting average over 17 days. He has struck out 20 times in that stretch. On September 3 against the Cardinals, he went 0-for-4 with four more strikeouts as the Dodgers lost 8-6 in 10 innings. The left knee is the identified issue. Rest discussions, Roberts confirmed, are actively happening.

The Scope of the Slump

To understand why the Ohtani slump matters, it helps to understand where he was before it. His 2026 hitting season, overall, has been strong — a .941 OPS across roughly 400 plate appearances, with a home run pace that, while below his peak seasons, remained productive. On the mound, he has been exceptional: 8-2 record, 1.79 ERA, 0.95 WHIP, 95 strikeouts against 26 walks in 85.2 innings across 14 starts.

The slump is a 17-day window, not a season-long regression. But the timing makes it more concerning than the numbers alone suggest. This is September. The Dodgers are heading into October. Whatever is wrong with Ohtani — whatever is making him .137 with 20 strikeouts over this stretch — is happening at the exact moment when they need him at his best.

The Case for Resting Him

The Dodgers are 82-57. They have a postseason spot locked up. The remaining 25 games of the regular season will not change their playoff positioning in any material way — they're not chasing a division title that requires winning a specific number of games. They are playing out a schedule.

That context makes resting Ohtani easier to justify than it would be for a team in a race. If the left knee is the issue, September at-bats against pitchers who are working him differently because they know something is off are not the most efficient use of his legs. Three or four weeks of targeted rest, treatment, and maintenance could be worth more to the Dodgers in October than 25 regular season games played at 70 percent.

Postseason rosters are different from regular season rosters. The at-bats matter more. The pitching matchups are better. The margin for error is smaller. A fully healthy Ohtani in October is a multiplier for everything the Dodgers do. A compromised Ohtani going 7-for-51 against playoff pitching is a different conversation entirely.

The Case Against

Rest is not a reset button. Elite athletes who step away from competition for weeks often require time to re-find their timing, their rhythm, their feel for game situations. Ohtani has not been on a long absence this season. Putting him on one in September — even a planned, controlled one — introduces a variable the Dodgers cannot fully predict.

There is also the question of what rest looks like in practice. If the left knee is the issue, rest may mean reduced baseball activity, modified workouts, and careful monitoring. That is not the same as playing every day and remaining sharp. Roberts and the Dodgers medical staff are navigating a situation where the cure and the disease both carry risk.

Roberts' Position

"Not close to optimal" is a specific phrase. It is not "day-to-day" or "dealing with some discomfort." It is a manager acknowledging that his best player is not performing at the level the Dodgers need him to perform at, and that the team is actively discussing what to do about it. That language does not come from a place of confidence. It comes from a place of genuine concern.

The decision Roberts makes over the next week may be the most consequential managerial call of the Dodgers' season — not because of what it means for September, but because of what it means for October.

What's Next

Watch for an announcement about Ohtani's status over the next few days. If the Dodgers move him to a rest protocol, it will be framed carefully — as a managed approach to get him healthy for the postseason, not as a shutdown. If they keep running him out, watch the at-bat quality and the knee more closely than the box score line.

The Dodgers have built for October. Whether Shohei Ohtani arrives there healthy enough to be the player they built around is now the only remaining question about their season that actually matters.
