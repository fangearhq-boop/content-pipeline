Yordan Alvarez and the Triple Crown That Nobody Seems to Want to Talk About

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | September 4, 2026

Yordan Alvarez is leading the American League in batting average, home runs, and RBIs. All three categories. Simultaneously. With 25 games remaining in the regular season. And the conversation around it has been quieter than it should be, because the leads feel fragile and the challengers are real and the whole thing could unravel in any given week.

That fragility is part of what makes the Triple Crown worth watching. The last American League player to win it was Miguel Cabrera in 2012. Before Cabrera, you have to go back to Carl Yastrzemski in 1967. It is not just rare. It is generational-gap rare — the kind of achievement that happens in one decade per era if it happens at all.

The Three Leads and Their Challengers

Alvarez's Triple Crown numbers as of September 4: .322 batting average, 36 home runs, 91 RBIs. Let's examine each category and where the threats stand.

In batting average, Alvarez leads at .322 with his nearest challenger, Simpson, at .308 — a 14-point gap. That sounds comfortable, but batting average can swing dramatically in small samples. A cold week for Alvarez while Simpson heats up can close 14 points faster than the calendar moves. The lead is real. It is not safe.

In home runs, Alvarez leads with 36. Both Misael Caminero and Ben Rice of the Yankees sit at 35, one behind. One home run. In a 25-game stretch where any of the three could hit three or four over a long weekend, that margin is essentially nothing. The home run race could swap leaders multiple times in the final month without anyone blinking.

In RBIs, Alvarez is tied with Pete Alonso at 91. Tied. Not leading — tied. RBIs are the most team-dependent of the three categories, which makes them hardest to project, but an Alonso who runs hot in September while Alvarez goes through a quieter stretch could take that category outright.

Three leads. Three active threats. The pressure is different from chasing a milestone because Alvarez has to protect all three at once while playing baseball normally. There is no dial that says "go get an RBI today." The game provides what it provides.

The Historical Weight

It's worth spending a moment on why the Triple Crown matters beyond the obvious. The batting average, home runs, and RBI leaders in any league represent the three traditional pillars of offensive production. Winning all three in the same season means a player has dominated every corner of the offensive game simultaneously — hitting for contact, hitting for power, and driving in runs. The fact that these three skills increasingly diverge among modern players — with power hitters striking out more, contact hitters hitting fewer home runs, and RBI totals depending heavily on lineup protection — makes the simultaneous peak all the more unlikely.

Cabrera did it in 2012 in his age-29 season with a .330 average, 44 home runs, and 139 RBIs. He won the AL MVP unanimously. If Alvarez wins the Triple Crown, he will win the MVP. Those two things are inseparable, and every voter already knows it.

The Astros' Role

Alvarez doesn't exist in a vacuum. The Houston Astros will influence how his numbers develop over the final 25 games. Lineup protection, who bats behind him, how aggressively opposing pitchers challenge him versus pitching around him — all of it affects the RBI total most directly and the plate appearance quality for the batting average and home run columns.

The Astros are a competitive team, which means Alvarez will be in games that matter. That's both help and hindrance — the lineup quality ensures he gets pitches to hit, but it also means September games carry weight that could influence how managers approach facing him in key spots.

What's Next

Twenty-five games. Three leads. All under threat. Alvarez is in the middle of a chase that the American League hasn't seen in 14 years, and it will run right down to the final days of the regular season. Watch the standings in all three categories. This one is not resolved yet.
