### Long-Form Post Window: 9:00 AM ET Pete Crow-Armstrong is two home runs and eight stolen bases away from one of the rarest achievements in baseball history. The 40-40 club has exactly five members. Jose Canseco did it first in 1988. Barry Bonds in 1996. Alex Rodriguez in 1998. Alfonso Soriano in 2006. Ronald Acuña Jr. in 2023. That's it. Nineteen years passed between Soriano and Acuña. Three years later, we might be watching it happen again. PCA enters September at .283 with 38 home runs and 32 stolen bases. He has 25 games left. The math is almost done — but the Cubs (82-57) are fighting for a Wild Card position, and every lineup decision, every green light, every at-bat in a close game carries playoff weight. That's what makes this different from chasing a milestone on a losing team. PCA isn't just playing for history. He's playing for October. And the Cubs need him for both. Two home runs. Eight stolen bases. Twenty-five games. This is the most interesting chase in baseball right now.

Ohtani's October Cloud — Will the Dodgers Rest Their Star? (Tier 2 — FOLLOW UP)
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Window: 10:30 PM ET Dave Roberts said it plainly: Shohei Ohtani is "not close to optimal." For 17 days, Ohtani has been one of baseball's biggest stories for all the wrong reasons. Since August 18, he's gone 7-for-51 (.137) with 20 strikeouts. On September 3 against the Cardinals — in a game the Dodgers lost 8-6 — he went 0-for-4 with four more strikeouts. The left knee is the issue. Rest discussions are happening. And the Dodgers, at 82-57 with a playoff spot locked up, now face the central question of their October preparation: do you keep running Ohtani out there through September, or do you sit him down and let him get right? The argument for resting him is obvious. A healthy Ohtani in October is worth far more than a compromised Ohtani in late September regular season games that don't affect the standings. The argument against is harder to quantify — rust, rhythm, the difficulty of just switching a player of his caliber back on after extended rest. Roberts hasn't announced a decision. But "not close to optimal" isn't nothing. It's a signal that the Dodgers are thinking seriously about what October needs from their franchise player. Whatever they decide, Ohtani's health is now the biggest storyline in Los Angeles baseball.
