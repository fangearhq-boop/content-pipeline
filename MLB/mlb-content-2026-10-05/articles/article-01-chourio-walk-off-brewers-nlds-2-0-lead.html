Chourio Walk-Off Single Sends Brewers to 2-0 NLDS Lead Over Padres

By Marcus Cole | October 5, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

There are moments in October baseball that make the sport feel like a different game. The Milwaukee Brewers produced one Saturday night at Petco Park. Jackson Chourio stepped to the plate in the bottom of the ninth inning with two outs, two strikes, and the bases loaded. Across from him was Mason Miller, the San Diego closer whose fastball regularly touches 104 miles per hour. Chourio swung. The ball left his bat at 101.3 miles per hour. It found the right side of the infield. Christian Yelich and Sal Frelick scored. Brewers 4, Padres 3. Milwaukee leads the NLDS 2-0.

The At-Bat

Miller entered in a save situation, protecting a 3-2 Padres lead. He is as hard to hit as anyone in the game — his velocity puts him in the conversation for the most dangerous reliever available in October. The count worked to two strikes. Chourio had nowhere to go except into the ball.

When it left his bat, the numbers told the story. A 101.3 mph exit velocity is not a soft single up the middle. That is a ball hit with authority off of a man throwing 104 mph. Jake Cronenworth could not stop it. The ball rolled through, and the inning exploded into a run, then another, then a celebration near home plate that lasted the better part of ten minutes.

Chourio, 22 years old, did not appear rattled at any point in the sequence. He stepped into the box as if the moment were ordinary. It was not ordinary. Nothing about that at-bat was ordinary.

How the Game Got There

Milwaukee entered the ninth trailing 3-2, the Padres clinging to a lead they had built over earlier innings. The Brewers loaded the bases — forcing Miller into a situation where there was no comfortable pitch, no location he could throw to without risk. Chourio was the batter who had to make something happen.

He did. And the Brewers, who have been playing October baseball with a quiet confidence this whole week, added another game to what is becoming a defining run. They are two wins away from the National League Championship Series.

What It Means for the Series

A 2-0 lead in a best-of-five series is not a guarantee. The Padres have the offense and the arms to work their way back. But the series now moves to Milwaukee, where American Family Field will be loud and where the Brewers will hold serve with their own pitching staff.

San Diego must win Game 3 to stay alive. The pressure has shifted completely. The team that was supposed to be the more dangerous offense, the team with Miller in the bullpen, is now in an elimination scenario heading on the road.

What's Next

Games 3 and 4 of the NLDS shift to Milwaukee, with Game 3 scheduled for Tuesday. The Brewers need two more wins. The Padres need to win three consecutive games to advance. Milwaukee's pitching staff, which has been the foundation of this entire run, gets to work in front of a home crowd. Chourio's walk-off is the signature moment. The series is far from over, but the Brewers are firmly in control.
