Michael Harris II's Three-Hit Night Lifts Braves Past Dodgers, Ties NLDS 1-1

By Jake Torres | October 5, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Michael Harris II is known in baseball circles primarily as one of the better center fielders in the game — quick, instinctive, capable of taking extra-base hits away with the kind of routes that make coaches exhale. Saturday night at Dodger Stadium, he showed the other side. Harris went 3-for-4 at the plate, scored a go-ahead run, drove in an insurance run, and helped the Atlanta Braves beat the Los Angeles Dodgers 3-2 to even the National League Division Series at one game apiece.

The Performance

Harris' night was built on contact and timing. In the fifth inning, with the Braves trailing, he tripled and stood on third base. Starter Kris Bubic threw a wild pitch. Harris scored. Atlanta led 2-1.

In the seventh, with the game still within reach, Harris came up again with two outs and a runner on base. He lined a double off Edgardo Henriquez — 3-1 Atlanta, and suddenly the Braves had a cushion. Three hits. A triple. A run scored. A run driven in. It was the kind of night that changes how people think about a player going into the rest of a series.

The Ninth Inning

It did not end cleanly. Max Muncy came to the plate in the bottom of the ninth with two outs, two strikes, and the Dodgers trailing by one. He hit a home run off Didier Fuentes. Just like that, Los Angeles was within a run, trailing 3-2, with the stadium at full noise.

Raisel Iglesias entered to close the game. He faced Josue De Paula. De Paula fouled out. It was Iglesias' second postseason save. Atlanta had held.

The margin was thinner than the Braves would have preferred. The final score was tighter than the seventh inning suggested it would be. But a win is a win, and the Braves earned this one.

Series Context

The NLDS opened with a Dodgers win. Los Angeles was the favorite going in, and Game 1 reinforced that perception. Game 2 shifted the series in a hurry. Atlanta is not behaving like a team that expects to lose. Harris' performance — and the composure Iglesias showed in the ninth — suggests the Braves have an answer for whatever Los Angeles brings.

The series now moves to Los Angeles for Games 3 and 4. The Dodgers get their home crowd back. Atlanta goes in having demonstrated it can win in that building.

What's Next

Games 3 and 4 of the NLDS are at Dodger Stadium, beginning Tuesday. Both teams have shown they can win on the road — Atlanta took one in Los Angeles, and the series is now a genuine coin flip. Harris has established himself as a name to watch for the remainder of this series. He goes to Los Angeles having proved something about his October game.
