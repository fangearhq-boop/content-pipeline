Jacob Misiorowski Throws 104.8 MPH — The Fastest Postseason Pitch Since Tracking Began

By Marcus Cole | October 5, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Numbers like 104.8 miles per hour used to belong to legends. Aroldis Chapman held that velocity on a list that almost no one else could touch. Today, a Milwaukee Brewers reliever named Jacob Misiorowski has thrown the fastest pitch in postseason history since Major League Baseball began tracking velocity in 2008. His fastball hit 104.8 mph during the NLDS — surpassing the record that had been set just days earlier by San Diego's Mason Miller at 104.5 mph. These are different times.

The Record in Context

When Aroldis Chapman came up in 2010 and began throwing in the low 100s with regularity, he represented something that felt singular. A left-handed pitcher with that kind of arm was not supposed to exist. He held the postseason velocity record for the better part of fifteen years at 104.2 mph.

Then the 2026 postseason arrived. Mason Miller of the Padres, already known as one of the hardest-throwing relievers in the game, appeared in the Wild Card round and was clocked at 104.5 mph. The record that Chapman had held since 2010 was gone in an instant.

Days later, Misiorowski threw 104.8. The record lasted less than a week.

What This Means

Two pitchers in the same postseason have thrown harder than anyone had thrown in any postseason in the sixteen years of tracking. This is not coincidence. The velocity ceiling in professional baseball has been rising for a generation, driven by a combination of biomechanics coaching, development pipelines that identify and optimize high-spin arms, and a genuine shift in how organizations value pure power as a trait in their relievers.

Misiorowski is not a household name in the way Miller is. But he is a Milwaukee Brewers reliever who is throwing in October, pitching in a series in which the Brewers lead 2-0, and doing it at a velocity that was considered physically implausible not long ago.

The record books say 104.8. The pitcher is 25 years old. The question now is whether this particular number stays on the books for longer than a week.

The Brewers' October Run

Misiorowski's record exists within a larger context: Milwaukee is playing well. The Brewers hold a 2-0 series lead over San Diego after Jackson Chourio's walk-off single Saturday night. The pitching staff has been excellent throughout. Misiorowski's role in this bullpen is part of why — he is another high-leverage arm in a deep group that the Brewers have assembled.

His velocity record is notable on its own. It is more notable because of the team it supports.

What's Next

The Brewers return to Milwaukee with a 2-0 series lead, Misiorowski still available in their bullpen. The record will stand until another arm in another October game tops it. For now, 104.8 miles per hour is what the postseason looks like at its fastest. Misiorowski owns that number, and Milwaukee intends to keep using him to defend it.
