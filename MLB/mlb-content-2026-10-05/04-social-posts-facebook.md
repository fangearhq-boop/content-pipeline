### STORY 1: Chourio Walk-Off — Brewers 2-0 NLDS Lead

#### Long-Form Post — 8:00 AM ET

Two outs. Two strikes. Bases loaded. Mason Miller — the hardest-throwing closer in baseball — standing 60 feet away throwing 104 miles per hour.

Jackson Chourio lined it back at 101 miles per hour. The ball skipped past Jake Cronenworth. Christian Yelich and Sal Frelick scored.

That is one of the best at-bats of this entire postseason. Chourio, 22 years old, matched heat with heat — and won. The Brewers now head home to American Family Field with a chance to close out the series.

Games 3 and 4 are in Milwaukee. The Brewers are very much alive — and very much in control.

### STORY 2: Michael Harris II — Braves Tie NLDS 1-1

Max Muncy hit a 2-strike, 2-out homer off Didier Fuentes to pull Los Angeles to within one. And then Raisel Iglesias got Josue De Paula to foul out. Series tied, 1-1.

Games 3 and 4 are in Los Angeles. The Braves go there with confidence. Harris was the reason last night — expect his name to matter for the rest of this series.

### STORY 4: Misiorowski 104.8 MPH Postseason Record

#### Long-Form Post — 3:00 PM ET

Here is a baseball sentence that would not have made sense 20 years ago: the fastest pitch ever recorded in a postseason game is 104.8 miles per hour, thrown by a Milwaukee Brewers reliever named Jacob Misiorowski.

It happened this postseason. It broke the previous record of 104.5 mph — which had been set just days earlier by San Diego's Mason Miller in the Wild Card round.

Before Miller, the record belonged to Aroldis Chapman at 104.2 mph — set in 2010, more than fifteen years ago.

The velocity ceiling in baseball has been rising for a generation. Misiorowski's 104.8 is the current peak. The question now is whether it holds through October.

### STORY 5: White Sox Rotation — Youth Movement in the Postseason

#### Long-Form Post — 6:00 PM ET

The Chicago White Sox lost 121 games in 2024. They lost more than 100 in 2023 and in 2025.

They are playing postseason baseball in October 2026 — and they are the first team in MLB history to reach the playoffs after three consecutive 100-loss seasons.

Hagen Smith is 23 years old and started Game 1 of the ALDS. Noah Schultz, Grant Taylor, and Sean Burke are all in the rotation conversation. Luis Castillo — their ace — is coming back in 2027 after missing this season.

What Chicago is building is not a fluke. The youth is real. The talent is real. The history is real.

Whether they win this series or not, the White Sox rotation is one of the most fascinating stories in baseball right now.
