### Long-Form Post Pete Crow-Armstrong is making history one at-bat at a time. ⚾ On Wednesday night, PCA went 3-for-4 with two home runs — Nos. 43 AND 44 — as the Cubs beat the Braves 8-4. The 40-40 side of the ledger: he sits at 37 stolen bases. Needs 3 more. With approximately 10 games left, the math is very much alive. The list of players who have — Barry Bonds, Jose Canseco, Alex Rodriguez, Alfonso Soriano, Carlos Beltran, Eric Davis — reads like a power-speed Hall of Fame. The Cubs hold the NL Wild Card 1 spot at 84-68. Every game matters now — and for Cubs fans, every stolen base attempt will have the whole ballpark holding its breath. ### Image Post Caption 44 home runs. 37 stolen bases. 3 steals from history. ### Long-Form Post The Yankees caught a scare Wednesday night — and now the October countdown has a health question mark attached. ⚾ Aaron Judge left the team's game against the Minnesota Twins in the sixth inning with right lower-leg tightness. He had been 0-for-2 with two strikeouts when manager Aaron Boone lifted him for pinch-hitter Heliot Ramos. Judge said he noticed the discomfort in the first inning while playing the field. Boone's assessment: "Hopefully it's something we were able to catch." No timetable for a return was announced. Here's the context that makes this story significant: Judge returned from a fractured right rib just nine days ago on September 8. He had missed all of June, July, and August — a 99-day IL stint. He played fewer than 10 days at full health before a new injury sent him to the bench. The Yankees have already clinched their 61st postseason berth (ninth in 10 seasons). They are not playing for anything in these final two weeks except to have their best player healthy for October. With the Wild Card beginning September 29, the organization's posture is clear: protect Judge. The risk of losing him for the postseason entirely outweighs any regular-season benefit. Whether this becomes a serious concern or a short-term maintenance issue will define the Yankees' October ceiling. ### Image Post Caption Aaron Judge exits Wednesday's game with lower-leg tightness.
