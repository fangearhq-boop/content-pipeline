Aaron Judge Exits With Lower-Leg Tightness — Yankees Managing Their Star for October After 99-Day Absence

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | September 17, 2026

Nine days after returning from a fractured rib, Judge left Wednesday's game against Minnesota with a new injury — and the Yankees are now clearly prioritizing October over regular-season at-bats.

Aaron Judge returned from a fractured right rib on September 8 after missing all of June, July, and August. Nine days later, he left Wednesday's game against the Minnesota Twins in the sixth inning with right lower-leg tightness. He was 0-for-2 with two strikeouts when manager Aaron Boone lifted him for pinch-hitter Heliot Ramos. Judge said he noticed the discomfort in the first inning while playing the field.

Boone's postgame comment: "Hopefully it's something we were able to catch." No timetable for a return was announced.

The Timeline That Makes This Concerning

The sequence is difficult to ignore. Judge was placed on the injured list in late May with a fractured right rib — a stress fracture that kept him out for 99 days. He did not play a game from early June until September 8. He returned to the lineup less than two weeks ago and has now exited with a separate lower-leg issue.

At 34, managing through multiple injury events in a compressed window is the kind of storyline that shapes a postseason. The Yankees need Judge healthy — not for the final 10 regular-season games, which are meaningless in standings terms, but for the Wild Card and beyond. Whether the lower-leg tightness is a minor maintenance issue or something more significant will determine whether the Yankees' October ceiling looks like what they expected entering the year.

What the Yankees Have Without Judge

New York has already clinched their 61st postseason berth — ninth in 10 seasons. The roster around Judge was built to compete in October regardless of individual health, but Judge is the franchise centerpiece. His presence in the lineup changes how opposing pitchers approach New York's offense, creates RBI opportunities for the hitters behind him, and gives the team an offensive dimension that no replacement player can replicate.

The decision to lift Judge in the sixth inning rather than test the injury further signals that the organization is not willing to risk a more serious outcome. That is the correct call for a team with 12 days until the Wild Card begins — the time to protect the most important asset on the roster is exactly these final two weeks of the regular season.

October Starting September 29

The American League Wild Card Series begins September 29. That is 12 days from now. The Yankees need to know, before that date, what version of Aaron Judge they will have available. A fully healthy Judge — even one who has faced two separate injury events in the past four months — gives New York a different postseason ceiling than an absent or compromised one.

Boone's "hopefully we caught it" framing suggests cautious optimism from the manager's perspective. Whether that optimism is borne out over the next 12 days will be the most important Yankees storyline between now and the first postseason pitch.

What's Next

The Yankees will evaluate Judge over the next several days and make a decision about whether he plays any more regular-season games. Given the stakes, sitting him entirely until the postseason is a reasonable option — taking him off the field to preserve whatever health he has for October. No timetable was announced Wednesday night, which means the situation will be updated daily. For Yankees fans tracking the postseason picture, the lower-leg update is the number to watch between now and September 29.
