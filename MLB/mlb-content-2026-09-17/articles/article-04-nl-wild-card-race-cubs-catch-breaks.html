NL Wild Card Race: Cubs Catch Breaks as Phillies, Padres and Diamondbacks All Lose Sept. 17

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | September 17, 2026

Chicago's Wild Card 1 lead grows to 1.5 games after all three NL Wild Card rivals fall on the same night — 10 games remain, Wild Card starts Sept. 29.

Sometimes the scoreboard gives you everything at once. On September 17, the Cubs watched all three of their NL Wild Card rivals lose — the Phillies, the Padres, and the Diamondbacks all fell on the same night, and Chicago's lead in the Wild Card 1 race grew accordingly. The Cubs sit at 84-68, holding WC1 by 1.5 games over Philadelphia, with San Diego 1.5 games back in WC3 and Arizona 4.0 games out.

Ten games remain. The Wild Card Series begins September 29. This is the part of the season where every game on the scoreboard matters as much as every game on the field.

The Wild Card Standings

The NL Wild Card picture entering the final 10 games:

The Chicago Cubs (84-68) hold the top Wild Card position with a 1.0-game lead over the Philadelphia Phillies (83-68) and a 1.5-game lead over the San Diego Padres (82-69). The Arizona Diamondbacks (80-72) are 4.0 games out and fading fast — the math is getting very difficult for Arizona at this stage.

The three Wild Card positions guarantee playoff spots, but the seeding matters: the No. 4 through No. 6 NL seeds play each other in the Wild Card round, with the higher seed hosting. The Cubs are currently positioned to host a Wild Card Series — a significant advantage given the energy at Wrigley Field in October.

The Rest of the NL Bracket

The top three NL seeds are effectively set: the Brewers as No. 1 (NL Central champs, 94-57), the Dodgers as No. 2, and the Braves as No. 3. Those three programs receive first-round byes and will watch the Wild Card Series from the stands before the NLDS begins.

Whoever wins the Cubs-Phillies-Padres race for Wild Card spots will face the reality of a three-game series on the road — or at home if Chicago maintains WC1. The ability to host a Wild Card Series gives the Cubs an important edge they earned by being the best of the three Wild Card teams over the course of the season.

The Sept. 25-27 Series to Watch

The most consequential upcoming series in the NL Wild Card race: the San Diego Padres host the Arizona Diamondbacks September 25-27 at Petco Park. If the Padres and D-backs are still mathematically alive, that three-game set could function as a de facto elimination series. The Cubs' schedule over those final days will run in parallel — making late September one of the most interesting stretches of the regular season finish.

What's Next

The Cubs play approximately 10 more games with Wild Card implications on the line every night. PCA's 40-40 chase gives the team a historical subplot, but the primary story is the standings. Chicago has positioned itself as the Wild Card leader with 10 games to play — a position earned by surviving the Sept. 17 scoreboard sweep. The next challenge arrives immediately: the Cubs' own games over the next week, and the nightly obligation to check where Philadelphia and San Diego stand. October baseball at Wrigley depends on surviving September first.
