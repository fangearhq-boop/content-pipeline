Yordan Alvarez's Triple Crown Is Slipping: .310 BA, 38 HR, 3 Behind Caminero With 10 Games Left

By Ryan Calloway | Ballpark Banter | September 17, 2026

Alvarez still leads the AL in batting average — but a six-week cold streak has put the Triple Crown out of reach unless he can reverse course in the final 10 games.

In early August, Yordan Alvarez had one of the best Triple Crown cases in the American League in years. His batting average led the AL. His home run total was close enough to force conversations. His RBI production had been among the best in the game all season. The last AL Triple Crown was Miguel Cabrera in 2012, and Alvarez looked like the player who might end the 14-year drought.

Then August happened. Since August 2, Alvarez is hitting .242 with 3 home runs and 12 RBI in 35 games. Six weeks of cold baseball that turned a legitimate Triple Crown chance into a long-shot math problem with approximately 10 games left.

The Triple Crown Numbers

Batting average: Alvarez sits at .310, still leading the American League. This is the category he has the best chance of holding — Chandler Simpson of the Tampa Bay Rays is within approximately five points, but Alvarez's lead is real.

Home runs: 38, trailing Junior Caminero of the Tampa Bay Rays, who leads the AL with 41. Caminero hit his 41st on September 16. With 10 games left, erasing a three-home-run deficit while a hot hitter continues to drive the ball is extremely difficult. It is not mathematically impossible — but it requires Alvarez to heat up while Caminero cools down.

RBI: Approximately 96, trailing Pete Alonso of the Baltimore Orioles, who leads the AL at approximately 100. The RBI gap is similarly difficult to close — a four-RBI deficit with 10 games left demands a dramatic run production surge against a backdrop of limited opportunities.

The Cold Stretch That Changed Everything

The most damaging data point in Alvarez's Triple Crown bid is the six-week slide. A .242 average, 3 home runs, and 12 RBI over 35 games represents one of the worst extended stretches of his career. The timing could not have been worse — August and September are when champions separate themselves, and Alvarez has been invisible by his own standards during the run that matters most.

The Astros are not in a pennant race at this stage, which means Houston's September games lack urgency. Whether that context affects Alvarez's at-bat approach is speculative — but the cold numbers are not.

What It Would Mean

The Triple Crown — leading the league in batting average, home runs and RBI simultaneously — is one of the rarest achievements in baseball. Only 18 players in AL/NL history have accomplished it. Cabrera's 2012 season was celebrated as a generational achievement, and the 14 years since have produced no repeat. Whoever wins the Triple Crown next will own a distinction that connects them to a lineage of the game's greatest offensive players.

Alvarez had the chance to be that player in 2026. The window has not fully closed — but it is nearly there.

What's Next

The Astros play approximately 10 more regular season games. For Alvarez to win the Triple Crown, he would need to hit multiple home runs while Caminero stalls, drive in runs at an elevated rate while Alonso's RBI total plateaus, and maintain his batting average lead. Any one of those would be possible. All three at once, while coming out of a six-week cold streak, is the order of difficulty that makes this race feel like it has already been decided. The batting title is still his to lose. The rest of the Triple Crown may have slipped away.
