Brewers Clinch Fourth Consecutive NL Central Title — First Team to Win Four Straight Since Division Creation in 1994

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | September 17, 2026

Milwaukee beat Pittsburgh 5-1 at PNC Park on Tuesday night while the Cubs lost to Atlanta — and the NL Central belongs to the Brewers for the fourth year running.

The Milwaukee Brewers clinched the NL Central division title on Tuesday, September 15, doing it the way this team does everything: on the road, with their best pitcher dealing, and with an efficiency that makes it look routine. Jacob Misiorowski struck out 7 in 5 shutout innings. The Brewers beat the Pittsburgh Pirates 5-1 at PNC Park. The Cubs lost to Atlanta the same night. The math was done.

Four consecutive NL Central titles. No team has ever done that in the 32-year history of this division — not the Cardinals with their powerhouse rosters, not the Cubs in their 2015-2020 window, not anyone. Until the Brewers.

The Record That Stands Alone

The NL Central was created as part of baseball's three-division alignment in 1994. In the 32 years since, no team had ever won four straight division titles in this division. The Cardinals won multiple back-to-back and three-year runs. The Cubs' 2016-2019 stretch included two division titles and two Wild Cards. But four consecutive? That belonged to no one.

It belongs to the Brewers now. Milwaukee won the NL Central in 2023, 2024, 2025, and 2026. The sequence was not supposed to be possible for a mid-market franchise that does not spend like the Dodgers or Yankees — but the Brewers' development pipeline and the work of their front office has made it real.

Misiorowski on the Clinch Night

If you were going to script a clinch night for the 2026 Brewers, you would script Jacob Misiorowski on the mound. He is the best pitcher in baseball this season — 19-8, 1.95 ERA, 236 strikeouts, the runaway Cy Young frontrunner — and he delivered exactly the kind of performance the moment called for.

Five innings, zero runs, seven strikeouts. It was the kind of outing that makes opposing hitters feel helpless — the same outing, replicated again, that Misiorowski has been giving Milwaukee all season. Even on a clinch night with champagne waiting in the clubhouse, he was locked in.

The Brewers finished the night at 94-57, the best record in baseball and the first team in any division to clinch in 2026. They are the NL's No. 1 seed with a first-round bye. They will play October baseball at home, with the deepest pitching staff in the league and a lineup that has outperformed preseason expectations all year.

What the Clinch Means for October

The No. 1 seed gives the Brewers home-field advantage throughout the NL playoffs. It also gives them the first-round bye, which means Misiorowski, their rotation, and their bullpen will have additional rest before the NLDS begins. For a team that runs through its pitching, that rest is a structural advantage.

The NL playoff bracket will include the Brewers as the top seed, the Dodgers second, and the Braves third — all set. The three Wild Card spots are still being decided among the Cubs, Phillies, Padres, and Diamondbacks. Whoever emerges from that race will face the Brewers' bracket in October.

What's Next

With the division clinched, the Brewers can manage their roster for October. Misiorowski's remaining starts will be managed with an eye toward his postseason workload. The final 10 games of the regular season are about optimization, not desperation — a position earned through four consecutive division titles and a franchise-best 94 wins. The October journey begins wherever the Wild Card bracket lands them. For the 2026 Brewers, the regular season is already a success. The real work starts in late September.
