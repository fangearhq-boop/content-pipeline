PCA Hits Home Runs 43 and 44, Breaks Cubs Franchise Record — 3 Steals From 40-40 Club

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | September 17, 2026

Pete Crow-Armstrong is now the home run king of Cubs history — and the 40-40 club is three stolen bases away.

Wednesday night at Wrigley Field, Pete Crow-Armstrong did something no Chicago Cub has ever done. He hit home run No. 43, then came back and hit No. 44 — in the same game — erasing Billy Williams' franchise record for home runs by a left-handed hitter that had stood since 1970. The Cubs beat the Atlanta Braves 8-4. PCA went 3-for-4 with 3 RBI and 3 runs scored. The ballpark shook with the kind of noise you make when history is being made.

The 40-40 scoreboard entering September 17: 44 home runs, 37 stolen bases. Three steals from a club that has only ever had six members.

Breaking the Record

Billy Williams hit 42 home runs in 1970 — a number that had stood as the Cubs' franchise record for left-handed hitters for 56 years. It was a mark that had survived generations of great Chicago players and seemed like the kind of number that might never fall.

PCA ended that on Wednesday. Home run No. 43 cleared it. No. 44 extended it. He now holds a record that will be measured against whatever he does for the rest of his career. For a player who arrived in 2024 as a toolsy outfield prospect, reaching this moment in Year 3 of his career is remarkable.

The 40-40 Math

The 40-40 club is six members: Barry Bonds (1996), Jose Canseco (1988), Alex Rodriguez (1998), Alfonso Soriano (2006), Carlos Beltran (2003), and Eric Davis (1987). That list represents some of the most complete players in baseball history — men who combined elite power with elite speed at the same time.

PCA needs three more stolen bases. With approximately 10 games remaining in the regular season, he has time. Whether he gets enough baserunning opportunities in those games is the variable. The Cubs are playing meaningful Wild Card games, which means lineup construction and situational play could limit his stolen base attempts. Every time he reaches base in September, the entire calculation shifts.

The Cubs Picture

PCA's historic run is unfolding in the middle of a pennant race. The Cubs hold the NL Wild Card 1 spot at 84-68, with the Phillies 1.0 game back in WC2 and the Padres 1.5 back in WC3. Every Cubs game in September carries Wild Card stakes — which means every at-bat, every stolen base attempt, every decision by manager Craig Counsell has layers to it.

The Brewers have already clinched the NL Central and the No. 1 NL seed. The Cubs' postseason path runs through the Wild Card — and whatever PCA does between now and September 29 will define both the history books and the bracket.

What's Next

The Cubs play approximately 10 more regular season games. Every night that PCA takes the field, the 40-40 countdown continues. He needs three steals from roughly 10 chances — a pace that is achievable but not guaranteed. The first stolen base of the three will generate enormous attention. The third will put PCA in company that only six players have ever occupied. For Cubs fans, the most important number on the scoreboard right now is not on the scoreboard at all: it is the stolen base total, updated game by game, sitting at 37 and counting.
