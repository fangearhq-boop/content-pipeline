Tarik Skubal's Market Opens Tonight — Every Team Can Now Make Their Pitch

By Marcus Cole | November 2, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

At 5 PM Eastern time tonight, baseball's offseason changes. The five-day exclusive negotiating window — which allowed only the teams that traded for Tarik Skubal before the 2026 deadline to negotiate with him — expires. From that moment, every organization in Major League Baseball is free to make their offer to the best pitcher in the game. The race for the most coveted free agent arm since Max Scherzer begins in earnest.

What Skubal Brings to the Table

The résumé is not subtle. Skubal went 16-5 with a 1.80 ERA and 252 strikeouts over 174.2 innings in 2026, winning his second consecutive AL Cy Young Award. He is 29 years old, which means a team signing him to a long-term deal is getting peak years. He has not had a significant injury. He gets swings and misses at an elite rate, generates ground balls when he needs to, and has demonstrated the ability to pitch in October, including a dominant World Series start with the Dodgers.

The average of 27 contract projections landed at 8 years and $355 million. ESPN has reported that the first financial standard Skubal wants to clear is widely believed to be $325 million. For context, that would make him one of the highest-paid pitchers in history on a per-year basis.

The Mets Are the Frontrunner

One insider, Connor Rogers, went further than most when he said the New York Mets are "committed" to landing Skubal. That language is unusual in free agency reporting — insiders typically hedge with phrases like "strong interest" or "making a serious push." "Committed" implies a decision, not a preference. Steve Cohen's Mets have the financial capacity to set a record, and adding Skubal to a rotation that already has pieces in place would make them an immediate World Series contender.

ESPN's analysis called the Mets one of the two likeliest landing spots, alongside the Dodgers. The Dodgers gave up real prospects to acquire Skubal before the deadline and clearly believe in him — the question is whether they want to invest $300-plus million in a starting pitcher after already stretching their payroll.

The Other Suitors

Beyond the Mets and Dodgers, the Tigers represent an interesting emotional case. Skubal was drafted by Detroit, spent his entire pre-trade career there, and said publicly that he would love to return. Whether Scott Boras allows that sentiment to translate into a below-market deal is a separate question. Boras clients tend to maximize value, and nostalgia has a price cap.

The Cubs, Phillies, and Blue Jays have all been mentioned as teams in serious pursuit. ESPN called the Phillies at least a dark horse candidate. The Cardinals were predicted by Sportsnaut as a possible landing spot, though a team that has not signed a $100 million free agent in 15 years would need to make a significant organizational commitment to compete for Skubal.

The CBA Wildcard

Every team at the table tonight is operating with one eye on December 1. The MLB collective bargaining agreement expires that day at 11:59 PM, and a lockout is widely expected if no deal is reached. A lockout would freeze all player movement, meaning any deal that has not been signed would sit on ice until a new agreement is reached. For a player of Skubal's caliber, both sides have strong incentives to complete a deal before that deadline — the player gets certainty, and the team locks in the most coveted asset in the offseason before the market potentially stalls.

The next four weeks will tell the story of this winter. Tonight is the first chapter.

What's Next

With the market now fully open, expect the pace of Skubal reporting to accelerate dramatically. Teams will begin submitting formal offers in the coming days. The GM meetings on November 10-12 in New York could produce a framework or announcement. The December 1 deadline creates a hard clock. If history is any guide — Boras clients often push their negotiations to the edge of a market or the edge of a calendar — Skubal's deal may not be done until the final days before December 1. Or it could be done by Thanksgiving. The only certainty is that tonight, the race is officially on.
