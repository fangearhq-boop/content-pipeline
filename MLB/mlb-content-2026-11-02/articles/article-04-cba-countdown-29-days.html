29 Days: The CBA Countdown That Could Freeze Baseball's Entire Offseason

By Marcus Cole | November 2, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

In 29 days, the most consequential free agent market in recent MLB history could come to a sudden stop. The collective bargaining agreement between Major League Baseball and the MLB Players Association expires December 1 at 11:59 PM. If no new deal is in place at that moment, a lockout begins — and with it, the freeze of all player movement, signings, and trades. Tarik Skubal, Teruaki Sato, Pete Alonso, and dozens of other unsigned free agents would be left in limbo until a new agreement is reached.

Where the Negotiations Stand

The core disagreement is structural. Owners have proposed a $245 million salary cap with a $171 million floor — a mechanism that would fundamentally change how all 30 teams are permitted to spend on player payroll. For owners, a cap represents cost certainty and competitive balance. For players, it represents a ceiling on compensation and a tool that has historically suppressed salaries in other professional leagues.

The MLBPA opposes any hard cap. Their counter framework centers on a luxury tax threshold of approximately $300 million — a high number that still allows large-market teams to spend freely while penalizing the most extreme outliers. The union has also pushed for a minimum salary increase from $780,000 to $1 million, a change that would benefit the most underpaid players in the system.

Industry insiders estimate the odds of a deal being reached before December 1 at approximately 5%. That is not a number that suggests optimism.

The History the Players Remember

The last MLB lockout began December 2, 2021 and lasted 99 days, ending March 10, 2022 — eliminating portions of spring training and the first week of the regular season. Players remember that episode clearly, and it has informed the union's strategy in these negotiations. The MLBPA believes it caved too quickly in prior rounds of bargaining and has signaled a greater willingness to hold its position this time.

The 99-day timeline is the baseline scenario. A shorter lockout is possible if the sides are closer than public posturing suggests. A longer one is equally possible if the fundamental cap-versus-no-cap disagreement remains unresolved.

What a Lockout Means for the Offseason

A lockout freezes all player movement. No signings. No trades. No contract extensions. Minor league contracts for players who have not yet signed would also be halted. The Skubal negotiation, wherever it stands, would stop. The Sato posting window, which has a 45-day deadline from November 1, could be suspended or complicated by the terms of any new agreement. Teams that rushed to close deals before December 1 would have an advantage; teams that played for maximum leverage and waited would be left on hold.

Spring Training 2027 is currently scheduled to begin February 19. A lockout of the 2021-22 duration would push that back by weeks. A prolonged dispute could threaten Opening Day.

What's Next

The GM meetings on November 10-12 represent the first significant gathering of the offseason and the last real opportunity for informal progress before the calendar becomes truly urgent. The QO deadline on November 17 will clarify the compensation structure for several free agents. After that, the clock runs with increasing speed toward December 1. Both sides have made their positions clear. Whether those positions move in the next 29 days will determine whether the 2026-27 offseason is remembered for a historic free agency class or for being frozen before it could fully play out.
