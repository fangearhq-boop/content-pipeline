Teruaki Sato Is Available — Seven Teams Are Chasing Japan's Triple Crown Winner

By Jake Torres | November 2, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The Hanshin Tigers posted Teruaki Sato on Saturday, and the MLB market is already treating him as one of the most intriguing players to come out of Japan in years. The 2026 NPB Triple Crown winner — .306 batting average, 39 home runs, 106 RBI — brings the kind of middle-of-the-order production that seven teams have reportedly identified as a priority. The 45-day negotiating window is now open. The race has started.

What Made Sato Japan's Best Hitter

Sato posted a .992 OPS in 143 games for the Hanshin Tigers in 2026. His .609 slugging percentage represents elite power at the NPB level, and a .383 on-base percentage shows he can work counts and take his walks. He won the Triple Crown — leading the Central League in batting average, home runs, and RBI — which had not been accomplished in the NPB for several years before his run.

The main concern for evaluators is his 26.7% strikeout rate. That is high even by American League standards, where strikeout rates league-wide have climbed significantly over the past decade. Whether his bat speed and approach can translate against major league pitching — where breaking balls are sharper and fastball commands tighter — is the central question every team is trying to answer.

The Mets: First Base Is Open

Pete Alonso declined his $24 million option with the Mets this week, leaving a significant hole at first base for a franchise trying to compete for a World Series title. Sato projects as a first baseman at the MLB level, which makes the Mets' interest immediate and structural rather than speculative. Steve Cohen has the financial capacity to absorb a significant contract, and replacing Alonso with a player who hit 39 home runs against elite competition is a logical move.

Multiple reports have linked the Mets to Sato as one of the frontrunning teams in the race.

The Yankees: Third Base Needed Urgently

New York's third base situation was one of the worst in the American League in 2026. Sato can play the hot corner, which is where the Yankees' interest originates. One insider delivered a telling prediction that the Yankees would be in, though another expressed doubt about how aggressively the organization would pursue him given their other offseason priorities.

If the Yankees land Sato at third base while also addressing other needs in the rotation market, they would immediately become a more formidable offensive unit.

The Rest of the Field

Japanese outlet Sports Hochi listed seven teams with genuine interest: the Mets, Yankees, Dodgers, Mariners, Blue Jays, Phillies, and Giants. The Dodgers are involved in nearly every major international pursuit and have the roster flexibility and financial resources to absorb Sato regardless of his position. The Mariners have a history of signing Japanese players and have a stated need for middle-of-the-order production. The Blue Jays and Phillies add market pressure to the negotiation.

What's Next

The 45-day posting window began November 1, which means teams have until approximately December 16 to complete a deal. However, the December 1 CBA expiration complicates the timeline significantly. If a lockout begins before a deal is signed, negotiations could be halted or limited by the terms of the new labor agreement. Teams that want Sato have strong incentives to move quickly. The next two weeks — before the December 1 cliff — may determine whether this signing happens in this offseason cycle or gets pushed to the other side of a new CBA.
