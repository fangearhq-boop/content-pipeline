Free Agency Day 3: Giolito, Eflin, Gray, and Canning Join a Stacked Rotation Market

By Ryan Calloway | November 2, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The MLB free agent pitching market was already headlined by Tarik Skubal. Now it is deep. Option deadlines over the weekend produced a fresh wave of starting pitchers officially entering the open market, giving teams multiple legitimate rotation options at every price point. For the organizations that miss on Skubal — or cannot afford to compete at his level — the next tier of arms just got significantly more interesting.

The New Arrivals

Lucas Giolito and Griffin Canning were both released by the Padres, joining the free agent pool alongside their former teammates. Giolito, a former White Sox All-Star who touched elite levels of performance earlier in his career, has battled injuries in recent seasons that complicated his market. He is a reclamation-project candidate for teams with pitching development infrastructure and patience.

Canning is a different profile — a right-hander who provides reliable innings when healthy but has not established himself as a frontline starter. His value lies in depth and durability for teams that need rotation insurance rather than a true No. 2 or No. 3.

Sonny Gray and Zach Eflin declined their respective options, choosing the open market over team security. Gray, in the late stages of a long career, has remained effective in shorter stints and will attract interest from contenders looking to add a veteran presence behind their top starters. Eflin is one of the more reliable innings-eaters available at his career stage — a No. 4 or No. 5 starter who stays healthy and eats innings without drama.

What This Means for the Market

The arrival of these pitchers alongside the already-existing 155-player free agent pool creates a deep but tiered market. Skubal is the clear top of the class. Below him, there are legitimate No. 2 and No. 3 starters available at various price points. Teams rebuilding their rotations have options beyond just competing for the headliner.

For contending teams that need to fill multiple rotation spots, the combination of Skubal plus a secondary pick from this group — Eflin, Gray, or even Giolito on a prove-it deal — becomes a viable strategy. The depth of the market benefits buyers and complicates it for sellers: when there are multiple options, no single player can command the premium a thinner market would produce.

GM Meetings as the First Acceleration Point

The GM meetings run November 10-12 in New York. This is the first major gathering of baseball's decision-makers since the World Series ended, and it typically serves as a catalyzing moment for the early phase of the offseason. Deals announced at or immediately after the GM meetings are common. For the pitching market, these eight days between now and November 10 represent the quiet before the storm.

The QO deadline on November 17 is another marker — qualifying offers to eligible players must be extended or declined, which will clarify the compensation picture for several of the most attractive free agents and shift the decision calculus for both players and teams.

What's Next

The next ten days will see the free agent pool stabilize as most option decisions are finalized. The focus will shift to the GM meetings, where informal conversations become formal frameworks. For the rotation market specifically, expect Skubal's deal to define the ceiling and Gray and Eflin's signings to establish the mid-market floor. Teams with urgent rotation needs — and there are several in the American and National Leagues — are operating with a clear urgency created by the December 1 CBA deadline. The window for getting deals done before a potential freeze is narrowing.
