Pete Alonso Is on the Market — Here Are His Best Fits at 28 With 226 Career Home Runs

By Jake Torres | November 2, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Pete Alonso turned 28 this summer. He has 226 career home runs. He has hit 40 or more home runs in three separate seasons. He declined the Mets' $24 million option, choosing to bet on himself in a free agent market that he believes will pay him significantly more. He is right about that. Scott Boras, his agent, will make certain of it. The question is not whether Alonso gets paid — it is where he goes and what the contract looks like.

The Contract Landscape

Projected range: $150 million to $200 million over four to five years. That puts the average annual value somewhere between $35 million and $40 million — which would rank among the highest for a first baseman in MLB history. For context, Alonso's three 40-plus homer seasons demonstrate a level of sustained power production that is increasingly rare even in a league where home run rates have been elevated for years.

The CBA expiration on December 1 creates urgency for everyone. Alonso and Boras benefit from a market where teams are motivated to close deals before a potential freeze. If they can generate a bidding war in the next three weeks, the result could push toward the top of that projected range.

Best Fit No. 1: Red Sox

Veteran insider Bob Nightengale called the Red Sox the "perfect fit" for Alonso in a column this week, and the case is compelling. Boston needs a middle-of-the-order presence. Fenway Park's short left-field wall — the Green Monster — is designed for right-handed power hitters, and Alonso is among the best right-handed power hitters in the game. The Red Sox have the financial capacity to compete at Alonso's projected price point and the organizational motivation to add a marquee player after seasons of mixed results.

Best Fit No. 2: Yankees

The Yankees perennially seek right-handed power at first base, and Alonso represents a proven solution. He would project as the middle-of-the-order bat the Yankees have been missing when their lineup has been inconsistent. One insider named the Yankees as a top destination for Alonso, and the organizational fit at the position is straightforward.

The Mets Factor

The Mets declined Alonso's option after years of productive service, and it is worth examining whether a reunion is possible under the right terms. However, the Mets are simultaneously pursuing Teruaki Sato, who projects as a first baseman at the major league level. If the Mets land Sato, Alonso becomes an ex-Met looking for a new home permanently. If they miss on Sato, Alonso suddenly becomes a logical candidate to return to Flushing under a new, longer deal. The Mets' Sato pursuit is effectively a hedge — they are building a path that does not require Alonso to return, but is not fully closed to him either.

What's Next

Alonso's market will develop alongside the broader offseason calendar. With the GM meetings on November 10-12 and the QO deadline on November 17, expect his name to generate significant reporting over the next three weeks. The December 1 CBA deadline means Boras will want a deal done quickly — his clients rarely benefit from a frozen market. A signing before Thanksgiving is not out of the question. The Red Sox and Yankees are the names to watch, but do not rule out a surprise team that comes in aggressive once the market fully opens and teams begin making commitments.
