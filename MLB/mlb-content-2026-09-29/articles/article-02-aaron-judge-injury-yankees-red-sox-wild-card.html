Aaron Judge's Calf Injury Clouds Yankees' Wild Card Opener Against Red Sox Tonight

By Jake Torres | September 29, 2026

Aaron Judge will almost certainly not play tonight when the Yankees open the Wild Card series against the Boston Red Sox at 8 PM ET. That sentence would have seemed unthinkable when the season began. Now it is simply the largest variable in the most-watched game of Wild Card Day.

The Yankees' captain and three-time American League MVP last played on September 16, leaving a game with what has since been diagnosed as a strained right calf — specifically the soleus muscle. Manager Aaron Boone said on Monday that the team is "planning on him not being a part of" the Wild Card series, though he left a narrow opening, noting that Judge intended to take batting practice on the field Tuesday as a final evaluation.

A Season Defined by Absence

The 2026 season was already one Judge would rather forget. He played in only 66 games — the lowest total of his major league career, not counting the shortened 2020 season or his brief call-up in 2016. Earlier in the year, a right rib stress fracture kept him on the injured list for an extended stretch. He returned, produced when healthy, and then the calf ended his regular season with two weeks remaining.

The Yankees qualified for the postseason anyway, finishing as the No. 4 AL seed. They drew the Red Sox — the No. 5 seed — in a rivalry matchup that should have been straightforward viewing for New York. Without Judge, the calculus is considerably more uncertain.

Judge has pushed to return. He told reporters that he wanted to make his case to be in the starting lineup, that he believed he could help the team even if not at 100 percent. But the framing from Boone on Monday — "planning on him not being a part of it" — suggests the medical staff has counseled caution over hope.

The Yankees Without Their Best Player

How do the Yankees compete in a best-of-three series without the centerpiece of their offense? The answer depends on which other hitters are producing and whether the pitching can compensate for the absence of the lineup's most dangerous threat.

New York's rotation and bullpen have been strong enough to win games in a short series. The Wild Card format rewards teams with dominant pitching even when the offense is incomplete. But the Red Sox know Judge is likely out, and they will design their approach accordingly — throwing no extra strikes to the middle of the order, keeping everything sharp, and betting that New York cannot overcome the gap without its captain.

For the Red Sox, this is as close to a favorable draw as the No. 5 seed could have asked for entering the Wild Card round. They are the underdog on paper, playing in Yankee Stadium, with the full knowledge that their opponent's best player is watching from the dugout.

What's Next

Yankees vs. Red Sox Wild Card Game 1 begins tonight at 8 PM ET on NBC/Peacock. If Judge's batting practice session goes well enough that the team reverses course and activates him, it will be one of the stories of Wild Card Day. If he does not play — which remains the more likely outcome — the Yankees face a best-of-three series as a team still capable of winning, but measurably diminished from what they are at full health.

The Wild Card series runs through October 1. For one of baseball's most storied franchises, the next 72 hours without their captain will define what this team actually is when it matters most.
