From 121 Losses to October Baseball: The White Sox's Impossible Turnaround

By Ryan Calloway | September 29, 2026

On Opening Day 2026, the Chicago White Sox had a 1.1% probability of making the playoffs. By September 24, they clinched a postseason berth with a 9-1 win over Kansas City. Tonight they play Game 1 of the Wild Card series at Houston.

No team in Major League Baseball history has made the playoffs after three consecutive seasons with 100 or more losses. The White Sox just did it.

The Numbers That Make This Remarkable

From 2023 through 2025, the Chicago White Sox lost 324 games. In 2024 alone, they finished 41-121 — the most losses by any team in a 162-game season in Major League Baseball history, surpassing the 1962 expansion Mets. A franchise that had been a championship contender in living memory had become the defining example of organizational dysfunction.

The 2026 White Sox were not expected to fix any of this in one season. Their projected playoff odds in April reflected that reality: 1.1%. That number was not pessimism — it was an honest assessment of what the organization had accumulated through three years of rebuilding at the wrong pace.

What happened instead was baseball. They played better than expected. The young talent developed faster than projected. The roster decisions made over the rebuild started producing wins at the right time. By the final week of September, Chicago had a winning record at 84-78 and a postseason berth secured. The gap between 1.1% probability and "playing in October" is not measured in statistics alone.

The City Gets Both Teams

For the first time since 2020 — and only the fourth time in their shared history — both Chicago teams are in the postseason in the same year. The Cubs reached the playoffs as the No. 5 NL seed. The White Sox qualified from the AL Wild Card. The city last saw both teams in October in 1906, 2008, and 2020.

There is something about a baseball city having both teams competitive at the same time that transcends box scores. Chicago's North Side and South Side both have a reason to watch October baseball tonight. That has not been the case often, and it has never come with as dramatic a backdrop for one side as it does for the 2026 White Sox.

What's Next

White Sox at Astros, Game 1, is tonight at 5:00 PM ET on Peacock/NBCSN at Minute Maid Park. The White Sox face a Houston team that made its own history this year — the first sub-.500 division champion in MLB history. The series is best-of-three with the Astros hosting all games.

Whatever happens in this series, the 2026 White Sox have already written their story. The records they set in 2024 will be remembered differently now — not as the final chapter of a failed rebuild, but as the low point before a team that refused to accept that outcome came back. Three straight 100-loss seasons to the playoffs. It had never been done. It has been done now.
