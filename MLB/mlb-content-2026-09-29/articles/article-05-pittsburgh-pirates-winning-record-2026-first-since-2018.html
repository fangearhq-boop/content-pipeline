Pittsburgh Pirates End Seven Losing Seasons With 82-80 Finish, First Winning Record Since 2018

By Jake Torres | September 29, 2026

On the last Sunday of the 2026 regular season, the Pittsburgh Pirates beat the Detroit Tigers 4-2. Their final record: 82-80.

Two games over .500. No playoff berth. No postseason celebration. But for a franchise that had not finished with a winning record in eight years, those 82 wins represent something worth acknowledging.

Eight Years of Losing

The Pittsburgh Pirates last finished above .500 in 2018, when they went 82-79. The seven seasons that followed were uniformly losing ones — a stretch of consecutive below-.500 records that stretched from 2019 through 2025, encompassing multiple rebuilds, front-office changes, and the kind of slow organizational frustration that tests fan loyalty without delivering results.

Pittsburgh's three rivers identity is baseball-steeped, and PNC Park remains one of the most beautiful venues in the sport. The fans never stopped showing up. The losses accumulated regardless. For seven straight years, September offered no reason for optimism about the standings — only discussions of prospects and potential that never quite materialized into wins.

How They Got There

The Pirates went 15-9 in September 2026 — a closing run strong enough to push them definitively over .500 with time remaining to secure the milestone. Their final win came against Detroit in a game started by young arms Jared Jones and Konnor Griffin, two of the pitchers who represent the core of Pittsburgh's foundation heading into 2027.

The 11-win improvement over the prior season is the clearest evidence that the direction has genuinely changed. From one year to the next, the Pirates added wins in a context where most rebuilding teams struggle to show consistent progress. The final record — 82-80, third in the NL Central behind the dominant Milwaukee Brewers — reflects a team that is no longer losing first and asking questions later.

What's Next

The Pirates finished two wins short of a Wild Card spot in the NL. They were not playing October baseball — but they finished the regular season knowing they were competitive enough to have been in the conversation.

Pittsburgh's offseason will begin with a rare source of leverage: a winning foundation, young pitching with demonstrated big-league production, and a fan base that has waited through eight years of losing to feel good about their team again. The 2027 season begins with the question of whether this is a one-year exception or the start of a new era in Pittsburgh. Eighty-two wins in 2026 says the answer might be the latter. Baseball will provide the verdict over the next several seasons. For now, Pittsburgh can simply celebrate finally being above .500.
