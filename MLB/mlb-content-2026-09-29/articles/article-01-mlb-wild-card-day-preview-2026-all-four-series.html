Wild Card Day 2026: Your Complete Guide to All Four Series Opening Tonight

By Marcus Cole | September 29, 2026

The 2026 MLB postseason begins today, and the Wild Card card is more compelling than most October schedules manage. Four series, four game ones, four different storylines that stretch from the historically bizarre to the historically inevitable.

All games are on NBC, Peacock, or NBCSN. Here is everything you need to know before the first pitch.

Phillies at Braves — 2:00 PM ET

The Philadelphia Phillies clinched the final National League Wild Card spot on the last day of the regular season, holding on with a 7-3 win over the Tampa Bay Rays. They come into Atlanta as the No. 6 NL seed, facing the No. 3 Braves, who have home-field advantage for all three possible games.

The Phillies have been in this situation before — they made postseason runs in recent years and know how to compete in October. The Braves have the advantages: home crowd, higher seed, and a lineup built to punish Wild Card opponents who arrived on a late-season roll. This is the afternoon game, but it may set the tone for the NL side of the bracket.

White Sox at Astros — 5:00 PM ET

There is no polite way to describe what this matchup represents historically: the first sub-.500 division champion in MLB history (Houston, 81-81) hosting the first team in MLB history to reach the playoffs after three straight 100-loss seasons (Chicago White Sox, 84-78). Someone is going to win this series. That fact alone is remarkable.

The Astros clinched the AL West on the final day with a 9-0 win over the Athletics — their ninth postseason in 10 seasons, fifth AL West title in six years, all while never finishing above .500 this year. They have home-field advantage and institutional postseason experience. The White Sox had a 1.1% playoff probability on Opening Day. Both of these things are true simultaneously. The 5 PM slot at Minute Maid Park is where baseball's strangest 2026 subplot gets resolved.

Yankees vs. Red Sox — 8:00 PM ET

The signature rivalry in American sports, in the postseason, in primetime. The Yankees are the No. 4 AL seed. The Red Sox are No. 5. This is precisely the kind of Wild Card matchup that keeps casual fans engaged with October baseball.

The caveat everyone will be discussing before first pitch: Aaron Judge. The Yankees' captain and three-time AL MVP last played on September 16 with a right calf strain, and manager Aaron Boone said on Monday that the team is "planning on him not being a part of" the Wild Card series. Judge was hoping to take batting practice Tuesday as a last-ditch test, but New York's preparation has been based on his absence. The Red Sox know this. The primetime audience will be watching the Yankees try to overcome their most significant absence of the season in a best-of-three with no room for error.

Cubs at Padres — 10:00 PM ET

The late-night card belongs to the West Coast, where the No. 4 NL seed Padres host the No. 5 Cubs at 10 PM ET. The Cubs are back in the postseason. The Padres have home-field advantage in San Diego for all three games of the series.

This is the game for West Coast fans and those who prefer their October baseball late. It is also a matchup between two franchises that have learned to treat October as an expectation rather than a surprise — both have been here recently and know what postseason intensity requires.

What's Next

The Wild Card series runs through October 1, with potential Game 3s available in each series. The four winners advance to the Division Series, where the No. 1 Brewers (103-59) and No. 2 Dodgers have first-round byes on the NL side, and the No. 1 Rays and No. 2 Guardians wait on the AL side.

Four series begin today. Twelve possible games remain before the field narrows to eight. For fans of every competing team, the next three days will either justify the season or end it. Wild Card Day is exactly that kind of baseball — nothing padded, nothing safe, every game a must-win from the moment the lineup cards are exchanged.
