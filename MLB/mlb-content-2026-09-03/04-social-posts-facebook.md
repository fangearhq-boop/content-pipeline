### STORY 1: PCA 40-40 Chase

#### Long-Form Post
Pete Crow-Armstrong needs 2 home runs and 8 stolen bases in 26 games to accomplish something only five players in MLB history have ever done.

The 40-40 club is not a group with a lot of members. Jose Canseco, in 1988, was the first. Barry Bonds joined in 1996. Alex Rodriguez did it in 1998. Alfonso Soriano in 2006. And Ronald Acuña Jr., most recently, in 2023. That's it. That's the whole list across more than a century of professional baseball.

Pete Crow-Armstrong, 24 years old, is standing at 38 home runs and 32 stolen bases with 26 games to play. His -1100 NL MVP odds suggest the award is already spoken for. But 40-40 — that's a different kind of legacy. That's history that doesn't fade.

The Cubs are locked into the No. 1 Wild Card spot. They're not playing for seeding every night. PCA is playing for his name in a sentence that includes five of the greatest players to ever do it.

26 games. He needs a pace of about 1 HR every 13 games and 1 SB every 3.3 games. That's well within his capability. The question isn't whether PCA can do this. The question is whether September will cooperate. ⚾

### STORY 2: Misiorowski — Cy Young Locked?

#### Long-Form Post
There were some who wondered, after August, whether Jacob Misiorowski's Cy Young case had a crack in it.

His average velocity had dipped from 100.5 mph to 98.7 mph. In a pitcher whose entire dominance is built on elite velocity, that's the kind of number that gets noticed. Was he tiring? Was there an injury lurking? Would September prove to be the moment the 24-year-old starter hit the wall?

Tuesday night at Wrigley Field answered the question. Eight strikeouts. Scoreless innings. And a 101.5 mph fastball that registered as the hardest pitch from a starting pitcher this season. Misiorowski didn't just win the game — he put to rest the one lingering doubt about his NL Cy Young campaign.

Season line: 14-5, 1.73 ERA, 222 strikeouts, 0.75 WHIP. Those are numbers that haven't been assembled by a starter in generations. The Brewers are in first place in the NL Central. And their ace is the most dominant pitcher in the National League with 26 games to go.

The Cy Young race isn't a race at this point. It's a coronation. ⚾

### STORY 3: Cardinals 8-6 Dodgers — Saggese's Big Night

#### Long-Form Post
In the sixth inning of Wednesday night's game at Dodger Stadium, Yoshinobu Yamamoto was cruising with a no-hitter intact. Then Thomas Saggese stepped in.

Saggese sent a first-pitch Yamamoto sinker into the center-field seats — a two-run home run that not only ended the no-hitter but gave the Cardinals their first lead of the game. It was the kind of swing that shifts an entire ballgame's momentum.

Four innings later, with the game tied and the Cardinals in the 10th inning, Saggese was at the plate again. Two strikes. Automatic runner José Fermín at third. Reliever Brock Stewart on the mound. Saggese dropped an infield single to give St. Louis the lead — and Joshua Báez doubled in two more for insurance.

Cardinals 8, Dodgers 6.

The loss extended Los Angeles's recent skid to six losses in their last eight games. Shohei Ohtani went 0-for-4 with four strikeouts, and manager Dave Roberts mentioned a left knee issue and the possibility of rest. The Dodgers (82-57) are safe in the playoff picture, but this pattern — losing close games, struggling with runners in scoring position, Ohtani not himself — is worth watching as October approaches.

For the Cardinals (70-70), still fighting for a Wild Card spot, Wednesday night's win was everything. Leo Bernal hit his first MLB home run in just his second big league game. Thomas Saggese broke up a no-hitter AND won the game. September in baseball is unlike anything else. ⚾

### STORY 4: Alvarez Triple Crown — 26 Games to Make History

#### Long-Form Post
Nobody has won baseball's Triple Crown in the American League since Miguel Cabrera did it for the Detroit Tigers in 2012 — 14 years and counting since the last time any hitter led a league in batting average, home runs, and RBIs in the same season.

Yordan Alvarez of the Houston Astros is trying to end that drought.

Entering September, Alvarez leads the AL in all three categories: .322 batting average, 36 home runs, and 91 RBIs. The challenge? Three different players are close in three different categories. Junior Caminero sits just one home run behind at 35. Chandler Simpson is 14 points behind in batting average at .308. And Pete Alonso is hovering right around Alvarez's RBI total.

A Triple Crown requires leading in all three when the final out is recorded — and the arithmetic can change in a single series. A cold week from Alvarez or a hot streak from any of his rivals can reshuffle the leaderboard entirely.

The Astros have 26 games remaining. If Alvarez maintains his pace in all three categories and holds off his competition, he will become only the second player in 14 years to achieve one of baseball's most elite individual accomplishments. The last time it happened, Cabrera went on to win the AL MVP in a unanimous vote.

This is what September baseball is made for. ⚾

### STORY 5: Ohtani "Not Close to Optimal" — Dodgers' October Question

#### Long-Form Post
The numbers are hard to ignore.

Since August 18, Shohei Ohtani has gone 7-for-51 at the plate. That's a .137 batting average over 16 games, with 20 strikeouts. For context: Ohtani entered that stretch as one of baseball's best hitters and the leading PCA competitor for NL MVP. What's been happening since is something entirely different.

After a 0-for-4 night with four strikeouts against the Cardinals on Wednesday, Dodgers manager Dave Roberts confirmed the worst of what fans had been suspecting: Ohtani is "not close to optimal." A left knee issue is contributing to the decline at the plate. Roberts mentioned that the team is considering rest, and that he'd be speaking to both Ohtani and the training staff to determine how to best handle the situation.

The Dodgers (82-57) are still a clear playoff team. They're not in danger of missing October. But the version of Shohei Ohtani who shows up to the postseason matters enormously for a team that views him as its most important player.

The Dodgers have three-plus weeks of regular season to get Ohtani right. It starts with Thursday's conversation between Roberts and his star. If rest is what it takes, the logic would be to use it now — not in the NLDS. ⚾
