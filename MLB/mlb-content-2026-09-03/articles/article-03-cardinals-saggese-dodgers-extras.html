Saggese Breaks Up the No-No, Then Wins It in the 10th: Cardinals 8, Dodgers 6

By Ryan Calloway

September 3, 2026

Thomas Saggese had a night that Cardinals fans will be telling stories about.

In the sixth inning at Dodger Stadium, Yoshinobu Yamamoto was working with a no-hitter intact, looking like the version of himself who had dominated baseball for the better part of three seasons. Then Saggese stepped in and hit a first-pitch sinker into the center-field seats for a two-run home run — ending the no-hitter, cutting the deficit, and turning the game entirely upside down.

Four innings later, with the score tied at six and an automatic runner on the bases in the 10th, Saggese came through again. Two-strike count, Brock Stewart on the mound: an infield single to second that scored José Fermín from third and gave St. Louis a 6-5 lead. Joshua Báez followed with a pinch-hit double to push in two more insurance runs. Cardinals 8, Dodgers 6. Final.

The Dodgers' September Slide

Los Angeles is 82-57 and safely in the postseason picture — but the Dodgers have lost six of their last eight games, and Wednesday was a particularly uncomfortable defeat. Yoshinobu Yamamoto had a no-hitter through five innings before Saggese broke it up. Freddie Freeman homered. Mookie Betts homered. And yet the Dodgers still found a way to lose.

Shohei Ohtani went 0-for-4 with four strikeouts, and manager Dave Roberts confirmed after the game that Ohtani is "not close to optimal" due to a left knee issue. Roberts indicated he'd be having conversations with Ohtani and the training staff about potentially giving the superstar some rest before October.

In the Cardinals' dugout, none of that mattered. They came in as a .500 team with Wild Card dreams, and they left Dodger Stadium with a dramatic extra-innings win in which their third baseman was the hero — twice.

Leo Bernal's Moment

Lost slightly in the drama of Saggese's performance was a quieter kind of milestone: Leo Bernal hit his first MLB home run on Wednesday night, just his second game in the big leagues after debuting on Tuesday. For the Cardinals, a team fighting tooth and nail for a playoff spot, moments like Bernal's debut home run carry extra meaning in September.

At 70-70, St. Louis is still alive in the Wild Card race. Every win in September has the weight of a playoff game behind it.

What's Next

The Cardinals (70-70) continue their road trip against the Dodgers on Thursday, looking to take the series. The Dodgers (82-57) are expected to discuss Ohtani's status with trainers Thursday morning. For St. Louis, a Wild Card spot requires staying hot through September — and Wednesday's dramatic comeback is exactly the kind of win that defines late-season momentum. Watch the Cardinals' September schedule closely: they play most of their remaining games against playoff-caliber opponents, making every game a crucial barometer of their postseason viability.
