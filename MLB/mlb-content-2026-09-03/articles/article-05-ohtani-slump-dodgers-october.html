Ohtani "Not Close to Optimal": Dodgers Navigate Their Star's September Slump

By Jake Torres

September 3, 2026

Shohei Ohtani has gone 7-for-51 at the plate since August 18. That's a .137 batting average over 16 games, with 20 strikeouts. On Wednesday, he went 0-for-4 with four strikeouts against the Cardinals — contributing to a 8-6 loss in 10 innings that extended Los Angeles's recent skid to six losses in their last eight games.

After the game, Dodgers manager Dave Roberts confirmed what the numbers had been suggesting: Ohtani is "not close to optimal." A left knee issue is affecting his performance at the plate, and Roberts indicated the team is exploring whether rest is the right approach heading into the final month of the season.

"I'll talk to Shohei tomorrow and talk to the trainers," Roberts said. "We need to figure out how we handle this and get him back on track."

What's Going Wrong

Ohtani's swing during the slump has been notably out of sync by his own elite standards. The left knee issue — separate from the torn UCL that ended his pitching career earlier and led to his Tommy John surgery — appears to be affecting his ability to drive through his lower body with full force. The result is a hitter who looks like a compromised version of himself: chasing pitches he normally takes, missing pitches he normally drives, and unable to generate the exit velocity that makes him one of baseball's most dangerous hitters.

The Dodgers (82-57) are not in any postseason jeopardy — they're comfortable in the NL playoff picture regardless of what happens the rest of September. That context makes the calculation clearer: if rest now gives them a better version of Ohtani in October, the cost of missing regular-season games is minimal.

The October Stakes

Ohtani pitched through a knee issue for a period of the season before being shut down from pitching duties for the remainder of the year. As a hitter, he has continued to play — but the September slump suggests that simply playing through pain is not producing the results the Dodgers need. A fully healthy Ohtani hitting at his normal production level is one of the most dangerous players in a postseason lineup. An Ohtani at 70 percent, against October pitching, is a more uncertain proposition.

Roberts's willingness to publicly discuss rest — rather than the typical manager's formula of "he's fine, just working through it" — suggests the Dodgers are taking this seriously. The final 26 games are an opportunity to reset Ohtani's body and mind before the calendar turns to October.

What's Next

Dave Roberts was expected to meet with Ohtani and the Dodgers' training staff on Thursday to discuss options, including possible rest days. The Dodgers (82-57) continue their homestand through the week. Watch for lineup decisions and whether Ohtani appears in the starting lineup Thursday — any absence could signal the team is actively managing his knee load. The goal is straightforward: get Ohtani healthy before the playoffs begin. If September is the price, the Dodgers should be willing to pay it.
