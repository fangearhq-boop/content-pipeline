Misiorowski Silences the Doubters at Wrigley — and the NL Cy Young Is His

By Jake Torres

September 3, 2026

There was a question lingering around Jacob Misiorowski heading into September. It wasn't about his ERA or his strikeout rate or his dominance in the National League. It was about one number: 98.7.

That was Misiorowski's average fastball velocity in August — down from 100.5 in the earlier months of the season. For a pitcher whose entire existence is predicated on elite velocity, a 1.8 mph dip is the kind of data point that generates conversations about workload management, fatigue, and whether a 24-year-old starter in his second full season was beginning to show cracks under the weight of a historic campaign.

On Tuesday night at Wrigley Field, Misiorowski posted a 101.5 mph fastball. Eight strikeouts. Zero runs. Conversation over.

The Season Line

It helps to step back and appreciate what Misiorowski has done this year in full context: 14-5. 1.73 ERA. 222 strikeouts. 0.75 WHIP. A .151 batting average against. A 37.8 percent whiff rate. Those numbers weren't built in one or two outings — they've been his baseline for months.

The Brewers are now in first place in the NL Central, with Tuesday's win over the Cubs as the game that put them there. Misiorowski didn't just pitch his team into a postseason position — he pitched them into the division driver's seat with 26 games to go.

At 24 years old, in just his second big-league season, Misiorowski is doing something that pitchers twice his age spend entire careers trying to accomplish.

The Cy Young Race

The NL Cy Young Award field is not particularly close at this point. Misiorowski's -1400 odds at the major sportsbooks reflect the reality: there is no meaningful challenge to his candidacy. His ERA, strikeout total, WHIP, and wins all lead or rank among the top in the National League. The velocity question was the last open door, and Tuesday's 101.5 mph reading closed it.

What remains for September is legacy-building. Can Misiorowski maintain this level through the final month and produce a full-season stat line that ranks among the greatest Cy Young campaigns in the award's history? At 14-5 and 1.73 with 222 strikeouts and 26 games remaining, the answer is almost certainly yes.

What's Next

Misiorowski will continue his rotation with the Brewers — who are now in first place in the NL Central — over the final 26 games of the season. His next start is expected to come around September 7 or 8. Watch the velocity readings after every outing: if Tuesday's 101.5 mph was the beginning of a September surge rather than a single-game outlier, the NL Cy Young race is already at its conclusion. The Brewers need Misiorowski healthy and dominant in October — and after Tuesday, there's every reason to believe he will be.
