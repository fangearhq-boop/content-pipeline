Yordan Alvarez and the Triple Crown: 26 Games to Do What No One Has Done Since 2012

By Marcus Cole

September 3, 2026

Miguel Cabrera won the last Triple Crown 14 years ago. He led the American League in batting average, home runs, and RBIs simultaneously — then went on to win the AL MVP in a unanimous vote. No one in either league has done it since.

Yordan Alvarez is trying to end that drought.

Entering the final 26 games of the 2026 regular season, the Houston Astros first baseman leads the American League in batting average (.322), home runs (36), and RBIs (91). Not two of the three — all three. Simultaneously. That's the definition of the Triple Crown, and Alvarez has maintained the lead in all three categories through the dog days of summer and into September.

The Threats

The math is close enough to keep the race genuinely interesting. Junior Caminero sits one home run behind Alvarez at 35. Ben Rice of the New York Yankees is also at 35 — meaning a two-home-run night from either player could pull even or take the lead overnight. In the batting average race, Chandler Simpson of the Tampa Bay Rays sits at .308 — 14 points behind — and is a hot week away from making it a real competition. In RBIs, Pete Alonso is right at 91 as well, tied with Alvarez.

The Triple Crown requires leading all three categories on the final day of the season. Alvarez must not only maintain his current leads but also hold off three different players in three different categories over the course of a month. A cold week from Alvarez plus a hot week from any rival reshuffles the entire picture.

The Historical Weight

The Triple Crown's rarity isn't an accident of sample size — it's a structural difficulty. Batting average favors contact hitters; home runs favor power hitters; RBIs favor protection hitters who bat in run-producing situations. Being at the top of all three simultaneously requires a combination of skills, circumstances, and sustained performance that almost never aligns. The players who have done it — Frank Robinson, Carl Yastrzemski, Cabrera — are considered among the greatest hitters in the history of the sport.

Alvarez, at 29, is in the prime of a career that has already included MVP and World Series accomplishments. A Triple Crown would be the capstone of a legendary individual season.

What's Next

The Astros have 26 games remaining in the regular season. Alvarez's home run, batting average, and RBI totals will be tracked nightly by fans, analysts, and opponents alike. The closest competition is in the home run category, where both Caminero and Rice trail by just one. Watch for Houston's remaining schedule: games against AL East opponents and pitching-rich lineups could suppress the home run race for all parties. If Alvarez is going to win the Triple Crown, he needs to get and stay hot in the final three weeks — and stay healthy doing it.
