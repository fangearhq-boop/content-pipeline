Pete Crow-Armstrong's 40-40 Chase: Two Home Runs and Eight Stolen Bases Stand Between History and September

By Marcus Cole

September 3, 2026

The math is right there. Pete Crow-Armstrong needs 2 home runs and 8 stolen bases across 26 remaining games — and if he gets them, he joins a list so short that you could count its members on one hand.

The 40-40 club has exactly five members in the history of Major League Baseball: Jose Canseco (1988), Barry Bonds (1996), Alex Rodriguez (1998), Alfonso Soriano (2006), and Ronald Acuña Jr. (2023). For over a century, in a sport with thousands of seasons and tens of thousands of player-years, only five players have hit 40 home runs and stolen 40 bases in the same campaign. That's the company PCA is trying to join.

Currently sitting at approximately 38 home runs and 32 stolen bases, the 24-year-old Cubs center fielder is the -1100 NL MVP favorite — a race that, absent some extraordinary turn of events, is already settled. The NL MVP trophy is a matter of formality. Forty-forty is the next mountain.

The Pace and the Math

To reach 40-40, PCA needs 2 more home runs in 26 games — a pace of roughly one home run every 13 games. For comparison, he's been hitting home runs at a pace of about one per 3.6 games this season. Two in 26 is an extremely low bar for a player of his caliber.

The stolen base math is slightly more demanding: 8 more in 26 games means about one every 3.3 games. PCA has been stealing at roughly that pace or better all season. There's no reason to think it won't continue.

The real variable isn't performance — it's health. PCA plays in essentially every game, but the final month of a long season is when little things become bigger things. An ankle tweak, a day off for rest, a manager protecting his player for October. Any of those interrupts the count.

What It Would Mean

Five players in history. Twenty-six games. The Cubs are locked into the No. 1 Wild Card position in the NL, so PCA has the runway to keep playing. His team doesn't need him to manufacture wins at all costs — they need him healthy for October. The fact that 40-40 is simultaneously within reach and not the team's priority creates an interesting tension for September.

What PCA has done this season already stands among the great individual campaigns in recent memory: .283 batting average, 38 home runs, 91 RBIs, and 32 stolen bases with an NL MVP award all but secured. Adding 40-40 to that line would transform a great season into a historic one — and PCA's name into a sentence alongside Canseco, Bonds, A-Rod, Soriano, and Acuña.

What's Next

The Cubs have 26 games remaining, beginning September 3. Watch PCA's home run and stolen base counts closely — the 40-40 milestone could happen in a single game if he goes deep and swipes a bag. With two more home runs needed, the next time PCA goes back-to-back or hits one with a stolen base opportunity, September could deliver something that baseball hasn't seen since Ronald Acuña Jr. three years ago. The countdown is official.
