Milwaukee's 44-Year Wait: The City Behind the Brewers Has Finally Made It Back

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | October 22, 2026

The last time a World Series game was played in Milwaukee, Paul Molitor was 26 years old and Robin Yount had just won the American League MVP award.

That was 1982. County Stadium. Harvey Kuenn managed the Brewers to within two games of a championship before the St. Louis Cardinals took Games 6 and 7 to win the title. Milwaukee went home with nothing.

Forty-four years later, the city is back.

What 1982 Meant — and What Was Lost

The 1982 Milwaukee Brewers were one of the best teams in franchise history. Yount batted .331 with 29 home runs and 114 RBI to win MVP. Molitor hit .355 in the World Series itself. Pete Vuckovich won the AL Cy Young. Cecil Cooper, Gorman Thomas, and Ted Simmons gave the Brewers one of the most feared lineups in baseball.

They pushed the Cardinals to a seventh game and lost, 6-3. It would be the last time Milwaukee hosted a World Series game for more than four decades.

In the years that followed, the Brewers made the playoffs eight more times but never returned to the Fall Classic. The club moved from the American League to the National League in 1998. County Stadium was demolished in 2000. American Family Field — opened in 2001 as Miller Park, renamed in 2021 — became the new home. A new ballpark, but the same drought.

What This Season Has Meant to Milwaukee

The 2026 Brewers won 103 games during the regular season — the best record in baseball — and drew 2,788,934 fans to American Family Field, a 5.2% increase over 2025. The building was alive all season in a way that hadn't been felt in years.

When the Brewers swept the Los Angeles Dodgers 4-0 in the NLCS, Milwaukee erupted. Fans watched the road games from inside American Family Field itself — watch parties that drew thousands of people who weren't willing to stay home. They sat in the seats, watched on the screens, and roared like they were watching live baseball from the third-base line.

In a way, they were. It was their team. Their stadium. Their October.

The Fans Who Remember, and the Fans Who Don't

There is something particular about a 44-year wait that separates it from other championship droughts. Forty-four years means the majority of Brewers fans alive today have never seen their team in the World Series. It means there are season-ticket holders in their 30s and 40s who grew up hearing their parents talk about 1982 but never experienced it themselves.

And it means there are fans in their 50s and 60s who were in County Stadium when Robin Yount walked to the plate in Game 7 — who remember exactly where they sat, exactly how the air felt, exactly what it cost when the Cardinals took the lead — who have been waiting since then for another shot at this moment.

Tomorrow night at American Family Field, both groups get their night.

The Building That's Never Seen This

American Family Field has hosted playoff baseball. It has hosted some of the loudest crowds in Milwaukee Brewers history. But it has never hosted a World Series game. That changes Friday night, when the Rays come to town for Game 1 at 8:07 PM ET on FOX.

The stadium holds approximately 41,900 fans. Every one of those seats will be filled.

This is what 44 years of waiting looks like when it finally ends.
