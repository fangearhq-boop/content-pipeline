Pat Murphy vs. Kevin Cash: Two Different Philosophies, One World Series Trophy

By Ryan Calloway | Ballpark Banter | October 22, 2026

Inside every World Series, there is a baseball battle and there is a philosophical one. The 2026 Fall Classic between the Milwaukee Brewers and Tampa Bay Rays has a managerial contrast that makes the second battle just as compelling as the first.

In one dugout: Pat Murphy. Old-school discipline. Trust the player. Trust the process. Win the right way.

In the other: Kevin Cash. Analytics-driven deployment. Matchup optimization. Front-office-aligned roster management.

Two completely different paths to the same destination. One trophy waiting at the end.

Pat Murphy: The Old-School Architect

Pat Murphy built the 2026 Milwaukee Brewers the way teams used to be built. Not by committee, not by algorithm — by knowing his players, trusting his roster, and staying out of the way when the talent was good enough to take over.

Sports Illustrated featured Murphy's approach specifically heading into this World Series, noting the "trusting the process" mentality that his coaches and players have embraced. His approach to player development is hands-on in the traditional sense: relationships, accountability, and the belief that a team built the right way will eventually find its ceiling.

The results speak for themselves. The Brewers won 103 games in the regular season — the best record in baseball. They swept the Los Angeles Dodgers 4-0 in the NLCS. They did it with a group of players, several of whom were homegrown through Milwaukee's farm system, who believe in each other and in what Murphy has built.

Murphy does not use analytics as a crutch. He has a coaching staff and a front office that provides information, but the decisions on the field — when to pull a pitcher, when to let a hitter face a tough matchup, when to trust a player who's struggling — belong to him. His players know that. And they play differently because of it.

Kevin Cash: The Analytics-First Deployer

Kevin Cash has managed the Tampa Bay Rays in one of the most analytically progressive environments in professional baseball for years. The Rays were pioneers in the opener era. They were among the first organizations to fully embrace pitch sequencing data, defensive shifting, and roster construction built around player value relative to salary.

Under Cash, the Rays have already been to two World Series — losing to the Dodgers in 2020 and to the Phillies' predecessor club in 2008 under a different manager. (Cash was hired in 2015.) He has won multiple Manager of the Year awards and has delivered consistent postseason results with payrolls far below what larger-market teams spend.

Cash's strength is deployment. He will start a reliever before the opposing lineup's dangerous hitters, pull a starter who is one time through the order from struggling, and make decisions that look counterintuitive in real time but prove correct by the next morning. His lineup construction changes game to game based on matchup data.

Where Murphy trusts feel, Cash trusts numbers. Where Murphy builds a culture and lets it breathe, Cash optimizes in the moment.

The Philosophical Stakes

The old-school vs. analytics debate has been part of baseball for two decades now. But the framing is usually more complicated in practice — most teams use both, and most successful managers blend elements of each approach. The 2026 World Series is unusual because the contrast between Murphy and Cash is genuinely clean.

CBS Sports described this series as two clubs that are a "mirror image" of analytically driven organizations — but even that framing undersells how different the two managers' styles are at the field level. The front offices may both use data. The dugouts operate on different theories of baseball leadership.

There is no correct answer. Murphy's team won 103 games. Cash's team beat the Yankees and Astros to reach the Fall Classic. Both methods got here. One of them will leave with a ring.
