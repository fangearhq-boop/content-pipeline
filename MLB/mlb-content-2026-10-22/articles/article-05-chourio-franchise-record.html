Jackson Chourio's Franchise Record: Milwaukee's Kid Is Carrying October

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | October 22, 2026

Jackson Chourio is 22 years old. He has hit more home runs in the postseason than any player in Milwaukee Brewers history.

On Friday night at American Family Field, he will take the field in the Fall Classic.

The Record

Chourio entered the 2026 World Series with 6 career postseason home runs — a new Milwaukee Brewers all-time franchise record. He passed William Contreras during this postseason run to set the mark outright.

Contreras himself hit 3 home runs in the 2026 playoffs. The fact that Chourio needed to pass someone who had already been so productive tells you something about both players — and about how deep this Brewers lineup goes in October.

The previous record had been held by Contreras, who set it through multiple postseason runs as one of Milwaukee's most reliable bats. Chourio, in his early 20s and still growing into his ceiling, passed him in a single playoff run.

Who Is Jackson Chourio

Chourio signed with the Milwaukee Brewers as an international amateur and has developed into one of the best young outfielders in baseball. He plays with speed, athleticism, and a bat that generates hard contact at a rate most 22-year-olds have not yet figured out how to produce.

He has been one of the most talked-about young players in the National League since arriving, and the postseason has only accelerated that conversation. October baseball is supposed to be the great separator — the place where the pressure separates the genuine elite from the players who look good in August. Chourio has not flinched.

Six home runs. A franchise record. On a stage that most veterans never see.

What the Record Means

A franchise postseason home run record is different from a regular-season record. You cannot accumulate postseason plate appearances unless your team keeps winning. Every October home run Chourio has hit was hit in a high-leverage moment when the Brewers had something on the line.

He did not build this record in blowouts in June. He built it game by game, in the kind of moments that define careers. And he is 22 years old with what figures to be a decade of prime postseason years still ahead of him.

The record he set this October may not stand for long — if he continues to do what he has done, he will be the one to break it.

Tomorrow Night at American Family Field

The 2026 World Series begins Friday, October 23, at 8:07 PM ET on FOX. Jackson Chourio will be in center field — or wherever Pat Murphy needs him — when Jacob Misiorowski throws the first pitch of Milwaukee's first Fall Classic game in 44 years.

The record is already his. Now comes the moment that matters most.
