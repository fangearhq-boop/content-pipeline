World Series Game 1 Is Tomorrow: Everything You Need to Know About Brewers vs. Rays

By Ryan Calloway | Ballpark Banter | October 22, 2026

The wait is almost over. The 2026 World Series begins Friday night at American Family Field in Milwaukee, and before first pitch — scheduled for 8:07 PM ET on FOX — there are a few things every fan should know heading into Game 1.

Here is your complete Day-Before preview.

Game 1 Basics

The Milwaukee Brewers host the Tampa Bay Rays in Game 1 of the 2026 World Series on Friday, October 23. First pitch is at 8:07 PM ET. The game will be broadcast nationally on FOX.

Milwaukee enters as the home favorites at -160, with Tampa Bay listed at +140.

The full series schedule, per MLB:

  • Games 1 & 2: October 23–24 at American Family Field, Milwaukee
  • Games 3, 4 & 5*: October 26–28 at Tropicana Field, Tampa Bay
  • Games 6* & 7*: October 30–31 at American Family Field, Milwaukee

(*if necessary)

The Starting Pitchers

Milwaukee has confirmed Jacob Misiorowski as their Game 1 starter. The 23-year-old right-hander had one of the best seasons of any pitcher in baseball in 2026, earning his second straight All-Star selection and cementing himself as the Brewers' undisputed ace. He has been managed carefully in the postseason — averaging around 4 to 4.5 innings per start — but this is the stage he was built for.

In his NLCS starts, Misiorowski posted a 3.24 ERA. Arm fatigue has been an acknowledged factor in his shorter outings this October, which means Milwaukee's historically dominant bullpen will be called on early and often.

For Tampa Bay, Drew Rasmussen is widely expected to start Game 1, though the Rays had not officially confirmed their starter as of October 22. If Rasmussen gets the ball, he arrives as arguably the hottest pitcher in this entire postseason. He posted a 1.29 ERA in 15 innings during the playoffs, including a near no-hitter against the Yankees in ALDS Game 1 — six-plus innings, 10 strikeouts, a 1-0 win. In ALCS Game 5, he went seven innings and allowed just one run. He has won 14 of his last 15 decisions.

Neither pitcher has ever appeared in a World Series. Neither has ever thrown a pitch in the Fall Classic. That changes tomorrow night.

What the Numbers Say

The Brewers posted the best record in baseball during the regular season at 103-59 and went 4-0 at home in the 2026 postseason before reaching the World Series. Their bullpen — featuring DL Hall, Aaron Ashby, Thyago Uribe, and closer Devin Megill — posted a 0.38 ERA in 23.2 postseason innings, one of the best bullpen stretches in the Wild Card era.

Milwaukee's lineup is led by Jackson Chourio (6 career postseason home runs, a new Brewers franchise record), Brice Turang (100 RBI in 2026), NLCS MVP Jake Bauers, and William Contreras, who added 3 home runs in this playoff run.

Tampa Bay's offense runs through Junior Caminero, the 22-year-old two-time All-Star who hit .265/.356/.490 in 51 games despite an injury-limited 2026 season. His two-run home run off the left-field foul pole in ALCS Game 6 was one of the defining moments of their playoff run. Yandy Díaz, Jonathan Aranda, and Richie Palacios round out a lineup that has been built to manufacture runs against elite pitching.

The Storylines That Matter

This is the first World Series appearance for both franchises since the Brewers' 1982 trip (they lost to the Cardinals in seven games) and the Rays' most recent run in 2020 (they lost to the Dodgers in six games). Neither team has ever won a World Series title.

It is also the first time American Family Field — which opened in 2001, replacing the old County Stadium — will host a World Series game. The last time Milwaukee hosted a Fall Classic game was 44 years ago.

The city will be loud. The crowd will be electric. And two young, talent-loaded rosters — neither of which has ever done this before — will find out tomorrow night which one is ready.
