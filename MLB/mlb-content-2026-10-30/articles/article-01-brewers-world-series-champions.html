Milwaukee Brewers Win the 2026 World Series: A Championship 57 Years in the Making

By Marcus Cole | October 30, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The Milwaukee Brewers are World Series champions. On Thursday night at Tropicana Field in St. Petersburg, they defeated the Tampa Bay Rays 4-1 in Game 5 to claim the first World Series title in the franchise's 57-year history. For a city and a fan base that has waited since the organization's founding in 1969, the moment arrived on a warm Florida night — and it could not have been more earned.

Misiorowski Sets the Tone

Brandon Misiorowski took the ball for Milwaukee in the most important game of the franchise's history and delivered exactly what the Brewers needed. The right-hander — who led the American League in both ERA (1.80) and strikeouts (252) during the regular season — shut down the Rays for 5.2 scoreless innings before being pulled with a calf cramp. The injury cut short what was becoming a dominant performance, but Misiorowski had done enough.

Misiorowski's regular season was one of the finest pitching campaigns in recent memory: a 16-5 record, 252 strikeouts in 174.2 innings, and a fastball that reached 103.5 mph in Game 1 of the Series — the hardest pitch recorded in World Series history in the Statcast era. He will miss several weeks recovering from the calf strain, but his place in Milwaukee baseball history is already secured.

Trevor Megill came on after Misiorowski's exit and handled the final two innings for the save, keeping the Rays off the board and preserving the victory.

Contreras Claims the MVP

If one player defined the 2026 World Series for Milwaukee, it was William Contreras. The catcher hit .381 for the series, going 10-for-13 with 4 RBI. He worked the automatic ball-challenge system effectively throughout, extending at-bats and frustrating Rays pitchers. On the defensive side, Contreras threw out four runners attempting to steal — aided in part by opposing baserunners who tested him with slow trots down the line.

Contreras was named World Series Most Valuable Player, a distinction that felt unanimous from the first inning of Game 1. His performance behind the plate helped frame a Brewers pitching staff that went 11-2 across the postseason — one of the most dominant runs in recent October history.

Cooper Pratt contributed 2 RBI in the clinching game, providing the offense that gave Misiorowski and the bullpen room to work.

A Championship the City Has Earned

Milwaukee's relationship with baseball runs deep, but the franchise had never won it all. Their only previous World Series appearance, in 1982, ended in a seven-game loss to the St. Louis Cardinals. They returned to the Fall Classic in 2026 for the first time since then — and this time, they did not come home empty-handed.

The Brewers finished the postseason at 11-2, a record that reflects just how dominant they were from the wild card round through the clincher. A championship parade in Milwaukee is expected in the coming days, with the city poised to celebrate in a way it has never had the chance to before.

What's Next

The Brewers will celebrate their first World Series title before turning attention to an unusual offseason. The MLB collective bargaining agreement expires December 1, and a lockout is widely expected. Milwaukee will need to navigate that uncertainty while also managing the health of Misiorowski, whose calf injury timetable is still being assessed. The Brewers enter this offseason as the reigning world champions — and with more than enough to feel good about heading into 2027.
