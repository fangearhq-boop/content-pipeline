Tarik Skubal: The Most Coveted Free Agent Pitcher in Years Hits the Open Market

By Marcus Cole | October 30, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

With the World Series over and MLB free agency officially open, one name stands above every other starting pitcher on the market: Tarik Skubal. The two-time American League Cy Young Award winner — back-to-back in 2025 and 2026 — enters this offseason as arguably the most sought-after free agent starter in years, with teams lining up to make a run at one of the most dominant left-handed pitchers of his generation.

The Numbers That Command Nine Figures

Skubal's 2026 regular season was exceptional by any measure. He finished 16-5 with a 1.80 ERA over 174.2 innings, leading the American League in both ERA and strikeouts with 252 Ks. His performance was punctuated by a 103.5 mph fastball in World Series Game 1 — a pitch that set the Statcast-era record for fastest in a World Series game.

Those numbers don't just make him the best pitcher available. By many projections, they make him the kind of arm that changes franchises. Analysts have floated contract projections ranging from $350 million to $400 million over the life of a deal, which would set records for pitcher average annual value. The market has never seen a pitcher like this enter free agency.

Who Is Lining Up to Sign Him

The Dodgers have emerged as the early frontrunner in prediction markets, carrying roughly 38% odds of landing Skubal according to one projection model. Los Angeles brings the financial resources and the established track record of landing marquee free agents, making them the default favorite for any top-of-market acquisition.

The Phillies are viewed as a legitimate dark horse, with a roster that is a frontline starter away from annual World Series contention. The Braves and Yankees have also been connected to Skubal in reporting, and several other large-market teams are expected to enter the bidding once the five-day exclusive negotiating window passes and the broader market opens.

A health caveat has been noted in some reports, with one analyst flagging a recent surgery as a potential factor in contract negotiations. That detail has not stopped teams from projecting record-setting commitments.

The CBA Wrinkle

Skubal's free agency plays out against a ticking labor clock. The current collective bargaining agreement expires December 1, 2026, and a lockout is widely considered close to inevitable. If MLB owners impose a lockout at the CBA deadline, it would freeze all free agent negotiations — including any deal for Skubal — until a new agreement is reached.

That timeline creates an unusual incentive: any team that wants Skubal may push to close a deal before December 1 rather than risk the lockout halting talks for weeks or months. Speed has suddenly become a factor in what was expected to be a patient, deliberate bidding process.

What's Next

The five-day exclusive window runs through early November, after which teams can formally negotiate and sign free agents. Given the looming CBA deadline, expect activity on the Skubal front to accelerate quickly. The team that lands him will be acquiring arguably the best starting pitcher in baseball — and paying accordingly.
