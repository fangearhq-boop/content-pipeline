Rays Fall Short in Game 5: A Historic Run Ends, Questions Begin

By Jake Torres | October 30, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The Tampa Bay Rays will not be celebrating a World Series championship. But before the Milwaukee Brewers closed them out 4-1 in Game 5 on Thursday night at Tropicana Field, the Rays did something that 0-for-44 teams before them could not: they extended a World Series from a 0-3 deficit to five games. Now, with the postseason over, questions about what comes next in Tampa loom large.

The Run That Made History

Down three games to none, the Rays clawed back with a 12-inning thriller in Game 4 that became one of the most memorable games of the Series. Ben Williamson's walk-off single in the 12th inning delivered the Rays their survival moment, making Tampa Bay only the fifth team in World Series history to force a Game 5 after falling 0-3.

Cooper Pratt's home run in the top of the 12th had given Milwaukee a temporary lead before Williamson's heroics reversed the script. It was the kind of late-game drama that Series lore is built on. Unfortunately for the Rays and their fans, history was not on their side: the four teams that had previously forced a Game 5 from 0-3 all went on to lose.

Tampa Bay became the fifth to try, and the fifth to fail.

The McClanahan Shadow

One of the biggest storylines hanging over Tampa Bay's entire postseason run was the absence of Shane McClanahan. The left-hander, who battled back and calf issues throughout the 2026 season after returning from surgery, missed the entire World Series on the injured list.

McClanahan's presence — or lack thereof — shaped the entire rotation picture for the Rays during the series. What his timeline looks like heading into spring 2027 is one of the central questions facing the organization this offseason. A healthy McClanahan is the difference between a fringe playoff team and a legitimate pennant contender.

An Offseason of Decisions

Beyond McClanahan's health, the Rays enter the offseason with significant roster decisions ahead. The five-day exclusive negotiating window for free agents has opened, and several players who were part of this postseason run will be evaluating their options on the open market.

The Rays' model of developing homegrown talent and operating efficiently remains intact. But this World Series run — and the taste of coming within one win of a championship — will create pressure to spend in ways Tampa Bay has historically avoided. Whether ownership responds to that pressure is the defining question of the offseason.

What's Next

The Rays will spend the coming weeks assessing their roster, evaluating free agent priorities, and monitoring McClanahan's recovery timeline. With the CBA expiring December 1 and a lockout looming, the window to sign players before a potential work stoppage is narrow.
