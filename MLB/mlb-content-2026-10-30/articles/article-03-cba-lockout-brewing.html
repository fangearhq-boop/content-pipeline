The MLB CBA Expires in 32 Days. A Lockout Is Coming. Here's What It Means.

By Ryan Calloway | October 30, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The Milwaukee Brewers just won the World Series. The Hot Stove is officially open. And looming over all of it, like a storm on the horizon that nobody can look away from, is this: the Major League Baseball collective bargaining agreement expires December 1, 2026. A work stoppage is widely considered close to inevitable. For the first time since 1994-95, the sport may face a winter — and potentially a spring — without baseball.

Where the Two Sides Stand

The core dispute is one of the most fundamental in professional sports labor: owners want a hard salary cap; the players' union refuses to accept one. MLB's ownership group has proposed a hard cap of $245.3 million. The MLB Players Association has opened negotiations near a $300 million luxury tax threshold — not a cap, but a softer ceiling that the union's preferred framework has long relied on.

The gap between $245 million and $300 million is significant. But the philosophical distance is even larger. The union has rejected the concept of a hard cap for decades, viewing it as a structural ceiling on player earnings. Owners argue a cap would create competitive balance and a more sustainable economic model. Those positions have not moved in months of talks.

What the Insiders Are Saying

The outlook from those close to the negotiations is grim. One prediction market placed the odds of a new CBA being agreed to before the December deadline at approximately 5%. Commissioner Rob Manfred has said publicly that an offseason lockout "is likely to become the norm." The MLBPA's leadership is reportedly prepared for a work stoppage and has built what analysts describe as a significant strike fund to support players — particularly non-roster and fringe-level players — through a prolonged stoppage.

The last work stoppage, in 2021-22, lasted 99 days and delayed the start of the season. That shutdown resulted in a deal that satisfied neither side fully and arguably set the stage for the current standoff.

What a Lockout Would Mean for This Offseason

A lockout beginning December 1 would immediately freeze all transactions. Teams could not sign free agents, make trades, or negotiate extensions. The 144 free agents currently on the market — including Tarik Skubal, Randy Arozarena, Pete Alonso, and Michael King — would be in limbo until a new agreement is reached.

If the lockout extends into January or February, spring training would be in jeopardy. If it stretches into March or April, regular season games could be lost for the first time since 1995. The MLBPA and ownership understand these stakes, which is why some observers believe a deal could still come together in the final weeks — but the track record of these negotiations does not encourage optimism.

What's Next

The calendar is the only true forcing mechanism. Owners and players have 32 days from today to reach a new agreement before the CBA expires. Talks are ongoing, but the two sides remain far apart on the central issue of a salary cap. Fans, free agents, and front offices are all watching the same clock — and bracing for the same outcome. Whether baseball finds a way to avoid another work stoppage, or repeats the failures of 2021, will define the next several months of the sport.
