MLB Free Agency Is Open: 144 Players Hit the Market as the Hot Stove Ignites

By Jake Torres | October 30, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The champagne in Milwaukee has barely dried. The World Series ended Thursday night, and by Friday morning, the 2026-27 MLB offseason was already in motion. The five-day exclusive negotiating window between teams and their own free agents has opened, and 144 players have been officially declared free agents — kicking off what could be the most consequential, and most chaotic, Hot Stove season in recent memory.

The Headliners

Two names tower above the rest of this free agent class. Tarik Skubal — who led the American League in ERA (1.80) and strikeouts (252) this season and claimed his second consecutive Cy Young Award — is the most coveted starting pitcher on the market. Contract projections range from $350 million to $400 million. The Dodgers have been identified as the early frontrunner, with the Phillies, Braves, and Yankees also reported to be monitoring the situation closely.

Randy Arozarena is the best bat available. The outfielder, who spent this season with the Seattle Mariners, is expected to test the open market and has been linked to a preference for the East Coast. Analysts project a three-year deal in the range of $18 million to $22 million per season. The Yankees, Phillies, and Red Sox are among the teams connected to his name.

Notable Opt-Outs and New Entrants

Pete Alonso opted out of the final year of his contract with the New York Mets, declining a $24 million option to re-enter the market. Alonso had signed a two-year, $54 million deal with the Mets that included the opt-out clause. He is now seeking a longer commitment from his next employer, reportedly targeting a deal in the seven-year range.

Michael King of the San Diego Padres also opted out of his contract, with the Chicago Cubs among the teams connected as potential suitors. Analysts project a multiyear deal of $100 million or more for the right-hander. Clay Holmes and Matthew Boyd also declined player options with the Cubs, adding to the pitching market's depth.

The CBA Shadow

There is one enormous caveat hanging over every transaction this offseason: the collective bargaining agreement expires December 1. A lockout is considered close to inevitable by league insiders and labor analysts. If one begins, free agency freezes — no signings, no trades, no negotiations until a new deal is reached.

That reality gives this month a compressed, high-stakes feel unlike most offseasons. Teams that want to land premier free agents will need to move before December or accept the uncertainty of waiting. Every deal agreed to before the lockout represents one less variable in a winter full of them.

What's Next

The five-day exclusive window closes shortly, at which point all players can negotiate with any team. The next 30 days will determine whether the biggest names — Skubal, Arozarena, Alonso — find new homes before the CBA clock runs out or carry their search into what could become a frozen market. The Hot Stove is on. How long it stays lit is anyone's guess.
