First Time Since 1980: Both World Series Teams Have Never Won a Championship

By Ryan Calloway | October 26, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The Milwaukee Brewers and Tampa Bay Rays are playing for the 2026 World Series championship. One of them is going to win it. And for the first time since 1980, that winner will be a franchise that has never held a championship before.

This Fall Classic, regardless of who wins, will produce a first-time champion. That hasn't happened in baseball since the Philadelphia Phillies defeated the Kansas City Royals in six games in 1980 — the first title for both franchises in a single series.

The Brewers' Long Wait

Milwaukee has been playing professional baseball since 1969, when the franchise arrived as the Seattle Pilots before relocating to Wisconsin. Their only other World Series appearance came in 1982, a seven-game classic against the St. Louis Cardinals that ended with the Brewers one game short of a title.

That 1982 team — with its "Harvey's Wallbangers" nickname and one of the most powerful lineups of the era — came closer than any Milwaukee team since. The Brewers have been building back to this moment ever since, through rebuilds and near-misses and one of the most consistent bullpen cultures in the sport. In 2026, the pieces finally came together.

The Rays' Three Near-Misses

Tampa Bay's story has its own distinct character. The Rays are one of baseball's youngest franchises — they've existed since 1998 as an expansion team — but they have already reached three World Series. In 2008, they lost to the Phillies. In 2020, they lost to the Dodgers in six games in the pandemic-year Fall Classic. Now in 2026, they find themselves down 2-0 against a Milwaukee team that is one win from their first championship.

No franchise in baseball history has lost its first three World Series appearances. The Rays have a chance to avoid that distinction — but only if they win four of their next five games.

The Weight of History

When baseball fans talk about franchises without championships, they usually mean teams that have never been in a World Series. The Brewers and Rays are different — both have been close. Both have felt what it's like to be within a few wins of a title and come up short.

Tonight, at Tropicana Field, one franchise begins the process of either extending that drought or ending it. The other has already won Games 1 and 2 and is three wins from the most meaningful night in their city's baseball history.

What's Next

Game 3 is tonight at 8 PM ET on FOX from Tropicana Field. The Brewers lead 2-0. Freddy Peralta starts for Tampa Bay; Robert Gasser starts for Milwaukee. However this series ends, its final game will feature a franchise celebrating a first. That's what makes the 2026 World Series worth watching all the way to the last out.
