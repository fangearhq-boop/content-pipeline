Jacob Misiorowski Has the Most Historic Arm in Baseball. His Postseason Results Are Another Story.

By Marcus Cole | October 26, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The first pitch of the 2026 World Series traveled 103.5 miles per hour. It was the fastest pitch ever recorded in the tracking era of the Fall Classic. The pitcher who threw it was Jacob Misiorowski, and it arrived on the very first delivery of Game 1, to the very first batter of the series.

Misiorowski set a record before he'd even thrown a single competitive pitch in October. The problem is, October hasn't been kind to him since.

The Records Are Real

During the 2026 regular season, Misiorowski threw 1,115 pitches above 100 miles per hour — an MLB record. For context, most pitchers who touch 100 mph at all do it a handful of times per start. Misiorowski does it on a routine basis, with a velocity that exceeds anything else in the sport.

The 103.5 mph pitch that opened the World Series topped the all-time tracking-era record for Fall Classic velocity. That record now belongs to him — regardless of how the rest of October goes.

But Hitters Have Adjusted

There's a split in Misiorowski's numbers that explains why a pitcher with a historic arm has struggled to close out postseason starts. In the regular season, his four-seam fastball generated a 40.2 percent whiff rate — among the best marks in baseball. In the playoffs, that number has fallen to 16.1 percent.

The velocity is still there. The swing-and-miss is not. Opposing hitters, given the extra preparation time and the analytical resources available in October, appear to have locked in on the timing of his elite fastball. When the best pitch in your repertoire stops missing bats, length becomes a problem — and Misiorowski has not completed five innings in either of his postseason starts.

A Talent Worth Watching Long-Term

None of this diminishes what Misiorowski represents for Milwaukee's future. He's 22 years old. He throws 103.5 miles per hour. He set an MLB record for 100-mph pitches in a single season. The ceiling on this pitcher is as high as any arm in baseball.

The Fall Classic is a learning experience for a young pitcher with historic raw ability. The Brewers have won two straight one-run games with him as the nominal starter. He doesn't have to be elite for Milwaukee to win — but getting more innings from him would help.

What's Next

Misiorowski is not scheduled to start Game 3 tonight, with Robert Gasser getting the ball for Milwaukee. If the series extends to Games 5 through 7, expect Misiorowski to be back on the mound. The second time opposing hitters see him in October will be the real test of whether he's made an in-series adjustment.
