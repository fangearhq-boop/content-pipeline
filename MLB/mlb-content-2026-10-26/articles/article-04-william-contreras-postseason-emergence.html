William Contreras and the Art of the Slow Trot: Milwaukee's Most Entertaining October Star

By Jake Torres | October 26, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

When William Contreras hits a home run, he doesn't run the bases. He walks them. He savors them. He makes every step count, and by the time he crosses home plate, every person in the ballpark has had time to appreciate what just happened.

It has become the signature of the Brewers' 2026 World Series run. And given his production, he's earned every slow step.

The Numbers Speak

Through the NLCS, William Contreras has batted .367 this postseason with three home runs. In the NLCS against the Dodgers alone, he went 9-for-23 with a .481 on-base percentage and a 1.264 OPS. He hit a 436-foot home run in the NLCS that provided a critical spark in that series.

Milwaukee's offense leads all 2026 postseason teams in batting average, and Contreras has been its engine. When the catcher behind the plate is also the most dangerous bat in the lineup, it creates a problem for every opposing pitcher in October.

The Transformation From His Previous Postseasons

The striking thing about Contreras's October performance is what came before it. In his first 20 career postseason games before 2026, he batted .211 with a .639 OPS — a below-average hitter when the stakes were highest. Something changed heading into this year's run.

Contreras hasn't explained it beyond the normal athlete's language about being in the right mental space. But the results are undeniable: a player who struggled in the postseason has become the most valuable bat on one of the World Series' most impressive offensive teams.

The Slow Trot as Statement

The home run celebrations have become a talking point in their own right. Contreras doesn't hurry. He watches the ball clear the fence, flips or drops his bat with deliberate calm, and begins a trot that clocks in somewhere between leisurely and theatrical. Opposing fans find it irritating. Brewers fans love it.

In a sport where unwritten rules used to demand immediate hustle around the bases, Contreras's trots feel like a statement: this is his moment, and he's going to enjoy it. Through the end of the NLCS, at least, nobody has been in a position to complain.

What's Next

The World Series continues tonight with Game 3 at Tropicana Field, 8 PM ET on FOX. The Brewers lead 2-0, and Contreras is expected to be in the lineup for Milwaukee. If he hits another home run, you'll have plenty of time to watch him round the bases.
