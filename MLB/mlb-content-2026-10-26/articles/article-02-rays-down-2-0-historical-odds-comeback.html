Rays Down 2-0: What History Says About Their World Series Comeback Chances

By Ryan Calloway | October 26, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The Tampa Bay Rays need to win four of the next five games to win their first World Series championship. The Milwaukee Brewers need one more win in their favor to end the series early. History strongly favors Milwaukee — but the Rays haven't been eliminated yet.

What the Numbers Say

Teams that go up 2-0 in the World Series have won 77 of 92 Fall Classics — roughly 84 percent of the time. Only 10 clubs in baseball history have climbed out of an 0-2 hole to win the World Series. The most recent was the 1996 New York Yankees, who dropped the first two games in Atlanta before winning four consecutive to take the title. The 1985 Kansas City Royals and 1986 New York Mets also managed the comeback, among others.

The math is not in Tampa Bay's favor. But 0-2 comebacks happen — about once per decade, on average.

What the Rays Have Going for Them

The next three games — Games 3, 4, and 5 — are all scheduled at Tropicana Field. That matters. The Rays are 3-1 at home this postseason, and Tropicana has been a difficult environment for opposing offenses throughout October.

Tonight, Freddy Peralta gets the ball. The Rays' ALCS MVP is 2-0 with a 1.08 ERA this postseason, and Tampa Bay won all nine of his final regular-season starts. Manager Kevin Cash deliberately held Peralta out of Games 1 and 2 in Milwaukee to have him ready for the home games. If there's a Rays pitcher capable of resetting this series, it's him.

Ryan Rasmussen, who posted a 1.29 ERA across 21 innings in prior postseason starts, is also available for Game 4 or in a high-leverage spot tonight. The Rays' pitching hasn't been their problem in this series — getting runs has been.

What Tampa Bay Needs to Fix

Games 1 and 2 both ended in one-run losses for the Rays. They came out of Game 2 with a 4-0 first-inning lead and still lost. The Milwaukee bullpen — operating at a 1.33 ERA across more than 40 postseason innings — is as good as any relief corps in recent memory. To win four straight, the Rays need to find a way to break through that pen before the late innings.

What's Next

Game 3 is tonight at 8 PM ET, FOX. A Rays win makes this a series; a Brewers win puts Milwaukee one victory from their first championship. The Rays' path back runs through Tropicana Field over the next few days — and tonight is the most important game they've played all postseason.
