William Contreras Is Running the NLCS: How Milwaukee's Catcher Became the Series' Most Important Player

By Jake Torres | October 13, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The Milwaukee Brewers have home run hitters, a lights-out bullpen, and a rotation deep enough to grind through a seven-game series. But the player who has defined their NLCS against the Los Angeles Dodgers is the catcher who has spent his career building quietly toward exactly this kind of stage.

William Contreras — born in Venezuela, developed by the Braves, and traded to Milwaukee before the 2024 season — is two games into the NLCS and has already delivered the two most important swings of the series. He hit the solo home run that tied Game 2 in the fifth inning. He lined the go-ahead RBI single one inning later. Without those two at-bats, the Brewers might be going home from a 0-2 series instead of leading one.

The Career Behind the Moment

Contreras entered the majors with Atlanta and immediately established himself as one of the better offensive catchers in the National League. He made his first All-Star team in 2022 with the Braves. Then came the trade to Milwaukee before 2024 — a move that surprised some observers who wondered whether a mid-tier offense could get the most out of him.

The answer came quickly. In his first season with the Brewers, Contreras hit .281 with 23 home runs and 92 RBI, earned a second All-Star selection, and finished fifth in NL MVP voting. He had found a home, a lineup that featured his strengths, and a manager in Pat Murphy who trusted him in high-leverage situations. The fit was obvious almost immediately.

Entering the 2026 NLCS, Contreras carried a career .275 batting average, 89 home runs, and 348 RBI across 699 games. Those are the numbers of a steady, reliable producer — not a superstar in the traditional sense, but the kind of player who makes everything around him function better. During the 2026 regular season, he hit .308 across 49 games, picking up where his 2024 season left off.

What Made Game 2 Different

Game 2 of the NLCS was not trending the Brewers' way in the early innings. Milwaukee starter Logan Henderson had given the Dodgers an early lead, and the Brewers' offense had been quiet. The crowd at American Family Field was tense.

With the Brewers trailing in the fifth, Contreras stepped in and turned on an inside fastball, driving it into the left field seats. Solo home run. Tie game. It was the kind of swing that changes the temperature of a series — not just the score of a game.

One inning later, he came up again with runners on base. This time, he lined a single to left-center. The go-ahead run scored. The Brewers led 5-4, and four Milwaukee relievers proceeded to retire the final 15 batters in order. Those two plate appearances — roughly ten minutes of actual game time — have defined the NLCS.

"He's been doing this all year," Brewers manager Pat Murphy said after the game. "He's one of the best in the game at this position and he shows it every night."

The Bigger Picture

What makes Contreras's performance meaningful beyond the box score is what it reflects about the Brewers' identity. Milwaukee is a franchise that builds through pitching, defense, and process. They do not win with one-dimensional power or a single superstar. They win because their components — rotation, bullpen, defense, lineup — all perform their roles.

Contreras occupies the most demanding role in that structure. He calls the games. He manages the pitching staff through an October that has seen two dominant performances against a Dodgers lineup that includes Shohei Ohtani, Mookie Betts, and Freddie Freeman. He is, in the truest sense, the on-field conductor of everything the Brewers do.

The fact that he is also delivering the biggest offensive swings in the series is almost an afterthought — except that it is the reason the Brewers are where they are.

What's Next

The series shifts to Dodger Stadium for Games 3 and 4. The Brewers lead 2-0 and are two wins from the World Series. Milwaukee will send Robert Gasser to the mound Wednesday night against Blake Snell, the Dodgers' returning ace.

Contreras will crouch behind the plate, read the hitters, and call the game. And if Milwaukee needs a swing to change an inning, history suggests he will deliver that too.

Two-time All-Star. Career .275 average. And now, the engine of an NLCS run that has the Brewers on the doorstep of October's final stage.
