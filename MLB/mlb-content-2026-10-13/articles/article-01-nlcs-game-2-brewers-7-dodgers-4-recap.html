Contreras Delivers Again: Brewers Beat Dodgers 7-4, Lead NLCS 2-0

By Jake Torres | October 13, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The Milwaukee Brewers are two wins away from the World Series, and the Los Angeles Dodgers are running out of ways to stop them.

Milwaukee beat Los Angeles 7-4 in Game 2 of the National League Championship Series on Monday night at American Family Field, taking a 2-0 series lead heading to Dodger Stadium for Games 3 and 4. William Contreras was again the difference — his solo home run in the fifth inning tied the game, and his RBI single one inning later put the Brewers ahead for good.

The Dodgers, featuring the NL's most dangerous lineup with Shohei Ohtani, Mookie Betts, and Freddie Freeman, went hitless in four at-bats with runners in scoring position and managed just three hits all night.

Contreras Seizes the Moment

After Milwaukee starter Logan Henderson gave the Dodgers an early lead, the Brewers offense clawed back. It was Contreras who provided the turning point.

In the fifth inning, with the Brewers trailing and the crowd at American Family Field growing anxious, Contreras turned on an inside fastball and drove it into the left field seats. Tie game. The Brewers had life.

One inning later, he came up again with runners on base. He lined a single to left-center, scoring the go-ahead run and giving Milwaukee the lead they would never relinquish. In a series full of consequential swings, it was the most consequential of all.

"He's been doing this all year," Brewers manager Pat Murphy said. "He's one of the best in the game at this position and he shows it every night."

For Contreras, the moment was the culmination of a career arc that has steadily built toward exactly these kinds of stages. Traded from Atlanta to Milwaukee before 2024, he slashed .281 with 23 home runs and finished fifth in NL MVP voting that season. In 2026, he hit .308 during the regular season before transitioning seamlessly into postseason mode.

A Bullpen Without a Flaw

The Brewers' bullpen has been the story of this NLCS. Monday night, four Milwaukee relievers combined to retire the final 15 Los Angeles batters in order. From the moment Contreras gave the Brewers the lead in the sixth, the Dodgers did not mount a credible threat.

That scoreless run — 15 consecutive batters, through Ohtani, Betts, and Freeman — reflects something structural about how Milwaukee's pitching staff is built. The Brewers led the NL in bullpen ERA during the regular season. In October, they have been even better.

For the Dodgers, the math is sobering. Two games. Minimal production from their most dangerous hitters. Zero wins. Their best players have been stymied by a Milwaukee pitching approach that changes speeds, moves the ball corner-to-corner, and refuses to give hitters anything comfortable to hit.

What's Next

Game 3 moves to Dodger Stadium on Wednesday, October 14, at 8 PM ET on FS1. Los Angeles will send Blake Snell — their ace who returned from elbow surgery to post a 1.13 ERA in eight starts — against Milwaukee's Robert Gasser. For the Dodgers, it is not yet a must-win. But given the history of teams coming back from 0-2 in a series, it is close.

Milwaukee sent a message in Game 2. Their catcher is hitting. Their bullpen is locked in. And a franchise that won 103 regular-season games is playing its best baseball in October when it counts most.
