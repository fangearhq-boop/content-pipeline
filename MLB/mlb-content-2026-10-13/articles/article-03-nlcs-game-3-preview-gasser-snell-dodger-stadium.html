NLCS Game 3: Blake Snell's Comeback Faces Its Biggest Test Yet at Dodger Stadium

By Marcus Cole | October 13, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

There is no way to understand what Wednesday night's NLCS Game 3 means for the Los Angeles Dodgers without first understanding what Blake Snell means to the Los Angeles Dodgers.

He missed most of the season with elbow surgery. He came back in late summer, and the results were almost incomprehensible — 4-0, a 1.13 ERA, 50 strikeouts in 39.2 innings across eight starts. He was, by any objective measure, the most effective pitcher in baseball for the second half of 2026. His return gave the Dodgers back something they had been missing: a certified ace who could carry them through a postseason start on his own.

Wednesday night at Dodger Stadium, 8 PM ET on FS1, is when Snell's comeback story gets its defining chapter.

The Situation

Milwaukee leads the NLCS 2-0 following wins in Games 1 and 2. In Game 2, the Brewers beat the Dodgers 7-4 at American Family Field, with William Contreras providing the key blows and the bullpen retiring the final 15 batters in order. Los Angeles has managed just 3 hits in each of the two games and has gone hitless in eight at-bats with runners in scoring position across the series.

No team has come back from an 0-3 deficit in a best-of-seven postseason series. The Dodgers are not there yet. But one more loss Wednesday, and they will face elimination from the next defeat. The math is not friendly.

The Dodgers need Snell to be everything he was this summer.

Gasser's Role

Milwaukee sends Robert Gasser to the mound. The 25-year-old left-hander finished the regular season 5-5 with a 3.87 ERA and 103 strikeouts in 109.1 innings. He is the kind of starter that does not give up runs in bunches — a contact-managing pitcher who lives in the zone, limits walks, and trusts his defense to make plays.

Gasser does not have Snell's strikeout ceiling. His ground-ball rate is excellent, which plays well in a game where the Brewers' defense has been reliable all postseason. He will not overpower the Dodgers' lineup, but he does not need to. If he limits Los Angeles to two or three runs, Milwaukee's offense — which has scored 11 runs in two games — should be more than capable of providing the margin.

The Brewers' strategy in Game 3 is the same as Games 1 and 2: get five or six quality innings from the starter, hand it to the bullpen, and trust the process.

Snell vs. the Brewers

The Brewers' lineup has made quality pitching look ordinary throughout this postseason. In the NLDS against San Diego, they reached early and often. In the NLCS, they have found ways to score against whoever Los Angeles sends to the mound.

Snell's last start — in the NLDS — was not his best. He threw 3.2 innings, allowed one run on three hits, walked five batters, and struck out five. He survived, but the walk total was a concern. Against Milwaukee's disciplined approach, five-pitch at-bats and deep counts are a problem. The Brewers rank among the NL's best teams at working counts and forcing quality strikes. They will not be impatient against Snell.

If Snell is the version that posted a 1.13 ERA over eight starts, the Dodgers have a real game. If his command wanders the way it did in the NLDS, the Brewers will capitalize on every mistake.

What's Next

Wednesday night at Dodger Stadium is the kind of game that postseason baseball is designed to produce. A Brewers win would push Milwaukee to within one game of the World Series. A Dodgers win shifts the narrative and makes the series genuinely competitive again. Either way, 55,000 fans at Chavez Ravine will be the loudest crowd of the postseason.

Game 3. October 14. 8 PM ET on FS1. The Dodgers' season hangs on every pitch.
