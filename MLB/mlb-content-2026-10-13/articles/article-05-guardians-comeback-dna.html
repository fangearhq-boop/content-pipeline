Comeback DNA: The 2026 Cleveland Guardians Have Done This Before, and They'll Do It Again

By Ryan Calloway | October 13, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Every franchise talks about resilience. The 2026 Cleveland Guardians have actually demonstrated it — twice in October, against two different opponents, in circumstances that would have ended most teams' postseason runs before they got started.

The evidence is not a slogan. It is a box score from October 10, a scoreboard from Tropicana Field on Monday night, and a 1-0 ALCS lead that not many people predicted before the series began.

The ALDS: An Impossible Comeback

Cleveland's path to the ALCS was supposed to be simple. The Chicago White Sox — the team that made baseball's most improbable playoff run, reversing back-to-back 100-loss seasons with a remarkable 2026 regular season — were the story. The White Sox were the underdogs, the Cinderella, the team that nobody expected.

Then the White Sox won Games 1 and 2 at Progressive Field. The script was writing itself. The miracle run would continue. The Guardians, at home, in their own park, were down 0-2.

Cleveland won three straight.

Game 3 in Chicago. Game 4 at home. And Game 5 on October 10 in front of their own fans — a game that became one of the most dominant pitching performances in postseason history. Cleveland's pitchers combined to strike out 21 White Sox batters in nine innings, setting an all-time postseason record for a nine-inning game. Gavin Williams alone struck out 12. José Ramírez hit the go-ahead home run in the fourth inning, and the Guardians closed out a 2-0 shutout that sent them to the ALCS.

They became the 11th team ever to come back from 0-2 in a best-of-five series. The last team to accomplish it was the 2017 New York Yankees — who did it against the Cleveland franchise itself.

The ALCS: Same Story, Different Opponent

Monday night at Tropicana Field, Cleveland was facing a Tampa Bay Rays team that went into Game 1 as -170 favorites. The Rays had home-field advantage. They had a lineup full of dangerous hitters. They had Drew Rasmussen starting in front of their crowd.

Cleveland trailed 2-0 after Rasmussen's early command.

Then the Guardians did what the Guardians do. Chase DeLauter hit a solo home run. Patrick Bailey hit a solo home run. Nathaniel Lowe delivered a two-run single in the eighth inning to break a 2-2 tie. Jo Adell added a two-run double in the ninth to close out a 7-3 win. Hunter Gaddis earned the win in relief, and the Cleveland bullpen — again — was the difference.

The Guardians, road underdogs in Game 1 of a series, came back to win and take the series lead. Again.

Why This Is Different From Luck

Comebacks happen in baseball. Every October produces teams that overcome deficits, win games they were not supposed to win, and advance further than the odds suggested. What separates the 2026 Guardians from a lucky run is the structural consistency behind it.

Cleveland has the best bullpen in the American League by ERA. Their starting pitching — led by Parker Messick, who starts Game 2 tonight against Freddy Peralta — is deep enough that they can absorb a bad start and survive. Their lineup is patient and disciplined, built around hitters who grind at-bats, work counts, and capitalize on mistakes late in games. They do not rely on one player or one moment.

When they trailed 2-0 in the ALDS, that structure held. When they trailed 2-0 Monday at Tampa Bay, the structure held again. The common thread is not luck or momentum. It is a roster constructed to win close games in October.

What's Next

Game 2 of the ALCS is tonight at Tropicana Field at 8 PM ET on TBS. Parker Messick — a Tampa native, a 2026 All-Star, and the best pitcher Cleveland has — starts with a chance to put the Rays in an 0-2 hole at home.

A 2-0 ALCS lead, given what this team did in the ALDS, would be nearly insurmountable. The Guardians have shown they can win when they fall behind. They haven't shown anyone what they look like when they don't have to.

That may be the most frightening thing about the 2026 Cleveland Guardians.
