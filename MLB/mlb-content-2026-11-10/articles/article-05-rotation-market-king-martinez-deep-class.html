Beyond Skubal: The 2026-27 Starting Pitching Market Has Something for Every Budget

By Marcus Cole | November 10, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The headlines belong to Tarik Skubal and his projected eight-year, $355 million deal. But the 2026-27 starting pitching free-agent market is unusually deep across multiple tiers — a function of several opt-outs, option declines, and outright releases converging in the same offseason. For teams that cannot compete for Skubal at north of $44 million per year, the rotation market still offers meaningful options.

Tier 2 — Legitimate No. 2 Starters

Michael King is the most compelling name below Skubal. King posted a 3.21 ERA across 32 starts for San Diego in 2026 — elite-adjacent numbers that would make him a clear frontline starter on most rosters. He opted out of his contract with the Padres, which included a $28 million option for 2027 (with a $5 million buyout), because he believes the open market offers more. At 31, he is in the prime of his career with no known durability concerns. Teams looking for a proven No. 2 starter on a multi-year deal will find a competitive market for his services.

Nick Martinez also opted out of his deal. Martinez has been a reliable innings-eater over multiple seasons, capable of providing 160-180 innings with an ERA in the low-to-mid 3s. His market is likely similar in structure to King's but at a lower annual value.

Tier 3 — Depth and Innings

Logan Gray had his team option declined by the Padres. Seth Eflin had his option declined as well. Both are legitimate major league starters who provide teams with flexibility — either as a No. 4 or No. 5 starter, or as an innings-bridge option who can eat starts while a prospect develops.

Further down the market, the Padres released both Lucas Giolito and Andrew Canning. Giolito, a former top-of-rotation arm, has shown signs of his old form at points but has been inconsistent. At the right price on a short-term, incentive-laden deal, he could be a reclamation project worth pursuing for a team that values upside over certainty.

What This Means for Buyers

The depth of this market benefits mid-market teams significantly. A club like the Cardinals, Guardians, Brewers, or Blue Jays — teams that need rotation upgrades but cannot compete with the Mets or Dodgers for Skubal — can still dramatically improve their pitching staff in this market. King at four years and $80-90 million is a realistic option for any of those franchises. Martinez at three years and $45-55 million is similarly attainable.

The compressed CBA timeline also creates opportunity. If a lockout freezes transactions December 1, any team that has not signed rotation help will be frozen out of the market for months. The incentive to act quickly — particularly for teams that have clear rotation needs — is real.

What's Next

The GM Meetings this week will produce additional market intel on where these players fit. King and Martinez are likely to hear from multiple teams by the end of the week. The QO deadline on November 17 will clarify whether Suzuki, Arozarena, or others affect the market composition. And the shadow of the December 1 CBA deadline will push the entire market toward resolution — or freeze it. Either way, the rotation market will look dramatically different two weeks from today.
