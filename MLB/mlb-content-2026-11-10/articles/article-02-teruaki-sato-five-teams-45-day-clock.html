Teruaki Sato's MLB Market: Five Teams Bidding, 45-Day Clock Running Against Lockout

By Marcus Cole | November 10, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Teruaki Sato hit .306 with 39 home runs and 106 RBI in Japan's NPB in 2026 — winning the Triple Crown. The Hanshin Tigers posted him on November 1, starting a 45-day window that expires December 16. That window runs directly into the December 1 CBA deadline, creating a compressed timeline for one of the most intriguing international free agents in recent years. Five teams are most closely linked to him: the Dodgers, Mets, Padres, Phillies, and Yankees.

The Teams and Their Needs

The Mets are the most frequently mentioned destination. Reports suggest the organization views Sato as a potential first baseman — a need created when Pete Alonso signed with Baltimore last winter. If Sato can play first base competently, the Mets get a power bat at a position of need without paying for a traditional first baseman. Jon Heyman named the Mets among his top candidates, as did multiple other reporters.

The Dodgers are connected to nearly every major free agent, and Sato is no exception. Los Angeles already has significant depth in the infield, so the fit is less obvious than the Mets, but the Dodgers' willingness to absorb top talent regardless of position fit makes them a constant threat to sign anyone at any time.

The Phillies have shown interest in adding a right-handed power bat. The Yankees are evaluating Sato at third base — a position where their depth has thinned. The Padres continue to reshape their lineup after multiple departures, and Sato's left-handed power and on-base skills fit the type of player they typically target.

What the Contract Could Look Like

Contract projections for Sato cover a wide range, reflecting genuine uncertainty about how his skills translate from NPB to MLB. Jon Heyman projected four years and $80 million. Bleacher Report's Joel Reuter projected five years and $100 million. ESPN's Kiley McDaniel projected five years and $115 million. The variance is primarily about how much credit evaluators give to the strikeout rate — 26.7% in NPB — and how they expect his defense to play at third base in the American or National League.

For comparison, the last major NPB hitter to sign a large MLB deal was Munetaka Murakami, now slugging at first base for the Chicago White Sox. Sato is often compared to Murakami in terms of raw offensive profile, though the two have different defensive positions and contact rates.

The Lockout Deadline

Sato's 45-day window expires December 16 — but the CBA expires December 1. If a lockout hits and all transactions freeze before he signs, Sato must return to Hanshin for the 2027 season. That outcome would benefit the Hanshin Tigers and no one else. Both parties — the signing team and Sato's representation — have strong incentives to complete a deal by November 30. Watch for the market to accelerate in the final week of November.

What's Next

The GM Meetings this week will produce additional reporting on Sato's market. Teams that hold face-to-face meetings with his representation will give the market a clearer shape by the end of the week. The critical period is November 15-30 — once the QO deadline passes on November 17 and the CBA conversation reaches its final stage, the Sato signing could become the main event of the pre-lockout sprint. At $100 million or more, he represents one of the most significant investments in NPB talent since the posting system was formalized.
