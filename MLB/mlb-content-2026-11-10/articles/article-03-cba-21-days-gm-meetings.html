CBA Clock: 21 Days Out, GM Meetings Are Baseball's Last Shot Before the Lockout

By Jake Torres | November 10, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The MLB collective bargaining agreement expires December 1 at 11:59 PM Eastern. The GM Meetings, which opened Monday in Las Vegas and run through Wednesday, represent the last major gathering of team executives before that deadline. Whether they also represent the last chance to avoid a work stoppage is an open question — but most observers would put the odds of a deal at roughly 5 percent.

What Both Sides Want

The core dispute has not changed in months. The owners want a salary cap — their current proposal puts the 2028 figure at $258.2 million, with a $171 million floor. The floor is designed to appeal to players by guaranteeing minimum payrolls across all 30 clubs. The cap, as far as the MLBPA is concerned, is a non-starter.

The union's position: no cap, full stop. In place of a cap, the MLBPA wants a luxury-tax threshold raised to approximately $300 million, well above the current level, to create more room for teams to spend without triggering competitive balance penalties. The union also wants a minimum salary increase from $780,000 to $1 million for entry-level players.

These positions are not new. They were the same positions both sides held at the start of negotiations. The GM Meetings will not resolve them, but the face-to-face context may generate enough goodwill to accelerate the final stages of bargaining — if the will to deal exists on both sides.

The Last Lockout and Why It Matters

The 2021-22 MLB lockout lasted 99 days. It began December 2, 2021, the day after the previous CBA expired, and ended March 10, 2022, when the parties reached a five-year deal. The lockout shortened spring training and pushed opening day back by one week. No regular-season games were canceled, but the offseason was frozen for three months.

The 2027 timeline is tighter. Spring Training is scheduled to begin February 19. A lockout starting December 1 that matched the 2021-22 duration would run through early March — directly into the spring training window. Canceled spring training games are one thing. A shortened spring creates roster-preparation concerns that front offices have already begun gaming out.

The Free-Agency Incentive

One factor pushing toward a quick resolution — or at least a quick resolution of the most prominent free-agent deals — is the market itself. Tarik Skubal and Teruaki Sato both need to sign before December 1 to avoid having their negotiations frozen. Multiple analysts have reported that both sides of the most prominent free-agent negotiations are aware of the deadline and are working to get deals done before it hits. That could mean a flurry of signings in the final two weeks of November, regardless of whether a CBA deal materializes.

What's Next

Owners meetings are scheduled for November 17-19 — immediately after the qualifying offer deadline. Those meetings typically involve more league-wide policy discussions, but with the CBA expiration 12 days away at that point, labor negotiations will dominate. If no agreement is reached by November 30, the lockout begins at midnight. Watch for any joint statements from the league and MLBPA this week — a positive statement from the GM Meetings would be meaningful. Silence would be less encouraging.
