Skubal at GM Meetings: Mets and Dodgers Are the Primary Bidders in $355M Race

By Ryan Calloway | November 10, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The MLB General Manager Meetings opened Monday in Las Vegas, and there is only one conversation dominating the hallways: Tarik Skubal. The two-time Cy Young Award winner is the most coveted free agent on the market, and the field is narrowing. According to ESPN's Kiley McDaniel, the Mets and Dodgers have emerged as the primary bidders, while the Cubs have reportedly stepped back from the chase. The contract number most commonly cited — an average of eight years and $355 million, based on a survey of 27 insiders by ESPN — would shatter the record for annual value for a pitcher.

The Front-Runners

The Mets and Dodgers are both positioned to meet the financial demands that an eight-year deal at $355 million represents. For the Mets, Skubal would address a rotation that has struggled to maintain consistency at the top. The organization reportedly approached the market with a commitment to landing a true ace, and Skubal represents the best available option for a team built around an elite offensive core.

For the Dodgers, adding Skubal would be consistent with their historical approach: deploy resources toward elite talent regardless of roster depth, then manage around the acquisition. Los Angeles acquired Skubal from the Tigers during the season and has the inside track of familiarity with how he operates. The question is whether they are willing to commit to eight years and the kind of financial commitment that would represent even by their standards.

The Cubs, who had been mentioned frequently in early offseason conversations around Skubal, have reportedly made the decision to step back. Reports from one baseball outlet suggest the Cubs decided they could not win a bidding war against the Mets and Dodgers, and have shifted focus elsewhere. The Tigers remain in a monitoring posture — there has been speculation about Skubal returning to Detroit on a home-market discount, but no reports of a serious competing offer at the projected figure. The Yankees are described as being in consideration.

The CBA Timeline

The collective bargaining agreement expires December 1 at 11:59 PM ET. If the owners and MLBPA fail to reach a new deal, all player transactions — signings, trades, roster moves — freeze. The industry expectation, reflected in multiple reports, is that Skubal will sign before that deadline. The parties involved have incentives to move quickly: a lockout could delay the signing and complicate whatever term-and-value structure both sides are negotiating.

ESPN's Jeff Passan has reported that he expects a Skubal signing before December 1. With the GM Meetings running through Wednesday, executive conversations this week could lead to formal offers as early as mid-November.

What He Would Be Getting

Tarik Skubal won back-to-back American League Cy Young Awards in 2025 and 2026 — a rare back-to-back achievement that places him in elite historical company. He posted a combined ERA below 2.80 across both seasons, struck out more than 250 batters in 2026, and has led the American League in both ERA and strikeouts in recent years. He is 27 years old, left-handed, and has a fastball that consistently runs above 95 miles per hour with a slider that grades as an elite out pitch.

An eight-year deal would carry him through his age-35 season. Pitching contracts of that length carry inherent risk — multiple starting pitchers on long-term deals have encountered durability issues mid-contract — but Skubal has shown no such warning signs. He made 32 starts in 2026 and has not been on the injured list in either of his Cy Young seasons.

What's Next

The GM Meetings run through Wednesday, November 12. Executive conversations this week will not produce a signing — deals of this magnitude require more process — but they establish the competitive landscape. Watch for formal offers to emerge in the Nov. 15-20 range, with the Dec. 1 CBA deadline creating urgency. If the Mets and Dodgers are genuinely the two finalists, this could become a negotiation that moves quickly once both sides make formal submissions. The biggest pitching contract in baseball history could be a week or two away.
