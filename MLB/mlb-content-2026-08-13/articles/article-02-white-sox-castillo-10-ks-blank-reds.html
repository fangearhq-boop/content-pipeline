Luis Castillo Fans 10, White Sox Blank Reds 5-0 — AL Central Lead Holds at 62-56

By Marcus Cole

August 13, 2026

One night after dropping a 5-4 extra-inning game to the Cincinnati Reds on Pope Hat Night, the Chicago White Sox came back and answered with a statement. Luis Castillo took the mound on August 12 and was as sharp as any starter in the American League has been all month — seven innings, ten strikeouts, no runs, done. Final score: White Sox 5, Reds 0.

Chicago is 62-56. Detroit is 3.5 games back. Minnesota is 4.0 back. The White Sox remain in first place in the AL Central.

Castillo's Performance

Ten strikeouts in seven innings is the kind of number that doesn't require much framing. Castillo worked efficiently through a Reds lineup that had forced extra innings 24 hours earlier, and the White Sox defense gave him nothing to worry about. The shutout was clean and decisive — the kind of performance that reminds the rest of the AL Central what this team looks like when it's operating correctly.

Castillo arrived in Chicago at the trade deadline, one of the White Sox's most significant additions in a summer that saw them take the unusual step of being buyers after years of rebuilding. He's not just a rotation piece — he's a statement about organizational intent. Wednesday night, he pitched like it.

The Bigger Context

The numbers on the AL Central leaderboard are worth sitting with for a moment. The Chicago White Sox went 41-121 in 2024. That was the worst record in modern Major League Baseball history — a season so thoroughly lost that it prompted a full organizational reset. They followed that with 60-102 in 2025, a 19-game improvement that still looked like losing.

Now they're 62-56, in first place in August, with less than 45 games to play. The conversation has shifted from rebuilding to October. Colson Montgomery is hitting, the rotation has depth, and a deadline-acquired Luis Castillo just struck out 10 on a Wednesday night against a team that had beaten them the previous evening.

The Detroit Tigers are 3.5 back. The Minnesota Twins are 4.0 behind. Neither club has gone away. But neither club has a starter who just threw a 10-strikeout shutout, either.

Series Result

The White Sox split the two-game mini-series with Cincinnati — dropping the Tuesday game 5-4 in 10 innings on Elly De La Cruz's walk-off sacrifice fly, then bouncing back with Wednesday's shutout. A split against a competitive Reds club is a reasonable outcome, and the manner of the win — dominant pitching, not a blown chance to lose cleanly — is exactly what a pennant-contending team wants to take into the next series.
