Cubs 70-50, Wild Card Lead Grows to 6 — August Is Theirs to Lose

By Ryan Calloway

August 13, 2026

The Chicago Cubs are 70-50. Their NL Wild Card lead stands at approximately 6 games over both the San Diego Padres and Arizona Diamondbacks, who are hovering around 64-57. With roughly 42 games remaining, Chicago has built the kind of cushion that matters in August: enough room to absorb a bad series without losing ground, while still creating pressure on everything chasing them.

This is a Cubs team that made the National League Wild Card race look more complicated than it needed to be earlier in the season, then took control at the trade deadline and has not let go.

The Deadline Additions Are Delivering

Three of the Cubs' August 3 acquisitions are now woven into the fabric of this team's daily operation. Kevin Gausman, acquired from the Toronto Blue Jays, has joined the rotation and given Chicago a top-of-the-order arm they didn't have before the deadline. Clay Holmes, brought over from the New York Mets along with Tyrone Taylor, has added depth and reliability to the backend of the bullpen. Braxton Garrett, the left-hander from Miami, rounds out a staff that now has genuine late-season depth.

These weren't moves made out of desperation. They were moves made from a position of strength by a team that already held a Wild Card spot and wanted to hold it all the way through October. The result is a roster that feels sturdier now than it did before August.

The Wild Card Picture

The San Diego Padres and Arizona Diamondbacks currently hold the other two NL Wild Card positions. Both clubs are around 64-57, roughly 6 games behind the Cubs in the standings. The Phillies — who play tonight in the Field of Dreams game — remain in contention as well, adding urgency to how the picture looks over the next two weeks.

The Cubs don't need to run the table to reach October. They need to play competitive baseball in August and not let a double-digit lead collapse. That's a significantly more manageable task than where they were in June, when the Wild Card race was legitimately crowded and Chicago's lead was measured in single games.

What 42 Games Looks Like

Forty-two games is roughly a quarter of a season. Enough time for a team 6 games back to close the gap — but only if the team in front stops winning entirely, which Cubs teams built like this rarely do. Gausman, Holmes, and Garrett aren't going anywhere. The lineup that won 70 games is intact. The coaching staff that managed this team to a deadline position of strength isn't making wholesale changes in August.

The Cubs are not perfect. No 70-win team is. But in the National League Wild Card race, imperfect and 6 games up is a good place to be in mid-August.
