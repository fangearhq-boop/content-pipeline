Tarik Skubal Is 0-1 in Two Dodger Starts — And the Adjustment Window Is Open

By Marcus Cole

August 13, 2026

Two starts into his tenure as a Los Angeles Dodger, Tarik Skubal is still looking for his first win. His personal record in blue is 0-1. The Dodgers are 1-1 in his outings. Neither figure captures what the two-time AL Cy Young Award winner has actually looked like — which is good but not yet Skubal.

The adjustment window is open. His next start arrives in the next few days. And in the NL West standings, the Dodgers need their most expensive trade-deadline acquisition to find his form before October becomes a real conversation.

Two Starts, Two Outcomes

Start one came on August 4 at Wrigley Field against the Chicago Cubs. Skubal went six innings, allowed four hits and two earned runs, struck out six, walked one, and threw 85 pitches. By any reasonable standard, that's a solid start. The Dodgers gave him one run of support and lost 5-1. He took the loss through no particular fault of his own.

Start two came on August 10, his Dodger Stadium home debut against the Kansas City Royals. This one was spottier. Skubal worked five innings, allowed three earned runs on four hits, fanned six again, and threw 95 pitches before exiting. He acknowledged his fastball command was off in places — "spotty" was the word he used. The Dodgers rallied to win 6-5 without him, which means Skubal got a no-decision.

Six strikeouts in each start. Three earned runs in one, two in the other. The output is passable by most standards. By Skubal standards — which are those of a pitcher who posted a 2.79 ERA in 2026 with Detroit before the trade — it's a work in progress.

The 1,000th Strikeout and What It Represents

On July 29, still wearing a Tigers uniform, Skubal recorded his 1,000th career strikeout. The batter was Taylor Ward of the Baltimore Orioles, the very first hitter of the game. It was a milestone that belongs to his Detroit career — to the two Cy Young seasons, the development years, and the ace emergence that made him the most coveted arm at the trade deadline.

Everything since has been Los Angeles. His 1,001st strikeout came in a Cubs win. His 1,012th came in a Royals loss he didn't take. The numbers keep accumulating. The wins don't yet.

What the Dodgers Need

The Dodgers traded three of their better prospects — Zyhir Hope, River Ryan, and Brady Smith — to acquire Skubal for a reason. They needed a frontline starter capable of carrying the rotation through October with Glasnow and Ohtani in various stages of uncertainty. Skubal, at his best, is exactly that. He just needs to find his best in a new uniform, with a new catcher, on a team he's still learning.

Two starts is a small sample. The fastball command will come. The wins will follow. That's the reasonable expectation for a pitcher of Skubal's caliber in a normal adjustment period. The Dodgers have enough time for a normal adjustment period, but not an unlimited one.
