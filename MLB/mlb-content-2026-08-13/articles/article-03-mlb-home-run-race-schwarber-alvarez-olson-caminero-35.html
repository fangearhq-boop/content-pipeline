Four Players. Four Teams. All Tied at 35. The MLB Home Run Race Just Got Very Interesting.

By Jake Torres

August 13, 2026

There is no clear leader in the 2026 MLB home run race. Heading into August 13, four players from four different teams sit at exactly 35 home runs apiece: Kyle Schwarber of the Philadelphia Phillies, Yordan Alvarez of the Houston Astros, Matt Olson of the Atlanta Braves, and Junior Caminero of the Tampa Bay Rays.

Four players tied for the MLB lead in mid-August isn't unusual in isolation. A four-way tie, with each player representing a genuine contender or story, is not something that happens on a schedule. The 2026 race arrived here organically, and now somebody has to pull away.

Who They Are

Schwarber is the most recognizable name in this group, and the most prolific recent history. He hit 56 home runs in 2025 to lead the National League, becoming just the 18th player in Major League Baseball history to reach 20 home runs in his team's first 49 games this season. He is 35 years old and is having one of the best seasons of a very good career. Tonight, he's playing in the Field of Dreams game in Dyersville, Iowa, wearing a 1939-41 Phillies throwback under the Netflix lights.

Alvarez is the most feared hitter in the American League, a player whose swing produces damage that statistical models still strain to fully describe. Olson has been one of baseball's most consistent power producers since he arrived in Atlanta. Caminero, 22, is the youngest of the group — the breakout name in a Rays organization that keeps producing them, a right-handed bat with raw power that has translated faster than most expected at the major league level.

Four different archetypes. Four different situations. All at 35.

Where the Race Goes From Here

History suggests that a four-way tie in mid-August won't stay that way for long. August and September have a way of separating hitters who accumulate home runs early from those who sustain them late. Schwarber — given his second-half track record and the fact that he hit 56 last season — is probably the consensus choice to lead the group come September 1.

But Olson's consistency as a Brave, Alvarez's sheer power output, and Caminero's explosive upside make this a genuine race, not a coronation. All four players are on teams that still have meaningful games to play. Home runs in September, when the stakes are higher and the matchups get harder, mean something different than they do in April.

The Schwarber-Iowa Footnote

Among tonight's field of storylines surrounding the Field of Dreams game in Dyersville, Iowa, the one that arrives most naturally is this: the player who is tied for the major league home run lead — Schwarber — is the same player suiting up in a throwback Phillies jersey in a Iowa cornfield under a Netflix broadcast, with Hall of Famers Mike Schmidt and Steve Carlton in attendance.

Schmidt hit 548 career home runs. Carlton is in the Hall of Fame as one of the greatest pitchers of his era. Schwarber, if he keeps hitting at this pace, is writing his own case for what comes after his playing career. There's enough symmetry there to notice.
