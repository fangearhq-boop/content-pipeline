Jacob Misiorowski's 2026 Season Makes the AL Cy Young Award Case Undeniable

By Marcus Cole | October 29, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

A Yahoo Sports headline from Wednesday night called Jacob Misiorowski "Cy Young in-waiting." That framing was generous to everyone else still in the conversation.

Misiorowski's 2026 season — capped by a dominant Game 5 performance before a calf cramp forced him out — is the kind of year that ends the AL Cy Young debate before it begins. The numbers say it. The postseason reinforced it. There is no credible argument for anyone else.

The Regular Season by the Numbers

In 30 starts, Misiorowski went 16-5 with a 1.80 ERA across 174.2 innings pitched. He struck out 252 batters, leading Major League Baseball in both ERA and strikeouts. He is the first pitcher to lead MLB in both categories in the same season in recent memory.

The 1.80 ERA tells you how rarely opponents scored on him. The 252 strikeouts tell you why. Misiorowski gets hitters out before they can do damage — swing and miss at a rate that makes contact management almost a secondary concern. When he was on, which was most of the time, he was simply unhittable.

His fastball played a central role. In World Series Game 1, he hit 103.5 miles per hour on the radar gun — the fastest pitch recorded in the Statcast era during a World Series game.

A Postseason ERA Under 1.00

If the regular season made the case, the postseason sealed it. Misiorowski entered Game 5 with a postseason ERA under 1.00. He had been brilliant against the Padres, dominated the Dodgers, and outpitched everyone Tampa Bay threw at him in the World Series.

In Game 1 of the Fall Classic, he threw five innings, allowing one earned run on four hits with three strikeouts. In the clincher, he was even better: five and two-thirds scoreless innings, one hit, two walks, two strikeouts, before a calf cramp ended his night.

He left with a 4-0 lead in a game the Brewers would win 4-1. The only run scored after he was gone. He did his job so completely that the outcome was decided before he walked off the mound.

The Competition

The two-time reigning AL Cy Young Award winner, Tarik Skubal, spent only part of the 2026 season with Detroit before being traded to the Dodgers at the deadline. His Tigers numbers — 7-5, 2.79 ERA, 116 strikeouts in 96.2 innings — were strong but incomplete for a full-season award. His ERA with the Dodgers was not submitted as part of a Tigers campaign for the award, and no pitcher split between two teams in midseason has made a compelling Cy Young case against a pitcher of Misiorowski's caliber.

There is no other name that meaningfully challenges what Misiorowski did from April through October.

What's Next

The Cy Young Award for the American League will be announced in the coming weeks. Misiorowski is the clear frontrunner and likely winner. The injury — a calf cramp that forced him from Game 5 — is not expected to be serious, and he should be fully healthy before spring training begins.

He is 24 years old, under team control, and coming off the most dominant single season by a Brewers pitcher in modern franchise history. The 2027 season cannot start soon enough for Milwaukee fans who just watched him help end a 57-year championship drought.
