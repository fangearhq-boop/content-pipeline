Tampa Bay Rays Fall One Win Short of the Greatest Comeback in World Series History

By Ryan Calloway | October 29, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

No team has ever come back from three games down to win the World Series. The 2026 Tampa Bay Rays got one game closer than almost anyone thought they could.

After losing Games 1, 2, and 3 to fall into a 3-0 hole against the Milwaukee Brewers, Tampa Bay staged a remarkable Game 4 comeback and forced a deciding fifth game. On Wednesday night, the Brewers ended it, 4-1. But what the Rays showed in the final two games of the Fall Classic made their season impossible to dismiss.

Game 4: The Night Tampa Bay Refused to Quit

The Rays entered Game 4 staring at elimination in a World Series they were widely expected to lose. They played like none of it mattered.

Cooper Pratt gave the Brewers a lead with a home run in the top of the 12th inning. Tampa Bay could have folded. Instead, Ben Williamson stepped to the plate in the bottom of the 12th and lined a walk-off single, scoring the winning run to keep the series alive.

It was a historic moment. Tampa Bay became only the fifth team in World Series history to win Game 4 while trailing 3-0. No team before them had ever turned that into a championship. The 2026 Rays came closer than any of the previous four.

What Might Have Been Different

Shane McClanahan was on the injured list for the entire World Series. His absence fundamentally changed what Tampa Bay could do with its rotation. McClanahan, one of the American League's best left-handed starters, would have been the obvious choice to start Game 5 — or to anchor a late-inning bullpen option — and his unavailability forced the Rays to stretch lesser alternatives.

Whether his presence changes the outcome is impossible to say. But it is the dominant what-if question of the series. A fully healthy Tampa Bay roster, with McClanahan in the rotation, may have been a different kind of challenge for Milwaukee.

A Season Worth Celebrating

The 2026 Rays were not supposed to be here. They play in one of baseball's smallest markets with one of its most limited payrolls. Getting to the World Series required winning a playoff series, then another, then another — beating better-funded teams along the way through pitching depth, manufacturing runs, and the kind of execution that comes from organizational discipline built over years.

They reached the Fall Classic. They extended a dominant Brewers team to five games. They forced a 12-inning Game 4 and won it on a walk-off. The 2026 season added a chapter to the ongoing story of what Tampa Bay can accomplish when operating at its best.

What's Next

The Rays will enter the offseason as a team that just played in the World Series. That is a recruiting tool, a point of organizational pride, and a foundation for 2027. McClanahan's recovery will be the most closely watched development — if he is healthy by spring training, Tampa Bay's rotation outlook changes considerably.

The front office will evaluate the roster, address free agency within their constraints, and look for the creative moves that have defined the Rays' model for decades. They did not win the championship. But they gave their city something to hold onto, and they gave the game one of its best stories of the year.
