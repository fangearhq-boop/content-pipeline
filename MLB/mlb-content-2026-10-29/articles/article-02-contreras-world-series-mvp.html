William Contreras Named World Series MVP After Two-Way Masterpiece

By Ryan Calloway | October 29, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Pat Murphy saw it coming before anyone else said it out loud.

After Game 1 of the World Series, the Milwaukee Brewers manager offered an unprompted assessment of his catcher: "If people aren't noticing Contreras, like he was maybe the MVP of the Dodgers series." That was before the Fall Classic was even a day old. By the time the Brewers clinched in five games, there was no debate left. William Contreras was the World Series MVP.

The Numbers That Won the Award

Contreras batted .381 across the five-game series and drove in four runs. He went 10-for-13 on automatic ball challenges — winning those pitcher-batter confrontations at an elite rate. Behind the plate, he threw out four Tampa Bay baserunners, neutralizing a Rays team that had built its identity around aggressive base running.

No single number captures what Contreras did in this series. It was the totality of his performance — contributing offensively every night, dominating defensively, and making the game easier for a pitching staff that was already historically good.

Game by Game

In Game 1, a tense 3-2 Brewers victory, Contreras went 2-for-3 with an RBI and a walk. That start set the tone. He was productive in a close game that Milwaukee needed to win to seize control of the series.

Game 3 was when the series turned decisively. The Brewers won 10-1, with Contreras going 3-for-5 with an RBI. Milwaukee's 2-out, 8-run fifth inning set a World Series record, as did their seven consecutive 2-out hits in that frame. Contreras was in the middle of it.

Through every game, he also brought something that numbers do not capture: the slow home run trot. When Contreras hits one out, he walks. He savors the moment with the unhurried confidence of someone who knows exactly what he is. It became one of the defining images of the 2026 postseason.

A Two-Way Impact That Changed the Series

World Series MVP awards often go to the hitter who got the most clutch hits or the pitcher who dominated on the mound. Contreras won it by doing both jobs — offense and defense — better than anyone on either roster for five consecutive games.

The four caught stealings he recorded were not incidental. Tampa Bay's running game was part of their offensive identity. Contreras shut it down. When the Rays could not manufacture runs on the bases, they were left to rely on a depleted pitching staff — with Shane McClanahan on the injured list — to keep pace with Milwaukee's lineup. They could not.

Murphy recognized it early and said it publicly. The voters agreed when it mattered.

What's Next

Contreras is under contract with Milwaukee and expected to remain the anchor of a championship-winning battery. Extension conversations between the Brewers and his representatives are likely to begin this offseason, with both sides possessing strong motivation to get something done before the CBA expires December 1 and potentially freezes the market.

He is 27 years old and at the peak of his abilities. The 2026 World Series is likely just one entry in a career that still has a long way to run. For now, he holds the trophy, the MVP award, and a place in Milwaukee baseball history.
