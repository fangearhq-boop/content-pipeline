MLB Free Agency Is Open — And the CBA Deadline Is Already Counting Down

By Jake Torres | October 29, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Twenty-four hours after the Brewers won the World Series, the hot stove is already burning.

MLB free agency officially opened today. Teams have entered a five-day exclusive window to negotiate with their own departing free agents before the broader market opens. But there is a hard deadline hanging over every conversation: the collective bargaining agreement between MLB and the Players Association expires on December 1, 2026. A lockout is widely expected. Teams that want to get deals done before a potential freeze have weeks, not months.

The Players Available

Two names stand above the rest in this market.

Randy Arozarena is considered the best available bat. The outfielder played the 2026 season with the Seattle Mariners on a one-year, $15.65 million deal he signed to avoid arbitration. He is now a free agent and expected to command a significant multiyear contract. Teams linked include the Philadelphia Phillies, the Boston Red Sox, and the Atlanta Braves, per multiple reports. He is regarded as the top position player on the market in a class that does not run especially deep.

On the pitching side, Tarik Skubal is the marquee name. The two-time reigning AL Cy Young Award winner — he won back-to-back in 2024 and 2025 — was traded from Detroit to the Los Angeles Dodgers at the 2026 trade deadline. In 96.2 innings with the Tigers before the deal, he went 7-5 with a 2.79 ERA and 116 strikeouts. He is now a free agent. His one-year, $32 million deal with Detroit was the product of a record arbitration ruling in February; the free agent contract he signs this winter is expected to be far larger — projections have ranged from $350 million to $420 million.

The CBA Complication

None of the normal offseason timelines apply this year. The collective bargaining agreement expires December 1, and a work stoppage is widely anticipated. Owners are expected to push for a salary cap — a demand that the Players Association has historically rejected. If both sides cannot reach a deal, a lockout would freeze all transactions.

That means teams looking to sign Arozarena, Skubal, or any significant free agent effectively have about four weeks to get deals finalized before the window potentially closes. The Winter Meetings, traditionally scheduled in early December, were set for December 7-9 in San Diego, but most observers expect them to be cancelled if a lockout takes effect before then.

The practical result: this will be the most compressed and urgent hot stove market in years. Teams that move slowly risk losing their targets entirely.

What Teams Are Watching

For contending teams that fell short in October — including teams like the Dodgers and Mariners, who made deep playoff runs — the question is how aggressively to pursue marquee free agents under time pressure. A deal with Skubal or Arozarena signed before December 1 survives a lockout; one that hasn't closed doesn't.

Rebuilding teams face a different calculus: this offseason may be a good time to sell assets to win-now clubs willing to pay a premium because the window is so short.

What's Next

The exclusive five-day re-signing window closes in early November. After that, teams can negotiate with any free agent from another club. Expect significant activity in the first two weeks of November as teams race to complete marquee deals. By late November, the market will either be roaring or frozen, depending on whether labor talks produce a new CBA before the December 1 deadline.

The Brewers, fresh off their championship, will be evaluating which pieces to prioritize keeping and where they need reinforcement. Even champions don't stand still in a market this compressed.
