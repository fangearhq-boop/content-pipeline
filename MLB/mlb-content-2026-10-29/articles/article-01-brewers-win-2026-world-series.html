Milwaukee Brewers Win First World Series Title in 57-Year Franchise History

By Jake Torres | October 29, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The Milwaukee Brewers are World Series champions.

On Wednesday night at Tropicana Field in St. Petersburg, the Brewers beat the Tampa Bay Rays 4-1 in Game 5 to claim the franchise's first World Series title in 57 years of existence. It is the culmination of a patient rebuild, a generation of homegrown talent, and one of the most dominant postseason runs in recent memory.

How Game 5 Unfolded

Jacob Misiorowski took the ball with everything on the line and delivered. The 24-year-old right-hander threw 5 and two-thirds scoreless innings, surrendering just one hit and two walks while striking out two, before leaving with a calf cramp. He gave his team exactly what it needed: a chance.

Cooper Pratt provided the offensive production, collecting two hits and driving in two runs. The rookie had already homered in Game 4, and he kept his hot bat going when the series was on the line.

Closer Trevor Megill entered in the eighth and worked a two-inning save to finish it. He allowed one run — an RBI double by Chandler Simpson in the ninth — but nothing more. When the final out was recorded, the Brewers' 57-year wait was over.

Behind the plate for every moment: William Contreras, who would be named World Series MVP. He framed every pitch, threw out baserunner after baserunner throughout the series, and contributed at the plate with a .381 batting average and four RBI across five games.

57 Years in the Making

The franchise that became the Milwaukee Brewers was born in 1969 as the Seattle Pilots, a one-year expansion team that relocated to Wisconsin before its second season. They became the Brewers in 1970 and spent decades as one of baseball's perennial also-rans.

Their only previous World Series appearance came in 1982, when a Brewers team led by Robin Yount and Paul Molitor lost to the St. Louis Cardinals in seven games. That loss haunted a franchise and a city for more than four decades.

The 2026 Brewers erased it Wednesday night.

An 11-2 Postseason Run

Milwaukee's path to the championship was dominant from start to finish. The Brewers went 11-2 in the postseason, winning series against the San Diego Padres, the Los Angeles Dodgers, and the Rays. They never trailed in a series until Tampa Bay extended the World Series to a fifth game with their stunning 12-inning Game 4 victory.

That setback made Wednesday's win more meaningful. The Brewers responded to adversity, came back to Tropicana Field, and closed it out. The test only strengthened the legacy of this championship run.

This team was built through player development. Misiorowski is homegrown. So is much of the core that surrounded him. General manager Matt Arnold and manager Pat Murphy built something sustainable — a team that was competitive enough to win now while remaining intact for years to come.

What's Next

The celebration in Milwaukee will last days. The championship parade is expected to draw hundreds of thousands to the city.

On the baseball side, attention turns quickly to the AL Cy Young Award, where Misiorowski is the overwhelming frontrunner after his 16-5, 1.80 ERA, 252-strikeout regular season was backed up by a postseason ERA under 1.00. The award will be announced in coming weeks.

The offseason also begins today, with free agency officially open. The Brewers will need to evaluate their roster and determine which pieces to prioritize re-signing. With the collective bargaining agreement expiring December 1, they — and every other team — are working against a hard deadline.

But none of that matters tonight. Tonight belongs to Milwaukee, Wisconsin, and 57 years of waiting finally rewarded.
