11 Days Until the MLB Lockout: What Fans Need to Know

By Ryan Calloway

November 20, 2026

The MLB collective bargaining agreement expires in 11 days — December 1 at 11:59 PM Eastern. When it does, baseball fans are likely to wake up on December 2 to a locked-out sport: no transactions, no trades, no signings, and no official communication between teams and players. The owners' meetings in New York concluded this week with no reported progress. The last MLB lockout lasted 99 days. Here is what every fan needs to understand about what comes next.

What Is Actually Being Fought Over

The core dispute is a salary cap. MLB commissioner Rob Manfred has stated that the owners will not ratify a new collective bargaining agreement without a cap-and-floor system similar to those in the NBA and NFL. The owners' most recent proposal reportedly included a $258.2 million cap and a $180.2 million floor, with implementation delayed to 2028.

The MLB Players Association has called a salary cap a non-starter. The union argues that a cap would artificially suppress player earnings at a time when MLB revenues have never been higher. Union executive Tony Clark has signaled the MLBPA will not accept any mechanism that limits total player compensation — and estimates suggest a cap structure would cost players roughly $400 million annually compared to the current system.

Other disputed issues include maximum contract length (owners want a five-year cap), the qualifying offer system (the MLBPA wants it eliminated), and minimum salary increases. But the cap is the wall that neither side has found a way around.

What Happens on December 2

If the CBA expires without a deal, the commissioner locks the players out. That means every pending transaction in baseball freezes. Free agents who have not signed — including Tarik Skubal, Seiya Suzuki, Teruaki Sato, Jazz Chisholm, and Randy Arozarena — are stuck. Teams cannot sign, trade, or officially communicate with players under contract. The annual Rule 5 Draft is likely canceled. Arbitration hearings are suspended.

Minor league operations continue under a separate agreement. Spring training facilities are closed to players on the 40-man roster. No on-field work happens until a new deal is reached.

What the 2021-22 Lockout Tells You

The last MLB work stoppage began December 2, 2021, and ended March 10, 2022 — 99 days. The agreement was reached under deadline pressure after the first two weeks of the regular season were officially canceled. When the deal was reached, teams had to rapidly complete rosters and shortened spring training in roughly two weeks before a delayed opening day.

The 2026-27 situation has significant differences — the salary cap demand is a larger philosophical ask than the disputes in 2021 — but the precedent is instructive. Baseball's economic ecosystem does not function well during a lockout, and both sides have financial incentives to reach a deal before games are lost.

The March Deadline

The informal deadline before regular-season games are at risk is early to mid-March 2027. If a deal is not reached by then, the commissioner faces a choice between beginning the season without a CBA or canceling games — an outcome no one wants. The 99-day clock from December 2 puts that deadline in early March, which is exactly the edge of the danger zone.

What's Next

Watch the next 11 days closely. Any movement in the core dispute — particularly any softening of the salary cap position from either side — would be significant. A deal before December 1 would be the best-case scenario for fans and players alike. A lockout beginning December 2 would put everything at risk: the Hot Stove market, spring training, and potentially the first weeks of the 2027 season. Baseball has been here before. It has never been a comfortable place to be.
