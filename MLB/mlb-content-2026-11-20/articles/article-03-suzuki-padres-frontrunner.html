Jeff Passan Reports: Padres Meet with Suzuki and 'Could Be the Favorites' to Sign Him

By Marcus Cole

November 20, 2026

The Seiya Suzuki market just moved. ESPN's Jeff Passan reported that the San Diego Padres held a meeting with Suzuki and "could be the favorites" to sign the outfielder. That framing — almost certainly the favorites — is as strong a projection as you typically see from Passan before a deal is announced, and it represents a significant shift from the narrative that had placed the Blue Jays and Cubs at the front of the race.

Suzuki, who officially declined the Cubs' qualifying offer two weeks ago, is projected for approximately four years and $109.9 million by Spotrac. He enters the market as one of the sport's better two-way outfielders after four seasons in Chicago and an earlier distinguished career in Japan's NPB.

Why San Diego?

The Padres have built their core around Manny Machado and Fernando Tatis Jr. — a foundation capable of competing for a World Series in a fully loaded National League West. What San Diego has needed for two seasons is a reliable third bat in the outfield who can play quality defense and reach base against right-handed pitching. Suzuki fits that profile precisely.

San Diego's recent financial flexibility — created in part by shedding bloated contracts and leaning on the farm system — gives the organization room to offer Suzuki the kind of four-year commitment that would appeal to a player seeking a long-term home without the full complexity of a mega-deal. The Padres can be aggressive without being reckless.

The Mariners' Withdrawal

The Seattle Mariners had been mentioned as a natural destination for Suzuki — a Japanese player in a city with a large Japanese-American community and a franchise with a strong history of connecting with Japanese stars. MLBTradeRumors reports, however, that the Mariners are unlikely to offer a long-term deal for Suzuki. Seattle is simultaneously trying to retain other pieces of their roster and cannot commit the full term Suzuki is seeking.

That Seattle hesitancy creates a vacuum that San Diego appears to be filling. The Mariners' interest without a long-term offer effectively takes them out of the most likely bidder category — leaving Suzuki's market to teams that can commit to the full four-year arc.

The Blue Jays' Complication

Toronto has been pursuing Seiya Suzuki alongside Jazz Chisholm — a dual pursuit that creates an interesting roster-building tension. The Blue Jays have the resources for both, but adding both Suzuki and Chisholm simultaneously would represent a significant payroll commitment in a year where the organization is already reshaping its core. If the Padres move quickly on Suzuki, Toronto pivots harder to other options. If Suzuki's market stalls, the Blue Jays could re-emerge as the primary bidder.

What's Next

Suzuki's market, like every other major free-agent market right now, is running against a December 1 wall. The CBA expires in 11 days, and a lockout would freeze all transactions. If the Padres are indeed the frontrunners, expect movement before December 1 — Passan's framing suggests a deal is closer than the market's public reporting has acknowledged. For the Cubs, losing Suzuki after declining his qualifying offer means rebuilding both their outfield and their entire starting rotation under a rapidly closing deadline. For San Diego, signing Suzuki would be one of the most consequential additions of the Peter Seidler era.
