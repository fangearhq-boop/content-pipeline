Teruaki Sato Can Be Signed During the Lockout — Here's Why His Dec. 16 Deadline Matters More Than Ever

By Jake Torres

November 20, 2026

When the MLB collective bargaining agreement expires December 1 and the expected lockout begins December 2, every pending transaction in baseball freezes. Free agents who haven't signed are stuck. Teams cannot negotiate, trade, or officially interact with players on their own roster.

Teruaki Sato is the exception. And that exception makes him the most important name in baseball for the second half of November and the first two weeks of December.

Why Sato Can Sign During the Lockout

Sato's availability in the United States is governed by the NPB (Nippon Professional Baseball) posting agreement — a separate international framework that exists entirely outside the MLB-MLBPA collective bargaining structure. The CBA governs the terms and conditions of employment for MLB players. The posting system is a bilateral agreement between NPB and MLB that predates the current labor dispute and operates independently of whatever agreement exists between owners and the players' union.

Practically speaking: when the lockout begins, Sato's 45-day posting window stays open. Teams can still negotiate with Sato and his representatives and sign him to a major league contract. His December 16 deadline does not move because of labor action. The lockout does not apply to him.

This was confirmed by the structure of previous posting periods — during the 2021-22 lockout, there were no major posted Japanese players with active windows, so the question was largely theoretical. This year, with Sato's window running from November 1 through December 16, the question is live and consequential.

Who Is Teruaki Sato?

Sato, 25, won the Japanese Central League Triple Crown in 2026, posting a .308 batting average with 39 home runs for the Hanshin Tigers. His projected contract value ranges from $80 million (Heyman's floor estimate) to more than $120 million (analyst Jim Bowden's projection). Teams linked include the Mets, Yankees, Dodgers, and Phillies.

The knock on Sato is defensive positioning — he played third base in Japan, and there are questions about whether his arm plays at that position in the majors. The more likely translation is a move to left field or designated hitter, which slightly complicates his value proposition for teams looking for multi-positional flexibility. That said, a Triple Crown winner at 25 is a generational bat, and the market for that kind of talent is deep regardless of where he plays defensively.

The Teams Watching the Clock

For teams that miss out on Tarik Skubal — the top free-agent prize of the offseason — Sato becomes the consolation signing that might not feel like a consolation at all. A team that gets neither Skubal nor Sato by December 16 faces a lockout market that is effectively frozen until a new CBA is ratified, potentially in spring.

The Mets are already in on both Skubal and Sato, per reports. If the Mets sign Skubal in the next few days, their interest in Sato likely cools — there is a payroll reality even for Steve Cohen. The Yankees, Dodgers, and Phillies all have lineup gaps that Sato could fill, and all three would benefit from getting a deal done before the December 16 wall.

What Happens If He Goes Unsigned

If December 16 arrives and Sato has not signed with an MLB team, he returns to the Hanshin Tigers for the 2027 NPB season. He cannot be re-posted until the following offseason. For the teams pursuing him, that means a one-year delay on a player who will be 26 next offseason and whose contract projections are unlikely to decrease.

No team wants to be in that position. The window is 26 days from today. The only mechanism that stops the clock is a signed contract.

What's Next

Watch for acceleration in Sato's market in the first two weeks of December — after the lockout begins. Teams that struck out on Skubal, Suzuki, or Chisholm will pivot to Sato with urgency. The deadline is December 16. It does not move for the lockout, for negotiations, or for anything else. That binary reality — sign him or lose him for a year — makes Sato the clearest deadline in an otherwise frozen offseason market.
