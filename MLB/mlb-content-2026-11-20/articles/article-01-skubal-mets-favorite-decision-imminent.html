Mets Are the Industry Favorites for Skubal — And the Decision Is Expected Within Days

By Jake Torres

November 20, 2026

Tarik Skubal still has not signed. That is now the most consequential unsigned status in professional sports. As of November 20, multiple sources describe the New York Mets as the "industry favorites" to land the best starting pitcher on the free-agent market — but the Dodgers have not conceded, the Blue Jays remain active, and a CBA expiration in 11 days is turning a free-agent negotiation into a timed event with real consequences.

The contract projections have ranged from $325 million on the low end to as high as $429 million. Either figure would shatter the existing record for a pitcher — Yoshinobu Yamamoto's 12-year, $325 million deal with the Dodgers in 2023. At $44 million per year, Skubal's projected average annual value would nearly double Yamamoto's $27 million AAV.

What the Meeting Said

The Mets sent a high-powered contingent to Orange County, California, for a face-to-face meeting with Skubal and his agent Scott Boras. Multiple insiders described the session as going "very well" for New York's side. Steve Cohen's willingness to spend has never been in question — the Mets owner has repeatedly shown he will pay whatever it takes to field a championship-caliber roster. That credibility matters in a negotiation of this scale.

The Mets need Skubal in a way few other teams do. After finishing the 2026 season with a rotation question mark and a payroll built for immediate contention, landing Skubal would signal a commitment to 2027 at the highest possible level. If the Mets can pair Skubal with their existing core, they become immediate World Series contenders.

The Dodgers' Retreat

Reports from November 19 indicate the Dodgers are not willing to enter a bidding war with the Mets and Blue Jays at Skubal's preferred contract length. Los Angeles reportedly wants a shorter deal — a structure that would reduce their long-term commitment but falls short of what Skubal is seeking. That gap has effectively moved the Dodgers from "frontrunner" to "interested observer" in the current reporting.

The Dodgers remain dangerous in any negotiation simply because of their infrastructure and track record of persuading free agents to take slightly lower numbers for the right situation. But if Skubal is set on the longest possible term, Los Angeles has a structural disadvantage against a team like the Mets that is willing to match or exceed his ask.

The Blue Jays' Position

Toronto has been active at every stage of this free-agent market — pursuing Skubal alongside Seiya Suzuki and Jazz Chisholm simultaneously. The Blue Jays' presence keeps the bidding competitive and prevents the Mets from lowballing on the assumption that only one team is in. For now, the Blue Jays are in the final group. Their ceiling on a Skubal offer remains unclear.

The Deadline That Changes Everything

MLB's collective bargaining agreement expires December 1 at 11:59 PM ET. A lockout beginning December 2 would freeze all free-agent transactions — meaning Skubal either signs before midnight December 1 or waits until a new CBA is ratified, which could be months away. That deadline transforms this from a standard negotiation into a sprint with a hard wall.

Skubal's leverage is considerable: his value does not diminish during a lockout, and teams will want him just as badly in March as they do today. But waiting also carries risk — spring training is disrupted, the 2027 season schedule is uncertain, and the financial complexity of a record-setting deal becomes harder to structure under a new and potentially different CBA framework.

What's Next

Expect a resolution before December 1. The combination of lockout pressure, the Mets' aggressive posture, and Boras's historical preference for getting deals done before free-agent value is impacted by a work stoppage all point toward a signing in the next week to ten days. When Skubal signs, it will be the defining moment of the 2026-27 Hot Stove season — a record-breaking deal that resets the market for starting pitchers for years to come. The industry says Mets. The contract math says historic. Watch for the announcement any day now.
