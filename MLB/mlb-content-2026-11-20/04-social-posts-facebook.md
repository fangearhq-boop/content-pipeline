### STORY 2: CBA Countdown

**Long-Form Post (8:00 AM ET)**
You have 11 days to watch major league free agency happen in real time ⏰

MLB's collective bargaining agreement expires December 1 at 11:59 PM Eastern. When it does, Commissioner Rob Manfred is expected to lock the players out within minutes — and every pending transaction in baseball freezes.

Here's what's at stake: Tarik Skubal (ace LHP, $325M–$429M projection), Seiya Suzuki (Japanese star, $110M projection), Teruaki Sato (NPB Triple Crown, $80M-$120M), Jazz Chisholm (elite infielder, $46M projection), Randy Arozarena (outfielder, $100M+ ask), and dozens of other free agents who have not yet signed.

What is the fight about? One thing: a salary cap. Owners want a cap and floor — a structure similar to the NBA and NFL. Commissioner Manfred has said MLB will not ratify a new CBA without both. The MLBPA has called a salary cap an absolute non-starter, arguing it would cost players roughly $400 million in earning potential annually.

The last MLB work stoppage (December 2021 to March 2022) lasted 99 days. The 2022 season lost its first two weeks. If this lockout follows the same pattern, spring training doesn't start until mid-March and the regular season begins late.

The informal deadline for 2027 games to be played is early March. That's the deadline no one wants to hit.

Watch the next 11 days very carefully. This is the most consequential window in baseball before the actual season.

### STORY 4: Teruaki Sato's Lockout Loophole

**Long-Form Post (3:00 PM ET)**
Here's the most important thing most baseball fans don't know about the MLB offseason 🏮

Teruaki Sato can be signed during the MLB lockout.

Here's why: Sato's availability in the United States is governed by the NPB posting agreement — a separate international framework that exists entirely outside the MLB-MLBPA collective bargaining agreement. When the CBA expires December 1 and the lockout begins December 2, Sato's 45-day posting window (which opened November 1 and closes December 16) stays active. Teams can still negotiate with Sato and his representatives and sign him to a major league contract — even with the lockout in effect.

This is not a loophole in the pejorative sense — it's how the system is designed. Japanese posted players have always sat in a different legal category than domestic free agents. But the practical effect is significant: Sato becomes uniquely valuable for any team that misses out on Tarik Skubal.

Sato, 25, won the Japanese Central League Triple Crown in 2026, posting a .308 average with 39 home runs. Projections range from $80 million (Heyman's floor) to more than $120 million (Bowden). Teams linked: Mets, Yankees, Dodgers, Phillies.

The deadline is December 16. If Sato is unsigned by then, he returns to the Hanshin Tigers and cannot be posted again until next offseason. That's a one-year delay for every interested team.

The countdown is real. Watch for Sato news to accelerate over the next three weeks.

### STORY 5: Arozarena's Market Problem

**Long-Form Post (6:00 PM ET)**
Randy Arozarena is a genuinely excellent baseball player who may have overestimated his market 🤔

The two-way outfielder declined the qualifying offer earlier this month and hit free agency asking for well over $100 million, per Jeff Passan. The problem: suitors are balking. The Mariners — the team many expected to retain him after his 23-home-run, 41-steal season in Seattle — are reportedly unwilling to offer a long-term deal.

The numbers are interesting. Arozarena is 29 years old, a switch-hitter, plays solid left field, and has historically been one of the more exciting players in the sport. His 2026 slash line (.246/.328/.407) was down from his peak, and that may be contributing to market hesitancy. Add the qualifying offer compensation attached to his signing (the team that signs him loses a draft pick), and you have a player whose actual market value may be significantly below his ask.

The deadline pressure is real: the lockout on December 2 would freeze all transactions, meaning Arozarena has approximately 11 days to get a deal done or wait until spring to restart negotiations.

Teams like the Cubs (need an outfielder), Blue Jays (possibly, if they miss on Suzuki), and Orioles have been mentioned in connection with outfield needs. But none have made a public push for Arozarena at his asking price.

Something has to give — either Arozarena's ask comes down, or a team comes up to meet it. The clock is ticking.

**Caption Post**
