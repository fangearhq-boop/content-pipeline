Pete Crow-Armstrong's MVP Case: 32 Games Left and a Season Worth Remembering

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | August 24, 2026

There was a cold stretch. For roughly ten days, Pete Crow-Armstrong went 4-for-36, and the narrative around the NL MVP race shifted. Shohei Ohtani's name came up more. The betting markets moved. That is what happens in a long season — the narrative follows the most recent performance, even when the most recent performance is a fraction of the whole.

With 32 games left, the whole tells a story worth examining more carefully than the slump.

The Season Line

Pete Crow-Armstrong enters the final 32 games of 2026 batting .281 with 30 home runs, 78 RBIs, 89 runs scored, and 31 stolen bases. His fWAR — Wins Above Replacement, the comprehensive value metric — sits at 7.7, the highest among NL position players. His Outs Above Average in center field is 22, placing him among the top defensive outfielders in the sport.

The 30-30 combination alone is historic in Chicago's context. No Cubs player has ever posted back-to-back 30-30 seasons. Crow-Armstrong has done it two years running. The individual achievement carries weight beyond a single season stat line.

The Case Against the Case

Shohei Ohtani's 2026 season generates value in a direction the traditional MVP framework struggles to account for. As a pitcher and designated hitter simultaneously, Ohtani has accumulated 6.4 fWAR while contributing meaningful production from two roster spots simultaneously. His offensive numbers are elite. His presence in any lineup is uniquely disruptive.

The counterargument is positional context. Crow-Armstrong plays the most demanding defensive position on the field, produces at an elite level offensively and defensively, and has done it over a full 162-game schedule. The DH does not field. That defensive production — 22 OAA, the kind of catch that saves games without appearing on a box score — is a real argument when the numbers are close.

The Cubs are 74-56 and hold the NL Wild Card No. 1 seed. Team success has historically been a tiebreaker in tight MVP races. Chicago's playoff position is another argument in PCA's column.

What the Final 32 Games Need to Show

If Crow-Armstrong finishes the season at his current production rate, he finishes with approximately 34-35 home runs, 85 RBIs, and a batting average that could settle anywhere between .280 and .295 depending on the final stretch. That is a genuinely excellent season by any standard. Whether it wins the MVP depends on what Ohtani does in Los Angeles over the same stretch.

The cold stretch of the last week or two needs to end. Not because 4-for-36 changes the season's meaning — it doesn't — but because the voters who see the second half of September will weigh recent performance alongside cumulative production. Finishing well matters. The Cubs need him to finish well. So does his MVP case.

What's Next

Thirty-two games. Pete Crow-Armstrong has built one of the most complete center field seasons in Cubs history. The NL MVP award has not been announced. What happens between now and the final day of the regular season will determine whether the accumulated evidence — the WAR, the stolen bases, the home runs, the defense — is enough to hold off a player doing something the award framework was not designed to evaluate. The case is still his to make.
