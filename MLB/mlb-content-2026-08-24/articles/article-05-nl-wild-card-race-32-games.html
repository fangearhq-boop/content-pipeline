NL Wild Card Update: Cubs Lead, But the Race for the Final Spot Is Tightening

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | August 24, 2026

With approximately 32 games remaining in the 2026 regular season, the National League Wild Card race has a clear leader, a strong second, a competitive third, and one team scrambling to hold on. The picture has not changed dramatically in the last two weeks — but the margin between the teams in third and fourth is narrow enough that it will require attention through the end of September.

The Standings

The Chicago Cubs sit at 74-56 in the No. 1 Wild Card position, two games ahead of the Philadelphia Phillies at 72-58. The San Diego Padres hold the third and final Wild Card spot at 70-60. The Arizona Diamondbacks are approximately one game behind San Diego, sitting around 69-61 and pressing for the position that would give them a playoff berth.

The Cubs' two-game cushion over the Phillies is meaningful but not commanding. Philadelphia has the roster depth to make up two games in 32 remaining, and the Cubs have been inconsistent through portions of August. The Wild Card No. 1 position is Chicago's to hold, not to take for granted.

Why Seeding Matters for the Cubs

The difference between the No. 1 and No. 2 Wild Card seeds is not merely symbolic. The No. 1 seed avoids the Milwaukee Brewers — 81-49, the best record in baseball — in the first round of the playoffs. Milwaukee has Jacob Misiorowski (12-5, 1.75 ERA) at the front of its rotation. Drawing the Brewers in a Wild Card series is, for most teams, a near-certain exit.

If the Cubs, Phillies, and Padres all hold their current spots, and Milwaukee advances as the NL Central champion (which is nearly certain at 81-49), the No. 1 Wild Card team would face the No. 3 seed Padres in the first round. The No. 2 team would face the Brewers. For a Cubs team with legitimate October ambitions, maintaining the top Wild Card position is not about pride. It is about playoff survival calculus.

The Live Competition

The actual race — the one where games matter most — is between San Diego and Arizona for the third Wild Card spot. The Diamondbacks are one game behind with 32 to play, which is a deficit they can erase on any given weekend series. Both franchises have enough talent to finish above .500 through September. The question is which team flinches first under the pressure of a playoff-chasing schedule.

The Padres' advantage is that they are already in. They can play to hold position rather than close ground. The Diamondbacks must gain. That asymmetry typically favors the team with the cushion, but the margin is small enough that a three-game series loss could flip the dynamic overnight.

What's Next

Thirty-two games. The Cubs want to stay No. 1 and avoid a first-round meeting with Milwaukee. The Phillies want to climb one position and do the same. The Padres want to hold their spot. The Diamondbacks want to take it. By the final weekend of the regular season, this race will likely be resolved — but September in the NL Wild Card picture rarely resolves itself quietly.
