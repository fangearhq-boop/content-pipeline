Harris Homers, Mahle Delivers as Braves Beat Brewers 4-2 in Little League Classic

By Ryan Calloway | Ballpark Banter | August 24, 2026

Sunday night in Williamsport, Pennsylvania, the Atlanta Braves beat the Milwaukee Brewers 4-2 in the 2026 MLB Little League Classic at Journey Bank Ballpark at Historic Bowman Field. Both franchises made their first-ever appearance in the Williamsport showcase. Tyler Mahle delivered five strong innings on the mound. Michael Harris II provided the decisive blow in the ninth. Raisel Iglesias closed it out for save No. 27. The Braves were not dominant — they have been far from it lately, winning just their second game in the last seven — but on a Sunday night in Little League country, they got the job done.

Mahle's Five-Inning Effort Sets the Stage

Tyler Mahle earned the win in a performance that was more efficient than overwhelming. Working five innings, he allowed one run on six hits while striking out four and issuing two walks. The right-hander, who came to Atlanta via the trade market earlier in the season, kept Milwaukee's lineup off-balance through the middle innings and gave the Braves' bullpen enough rest to handle the back half of the game. His record improved to 5-10 — a win-loss number that understates the quality of his outings this summer.

One notable moment: Garrett Mitchell, the Brewers center fielder, took a 94-mile-per-hour Mahle fastball to the arm while attempting to bunt in the fourth inning and left the game with a bruised forearm. The Brewers (81-49), who own the best record in baseball and are playing for seeding down the stretch, can ill afford to lose a starter in a showcase setting like Williamsport.

Harris Puts It Away in the Ninth

With the game at 3-2 in the ninth inning, Michael Harris II stepped to the plate against Aaron Ashby. Ashby fell behind in the count and came back with a slider — and Harris drove it over the right field fence for his 21st home run of the season. The solo shot pushed the lead to two, putting the game effectively out of reach for a Brewers lineup that had managed just two runs all night.

Raisel Iglesias took care of the rest. The Braves closer worked around the eighth inning with a runner on third base — getting pinch-hitter Gary Sanchez to pop out on a crucial pitch — then retired the side in the ninth to earn his 27th save. Iglesias has been one of the more consistent closers in the NL, and Sunday's performance was a representative example of why Atlanta has trusted him in high-leverage situations.

The Williamsport Context

The MLB Little League Classic has become one of the summer's most anticipated showcase events — a game played at the birthplace of Little League Baseball, surrounded by the ongoing Little League World Series. Both the Braves and Brewers made their Williamsport debuts Sunday, an occasion that carries its own significance independent of the standings.

Jacob Misiorowski, Milwaukee's Cy Young frontrunner at 12-5 with a 1.75 ERA, was not in the starting rotation for Sunday's game. He served as a featured guest in the broadcast booth, watching his team fall while the young players competing in the Little League World Series filled the stands behind home plate. His next start — back at a major-league park, with full rest — will carry the full weight of what he's built this season.

What's Next

The Braves improve to their best in recent memory — just their second win in the last seven games, but still the NL East leaders. The Brewers at 81-49 absorb a minor blip in the context of a season that remains historically excellent. Misiorowski returns to the rotation next week. And the Little League Classic, as always, provides a night when the best players in the world get to play in the ballpark where it all started.
