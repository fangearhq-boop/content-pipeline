While His Team Played in Williamsport, Misiorowski Watched From the Booth — His Numbers Remind You Why He's the Cy Young Favorite

By Ryan Calloway | Ballpark Banter | August 24, 2026

Jacob Misiorowski was not in the bullpen Sunday night. He was in the broadcast booth at Historic Bowman Field in Williamsport, Pennsylvania, serving as a featured guest while his Milwaukee Brewers lost 4-2 to the Atlanta Braves in the MLB Little League Classic. He watched his team fall. He made television. He did not throw a pitch.

None of that changes what he has built this season, or what he will carry back to a major league mound when his turn comes around again.

The Season Line

Misiorowski is 12-5 with a 1.75 ERA. His strikeout total has surpassed 210. His WHIP is 0.73 — a number that belongs in a historical comparison, not a current-season context. The betting markets have set his NL Cy Young odds at -550, which is not the language of a frontrunner. It is the language of a foregone conclusion with 32 games left to run.

To understand the ERA and WHIP in context: the league average ERA for starting pitchers in the American and National Leagues in 2026 is tracking around 4.20. Misiorowski's 1.75 is not a slight improvement on the average. It is a historical outlier relative to his era, and it has been sustained over more than 150 innings.

The Numbers Behind the Numbers

The velocity is real and quantifiable. In a single start earlier in August, Misiorowski threw 33 fastballs at 103 miles per hour or above — more than twice the previous single-game record since pitch tracking began. That start did not represent him throwing harder than usual. It represented the documentation catching up with what scouts had already been reporting since April.

The strikeout dominance extends beyond pure totals. Misiorowski has recorded 10 or more strikeouts in a game nine times in 2026, tying a Brewers single-season franchise record set by Corbin Burnes in 2022. His rate statistics — strikeouts per nine innings, strikeouts per plate appearance, swinging-strike rate — rank among the best in the sport.

The Team Around Him

The Milwaukee Brewers are 81-49 with the best record in Major League Baseball. That record does not belong entirely to Misiorowski — a rotation, a bullpen, and an offense build a 130-game body of work, not one starter. But his presence in the rotation every fifth day has been the stabilizing force of a championship-caliber team. On the days he takes the ball, the Brewers win at an exceptional rate. On the days he doesn't — like Sunday night in Williamsport — the outcomes are more variable.

The Cy Young Award has historically rewarded winning pitchers with elite numbers. Misiorowski has both. The only reasonable debate is whether any other pitcher is close enough to make this a genuine race. The -550 odds suggest the market does not believe they are.

What's Next

Williamsport was Sunday. His next start is the one that matters — back in a major league park, back on a mound, back in the middle of a pennant race. Jacob Misiorowski has produced one of the most dominant pitching seasons in recent memory. Thirty-two games remain. The Cy Young case is already made. What happens in September is about whether the historical footnotes grow larger or simply hold where they are.
