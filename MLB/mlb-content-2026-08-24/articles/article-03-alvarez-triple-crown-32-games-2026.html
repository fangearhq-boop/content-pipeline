Yordan Alvarez Has 32 Games to Make History the Astros and Baseball Haven't Seen Since 2012

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | August 24, 2026

Miguel Cabrera did it in 2012. No position player in Major League Baseball has done it since. Fourteen years later, with 32 games remaining in the 2026 regular season, Yordan Alvarez of the Houston Astros leads the American League in batting average, home runs, and RBIs simultaneously.

The Triple Crown is not a statistics category. It is an achievement so rare that an entire generation of baseball fans grew up never seeing it happen. Alvarez is 32 games away from changing that.

The Numbers

Alvarez enters the final stretch of 2026 batting .329 with 36 home runs and 88 RBIs — tops in the American League in all three categories. His position at the summit of all three simultaneously is what separates a very good season from a genuinely historic one.

The difficulty of the Triple Crown is not about individual excellence. It is about sustaining three different types of excellence at the same time, in a 162-game environment where every slump costs ground in one category even as another climbs. A power hitter who goes cold temporarily loses ground in batting average. An average hitter who walks frequently loses ground in RBIs. Alvarez, somehow, leads in all three.

What It Would Mean

Cabrera's 2012 Triple Crown — .330 average, 44 home runs, 139 RBIs with the Detroit Tigers — was one of the most celebrated individual performances of the last two decades. It effectively settled his MVP case before the vote was cast. No one has come close to matching the achievement in the 14 seasons since.

The modern game's structural tendencies work against it. Launch-angle optimization pulls hitters away from contact work, depressing batting averages across the board. The prevalence of high-strikeout pitching reduces the gap between contact and power hitters, making it harder to lead in both average and home runs. Alvarez, a left-handed hitter with rare combination of raw power and barrel control, is the kind of player the Triple Crown was designed for — before baseball's analytics revolution tried to make the type obsolete.

The Competition

Kyle Schwarber of the Philadelphia Phillies leads the NL with 37 home runs — one ahead of Alvarez — but operates in the other league, which is not a factor in the AL race. Within the American League, no hitter is within meaningful striking distance of Alvarez in the home run category. The batting average competition is thin; very few of the game's power producers are simultaneously threatening batting titles. The RBI lead is the most vulnerable — a cold stretch in August could allow a run-producing rival to close ground — but Alvarez has not shown a prolonged cold stretch since the early weeks of the season.

The Astros are competing for playoff position in the AL West, which means Alvarez is earning these numbers in games that count. This is not a guaranteed playoff berth driving lineup protection. These numbers are being produced under real pressure.

What's Next

Thirty-two games is a long time in baseball. Injuries happen. Slumps happen. The lead in each category narrows in ways that are difficult to predict. What Yordan Alvarez has built through the first 130 games of the 2026 season represents one of the most complete offensive performances in recent American League history. Whether it finishes as a Triple Crown depends on those final 32 games — and on whether any rival can close the gap in any of the three categories. The last man to do this was Miguel Cabrera. The next may be Yordan Alvarez.
