### STORY 1: Little League Classic — Braves 4, Brewers 2

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 8:00 AM ET

This one was worth staying up for.

Sunday night at Journey Bank Ballpark at Historic Bowman Field in Williamsport, Pennsylvania, the Atlanta Braves beat the Milwaukee Brewers 4-2 in the 2026 MLB Little League Classic. First appearance in Williamsport for both franchises. One of those games that reminds you why baseball at this level still matters.

Tyler Mahle was the story for most of the night. The Braves right-hander threw five innings of one-run ball — allowing just six hits while striking out four — and earned his fifth win of the season. The Brewers managed to put runners on base but couldn't do enough against Mahle and the Atlanta bullpen.

The defining moment came in the top of the ninth. Michael Harris II stepped in against Milwaukee reliever Aaron Ashby with the score 3-2. Ashby went to 0-2, then tried a slider. Harris read it perfectly and hit it over the right field fence in right for a solo home run — his 21st of the season — giving Atlanta the two-run cushion that Raisel Iglesias would use to close things out.

Iglesias struck out a pinch-hitter and navigated the eighth inning with a runner on third, then worked a clean ninth to earn his 27th save of the season.

One footnote: Garrett Mitchell was hit by a Mahle fastball — 94 miles per hour — while attempting to bunt in the fourth inning and left the game with a bruised forearm. The Brewers (81-49) could least afford to lose a center fielder right now.

For Atlanta, it was just their second win in their last seven games. They're still the NL East leaders, but the cold stretch heading into September is worth monitoring.

Jacob Misiorowski, the Brewers' ace, was not in the rotation for Sunday's game — he watched from the broadcast booth as a featured guest. His next start will be the more representative version of what Milwaukee has going.

### STORY 2: Pete Crow-Armstrong NL MVP Race

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 10:30 AM ET

Thirty-two games left. Here's where Pete Crow-Armstrong's NL MVP case actually stands.

Yes, there was a cold stretch — the Cubs' center fielder went 4-for-36 over roughly 10 days, and the narrative briefly shifted toward Shohei Ohtani. That slump was real. But the season-long line is what gets weighed in November when MVP ballots are submitted.

And that line looks like this: .281 batting average, 30 home runs, 78 RBIs, 89 runs scored, 31 stolen bases, 7.7 fWAR. Plus 22 Outs Above Average in center field — elite defensive production that doesn't show up in the raw offensive numbers.

No player in Cubs history has ever posted back-to-back 30-30 seasons. PCA is doing it.

He leads NL position players in WAR. He's a legitimate contender for the NL batting title if the average stabilizes. His power, speed, and defense combination is the kind of five-tool production that voters reward.

Ohtani's case is built on a different model — a pitcher-DH who accumulates value in two directions. PCA's case is built on being the most complete position player in the NL. Both are legitimate MVP frameworks.

Thirty-two games. The Cubs are 74-56 and holding the NL Wild Card No. 1 spot. For PCA to win the MVP, the end of the season needs to look like his best work — not the cold patch that preceded it. Given the talent involved, that is far from a longshot.

### STORY 3: Yordan Alvarez Triple Crown

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 3:00 PM ET

Miguel Cabrera did it in 2012. No one in Major League Baseball has done it since.

Yordan Alvarez is 32 games away from potentially becoming the first player in 14 years to win the Triple Crown — leading his league in batting average, home runs, and RBIs in the same season.

As of Sunday's results, Alvarez leads the American League in all three categories. His batting average sits around .329. His home run total is 36. His RBI total is approximately 88. He is doing this while the Houston Astros are competing for playoff position — these numbers are not accumulated in garbage time.

The historical comparison: Cabrera hit .330 with 44 home runs and 139 RBIs in 2012. The numbers are not directly comparable — eras change, competition shifts — but the rarity is the same. Winning a batting title while leading your league in power is exceedingly difficult in an era built around strikeouts and launch angles.

Kyle Schwarber, who leads the NL with 37 home runs, is the only hitter in either league within meaningful distance of Alvarez in the home run race. In batting average, the competition is thinner — very few of the game's power hitters are simultaneously contending for batting titles.

Thirty-two games. One Triple Crown. One name. Watch it.

The betting markets have his Cy Young odds at -550. That means the market has him as a heavy favorite — not a favorite, a heavy favorite — to win the award. No other pitcher is close.

The Brewers are 81-49. They have the best record in baseball. That record belongs in large part to Misiorowski showing up every fifth day and doing something the sport hasn't seen in years.

Williamsport was Sunday's game. His next start is the one that matters.

### STORY 5: NL Wild Card Race

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 10:00 PM ET

With approximately 32 games left in the 2026 regular season, here's the NL Wild Card picture.

The Chicago Cubs are in the top spot at 74-56. They hold the No. 1 seed in the Wild Card race, which matters beyond pride: the No. 1 Wild Card team would avoid the Milwaukee Brewers (81-49, best record in baseball) in the first round of the playoffs.

The Philadelphia Phillies are second at 72-58, two games back of the Cubs. The San Diego Padres hold the third and final Wild Card spot at 70-60. The Arizona Diamondbacks are one game behind San Diego at approximately 69-61.

The race for the Cubs is about maintaining their seeding. Chicago has been a bit inconsistent in recent weeks but remains the clear NL Wild Card favorite for the top seed. Losing that top spot to the Phillies would not be a disaster — but it would change the playoff calculus in October.

The live competition is between San Diego and Arizona for the final Wild Card position. Both teams have legitimate cases over the next 32 games, and neither has enough margin to relax.

One interesting postseason math problem: if the Cubs, Phillies, and Padres all hold their spots, and Milwaukee advances through the NL Central (which it will almost certainly do at 81-49), the Cubs as No. 1 would face the third-seed Padres in the first round — while the Phillies take on the Brewers. For a Cubs team with playoff ambitions, keeping that No. 1 seed isn't about seeding. It's about not facing Misiorowski in round one.
