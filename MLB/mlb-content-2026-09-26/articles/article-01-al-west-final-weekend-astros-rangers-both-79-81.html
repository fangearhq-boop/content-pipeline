AL West Final Weekend: Astros and Rangers Both 79-81 After Friday Collapses — History in the Balance

By Marcus Cole | September 26, 2026

Both teams lost Friday. Both teams are 79-81. Two games remain.

The 2026 American League West race entered its final weekend in the most chaotic way possible. Texas collapsed in Minnesota — Jacob deGrom allowed 8 earned runs in the worst start of his year, and the Rangers fell 10-2 to the Twins. Houston wasn't better: the Astros dropped a 6-5 decision at Oakland. When Friday ended, neither team had any separation. The AL West title, still undecided, goes to the final two days of the regular season.

DeGrom's Disaster and the Stakes That Follow

Jacob deGrom's start in Minnesota was the kind of outing that can define a season's ending. He matched a career high by allowing 8 earned runs — a brutal performance at precisely the wrong moment. The Twins' Luke Keaschall had four hits including a double and a triple, driving in three runs. Texas, which needed a win to put pressure on Houston, left Minnesota with their backs further against the wall.

The Astros' loss at Oakland wasn't much better. A 6-5 defeat to a team with no playoff stakes is the kind of game that leaves a franchise questioning its readiness for October. But the math kept both teams identical: 79-81.

Why Houston Has the Edge

Even in a dead heat, the Astros hold a significant advantage over the Rangers: the tiebreaker. Houston won the regular season series against Texas, which means that if both teams finish with the same record, the AL West title goes to Houston. That shifts the burden entirely to Texas.

For the Rangers to win the division, they need to win their remaining games AND need the Astros to lose. A Rangers win combined with an Astros win still leaves Houston holding the tiebreaker. Texas doesn't just need to play well — they need help.

Saturday's schedule: Rangers at Twins, 3:10 PM ET. Astros at Oakland, 8:40 PM ET. If Houston wins Saturday, the AL West race is over.

A Historic Outcome Either Way

Whoever wins the AL West in 2026 will make history — and not in the way teams aspire to. The worst division winner in modern MLB history was the 2005 San Diego Padres, who won the NL West at 82-80. No team has ever won a division with a losing record. The worst possible outcome for the 2026 AL West winner is 81-81 — but given that both teams have already lost 81 games, the champion will almost certainly win fewer than the Padres did.

That's the peculiar geography of a division race where neither team could pull away all season. The AL West winner in 2026 will be the worst division champion in MLB history by record — and will still earn a home playoff series in the first round.

What's Next

Saturday at 3:10 PM ET, the Rangers play in Minnesota. At 8:40 PM ET, the Astros take the field at Oakland. One or both games may clinch the division Saturday. If not, Sunday's finale determines the winner. Whatever happens, this AL West race ends with a team making history — one of the worst division champions the sport has ever seen, heading into the playoffs anyway. Watch Saturday closely. It might be over by midnight.
