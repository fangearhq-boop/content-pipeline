AL Central Finale: Guardians Lead by One, White Sox Hold Tiebreaker — Two Games to Settle It

By Ryan Calloway | September 26, 2026

The American League Central title is going down to the final weekend — and the team that won Thursday night's clincher carries one of the most remarkable turnaround stories in baseball history into the division finale.

The Guardians lead the White Sox by one game heading into Saturday's action, but the race is more complicated than a one-game margin suggests. Chicago holds the tiebreaker. If both teams finish with the same record, the White Sox win the division — which means Cleveland needs to finish at least one game better, not merely match them. The Guardians have the lead; they also have the pressure.

The White Sox: Baseball's Most Improbable Playoff Team

Whatever happens this weekend, the Chicago White Sox have already done something no team in Major League Baseball history has ever done. They lost 121 games in 2024. One hundred and twenty-one losses — one of the worst single-season records in the modern era. The 2025 season wasn't much better.

Then came 2026. The White Sox rebuilt, retooled, and refused to accept that their 100-loss seasons defined their ceiling. When they clinched Thursday, they became the first team in MLB history to make the postseason after back-to-back seasons with at least 100 losses. The history books updated themselves in real time.

That context hangs over every remaining game for Chicago. Win the division, and the turnaround story gets an exclamation point. Finish as a wild card team, and the story is still extraordinary — but the Guardians will have earned the division crown.

The Guardians' Situation

Cleveland entered the weekend with a one-game advantage and the straightforward goal of defending it. But the tiebreaker complication changes the calculus. The Guardians can't afford to think of this as a simple "we lead by one" scenario — they have to think of it as "we need to stay ahead of a team that beats us in a tiebreaker."

The Guardians have the lead and the advantage of knowing exactly what they need to do: win games. Two wins, regardless of what Chicago does, clinches the AL Central for Cleveland. They control their own fate — which is more than can be said for most teams in the final weekend of the season.

Postseason Stakes Beyond the Division

Both teams have already secured postseason spots. What's at stake this weekend is seeding. The AL Central division winner gets the No. 2 seed in the American League, while the wild card team gets the No. 5 seed. That difference in seeding matters: the No. 2 seed hosts the Wild Card Series (three-game format) with home-field advantage, while the No. 5 seed plays on the road.

For a team with the White Sox's momentum and narrative, hosting a playoff series at Guaranteed Rate Field would be the fullest expression of the rebuild's success. For the Guardians, maintaining their lead keeps them as hosts too. Either way, Cleveland's AL Central fans get a home playoff series — the question is which team holds the division flag.

What's Next

Saturday and Sunday settle the AL Central. The Guardians need one game on Chicago, or simply two wins. The White Sox need to match or pass Cleveland in the standings while holding their tiebreaker edge. Two cities, two franchises, two paths to the same postseason stage. The regular season ends Sunday. The division race ends there too. Watch both teams' results side-by-side — this one isn't over until the last out Sunday.
