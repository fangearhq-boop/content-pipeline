Yankees Are 'Planning' Without Aaron Judge for Wild Card Series — But Judge Isn't Done Fighting

By Marcus Cole | September 26, 2026

The Yankees and their captain are delivering two different messages about October.

Manager Aaron Boone was direct on Friday when asked about Aaron Judge's availability for the American League Wild Card Series against the Boston Red Sox: "We're planning on him not being a part of that." That's as close to a definitive statement as a manager makes about an injured superstar. The planning is done without him. The lineup construction is built without him.

Judge's response was equally direct, in the opposite direction: "I'm not giving up."

The Injury and What It Means

Judge has a moderate calf strain — specifically in the soleus muscle of his right calf. He explained to ESPN's Jorge Castillo why this particular injury is especially problematic for a baseball player: "The soleus is a little more crucial with running and hitting." The soleus is deeper than the typical calf muscles most people know — it's the muscle involved in the push-off motion that's fundamental to both running and rotational hitting mechanics.

The Yankees placed Judge on the 10-day IL with the injury retroactive to September 17. On Monday, they gave him a platelet-poor plasma (PPP) injection, a treatment designed to accelerate healing by injecting plasma depleted of platelets into the injured area. It's a more targeted approach than standard platelet-rich plasma therapy, used in specific soft tissue injuries.

The 10-day IL window expires by September 27 — the final day of the regular season. The Wild Card Series begins Tuesday, September 29. There are exactly two days between the earliest he could be activated and the first pitch of the playoffs.

The Yankees Without Judge

New York has clinched a playoff berth and will host the Wild Card Series at Yankee Stadium. The opponent is Boston, a rivalry that carries its own weight regardless of who is on the field. But a Judge-less Yankees lineup against the Red Sox is a meaningfully different proposition than one with him hitting cleanup.

Boone's "planning without him" statement is partly a practical acknowledgment that the roster depth has to be built around what's available — and partly a way of managing expectations for fans and the team. It doesn't mean Judge is ruled out. "I'm not ruling it out either" follows the planning statement for a reason.

What's Next

The regular season ends Sunday. Judge's status will become clearer as the weekend progresses — whether he's taking batting practice, whether he's moving freely in the outfield, whether the Yankees feel confident enough in his recovery to activate him. Watch for an official update before Sunday's final game. The Wild Card Series against Boston starts Tuesday. Two days between the last out of the regular season and the first pitch of the playoffs — that's the window Judge needs to work with. He says he hasn't given up. The Yankees say they're planning otherwise. Someone will be right.
