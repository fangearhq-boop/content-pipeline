Yordan Alvarez's Triple Crown Chase Comes Down to the Final Weekend — and Junior Caminero Has the Lead in HRs

By Jake Torres | September 26, 2026

The last time anyone won the AL Triple Crown, Beyoncé was releasing her fourth studio album and the Detroit Tigers were winning the American League pennant. That was 2012. Miguel Cabrera was the man. Fourteen years later, Yordan Alvarez is chasing the same piece of history — and the final weekend of the regular season will decide whether he gets there.

Alvarez enters Saturday's games with a .311 batting average, 40 home runs, and 102 RBI. Those are extraordinary numbers. In most AL seasons, they'd make him the runaway MVP selection and clear leader in all three categories. But 2026 isn't a typical season, and Junior Caminero of the Tampa Bay Rays is standing between Alvarez and history.

The Caminero Problem

Junior Caminero leads the American League with 41 home runs — one more than Alvarez. The 23-year-old third baseman has been one of the most electrifying hitters in the sport all season. He's the second-youngest player in MLB history to have two 40+ home run seasons, behind only Hall of Famer Eddie Matthews, who did it in 1953 and 1954. Caminero is also third in the majors in home runs, behind Kyle Schwarber (Phillies, 45) and Pete Crow-Armstrong (Cubs, 42).

For Alvarez to win the Triple Crown, he'd need to finish at least one home run ahead of Caminero, while maintaining his leads in batting average and RBI. In two games. With the Rays also playing meaningful baseball at the end of the season.

It's possible. The math exists. But it requires Alvarez to hit at least one home run and Caminero to hit none — or Alvarez to hit two while Caminero hits one. Those are not guaranteed outcomes.

What History Would Mean

The American League Triple Crown has been claimed 16 times in history. Since Cabrera in 2012, fourteen years have passed without a single AL player leading the league in batting average, home runs, and RBI in the same season. The list of players who've come close without finishing is long — Jose Abreu, Vladimir Guerrero Jr., Trout in his prime — but none have pulled it off.

No Houston Astros player has ever won the Triple Crown in the franchise's history. Reaching back to the Colt .45s era, through the great seasons of Jeff Bagwell and Craig Biggio, through the power years of Lance Berkman and Carlos Lee, and into the modern dynasty — it has never happened. Alvarez winning would put his name in the same breath as Cabrera, Yaz, Frank Robinson, and the other immortals who own a Triple Crown.

What's Next

Two games remain in the 2026 regular season. Alvarez needs homers. Caminero needs to cool off. And the batting average and RBI races need to hold. For Alvarez, the next two days are a chance to do something extraordinary — something that hadn't happened in 14 years before he arrived at the plate this weekend. Watch his at-bats closely. Every swing matters.
