One NL Playoff Spot Remains — Phillies Hold Big Lead but D-backs Own the Tiebreaker

By Jake Torres | September 26, 2026

Eleven of baseball's twelve playoff spots are decided. One remains.

In the National League, the last available postseason berth comes down to the Philadelphia Phillies and the Arizona Diamondbacks — two franchises that have both tasted playoff baseball in recent years, fighting for what would be the sixth and final NL wild card spot. The Phillies are the heavy favorites. The D-backs hold the tiebreaker. The regular season ends Sunday.

The Phillies' Position

Philadelphia enters the final weekend at 86-70, with a magic number of 2 to clinch a playoff berth. That means the Phillies need any combination of wins by Philadelphia and losses by Arizona totaling two. They lead by 5 games with 6 remaining. The math is overwhelmingly in Philadelphia's favor.

The Phillies don't need to watch the scoreboard in Arizona. They need to take care of their own business in their final home series, and almost certainly, that's enough. A team that's 86-70 going into the final weekend does not typically blow a five-game lead.

The Diamondbacks' Tiebreaker and the Math That Matters

Arizona's situation requires almost everything to go right. The Diamondbacks (81-74) hold the tiebreaker over Philadelphia — a detail that becomes relevant only if both teams finish with identical records. And here's the catch: because Arizona holds that tiebreaker, the Phillies cannot simply match them. Philadelphia needs to finish at least one game better than Arizona, not tied.

In practice, that's an academic distinction given the five-game gap. But it's worth understanding the mechanics: the same intradivisional record tiebreaker that would save the D-backs in an identical-record scenario is also the reason the Phillies need to be a game ahead, not tied.

For Arizona, the math is straightforward: they need to win all of their remaining games and need the Phillies to lose four or five of their final six. It's possible in the abstract — the kind of collapse that happens in baseball maybe twice a decade. But possible is a long way from likely.

What's at Stake Beyond the Spot

The team that finishes in the sixth NL wild card spot will play the No. 3 NL seed in the Wild Card Series. The current No. 3 NL seed is the Cubs, who clinched earlier in the week at 88-71. That's the opponent waiting for whoever squeezes through — a Cubs team that spent the week making history of its own.

If the Phillies get there, they bring a lineup with legitimate power and a pitching staff capable of winning any single game. If the D-backs somehow make it, it would be one of the more dramatic late-season collapses-and-comebacks in recent memory. Either way, the sixth spot goes to whoever plays better baseball over six games.

What's Next

The Phillies' magic number is 2. The regular season ends Sunday. Before that last out is recorded, Philadelphia should almost certainly have clinched the final playoff spot in the National League. Arizona is playing for pride and the longest of long shots. Watch the standings this weekend — but don't be surprised if the Phillies close it out before Sunday's finale even begins. The last wild card spot gets filled this weekend.
