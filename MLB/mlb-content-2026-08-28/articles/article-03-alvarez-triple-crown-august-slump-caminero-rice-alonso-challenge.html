Yordan Alvarez Has Hit One Home Run in August. The Triple Crown Race Just Got a Lot More Interesting.

By Ryan Calloway | Ballpark Banter | August 28, 2026

Entering August, Yordan Alvarez looked like a man on a historic mission. The Houston Astros slugger was leading the American League in home runs, RBI, and batting average — a combination that hadn't produced a Triple Crown winner since Miguel Cabrera did it in 2012. For much of the summer, it felt inevitable.

One home run. That's how many Alvarez has hit all month.

The dominance that made the Triple Crown feel like a formality in July has given way to a genuine race. Alvarez still leads all three categories — .320 batting average, 36 home runs, and 90-91 RBI as of the most recent confirmed figures. But the margins have evaporated.

The Field Has Closed

Junior Caminero of the Tampa Bay Rays and Ben Rice of the New York Yankees both sit at 35 home runs — one behind Alvarez. Nine home runs in August between them would be enough to catch him if the pace continues. Pete Alonso of the Baltimore Orioles has been particularly relentless in the RBI column, driving in 22 runs in August alone, pulling essentially even with Alvarez in that race.

The batting average category remains Alvarez's most comfortable cushion. Neither Caminero, Rice, nor Alonso is challenging him there. But a Triple Crown requires leading all three simultaneously — and in two of those three categories, Alvarez's advantage is now measured in single digits.

The Astros Context

The backdrop makes this more complicated. Houston is 67-67, sitting precisely at .500 and on the edge of missing the postseason entirely. The Astros lost to the Yankees 5-1 on Thursday. Alvarez is chasing history on a team fighting for its playoff life — and those aren't always complementary pursuits.

Historically, the Triple Crown has been associated with contenders. Cabrera's 2012 Tigers went to the American League Championship Series. A player chasing the award on a bubble team doesn't fit the usual narrative. Whether that affects the outcome of the race is a separate question from whether it affects how the season is remembered.

What Alvarez Needs

To close out the Triple Crown, Alvarez needs a September resurgence — specifically in the home run column. One home run in a month is a slump by anyone's standards; for a player of his caliber, it's extraordinary. The question is whether the August cold snap reflects a mechanical adjustment teams have made to him, an injury he's managing quietly, or simply the variance that exists in even the best players' seasons.

Whatever the cause, the race is no longer his to cruise through. Every Caminero at-bat, every Rice plate appearance, every Alonso RBI opportunity now carries added weight. The Triple Crown was supposed to be inevitable. Now it's a fight.

What's Next

The AL home run race will tighten or open depending on Alvarez's September production. Watch his at-bat quality and exit velocity — if the underlying contact metrics stay strong, the August slump may be noise. If they've degraded, the field has a real chance. Alonso's RBI pace in August — 22 in one month — is genuinely remarkable and bears watching even if Alvarez regains his power. The race through all three categories will run right to the final week of the regular season.
