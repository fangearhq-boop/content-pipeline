Chourio Already Has More Career Two-HR Games at 21 Than Most Players Have at Any Age

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | August 28, 2026

Jackson Chourio went 4-for-5 with two home runs against the New York Mets on Thursday. It was his fifth career multi-home run game and his third of the 2026 season. He's 21 years old.

Let that sit for a moment. Five career games with multiple home runs. At 21. In a pennant race. Against a team he's done this to before.

What He Did Thursday

The numbers from the 8-2 Brewers victory: 4-for-5, two 2-run home runs, 4 RBI, 4 runs scored. The first home run came in the first inning off Sean Manaea — a 2-run shot that set the tone immediately. The second came in the eighth inning off Xzavion Curry, extending a lead that was already comfortable into a statement.

Manaea, the Mets starter, dropped to 4-6 after surrendering 6 runs on 8 hits in 5.2 innings. Much of the damage came from one player, one time through the order, and then again late in the game. Chourio was relentless.

The Mets Pattern

Here's the detail that makes Thursday's performance more than just a great game: Chourio also hit two home runs against the Mets in the 2024 playoffs. The postseason. As a teenager.

Two seasons apart, two different contexts — regular season pennant race in 2026, October elimination game in 2024 — and the result is the same. Chourio does damage against New York when the stage is biggest. That's not coincidence. That's a player who performs under pressure against legitimate competition.

The Bigger Picture at 21

Five career multi-home run games at age 21 is an extraordinary benchmark. Most players don't reach that number until their mid-to-late twenties, if they reach it at all. Chourio is doing it in his second full major league season.

The Brewers have won 8 of their last 11 games. Jacob Misiorowski is 14-5. Dustin May is targeting a Sunday return. Milwaukee is built to make a deep October run — and at the center of the lineup, Chourio's production level at 21 is what makes that ceiling feel genuinely unlimited.

Power, speed, contact — he has all three. He performs in pressure moments. He's done it against playoff-caliber competition. The only thing that keeps the historical comparisons in check is the fact that he's barely started.

What's Next

The Brewers continue their push toward October with one of the most exciting young players in baseball. Every Chourio at-bat in September and beyond is a reminder that what's happening right now — a 21-year-old playing like a star in the middle of a playoff race — is genuinely rare. Watch the home run total, watch the big-game moments, and watch Milwaukee. Chourio isn't just along for the ride. He's driving it.
