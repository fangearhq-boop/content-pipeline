PCA Has 29 of 37 NL MVP Votes. The Question Is Now Whether He Can Win It on a Team That Needs October.

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | August 28, 2026

Pete Crow-Armstrong has 29 of 37 first-place votes in the latest National League MVP poll. He leads Shohei Ohtani by 23 points — 177 to 154. He is listed at -190 on DraftKings to win the award. By every available metric, this award is his to lose.

The numbers are airtight: .278/.376/.554, 33 home runs, 83 RBI, 32 stolen bases, 7.7 fWAR in 132 games. He's posted a 171 wRC+ with a 1.014 OPS since July 3 — 14 home runs in that stretch alone. He leads all of Major League Baseball with a 24 Fielding Run Value in center field. There is no category where PCA's case has a weakness.

The One Variable: Team Success

There's a layer to this race that the numbers alone don't capture. PCA's MVP case has a team-success component that Ohtani's doesn't.

The Cubs are 76-58 with 32 games remaining, holding the top NL Wild Card spot. They had Thursday off. They open a home series against Cincinnati today. If the Cubs secure a postseason berth — and PCA continues producing at his current rate — the argument is effectively closed. There's no reasonable counter-narrative.

But if the team stumbles down the stretch and misses October? Ohtani voters get ammunition. "His team didn't make the playoffs" is the oldest argument in MVP history, and even if it isn't the most logical one — a player can't control what his teammates do — it's one that moves actual voters.

Where Ohtani Stands

Ohtani finished second in the latest poll with 154 points and 8 first-place votes. He's at +155 on DraftKings. The gap between PCA and Ohtani in both the poll and the betting markets is significant — this isn't a close race at the moment — but "not close" and "over" are different things in September.

Ohtani will continue putting up extraordinary numbers. He always does. The question is whether PCA can maintain his current production level for another 32 games and whether the Cubs can hold their wild card lead while he does it.

The Defense Differentiator

What often gets overlooked in PCA's MVP case is how rare the complete package is. Twenty-four Fielding Run Value in center field is exceptional by any standard — it means his glove alone has been worth more runs saved than almost any other defensive player in baseball. Combined with 33 home runs and 32 stolen bases, PCA is doing something that very few players have done in the modern era: elite defense, elite power, and elite speed, all in the same season.

That combination doesn't just make him the best player in the National League. It makes this one of the more compelling individual seasons in recent memory.

What's Next

Today's Cubs-Reds game at Wrigley Field is more than a regular-season contest — it's the start of a 32-game stretch that will either cement PCA's MVP case or crack it open again. Every Cubs win narrows the counter-argument. Every Cubs loss adds noise. Watch PCA's production through September, and watch the Cubs standings alongside it. The award is his to lose, but he'll need his team to make sure he doesn't have to.
