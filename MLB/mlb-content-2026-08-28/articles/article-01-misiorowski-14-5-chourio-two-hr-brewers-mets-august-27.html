Misiorowski Wins Again — But Watch the Velocity. Chourio Supplies the Power in Brewers' 8-2 Win.

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | August 28, 2026

Jacob Misiorowski got his 14th win of the season on Thursday, shutting down the New York Mets over six innings in Milwaukee's 8-2 victory. The dominant righty is now 14-5 on the year, and still the odds-on Cy Young favorite in his league. But something worth watching emerged in Thursday's outing — multiple reporters covering the game flagged reduced velocity and command issues compared to his usual standard.

That's not necessarily an alarm yet. Misiorowski still posted a quality start: 6 innings, 2 hits, 2 runs, 2 walks, 6 strikeouts. He won. But heading into the final stretch of a pennant race, the Brewers need him operating at full tilt.

The Chourio Show

The night belonged to Jackson Chourio anyway. The 21-year-old outfielder went 4-for-5 with two home runs — a 2-run shot off Sean Manaea in the first inning and another 2-run shot off Xzavion Curry in the eighth — to go along with 4 RBI and 4 runs scored.

It was Chourio's third two-homer game of the 2026 season and the fifth of his career. He's 21. And there's a specific opponent pattern: Chourio also hit two home runs against the Mets in the 2024 playoffs. When the stage matters and the Mets are on the mound, Chourio finds a way.

Manaea, who took the loss, dropped to 4-6 on the season after surrendering 6 runs on 8 hits in 5.2 innings. Chourio's first inning blast set the tone early and the Brewers never looked back.

The Velocity Watch on Misiorowski

The report on Misiorowski's velocity isn't sourced to any specific radar reading — multiple beat reporters noted the pattern, but no exact mph data has been confirmed. What is confirmed: this is the first outing this season where the velocity discussion has surfaced publicly.

Misiorowski still won. He still recorded 6 strikeouts. And the Brewers, who have won 8 of their last 11 games, are very much in the driver's seat heading toward October. But the margin for error narrows when your ace is operating below his peak.

Dustin May threw Thursday as well, reporting that he's "feeling better" after taking a 106 mph comebacker off his scapula last Tuesday. X-rays were negative and he wasn't placed on the IL. He's targeting his Sunday start. If he takes the mound in four days, Milwaukee's rotation depth question answers itself — at least for now.

Brewers Take the Series

The Brewers won the series against the Mets, extending their recent run of strong baseball. At 8-3 in their last 11 games, Milwaukee is building exactly the kind of momentum a team needs heading into September. With Misiorowski fronting the rotation and Chourio hitting like a 10-year veteran at age 21, October is not just the destination — it's beginning to feel like a real expectation.

What's Next

Dustin May's Sunday start is the immediate story to watch. If he takes the ball, the Brewers enter the final month of the regular season with a full and capable rotation. Misiorowski's velocity trend bears monitoring in his next outing — one data point is not a diagnosis, but a second one would raise the conversation considerably. For Chourio, the question is simply how high his ceiling goes. Thursday's performance suggests that answer keeps moving upward.
