Five Straight Series Wins. The Cardinals Are Making the NL Wild Card Race Uncomfortable for Everyone.

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | August 28, 2026

A month ago, the National League Wild Card picture felt settled. The Chicago Cubs held a comfortable lead, the Philadelphia Phillies and San Diego Padres were legitimate competition, and the Arizona Diamondbacks lurked in the background. Three spots. Four teams.

Add St. Louis to the conversation.

The Cardinals have won five consecutive series. They're playing with the kind of momentum that shifts perceptions — and standings. With 32 games remaining, the NL Wild Card race has more legitimate contenders than it has spots, and the math is about to start getting uncomfortable.

Where the Race Stands

The Cubs (76-58) hold the top NL Wild Card spot and had Thursday off, opening a home series against Cincinnati today. The Phillies sit at approximately 74-60, a game and a half back. The Padres are around 72-62. The Cardinals, buoyed by their five-series run, are trending toward the conversation — their exact record isn't confirmed independently, but the directional signal from multiple reports is consistent: St. Louis is playing their best baseball of the second half.

The Phillies were strengthened at the trade deadline by the addition of Luis Arraez. The Cubs added Holmes. The Padres are positioning themselves for a late push. This isn't a field of pretenders — five teams with legitimate claims to October baseball, and only three wild card spots available.

Why the Cubs Should Be Paying Attention

The Cubs' lead is real but not comfortable. At 76-58 with 32 games to play, they control their destiny — but only if they take care of business. Today's matchup against Cincinnati matters. So does the schedule: the Cubs will face multiple NL Wild Card rivals in the coming weeks, and each of those games carries double weight — not just for seeding, but for the standings themselves.

There's also a practical reason the Cubs want to avoid slipping: a potential first-round matchup with the Milwaukee Brewers. The Cubs know how Milwaukee plays. They'd prefer not to find out in October. Securing the best possible seeding means keeping distance from the teams behind them while the Brewers handle business in the NL Central.

The Cardinals Variable

Five straight series wins is the kind of run that changes a team's psychology. The Cardinals, who weren't part of the Wild Card conversation in late July, have injected themselves through sustained winning. Whether they can maintain it through September — against a schedule that will include tougher competition — is the open question. But the trend line is real, and the teams ahead of them should feel it.

What's Next

The NL Wild Card race figures to run deep into September without resolution. Watch the head-to-head matchups among the Cubs, Phillies, Padres, and Cardinals in the coming weeks — those are the games that will separate the three teams that make October from the one that doesn't. The Cubs play Cincinnati today, a winnable game that's also a chance to extend their cushion. Thirty-two games is enough time for anything to happen. The race is on.
