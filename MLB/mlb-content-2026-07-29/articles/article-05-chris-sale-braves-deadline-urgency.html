Chris Sale Starts Tonight's Doubleheader — and the Braves Have No Excuse Not to Be Buyers

By Jake Torres | July 29, 2026

Sale's July 28 start at Citi Field was rained out and rescheduled to tonight. At 37 years old and with one of the best ERAs in baseball, Sale is making the Braves' deadline case for them.

Chris Sale was supposed to pitch Tuesday night at Citi Field. The rain had other plans. So tonight, in the second game of a Braves-Mets doubleheader, the 37-year-old left-hander will take the mound in what shapes up as one of the best individual pitching matchups of the week — Sale facing the Mets in a game where both teams have something to play for.

The rescheduling is a minor logistical footnote. What isn't minor is what Sale has been doing all season.

A 37-Year-Old Having a Career Season

Chris Sale's 2026 ERA sits around 2.10-2.19, a figure that places him among the best starters in baseball. His most recent confirmed start — July 23 in San Diego against the Padres — produced six innings of work and 11 strikeouts in a 6-5 Atlanta win. He is, by most measures, pitching at the highest level of his career since his 2018 season with the Boston Red Sox.

That 2018 season is worth context: Sale was 29, at the peak of his physical prime, and widely considered the best left-hander in the American League. He would go on to win the 2018 World Series with Boston. What he's doing at 37 — after battling injuries through 2020-2022 — is arguably more remarkable given how few pitchers sustain this quality at this age.

The Braves Must Be Buyers

The Atlanta Journal-Constitution made the argument plainly: Sale's performance this season makes it "incumbent" on the Braves to be aggressive buyers at the August 3 deadline. A performance like Sale's doesn't happen every year. It may not happen again. When your ace is pitching at this level, you build around him — you don't coast.

The Braves' roster construction heading into the final stretch is the context that makes Sale's starts feel urgent. He is giving them every reason to compete. The front office's response to that gift will say a great deal about the direction of the franchise heading into August and beyond.

Tonight's Start

Sale takes on the Mets' Christian Scott in the doubleheader nightcap. Both teams are fighting for positioning in the NL playoff picture — making tonight's game a legitimate high-stakes encounter, not a mid-season meaningless outing.

Watch for how Sale manages his pitch count in a doubleheader context, and whether the Braves provide him any run support after being held close in recent games. Tonight is another chapter in what may be the best individual pitching season in the NL this year.

What's Next

The trade deadline is August 3. The Braves' moves in the next five days will determine whether Sale's excellence leads somewhere meaningful. Tonight's doubleheader nightcap at Citi Field is the latest opportunity to watch one of 2026's best stories unfold in real time. Don't miss it.
