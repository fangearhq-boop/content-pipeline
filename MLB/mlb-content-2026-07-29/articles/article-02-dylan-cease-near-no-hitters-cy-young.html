Dylan Cease's Back-to-Back Near No-Hitters Are the Most Dominant Pitching Stretch of 2026

By Jake Torres | July 29, 2026

In a three-week span, the Blue Jays ace carried no-hitters deep into back-to-back starts — and built the strongest AL Cy Young case in baseball.

There are good stretches. There are great stretches. And then there is what Dylan Cease has been doing.

In a span of 17 days, the Toronto Blue Jays right-hander carried a no-hitter into the 9th inning against the San Francisco Giants on July 8, striking out 11, then went out on July 25 against the Boston Red Sox and carried another no-hitter into the 6th inning before finishing with a complete game 1-hitter in a 6-0 Blue Jays victory.

Back-to-back near-no-hitters, in the same month, against two legitimate major league offenses. What Cease is doing has no analogue in the 2026 season — and it may be one of the more remarkable stretches in recent pitching history.

The Two Starts

The July 8 start against San Francisco was a showcase of Cease's ability to attack a lineup for nine innings. He held the Giants hitless through eight, striking out 11 along the way. A hit in the 9th inning broke up the no-hitter — but the performance still stands as one of the dominant individual starts of the season.

Then came July 25 in Boston. Cease went right back to work, retiring hitters in order through the early innings and carrying a no-hitter into the 6th. When the Red Sox finally broke through for their lone hit, Cease was unfazed — he finished the complete game, allowing no runs, and the Blue Jays won 6-0. From his first pitch to his last, it was another masterpiece.

The Cy Young Case

You don't need two near-no-hitters to make a Cy Young argument. But you especially don't need to look hard at the AL leaderboard to find Cease's name near the top. His 2026 season has been defined by consistency, durability, and an ability to dominate opposing lineups through the late innings.

The complete game 1-hitter on July 25 added another data point to an already compelling case. In an era dominated by bullpen management, openers, and six-inning outings called "quality starts," Cease is finishing what he starts — and doing it at the highest level.

The conversation at the top of the AL Cy Young ballot is real. Cease is in it.

Toronto's Rotation Weapon

The Blue Jays have a Cy Young candidate at the front of their rotation — a fact that changes how any AL club has to approach a potential playoff matchup with Toronto. A team with an ace this hot is a team nobody wants to face in a short series.

For a Blue Jays franchise that has been building toward a deep October run, Cease's dominance in July is the clearest signal yet that the rotation can carry this team.

What's Next

Cease's next start will be watched closely by AL rivals and Cy Young voters alike. Can he extend this stretch? And can the Blue Jays continue to capitalize? His July performances have set a standard that demands attention — and the baseball world is paying it. Keep an eye on mlb.com and the Blue Jays' upcoming schedule for his next mound appearance.
