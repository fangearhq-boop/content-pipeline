Can Kyle Schwarber Join the 60 HR Club? The MLB Home Run Leader Is Making a Real Case

By Marcus Cole | July 29, 2026

The Phillies outfielder is leading baseball in home runs at 35 years old, and with two months remaining, the conversation about 60 is legitimate.

Kyle Schwarber is baseball's home run leader. He won the Home Run Derby. He was named NL Player of the Month. And ESPN is already running features asking whether the 35-year-old Philadelphia Phillies outfielder can challenge one of the most exclusive numbers in the sport's history: 60 home runs.

Schwarber, sitting at approximately 33 home runs through late July, is on pace to challenge his own 2025 output — when he led the NL and all of baseball with 56 home runs. He is doing this at an age when most players are in decline, with a left-handed pull swing that is tailor-made for the right-field dimensions at Citizens Bank Park and the other bandboxes on the NL schedule.

The 60 HR Club

Sixty home runs in a single season has been achieved five times by five players. Roger Maris hit 61 in 1961 to break Babe Ruth's single-season record. Mark McGwire hit 70 in 1998 and 65 in 1999. Sammy Sosa crossed 60 three times (66 in 1998, 63 in 1999, 64 in 2001). Barry Bonds reached 73 in 2001. Aaron Judge hit 62 in 2022 to break the American League record.

Schwarber would have to maintain an extraordinary pace over the final two months of the season to join that group. But at 33+ home runs with roughly 60 games remaining, the math is not impossible. He would need approximately 27 more home runs in those 60 games — a pace he has reached before.

Why Schwarber Is Different

Power typically declines with age. Schwarber is defying that trajectory. At 35, he has not only maintained his home run production from prior seasons — he's escalating it. His 56-HR season in 2025 was the best of his career. His 2026 start suggests he intends to match or exceed it.

Part of the explanation is discipline. Schwarber has always had elite plate discipline for a power hitter, willing to work counts and punish pitchers who dare to challenge him in the zone. That approach ages better than raw athleticism — and it's keeping him at the top of every offensive leaderboard at an age when most peers are fading.

NL Player of the Month

The Player of the Month honor reflects not just the home run output but the full offensive package Schwarber has brought in July. He has been the best hitter in the NL for a sustained stretch — which, in a league with José Abreu, Yordan Alvarez, and other elite bats, is a meaningful designation.

What's Next

Schwarber and the Phillies have two months remaining. The next milestone to watch is 40 home runs — a number he has never reached in a single season. If he gets there by mid-August, the 60 HR conversation will get louder. Track his progress at mlb.com and follow the Phillies' schedule for his next at-bats. This is one of the defining individual stories of the 2026 season — and it's nowhere close to over.
