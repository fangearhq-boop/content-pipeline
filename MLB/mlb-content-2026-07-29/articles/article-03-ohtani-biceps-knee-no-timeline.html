Ohtani's Pitching Return Complicated Further: Right Biceps Now Reportedly Irritated

By Ryan Calloway | July 29, 2026

In addition to the left knee regression that sidelined him last week, Shohei Ohtani is now dealing with right biceps irritation. No timeline for a return to the mound.

The path back to the mound for Shohei Ohtani just got more complicated.

Already dealing with left knee irritation that caused him to skip a scheduled bullpen session on July 25 — what manager Dave Roberts described as "regression" after Ohtani threw approximately 30 pitches on July 22 — the Dodgers ace is now reportedly also dealing with right biceps irritation. Two injury areas. No timetable. He will not make his next scheduled pitching appearance.

Roberts has been clear about the standard the Dodgers require before Ohtani goes back on the mound: "We're not going to move forward until he feels 100% confident and we feel 100% confident."

Where Things Stand

Ohtani last pitched on July 3. Before the knee issue emerged, he was one of the best pitchers in baseball: 8-2 with a 1.79 ERA, 95 strikeouts in 85.2 innings. The stuff, the results, and the workload all pointed toward a historic two-way season.

Since then, he has been DHing exclusively. That hasn't stopped him from being productive — his slash line at the plate is .288/.397/.536, and his offensive impact remains elite. But the Dodgers signed him to be a two-way player, and the pitching half of that equation is indefinitely on hold.

The right biceps issue adds a dimension of concern that wasn't present last week. Knee irritation is one category of injury. Arm issues — particularly for a pitcher who generates the velocity and movement Ohtani does — are another. Roberts' insistence on 100 percent confidence from both camp suggests the medical staff is not taking shortcuts with their most important player.

Deadline Week Without the Ace

The August 3 trade deadline is five days away. The Dodgers are the most aggressive buyers in the market, in pursuit of a third consecutive World Series title. They are operating with Ohtani as a DH — not a pitcher — for a window that has no defined end.

Whether the Dodgers add a starting pitcher before August 3 may depend, in part, on how optimistic they are about Ohtani's return timeline. If there is genuine uncertainty about when — or whether — he returns to the rotation this season, adding a starter becomes a higher priority.

What's Next

There is no projected return date for Ohtani to the mound. The Dodgers have indicated they will provide updates as they come. For now, the most important player in baseball — from a two-way standpoint — is a designated hitter only, and the team's rotation plans will need to account for his absence in any playoff scenario. Watch for updates from Dave Roberts before and after games at Dodger Stadium and on the road.
