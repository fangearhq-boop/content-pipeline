MLB Trade Deadline Snapshot: Skubal Deal Likely, Miller Staying, Deadline Arrives in 5 Days

By Marcus Cole | July 29, 2026

Two of the most-watched names at the deadline have clarified — in opposite directions — with five days remaining before the August 3 cutoff.

The August 3 trade deadline is five days away, and the picture is finally coming into focus. Two of the most-discussed names in deadline speculation shifted Tuesday: a Tarik Skubal trade is now described as "likely" by Sports Illustrated, while USA Today's Bob Nightengale reports the San Diego Padres are "likely to keep" closer Mason Miller unless someone presents an impossible offer.

The twin developments reshape the market with two major names moving in opposite directions — one toward a blockbuster deal, one off the board.

Skubal: Tigers Want to Move Their Ace

Detroit's front office reportedly wants to trade left-hander Tarik Skubal, who hits free agency after 2026. The asking price is steep: a young, MLB-ready starting pitcher plus multiple prospects. Teams willing to meet that standard are putting themselves in position to land one of the most valuable starters available.

The Los Angeles Dodgers are the frontrunner, with the Brewers and Rays also in pursuit and the Yankees and Braves showing interest. The Dodgers — chasing a third consecutive World Series title — have the organizational depth and motivation to match Detroit's demands. Skubal's controllable years are gone after this season, which is precisely why the Tigers would rather sell high now than watch him walk away.

Miller: Padres Pulling Him Back

Mason Miller had been widely considered one of the most likely names to change addresses before August 3. The elite closer — a 0.89 ERA and a perfect 25-for-25 in save opportunities — generated heavy interest from teams that recognized his combination of dominance and controllability through 2029.

But Nightengale's reporting signals a reversal. The Padres are "likely to keep" Miller, reserving him only for an offer that qualifies as impossible to refuse. Given San Diego's own playoff aspirations, holding their lockdown closer makes sense: the value of Miller to a team trying to win is arguably greater than whatever prospects they'd receive in return.

What Else to Watch

The broader deadline landscape features the Los Angeles Angels conducting a genuine fire sale. Shortstop Zach Neto — who leads the American League with 69 runs scored and has three years of team control remaining — is available, as are right-handers Jose Soriano (3.43 ERA) and Reid Detmers (4.16 ERA), both under control through 2028. Reports indicate it is "increasingly likely" that at least one of the two pitchers moves by August 3.

CJ Abrams is a different story. The Washington Nationals shortstop — having a historic individual season with 27 home runs, tracking toward 42 HR, 127 RBI, and 26 stolen bases — is not actively being shopped. Washington sits at 53-52 and is in the NL Wild Card race, making it a difficult sell to move their franchise player while there's still a chance to compete.

The first major completed deal: the Boston Red Sox acquired infielder Curtis Mead from the Nationals in exchange for pitcher Connelly Early, signaling Boston's intent to upgrade their roster heading into August.

What's Next

The MLB trade deadline is August 3 at 6 p.m. ET. The next 120 hours will define the postseason landscape. Expect Skubal's destination to become clearer within 48 hours as Detroit and its suitors move toward a deal. The Angels' fire sale will likely produce at least one major transaction. And if the NL Wild Card race tightens or collapses, more sellers may emerge. Every team's next move is being made in real time — the deadline clock is running.
