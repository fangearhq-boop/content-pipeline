Drew Rasmussen Has Been Untouchable This Postseason — And He Hasn't Pitched Yet

By Marcus Cole | October 23, 2026

The Tampa Bay Rays are not sending their best pitcher to the mound tonight. That is either a bold tactical move or a quiet statement of confidence, depending on how you read it. Drew Rasmussen — 16-5, 2.67 ERA in the regular season, and one of the most dominant postseason performers of the last decade — will pitch Game 2 of the World Series. The Rays are saving him.

For Brewers fans hoping Milwaukee gets through the opener with momentum, the reward waiting for them tomorrow is arguably a harder challenge.

The Numbers From the Postseason

Rasmussen pitched 21 innings in the 2026 playoffs before the World Series began. His ERA across those innings was 1.29. That places his October performance in a class with the best postseason runs a starter has put together in recent memory.

The signature moment came in Game 1 of the American League Division Series against the New York Yankees at Tropicana Field. Rasmussen carried a no-hitter deep into the game, and by the time he left the mound he had thrown one-hit baseball over eight-plus innings, striking out 10. The Rays won 1-0. It was the kind of start that makes grown baseball fans stop what they are doing and just watch.

In ALCS Game 5, he followed with seven innings and one run allowed to put the Rays in position to clinch in Game 6. He did his job and stepped aside. Freddy Peralta closed it out and won ALCS MVP, but Rasmussen had put the series in position to be won.

The Regular Season Context

Rasmussen's postseason dominance is not a surprise if you watched him pitch all year. He was a two-time All-Star in 2026 and went 16-5 with a 2.67 ERA. He struck out hitters consistently and kept the ball in the park, which is exactly what you need against the Brewers offense.

Milwaukee ranked among the best offensive teams in the National League this season, led by Jackson Chourio, William Contreras, Sal Frelick, and NLCS MVP Jake Bauers. Against those hitters, Rasmussen's control and pitch sequencing will be the key variable. His analytics profile suggests elite late-start command. If he locates his fastball below the zone and uses his slider on both sides of the plate, the Brewers' lineup will struggle.

The Strategic Logic

The Rays' decision to open with Nick Martinez is not an admission that they fear Milwaukee in Game 1. It is a recognition that winning a seven-game series requires managing resources across the full slate. Martinez is a quality starter who can give Tampa Bay five or six innings. That keeps the bullpen fresh, preserves Rasmussen for the second Milwaukee game, and keeps Freddy Peralta available for Game 3 at Tropicana Field — where the Rays are historically strong.

Tampa Bay under manager Kevin Cash has consistently been one of the most analytically sophisticated teams in baseball. This rotation deployment has Cash's fingerprints all over it.

What's Next

Game 1 is tonight at 8:07 PM Eastern on FOX. Drew Rasmussen is expected to start Game 2 tomorrow night at American Family Field — the second and final World Series game in Milwaukee before the series shifts to Tropicana Field. If the Rays win one of the first two Milwaukee games and then get Rasmussen at home in Game 3, their rotation set up is exactly where they want it.
