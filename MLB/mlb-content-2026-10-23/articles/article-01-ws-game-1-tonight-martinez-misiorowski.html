World Series Game 1 Is Tonight — Nick Martinez vs. Jacob Misiorowski at American Family Field

By Jake Torres | October 23, 2026

The 2026 World Series begins tonight. After weeks of postseason baseball — six series, dozens of elimination games, walk-offs and near no-hitters — it comes down to this: the Tampa Bay Rays visiting the Milwaukee Brewers at American Family Field, first pitch at 8:07 PM Eastern on FOX.

Neither franchise has ever won a World Series. One of them ends that streak tonight, or at least takes the first step toward ending it.

The Starting Pitching Decision

ESPN confirmed that the Tampa Bay Rays will send Nick Martinez to the mound in Game 1 — a notable choice over postseason ace Drew Rasmussen, who many analysts expected to start. The move is strategic: Rasmussen, who posted a 1.29 ERA across 21 playoff innings, is now fully available for Game 2 tomorrow night, still at American Family Field.

Martinez has been one of the quiet stories of this Rays postseason run. He signed a one-year, $13 million deal with Tampa Bay after two seasons with the Reds and delivered in the regular season and through two strong postseason starts. He is not Rasmussen — no one in the rotation is — but Martinez has given Tampa Bay exactly what they needed when asked.

For Milwaukee, Jacob Misiorowski starts on extended rest after the Brewers swept the Los Angeles Dodgers in four games in the NLCS. The presumptive NL Cy Young winner led the league in ERA (1.80) and strikeouts during the regular season. His postseason starts have featured shorter outings than his regular-season form — he averaged roughly 4 to 4.5 innings per start in October — but with 11 days of rest since his last appearance, the Brewers expect a sharper, deeper outing tonight.

Milwaukee's Home-Field Edge

The Brewers enter tonight 4-0 at American Family Field this postseason. They swept the San Diego Padres and swept the Dodgers — winning both series at home without needing a seventh game. The crowd at American Family Field has been the loudest in the ballpark's 25-year history this October, and Milwaukee drew 2,788,934 fans during the regular season, up 5.2 percent from the year before.

The last time Milwaukee hosted a World Series game was 1982, when the Brewers fell to the Cardinals in seven games at old County Stadium. A full generation of Brewers fans has grown up without that experience. Tonight, that changes.

Tampa Bay has won three of three games at American Family Field this regular season, outscoring Milwaukee 16-7 in those matchups. Road experience in this building is not a problem for the Rays.

The Numbers That Matter

Milwaukee enters as a -162 favorite on the moneyline with Tampa Bay at +134. That spread reflects the Brewers' 103-win regular season — the best record in all of baseball — combined with their undefeated home record this postseason. The Opta projection model gives Milwaukee a 61.2 percent probability of winning the series.

The bullpens are the wild card. Milwaukee's relief corps posted a 0.38 ERA across 23.2 innings in the NLCS — one of the best single-series bullpen performances in recent memory. DL Hall, Aaron Ashby, and closer Abner Uribe have been automatic. Tampa Bay's depth is also strong, featuring Peralta, Rasmussen, and Martinez across the first three games.

What's Next

Game 1 is tonight at 8:07 PM Eastern on FOX from American Family Field in Milwaukee. Game 2 follows tomorrow night, also in Milwaukee, where Drew Rasmussen is expected to start for the Rays. The series shifts to Tropicana Field in St. Petersburg for Games 3 through 5 (October 26 through 28), then returns to Milwaukee for Games 6 and 7 if needed. One World Series title has never belonged to either of these franchises. After tonight, the picture becomes clearer.
