Franco Caminero Is the Rays' X-Factor in the World Series — And He Already Has His Signature Moment

By Ryan Calloway | October 23, 2026

If you missed most of the 2026 regular season and came back for the playoffs, the first time you saw Franco Caminero at the plate you would have thought he had been one of the best players in baseball all year. You would not have been entirely wrong. The parts of 2026 he played were exactly that. The problem was he did not play enough of them.

Entering the World Series, Caminero is the most important question mark in the Tampa Bay lineup — and potentially the single biggest factor in deciding the series.

Two Seasons in One

Caminero's 2025 season was one of the most impressive breakout years an AL third baseman has produced in recent memory. He hit 45 home runs, drove in 110 runs, made his first two All-Star Games, and established himself at 21 years old as a legitimate franchise cornerstone. The expectations coming into 2026 were enormous.

Then injuries intervened. He appeared in only 51 games during the regular season, slashing .265/.356/.490 with 13 home runs and 27 RBI. Those numbers in 51 games project to roughly 42 home runs and 88 RBI over a full season — still excellent, still dangerous. But he only played 51 games. There were questions about his health, his timing, and whether the 2025 version was coming back.

October answered those questions emphatically.

The Foul Pole Moment

In ALCS Game 6 against the Cleveland Guardians, with the Rays one win from the World Series, Caminero stepped to the plate in the seventh inning and hit a two-run home run off the left field foul pole. The Rays went on to win 7-3, clinching the American League pennant. Freddy Peralta won ALCS MVP for what he did in the circle, but the foul pole shot was the moment that made it secure.

That is who Caminero is. In the moments that matter most, he finds something. The 2025 season was full of those moments — home runs in September, big swings in the middle of the lineup when Tampa Bay needed runs. The 2026 regular season was interrupted. The postseason has been clarifying.

The Challenge: Milwaukee's Pitching

The Brewers present a genuine problem for any opponent's offense. Milwaukee's bullpen posted a 0.38 ERA across 23.2 innings in the NLCS — one of the best single-series relief performances in the modern era. DL Hall, Aaron Ashby, and the rest of the Brewers' backend have been dominant. Misiorowski, when he is on, is the best starter in the National League.

Getting to that pitching requires patience, quality at-bats, and power. Caminero brings all three. His walk rate is elite for a player his age, his contact quality metrics rank in the top tier of the league, and his raw power — as evidenced by the 45-home-run 2025 campaign — is genuine. If any Rays hitter can solve Milwaukee's staff, it is the 22-year-old third baseman.

What's Next

Game 1 is tonight at 8:07 PM Eastern on FOX at American Family Field. The World Series runs through October at the latest, with Games 1 and 2 in Milwaukee, Games 3 through 5 in Tampa Bay, and Games 6 and 7 if necessary back in Milwaukee. Every Franco Caminero at-bat in this series will be worth watching. The Rays need his October form to keep going.
