Freddy Peralta Is Coming Back to Milwaukee — This Time as the Enemy

By Ryan Calloway | October 23, 2026

Eight seasons. That is how long Freddy Peralta called American Family Field home. He grew up as a pitcher in Milwaukee, went from raw prospect to ace, and became one of the most popular players the franchise had seen in decades. The Brewers fans loved him. And then, this January, Milwaukee traded him away.

Tonight, Peralta returns to Milwaukee — wearing the wrong uniform, carrying the wrong logo on his hat, and carrying ALCS MVP honors with him.

The Trade That Came Back to Haunt

Peralta's departure was a business decision. He was eligible for free agency at the end of this season and the Brewers, assessing their long-term financial picture, sent him to the New York Mets in January. By January standards, it was a reasonable move — teams make similar decisions every winter, protecting future payroll flexibility by trading veterans with one year left on their timelines.

Then the Mets dealt him to Tampa Bay at the trade deadline. And then Peralta caught fire.

In the American League Championship Series against the Cleveland Guardians, Peralta was untouchable. He went 2-0 with a 0.75 ERA across three appearances that included two starts. In the series clincher — Game 6 — he threw 5.1 innings, the Rays won 7-3, and the ALCS MVP hardware went to the pitcher Milwaukee let go ten months earlier.

Eight Seasons, Eight Memories

Peralta's tenure in Milwaukee reads like a coming-of-age story. He arrived as a young arm from the Dominican Republic and developed into one of the best strikeout pitchers in the National League. His fastball-curveball combination set him apart, and fans at American Family Field celebrated his most dominant outings the way you celebrate someone you watched grow up. In his final season in Milwaukee, he went 17-6 with a 2.70 ERA.

When the trade was announced in January, there was disappointment — the kind of quiet sadness that comes with a sports business decision that you understand intellectually but still don't want to accept. Peralta said the right things. The Brewers said the right things. Everyone moved on.

Except now everyone has to face him.

When Will He Pitch?

Nick Martinez is starting Game 1 for Tampa Bay tonight. Drew Rasmussen is expected for Game 2 tomorrow. Based on rotation logistics and the AP reports following the Rays' ALCS clincher, Peralta would be available for Game 2 on regular rest or Game 3 when the series shifts to Tropicana Field in St. Petersburg.

Whenever he takes the mound in this series, the same question applies: can the Brewers offense — Chourio, Turang, Bauers, Contreras, the whole lineup — figure out the pitcher they watched grow up in Milwaukee, now throwing as hard as he ever has for a team that just beat Cleveland in six games?
