A First-Time Champion Is Guaranteed — The Historical Weight of Brewers vs. Rays

By Jake Torres | October 23, 2026

When the final out of the 2026 World Series is recorded, a city will celebrate its first baseball championship in franchise history. That much is certain. The Milwaukee Brewers and the Tampa Bay Rays have never won the Fall Classic. Tonight, the series opens, and one of them begins walking toward a party that has been a long time coming.

The Brewers' 44-Year Wait

Milwaukee's last World Series appearance came in 1982 at old County Stadium. The Brewers — then an American League team — won the pennant on a memorable run led by Robin Yount and Paul Molitor, who hit .355 in that Fall Classic. They pushed the St. Louis Cardinals to seven games before losing. It was the closest this franchise has ever come.

In the decades that followed, the Brewers moved to the National League in 1998 and began rebuilding under a series of managers and front office configurations. They had a few excellent individual seasons. They made the playoffs in 2008, 2011, and in a handful of years after that. But the World Series stayed out of reach.

This year felt different from the first month of the season. Milwaukee went 103-59 — the best record in baseball — and rolled through the Padres and Dodgers in the playoffs without losing a game at home. The city has been electric in a way that its residents say they have never experienced. Watch parties filled American Family Field during the Dodger series. The franchise built a young core, the ownership invested, and 2026 became the year.

The Rays' Three Tries

Tampa Bay has been to the World Series twice before this. In 2008 — only their 11th season in franchise history — the Rays shocked the baseball world by reaching the Fall Classic before losing to the Philadelphia Phillies in five games. In 2020, they made it back, built a two-games-to-one lead over the Los Angeles Dodgers, and then lost four consecutive games to fall short again.

The Rays have a peculiar postseason legacy. They are widely regarded as one of the best-run organizations in baseball — consistently competitive on a constrained payroll, regularly developing elite pitching, and pioneering analytical deployment strategies that the rest of the league has adopted. And yet, when the World Series ends each October, they have nothing to show for it.

This is Year Three. If Tampa Bay wins this series, the organizational satisfaction will be enormous — proof that doing things right, for a long time, eventually produces the trophy.

Five Franchises Without a Title

Milwaukee and Tampa Bay are two of five Major League Baseball franchises that have never won a World Series. The others include the Colorado Rockies, San Diego Padres, and Texas Rangers — though the Rangers won in 2023, reducing the group. The Brewers and Rays both have passionate fan bases and long organizational histories of near-misses. Whoever wins this series answers a question that has been hanging over one of these cities for decades.
