### STORY 4: First-Time Champion Guaranteed

**Long-Form Post (3:00 PM ET)**

When the final out is recorded in this World Series, a city is going to celebrate its first championship in franchise history. That is guaranteed.

The Milwaukee Brewers last appeared in the World Series in 1982. They pushed the St. Louis Cardinals to seven games at old County Stadium before falling. Since then, a generation of Brewers fans has grown up without ever seeing their team win it all. The franchise joined the National League in 1998 and has rebuilt multiple times, finally arriving at this moment with their best team in decades — 103 regular-season wins, the most in baseball.

The Tampa Bay Rays have appeared in two previous World Series. In 2008, they shocked the baseball world by reaching the Fall Classic in only their 11th franchise season before losing to the Philadelphia Phillies in five games. In 2020, they made it back, building a two-games-to-one series lead before the Los Angeles Dodgers won four straight. The Rays have been close twice without closing.

Tonight opens the third chance for Tampa Bay and the second for Milwaukee. The historical weight is equal on both sides. What is different now: neither team looks like a longshot. Both are legitimate contenders. Both have the arms, the depth, and the experience.

One of them is going to celebrate in the streets of their city this October.

### STORY 5: Franco Caminero — The X-Factor in This Series

**Long-Form Post (6:00 PM ET)**

If you want to find the player most likely to swing this World Series in Tampa Bay's direction, look at the third baseman.

Franco Caminero's 2025 season was a revelation. He made his first two All-Star Games, finished with 45 home runs and 110 RBI, and established himself as one of the best young hitters in the American League at just 21 years old. This year, nagging injuries limited him to 51 games — but in those games, he was still dangerous: a .265/.356/.490 slash line with 13 home runs and 27 RBI.

Then came October.

In ALCS Game 6 — with the Rays one win from the World Series — Caminero hit a two-run home run off the foul pole in the seventh inning that proved to be the decisive blow in Tampa Bay's 7-3 victory. It was the kind of moment that announces a player to a national audience.

Milwaukee's pitching staff is elite. The Brewers bullpen posted a 0.38 ERA across 23.2 innings in the NLCS against the Dodgers. Misiorowski is the best starter in the National League. Getting to this staff is not easy.

But Caminero has done hard things before. The World Series is where legacies are made. Tonight, he gets his shot.
