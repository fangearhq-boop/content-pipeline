Matt Olson's 900th: The Iron Man Milestone That Keeps Growing

By Marcus Cole

August 10, 2026

On Sunday afternoon in New York, the Atlanta Braves beat the Yankees 2-1. Matt Olson played first base. He hit, he fielded, he did what he does. And in doing so, he played in his 900th consecutive Major League Baseball game.

Nine hundred. Five years and change of showing up, staying healthy, and playing through every game the schedule produces. No other active player in baseball is within hundreds of games of that number.

How Long a Streak Like This Takes

Olson's consecutive games streak started on May 2, 2021 — while he was still an Oakland Athletic, before the trade that brought him to Atlanta, before the Braves built one of the NL's most consistent rosters around him at first base. In the more than five years since that first game, he has played through the physical grind of an MLB season over and over: spring training to October, followed by another spring training, another summer, another full schedule of games.

He surpassed Dale Murphy's Braves franchise record earlier in his streak. Last week, he passed Stan Musial (895 consecutive games) to move into sole possession of 8th place on the all-time MLB list. Now the 900th game adds to what was already an extraordinary run.

Where He Stands All-Time

The all-time consecutive games list in Major League Baseball is headlined by Cal Ripken Jr.'s record 2,632 — a number so large it barely registers as achievable. Below Ripken come names from across baseball's eras: Everett Scott (1,307), Steve Garvey (1,207), Billy Williams (1,117), Joe Sewell (1,103), and others who defined what it meant to be available for their team every single day.

Olson is now 8th. The next name ahead of him on the list is Joe Sewell at 1,103 consecutive games — a target that would require Olson to maintain his streak through most of the 2028 season. If he does, he'll climb further. For now, 900 is a number worth stopping to appreciate.

The Player Behind the Streak

Olson doesn't seek attention for the consecutive games total. He plays, and the number grows. But context matters: the streak exists alongside a career that features consistent offensive production at first base, multiple All-Star appearances, and membership on a Braves team that has competed at a high level throughout his time in Atlanta.

The streak isn't what defines Olson as a player — his bat and his glove do that. But it does define something about his professional character: a commitment to availability that has become one of the sport's quiet ongoing stories.

What's Next

Olson and the Braves continue their season with games remaining across a competitive NL East stretch run. The next round-number milestone — 950 games — would arrive in October. Joe Sewell's 1,103 games, if Olson continues, would arrive sometime in 2028. For now, the Braves beat the Yankees 2-1, Olson played his 900th straight, and the streak continues.
