Colson Montgomery's 26th Homer Fuels White Sox Win — Chicago's AL Central Lead Grows to 3.5 Games

By Ryan Calloway

August 10, 2026

One year ago, the Chicago White Sox were historically bad. They finished 41-121 in 2024 — the worst record in modern Major League Baseball history. On Sunday, they beat the Cleveland Guardians 5-3 behind Colson Montgomery's 26th home run of the season and now lead the American League Central by 3.5 games.

The turnaround has been one of 2026's most compelling sports stories, and Montgomery has been at the center of it.

Montgomery's Moment

In the third inning Sunday, Montgomery connected on a three-run shot that capped a four-run Chicago rally and gave the White Sox the separation they'd need. It was his 26th home run of the season — a figure that places him among the AL's productive young position players — and it came at a moment when Chicago needed the offense to carry the game.

Montgomery came into 2026 as one of baseball's most anticipated young players. He was a top prospect, a first-round pick by the White Sox in 2021, and someone the organization had built toward during the rebuild that produced the historically difficult 2024 season. This is the payoff — a young shortstop hitting 26 home runs with a quarter of the season left, contributing to a division race that no one predicted Chicago would be leading.

The AL Central Picture

Chicago's 3.5-game lead over the second-place Detroit Tigers is the most cushion the White Sox have had at any point this season. The Cleveland Guardians and Minnesota Twins are four games back. None of the division's three trailing teams are eliminated — the AL Central will be decided in September — but the White Sox have built enough of a buffer that they control their own destiny heading into the season's final third.

Detroit has been the most consistent challenger, and any series between these two teams will carry division race weight through August and September. But for now, Chicago has the lead, and they've earned it.

The Comeback in Context

The 2025 White Sox were not much better than 2024 — a continuing rebuild that required patience from an organization that had mortgaged its present for a future that kept receding. The 2026 version, with Montgomery healthy and contributing, with trades and development paying off, is the team that justified that patience. From the worst record in the sport to division leaders in 24 months is as steep a turnaround as baseball produces.

What's Next

The White Sox continue their season with a slate of late-summer games against AL Central competition. The division lead is real, but six weeks of baseball remain. Montgomery and Chicago's pitching staff will determine whether this team can finish what they've started. Sunday was another step in the right direction.
