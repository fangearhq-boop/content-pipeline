One Step Forward, One Step Back: Dodgers Drop Series Finale to Arizona 4-2

By Marcus Cole

August 10, 2026

On Saturday, Shohei Ohtani walked off the Arizona Diamondbacks in the 10th inning and the Los Angeles Dodgers ended a seven-game losing streak. Twenty-four hours later, they lost to the same team 4-2 and the questions came flooding back.

That is the Dodgers' pattern right now: one win that briefly restores confidence, followed by a loss that strips it away. For a team that entered August as a two-time defending World Series champion — and one that added Tarik Skubal and Adley Rutschman at the trade deadline — the gap between expectation and reality has been one of baseball's more unsettling stories.

Sunday's Game

Arizona took the series finale 4-2, and the Dodgers' offense offered little to change the outcome. Ohtani remained the most dangerous hitter in the lineup — he always does — but the D-backs' pitching kept the Los Angeles attack from mounting any meaningful rally. The Dodgers managed two runs and not much else.

The loss closed out a weekend series that could have been a reset and wasn't. Ending the losing streak on Saturday was a relief. Losing Sunday — to the same opponent, in the same series — was a reminder that one win doesn't repair a team's underlying issues, whatever those issues turn out to be.

What's Happening in Los Angeles

The Dodgers haven't fallen out of the NL West race. They still lead the division, and the NL bracket hasn't reshuffled around them. But the team that was supposed to be an October juggernaut has not played like one for stretches of this month, and the talent on the roster makes that especially difficult to explain.

Blake Snell missed time with an injury — that's a real piece of the puzzle. Skubal, acquired at the deadline in part to stabilize the rotation for exactly these moments, has not yet made his Dodger debut. He is expected to start Tuesday, and his first outing in a Los Angeles uniform will be watched with a level of attention that most mid-August starts don't generate.

Rutschman, meanwhile, brings another dimension to a lineup that already features Ohtani. The pieces are here. The results haven't matched.

Skubal's Debut

The Dodgers acquired Skubal from the Detroit Tigers at the trade deadline with the expectation that he would be part of their playoff rotation. Before he can be that, he needs to pitch in a Dodger uniform, and Tuesday provides that opportunity. If he is the version of Skubal that made him one of baseball's best starters — and there's no reason to think he won't be — the rotation looks very different going into September.

The window for recalibration is narrow but still open. The Dodgers have the talent to be a legitimate October contender. Skubal's debut is the next reference point. What happens from there will tell a lot about whether this team finds its footing before the regular season ends.

What's Next

Los Angeles heads into the week with Skubal's Tuesday start as the most anticipated item on the schedule. The Dodgers are still in first place in the NL West. The division isn't running away from them. But a team with their payroll and their roster has no margin for continued inconsistency if they want to look like themselves by October.
