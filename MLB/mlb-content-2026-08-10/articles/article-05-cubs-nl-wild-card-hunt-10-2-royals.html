Cubs Roll Past Royals 10-2, Sit Atop NL Wild Card with Six-Game Cushion

By Jake Torres

August 10, 2026

The Chicago Cubs have a 68-50 record. They lead the NL Wild Card standings by six games. They are a genuine playoff contender. And on Sunday, they beat the Kansas City Royals 10-2 to make all of the above feel more true.

The margin of victory — eight runs — was almost beside the point. What matters is the continued accumulation of wins at a pace that has separated the Cubs from a competitive field chasing the last spots in the National League postseason.

Where the Cubs Stand

At 68-50, the Cubs hold the top NL Wild Card position. The Arizona Diamondbacks and Philadelphia Phillies are tied at 62-56 — six games back — competing for the second and third wild card spots. With 44 games remaining in the regular season, the Cubs have enough cushion that they control their own destiny. Playing .500 baseball from here gets them in. Playing the way they have been gets them the No. 4 seed and meaningful home field considerations in the bracket.

Six games is not a lead you sleepwalk across the finish line with, but it is a lead that puts the Cubs in a position that teams spend full seasons trying to build. They've built it. Now they hold it.

The NL Postseason Picture

Context matters for what the Cubs' wild card position means in October. The Atlanta Braves lead the NL East by 8.5 games and are tracking toward the top seed in the National League bracket. The Milwaukee Brewers lead the NL Central. The Dodgers, for all their August inconsistency, still lead the NL West.

That configuration puts the Cubs as the likely No. 4 seed — which means road games in the wild card round and a bracket that would send them toward one of the division leaders in the Division Series if they advance. It is not the easiest path, but it is a postseason path, and the Cubs have spent most of this season building one.

Why 68-50 Is Real

Some teams build wild card leads with soft scheduling or streaky play that doesn't hold up. The Cubs' record reflects sustained performance across the full season. At 68-50, they are 18 games over .500 — a figure that doesn't emerge from favorable matchups alone. They've been consistently good, not occasionally great, and that's a more reliable foundation for a September run than the alternative.

The schedule tightens in September. The teams chasing them — Arizona and Philadelphia — are not going away. But the Cubs have the lead, they have the record to back it up, and Sunday's 10-2 win over Kansas City was another brick in the foundation.

What's Next

The Cubs enter the final six weeks of the regular season with 44 games left and the clearest view of the postseason of any wild card team in the NL. The competition is real, but so is Chicago's lead. Keep watching.
