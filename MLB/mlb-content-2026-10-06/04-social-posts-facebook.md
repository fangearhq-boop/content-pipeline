### STORY 2: Pete Crow-Armstrong Joins the 40-40 Club

**Long-Form Post (10:30 AM ET)**

Forty home runs. Forty stolen bases. In the same season.

Only six players in the history of Major League Baseball had done that before Pete Crow-Armstrong did it in 2026. The list reads like a Hall of Fame exhibit: José Canseco (1988). Barry Bonds (1996). Alex Rodriguez (1998). Alfonso Soriano (2006). Ronald Acuña Jr. (2023). Shohei Ohtani (2024).

And now: Pete Crow-Armstrong, 2026, with 45 home runs and 40 stolen bases.

He is also the first Cub in franchise history — a franchise that has existed since 1876 — to achieve a 40-40 season.

The calendar does not care about milestones. The Padres swept the Cubs 2-0 in the NL Wild Card Series that concluded October 1. Crow-Armstrong went 0-for-7 with 4 strikeouts in the two games, a jarring contrast to a regular season that placed him in genuinely elite historical company.

That is baseball. Historic regular seasons and early playoff exits can share the same October. The statistics are permanent. The 40-40 will be in the record book long after this week is forgotten.

### STORY 4: 2026 Season Milestones You Might Have Missed

**Long-Form Post (3:00 PM ET)**

The Wild Card races and playoff runs dominated the September news cycle, but several remarkable individual milestones from the 2026 regular season deserve recognition as the curtain comes down on the regular season.

**Tarik Skubal, Detroit Tigers.** On July 29 at Baltimore, Skubal recorded the 1,000th strikeout of his MLB career. He has established himself as one of the most dominant starting pitchers in the American League — a career milestone that arrived quietly during a season full of noise elsewhere.

**Cristopher Sánchez, Philadelphia Phillies.** Sánchez extended a consecutive scoreless innings streak to 41⅔ innings, breaking a Phillies franchise record that had stood for 115 years. The previous holder: Grover Cleveland Alexander, who set the mark in 1911. To break a record that old is to find yourself in genuinely rare historical territory.

**Munetaka Murakami, Chicago White Sox.** In his first 21 MLB games, the Japanese slugger hit 7 home runs — breaking Shohei Ohtani's previous record of 5 home runs by a Japanese-born player through his first 21 games. He is now in the ALDS with a White Sox team one win from the ALCS.

**José Ramírez, Cleveland Guardians.** Ramírez became the first Guardian in franchise history to hit a home run against all 29 other MLB teams — a milestone that speaks to consistency, longevity, and the kind of performance that makes people forget to be properly amazed.
