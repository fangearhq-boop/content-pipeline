White Sox and Rays Both Lead ALDS 2-0, Each One Win From the ALCS

By Marcus Cole | October 6, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Two American League Division Series are unfolding identically: a team with a 2-0 lead, an opponent that needs to win three straight to survive, and a Game 3 on October 7 that has the feel of a must-win for both losers. The Chicago White Sox lead the Cleveland Guardians 2-0. The Tampa Bay Rays lead the New York Yankees 2-0. One of these stories is about a miracle. The other is a statement of dominance.

White Sox: Three Years, One Win From Everything

No team in the history of Major League Baseball made the playoffs after three consecutive 100-loss seasons before the 2026 White Sox. In 2023, they lost 101. In 2024, they lost 121 — an all-time record. In 2025, they lost 102. Each year, the organization argued the losses were intentional: clearing out the roster, drafting high, rebuilding from scratch. The 2026 season was supposed to be the first year the new core competed seriously.

It is now October 6, and they are one win from the American League Championship Series.

Chicago beat Cleveland 3-0 in ALDS Game 1 on October 3 and followed that with a 4-3 win in Game 2 on October 5. The Guardians have not led in either game for longer than fleeting moments. Game 3 is scheduled for October 7 — and Cleveland, at home, needs a win to keep the series alive.

Rays: Eliminating the Yankees Without Facing Aaron Judge

The Rays are doing something quietly extraordinary: they lead the Yankees 2-0 in the ALDS, and Aaron Judge — the reigning AL MVP, the Yankees' reason for being a contender — has not played a single postseason inning. Judge was left off the Wild Card Series roster with a right calf and soleus strain (last played September 16) and his availability for the ALDS has been uncertain throughout.

Tampa Bay won Game 1, 1-0, on October 3 and took Game 2, 5-2, on October 5. The Rays have outscored New York 6-2 in the series. Whether Judge returns for Game 3 or not, the Rays have demonstrated they do not need the Yankees to be shorthanded to win — they are winning on their own terms.

Game 3 is October 7. One win ends it.

What's Next

If both leads hold through Wednesday, the American League Championship Series would feature either the White Sox or Rays against each other or against a team from the remaining bracket. The White Sox reaching the ALCS would be one of the most improbable runs in baseball history. The Rays doing it would confirm a franchise-building model that operates at a fraction of the Yankees' payroll.

The teams facing elimination — Cleveland and New York — have had little to show for it so far. Game 3 is their last chance to change that narrative. October baseball gives them a chance to respond. The numbers so far do not suggest they will.
