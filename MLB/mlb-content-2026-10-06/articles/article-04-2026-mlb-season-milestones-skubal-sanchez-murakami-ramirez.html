2026 MLB Season Milestones: Skubal, Sánchez, Murakami and Ramírez Left Their Mark

By Marcus Cole | October 6, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The playoff storylines dominate October, but every completed season leaves behind a trail of individual achievements that deserve their own accounting. The 2026 regular season produced four milestones worth remembering — a pitcher reaching a career landmark, a streak that broke a century-old record, a newcomer who outpaced one of baseball's most famous rookies, and a veteran who completed a quiet career achievement.

Tarik Skubal's 1,000th Strikeout

On July 29 against the Baltimore Orioles, Detroit's Tarik Skubal recorded the 1,000th strikeout of his major league career. It is a milestone that marks sustained excellence — not a single flashy night, but an accumulation of quality over time that separates starting pitchers who fill innings from those who define rotations.

Skubal has established himself as one of the most reliable starting pitchers in the American League. The 1,000-strikeout milestone arrived at a pace that suggests the number will keep rising in seasons ahead.

Cristopher Sánchez Breaks a 115-Year-Old Record

Grover Cleveland Alexander set a Phillies franchise record for consecutive scoreless innings in 1911. His mark stood for 115 years. Then Cristopher Sánchez broke it in 2026, extending his scoreless streak to 41⅔ innings — a run that rewrote a section of Phillies history that had seemed permanently settled.

The feat places Sánchez in an elite category of modern pitching achievement: surpassing a record that belongs to a Hall of Famer requires sustained perfection over an extended stretch of starts. The fact that it took 115 years for anyone in Philadelphia to match Alexander — and that Sánchez did it — is worth pausing to appreciate.

Munetaka Murakami Outpaces Ohtani's Early Record

When Munetaka Murakami arrived in Chicago with the White Sox for the 2026 season, the natural comparison was to other Japanese hitters who had made the transition to MLB. The benchmark many pointed to was Shohei Ohtani's early power production — 5 home runs through his first 21 games in an MLB uniform.

Murakami hit 7 home runs in his first 21 MLB games, surpassing Ohtani's mark. He is now in the ALDS with a White Sox team one win from the ALCS — contributing to a run that has already defied every reasonable expectation for the franchise.

José Ramírez: Home Runs Against All 29 Other Teams

Cleveland's José Ramírez quietly completed a career achievement in 2026: he became the first player in Guardians franchise history to hit a home run against all 29 other MLB teams. It is the kind of milestone that reveals itself through consistency over years — not one night when everything breaks right, but a career of showing up against every opponent, in every ballpark, and delivering.

Ramírez has been one of the most durable offensive forces in the American League for nearly a decade. The all-29-teams home run milestone is a fitting marker for a career built on that consistency.

What's Next

The individual achievements of the regular season are now part of the record. What comes next — for Skubal, Sánchez, Murakami, Ramírez, and every other player who left their mark in 2026 — is the 2027 season, and whether the individual milestones translate into the team-level success that ultimately defines baseball careers.
