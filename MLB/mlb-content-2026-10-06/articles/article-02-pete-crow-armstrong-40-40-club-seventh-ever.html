Pete Crow-Armstrong Joins the 40-40 Club: Seventh in MLB History, First Cub Ever

By Jake Torres | October 6, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Baseball has existed in its modern form for roughly 150 years. In all of that time, only six players had ever finished a season with 40 or more home runs and 40 or more stolen bases. Pete Crow-Armstrong of the Chicago Cubs is now the seventh.

Crow-Armstrong finished the 2026 regular season with 45 home runs and 40 stolen bases — joining José Canseco (1988), Barry Bonds (1996), Alex Rodriguez (1998), Alfonso Soriano (2006), Ronald Acuña Jr. (2023), and Shohei Ohtani (2024) in one of baseball's most exclusive statistical societies. He is also the first Cub in franchise history to reach 40-40, a franchise that has been playing professional baseball since 1876.

The Numbers in Context

A 40-40 season requires a player to be simultaneously one of the best power hitters and one of the best baserunners in the sport. Home run totals and stolen base totals have historically peaked at different periods in the game's history — the power era and the speed era didn't always coexist. That is part of why the 40-40 remains rare even in an era of analytically optimized rosters.

Crow-Armstrong's 45 home runs and 40 stolen bases in the same season represent the kind of two-way offensive production that teams spend years trying to build around. He did it in his third full major league season.

The Playoff Contrast

The calendar does not reward individual achievement with favorable playoff draws. The Cubs were swept 2-0 by the San Diego Padres in the NL Wild Card Series that concluded October 1. Crow-Armstrong went 0-for-7 with 4 strikeouts in the two games — a postseason performance that stood in stark contrast to everything his 2026 regular season had represented.

Michael King carried a no-hitter into the seventh inning of Game 1 against Chicago, with Crow-Armstrong among those unable to solve the San Diego ace. The Cubs were held scoreless across the two-game series. Their exit was swift and complete.

It is a familiar position for franchise history: individual brilliance alongside collective disappointment. The 40-40 belongs to Crow-Armstrong and the record books. The Cubs will need to answer in the offseason how to build a team around him that advances further than the Wild Card round.

What's Next

With the regular season finished, attention shifts to the NL MVP race — a race in which Crow-Armstrong's 40-40 season makes him a significant candidate. The Cubs' postseason plans, roster construction, and offseason approach will become clear over the coming weeks.

The 40-40 is permanent. Crow-Armstrong's name is in the record book alongside players who defined their eras. What he and the Cubs do with 2027 is the question October leaves open.
