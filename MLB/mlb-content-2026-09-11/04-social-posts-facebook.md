### STORY 1: PCA 40-40 Chase

**FB Long-Form (8:00 AM)**
The window is closing. But Pete Crow-Armstrong is still very much in it.

The Cubs outfielder sits at 41 home runs and 35 stolen bases with approximately 15 games remaining in the 2026 regular season. He needs five more stolen bases to join the most exclusive club in baseball history: the 40-40 club.

The six current members — José Canseco (1988), Barry Bonds (1996), Alex Rodriguez (1998), Alfonso Soriano (2006), Ronald Acuña Jr. (2023), and Shohei Ohtani (2023) — represent some of the most complete offensive seasons ever assembled. A player reaches 40-40 not by accident, but by combining elite power with elite speed and the discipline to maintain both all season long.

Crow-Armstrong has done that. At 41 HR, he has already cleared the first bar. His 35 stolen bases, meanwhile, put him on a pace of roughly one steal every three games. He needs five more in approximately 15 games. The math is tight but it works.

If he gets there, he would be the youngest player in history to join the 40-40 club, the first Cub to hit 40 HR since Derrek Lee in 2005, and the first new member of the club since Ohtani in 2023. The NL MVP race already belongs to him at -1100 odds. The 40-40 would be the cherry on top.

Watch his legs. Every stolen base matters. The Cubs need him regardless. The history would just be a bonus.

### STORY 2: Aaron Judge — Career HR Milestone

**FB Long-Form (10:30 AM)**
Aaron Judge is healthy, back in pinstripes, and chasing one of baseball's most meaningful career milestones.

The Yankees captain returned to the lineup on September 8 after spending 99 days on the injured list with a broken rib — one of the longer absences of his career. In the days since his return, Judge is easing back in, missing September 10's game but returning to the lineup as New York navigates the final stretch of the regular season.

The milestone in view: 400 career home runs. Judge entered 2026 with approximately 385 home runs on his career ledger. With his first full healthy stretch since May 31, the 400 mark becomes realistic before this season ends or early in 2027. When he is right, Judge is the most dangerous power hitter in the American League — and arguably in all of baseball.

The Yankees, sitting at 83-62, hold the first AL Wild Card spot with roughly a three-game lead over Boston. Judge's presence in the lineup changes what the Yankees look like defensively and offensively down the stretch. His at-bats matter for the win column. The milestone chasing is secondary.

But the 400th career home run, whenever it comes, will be a moment. For a franchise that has celebrated more postseason glory than any other, it will add yet another chapter to a storied history.

### STORY 3: Yordan Alvarez Triple Crown

**FB Long-Form (12:30 PM)**
The Yordan Alvarez Triple Crown chase just got complicated in every category.

Through most of the season, Alvarez held a lead in all three American League Triple Crown categories — batting average, home runs, and RBIs. Now, with approximately 15 games remaining, each category has a legitimate challenger.

In batting average, Alvarez still leads the AL at approximately .315, with Tampa Bay's Chandler Simpson sitting 5-6 points behind at around .308. The gap is real but not comfortable.

In home runs, Junior Caminero of the Rays has pulled ahead. Caminero now leads with 39, while Alvarez has 38. One home run separates them. Both are producing for playoff contenders, meaning each will have opportunities in meaningful games.

In RBIs, Pete Alonso of the Baltimore Orioles has drawn even with Alvarez at approximately 95. Both players have been producing in high-leverage situations. A single late-inning RBI from either could tip the race.

The last American League player to win the Triple Crown was Miguel Cabrera in 2012 — fourteen seasons ago. If Alvarez wins all three categories, he would join Cabrera as the only AL Triple Crown winners of the 21st century.

He needs to hold three leads against three challengers with 15 games left. That's the challenge in front of the best hitter in the American League.

### STORY 4: Misiorowski — Still the Cy Young Standard

**FB Long-Form (3:00 PM)**
Jacob Misiorowski had the worst two-week stretch of his season. The Cubs beat him twice. His ERA ticked above the historic mark he had been guarding all year.

Then he got right.

After rough outings at Wrigley (September 2, 4 IP, 5 ER) and at American Family Field (September 8, his career-worst), Misiorowski came back with a dominant performance in his most recent start. The velocity was there. The swing-and-miss was there. The ERA settled back near 1.97, still the best mark among any qualified NL starter. His strikeout total sits around 236, still leading all of Major League Baseball.

The Cy Young odds barely moved. At -700, Misiorowski is the clear and runaway frontrunner. Chris Sale is the only other pitcher in the conversation at +500. Everyone else is a distant third or worse.

Misiorowski is 24 years old, and he has put together what any fair-minded observer would call one of the best pitcher's seasons in recent memory. An ERA under 2.00, the MLB strikeout lead, and a WHIP that was below 0.80 for most of the year — all for a Brewers team that has dominated the NL Central.

The Cubs found a way to rough him up twice. But they could not take the award. With three weeks left, the Cy Young is his to lose.
