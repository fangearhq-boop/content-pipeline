Aaron Judge Is Back — And 400 Career Home Runs Are in Sight

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | September 11, 2026

Aaron Judge is healthy, back in pinstripes, and chasing one of baseball's most meaningful career milestones.

The Yankees captain returned to action on September 8 after spending 99 days on the injured list with a broken rib — one of the longer absences of his career. He rested on September 10 as the Yankees managed his workload, but he is back in the lineup and available for the final stretch of the regular season.

The milestone in view: 400 career home runs. Judge entered the 2026 season with approximately 385 home runs on his career ledger. Through late May, he was producing at his customary pace before the rib injury shut him down. With a full healthy stretch now available before the end of 2026, and an entire 2027 season if he needs it, the 400 mark is within reach.

What 400 Means

Four hundred career home runs is a significant threshold in baseball. It is not the Hall of Fame cutoff, and it does not define a career, but it is the number that marks elite long-term production — the level that separates someone who hit a lot of home runs from someone who hit home runs at a historically sustainable rate over many years.

For Judge, reaching 400 would be another chapter in a career that has already produced one of the great individual seasons in baseball history. His 62-home run campaign in 2022 broke the American League record that had stood since Roger Maris in 1961. He has been the most feared power hitter in the American League — and by many measures in all of baseball — for the better part of a decade.

The 400 milestone would land him in company with the most productive sluggers in the sport's history. That it is coming in his mid-30s, after an injury-interrupted season, speaks to the durability of his production even in difficult years.

The Yankees Need Him Now

The milestone is secondary, though, to what the Yankees actually need from Judge in September.

New York is 83-62, holding the first American League Wild Card spot with approximately a three-game lead over the Boston Red Sox. The Yankees are in the postseason picture but not safely so, and the final two weeks of the regular season will determine whether they enter October with momentum or barely holding on.

Judge changes what the Yankees look like on both sides of the ball. In right field, his presence anchors the outfield defense. At the plate, his at-bats force opposing pitchers into decisions that affect the entire lineup — pitch to Judge carefully, and the hitters behind him benefit. Pitch to him in the zone, and the result is often a warning-track shot or worse.

His 99 days away were not simply a personal loss. They were a subtraction from the Yankees lineup that affected every game he missed. His return is an addition that will be felt immediately.

The Career Arc

Judge is in his mid-30s, at the age when most power hitters begin to decline. What makes the 400 milestone interesting is that it represents not just what he has accomplished but what he might still do. With reasonable health, 500 career home runs enters the conversation for a player of Judge's caliber and contract situation. But that is further down the road.

For now, the number is 400. And with the Yankees in a wild card race and Judge healthy enough to play, the circumstances are right for the milestone to arrive either late in 2026 or in the early weeks of next season.

What's Next

Track Judge's at-bats in September. The Yankees need wins. The milestone is approaching. Both of those things are true at the same time, and they will play out in parallel over the final two weeks of the regular season.

When the 400th career home run comes — whether it is this month or in 2027 — it will mark a significant moment for one of the game's defining players. The wait after 99 days on the injured list may feel long. But 400 is close.
