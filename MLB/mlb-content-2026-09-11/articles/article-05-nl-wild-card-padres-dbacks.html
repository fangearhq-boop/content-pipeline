The NL Wild Card Race Is Down to Two — and They Play Each Other Last

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | September 11, 2026

The National League's third wild card spot has narrowed to a two-team race, and the final say may come in the final weekend of the regular season.

The San Diego Padres currently hold the third NL Wild Card position. The Arizona Diamondbacks sit just 0.5 games behind them. The Miami Marlins, once a distant threat, have fallen four games back and are no longer a realistic factor. This is San Diego versus Arizona, and it has been for most of September.

What makes the drama almost too perfect: the Padres host the Diamondbacks at Petco Park for their final series of the 2026 regular season, September 25-27. If the two teams are anywhere near each other in the standings — and every indicator suggests they will be — that three-game set could function as a de facto play-in for the final postseason berth in the National League.

How We Got Here

The NL postseason picture at the top has been settled for some time. The Milwaukee Brewers locked up the NL Central. The Los Angeles Dodgers are NL West champions. The Atlanta Braves claimed the NL East. Those three division titles are done.

The three wild card spots have filled in behind them. The Philadelphia Phillies hold the first NL Wild Card with a comfortable margin. The Chicago Cubs sit in the second Wild Card position with approximately a five-game cushion. Those two spots are essentially locked.

The third spot is the drama. San Diego and Arizona have been trading blows for weeks. The Padres have held the spot more often than not, but the Diamondbacks have refused to fall back. At 0.5 games with approximately 15 games remaining, every series for both clubs matters enormously.

The Math Ahead

Before they play each other, both teams have games against the rest of the NL. Every game counts the same in the standings. A Padres win against a third team while Arizona loses closes the gap. A Diamondbacks win while San Diego drops a game tightens it further. The head-to-head final series will be the coda, but the three weeks leading up to it will do as much to shape the standings.

The Wild Card Series begins September 29. The regular season concludes around September 27-28. That timeline leaves almost no margin. A team that goes into the final weekend with even a one-game lead needs to be careful. A team down two games needs to sweep. The structure virtually guarantees that the Petco Park series will matter when it arrives.

What Each Team Needs

The Padres, as the team currently holding the spot, need to hold serve. They are playing at home in the final series, which gives them a meaningful advantage — Petco Park is their environment, and they know how to win there. But holding a 0.5 game lead through two more weeks is not a certainty for any team. San Diego needs to take care of its pre-final-series schedule while staying healthy and sharp enough to win when Arizona arrives.

Arizona needs to either catch San Diego in the standings before the final series or arrive at Petco Park within striking distance. A sweep of the three-game series would give the Diamondbacks the edge. Split the series and they may still fall short. The Diamondbacks have been here before — they know what elimination pressure looks like — but the margin for error is thin.

What's Next

Watch both teams' results between now and September 25. Every Padres win or Arizona loss creates a buffer. Every Padres loss or Arizona win tightens it. The standings may shift several times before the decisive series begins.

And if they arrive at Petco Park within a game of each other, the final weekend of the 2026 regular season will be must-watch baseball. The winner goes to the Wild Card round on September 29. The loser goes home. That series could decide it all.

The NL's final postseason seat will be earned on the field. It may take all 27 outs of the last weekend to determine who gets to sit in it.
