Yordan Alvarez's Triple Crown Chase Has Never Been Harder

By Ryan Calloway | Ballpark Banter | September 11, 2026

For most of the 2026 season, Yordan Alvarez held a clear lead in all three American League Triple Crown categories. He was the batting average leader, the home run leader, the RBI leader. The question was not whether he would win all three — it was how comfortable his margin would be.

That question has a different answer now.

With approximately 15 games remaining, Alvarez is still in the Triple Crown race, but each category has a legitimate challenger. The margin he built over the course of six months has eroded in the final stretch, and the award that looked like a foregone conclusion now requires a strong finish to secure.

Three Categories, Three Challengers

In batting average, Alvarez leads the American League at approximately .315. The nearest challenger is Tampa Bay's Chandler Simpson, who sits roughly 5-6 points behind at around .308. That gap is real. In 15 games, a player would need to run significantly hot to climb five points in batting average. But it is not comfortable, and a cold stretch from Alvarez while Simpson heats up could close it.

In home runs, Alvarez has been passed. Junior Caminero of the Tampa Bay Rays now leads the American League with 39 home runs, one more than Alvarez's 38. The lead changed hands recently, and it is now Alvarez who is chasing rather than defending. Caminero is producing for a Rays team in a pennant race, which means he will have every opportunity to continue. Alvarez needs to hit home runs in the coming weeks simply to get back to even — and then pass him.

In RBIs, Pete Alonso of the Baltimore Orioles has pulled even with Alvarez at approximately 95. This is the closest of the three races and the hardest to predict. Both players are performing in high-leverage situations. A single late-inning RBI from either could tip the balance, and with 15 games remaining, the final number in this category will almost certainly be close.

The Weight of What He's Chasing

The last American League player to win the Triple Crown was Miguel Cabrera in 2012. That was fourteen seasons ago. Cabrera's achievement was celebrated as one of the singular offensive accomplishments of the modern era — a reminder that winning all three categories simultaneously is not just difficult, it is exceedingly rare. In the decades since Cabrera, some of the greatest hitters in the game have come close without getting there.

If Alvarez wins all three categories, he would join Cabrera as the only American League Triple Crown winner of the 21st century. It would validate an already extraordinary season and cement his standing as the most complete hitter in the game today.

But the math has gotten harder. He needs to either maintain or retake leads in categories where challengers have closed or taken over. That is the job for the final 15 games.

What Works in His Favor

Alvarez is playing for an Astros team that is fighting to stay in first place in the AL West. Meaningful games, lineup protection, and high-leverage at-bats are all on his schedule for the rest of the season. He will not be sitting out games or batting in lower-pressure spots. The circumstances are right for him to produce.

He also has a track record of performing in September. Alvarez is not a player who tends to fade when the calendar turns to the final month. He has been one of the most consistent offensive producers in the game across multiple seasons, and that consistency is exactly what the Triple Crown chase requires.

What's Next

Watch all three statistical columns every day for the next two weeks. The batting average race requires Alvarez to stay hot while Simpson cools. The home run race requires him to pass Caminero, which means getting back to even first. The RBI race is a toss-up that will be settled on specific plate appearances in specific moments.

The Triple Crown was close to certain in June. It is a genuine competition in September. That is the nature of a long season, and it is exactly the kind of late drama that makes the final weeks of a pennant race worth watching.
