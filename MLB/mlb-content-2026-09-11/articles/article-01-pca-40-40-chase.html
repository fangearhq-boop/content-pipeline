Pete Crow-Armstrong Is Five Stolen Bases From Baseball History

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | September 11, 2026

The window is closing. But Pete Crow-Armstrong is still very much in it.

The Cubs outfielder entered September 11 with 41 home runs and 35 stolen bases, with approximately 15 games remaining in the 2026 regular season. He needs five more stolen bases to join the most exclusive club in baseball history: the 40-40 club.

Only six players have ever done it. José Canseco launched the club in 1988, when his 42 home runs and 40 stolen bases redefined what a baseball player could be. Barry Bonds followed in 1996. Alex Rodriguez in 1998. Alfonso Soriano turned the trick in 2006. Then a long drought, nearly two decades without a new member, until Ronald Acuña Jr. and Shohei Ohtani both reached 40-40 in the remarkable 2023 season.

Six players. Fifteen seasons between the fifth and sixth members. And now, in 2026, Pete Crow-Armstrong stands five stolen bases from becoming No. 7.

The Math Works

Crow-Armstrong has been running all season. His 35 stolen bases through the first 147 games of the year work out to roughly one steal every three games. With approximately 15 games remaining, he needs five more. At his seasonal pace, that requires nothing unusual — just continuation of what he has already been doing.

The home run side of the ledger is already settled. At 41, he cleared the 40 mark some time ago and has been adding to the cushion. He would need to stop hitting home runs entirely for the power half of the equation to become a concern, and that is not happening on a Cubs team pushing hard for a Wild Card berth.

What matters now is the stolen base column. Every time Crow-Armstrong reaches base, every time he sets up on first with the count going his way, the question is the same: is this the steal?

What It Would Mean

If Crow-Armstrong reaches 40-40, the historical footnotes alone are remarkable. He would be the youngest player ever to join the club. He would be the first Cub to hit 40 home runs since Derrek Lee in 2005, back when the Cubs were a different franchise and a different era of baseball. He would end a three-year drought in the 40-40 club and give it a seventh member.

And he would do it as the near-certain NL Most Valuable Player. The oddsmakers have Crow-Armstrong at -1100 in the NL MVP race, a number that reflects a season with essentially no credible challenger. The 40-40, if it comes, would be the cherry on top of an MVP campaign that was already being discussed in historic terms.

The Cubs Need Every Bit of It

Chicago is not playing for milestones. The Cubs hold the second National League Wild Card spot and are trying to hold it through the final weeks of a crowded NL race. Crow-Armstrong is central to everything they do — his combination of power, speed, and on-base skill makes him the engine of the lineup.

Every stolen base he attempts in the coming days serves two purposes simultaneously. It could extend a Cubs rally or set up a run that matters in the standings. And it could move him one step closer to history.

Those two things do not conflict. In Crow-Armstrong's case, they are the same thing.

What's Next

Watch Crow-Armstrong on the basepaths for the next three weeks. The Cubs have approximately 15 games remaining, and every time he reaches first base is a moment with historical stakes attached. Five steals. The 40-40 club. The youngest member ever. It is right there.

Whether he gets there or not, 2026 has already been a season to remember for the 23-year-old Cubs outfielder. But history is still on the table, and the window has not closed yet.
