Jacob Misiorowski Bounced Back. The Cy Young Was Never Going Anywhere.

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | September 11, 2026

Jacob Misiorowski had the worst two weeks of his 2026 season. Then he fixed it.

The Milwaukee Brewers right-hander had built one of the most dominant pitching seasons in recent memory — an ERA under 2.00 for most of the summer, strikeout totals that led all of baseball, a WHIP that hovered below 0.80. He had gone stretch after stretch without allowing more than three earned runs in a start. He looked like a pitcher in complete, almost eerie control of his craft.

Then the Cubs showed up on the schedule twice, and things got complicated.

On September 2 at Wrigley Field, Misiorowski lasted just four innings, giving up five earned runs. It ended a long streak of quality starts and pushed his ERA above the historic level he had been protecting. On September 8, pitching at American Family Field, Chicago roughed him up again in what became his worst outing of the season.

Two bad starts. Two Cubs victories. And then Misiorowski got right.

The Bounce-Back

In his most recent start following the rough stretch, Misiorowski was dominant. Reports from the outing indicate he went 6.1 innings, gave up one earned run, and struck out nine batters. The velocity was back. The swing-and-miss was back. The command that made him so difficult to handle all season was back.

His ERA settled back near 1.97, still the best mark among any qualified National League starter. His strikeout total sits around 236, still leading all of Major League Baseball. His WHIP remains exceptional. The two Cubs starts were a dip in a long, dominant season — not a trend, not a crisis, not a sign of something breaking down.

Misiorowski corrected it in one start. That, too, is what elite pitchers do.

The Award Is His

The National League Cy Young market has not moved. Misiorowski remains the overwhelming favorite at -700. Chris Sale is the only other pitcher in serious conversation, listed at +500. Everyone else is a distant third at best.

A -700 Cy Young price is not a forecast of a close race. It is an acknowledgment that barring a historic collapse — multiple more outings like September 2, a significant injury, something dramatic — the award is going to Milwaukee. The two Cubs starts were rough, and they barely moved the number. That is how much of a lead Misiorowski built over the course of the first five months of the season.

He is 24 years old. He has a 14-5 record. His ERA has led the NL for virtually the entire season. He strikes out more batters than anyone in baseball. And he has shown, in his most recent start, that when the Cubs finally got to him, it was an interruption rather than a turning point.

The Brewers Picture

Milwaukee enters the final three weeks of the regular season at 89-56, firmly in command of the National League Central. The Brewers have the best record in the division and have been one of the dominant teams in the National League all year. Misiorowski is the centerpiece of a rotation that has made that possible.

The Cubs, for all their success in roughing him up over two starts, are chasing Milwaukee in the standings. That context matters. Misiorowski has been winning games that mattered throughout the season, not just accumulating statistics on a mediocre team. The individual numbers are extraordinary. They come with team success behind them.

What's Next

Misiorowski has approximately three starts left in the regular season. Each one is an opportunity to either hold his numbers or improve them heading into the Cy Young vote. His ERA is historic. His strikeout total is historic. All he has to do is keep pitching the way he has pitched for the previous 27 starts before September 2.

The bounce-back start suggested he can do exactly that.

The Cy Young Award will go to Milwaukee in 2026. The Cubs found a way to beat Misiorowski twice. They could not beat the season he is having, and they could not take the award. Three weeks left. It is his.
