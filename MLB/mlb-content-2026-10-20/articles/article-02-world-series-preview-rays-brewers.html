World Series Preview: Rays vs. Brewers — No. 1 Seeds, No Champions Yet

By Ryan Calloway | October 20, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

For the first time in recent memory, the 2026 World Series is guaranteed to end with a team hoisting the Commissioner's Trophy for the very first time.

The Tampa Bay Rays, who clinched the American League pennant Monday night with a 7-3 win over Cleveland in Game 6, will face the Milwaukee Brewers starting Friday, October 23 at American Family Field. Both teams entered the 2026 season as pennant favorites. Both are No. 1 seeds. Neither has ever won a World Series.

One of them will in the next two weeks.

The Brewers: Baseball's Best Team

Milwaukee's path to the World Series was relentless. The Brewers won 103 games during the regular season, the best record in baseball, and carried that momentum through October without slowing down. They swept the San Diego Padres in four games in the NLDS. They swept the defending champion Los Angeles Dodgers in four games in the NLCS, rallying from a 4-0 deficit in the clinching Game 4 to win 6-4 at Dodger Stadium.

Jake Bauers, the first baseman who nearly retired before the 2026 season, was named NLCS Most Valuable Player. Jackson Chourio hit his sixth career postseason home run during the NLCS, breaking the franchise's all-time record. Brice Turang drove in 100 runs during the regular season. Manager Pat Murphy built a team that was never behind when it mattered most.

The Brewers' last World Series was in 1982, when they lost to the St. Louis Cardinals in seven games. That franchise has been waiting 44 years for another shot at the title.

The Rays: Built for October

Tampa Bay's journey was different but just as compelling. They beat the New York Yankees in the American League Division Series before grinding past a Cleveland Guardians team that had already knocked out the Chicago White Sox in a comeback ALDS. The Rays have been here before — 2008 (lost to Philadelphia) and 2020 (lost to the Dodgers) — and left both times without the title.

What makes this version of Tampa Bay formidable is October pitching. Drew Rasmussen posted a 1.29 ERA over 15 postseason innings before the World Series even began. The bullpen has been reliable. They don't beat themselves.

What's Next

Game 1 is Friday, October 23 at American Family Field in Milwaukee (8PM ET, FOX). Games 1 and 2 are in Milwaukee, followed by Games 3 through 5 in Tampa Bay (Oct. 26-28). If necessary, Games 6 and 7 return to Milwaukee on October 30 and 31. The entire series airs on FOX.

The probable Game 1 pitching matchup — Drew Rasmussen for Tampa Bay against Jacob Misiorowski for Milwaukee — is one of the better duels the World Series has seen in years. Both are two-time All-Stars. Both were dominant in 2026. The team that wins that first game may set the tone for everything that follows.

After 57 years of waiting in Milwaukee and three near-misses in Tampa Bay, someone is finally going to win it all. The World Series starts Friday night.
