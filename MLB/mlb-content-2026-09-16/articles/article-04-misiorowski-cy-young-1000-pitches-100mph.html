The First Pitcher Ever to Throw 1,000 Pitches at 100 MPH Is Also the Best in Baseball

By Ryan Calloway | Ballpark Banter | September 16, 2026

Jacob Misiorowski's 2026 season leads all MLB starters in ERA and strikeouts — and he just crossed a threshold no pitcher has ever reached in the tracking era.

Jacob Misiorowski pitched against the Pittsburgh Pirates on Tuesday for the Milwaukee Brewers, who lead the National League Central at 93-57. He is 19-8 with a 1.95 ERA — the best mark in Major League Baseball. He has 236 strikeouts — also the best in baseball. And at 24 years old, he has become the first pitcher in the Statcast tracking era to throw 1,000 pitches at 100 miles per hour or faster in a single season.

His Cy Young odds sit at -700. There is no serious case against him at this point in the season.

The Milestone That Defines the Season

One thousand pitches at 100 miles per hour or faster. That number requires context: most elite fastball pitchers reach 100 mph on select occasions, when the game situation demands maximum effort or when the arm is fresh in early innings. Sustaining that velocity across the full breadth of a start, in start after start, across an entire season, to a four-digit pitch count — that is something different. The Statcast tracking system has measured pitch velocity since 2015. No pitcher had ever reached 1,000 pitches at 100+ mph in a single season before 2026. Misiorowski did it.

The velocity story is inseparable from the strikeout story. His 236 Ks lead all MLB pitchers, and he is within striking distance of franchise history. Ben Sheets set the Brewers' single-season strikeout record in 2004 with 264. Misiorowski needs 28 more to surpass it. With approximately five or six starts remaining, the record is in play.

The Team Around Him

The Brewers' 93-57 record reflects the organizational depth that surrounds Misiorowski, but it also reflects what it means to have the best pitcher in baseball at the top of a rotation. Games he starts are games the Brewers expect to win. He has delivered on that expectation across 27 starts with a consistency that has made Milwaukee's division title feel inevitable since July.

The NL No. 1 seed and first-round bye are locked in. The Brewers will wait for the wild card round to conclude before playing in October. That rest — combined with Misiorowski potentially available to start Game 1 of the NL Division Series on full rest — is as good a position as any team in baseball can ask for entering the postseason.

What's Next

The regular-season stretch run is about two things for Misiorowski: protecting the ERA lead and chasing Sheets' strikeout record. Each start from here is a piece of history being written. When the postseason begins, he will step to the front of the Brewers' rotation as the best pitcher in baseball — a pitcher who has spent six months proving, in numbers that have never been posted before, that the designation is accurate.
