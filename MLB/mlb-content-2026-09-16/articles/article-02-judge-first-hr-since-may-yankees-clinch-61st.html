Aaron Judge Ends 114-Day HR Drought as Yankees Clinch Record 61st Postseason Berth

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | September 16, 2026

Judge launched a 415-foot, 3-run home run in the 8th inning Tuesday — his first since May 24 — and the Yankees beat Minnesota 8-3 to clinch their 61st trip to the postseason.

His last home run before Tuesday night was on May 24. Between then and now, Aaron Judge spent 99 days on the injured list with a rib stress fracture, returned to the lineup on September 8, played several games without leaving the yard — and then on Tuesday, in the 8th inning against the Minnesota Twins, launched a ball 415 feet for a 3-run home run.

The Yankees won 8-3. They clinched their 61st postseason berth in franchise history — a record. It was their 9th trip to the playoffs in the last 10 seasons.

The return is complete.

What 114 Days Looks Like

Judge has 18 home runs this season — a total suppressed by an injury that wiped out his entire second half until Sept. 8. In a full, healthy season he would be one of the primary HR threats in the American League. Instead, he spent the summer rehabilitating a rib stress fracture while the Yankees tried to stay afloat without him.

They did. The Yankees held their position in the AL Wild Card race through the summer, relying on the rest of the roster to carry the load. Judge returned September 8 to a standing ovation at Yankee Stadium. He was 0-for-3 that day. He was working himself back.

Tuesday was different. A 3-run home run in the 8th inning of a clinching win is not an at-bat that requires analysis. It is the kind of moment that announces a player's return to form — a statement hit in a meaningful game, 415 feet, and a 8-3 final that sent the Yankees to October again.

Franchise History Made

The record 61st postseason berth is worth sitting with for a moment. The Yankees have been to the playoffs more times than any franchise in American professional sports. That record — the 61st — is theirs alone now. And they clinched it with the best player in their history returning to do what he does best.

Nine postseason appearances in 10 seasons reflects organizational consistency of the highest order. Individual seasons, individual players, individual injuries — they can derail a window. For New York, the window has stayed open regardless.

What's Next

The Yankees still have approximately 10 regular-season games to play. Judge's health and form will be the most-watched storyline entering October — a postseason run requires a fully healthy Judge in the middle of their lineup, and Tuesday's home run was the first sign since May that the version of him who makes lineups dangerous is back. The Yankees have their postseason berth. The question now is how deep they go — and how much of the Aaron Judge who hit that ball 415 feet Tuesday shows up to answer it.
