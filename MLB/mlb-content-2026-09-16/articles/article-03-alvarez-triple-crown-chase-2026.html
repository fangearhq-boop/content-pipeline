The Triple Crown Nobody's Talking About: Yordan Alvarez and the Last 10 Games of His Season

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | September 16, 2026

Alvarez leads the AL in batting average, trails in home runs by two and is within striking distance in RBI — with 10 games left to close the gap and make history nobody has made since Cabrera did it in 2012.

The last American League Triple Crown winner was Miguel Cabrera in 2012. He hit .330, swatted 44 home runs, and drove in 139 runs for the Detroit Tigers. He did it in a season when people were watching for it, celebrating it, and writing about it every day down the stretch. That was 14 years ago.

Yordan Alvarez is currently leading the American League in batting average at .311. He has 38 home runs. He is within range in RBI. With approximately 10 regular-season games left on the schedule, the math for a Triple Crown still works — barely, and narrowly, but it works.

Where He Stands

The batting average lead is not in question. Alvarez's .311 mark has held up as the best in the American League for weeks. That is not nothing — batting titles are not easy to win, and maintaining a lead this late into September reflects the consistency of a hitter who does not go through extended slumps when it matters most.

The home run picture is more complicated. Yandy Caminero of the Tampa Bay Rays hit his 40th home run in walk-off fashion, putting him two home runs ahead of Alvarez at 40-38. Closing a two-homer gap in 10 games is possible for a power hitter of Alvarez's caliber, but it requires production while Caminero simultaneously stalls. Neither is guaranteed.

RBI is the category where the math still works most cleanly. Pete Alonso of Baltimore leads the American League, and the gap is closeable with a strong final 10 games. Alvarez is the type of hitter who drives in runs in bunches — one big series could move the needle significantly.

The Historical Weight

There is a reason the Triple Crown has been won only once in the last 14 years. It requires three things — contact, power, and run production — to peak simultaneously across a 162-game sample. For any one of the three to align with the other two at season's end requires a level of sustained excellence that almost no player achieves. Cabrera in 2012 was the exception. Before him, Carl Yastrzemski won the last AL Triple Crown in 1967 — and before that, Frank Robinson in 1966.

Alvarez is one of the few hitters in the game capable of putting all three categories together. That he is within range heading into the final week is not luck. It is the product of a season in which he has been among the best hitters in baseball.

What's Next

Every Alvarez at-bat from here carries two storylines: what it means for the Astros' playoff positioning and what it means for the triple crown count. The next 10 games will settle it. If the numbers come together — if Alvarez passes Caminero in HR, closes the gap in RBI, and holds the batting average lead — he will have accomplished something no American League hitter has done in 14 years. That is the story worth watching at the end of this regular season.
