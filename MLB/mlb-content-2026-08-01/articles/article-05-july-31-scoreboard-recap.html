July 31 MLB Recap: Sal Stewart Walk-Off, Ohtani Homers, Ramirez Makes History

By Jake Torres

August 1, 2026

Friday night across Major League Baseball delivered two finishes worth talking about, a milestone home run, and a standings development in the American League Central. Here is how July 31 wrapped up.

Stewart Walk-Off: Reds 8, Pirates 7

The night's most dramatic moment came in Cincinnati, where Sal Stewart hit a walk-off two-run home run in the bottom of the ninth inning to give the Reds an 8-7 win over the Pittsburgh Pirates. Stewart was the story of the night for the Reds — he drove in four runs total in the game, capping the evening with the walk-off that gave Cincinnati the comeback victory.

The win was exactly the kind of late-game drama that makes summer baseball. The Pirates had control of the game heading into the final frame, but Stewart ended it in the most decisive way possible.

Red Sox Over Dodgers — Ohtani Hits No. 23

The marquee matchup of the night went to Boston. The Red Sox defeated the Los Angeles Dodgers in a game that featured one of the biggest individual highlights of the evening: Shohei Ohtani hit home run No. 23 of the season as Los Angeles's designated hitter. Even in a loss, Ohtani continued to do what he has done all season as a DH — hit the ball hard and often. His 23rd home run is a reminder that whatever is happening with his pitching return, his bat has not missed a beat.

The Red Sox win was the result, but Ohtani's home run was the moment.

White Sox Acquire Urquidy — Lead the AL Central at 57-51

The Chicago White Sox made a deadline move ahead of Sunday's 6 PM ET cutoff, acquiring starter Jose Urquidy from the Pittsburgh Pirates to add to their rotation. With the addition, the White Sox sit at 57-51, holding first place in the American League Central. It is one of the more unexpected standings stories of the 2026 season — a team that has rebuilt faster than almost anyone projected now in legitimate postseason position heading into August.

The Urquidy pickup is a statement that Chicago intends to stay there.

Ramirez Makes History

Jose Ramirez hit a home run on July 31 that moved him into a category no Cleveland Guardian has ever occupied: he became the first player in franchise history to hit a home run against all 29 other MLB clubs. Ramirez has been one of the most consistent performers in baseball for the better part of a decade, and milestones like this one put a number on just how long he has been delivering at an elite level. Hitting a home run against every other team in baseball requires not just power, but durability and longevity — the kind of sustained presence in the lineup that only a handful of players manage.

What's Next

August 1 begins the final stretch of the regular season with the trade deadline 48 hours away. The White Sox will look to hold their AL Central lead while the Reds, buoyed by Stewart's walk-off, return to action. Ohtani and the Dodgers regroup ahead of Sunday's deadline, where Los Angeles's pursuit of Tarik Skubal could define their October. The next two days will be among the most active in the baseball calendar.
