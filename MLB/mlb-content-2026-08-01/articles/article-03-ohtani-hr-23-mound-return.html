Ohtani Hits HR No. 23 as DH — Mound Return Could Come as Early as This Weekend

By Ryan Calloway

August 1, 2026

Shohei Ohtani hit home run No. 23 of the season on July 31, a deep shot as the Los Angeles Dodgers' designated hitter against the Boston Red Sox. The Red Sox won the game, but Ohtani's bat — which has remained one of the most feared in baseball regardless of what's happening with his arm — delivered another reminder of what he is as an offensive force.

The bigger story, though, remains on the mound. Ohtani has not pitched since July 3. And on Saturday, Dodgers manager Dave Roberts offered the first concrete hope of a return: he said he hopes Ohtani can "start throwing, playing catch again and building up" as early as this weekend — the August 1–3 window.

Two Issues, One Timeline

Ohtani has been managing two separate physical concerns. He has left knee soreness and a biceps issue. Neither has been severe enough to warrant an IL placement — he has remained in the lineup as a designated hitter — but both have kept him off the mound for four full weeks.

Roberts' framing on Saturday was careful. The word "hope" was doing a lot of work. He said he hopes Ohtani can start throwing — not that he will. The distinction matters for a player managing a biceps problem, where a progression from playing catch to full bullpen sessions to live hitters to game action takes weeks to build properly.

What the statement does suggest is that the Dodgers believe Ohtani's issues have not gotten worse, and that the path toward resuming activity is at least visible on the horizon. That is more than could be said two weeks ago.

Why the Dodgers Are Chasing Skubal

The direct line from Ohtani's pitching uncertainty to the trade deadline is impossible to ignore. The Dodgers are among the most aggressive bidders for Detroit Tigers ace Tarik Skubal, who ESPN reports is going to be traded before the August 3 deadline. Skubal's acquisition would give Los Angeles a true No. 1 starter — a two-time AL Cy Young winner — to anchor a rotation that, without a pitching Ohtani, has a significant gap at the top.

Even if Ohtani begins throwing this weekend and builds back toward full health, the Dodgers' timeline for having him as a reliable starting pitcher in October is compressed. A throwing session in early August needs to progress to a bullpen, then to a simulated game, then to live action — all while keeping both the knee and the biceps healthy. The math is tight. Landing Skubal provides insurance regardless of how Ohtani's recovery unfolds.

What's Next

Watch for any update this weekend on whether Ohtani has begun throwing. Even a brief update from Roberts confirming a light catch session would be a meaningful step and would shift the narrative from "uncertain" to "on track." His next scheduled DH appearance will continue to give the Dodgers lineup its most dangerous bat while the pitching recovery unfolds. The August 3 trade deadline — and whatever the Dodgers land — will give a clearer picture of how the organization is planning for October with and without Ohtani on the mound.
