Dylan Cease Is the AL's Pitcher of July — and the Cy Young Case Is Real

By Jake Torres

August 1, 2026

When the Toronto Blue Jays signed Dylan Cease as a free agent after the 2025 season, the expectation was a good pitcher on a good team. What has unfolded instead is one of the more compelling pitching stories in the American League — and a genuine AL Cy Young Award case that is getting harder to dismiss.

Cease is 6-4 with a 2.56 ERA and 1.13 WHIP in 98.1 innings across 17 starts. He has struck out 148 batters. And in July, he was the best pitcher in baseball by any strikeout measure: 49 strikeouts in six starts, the most of any pitcher in the major leagues during the month.

The All-Star Distinction

The Blue Jays sent Cease to the All-Star Game as the American League's starting pitcher — a recognition of what he had become in the first half of 2026. That appointment came with a historical footnote: Cease became the first Toronto Blue Jays pitcher to start the MLB All-Star Game since Roy Halladay did it in 2009.

Halladay is in the Baseball Hall of Fame. The company is elite. The fact that Cease stepped into that lineage is not just a resume line — it is a signal of how the baseball world views what he has done this season.

Building the Cy Young Case

AL Cy Young Award voting requires comparing Cease against his peers. The pitcher most often mentioned alongside him in this race has been Tarik Skubal of the Detroit Tigers, who has posted a 2.79 ERA with 116 strikeouts in 16 starts. Skubal is a two-time defending AL Cy Young winner.

But here is where the 2026 trade deadline changes the math: ESPN is reporting that Skubal IS getting traded before the August 3 deadline. Once Skubal moves mid-season to a new team, his full-season numbers will still count for award consideration — but the context shifts. Cease, by contrast, will have been a Blue Jay from opening day through the final out of the regular season. His consistency over the full season is a legitimate argument.

The ERA comparison (Cease at 2.56 vs. Skubal at 2.79) favors Cease. The strikeout total comparison (Cease 148 vs. Skubal 116 in fewer innings) favors Cease. The All-Star Game starting assignment went to Cease. And his July — 49 K in six starts — was the kind of sustained dominance that tends to stick with voters.

The Second Half Matters

Cy Young races are not decided in August. Voters will look at the final numbers in late September and weigh the full season in aggregate. Cease needs to sustain his level of performance across the second half. If he does — if the ERA stays under 3.00 and the strikeout pace holds — he will be a serious candidate regardless of what Skubal does in the postseason push.

Toronto is a legitimate contender, which means Cease will be pitching in meaningful games with everything to prove. That context only helps his case.

What's Next

Cease's next scheduled start will come in the first week of August. His second-half performance will be the central storyline for the Blue Jays and for the AL Cy Young race. Track his ERA, WHIP, and strikeout rate against Skubal's numbers — now in a new uniform — as the season moves toward its conclusion. If the numbers hold, the Cy Young case becomes the argument.
