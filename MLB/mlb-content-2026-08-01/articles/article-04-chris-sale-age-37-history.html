Chris Sale at 37 Is Making the History Books — The Numbers Are Remarkable

By Marcus Cole

August 1, 2026

Chris Sale turned 37 years old this past March. By most conventional baseball timelines, a pitcher of that age should be managing a decline — adjusting, leaning on experience, protecting innings. Sale is doing none of that. He is pitching some of the best baseball of his career, and the statistical company he now occupies is almost impossibly elite.

The Atlanta Braves left-hander is 12-6 with a 2.08 ERA, 1.02 WHIP, and 143 strikeouts in 117 innings across 20 starts. Over his last 12 starts, his ERA is 1.96. He was selected to his 10th career All-Star Game this season — a milestone that speaks to his sustained excellence across nearly a decade and a half at the major league level.

The Historical Context

Sale's 2.08 ERA is the third-lowest posted by any pitcher age 37 or older since 1950. The two pitchers ahead of him on that list are not just Hall of Famers — they are two of the greatest pitchers of any era. Roger Clemens posted a 1.40 ERA for the Houston Astros in 2005 at age 42. Justin Verlander posted a 1.73 ERA for the Houston Astros in 2022 at age 39.

Sale, at 37, sits in that company. That is not a minor statistical footnote. The list of pitchers who have posted sub-2.10 ERAs at or past age 37 since the middle of the 20th century is extraordinarily short — and Sale has now joined it.

The second milestone adds to the historical picture: Sale became the fastest pitcher in MLB history to reach 2,500 career strikeouts by innings pitched. The combination of strikeout volume and efficiency over his career has placed him in a statistical category that no pitcher has reached faster.

What It Means for Atlanta

For the Braves, Sale's 2026 season is the anchor of their deadline approach. Atlanta is a buyer entering the final 48 hours of the trade window — a contending team in the National League with a rotation built around one of the best pitchers in baseball. That position is only possible because Sale has performed at this level through the first four months of the season.

The Braves can pursue whatever additional pieces they want at the deadline with the confidence that their top starting pitcher is not a liability. He is the reason they are a contender. A 12-6 record and a 2.08 ERA from your ace heading into August is the kind of performance that makes a front office aggressive.

The Age Narrative

It is worth pausing on the pure anomaly of what Sale is doing. Most pitchers are finished, or declining sharply, by 37. The combination of strikeout volume — 143 K across 20 starts — and efficiency (2.08 ERA) at this age is not what the aging curve predicts. Sale has managed his body through significant injury history across his career, and he has arrived at 37 in perhaps his best sustained form since his Chicago White Sox peak.

Clemens at 42 and Verlander at 39 are the only comparisons since 1950. Neither of those names diminishes Sale. They frame, in the most historically precise terms possible, just how rare what he is doing actually is.

What's Next

Sale's next start is expected in the first week of August. The Braves' deadline additions — whatever they end up being — will fill out the roster around the pitcher who has made Atlanta's October ambitions possible. If Sale maintains this pace through September, he will be among the most serious NL Cy Young Award contenders and a legitimate candidate for any end-of-season accolade given to the best pitchers in baseball. Watch for his ERA as the second half unfolds.
