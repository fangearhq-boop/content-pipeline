## STORY 1: Trade Deadline — Skubal

### Long-Form Post
The clock is ticking. The MLB Trade Deadline is 48 hours away — Sunday, August 3 at 6 PM ET — and the biggest name still on the board is Tarik Skubal.

ESPN is now reporting that the Tigers ace IS getting traded. That's a significant shift from "likely" to confirmed-in-reporting. Skubal has put together one of the best pitching seasons in the American League: 2.79 ERA, 0.91 WHIP, 116 strikeouts in 96.2 innings across 16 starts — and he's a two-time AL Cy Young Award winner. The Dodgers, Yankees, Braves, Cubs, and Phillies are all listed as suitors. Detroit even scratched starter Casey Mize from Friday's scheduled start, potentially as a piece of the trade package.

While the Skubal decision looms, the market has already moved on other names. Here's what's already done ahead of the deadline: Minter to the Twins, O'Hoppe and Silseth to the Rangers, Urquidy to the White Sox, Pearson to the Royals, Halvorsen to the Dodgers, Civale to the Cubs, and Marinaccio to the Pirates.

And more players remain in play. Freddy Peralta (Mets, 4.99 ERA this season) was scratched from his own Friday start as trade discussions "intensified" around him. Mason Miller — the Padres closer with 28 saves in 28 chances, a 0.79 ERA, and 85 strikeouts in 45.2 innings — is another premium name generating significant interest.

The next 48 hours will define how multiple contenders' October ambitions take shape. Who wins the deadline?

## STORY 2: Dylan Cease

### Long-Form Post
It's time to talk seriously about Dylan Cease and the AL Cy Young Award.

In his first season with the Toronto Blue Jays, Cease has become one of the best pitchers in baseball. He's 6-4 with a 2.56 ERA, 1.13 WHIP, and 148 strikeouts across 17 starts. He led all of Major League Baseball with 49 strikeouts in the month of July — in just six starts.

The Blue Jays selected Cease as their AL All-Star Game starter, making him the first Toronto pitcher to start the Midsummer Classic since Roy Halladay did it in 2009. That's 17 years between ASG starters for a franchise with a long pitching tradition — and Cease earned it.

Now, with Tarik Skubal almost certainly heading to a contender via trade, the AL Cy Young conversation shifts. Skubal has been the other headliner in this race. If he finishes the season with a new team in a limited role, Cease's full-season numbers — especially his July dominance — could carry even more weight with voters.

The case is real. The second half starts now.

## STORY 3: Ohtani

### Long-Form Post
Shohei Ohtani did what Shohei Ohtani does on July 31 — he hit a home run. His 23rd of the season, as a designated hitter, in the Dodgers' game against the Red Sox. The Red Sox won the game, but Ohtani's bat remained as dangerous as ever.

But the bigger storyline remains on the mound.

Ohtani has not pitched since July 3. He's been managing both left knee soreness and a biceps issue — neither of which has led to an IL placement, but neither of which has cleared enough for him to pitch. Dodgers manager Dave Roberts said Saturday that he hopes Ohtani can "start throwing, playing catch again and building up" as early as this weekend (August 1–3). That's an encouraging sign, but it's also a hope, not a certainty.

The Dodgers' aggressive pursuit of Tarik Skubal is directly connected to this uncertainty. They cannot enter October counting on Ohtani as a starter. Getting Skubal — a two-time AL Cy Young winner — would give them the ace-level insurance they need. Whether Ohtani ultimately pitches in the postseason is the biggest question hanging over the defending champions.

## STORY 4: Chris Sale

### Long-Form Post
Chris Sale is 37 years old and putting together one of the most historically rare pitching seasons of the modern era.

The Atlanta Braves left-hander is 12-6 with a 2.08 ERA, 1.02 WHIP, and 143 strikeouts in 117 innings across 20 starts. His ERA over his last 12 starts is an even more remarkable 1.96. He was named to his 10th career All-Star Game this season.

But here's the number that puts Sale in all-time company: that 2.08 ERA is the third-lowest posted by any pitcher age 37 or older since 1950. The two pitchers ahead of him on that list? Roger Clemens, who posted a 1.40 ERA for the Houston Astros in 2005 at age 42, and Justin Verlander, who posted a 1.73 ERA for the Houston Astros in 2022 at age 39. Sale sits alongside two of the greatest pitchers in baseball history in one of the most elite statistical categories you can name.

He also became the fastest pitcher in MLB history to reach 2,500 career strikeouts by innings pitched.

For Atlanta — a team that needs to be a buyer at the August 3 deadline — Sale's performance is the anchor that makes the math work. He's not just good. He's historically good.

## STORY 5: July 31 Scoreboard

### Long-Form Post
A great night of baseball on July 31 across the league.

And Jose Ramirez hit a milestone — he became the first Cleveland Guardians player in franchise history to hit a home run against all 29 other MLB clubs. A neat achievement for one of the game's most consistent performers.
