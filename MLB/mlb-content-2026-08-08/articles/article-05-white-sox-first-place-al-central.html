The White Sox Are 59-52 and in First Place. This Turnaround Is Not a Fluke.

By Jake Torres

August 8, 2026

The 2024 Chicago White Sox went 41-121. It was the worst record in the history of modern Major League Baseball — a season that became a symbol for how completely an organization's player development pipeline can fail at the same time as its major league roster. The number 121 losses was the record. The conversation was about how bad it had gotten.

The 2026 Chicago White Sox are 59-52 and leading the American League Central.

What Changed

The rebuild was not invisible. The pieces were being assembled through 2024 and 2025 — a 60-102 season in 2025 that still represented a 19-win improvement from the year before. The process was real, even when the results were painful. Colson Montgomery emerged as a legitimate franchise centerpiece, hitting with the kind of power and consistency that earns consideration at the top of a batting order. The pitching staff, assembled from a combination of developed prospects and targeted acquisitions, held up better than outside projections suggested it would.

The trade deadline confirmed that the organization believed what the standings were showing. The White Sox were buyers. They acquired Luis Castillo and Doyle — players added not to fill a hole for a wild card race, but to complement a roster that had already earned first place. That decision carries meaning. You don't add at the deadline unless you believe you can go somewhere in October.

The Context of 59-52

Winning 59 games through August 8 while leading a division is not an extraordinary pace by historical standards. The AL Central is a competitive division and the race is not over. The Cleveland Guardians and Minnesota Twins are not going away. What makes 59-52 significant is not the absolute record — it is the trajectory and the organizational investment behind it.

From 41-121 to first place in two seasons is one of the most compressed turnarounds in recent baseball history. The only comparison points are teams that had extraordinary draft luck or made a single transformational trade that changed their competitive window overnight. The White Sox have done neither. They built through the system, absorbed the losses, added at the margins, and now find themselves in a position that almost no one outside their own building predicted entering the season.

What's Next

The White Sox continue their schedule through August with the AL Central lead in hand. Updated standings are available daily at mlb.com. Luis Castillo's role in the rotation, Colson Montgomery's development, and the performances of the deadline additions will all be tracked as the season moves into its final six weeks. If the White Sox hold this position into September, the conversation about this turnaround will shift from remarkable to historic.
