Seven Straight: The Dodgers' Losing Streak Has Reached a Level Nobody Expected

By Marcus Cole

August 8, 2026

Corbin Carroll Waldschmidt hit a 2-run walk-off home run off Yennier Díaz in the bottom of the final inning Thursday night in Arizona, and the Los Angeles Dodgers dropped their seventh consecutive game. The final score was 4-3. The Diamondbacks cleared the field. The Dodgers boarded a plane home with a losing streak that now stands as their longest since 2017.

Seven in a row. For the team that spent more than anyone in baseball this offseason, that number requires some explanation.

What's Missing

Shohei Ohtani is still not throwing. The elbow issue that has kept him limited to designated hitter duties has removed one of the most compelling offseason additions from the equation — not from the lineup, where Ohtani continues to hit, but from the mound, where the Dodgers had hoped to eventually deploy him in some capacity. That possibility is off the table for now.

Blake Snell is expected to return from the injured list on August 11. Tarik Skubal, acquired from Detroit at the trade deadline, is the other piece of the pitching puzzle the Dodgers assembled this summer. On paper, the rotation is still formidable. In practice, the team has lost seven consecutive games and the wins have stopped coming regardless of the opponent.

The Streak in Context

The 2017 reference point matters. That Los Angeles team still won 104 games and reached the World Series, losing to the Houston Astros in seven games. A midseason losing streak that ends before September is not a death sentence for a team with the Dodgers' talent. The question is whether what's happening now is a statistical blip — the kind of rough patch every team encounters in a 162-game season — or an indication that the roster is not functioning the way the front office planned.

Waldschmidt's home run was the fifth walk-off event in this seven-game stretch, a sign that games have been competitive but have consistently slipped away at the end. That is both more encouraging and more frustrating than being blown out. The Dodgers are close. They are just not closing.

What's Next

Los Angeles returns home after the Arizona series. Blake Snell's scheduled return on August 11 will be the first real test of whether the rotation depth can change the outcomes. Snell's addition, combined with Skubal and a healthy bullpen, gives the Dodgers the pieces to end this stretch quickly. Whether the offense can sustain rallies and the bullpen can hold leads in close games is the operational question that Thursday night did not answer. The Dodgers have the talent to turn this around. Seven games in August is not the last word — but it is a real streak from a team that had bigger plans for this month.
