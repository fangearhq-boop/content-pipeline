Yordan Alvarez's Triple Crown Chase: 15 Games to Catch Caminero and Alonso

By Marcus Cole | September 12, 2026

Houston Astros

Yordan Alvarez leads the American League in batting average. He does not lead the AL in home runs or RBI — yet. With approximately 15 games left in the regular season, the Houston Astros first baseman is in a position no AL hitter has been in since 2012: fighting for all three Triple Crown categories at once. To win it, he has to catch two players who are holding the leads he needs.

The math: Junior Caminero of the Tampa Bay Rays has 39 home runs to Alvarez's 38. Pete Alonso of the Baltimore Orioles has approximately 97 RBI to Alvarez's 95. Alvarez leads both by a comfortable margin in batting average at approximately .315. He has the hardest lead and needs to overturn two trails to complete the picture.

The Historical Context

The last American League Triple Crown winner was Miguel Cabrera, who hit .330 with 44 home runs and 139 RBI for the Detroit Tigers in 2012 — a season so dominant it earned him the AL MVP in a landslide. Before Cabrera, you had to go back to Carl Yastrzemski in 1967 for an AL Triple Crown. A 45-year gap separated those two winners. Alvarez would be the third AL Triple Crown winner since 1967 — and at Alvarez's age and trajectory, there's an argument it would rank alongside either of those performances.

Alvarez has had a complicated September by his own standards. His power and RBI production slowed after August — three home runs and 13 RBI since the beginning of the month — which opened the door for Caminero to take the HR lead and allowed Alonso to hold the RBI advantage. The batting average, however, has remained elite throughout.

The Competition

Caminero has been one of the surprises of the 2026 season. The 21-year-old Rays outfielder has posted 39 home runs in what is his first full major league season, and his power has held throughout September. For Alvarez to retake the HR lead, he needs to hit more home runs than Caminero over the remaining games — a sprint that comes down to individual at-bat quality more than any strategic adjustment.

Alonso's 97 RBI keeps him two ahead. The Orioles are in a playoff race of their own, meaning Alonso will continue to bat in meaningful situations with runners on base. Alvarez needs to close those two RBI with the Astros still playing meaningful games.

What's Next

Track Alvarez's home run and RBI totals daily as the Astros play their final 15 games. If Caminero cools off and Alonso's RBI pace drops, the window opens. If Alvarez heats up and the Astros give him premium at-bats with runners on base, the gap closes. The Triple Crown is not decided until the final out of the regular season — and with Cabrera 2012 and Yastrzemski 1967 as the precedent, every game counts.
