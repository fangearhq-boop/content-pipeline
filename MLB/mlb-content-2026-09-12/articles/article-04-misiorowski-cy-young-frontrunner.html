Misiorowski and the Brewers: Baseball's Best Pitcher Is Closing Out a Historic Year

By Ryan Calloway | September 12, 2026

Milwaukee Brewers

Jacob Misiorowski is 14-5 with a 1.97 ERA, a 0.79 WHIP, and 227 strikeouts. He leads Major League Baseball in ERA and strikeouts. His Cy Young odds are -700. This is, statistically, the most dominant pitching season in the National League in years — and the 24-year-old Milwaukee Brewers right-hander is the story of the NL this fall.

In his latest start, Misiorowski answered questions about a brief rough patch by delivering 6.1 innings against the Cubs with just one earned run and nine strikeouts. It was the kind of start that reminded everyone watching why the race for the Cy Young essentially ended months ago.

The Numbers That Set Him Apart

Misiorowski's 1.97 ERA is not just the best in the NL — it leads all of Major League Baseball. His 0.79 WHIP tells the same story from a different angle: fewer baserunners per inning than anyone else in the game. His 227 strikeouts lead MLB as well. The combination of all three leading the sport at once is exceptional; the combination in a 24-year-old in his second full big league season is extraordinary.

This season, Misiorowski broke the record for the fastest pitch ever thrown by a starting pitcher since Statcast tracking began in 2008. He has thrown more than 1,000 pitches at 100 miles per hour or faster in 2026 — a total that no other starting pitcher in the game has approached. The velocity is not just a party trick: Misiorowski pairs it with a slider that generates elite whiff rates and a curveball that changes the eye level on right-handed hitters.

The Two Rough Cubs Starts

In September, Misiorowski had back-to-back rough outings against Chicago — a five-run, four-inning performance at Wrigley on September 2, followed by another difficult start at American Family Field on September 8. For a pitcher who had held opponents to three runs or fewer for 26 consecutive starts before that, it was a notable sequence. The Cubs — the best offense in the NL — figured something out, at least temporarily.

His most recent start answered the question. Six-plus innings, one run, nine strikeouts. The blip appears to have been exactly that. Misiorowski's ERA still leads the league. The Cy Young is still his.

What's Next

Misiorowski will make two or three more regular season starts as the Brewers finish their schedule. Each start is both a tune-up for October and another line in the Cy Young résumé. Chris Sale remains at +500 but is not mounting a serious challenge. When the NL Cy Young is announced this fall, the only real question is how large Misiorowski's vote margin will be.
