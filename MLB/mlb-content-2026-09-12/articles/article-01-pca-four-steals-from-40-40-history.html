Four Steals From History: Pete Crow-Armstrong and the 40-40 Club

By Ryan Calloway | September 12, 2026

Chicago Cubs

With 41 home runs and 36 stolen bases, Pete Crow-Armstrong is four steals away from one of the rarest individual accomplishments in the history of Major League Baseball. Fifteen games remain on the Cubs schedule. The math says he can do it. The question is when.

The 40-40 club — players who have reached 40 home runs and 40 stolen bases in the same season — has six members across more than 150 years of professional baseball. José Canseco started it in 1988. Barry Bonds added his name in 1996. Alex Rodriguez followed in 1998. Alfonso Soriano joined in 2006. Ronald Acuña Jr. and Shohei Ohtani both did it in 2023. Pete Crow-Armstrong, entering today's series against the Pittsburgh Pirates at Wrigley Field, is knocking on that door.

Already Rewriting Cubs History

Crow-Armstrong has already placed himself in franchise history regardless of what happens over the next 15 games. At 24 years and 165 days old, he is the youngest player in Cubs history to reach 40 home runs in a single season — surpassing Hall of Famer Ernie Banks, who reached the mark at 24 years and 214 days in 1955. He passed Banks by 49 days.

He is also the first Cub to hit 40 home runs since Derrek Lee accomplished it in 2005 — a 21-year wait that Crow-Armstrong ended with authority. And on a broader scale, he is just the fourth player age 24 or younger in MLB history to reach 40 home runs and 30 stolen bases in the same season, joining Acuña, Rodriguez, and Canseco in that company.

The NL MVP odds sit at -1100. The individual-season argument is nearly airtight. The question that remains — the one that separates a great season from an all-time season — is the stolen base total.

The Math

Four steals in 15 games is not an extraordinary pace for a player who entered September with 36. Crow-Armstrong has been averaging multiple steals per week throughout the season. The game speed is already there. The remaining opponents include home series that give him full opportunity to run.

What could slow him down: the team's postseason positioning. The Cubs hold a Wild Card spot, and manager Craig Counsell could become conservative with Crow-Armstrong's baserunning once that spot is locked in. But with the race still tight, every game counts — and PCA knows what's at stake.

What's Next

Today's series against the Pirates begins at Wrigley Field. Each game Crow-Armstrong plays from here is another opportunity to add to the stolen base total. Track the daily updates on his stolen base count as the Cubs work through their final 15 games. When steal No. 40 arrives, it joins every other 40-40 name in the record book — and puts Pete Crow-Armstrong's 2026 season in the conversation for one of the finest individual years in Cubs history.
