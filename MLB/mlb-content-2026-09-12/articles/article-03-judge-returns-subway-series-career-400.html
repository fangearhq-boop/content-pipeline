Judge Returns to New York's Biggest Stage: Subway Series, 9/11 Anniversary, and 400 Career Home Runs in Sight

By Jake Torres | September 12, 2026

New York Yankees

Aaron Judge is back, and he has returned to a New York City game that carries more weight than most. Four days after stepping into the lineup for the first time since June 2, the Yankees captain faces the Mets in a Subway Series that falls on the 25th anniversary of September 11, 2001. There is no quieter backdrop in New York baseball right now.

Judge missed 99 days with a stress fracture in his right rib. He did not do a rehab assignment. He came straight back to a Yankees team that needs him. His return on September 8 — a 5-3 win over the Rockies, with Judge going 0-for-3 but playing through — was less about the box score and more about the signal: the captain is back, and the Yankees have 15 games to make a run.

The Number in Front of Him

Judge enters today sitting at approximately 385 career home runs. With 17 home runs in his abbreviated 2026 season, he has maintained a pace that puts 400 career HR in reach before season's end — or at worst, early in 2027. For context, 400 career home runs is a meaningful milestone that places a player in the conversation for the all-time HR leaderboard. Judge is 34 years old and still hitting at a Hall of Fame clip when healthy.

The stress fracture cost him three months of what could have been a dominant 2026 season. His statistics — .249 batting average, 17 HR, 38 RBI in 61 games — reflect an interrupted year, not a declining one. On a per-game basis, the production rate is consistent with his career numbers.

Yankees in the AL Wild Card Race

The Yankees entered September as the first seed in the AL Wild Card race, leading the Boston Red Sox by approximately three games. Judge's return could not have been better timed for a franchise that relies on his presence in the lineup in October. The Subway Series against the Mets — a city-wide event regardless of the standings — gives Judge his first chance to remind New York what it looks like when he's healthy and in the box.

The 9/11 anniversary game is an annual tradition in New York baseball, and this year's Yankee-Met matchup carries the full weight of that significance. It is the kind of game Aaron Judge was built for.

What's Next

Watch Judge's at-bat quality and home run pace over the next 15 games as the Yankees build toward October. Each home run moves him closer to 400 for his career. The Subway Series this weekend is the first high-profile test since his return. If Judge stays healthy through the regular season, the Yankees enter the Wild Card Series with their full lineup — and that changes the calculus for every team watching the AL bracket.
