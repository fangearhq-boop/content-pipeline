### STORY 1: Field of Dreams 2026 — Two Days Away

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 8:00 AM ET

Two days until baseball goes back to the cornfield.

On Thursday, August 13 at 7:30 PM ET — streaming on Netflix — the Minnesota Twins host the Philadelphia Phillies at the Field of Dreams site in Dyersville, Iowa. It's the first game at the historic location since 2022, and the first time Netflix has broadcast the event. Pregame coverage begins at 6:30 PM ET.

Tonight, the Iowa Cubs face the St. Paul Saints at the same Dyersville site at 7:30 PM ET on MLB Network — only the second minor league game ever played at the Field of Dreams location. Tomorrow, a High School All-American Game takes place there at 2 PM ET, managed by Jim Thome and Torii Hunter. The main event is Thursday, but Dyersville is hosting baseball all week.

The pregame Hall of Famer ceremony is the kind of baseball spectacle that only happens once every few years. For the Phillies: Mike Schmidt, Steve Carlton, and Scott Rolen. For the Twins: Joe Mauer, Rod Carew, Bert Blyleven, and Paul Molitor. And Jim Thome and Jim Kaat — who wore both uniforms during their careers — will represent both organizations. More than 25 Hall of Famers in total are scheduled to make the walk through the outfield corn and onto the field. That makes Thursday's pregame reportedly the largest collection of living Hall of Famers at one event since the 2008 All-Star Game at old Yankee Stadium.

Both teams wear throwback uniforms. The Phillies in gray road uniforms inspired by what the franchise wore from 1939 to 1941, with a centennial sleeve patch. The Twins in 1960s pinstripes from the franchise's original jersey design.

Kevin Costner narrates. Dyersville, Iowa becomes the center of the baseball world for one night.

Thursday. 7:30 PM ET. Netflix. Don't miss it.

### STORY 2: Tarik Skubal Dodgers Debut Recap

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 10:30 AM ET

Tarik Skubal made his Dodgers debut today at Wrigley Field, and he was everything Los Angeles paid for.

Six innings. Four hits. Two earned runs. Two walks. Six strikeouts. A 2.79 ERA before today. And a final score of Cubs 5, Dodgers 1.

The pitching was fine. The offense wasn't there.

Skubal, the two-time AL Cy Young Award winner who came to Los Angeles from Detroit at the trade deadline, showed in his first start in Dodger blue exactly why the organization gave up real prospect capital to acquire him. He kept the Cubs' lineup off balance for six innings, limited the damage, and gave his team a chance to win. What he couldn't do was hit.

The Dodgers scored one run in support of their new ace. One. Against a Cubs lineup that has been one of the NL's best all season. That's the story of Skubal's debut — not that he struggled, but that his team didn't back him up.

The trade package was Zyhir Hope, River Ryan, and Brady Smith. That's what it cost to get a two-time Cy Young winner to wear Dodger blue. For what Skubal gave them today — six innings of quality work in a hostile environment against a legitimate playoff contender — the cost makes sense. The team just has to give him something to work with.

He pitched like an ace. The Dodgers now have to play like a team that deserves one.

### STORY 3: Murakami HR Record

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 12:30 PM ET

Munetaka Murakami has now hit 7 home runs in his first 21 MLB games.

That number matters because it's more than any Japanese-born player has hit in their first 21 MLB games — including Shohei Ohtani, who launched 5 in his first 21 games during his 2018 debut season with the Angels.

Murakami isn't a surprise to anyone who followed NPB. He was a two-time Triple Crown winner in Japan's top professional league — one of the most decorated power hitters in NPB history. Before coming to the MLB, he was widely considered Japan's greatest home run threat since Sadaharu Oh. The only question was whether that power would translate across the Pacific.

It has.

Seven home runs in 21 games is a pace that would project to over 50 home runs across a full 162-game season. It's also a record — the most home runs by any Japanese-born player in their first 21 MLB games. Murakami reached that milestone while playing for a White Sox team that already has Colson Montgomery at 26 HRs and holds a 3.5-game lead in the AL Central.

The White Sox went 41-121 in 2024. In 2026, they have one of the most interesting lineups in the American League. Murakami is a big reason why.

### STORY 4: White Sox AL Central Lead — 50 Games to Go

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 3:00 PM ET

The Chicago White Sox lead the AL Central by 3.5 games over the Detroit Tigers with approximately 50 games remaining in the regular season.

That sentence would have been unthinkable a year ago. In 2024, the White Sox went 41-121 — the worst record in modern Major League Baseball history. They finished last in every significant offensive and pitching category. They traded away veterans and entered a rebuild that looked like it would take years to bear fruit.

It's bearing fruit.

Colson Montgomery has 26 home runs on the season. He came into 2026 as one of the game's most anticipated prospects, and he's delivered — powering a lineup that has found a way to win more often than it loses. Munetaka Murakami, the Japanese slugger making history in his first 21 MLB games, provides protection in that lineup that opponents didn't expect. The pitching staff has been good enough when it needed to be.

The AL Central is not finished. Detroit is 3.5 games back, Cleveland and Minnesota are within striking distance. Fifty games is enough baseball to completely change a division race. But the White Sox built this lead honestly — game by game, series by series — and they enter the final stretch as the team to beat.

Whatever happens in September, the 2026 White Sox have already answered the question about whether 2024 was the bottom. It was.
