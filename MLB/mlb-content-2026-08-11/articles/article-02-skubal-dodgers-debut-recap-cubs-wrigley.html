Tarik Skubal's Dodgers Debut: Six Strong, One Run, One Loss

By Jake Torres

August 11, 2026

Tarik Skubal made his Dodgers debut today at Wrigley Field, and he pitched like exactly the pitcher Los Angeles traded for.

Six innings. Four hits. Two earned runs. Two walks. Six strikeouts. A start that, on most days, wins a baseball game. The final score: Cubs 5, Dodgers 1.

The pitching was there. The offense was not.

What Skubal Gave Them

The two-time AL Cy Young Award winner — who posted a 7-5 record, 2.79 ERA, and 116 strikeouts in 96⅔ innings with Detroit before the trade — looked exactly like that pitcher in his first start in Dodger blue. He worked through the Cubs' lineup efficiently, kept traffic manageable for six innings, and gave his bullpen a workable situation entering the later innings.

The Dodgers acquired Skubal from the Detroit Tigers at the trade deadline, sending outfielder Zyhir Hope and pitchers River Ryan and Brady Smith to Detroit. That package reflected the price of adding a two-time Cy Young winner at the deadline. What Skubal delivered in his first start justified the acquisition — he pitched like a No. 1 starter should pitch, in a difficult ballpark, against a lineup that has been one of the NL's best all season.

What his teammates delivered was one run.

A Team That Needs to Match Him

The Dodgers have been one of baseball's most confounding teams in August. Two-time defending World Series champions, with Shohei Ohtani in the middle of their lineup and a newly reinforced rotation. They ended a seven-game losing streak on August 8 with an Ohtani walk-off, lost the series finale to Arizona on August 9, and today lost a 5-1 decision at Wrigley despite a quality start from their newest ace.

The pattern — strong pitching, limited offense, losses — is not new. The Dodgers have struggled to put runs together during this stretch of August in a way that doesn't match what their lineup is supposed to produce. Ohtani remains one of the best hitters in the game, but the lineup around him hasn't consistently produced at the level this team's roster suggests it should.

Skubal's job is to give the Dodgers innings and limit damage. He did that today. The offense's job is to score enough to win when he does it. Today, they didn't.

What's Next

The Dodgers continue their road trip following Tuesday's loss at Wrigley. Skubal will take his next turn in the rotation in approximately five days — his second start in a Dodger uniform, with the hope that the offense will show up to support the pitcher they gave up three prospects to acquire.

The debut was everything it needed to be from a pitching standpoint. The score wasn't. That's where the Dodgers are in August 2026 — getting quality starts, not getting the wins that should follow.
