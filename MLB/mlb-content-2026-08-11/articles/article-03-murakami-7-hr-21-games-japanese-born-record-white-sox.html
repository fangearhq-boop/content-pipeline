Murakami's Record: 7 HRs in His First 21 MLB Games — More Than Any Japanese-Born Player in History

By Ryan Calloway

August 11, 2026

Munetaka Murakami hit 7 home runs in his first 21 Major League Baseball games — more than any Japanese-born player in their first 21 games in MLB history, including Shohei Ohtani's 5 home runs in his debut season in 2018.

That record was set earlier in the 2026 season. Murakami debuted on March 26, Opening Day, and homered in his very first MLB at-bat. By game 21, he had 7. By game 25, he had 10. By game 55, he had 20 — the third-fastest pace to 20 home runs in MLB history, behind only Cody Bellinger (2017) and Wally Berger (1930).

The record stands even as Murakami has been on the injured list since mid-season, recovering from a Grade 2 right hamstring strain that has cost him approximately 35 games. He was approaching a rehab assignment with Triple-A Charlotte in early July. The White Sox are watching his recovery closely as they defend a 3.5-game AL Central lead with approximately 50 games remaining.

Who Murakami Is

Murakami did not arrive in the United States as a prospect hoping to prove himself. He arrived as one of the most accomplished power hitters in the history of Japanese professional baseball — a two-time Triple Crown winner in NPB, Japan's top professional league. He is widely regarded as Japan's greatest home run threat since Sadaharu Oh, the legendary Tokyo Giants slugger who holds the world professional baseball record.

The translation question — whether his power would carry from NPB to MLB pitching — was answered in the first three weeks of the 2026 season. Seven home runs in 21 games. Faster than Ohtani. Faster than any Japanese-born player who preceded him in Major League Baseball.

The injury interrupted what was becoming one of the most remarkable debut seasons for an international player in recent memory. But the record he set in those first 21 games is permanent, regardless of what the rest of the season holds.

What His Return Means for the White Sox

The Chicago White Sox went 41-121 in 2024 — the worst record in modern MLB history. In 2026, they lead the AL Central at 61-56, with Colson Montgomery at 26 home runs providing the lineup's most consistent power.

Murakami in the middle of that lineup would give Chicago something few AL Central opponents have had to plan against this season: two legitimate power threats hitting one after the other. His return — when it comes — changes the calculation for every pitcher facing Chicago's lineup through September.

The record he set in April is already historic. What he does when he comes back is the next chapter.

What's Next

Murakami continues his recovery from the hamstring strain. The White Sox have not confirmed a specific return date, but they are monitoring his progress as the AL Central race enters its final stretch. His 7 home runs in 21 games as a Japanese-born player record stands regardless of injury.

When he returns to the lineup, the White Sox's October case gets stronger. For now, the record is already written.
