50 Games Left: The White Sox Hold a 3.5-Game AL Central Lead, and This Is Where It Gets Real

By Marcus Cole

August 11, 2026

The Chicago White Sox are 61-56 and lead the AL Central by 3.5 games over the Detroit Tigers (58-60) with approximately 50 games remaining in the regular season.

A year after going 41-121 — the worst record in modern Major League Baseball history — Chicago is in first place. The question heading into the final stretch is no longer whether they belong. It's whether they can hold what they've built.

The Man at the Center

Colson Montgomery has 26 home runs on the season. The young shortstop came into 2026 as one of the game's most anticipated prospects — Chicago's answer to what the franchise would look like when it competed again. He is delivering. His 26th home run came last Sunday, a three-run shot that capped a four-run third inning in a 5-3 win over the Cleveland Guardians. That is what he does: hits important home runs in meaningful games.

Munetaka Murakami, the Japanese slugger who hit 7 home runs in his first 21 MLB games earlier this season, has been on the injured list with a hamstring strain but is approaching a return. When he's back, the lineup gets deeper. For now, Montgomery is carrying the offense's power identity.

The Race Behind Them

Detroit is 3.5 games back. Cleveland and Minnesota are approximately four games out. Fifty games is more than enough baseball to change a division race — anyone who watched the 2026 standings knows that leads this large have evaporated before. The AL Central is not finished, and the White Sox will need to earn every game of their lead between now and September.

The schedule matters. How the White Sox perform against their division rivals over the next month will largely determine whether this lead survives into late September. A 3.5-game lead is comfortable. It is not safe.

What's Next

The White Sox continue their 2026 season with approximately 50 games remaining. The AL Central race heads into its most consequential stretch of the year. Detroit, Cleveland, and Minnesota are close enough to make September interesting — and Chicago will need to play like a division winner to stay ahead of them.

In 2024, this organization lost 121 games. In 2026, they're the team to beat in the American League Central. The final 50 games will determine which story ends this season.
