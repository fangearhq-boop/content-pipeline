Misiorowski Returns to Wrigley: The Cy Young Frontrunner Answers Back on Extra Rest

By Jake Torres | September 7, 2026

Milwaukee Brewers

Jacob Misiorowski is scheduled to take the mound today or tomorrow in a rematch that matters more than a typical September start.

On September 3 at Wrigley Field, the Chicago Cubs handed Misiorowski the worst outing of his 2026 season — five runs, four walks, just four innings, and a final line that matched career worsts in every painful category. Worse, the start ended a streak of 26 consecutive appearances allowing three or fewer earned runs, a modern-era feat unmatched since Dwight Gooden opened the 1985 season in the same fashion.

Today, the stage is the same. The ballpark is the same. The opponent is the same. And Misiorowski comes in rested, prepared, and motivated.

What That Streak Meant — and What Its End Changes

The 26-start run of allowing three or fewer earned runs was the defining statistical achievement of an already-dominant season. His ERA sits at 1.97 — the lowest in baseball — with 222-plus strikeouts and a 0.75 WHIP that renders most offensive lineups irrelevant.

One bad start does not erase that. His NL Cy Young Award odds sit at -750, with Chris Sale of the Braves the only credible competitor at +850. The case was already built over five months of unrivaled performance.

But pitchers are remembered for how they respond. The Brewers hold first place in the NL Central. The postseason is functionally secured. What the team needs now is the version of Misiorowski that made all those other starts look easy — controlled, overpowering, and finishing what he starts.

The Rematch Context

The Brewers won the September 3 game 9-5 despite Misiorowski's exit, rallying after he left the mound. The offense bailed him out once. The staff's confidence in him never wavered — extra rest was given intentionally, a sign the organization treats this outing as a reset, not a referendum.

The Cubs, for their part, are fighting to hold Wild Card No. 1 position. Every game matters to them as well. This is not a placeholder mid-September start — both teams have real stakes, which makes the rematch at Wrigley a legitimate event.

What's Next

Misiorowski's start today or tomorrow gives him roughly three or four more regular-season outings to sharpen his Cy Young résumé before October arrives. One rough start in 27 is a footnote — unless it becomes two. Watch for his velocity (which touched 101.5 mph at his September 2 gem), his walk rate, and his ability to navigate the Cubs' lineup for the full game.
