NL Wild Card Race With 21 Games Left: Who's In, Who's Fading, and What It Takes to Survive

By Jake Torres | September 7, 2026

MLB Playoffs

The National League Wild Card race enters its September crunch phase with 21 games remaining, and the picture is clear in some places and frantic in others. Three spots. A half-dozen teams with a plausible claim. September 29 when it all starts to mean something in elimination games.

The Cubs hold Wild Card No. 1 by roughly half a game over the Phillies. Both teams have enough runway to be considered relatively secure — their margin over the rest of the field provides breathing room that most teams in the scrum below them do not have. The race at the top is more about seeding than survival.

The Real Battle: Wild Card No. 3

The Arizona Diamondbacks hold the third and final NL Wild Card spot by approximately half a game over the San Diego Padres. That half-game margin is the difference between postseason baseball and an early offseason. Both teams know it.

The Padres have been here before — late-season pressure situations, everything on the line, October experience in the organization's recent DNA. The D-backs, for their part, have been legitimate contenders all year and have no intention of ceding this spot without a fight.

What makes it genuinely uncertain is the schedule. Both teams still have games against playoff-caliber opponents. Neither team plays enough soft opposition in September to simply run off a winning streak by feasting on weak rosters.

Who's Fading

The St. Louis Cardinals entered this week at 71-72 — the definition of a team that needed to start winning last month. At 3-plus games back of WC3 with 21 left, the Cardinals likely need to win roughly 14 of their remaining 21 games and hope the teams above them lose at an unusual rate. That combination rarely materializes.

The Marlins, Pirates, and other fringe contenders face similar mathematical improbabilities. They are not mathematically eliminated, but the probability calculus has shifted sharply against them.

What's Next

The Wild Card Series begins September 29. Division Series follows October 3. Every game between now and then is a countdown. The Cubs and Phillies manage their leads. The D-backs and Padres play for their playoff lives. The Cardinals run out of time.

Check the standings daily — in a race this tight, a single series sweep in either direction reshuffles the entire bracket. The September standings are baseball's most compelling reading material.
