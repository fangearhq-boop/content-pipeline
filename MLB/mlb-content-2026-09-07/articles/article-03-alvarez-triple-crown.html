Yordan Alvarez's Triple Crown: Can He Hold All Three Categories for 21 More Games?

By Ryan Calloway | September 7, 2026

Houston Astros

The last time an American League player led all three Triple Crown categories at the start of September and finished with the hardware was 2012, when Miguel Cabrera became one of the most celebrated individual seasons in baseball history. Yordan Alvarez is chasing that legacy with 21 games left in the 2026 season.

The Houston Astros slugger currently leads the AL in batting average (.317), home runs (37, tied), and RBI (94, tied). All three categories are live. All three categories are contested. And all three categories need to survive approximately 21 more games for Alvarez to become the first AL Triple Crown winner in 14 years.

The Threats on All Three Fronts

On home runs, Tampa Bay's Yohandy Caminero sits at 37, tied with Alvarez for the AL lead. One Caminero multi-homer game could flip the category overnight. Alvarez needs to outpace him the rest of the way — or at least keep pace.

On RBI, Pete Alonso is also at 94, tied at the top. The RBI title often comes down to lineup protection and clutch-situation volume in September — factors that can swing unpredictably as rosters expand and pennant races shift approaches.

The batting average chase is the most volatile. Chandler Simpson of the Tampa Bay Rays sits at approximately .308 — roughly nine points behind Alvarez's .317. In September, with expanded rosters and bullpen-heavy games, batting averages can shift quickly. A 10-game slump for Alvarez or a 10-game tear from Simpson could close that gap in a hurry.

The Historical Weight

The Triple Crown — leading the league in batting average, home runs, and RBI simultaneously — is the rarest statistical achievement in baseball. It has happened 18 times in major league history, and only once since 1967 before Cabrera's 2012 season (Frank Robinson in 1966). Cabrera was the sole winner in modern times before now.

If Alvarez holds all three leads, he joins a list that includes Ruth, Williams, Mantle, Robinson, and Yastrzemski. That is not hyperbole — that is the company Triple Crown winners keep.

What's Next

Houston's remaining schedule, the opposition pitchers Alvarez will face, and the performance of Caminero, Alonso, and Simpson will all determine whether this chase goes the distance. Check the AL leaderboard daily — the race in all three categories is too close to ignore. Alvarez's 21 remaining games might be the most watched in the AL this September.
