PCA Hits No. 40: Seven Steals From the Most Exclusive Club in Baseball

By Marcus Cole | September 7, 2026

Chicago Cubs

Pete Crow-Armstrong hit his 40th home run of the 2026 season on Sunday, and now the only thing standing between him and baseball immortality is seven stolen bases.

PCA pulled a sweeper from Miami Marlins pitcher Tyler Phillips into the right-field seats in the fifth inning at Marlins Park, clearing the first and most dramatic half of the 40-40 threshold. The Cubs lost the game 10-3, but the loss was an afterthought. What matters now is the math.

He has 33 stolen bases. He needs 40. There are approximately 21 games remaining in the 2026 regular season. The arithmetic says 7-in-21 is doable. The history says it has been done before — but not often, and not by everyone who has tried.

The Club He's Chasing

The 40-40 club — 40 home runs and 40 stolen bases in the same season — has exactly six members in 138 years of professional baseball. Jose Canseco accomplished it first in 1988. Barry Bonds did it in 1996. Alex Rodriguez matched them in 1998. Alfonso Soriano joined in 2006. Ronald Acuña Jr. became the fifth in 2023, and Shohei Ohtani became the sixth in that same historic year.

No member of that club has done it since. No Cub has ever done it. PCA, at 24 years old, is poised to become the seventh.

He is already in rare company. By reaching 40 home runs and 33 steals, he has become the first Cubs player in franchise history with 40 homers and 30 steals in a single season. More broadly, he is only the fourth player age 24 or younger to reach 40 home runs with 30 steals, joining Acuña, A-Rod, and Canseco — a lineage of transformational talent.

The September Chase

Seven steals in 21 games is an average of one stolen base every three games. PCA has been running at an elite clip all season — 33 steals through the first 141 games — and his speed has not diminished alongside the power explosion. The question is not capability. It is opportunity.

Base-running decisions shift in September, when playoff races make managers conservative. PCA will need green lights on the basepaths, the right counts, and the patience to pick his spots rather than force the issue. Every steal will feel earned.

The NL MVP race adds another layer. PCA's Cubs hold NL Wild Card No. 1 position, and every PCA plate appearance now carries championship-round implications alongside historic ones. That double burden — club history and team stakes — is what September baseball is made of.

What's Next

Chicago continues its schedule with approximately 21 games remaining, and every PCA at-bat is a chance to add to the stolen base total. The benchmark is 40. The club has six members. Whether Pete Crow-Armstrong joins them in 2026 is the most compelling individual story in baseball's final month.
