Pete Crow-Armstrong did it.

The Chicago Cubs centerfielder hit his 40th home run of the 2026 season on Sunday, pulling a sweeper into the right-field seats off Miami's Tyler Phillips in the fifth inning. The Cubs lost the game 10-3 — but what PCA did will be remembered long after the final score fades.

He needs seven more stolen bases to become the seventh member of the 40-40 club.

For historical context: he is the first Cubs player in franchise history to hit 40 homers and steal 30 bases in the same season. He is only the fourth player age 24 or younger to reach that 40+30 combination, joining Acuña, A-Rod, and Canseco.

The math is simple. The execution is not. Let's see if PCA makes baseball history in September. 🐻⚾

### STORY 3: Alvarez — FB Long-Form
**Posting Window:** 12:30 PM ET
**Type:** Long-Form

It hasn't happened in the American League since 2012. Yordan Alvarez is trying to change that.

The Houston Astros slugger leads all three American League Triple Crown categories with 21 games remaining: .317 batting average, 37 home runs, and 94 RBI. The last AL player to win the Triple Crown was Miguel Cabrera of the Detroit Tigers, and that was 14 years ago.

The challenge is real:

On home runs, Tampa Bay's Yohandy Caminero is tied with Alvarez at 37. Any week where Caminero goes on a run and Alvarez cools off could flip that category.

On RBI, Pete Alonso is also at 94 — a tie that could break either way in a single series.

On batting average, Chandler Simpson sits at roughly .308, about nine points behind. That gap can close fast.

Yordan Alvarez has been one of the most consistent run-producers in baseball all season. The question now is not whether he's capable — it's whether September's schedule, the opposing pitchers, and pure luck will cooperate for 21 more games.

History is within reach. All three at once.

Shohei Ohtani is expected to be back in the Los Angeles Dodgers lineup today against the Cincinnati Reds.

He's been out since September 3 — four games away from the lineup with a combination of left knee soreness, right bicep discomfort, and neck tightness. Manager Dave Roberts had been cautious with his updates, moving from "hopeful, not confident" to "expected back Monday."

His bat needs work: Ohtani is 7-for-51 (.137 batting average) with 20 strikeouts since August 18. That's a rough stretch by any standard, and the physical discomfort has clearly been part of it.

The bigger picture: Roberts has confirmed that Ohtani's return to the pitching rotation this season is off the table. The knee and bicep combination makes that too risky. What the Dodgers get in September is Ohtani as a designated hitter — a world-class one, even in a slump — as they position for the postseason.

Los Angeles has a comfortable record at roughly 82-57. The playoff seed is secured. But the version of Ohtani they get for the Wild Card bracket matters enormously.

**Posting Window:** 3:00 PM ET
**Type:** Long-Form

The National League Wild Card race is entering its September crunch, and the math is getting tight.

The Cubs hold the NL's top Wild Card spot with approximately a half-game cushion over the Phillies. Both teams look relatively safe — the real anxiety is one rung below them.

The third and final Wild Card berth is a knife fight. The Arizona Diamondbacks hold that spot by roughly half a game over the San Diego Padres. The St. Louis Cardinals are 71-72, clinging to the fringes — they likely need to run the table to have any shot.

Wild Card Series: September 29. Division Series: October 3. That gives every team in this race approximately 21 games to write the ending they want.

For the Padres, this is a team that has been here before — late-season pressure, postseason baseball, everything on the line. For the D-backs, it's about holding on to what they've built.

For the Cardinals? At 71-72, it's now-or-never. And "never" is looking more likely with each passing series.
