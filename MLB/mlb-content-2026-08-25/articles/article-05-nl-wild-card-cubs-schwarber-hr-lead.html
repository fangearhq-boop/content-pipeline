NL Wild Card: Cubs Hold No. 1 Seed, Phillies' Schwarber Leads All of Baseball in Home Runs

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | August 25, 2026

The NL Wild Card standings as of August 25: Chicago Cubs 76-56, Philadelphia Phillies 73-59, San Diego Padres 71-61. Thirty games remain for most teams. The Cubs hold the No. 1 seed by three games over the Phillies and five over the Padres. The Arizona Diamondbacks are also in the picture, fighting with San Diego for the third guaranteed Wild Card position.

Sunday night, Kyle Schwarber hit a home run in a Phillies comeback win over the Cardinals. He is the major league home run leader. The No. 2 Wild Card team has the most productive power hitter in baseball, which is worth understanding in the context of what the Cubs and Padres are trying to manage over the next thirty games.

Why the Cubs Need No. 1

The Cubs' incentive to hold the top Wild Card seed has been a consistent storyline for weeks, and it comes down to one name: Jacob Misiorowski. The Brewers are 81-49, the best record in baseball, and will control the NL Central and likely home-field advantage deep into October. The No. 1 NL Wild Card team hosts a first-round series against the No. 4 seed. The Brewers, as NL Central champions, are almost certain to be the No. 1 seed overall in the NL — which means the Cubs' opponent in Round 1 depends entirely on where they finish in the Wild Card standings.

The No. 1 Wild Card team could avoid the Brewers until at least the NLCS. A No. 2 or No. 3 seed might face Milwaukee in the Division Series. No team in baseball wants to see Misiorowski at 1.68 ERA before the league championship round. The Cubs' 76-56 record is the arithmetic expression of that motivation.

The Phillies and Schwarber

Philadelphia sits at 73-59 — three games behind Chicago but with a comfortable cushion over the Padres for the No. 2 position. The Phillies have the offensive firepower to maintain that gap, particularly with Schwarber leading the major leagues in home runs. A Phillies lineup built around an ML home run champion generates runs, and in the NL Wild Card format where the No. 2 seed still gets a first-round home series, their position is defensible.

What Schwarber's HR lead doesn't solve is the Misiorowski question. The Phillies aren't necessarily afraid of that matchup — they've built one of the better rotations in the NL themselves — but no team schedules a first-round series against the Brewers with enthusiasm. The incentive to stay No. 2 or higher is real for Philadelphia, too.

The Padres-Diamondbacks Race

The live battle for the No. 3 Wild Card position is between San Diego (71-61) and Arizona. The Padres hold a slight edge in the standings, but the margin is small enough that the next few series could shift the picture. The No. 3 seed in the Wild Card has the least favorable first-round bracket, so both teams are competing as much for seeding leverage as for simple playoff inclusion.

The Padres lost key veteran presence when Ketel Marte was placed on the IL with a left knee injury, though his timeline for return has not been fully clarified.

What's Next

All three Wild Card positions are in active competition with thirty games remaining. The Cubs lead at 76-56, the Phillies follow at 73-59, and the Padres hold the third position at 71-61. Kyle Schwarber's home run surge gives the Phillies added firepower in the stretch run. The Cubs' incentive structure — stay No. 1, avoid Misiorowski as long as possible — sets the stakes for what is becoming one of the most competitive final months in recent NL history.
