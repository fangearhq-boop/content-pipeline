Misiorowski's 1.68 ERA Is the Kind of Number That Gets Written Into Baseball History

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | August 25, 2026

Jacob Misiorowski did not pitch on Sunday. He sat in the broadcast booth at Williamsport while Tyler Mahle took the mound for the Little League Classic — a reasonable assignment for a pitcher in his routine rest cycle between starts. His last outing came August 21 against the Braves: 6 innings, 0 runs, 4 hits, 2 walks, 6 strikeouts, win number 13. When that game ended, his season line read 13-5, 1.68 ERA, 216 strikeouts, 0.76 WHIP across 24 starts.

His next start is coming. Wednesday or Thursday is the most likely scenario. When it arrives, that 1.68 ERA is going to be the number everyone is watching.

What 1.68 Means in Historical Terms

Finishing a full starting rotation season with an ERA below 1.75 is an extremely rare accomplishment in the modern era. Pitchers do not routinely put up these numbers — the combination of expanded lineups, advanced hitter preparation, better bullpen usage, and pitch count management across 162-game seasons has made sustained ERA dominance harder to achieve at the level Misiorowski has maintained.

To be clear: with roughly six to seven starts remaining, Misiorowski's ERA is not locked in. A bad start — or two — could push the number above 2.00. But the distance between 1.68 and 2.00 requires a specific kind of bad streak to accomplish. He hasn't had one this season. His nine games with 10 or more strikeouts tie the Brewers' franchise single-season record set by Corbin Burnes in 2022. His 0.76 WHIP is historically excellent. His fastball — which he threw 33 times at 103 or more miles per hour in a single start earlier this season, a figure twice the previous single-game record — is not showing signs of deteriorating.

The Cy Young odds sit at -550 in his favor. That number is not a forecast for a competitive race. It's a recognition that the award conversation ended months ago.

What to Watch in the Final Starts

The number to track is not the win-loss record. Misiorowski is 13-5, and run support can complicate that in either direction over the season's final month. The number is the ERA, and specifically whether it finishes below 1.75 after 30-plus starts.

Each start from here carries additional weight for that calculation. Six innings of shutout ball keeps the ERA in place or lowers it. A rough outing where he gives up four or five runs in five innings — the kind of start every pitcher has eventually — moves the number up by a statistically significant amount when the denominator is in the low 160s of innings pitched.

Misiorowski has not had that start this season. His worst games have been controllable. That consistency, more than any individual performance, is the reason this ERA figure is where it is.

The Brewers' October Picture

Milwaukee is 81-49, the best record in baseball. The NL Central is settled. The Brewers are playing for home-field advantage through the postseason. Misiorowski's dominant second half means he enters October as the most feared pitcher any team in the NL could face in a playoff series. His next start on Wednesday or Thursday will be watched for exactly that reason — not just for what it does to the ERA, but for what it says about how sharp he looks heading toward the most important month of the season.

What's Next

The ERA watch begins now — six to seven starts remain to see whether the final number lands in a category that gets written into the historical record books alongside the Cy Young trophy that's already all but engraved.
