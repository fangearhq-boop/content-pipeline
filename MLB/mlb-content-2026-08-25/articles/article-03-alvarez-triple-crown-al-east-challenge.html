Alvarez's Triple Crown Bid Faces an AL East Challenger With 30 Games Left

By Ryan Calloway | Ballpark Banter | August 25, 2026

Yordan Alvarez leads the American League in batting average, home runs, and RBI. The current numbers: approximately .320, 36, and 90 — a Triple Crown bid that would be the first in major league baseball since Miguel Cabrera did it in 2012. That is a fourteen-year drought for one of the game's most demanding individual achievements, and with thirty games remaining, Alvarez is positioned to end it.

He is also, as of August 24, facing a specific challenge. The Washington Times reported that Alvarez's Triple Crown bid "will have to withstand a major challenge from the AL East." At least one AL East hitter is close enough in one category to make the final thirty games genuinely contested.

What a Triple Crown Actually Requires

The Triple Crown demands simultaneous leads in three separate statistical categories where hitters from across the entire American League are chasing the same numbers. Batting average is the most volatile of the three — a player's AVG can shift meaningfully in a thirty-game sample depending on whether they run into a cold stretch or go on a hot streak. Home runs and RBI are more cumulative, harder to close in a short stretch, but not immune to a rival going on a late-season surge.

Alvarez is at .320 in batting average (note: his average has fluctuated depending on recent at-bats), 36 in home runs, and 90 in RBI. All three lead the AL. But the category where an AL East rival is pressing has not been confirmed by available reporting. The Washington Times piece establishes the threat exists — the details of which category and which player will become clearer in the coming days as box scores accumulate.

The Historical Context

Cabrera's 2012 season was the last time any player pulled it off: .330/44/139, one of the most complete offensive seasons in recent history. Since then, hitters have come close — leading in two of three categories, or briefly leading all three before a late-season swing moved the standings — but no one has held all three through the end of the season.

The structural reasons are real. In an era of specialization and shifting defensive alignments, batting average has become harder to sustain at the level needed to pace an entire league. Home run records are more distributed — power is no longer concentrated in two or three players per league the way it was in earlier generations. And RBI depends partly on lineup construction and opportunity, not just individual skill.

All of that context is Alvarez's to overcome or confirm over the next thirty games. The AL East challenge makes it interesting.

What's Next

The Astros play thirty more games. Alvarez's Triple Crown bid will be updated with each day's box scores as the season enters September. The AL East rival pressing in at least one category will come into focus as the standings tighten. If Alvarez holds all three leads into the final week of the season, it becomes the baseball story of the year — a fourteen-year achievement drought ending in Minute Maid Park.
