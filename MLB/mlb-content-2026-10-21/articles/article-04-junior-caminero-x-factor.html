Junior Caminero Is Tampa Bay's X-Factor — and Milwaukee Knows It

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter

If you looked only at Junior Caminero's 2026 regular season numbers — .265 batting average, 13 home runs, 27 RBI in 51 games — you might wonder why the Brewers are spending so much time thinking about him. The numbers look like a diminished version of the player he was.

Then you watch ALCS Game 6, and the picture snaps back into focus.

The Player He Is

Two years ago, Caminero put up a 2025 season that announced him as one of baseball's elite bats: 45 home runs, 110 RBI, a first All-Star selection. He finished as a runner-up in the MVP voting. Opposing pitchers adjusted. He adjusted back. A second All-Star appearance followed in 2026 despite the slow start and injury-shortened season.

The .265 average and 13 home runs in 51 games reflect a player managing through a difficult year, not a player who lost the ability to hit. By the time October arrived, Caminero was healthy and locked in.

The ALCS Moment

In ALCS Game 6, with Tampa Bay facing elimination, Caminero put a 2-run home run off the foul pole. It wasn't a pulled grounder that snuck just fair — it was the kind of contact that reminded every scout and pitcher in baseball that when Caminero connects, the ball doesn't come back.

After that swing, opposing pitchers stopped treating him as a problem they'd solved during the regular season. The Rays' lineup suddenly had a different look to it. One player changed the calculus.

What Milwaukee Must Account For

The Brewers have the best pitching in baseball. But no pitching plan survives contact from Junior Caminero without a careful approach. He's the one Tampa Bay hitter who can change a series with a single at-bat — and Milwaukee's pitching staff will need to decide early how much they're willing to challenge him.

What's Next
