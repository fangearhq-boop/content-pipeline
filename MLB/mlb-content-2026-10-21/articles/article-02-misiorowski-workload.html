The Misiorowski Question: How Deep Can Milwaukee Push Its Ace in the World Series?

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter

Jacob Misiorowski is the best pitcher in baseball. His 2026 regular season numbers — 21-8, a 2.51 ERA, and 339 strikeouts to lead all of MLB in both categories — make that argument about as airtight as it gets. He's the NL Cy Young frontrunner and the reason Milwaukee's rotation was considered elite all season.

But heading into the World Series, the most important question in baseball isn't whether Misiorowski can dominate. It's how long the Brewers can use him.

The Arm Fatigue Factor

Misiorowski missed time in July with arm fatigue, and Milwaukee has been careful with him ever since. During the NLDS and NLCS, he averaged just 4-4.5 innings per start. Not because he was ineffective — his numbers remained excellent — but because the Brewers made a calculated decision to protect him for the long haul.

It's a sound strategy, and it's worked. Milwaukee's bullpen has carried the remaining innings with a 0.38 ERA through the playoffs. That unit — led by DL Hall, Aaron Ashby, Thyago Uribe, and closer Devin Megill — has been historically dominant. The Brewers don't need Misiorowski to throw seven innings. They need him to be sharp for four, and the bullpen handles the rest.

Will the Formula Hold Against Tampa Bay?

The Tampa Bay Rays present a unique challenge to this approach. Their lineup is patient, contact-oriented, and built to make pitchers work deep into counts. They grind at-bats, foul off pitches, and trust that eventually a starter will tire or a bullpen arm will crack.

Against any other Milwaukee ace, that formula might work. But there's no "eventually" when the ace leaves after four innings by design, replaced immediately by relief arms who've been lights-out all October.

The series outcome may hinge on whether Tampa Bay can crack the Brewers' relief corps before Misiorowski's curtailed starts catch up to Milwaukee.

What's Next

Misiorowski is Milwaukee's probable Game 1 starter on Friday, October 23 at 8:07 PM ET at American Family Field on FOX. How the Brewers use him — and for how long — will be one of the defining storylines of this Fall Classic.
