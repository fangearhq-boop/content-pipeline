World Series 2026: Brewers vs. Rays, Two No. 1 Seeds Chasing History

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter

For the first time in Major League Baseball history, two No. 1 seeds are meeting in the World Series. The Milwaukee Brewers and Tampa Bay Rays arrive at the Fall Classic as the best teams their respective leagues produced in 2026 — and neither franchise has ever held the Commissioner's Trophy.

Game 1 is Friday, October 23. First pitch at 8:07 PM ET at American Family Field in Milwaukee. The series airs on FOX.

Two Franchises Chasing the Same Thing

The Brewers last appeared in the World Series in 1982, falling to the St. Louis Cardinals in seven games. In their entire history, Milwaukee has never won a championship. Tampa Bay's situation is more recent and more painful — the Rays lost the 2008 World Series to the Phillies and haven't been back since. The franchise has never won it all.

So while October baseball often involves dynasties protecting legacies, this series is different. One of these clubs is going to win its first championship. That narrative alone makes it one of the more compelling Fall Classics in recent memory.

Mirror Images

The Brewers and Rays are remarkably similar in how they play the game. Both rely on contact hitting over raw power, aggressive baserunning that creates havoc on the bases, elite team defense, and dominant bullpen units. Both organizations are regarded as among the most analytically sophisticated in baseball — efficient, resourceful, and capable of getting maximum production out of carefully assembled rosters.

Milwaukee won four of its five home playoff games in 2026. Tampa Bay went 3-1 at home. In a series between evenly matched clubs, the team that protects its home field typically wins.

Vegas has Milwaukee as -160 favorites with Tampa Bay at +140. That spread reflects home-field advantage as much as anything else — there's very little separating these clubs on paper.

What's Next

Game 1 is Friday, October 23, at 8:07 PM ET from American Family Field in Milwaukee on FOX. Jacob Misiorowski is Milwaukee's probable Game 1 starter. Drew Rasmussen takes the ball for Tampa Bay. Game 2 is Saturday with Shintaro Henderson opposing Framber Peralta.

After decades of near-misses for both franchises, October baseball doesn't get bigger than this.
