Jake Bauers Is Your NLCS MVP, and That's Exactly the Point

By Marcus Cole | October 17, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

After the Milwaukee Brewers completed their sweep of the Los Angeles Dodgers in the NLCS, the Most Valuable Player award went to Jake Bauers. If that name took you a moment to place, welcome to the most perfectly Milwaukee story of the most perfectly Milwaukee postseason run.

Bauers is a first baseman. He is a career .250-range hitter. He is not the player who defines the series narrative on paper — he does not have Garrett Mitchell's go-ahead homer or David Hamilton's insurance shot. But he is the reason the Brewers are who they are, and the MVP award going to him says something important about why Milwaukee swept the Dodgers while Los Angeles struggled to answer.

Who Jake Bauers Is

Bauers represents the depth and versatility that has made this Brewers team more than the sum of its individual stars. He is the first baseman who shows up ready every night, who hits when the lineup needs it, who does not require the spotlight to contribute.

When asked about Game 4 — the game in which the Brewers trailed 4-0 before rallying to win 6-4 and complete the sweep — Bauers offered a line that will stick: "We knew we were going to win that game." Not bravado. Confidence. The confidence of a team that had been in similar positions four times during this NLCS and had found a way through each one.

That is what Bauers embodies. Consistency over flash. Performance over persona. Results over narrative. The Brewers have built an entire roster around that philosophy, and the NLCS MVP trophy going to a first baseman with a .250 career average is the most honest possible summary of it.

What the Brewers Are

The Dodgers had Shohei Ohtani — the most anticipated postseason performer of his generation, still managing the physical limitations of a long season, still one of the few players in the sport capable of changing a series with a single swing. The Brewers had Jake Bauers and a bullpen and a roster built like a team that expects to win rather than one waiting on a single player to make everything work.

That contrast is worth sitting with. Two very different approaches to building a postseason team, and in 2026, the Brewers' approach produced a sweep. Bauers is the symbol of that. He is not famous outside of Milwaukee. He does not carry the name recognition that Ohtani or Mookie Betts or any of the Dodgers' marquee players carry. And he is walking away from the 2026 NLCS with the MVP award, heading to a World Series, with one of the most memorable postgame quotes of the October.

What's Next

The World Series begins October 23 in Milwaukee. Bauers will be in the lineup, doing exactly what he does — hitting, playing first base, contributing to a team that wins because everyone does their job. He will not be the most recognizable name on the field. He will be in the conversation for another award by November if the Brewers finish what they started.

Jake Bauers is your NLCS MVP. If that surprises you, you have not been watching the Brewers closely enough.
