Brewers Sweep Dodgers in the NLCS, Reach World Series for First Time Since 1982

By Jake Torres | October 17, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The Milwaukee Brewers trailed 4-0 in the sixth inning of NLCS Game 4. They were playing at Dodger Stadium. They had trailed at some point in every game of the series. And they were one game away from ending a 44-year drought.

Garrett Mitchell hit a go-ahead home run off Tanner Scott in the sixth. David Hamilton added insurance in the ninth. The Brewers won 6-4 and completed a sweep of the Los Angeles Dodgers — the NLCS is over, and Milwaukee is going to the World Series for the first time since 1982.

The Comeback That Completed the Sweep

The Brewers had been here before in this series — playing from behind, needing the offense to wake up at precisely the right moment. Against the Dodgers' pitching staff and one of baseball's most formidable lineups, that was never a comfortable position. But it was the position Milwaukee kept finding itself in, and they kept finding ways out of it.

Game 4 was the most dramatic version of that dynamic. Shohei Ohtani hit a 2-run homer in the second inning — his first postseason home run of the entire NLCS run — and Los Angeles built a 4-0 lead that felt significant. The Brewers had trailed going into the late innings before. This time it was four runs, on the road, with the Dodgers' bullpen ready to protect it.

Scott, the Dodgers' lefthanded reliever, came in to face Mitchell in the sixth. Mitchell had already been one of Milwaukee's October answers. He delivered again — a go-ahead homer that changed the scoreboard and the series. The Brewers held on from there, won 6-4, and made history.

The Weight of 44 Years

The 1982 World Series ended with the St. Louis Cardinals winning in seven games. That is the last time Milwaukee reached the Fall Classic, and for two generations of Brewers fans, the wait has been a defining characteristic of following this franchise.

The 2026 version is different from the teams that came before. This group won 103 games during the regular season — the best record of any remaining team in the playoffs — and earned home-field advantage for the World Series. They are not a fluke. They earned every game of this October run, including four games against the Dodgers that required the kind of late-inning resilience that defines championship teams.

The Dodgers, for their part, entered October as one of the most formidable teams in baseball. Their 14 consecutive postseason appearances — beginning in 2013, including multiple World Series appearances and championships — make them one of the defining dynasties of modern baseball. The Brewers ended it. That context is not lost on anyone in Milwaukee or Los Angeles.

There is also a distinction that will be remembered in baseball history: the Brewers are the first team to sweep a best-of-7 series while trailing at some point in every game of it. They are also the first team to sweep the same team that swept them the prior postseason — the Dodgers eliminated Milwaukee in the 2025 playoffs by sweep. The Brewers returned the favor in 2026.

What's Next

The World Series begins Friday, October 23, at American Family Field in Milwaukee. The Brewers hold home-field advantage by virtue of their 103-59 record, best among remaining teams. Games 1 and 2 are in Milwaukee; Games 3, 4, and 5 are at the American League winner's park; Games 6 and 7 (if necessary) return to Milwaukee.

The ALCS is still in progress — Cleveland leads Tampa Bay 2-1, with Game 4 tonight at Progressive Field (8 PM ET on TBS). Whoever comes out of that series will face a Brewers team that just swept the defending dynasty and is playing the best baseball of any team left standing. Milwaukee has been waiting since 1982. The wait is over. The work begins again next Friday.
