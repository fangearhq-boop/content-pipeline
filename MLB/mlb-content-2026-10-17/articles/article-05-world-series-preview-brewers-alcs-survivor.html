World Series Preview: Milwaukee Is Ready — Who Are They Waiting For?

By Ryan Calloway | October 17, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The Milwaukee Brewers swept the Dodgers. They are in the World Series. They are waiting.

ALCS Game 4 is tonight at Progressive Field — the Cleveland Guardians lead Tampa Bay 2-1 after the game was rained out Friday and rescheduled for Saturday at 8 PM ET on TBS. One team will advance. One will go home. Whoever survives will fly to Milwaukee next week to face a Brewers team that just played its best baseball of the season in eliminating the defending dynasty.

The World Series begins Friday, October 23 at American Family Field. All games are on FOX at 8 PM ET. Milwaukee hosts Games 1 and 2, with Games 3 through 5 at the AL winner's park and Games 6 and 7 returning to Milwaukee if necessary. The Brewers earned home-field advantage by going 103-59 — the best record of any remaining team in the bracket.

What the Brewers Bring

The Brewers won the NLCS not because one player dominated it, but because the entire roster executed. Garrett Mitchell has been the signature moment-maker — he hit go-ahead homers in back-to-back NLCS games, including the decisive shot off Tanner Scott in Game 4. David Hamilton adds a dimension of speed and pop that disrupts opposing defensive alignment. The bullpen has been shutdown all postseason, limiting the Dodgers' lineup to tight margins in every game of a four-game sweep.

Jake Bauers, the NLCS MVP, represents the philosophy that defines this team: collective execution over individual stardom. The Brewers do not have a Shohei Ohtani. They have 25 players who show up prepared, execute the game plan, and find ways to win when they trail. That is a legitimate formula for a World Series championship, and the NLCS was the latest proof.

The Two Possible Opponents

If it's Cleveland: The Guardians are built in a similar mold to the Brewers — homegrown talent, late-inning drama, a team that wins the right way rather than the expensive way. Jose Ramirez is the offensive engine; the pitching staff, led by a depth-first approach, has kept them competitive against Tampa Bay. A Brewers-Guardians World Series would be a matchup of two of baseball's best small-market stories in 2026.

If it's Tampa Bay: The Rays bring the Friedman-era analytical approach and a pitching staff that, even without Shane McClanahan, has executed well enough to push Cleveland to a near-elimination game. Freddy Peralta's six scoreless innings in ALCS Game 2 were a reminder of what this Tampa staff can do. A Brewers-Rays World Series would be an analytics-heavy matchup that would reward detail-oriented fans.

Either way, Milwaukee is ready. They swept the best team in the National League. They are rested. They are playing at home.

What's Next

Watch ALCS Game 4 tonight at 8 PM ET on TBS. If Cleveland wins, the Guardians advance and the World Series matchup is set. If Tampa Bay wins, Game 5 is Sunday at 5 PM ET — also at Progressive Field in Cleveland. The series could end tonight or it could end Sunday. Either way, Milwaukee's wait ends next Friday.

The 2026 World Series is one week away. The Brewers have been waiting since 1982. The ballpark is ready. The roster is ready. The opponent is about to be determined.
