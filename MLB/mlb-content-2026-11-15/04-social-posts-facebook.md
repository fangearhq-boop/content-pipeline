#### Long-Form Post — 8:00 AM ET

The biggest week of this MLB offseason officially begins today.

Tarik Skubal — back-to-back Cy Young Award winner, 1.80 ERA in 2026, 252 strikeouts — is meeting with teams in California this week. The Mets go first. Owner Steve Cohen and president David Stearns are both expected in the room.

### STORY 3: Pre-Lockout Spending Spree

#### Long-Form Post — 12:30 PM ET

The free agent market moves slowly in November — until it doesn't.

This week, it's moving.

Jake Bauers: 4 years, $58 million to the Red Sox. Evan Phillips: 3 years, $35 million to the Braves. The Blue Jays are actively targeting Ketel Marte via trade. The Marlins have put Otto Lopez, Xavier Edwards, and Kyle Stowers on the market. Teams are calling the A's about Jacob Wilson.

Here's why it's happening now: December 1.

That's the day the current MLB-MLBPA CBA expires. When a lockout begins, all communication between players and clubs officially stops. No signings. No trades. No negotiations.

The last lockout (2021-22) ran 99 days and threatened Spring Training. Teams that didn't get their business done before December 2, 2021 spent the whole winter watching the clock.

No one wants to do that again.

The next 16 days are about to be as busy as any two-week stretch in baseball's offseason history. Hold on.

### STORY 5: Teruaki Sato — The Wild Card in a Wild Offseason

#### Long-Form Post — 6:00 PM ET

You may not know his name yet. By February, everyone in baseball will.

Teruaki Sato is the 2026 NPB Triple Crown winner for the Hanshin Tigers — a 25-year-old who hit .308 with a .613 slugging percentage and 39 home runs while also winning the batting title. He was officially posted for MLB teams on November 1, opening a 45-day signing window that runs through December 16.

That's after the December 1 lockout.

Which creates a fascinating question: Can teams structure a "lockout agreement" to sign him during the freeze? The answer is complicated — and worth watching closely.

Teams reportedly interested: the Mets, Yankees, Dodgers, Phillies, and Red Sox. Contract projections run from $80 million (Heyman) to more than $120 million (others). The main question scouts have raised is his defense at third base — most teams project him as a DH or corner outfielder.

He won't come cheap. But a Triple Crown winner at 25, posting these numbers, is as rare as it gets.
