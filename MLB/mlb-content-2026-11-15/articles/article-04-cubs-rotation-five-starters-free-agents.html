Every Pitcher in the Cubs' 2026 Rotation Is a Free Agent — All Five

By Marcus Cole | November 15, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The Chicago Cubs used five starting pitchers regularly in their 2026 rotation. All five are free agents. That is not a rotation turnover — it is a complete rotation reset, and it is the most urgent rebuilding challenge in baseball this offseason.

Cubs president of baseball operations Jed Hoyer has identified starting pitching as the No. 1 offseason priority. With a lineup that has real pieces and a bullpen with legitimate depth, the rotation is the one gap that determines whether the Cubs are a contender or a .500 team in 2027.

What Happened to the Original Plan

The Cubs were widely reported as interested in Tarik Skubal — the top free agent pitcher on the market — earlier in the offseason. They have since reportedly stepped back from that pursuit. The reasons have not been confirmed publicly, but multiple reports indicate the Cubs are no longer in the Skubal discussions, leaving them to pursue the second tier of the rotation market.

That second tier is not empty. Michael King, who posted a 3.21 ERA in 32 starts before opting out of his Padres deal, is one of the best available arms outside Skubal. Freddy Peralta, who split 2026 between the Mets and Rays with varying results, is another legitimate candidate. Tatsuya Imai, from Japan's NPB, represents a potentially cost-effective option for one of the five rotation spots.

The Math of the Problem

The Cubs need five starters. Even in the most optimistic scenario, they are unlikely to sign five quality free agents in this market. The realistic plan likely involves two or three free agent signings, one or two trades, and possibly a prospect ready to contribute from Triple-A.

What complicates the picture further: with the lockout expected December 1, the Cubs have 16 days to close deals on their top targets before the market freezes. Any starters not signed by November 30 become a problem to solve after the new CBA is reached — whenever that is. The 2021-22 lockout ran 99 days. Teams that did not complete their major offseason business before December 2, 2021 found themselves in difficult positions when Spring Training was threatened.

What's Next

Hoyer's first order of business this week is clarity on King and Peralta, the most likely Skubal alternatives. If King signs with another team before November 30, the Cubs' options narrow further. The 16-day sprint before the lockout is effectively a deadline for Chicago's rotation rebuild.

Wrigley Field is one of baseball's most storied venues. The Cubs' fan base deserves a rotation that matches the atmosphere. The next two weeks will tell us whether this front office can deliver one.
