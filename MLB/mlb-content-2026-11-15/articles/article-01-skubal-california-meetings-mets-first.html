Skubal's California Meetings Start This Week — and the Mets Go First

By Marcus Cole | November 15, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The offseason's most consequential free agent meetings are beginning. Tarik Skubal — back-to-back Cy Young Award winner, 1.80 ERA in 2026, 252 strikeouts across 174.2 innings — is meeting with teams in California this week. The New York Mets are first in the room.

Mets owner Steve Cohen and president of baseball operations David Stearns are both expected to attend the meeting in person, according to multiple reports. Jon Heyman of the New York Post has said the Mets are "probably the favorite" to land Skubal. No other team in this process has received that characterization from a reporter with Heyman's industry access.

Six teams in total are scheduled to meet with Skubal during the California series. The Dodgers, Padres, and Blue Jays have all been confirmed by multiple sources. Two additional teams have not been publicly identified. Skubal's representation has set a goal of closing a deal before December 1 — the date the current MLB-MLBPA Collective Bargaining Agreement expires and a lockout is widely expected to begin.

The Contract Projections

Contract projections for Skubal have been consistent across industry observers: eight years, in the range of $352 million, with an average annual value near $44 million. If accurate, that would shatter the record for a pitcher in terms of AAV — Yoshinobu Yamamoto's $27 million per year with the Dodgers would be eclipsed by more than $17 million annually.

No qualifying offer was attached to Skubal's free agency, which matters: the team that signs him will not forfeit a draft pick as compensation. That removes a meaningful barrier for teams like the Blue Jays, who protect draft capital carefully.

Why the Mets Are the Favorite

The Mets' positioning at the front of the meeting schedule is not accidental. Teams typically negotiate the meeting order with agents, and going first provides an opportunity to shape the conversation before other bidders enter. Cohen's willingness to personally attend — after spending lavishly to build the current roster — signals that this is a top organizational priority.

The Dodgers remain the perceived default for elite pitching free agents, given their recent track record. But Heyman's framing of the Mets as "probably the favorite" reflects reporting from within the process, not speculation. The Mets need a frontline ace. Stearns has been building toward exactly this moment.

What's Next

The Mets meeting this week is the beginning of the process, not the end. After six teams make their pitches, Skubal's camp will evaluate offers and potentially request follow-up conversations before choosing. Given the December 1 deadline, a signing announcement could come as early as the week of November 22 — or as late as November 30, right at the wire.

If Skubal signs before the lockout, it will be the defining move of the 2026-27 offseason. If the meetings run long and no deal closes before December 1, his situation freezes with the rest of the market. Watch closely this week.
