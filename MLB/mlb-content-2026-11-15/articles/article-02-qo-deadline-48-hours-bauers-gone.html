QO Deadline Is Monday — Bauers Already Said No. Here Come the Rest.

By Jake Torres | November 15, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Monday at 4:00 PM ET, the qualifying offer deadline arrives — and the first decision is already in.

First baseman Jake Bauers declined the $23.15 million, one-year qualifying offer from the Seattle Mariners and signed a four-year, $58 million contract with the Boston Red Sox, ESPN's Jeff Passan reported Friday. The math was simple: a guaranteed $58 million versus $23.15 million for one year with the Mariners. Bauers took the multi-year security, as any rational player would.

Seven players remain on the QO clock, all expected to follow the same path.

Who Is Still Deciding

The seven remaining qualifying offer recipients and their situations:

Randy Arozarena (OF, Mariners): The most valuable bat available on this list. Seattle's decision not to re-sign him — per earlier reporting — means he is a true free agent once he declines. Multiple teams have been linked to his market.

David Bednar (RP, Yankees): An elite closer who performed in a contract year. Teams bidding for a proven closer in a thin relief market will push his value past $23.15M in guaranteed years.

Ryan Jeffers (C, Twins): A below-average hitter but a defensive asset behind the plate. His market depends heavily on how teams value that defensive premium.

Seiya Suzuki (OF, Cubs): The Cubs extended the QO; Suzuki returning to Chicago would be an unusual outcome given the rotation rebuild the team is pursuing. Expect a decline.

Josh Lowe (OF), Angel Morejon (RP), Jake Rogers (1B/OF): All three are expected to decline, with multi-year deals on the horizon in a market where teams are rushing before the December 1 lockout.

The Historical Context

Of the 157 players who have ever received qualifying offers, only 18 have accepted — an acceptance rate of approximately 11.5 percent. Players typically accept only when their market is thin enough that the guaranteed $23.15 million represents fair value. In this case, with 16 days until a lockout, teams are motivated to sign players before the freeze. That urgency benefits the players, not the teams extending QOs.

This may also be the last set of qualifying offers issued under the current system. Yahoo Sports has reported that the QO mechanism is unlikely to survive the next CBA negotiation, meaning this year's recipients are potentially the last players ever to receive the offer in its current form.

What's Next

Once the Monday 4:00 PM ET deadline passes and all seven remaining players decline, they become unrestricted free agents with zero draft-pick cost for any signing team. Combined with the Skubal meetings happening this week and the pre-lockout urgency driving activity, the next two weeks of the MLB offseason will move faster than any comparable stretch in recent memory.

Watch Monday afternoon. The market is about to open wide.
