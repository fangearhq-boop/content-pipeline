The Pre-Lockout Sprint Is On: Teams Are Spending Before December 1 Changes Everything

By Ryan Calloway | November 15, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

December 1 is a hard wall. When the current MLB-MLBPA Collective Bargaining Agreement expires at 11:59 PM that night, all player-team communication officially stops. No signings. No trades. No negotiations. The last time this happened — the 2021-22 lockout — it ran 99 days and threatened Spring Training before a new deal was reached on March 10, 2022.

Teams remember. And they are not waiting around.

What Has Already Been Signed

The activity began in earnest this week. First baseman Jake Bauers signed a four-year, $58 million contract with the Boston Red Sox — declining the Mariners' $23.15M qualifying offer for a deal worth $35 million more in total guarantee (ESPN, Jeff Passan). The Braves signed reliever Evan Phillips to a three-year, $35 million deal. Both signings happened within a 48-hour window.

These are not small-market, low-risk moves. These are organizations committing significant guaranteed money on a short timeline because they believe the lockout is coming and they would rather pay a premium now than be frozen out of their target entirely.

What Is Moving This Week

The Blue Jays are "working hard to find offense" and have been connected to Ketel Marte via trade, per SportsNet. The Marlins have put multiple players on the market — outfielder Otto Lopez, infielder Xavier Edwards, and corner bat Kyle Stowers. Teams have called the Oakland A's about infielder Jacob Wilson. The Giants are declining calls about Logan Webb, apparently content to hold their ace.

The Skubal meetings happening this week — with the Mets first — represent the biggest potential transaction in the market. If Skubal signs before December 1, the two-week sprint will have included the most expensive pitcher signing in baseball history.

The Economics of Urgency

Compressed markets favor sellers. When buyers have a deadline and sellers know it, prices rise. This is as true in baseball as anywhere else. The Bauers deal — four years, $58M for a first baseman who accepted a qualifying offer offer — reflects a market paying a premium for certainty. The Braves' three-year deal for Phillips reflects similar logic.

Teams that have not identified their priority targets and begun conversations are already behind. The window is 16 days and counting.

What's Next

The next 16 days will be the most active stretch of the 2026-27 offseason. With qualifying offers expiring Monday, Skubal meetings this week, and the lockout deadline looming, expect announcement after announcement before November ends. After December 1, the market goes quiet until a new CBA is reached — whenever that turns out to be.
