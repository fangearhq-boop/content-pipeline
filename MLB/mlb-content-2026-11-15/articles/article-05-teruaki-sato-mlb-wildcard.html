Teruaki Sato: Triple Crown, 39 Home Runs, Age 25 — and He's Coming to MLB

By Jake Torres | November 15, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Somewhere in the offseason noise around Tarik Skubal, qualifying offers, and the approaching December 1 lockout, it is easy to overlook the most intriguing international story in this market. Teruaki Sato, the 2026 NPB Triple Crown winner for the Hanshin Tigers, was posted for MLB teams on November 1. His 45-day window runs through December 16 — after the lockout deadline.

At age 25, Sato batted .308 with a .385 on-base percentage and a .613 slugging percentage, hitting 39 home runs and driving in 106 runs. He won the NPB batting title, home run title, and RBI title in the same season — the Triple Crown, in its purest form. The numbers are not small-sample flukes. They are consistent with his trajectory through several NPB seasons.

The Teams and the Money

Reports have identified at least five teams with serious interest: the New York Mets, New York Yankees, Los Angeles Dodgers, Philadelphia Phillies, and Boston Red Sox. That is a who's-who of market-size franchises willing to pay for elite offense.

Contract projections vary widely. Jon Heyman has suggested a range around four years and $80 million as a floor. Others, including Jim Bowden, project north of $120 million based on comparable Japanese imports and what organizations have paid for similar power profiles.

The honest uncertainty in these projections reflects that Sato is a genuinely difficult player to price — elite offensive production in NPB, a 25-year-old age profile, and a position flexibility question that depends entirely on how good (or bad) his defense actually is in MLB conditions.

The Defense Question

The primary scouting concern raised about Sato is his work at third base. Most teams project him as a DH or corner outfielder at the major league level. For American League teams — where the DH is in every lineup — that is not a barrier. For National League teams without a natural DH spot built in, the defensive profile complicates fit.

Of the interested teams: the Mets, Yankees, and Red Sox have used the DH slot heavily. The Dodgers and Phillies are more complex fits given their existing corner bat depth. The shape of Sato's final market will depend in part on how the defense is evaluated in workouts and meetings.

The Lockout Wrinkle

Sato's 45-day window extends to December 16 — two weeks after the anticipated December 1 lockout start. How teams navigate that window legally is unclear. The standard practice during the 2021-22 lockout was that international posting agreements signed before the lockout were honored, but new posting windows did not operate during the freeze. If the CBA expires and a lockout begins December 1 without a Sato signing completed, the legal structure of his window becomes the subject of negotiation between MLB and the MLBPA.

Teams interested in Sato have an incentive to close a deal before December 1 for exactly this reason. That deadline pressure, combined with a competitive field of bidders, is likely to produce a signing announcement before the end of November.

What's Next

Sato is not the top story of this offseason — Skubal is. But he may be the most consequential player for the team that signs him. A 25-year-old Triple Crown winner available on the open market is rare at any price. The bidding will be intense, and the announcement will come fast once it happens.

Watch for Sato news in parallel with the Skubal meetings this week. This market has two marquee players — and both could be signed in the same two-week sprint before December 1.
