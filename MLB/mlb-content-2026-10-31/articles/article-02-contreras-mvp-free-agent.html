William Contreras Made History as the First Brewers World Series MVP. Now He Might Leave.

By Marcus Cole | October 31, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The award was unanimous in every way that mattered except formally. William Contreras was the best player of the 2026 World Series from the first inning of Game 1 through the final out of Game 5, and the official MVP designation confirmed what anyone watching already knew. He became the first Milwaukee Brewer ever to win the World Series Most Valuable Player award. And now, after one of the finest individual postseason performances by a catcher in recent memory, he might be pitching his services to someone else this winter.

The Series

Contreras hit .381 in the World Series, going 8-for-21 with 4 RBI and a stolen base. His offensive contributions were matched, and perhaps exceeded, by his defensive work. He threw out four Tampa Bay base stealers — in a series where several of the attempts came from the Rays specifically trying to exploit perceived vulnerabilities against the Brewers' battery. He went 10-for-13 on automatic ball challenges, a number that reflects his command of the strike zone and his ability to manage game situations in a system designed to challenge that skill.

The World Series capped an exceptional full postseason. Contreras hit .444 against the Padres in the NLDS and .357 against the Dodgers in the NLCS before arriving at .381 in the Fall Classic. His full postseason slash reflected a player who was locked in from October's opening round through its final game.

The award itself carries historical weight beyond the Brewers. Contreras became only the eighth catcher in history to win the World Series MVP, and the first to do so since Salvador Perez won it with the Kansas City Royals in 2015. Those names belong to a short list of players who defined a series on both sides of the ball.

The Regular Season Behind the October

Contreras was not just a postseason performer in 2026. He hit .270 with 18 home runs and posted a career-best 94 RBI across the regular season. For a catcher, that kind of offensive production while managing a pitching staff that went 11-2 in the postseason makes him one of the most complete players at his position in the sport. His 2026 season, from April through October, is the argument for him as the best catcher in baseball.

The Free Agency Question

Contreras earned $9.5 million in 2026. Reports indicate he is expected to test free agency this winter, which would make him one of the most significant additions to an already loaded market. A catcher who can hit for average and power, manage a pitching staff at the highest level, control the running game, and deliver in October — that player does not stay unsigned for long once the market opens.

For Milwaukee, the prospect of losing the player who just brought them their first championship is the price of winning. The Brewers will have to decide what Contreras is worth to them against a market that will pay a significant premium for exactly his skill set.

What's Next

The five-day exclusive negotiating window between teams and their own free agents runs through approximately November 4. After that, any team in baseball can approach Contreras. The CBA expiration on December 1 adds urgency — a lockout after that date would freeze all transactions, making the next four weeks the most important window for any signing in baseball, including one for the player who just won the World Series MVP.
