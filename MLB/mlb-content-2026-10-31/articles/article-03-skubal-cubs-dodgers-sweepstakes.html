Tarik Skubal Free Agency: Cubs and Dodgers Lead a $355 Million Sweepstakes

By Jake Torres | October 31, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The biggest question in baseball this winter is not whether Tarik Skubal will get paid. It is where. The two-time American League Cy Young Award winner is the No. 1 free agent in the 2026-27 offseason, and with the free agency market now officially open and teams already positioning themselves, the pursuit for one of the most dominant starting pitchers in the sport is moving fast — with an important deadline hanging over every negotiation.

The Market for Skubal

A panel of 27 MLB insiders projects Skubal's contract at an average of 8 years and $355 million, with some projections reaching as high as $500 million on the outer edge. NBC Sports' tracker projects 7 years, $400 million. At any point in that range, a Skubal deal would set the record for the richest contract ever given to a free agent pitcher.

The suitor list is long: the Chicago Cubs, Los Angeles Dodgers, New York Mets, Toronto Blue Jays, Philadelphia Phillies, and Skubal's former team, the Detroit Tigers. Skubal, who was traded from Detroit to the Dodgers before the 2026 trade deadline, said publicly that he "would love to return" to Detroit — a statement that complicates things for the teams pursuing him but confirms that his market will be as competitive as any since free agency became the sport's dominant economic force.

Why the Cubs Make Sense

ESPN called Chicago a "perfect fit" for Skubal, and the case is straightforward. The Cubs' rotation ranked 18th in ERA and 21st in strikeout rate in 2026. Adding a two-time Cy Young winner at the top of the staff does not just fill a need — it transforms a rotation from a liability into an asset. The Cubs have the market, the resources, and the organizational incentive to spend at the top of the pitcher market.

The Dodgers, meanwhile, traded for Skubal specifically to make a postseason run. The familiarity that comes from that half-season together gives Los Angeles a relationship-based advantage that other teams have to work to close. Whether they can outbid a market with this much depth of interest remains to be seen.

The CBA Clock

Every conversation about Skubal's free agency carries a December 1 asterisk. The current collective bargaining agreement expires at 11:59 PM on December 1, and a lockout beginning December 2 would freeze all transactions — including any deals not signed before the deadline. Teams and agents are aware of the clock. The urgency to close a deal before December 1, while also not sacrificing leverage by rushing, will define the tone of negotiations between now and the end of November.

For Skubal, the calculus is unusual. He is the most sought-after pitcher on the market, and he has every incentive to let the process play out. But if the lockout freezes the market for months — as the 2021-22 lockout did — the first contract signed after a new CBA could be in a different economic environment than the one that exists right now.

What's Next

The five-day exclusive window between teams and their own free agents runs through roughly November 4. After that, the full market opens. Skubal was not with the Dodgers long enough for them to hold exclusive negotiating rights in a meaningful sense, so the field is effectively open now. The next four weeks may be the most important in the recent history of pitcher free agency. The number that closes this deal will reset expectations for every starting pitcher who follows.
