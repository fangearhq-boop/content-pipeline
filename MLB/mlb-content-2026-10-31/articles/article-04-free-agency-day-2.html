MLB Free Agency Day 2: 155 Players on Market, Options Exercised, Freddy Peralta Among the Stars

By Ryan Calloway | October 31, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

The 2026-27 MLB free agency market did not slow down on Day 2. Fourteen more players entered the pool Friday, bringing the total to 155, and a wave of option decisions clarified which stars are staying put for 2027 and which are headed to market alongside Tarik Skubal and the rest of a deep free agent class. The offseason is moving quickly, and the clock toward the December 1 CBA deadline adds urgency to every conversation.

The Players Who Are Staying

Bo Bichette made his decision official Friday, exercising a $42 million player option to remain with the New York Mets for the 2027 season. The shortstop's choice to stay removes one of the most accomplished middle infielders from the market and signals that Bichette sees his future in New York. The Mets retain a cornerstone of their infield.

In Atlanta, the Braves exercised options on two pillars of their core. Ronald Acuña Jr. will return for 2027 under a $17 million option, and Ozzie Albies will remain at second base at $7 million. Both decisions preserve the Braves' lineup identity heading into an offseason where their rotation will need significant attention.

New Names on the Market

Michael King and Sonny Gray were among the 14 players who became free agents Friday after teams declined their options. King, a right-handed reliever who has been one of the more effective arms in recent seasons, joins a relief market that will draw significant interest. Gray, a veteran starting pitcher, enters a market thin on established starting options behind Skubal and, with the Brewers, Freddy Peralta.

Speaking of Peralta — the Brewers right-hander who was a key component of Milwaukee's World Series championship run is ranked the No. 2 overall free agent in the class by NBC Sports, trailing only Skubal. Peralta's market will be substantial. Teams looking for rotation help who cannot land Skubal will redirect their energy to Peralta, and at least some of them will make very competitive offers.

The Exclusive Window

Teams currently have a five-day exclusive negotiating window with their own free agents — a period that runs through approximately November 4. During this window, players cannot negotiate with other clubs, which allows teams to make their case for retention before the broader market opens. Once that window closes, the conversations that have been happening in parallel behind the scenes can become official offers from anyone in baseball.

For a market this deep and this expensive — with Skubal at the top and names like Peralta, King, Gray, and potentially Contreras behind him — the pace of the offseason is going to be dictated by how quickly teams either commit to the top of the market or pivot to the next tier.

What's Next

November 4 ends the exclusive window and opens the full market. From there, teams have until December 1 to complete any signings before the CBA expires and a lockout is expected to freeze transactions. The next 31 days represent the most active period this offseason will see before the calendar forces a pause. The option decisions have set the landscape. The real bidding is about to begin.
