CBA Countdown: 31 Days Until Baseball's Dec. 1 Deadline and a Likely Lockout

By Marcus Cole | October 31, 2026 | Ballpark Banter

Baseball's offseason is operating under a hard deadline that most casual fans have not yet registered. The MLB collective bargaining agreement expires at 11:59 PM ET on December 1, 2026 — 31 days from today. When that clock hits zero, the owners are widely expected to lock out the players, freezing all transactions and sending baseball's hot stove into an unexpected deep freeze at the worst possible moment for a market that just opened.

What the Two Sides Want

The dispute is as fundamental as labor disputes in professional sports get. The owners have proposed the first salary cap in MLB history since the 1994 attempt, setting it at $245 million with a $171 million floor. The Major League Baseball Players Association opposes any hard cap on salaries — a position they have held since the 1994 strike ended without a cap — and wants instead a luxury-tax threshold of approximately $300 million with no ceiling above it.

The gap between $245 million and $300 million sounds manageable in abstract, but the structural difference between a hard cap and a luxury-tax system is not just financial. A hard cap limits how much any team can spend. A luxury tax slows spending through financial penalties but does not prevent teams with resources from exceeding any threshold they choose to pay a price to cross. Those are philosophically different systems, and neither side has historically been willing to swap one for the other without a fight.

The owners have also proposed raising the minimum salary from $780,000 to $1 million, a measure that would benefit the players at the lowest end of the pay scale even as the overall cap structure represents a concession that the union has spent 30 years refusing to make.

What a Lockout Means in Practice

A lockout freezes baseball operations. Teams cannot sign free agents. They cannot complete trades. They cannot extend players under contract. The winter meetings, where deals typically accelerate, become a formality in a frozen market. Spring Training, currently scheduled to begin February 19, 2027, faces cancellation or delay if a new agreement is not reached before players are required to report.

The 2021-22 lockout offers the relevant precedent. That work stoppage began December 2 and ended March 10, costing Spring Training games. The regular season was preserved only because both sides reached a deal about 90 minutes before the first regular season games would have been officially cancelled. The timeline was that close.

If this lockout follows a similar arc, the best-case scenario is a deal reached in February or early March. The worst case extends further into the spring and begins affecting the regular season.

The Impact on the Free Agent Market Right Now

Every general manager, agent, and free agent in baseball is acutely aware of December 1. The rational strategy for teams that want to sign players — particularly at the top of the market, where deals like Tarik Skubal's projected eight-year contract take weeks to negotiate and finalize — is to close those deals before the calendar flips. The urgency is not artificial. A lockout freezes a negotiation in whatever state it was in, and picking it back up months later under a new CBA and new economic conditions is a meaningfully different calculation than finishing it now.

This is why the next 31 days will produce more significant contract announcements than almost any equivalent period in recent offseason history. Teams know what is coming. They are moving accordingly.

What's Next

Negotiations between the league and the union are ongoing, but reports have put the odds of a deal before December 1 at roughly five percent. That is not zero, and a last-minute agreement is not impossible — baseball has beaten unlikely odds before. But anyone planning the offseason should plan for a lockout beginning December 2 and being resolved sometime between February and April. The signing window between now and December 1 may be short. The teams that act quickly will be better positioned when the market eventually reopens.
