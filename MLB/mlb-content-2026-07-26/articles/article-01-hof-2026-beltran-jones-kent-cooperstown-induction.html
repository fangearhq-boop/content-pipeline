Carlos Beltrán, Andruw Jones, and Jeff Kent Join the Hall of Fame at Cooperstown

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | July 26, 2026

The Baseball Hall of Fame has a new class. Carlos Beltrán, Andruw Jones, and Jeff Kent were inducted Sunday at Clark Sports Center in Cooperstown, New York, in a ceremony that began at 1:30 PM ET and drew a record 61 returning Hall of Famers — the largest gathering in the event's history.

Three careers. Three cap choices. Three paths to the bronze plaque that defines a baseball legacy.

Carlos Beltrán — The Mets Cap

Beltrán chose the New York Mets cap, representing the franchise where he posted his highest career WAR as a single player — 31.1. Five of his nine All-Star selections came in a Mets uniform. All three Gold Gloves. Both Silver Sluggers. He was a switch-hitter who never compromised his production from either side of the plate and played for nine teams across a 20-year career.

Beltrán was elected by the Baseball Writers' Association of America in his fourth try on the ballot. His 2004 National League Championship Series — 8 home runs and 14 RBI, one of the greatest postseason runs in the sport's history — is the first thing most fans remember. His career total of 435 home runs and three Gold Gloves as a center fielder is the second.

He came from Puerto Rico, from a humble family, and became the player a Hall of Famer once predicted he would be before he had played a single major league game.

Andruw Jones — The Braves Cap

Jones chose the Atlanta Braves, where he earned all 10 of his consecutive Gold Gloves and spent 12 of his 17 major league seasons. He was elected by the BBWAA in his ninth try — one of the longer waits in the modern era. He made them wait. He did not make the argument himself. The game made it for him.

Ten consecutive Gold Gloves. 1998 through 2007, every single year, in center field for Atlanta. He said Sunday it was with the Braves that he first chased the dream he had since he was a child in Curaçao. That dream ends in Cooperstown.

The debate about whether he is the greatest defensive center fielder in history is not really a debate. The analytics, the eyeball test, and the consensus of everyone who watched him play the position all arrive at the same answer. No one has done it better.

Jeff Kent — The Giants Cap

Kent chose the San Francisco Giants cap, where he spent six of his 17 seasons — more than any other franchise — and won the 2000 NL MVP. He holds the all-time record for home runs by a second baseman: 351 hit from that position, 377 in his career. He was not elected by the BBWAA. He was selected by the Hall of Fame's Contemporary Baseball Era Committee in December 2025, entering the 2026 class.

The Giants are retiring his No. 21. Kent earned four Silver Sluggers and five All-Star selections. He is also the 90th alumnus of American Legion Baseball to be inducted into the Hall — a group that includes some of the sport's most decorated names.

He said his stops along the way were important, but it was in San Francisco where he had the most success and spent the most time.

The Ceremony

A record 61 Hall of Famers attended. Admission to the ceremony at Clark Sports Center was free and open to the public. The event was broadcast live on MLB Network, which has aired every induction ceremony since its launch in 2009. The Hall of Fame Weekend continues through July 27.

What's Next

With the Class of 2026 inducted, the Baseball Hall of Fame turns to its next ballot cycle. The BBWAA vote for the Class of 2027 will open to voters later this year. Jeff Kent's plaque joins those of Beltrán and Jones as the newest additions to the Hall's permanent collection in Cooperstown. The Giants will formally retire Kent's No. 21 in a ceremony at Oracle Park to be scheduled for the 2026-27 offseason.
