Kyle Schwarber Leads MLB with 32 Home Runs at 35 — On Pace to Threaten 60 Again

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | July 26, 2026

Kyle Schwarber is 35 years old. He leads Major League Baseball in home runs with 32.

Yordan Alvarez has 31. Ben Rice has 29. Every other name on the MLB home run leaderboard is younger. Most are significantly younger. Schwarber leads them all.

Last season, Schwarber led the National League with 56 home runs. He was 34 then. He is doing it again at 35. Power hitters are not supposed to age this way.

The Numbers

Through the Phillies' first 49 games this season, Schwarber had hit 20 home runs — a pace that ESPN projected would produce 66 over a full 162-game schedule. He has settled into a slightly more modest pace since, but 32 home runs in late July still places him on track for a total in the range of 55 to 60, depending on his production over the final two months.

There are eight instances in MLB history of a player hitting 60 or more home runs in a season. None of those players were 35. None of them had led their league with 56 the year before. Schwarber is not in the same tier yet, but he is in the conversation, and that is remarkable by itself.

Why He Can Still Do This

Schwarber has never been a tools-based home run hitter. He does not carry the natural athleticism of some sluggers his age. What he has is swing mechanics, pitch selection, and a disciplined approach that has become more refined over the course of his career, not less.

He hits left-handed to fields where the fences are reachable. He lays off breaking balls in the dirt. He punishes fastballs in the zone. He does not swing less often as he ages — he swings smarter. The Phillies' lineup protects him: Bryce Harper hits behind him, and Cristopher Sánchez's rotation-leading performance has kept the team in contention, which means teams cannot pitch around Schwarber without consequences.

Historical Context

The oldest player ever to lead a major league in home runs, heading into 2026, had done it in his early 30s. Schwarber at 35 is in territory that has no precedent. At some point, the decline is supposed to arrive — exit velocity drops, bat speed slows, the timing that made everything work starts to slip a frame.

It has not happened. Not yet. And with two months left in the season, the most honest assessment is to stop trying to explain it and just watch what he does.

What's Next

Kyle Schwarber and the Philadelphia Phillies are 56-48 in NL Wild Card position as of late July. The trade deadline on August 3 could reshape the Phillies' roster around him — they have been linked to multiple deadline acquisitions. The NL home run race — Schwarber at 32, Alvarez at 31, Rice at 29 — has two months to play out. The 60 HR conversation is not over. It depends entirely on what Schwarber does in August and September.
