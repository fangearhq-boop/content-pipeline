Pete Crow-Armstrong Breaks the Statcast Record for 5-Star Catches — and He Is Only 24

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | July 26, 2026

On July 21, Pete Crow-Armstrong made his 33rd career five-star catch. It was a routine play for him. It was the most such catches in Statcast history.

A five-star catch is a play where the fielder has a 25% or lower probability of making the catch at the moment the ball leaves the bat. Three of four center fielders, in the same situation, do not get there. Crow-Armstrong gets there — and he has been getting there at a pace no outfielder has matched since Statcast began tracking catch difficulty in 2016.

The Record

The previous record belonged to Kevin Kiermaier, a four-time Gold Glove winner and one of the most celebrated defenders of his generation. Kiermaier built his record over the course of a full career — years of diving catches and spectacular routes in center field.

Crow-Armstrong passed him in fewer than 62 opportunities. Every other player in the top 10 of all-time five-star catches needed at least 100 opportunities to reach their total. PCA needed fewer than two-thirds of that. He has made his MLB debut in September 2023. He has not finished two and a half full seasons.

He is 24 years old.

What Five-Star Catches Actually Mean

Statcast's catch probability model calculates the likelihood of a fielder catching a ball based on distance to travel, time available, and direction of movement at the moment of contact. A play labeled as five-star has a maximum 25% success rate across all fielders who have faced that type of batted ball in that type of situation.

Making one of these plays requires elite instincts, elite first-step reaction, and elite closing speed. Making 33 of them in fewer than 2.5 seasons — at a frequency that is measurably higher than any outfielder before him — is the statistical equivalent of a pitcher with a career no-hitter rate that makes the math difficult to process.

This is not a streak or a hot month. It is a career pattern, established early and sustained.

The Bigger Picture

Crow-Armstrong's offensive profile has also continued to develop since his debut. The Cubs have a player in center field who is simultaneously rewriting the sport's defensive record books while growing into a legitimate offensive contributor. The combination — elite defense and a developing offense — is the profile of an MVP candidate.

The record Crow-Armstrong broke was set by a player who was considered one of the best outfielders of his era. PCA surpassed him before he was old enough to have been a serious MLB prospect when Kiermaier was in his prime years. That is how young this is. That is how good this already is.

What's Next

Pete Crow-Armstrong holds the Statcast record for career five-star catches heading into August. The Chicago Cubs are 58-45 and in an NL Wild Card position, with the club's success closely linked to Crow-Armstrong's two-way contributions. MVP odds for PCA have been rising alongside his defensive accomplishments. The record he set on July 21 will not stay static — he will continue to add to it, and the number he reaches by the end of 2026 will set an even higher standard for whoever eventually tries to chase him down.
