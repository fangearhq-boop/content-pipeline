Ohtani Skips Sunday Bullpen, Roberts Cites 'Regression' — Pitching Return Now August at Earliest

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | July 26, 2026

Shohei Ohtani was scheduled to throw a bullpen session at Citi Field on Sunday. He did not throw. Manager Dave Roberts told reporters the session was skipped because of continued left knee discomfort — and used a word that changed the tone of the conversation: "regression."

"He just doesn't feel 100%," Roberts said, "because I think that the last 'pen that he had, there was a little bit of regression."

Ohtani will not return to pitching until the knee is fully right. Any timeline that had him back on the mound in late July is now gone. August is the earliest possibility, and even that is uncertain.

The Sequence of Events

Ohtani last pitched in a game on July 3. His line through that start: 8-2, 1.79 ERA, 95 strikeouts in 85.2 innings — among the best pitching seasons in baseball on a per-inning basis. He received an Orthovisc injection — a lubricant designed to relieve knee joint discomfort — during the All-Star break.

On July 22, Ohtani threw approximately 30 pitches in a bullpen session at Citizens Bank Park in Philadelphia. The session was described positively in initial reports. Roberts indicated a follow-up session would come within days — specifically targeting the July 26 window at Citi Field.

That session did not happen. The "regression" Roberts described has pushed back the entire timeline.

What It Means for the Dodgers

The Los Angeles Dodgers are 66-38 — the best record in baseball. They are built around Ohtani's two-way contribution. When he is on the mound, he is among the two or three most dominant starters in the game. Without his arm in the rotation for any extended period, the Dodgers must rely more heavily on Yoshinobu Yamamoto and depth options down the rotation.

He is still in the lineup as a designated hitter. He is hitting .288/.397/.536 in 2026 — above-average production that contributes to the offense. But the Dodgers spent record money to have Ohtani do what no one else can do: dominate a 40-man roster spot as both a top-three starter and a legitimate middle-of-the-order bat. The pitching half of that equation has been absent since July 3.

The Dodgers are aggressive buyers heading into the August 3 trade deadline and are widely expected to pursue pitching depth. Ohtani's extended absence may accelerate that calculus.

The Bigger Picture

Roberts has been measured throughout this process. "Remaining nimble" is the phrase the Dodgers have used. They are not rushing their best pitcher back onto a mound to get healthy for a July game when they have a 28-game lead in the NL West and a healthy Yamamoto behind him.

But "regression" is different from "cautious progress." The July 22 session was supposed to be the turning point. It was not. That is what changes the framing from recovery to setback.

What's Next

Ohtani will not pitch until his left knee is 100%. No new date has been set for his next bullpen attempt. The Dodgers face the Mets on Sunday at Citi Field before a return to Los Angeles for the next home stand. Roberts will provide updates as they become available. Any return to game action is August at the earliest. The Dodgers' next homestand and any bullpen session will be closely monitored.
