### STORY 1: MLB Hall of Fame Induction — Beltran, Jones, Kent

#### Long-Form Post — 1:30 PM ET

Today at Clark Sports Center, a mile south of the Baseball Hall of Fame in Cooperstown, Carlos Beltrán, Andruw Jones, and Jeff Kent take their place among the game's immortals.

A record 61 Hall of Famers are in attendance.

Beltrán wore a Mets cap. Five of his nine All-Star selections came in New York. Both Silver Sluggers. All three Gold Gloves. He posted a 31.1 WAR with the Mets — the franchise record for a single player. He played for nine teams over 20 seasons, switched hands at the plate with elite efficiency, and defined what a complete offensive player looked like in his era. He was elected by the BBWAA in his fourth try.

Jones wore an Atlanta Braves cap. He earned all 10 of his consecutive Gold Gloves as a Brave. If you ask anyone who watched him play in the 1990s and 2000s what the most remarkable thing was, they will say his routes in center field — not that he caught everything, but that he made every catch look effortless. He was elected in his ninth BBWAA try. He waited longer than almost anyone in the modern era. The plaque is his now.

Kent wore a Giants cap. He is the all-time home run leader at second base: 351 home runs hit from that position, 377 in his career. He won the 2000 NL MVP with San Francisco. He was elected by the Contemporary Baseball Era Committee last December. The Giants are retiring his No. 21.

Three very different careers. Three very different paths to Cooperstown. All three belong.

### STORY 2: Ohtani Setback — Skips Bullpen

#### Long-Form Post — 10:30 AM ET

Shohei Ohtani was scheduled to throw a bullpen session at Citi Field on Sunday. He didn't.

Manager Dave Roberts confirmed the session was skipped. His explanation: "He just doesn't feel 100%, because I think that the last 'pen that he had, there was a little bit of regression."

The last mound work was approximately 30 pitches in a bullpen at Citizens Bank Park on July 22. That session looked promising at the time. Whatever Ohtani felt in the days since changed the calculus. Roberts said Ohtani will not return to pitching until the knee is fully right.

Ohtani received an Orthovisc injection — a lubricant — in his left knee during the All-Star break. He has not pitched in a game since July 3, when he was 8-2 with a 1.79 ERA and 95 strikeouts in 85.2 innings — one of the best pitching lines in baseball.

He is still in the Dodgers' lineup as DH. He is still hitting .288/.397/.536. The Dodgers are 66-38 and built around his complete contribution. The pitching half of that contribution is now on hold indefinitely.

Any return to the mound is August at the earliest. Roberts is not rushing it. That is the right call. But "regression" is a word with weight when you are talking about a knee in an athlete at this level.

### STORY 3: Trade Deadline — Skubal, Abrams, Miller

#### Long-Form Post — 12:30 PM ET

Eight days. August 3 at 6 PM ET.

The Tarik Skubal trade market was supposed to be the headline story of the deadline. Two-time Cy Young winner. Best available starter by a significant margin. Dodgers, Braves, Phillies, Rays all had interest. But the Detroit Tigers have won 25 of their last 40 games since June 1, and the growing consensus among rival executives is that the Tigers will hold.

Detroit's math has changed. They were sellers. Now they might not be. Keeping Skubal and making a run at the AL Central is a real possibility. The Tigers' stance: hold out as long as possible.

Meanwhile, two other markets are active:

CJ Abrams (Nationals, SS): The Nationals are not shopping him, but they will listen. The Yankees, Brewers, and Red Sox have all made contact. Abrams is a two-time All-Star shortstop who is still cost-controlled, which makes him exactly what contenders want.

Mason Miller (Padres, closer): Conflicting signals. ESPN's Buster Olney has reported that executives believe a trade is inevitable. The Athletic has sources saying Miller "seems likely to remain." The Pirates, Mariners, and Yankees are the teams most often mentioned.

The Dodgers are planning to be aggressive buyers regardless. They have won back-to-back World Series and are chasing an unprecedented three-peat — something no team has done since the 1998-2000 Yankees. They have the payroll to move and the motivation to push. The deadline will be a story. The only question is which moves define it.

### STORY 4: Pete Crow-Armstrong Breaks Statcast 5-Star Catch Record

#### Long-Form Post — 3:00 PM ET

The best defensive center fielder alive just put the number on the board.

Pete Crow-Armstrong holds the Statcast record for career five-star catches. He broke Kevin Kiermaier's previous mark when he recorded his 33rd career catch with a 25% or lower catch probability — on July 21, against the Tigers.

Five-star catches are the hardest plays Statcast tracks. A catch with 25% or lower probability means 75% of fielders, in that same situation, would not make the play. PCA makes them look routine.

Here's what makes the record stunning: he got to 33 faster than any other outfielder in Statcast history. Every other player in the top 10 needed at least 100 opportunities to reach their total. PCA needed fewer than 62. He has been in the big leagues since September 2023. He is 24 years old. He has not finished two and a half full seasons.

Kevin Kiermaier was one of the most celebrated defenders of his generation — four Gold Gloves, a reputation as a center field legend. PCA has passed him and is not slowing down.

The Cubs have a legitimate MVP candidate in center field. He is doing something no outfielder has ever done at this age in this sport.

### STORY 5: Kyle Schwarber Leads MLB with 32 HR at Age 35

#### Long-Form Post — 6:00 PM ET

Kyle Schwarber is 35 years old and he leads Major League Baseball in home runs.

Not the NL. Not the NL East. All of baseball.

Schwarber has 32 home runs. Yordan Alvarez has 31. Ben Rice has 29. Every other name on the leaderboard is younger. Most of them are significantly younger.

Last season, Schwarber led the NL with 56 home runs. He was 34. At his current 2026 pace — 20 home runs through the Phillies' first 49 games earlier this season — ESPN projected he was on track for 66. He has since settled into a pace that, if maintained, puts him in the neighborhood of 55 to 60 again.

There are 8 instances in MLB history of a player hitting 60 or more home runs in a season. The last to do it twice in consecutive years would be a historic achievement. Schwarber is not there yet. But at 32 home runs in July, with two months of the season remaining, the conversation is legitimate.

The Phillies' lineup is built to protect him — Cristopher Sánchez leads the rotation, Bryce Harper is still Bryce Harper — and Schwarber is getting enough pitches to hit because the entire Philadelphia lineup forces opponents to throw strikes.

He is 35. He leads the league. Do not try to explain it. Just watch.
