### STORY 2: Joshua Baez — MLB History

#### Long-Form Post

In 150-plus years of professional baseball, nobody had ever done what Joshua Baez did on August 15th.

Baez, a Cardinals outfielder who had been called up from Triple-A days earlier, stepped to the plate in the first inning at Wrigley Field and launched a home run 449 feet to dead center field. That was his first career major league at-bat.

He came up again. Hit another home run.

Then again. Hit another home run.

Three at-bats. Three home runs. Five RBI. Cardinals win 8-4.

The MLB record books had never seen that before — not from anyone. Baez became the first player in baseball history to homer in each of his first three major league at-bats. He went 3-for-4 with 5 RBI on the night.

He did this at 34 home runs in Triple-A before his call-up, so nobody should be surprised that he can hit. But nobody does what he did on debut night.

Thirty family members were in the stands at Wrigley to watch it happen.

### STORY 3: Yordan Alvarez Triple Crown Chase

#### Long-Form Post

The last time any hitter won baseball's Triple Crown, smartphones had just gotten Siri and the Dallas Cowboys were 8-8.

That was 2012. That was Miguel Cabrera. It has been 14 years and counting since any hitter led their league in batting average, home runs, and RBI simultaneously at the end of a season.

Yordan Alvarez is doing it right now.

He leads MLB in batting average (.329), leads MLB in RBI (approximately 88, seven or more ahead of second place), and stands just one home run behind league leader Kyle Schwarber (who sits at 37 after hitting two home runs at the Field of Dreams game on August 14). Alvarez has 36.

The batting average lead is commanding. The RBI lead is commanding. The home run race is the only variable — and Schwarber, who hit 56 last year, is not giving that lead away easily.

The next time Alvarez hits a home run, he ties Schwarber. The next time he hits two, he leads. With 46 games remaining, the math is there. The question is whether he can do it.

### STORY 4: Brett Callahan Debut — 1912 Full Circle

#### Long-Form Post

On August 16th, a Detroit Tigers outfielder named Brett Callahan made his major league debut.

On the very first pitch of his first major league plate appearance, he hit a two-run triple off White Sox pitcher Sean Burke.

That made him the first Tiger to hit a triple in his very first MLB plate appearance in 114 years.

The last Tiger to do it was Ed Irwin in 1912 — and that particular 1912 game is one of the strangest in baseball history. It was played during Ty Cobb's suspension, after the Tigers went on strike in solidarity with Cobb and the team had to field a roster of amateur replacements rather than forfeit. The Tigers lost 24-2 to Philadelphia. The man who pitched all eight innings of that game for Detroit was Allen Travers, a college student at St. Joseph's University.

Brett Callahan also went to St. Joseph's University.

The Tigers lost yesterday's game 7-5. Callahan's triple in his first at-bat was the highlight. Baseball rarely writes something this circular.

### STORY 5: Misiorowski Cy Young Update

#### Long-Form Post

The Jacob Misiorowski Cy Young watch continues — and the Brewers are making sure he has every advantage heading into September.

Milwaukee is giving Misiorowski extra rest between starts, with his next outing expected around August 23. That gives their ace a few extra days of recovery to stay sharp through the stretch run.

The numbers he's put up are historic. He is 11-5 with a 1.76 ERA, 204 strikeouts in 133 innings, and a 0.74 WHIP. He reached 200 strikeouts faster than any pitcher in MLB history except Spencer Strider in 2022. He is the heavy NL Cy Young Award favorite at -550 betting odds.

The award race has been effectively decided. The only remaining question is how far Misiorowski takes the strikeout total before his final regular-season start.
