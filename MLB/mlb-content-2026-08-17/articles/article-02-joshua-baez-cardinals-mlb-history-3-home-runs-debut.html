Joshua Baez Just Made History Nobody Had Ever Made Before

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | August 17, 2026

In 150-plus years of professional baseball, no player had ever done what Joshua Baez did on August 15 at Wrigley Field.

Baez stepped to the plate in the first inning and launched a home run 449 feet to dead center field. His first career major league at-bat. His first career major league hit. A home run.

He came up again. Hit another home run.

Then came up again. Hit another home run.

Three at-bats. Three home runs. Five RBI. Cardinals 8, Cubs 4.

No player in the history of Major League Baseball had ever homered in each of his first three career at-bats. Not in any era. Not with any combination of pitching styles, eras, or ballparks. Baez put himself into a category of one on his debut night.

What He Did, In Order

The sequence matters. Baez was called up from Triple-A, where he had been the most powerful hitter in the minors — 34 home runs before the call-up, leading all of Triple-A. That production is why the Cardinals brought him up. Nobody predicted the debut he delivered.

His first at-bat: first homer, 449 feet, dead center at Wrigley. The kind of hit that clears the bleachers and raises a cheer from people in the streets outside the park. His second: another home run. His third: another. When the Cardinals dugout — seasoned veterans who have watched thousands of professional at-bats — started reacting with visible disbelief, that was a sign of how unprecedented the night was becoming in real time.

By the time the final out was recorded, Baez had gone 3-for-4 with 5 RBI and rewritten the record book on his very first night.

Thirty Family Members in the Stands

Thirty family members were in the Wrigley Field stands watching Joshua Baez make his major league debut. They saw a game that nobody in that park — and nobody anywhere in baseball — had ever witnessed before in terms of debut home run production.

The moment a player reaches the major leagues is significant regardless of what happens in the first game. What happened in Baez's first game, with thirty people who knew him longest watching from the seats, was a thing nobody could have scripted.

What It Means Going Forward

The Cardinals were playing at Wrigley Field, in a game with Wild Card implications, and they got a performance from their newest player that their rotation and lineup could not have anticipated. The Cubs are five games ahead of the Phillies and Padres in the NL Wild Card race, so the August 15 loss did not fundamentally alter their postseason standing. But the Cardinals, who took the series two games to one, got something more valuable than the win — they have a hitter who arrived in the big leagues proving, right away, that what he did in Triple-A was not a fluke.

For the Cardinals, and for baseball, August 15, 2026 now belongs to a name first-time fans had to look up when it happened: Joshua Baez.

What's Next

The Cardinals continue their road trip after the Cubs series. Baez's next starts will reveal whether the debut was an outlier or an opening statement. Triple-A production at 34 HRs suggests the latter, but Major League pitchers will now have film on him and will adjust. How he responds to that adjustment is the story that follows August 15.
