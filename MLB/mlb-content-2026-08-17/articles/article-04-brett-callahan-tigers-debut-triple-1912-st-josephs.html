Brett Callahan's First MLB Pitch Was a Triple — and the 1912 Connection Makes It Even Stranger

By Ryan Calloway | Ballpark Banter | August 17, 2026

On August 16, the Detroit Tigers called up Brett Callahan from Triple-A Toledo and put him in the lineup against the Chicago White Sox. He batted seventh and played left field.

On the first pitch he saw as a major leaguer, he hit a two-run triple off Sean Burke into the gap. That was his first at-bat. That was his first major league hit. Those were his first two major league RBI. On one pitch.

The Tigers lost the game 7-5 and were swept. But the debut was something baseball fans in Detroit will remember.

The 114-Year Gap

Callahan became the first Detroit Tiger to hit a triple in his first major league plate appearance in 114 years. The previous Tiger to do it was Ed Irwin, in 1912. He is the seventh player in Tigers franchise history to hit a triple in his first MLB game.

The year 1912 in Tigers history is not a random footnote. The game Irwin played in — the game that Callahan now shares a category with — is one of the most bizarre in baseball history.

The 1912 Ty Cobb Protest Game

In May of 1912, Ty Cobb was suspended after fighting a heckler in the stands. The Tigers' players — loyal to their teammate — went on strike in protest. Rather than forfeit the game, the team fielded a roster of amateur replacements. Seminary students, college players, anyone willing to put on a Tigers uniform for a game.

The Tigers lost that game to the Philadelphia Athletics, 24-2. It remains one of the most lopsided defeats in franchise history, played by men who had no business being on a major league field.

The man who pitched all eight innings of that game for Detroit was Allen Travers, a student at St. Joseph's University. He allowed 26 hits and 24 runs. He never pitched again in professional baseball.

The Full Circle

Brett Callahan attended St. Joseph's University.

The same school that produced Allen Travers — the man who pitched all eight innings of the most unusual game in Detroit Tigers history, in the game where Ed Irwin hit the last triple in a Tigers debut before Callahan's — now produced the man who closed that 114-year gap.

Baseball's history is often linear — player follows player, record follows record. Occasionally, a line bends and connects two distant points in a way that feels designed rather than coincidental. The St. Joseph's connection is the kind of detail that should not fit as perfectly as it does.

What's Next

Callahan's career is just beginning. His minor league stats before the call-up — .269/.365/.443 with 13 home runs and 56 RBI between Double-A Erie and Triple-A Toledo — suggest a player with real tools. The debut was remarkable in isolation. What happens over the rest of August and September will tell the fuller story of whether Callahan belongs at this level beyond the historic first pitch.
