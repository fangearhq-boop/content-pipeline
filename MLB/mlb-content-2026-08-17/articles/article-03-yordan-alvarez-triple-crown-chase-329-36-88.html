Yordan Alvarez Is Leading All Three Triple Crown Categories and Has Been for Weeks

By Jake Torres | Ballpark Banter | August 17, 2026

The last player to win baseball's Triple Crown was Miguel Cabrera in 2012. Since then, in 14 seasons, no hitter has led his league in batting average, home runs, and RBI all at once when the season ended.

Yordan Alvarez is leading all three right now. And with roughly six weeks left to play, the math is real.

Where the Numbers Stand

Alvarez is batting .329, a margin of more than .029 above the nearest competitor for the AL batting title. That is a commanding lead — not one that disappears in a three-game series. He leads the majors in RBI with approximately 88, somewhere in the range of seven or more ahead of second place. The RBI lead is also substantial and would require a sustained run of poor production from Alvarez combined with a sustained surge from a competitor to close meaningfully before October.

The home run category is the one where the math is tightest. Alvarez sits at 36. Kyle Schwarber leads MLB at 37 after hitting two home runs at the Field of Dreams game on August 14. One swing separates Alvarez from the tie. Two from the lead.

That is the race the Triple Crown hinges on.

What Schwarber Hitting Two at Field of Dreams Means

Schwarber has been the most consistent power hitter in baseball across the last two seasons. He hit 56 home runs in 2025. Coming into this season, the expectation was that he would challenge for the HR title again — and he has been in the lead for most of 2026. His two-homer night at the Field of Dreams game in Dyersville, Iowa moved him from a four-way tie at 35 (with Alvarez, Matt Olson, and Junior Caminero, as of August 11) to 37 and sole possession of the MLB lead.

Schwarber is not going to stop hitting home runs. He has hit 30 or more in six consecutive seasons. Alvarez closing the gap and passing him requires a differential performance over the remaining six weeks — something that is possible, but not certain.

Olson, meanwhile, has been surging in August with six home runs in the first half of the month. He remains close enough to make this a four-player conversation through September.

The 14-Year Wait

Cabrera's 2012 Triple Crown was the first in the American League since Carl Yastrzemski in 1967 — a 45-year drought that ended with one of the most dominant individual offensive seasons in modern history. Since Cabrera, the combination of factors required — a hitter who is elite at contact AND elite at power AND producing consistently throughout an entire season — has not aligned for anyone.

Alvarez is the best argument that any hitter has made since 2012. His batting average lead reflects a hitter who does not chase, who punishes pitches in the zone, and whose approach at the plate has remained consistent even as other hitters — including himself — have gone through multi-week cold stretches this season. His RBI lead reflects a hitter who produces when runners are on base. His home run total reflects a hitter who, even trailing by one at this late stage, could realistically close or pass the leader before the season ends.

None of those things are guaranteed. But the argument that he can win all three is a serious one, not a hope.

What's Next

Alvarez and the Astros continue play in the AL West race. The next time he hits a home run, he ties Schwarber at 37. The next update on where the Triple Crown race stands will be driven by daily box scores — one number that matters more than all the others is how close Alvarez and Schwarber's HR totals stay through September.
