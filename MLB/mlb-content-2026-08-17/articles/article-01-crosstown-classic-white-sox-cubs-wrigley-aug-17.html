Crosstown Classic at Wrigley: The Best Rivalry in Chicago Gets a New Meaning This Year

By Ryan Calloway | Ballpark Banter | August 17, 2026

The Crosstown Classic resumes tonight at Wrigley Field, and for the first time in a while, the game between the White Sox and Cubs carries weight on both sides of the city.

The White Sox (65-58) are first place in the AL Central, sitting on a season-high 5.5-game lead after completing a sweep of the Detroit Tigers over the weekend. The Cubs (72-53) are the No. 1 NL Wild Card team in baseball, five games ahead of the Phillies and Padres with about six weeks left in the season. First pitch is tonight at 7:05 PM CT at Wrigley Field.

Two Playoff Teams, One City

The frame that matters this year is simple: both teams are going to October. The question the Crosstown Classic answers is not which Chicago team survives the regular season, but which one enters the postseason with the better momentum and, frankly, the better storyline heading into September.

The White Sox swept the Tigers three games in Detroit — 9-5, 4-3, 7-5 — to extend their AL Central lead to a season-high. Their full-circle story from 121 losses in 2024 to first place in 2026 is one of the better redemption narratives in recent baseball history. Coming into Wrigley on a sweep is not a bad position.

The Cubs come in having dropped two of three at home to the Cardinals — including an 11-4 loss on Sunday that knocked some of the luster off their August. At 72-53, their Wild Card lead is still comfortable. But after the Cardinals series, they could use a strong start to this week.

The May Factor

The White Sox took the first Crosstown Classic series of 2026, winning 2 of 3 at Rate Field in May. The clincher came on a walk-off home run by Edgar Quero in the tenth inning — a moment that, in a rivalry game, registers longer than the typical regular-season result.

The Cubs get three games at home to even the series, starting tonight. The ballpark helps. Wrigley Field crowds have been loud all season for a Cubs team that fans expected to compete.

What to Watch

From the White Sox side, the question is whether their rotation can protect a lead that matters. Sweeping Detroit is one thing. Continuing that momentum into a three-game series at Wrigley against a team with legitimate bats is another test entirely.

From the Cubs side, the Cardinals series was a reminder that the NL Wild Card race has real competition. The Phillies and Padres are both at 67-58, five games back, and neither is going away quietly. A split or a loss of this series would not be a crisis — but a win of this series would serve as a useful statement heading into the final stretch.

For both clubs, August is the month where October seeds are set. Tonight is the first game of that argument in the middle of Chicago.

What's Next

The Crosstown Classic runs August 17-19 at Wrigley Field. The series continues Tuesday and Wednesday before the Cubs return home to face the Dodgers.
