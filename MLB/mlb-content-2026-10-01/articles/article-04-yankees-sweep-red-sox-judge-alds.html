Yankees Sweep Red Sox 18-2 Without Judge — Now Targeting ALDS Comeback

By Marcus Cole

October 1, 2026

The New York Yankees swept the Boston Red Sox in two games — outscoring them 18-2 — and their best player didn't even suit up.

Aaron Judge, nursing a moderate-grade right calf strain that has kept him off the field since September 16, was not on the Wild Card roster. The Yankees won anyway, and won convincingly. Game 1 was a 9-0 blowout. Game 2 was another 9-2 statement. New York is headed to the ALDS against the Tampa Bay Rays, and Judge is targeting a return.

Ben Rice Steps Into the Spotlight

If there's a player who stepped into the Judge-shaped void and filled it as well as anyone could have hoped, it was first baseman Ben Rice. The New Yorker — who grew up in Cohasset, Massachusetts, just outside Boston — delivered multiple home runs over the two-game series, including a grand slam in Game 1's 9-0 rout that sent the crowd at Yankee Stadium into a frenzy.

Starting pitcher Cam Schlittler — who, like Rice, grew up in Cohasset — was equally dominant in Game 1. Working 6.1 innings without giving up a walk or a run, with 12 strikeouts, it was the kind of postseason debut that builds legends. Two hometown kids from the same Massachusetts suburb, doing it together at Yankee Stadium, against the Red Sox. Baseball writes the best stories.

Judge's Status: Targeting the ALDS

The Yankees have been cautious with Judge throughout his rehabilitation from the calf injury. He participated in batting practice and live at-bats before the Wild Card Series, and did on-field running on September 29. But the decision was made to leave him off the roster for the two-game series against Boston, prioritizing a potential return in a higher-stakes round.

The ALDS against the Tampa Bay Rays is that round. Judge said the plan was always to target the Division Series, and the Yankees' dominant Wild Card performance gives everyone involved reason for optimism. The Rays are a significantly different challenge than the Red Sox, and having Judge — one of the best hitters in baseball — available could be the difference between advancing and going home.

What's Next
