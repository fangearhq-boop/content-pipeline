From 121 Losses to the ALDS: White Sox Complete Historic Sweep of Astros

By Ryan Calloway

October 1, 2026

In 2024, the Chicago White Sox lost 121 games. That number — a modern MLB record for futility — hung over the franchise like a dark cloud for an entire season.

On September 30, 2026, the White Sox won a playoff series. Their first since 2005.

Chicago swept the Houston Astros in the AL Wild Card round, completing a two-game series sweep to advance to the American League Division Series. For a team built almost entirely from young, inexperienced talent, it's a result that would have seemed like fantasy barely twelve months ago.

Colson Montgomery's Statement Homer

In Game 1, with Chicago batting ninth in the lineup, shortstop Colson Montgomery stepped to the plate. His September statistics were ugly — a .099 batting average, .210 slugging percentage, numbers that almost guaranteed he wouldn't be a factor. He hit a 432-foot home run anyway.

That shot — a solo blast that became the first White Sox postseason home run since Gavin Sheets went deep off Lance McCullers Jr. in the 2021 ALDS — captured everything that made this White Sox team hard to predict and even harder to contain. These players don't carry the weight of expectations. They just play.

Chicago went on to win Game 1 by a score of 6-3. Game 2 followed the same formula: disciplined at-bats, a relentless offensive approach, and pitching that kept Houston's lineup in check. The final score was 7-3 and the White Sox had their sweep.

A Franchise Reborn

General manager Chris Getz and manager Will Venable have built something genuinely different in Chicago. Seven of nine regular starters have under two years of MLB experience. The team was not supposed to compete this soon. But here they are.

The turnaround from 2024 to 2026 represents one of the most dramatic improvements any team has made in a short window. What's remarkable isn't just the results — it's how they've done it. No big free agent signings. No blockbuster trades. Just development, belief, and execution at exactly the right moment.

What's Next
