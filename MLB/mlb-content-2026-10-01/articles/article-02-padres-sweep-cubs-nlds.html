Padres Sweep Cubs, Advance to NLDS: San Diego Is Built for October

By Jake Torres

October 1, 2026

The Chicago Cubs came into the Wild Card Series with one of the most potent offenses in the National League. The San Diego Padres sent them home in two games and barely broke a sweat doing it.

San Diego completed a two-game Wild Card sweep of Chicago on September 30 with a 4-1 victory at Petco Park. Gavin Sheets delivered the decisive blow off the bench with a two-run home run, and the Padres' pitching staff did the rest, limiting a Cubs lineup that had terrorized pitchers all season to a single run on the night. The Cubs are eliminated. The Padres are in the NLDS.

Sheets Provides the Big Swing

San Diego's offense wasn't flashy over the two-game series, but it didn't have to be. In a series decided by pitching and situational hitting, Sheets provided the signature offensive moment — a pinch-hit two-run homer that gave San Diego the breathing room they needed and deflated any late-game momentum the Cubs were building.

The Padres' ability to get production from depth pieces like Sheets is part of what makes them so dangerous in October. It's never one player, never one lineup card. Manager is chess-moving with a full roster, and it shows.

Cubs' Offense Goes Quiet in San Diego

The Cubs managed only one hit against San Diego's pitching in Game 1 — a near no-hitter that set the tone for the series. They fared slightly better in Game 2 but couldn't string together the kind of multi-run innings that defined their regular season. Petco Park has a way of quieting offenses, and San Diego's pitching staff amplified that effect.

For the Cubs, this is a brutal ending to what was a genuinely strong year. The margin between playoff success and early exit is thin at this level, and San Diego represents one of the toughest first-round draws in the NL bracket.

NLDS Matchup: Padres vs. Brewers

The Padres advance to face the Milwaukee Brewers in the NL Division Series. Milwaukee earned a first-round bye as one of the top seeds in the National League and will be rested and ready. For San Diego, the question is whether the short turnaround after a two-game sweep is an advantage (fresh momentum) or a disadvantage (less preparation time).

Based on what the Padres just showed against Chicago, they look like a legitimate October threat regardless of matchup.
