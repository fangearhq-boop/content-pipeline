### Long-Form Post Baseball's offseason is running against the clock. ⏱️ The CBA — Major League Baseball's collective bargaining agreement — expires at 11:59 PM ET on December 1. When it does, the owners are widely expected to lock out the players, freezing all transactions until a new deal is reached. Where do talks stand? Not close. MLB's latest offer: a $258.2 million salary cap and $180.2 million floor, with implementation delayed until 2028 — not immediately. The MLB Players Association's response has been consistent: no salary cap, period. The union also wants earlier free agency and a higher luxury tax threshold (~$300M). The last lockout started Dec. 2, 2021 and lasted 99 days. It ended March 10, 2022, with minimal damage to the season. If this one drags on longer, the 2027 season could be affected. What freezes on Dec. 1: - All free agent signings (Skubal, Arozarena, Suzuki, everyone) - All trades (Seager, every deadline name) - Teruaki Sato's posting window (possibly) - Minor league contracts, extensions, everything Teams are already freezing trade discussions. The window to do business is 17 days long. ⏰ ### Image Post Caption 17 days until the CBA expires and owners lock out players. MLB offered a $258.2M cap. The union said no.
