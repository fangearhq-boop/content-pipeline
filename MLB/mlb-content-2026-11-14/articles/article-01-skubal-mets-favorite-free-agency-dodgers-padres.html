Skubal Free Agency: Mets Are "Probably" the Favorite as California Meetings Begin

By Ryan Calloway | Ballpark Banter | November 14, 2026

The Tarik Skubal sweepstakes is entering its most consequential stretch — and the New York Mets are increasingly looking like the team to beat.

Jon Heyman of the New York Post reported that the Mets are "probably" the favorite to land Skubal, the consensus best free agent pitcher on the market. ESPN's Kiley McDaniel named the Mets and the Los Angeles Dodgers as the "primary bidders," with Skubal heading to California to meet with both clubs. The Chicago Cubs, once considered a contender, have officially dropped out of the race.

The San Diego Padres have also emerged as a legitimate threat. Per Heyman, the Padres hold better odds than the Dodgers — an internal metric that has quietly become the most discussed number in baseball circles this week.

What a Skubal Deal Would Look Like

McDaniel projects Skubal landing an 8-year, $352 million deal — a $44 million average annual value. That would shatter Yoshinobu Yamamoto's record of $27 million AAV for a starting pitcher by $17 million, making Skubal's contract the most lucrative in MLB pitching history by a staggering margin.

The 29-year-old left-hander turns 30 on November 20. He carries no qualifying offer, no no-trade clause, and no team restrictions — a pure open-market free agency that any of the six bidding clubs can pursue without encumbrance. That lack of a qualifying offer means no team forfeits a draft pick to sign him.

The CBA Urgency Factor

Speed matters. The MLB collective bargaining agreement expires at 11:59 PM ET on December 1, and owners are widely expected to lock out players the moment it lapses. Any deal not completed before that deadline is frozen indefinitely — until a new CBA is reached.

That adds a 17-day window to the equation. Skubal, his representation, and all bidding clubs are motivated to close a deal before the lockout transforms a hot stove into a cold storage unit. The last MLB lockout, which began December 2, 2021, lasted 99 days.

The Mets have reportedly been in deep contact with Skubal's camp throughout the process. The Dodgers earn their in-person pitch during the California visit. The Padres have shown the kind of aggressive positioning that has made them a consistent presence in major free agency moves.

What's Next

Skubal's California trip is the clearest signal yet that this deal is close. When a pitcher of this caliber makes the rounds with specific teams, it typically precedes a signing within days or weeks — not months. The Mets have the financial infrastructure and front office motivation to close, having rebuilt into a legitimate National League contender.

The Dodgers, perpetual contenders in this market, will make their case with their track record of pitching development and postseason success. The Padres, meanwhile, may represent the wild card: a team with the financial flexibility and competitive window to outbid either rival if the price climbs.

Whatever happens next, it happens fast. The CBA clock is ticking, and Tarik Skubal is about to become the most expensive pitcher in baseball history.
