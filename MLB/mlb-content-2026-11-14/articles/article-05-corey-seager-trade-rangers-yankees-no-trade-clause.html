Rangers Exploring Corey Seager Trade — But He Has Full No-Trade Rights

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | November 14, 2026

The Texas Rangers are willing to trade Corey Seager. The question is whether Corey Seager is willing to go.

The Athletic's Ken Rosenthal and Will Leitch Grant first reported this week that the Rangers are exploring trading their star shortstop at the GM Meetings in Dallas. Texas is targeting a 2027 payroll near $193 million, and moving Seager — who is still owed between $155 million and $157.5 million over the next five seasons — would create significant flexibility for a franchise in transition.

There is just one problem: Seager has earned full 10-and-5 no-trade rights after spending 10 years in MLB and five seasons with the Rangers. No deal happens without his blessing. That gives Seager something rare in this market — the ability to decide his own future, regardless of what Texas wants.

Why the Rangers Want Out

Seager had the worst offensive season of his career in 2026, posting a .227 batting average and a .736 OPS — both career lows. He underwent elbow surgery after the season, adding health uncertainty to a contract that already looked heavy given his declining production.

The contract, signed when Seager was coming off a World Series MVP performance, made sense in the context of that moment. Five years later, with a 32-year-old shortstop coming off surgery and the worst offensive numbers of his career, the remaining obligation is increasingly difficult to justify for a team repositioning its payroll.

The Yankees Connection — and the Skeptics

Jon Heyman of the New York Post reported that the Yankees have talked to the Rangers about Seager, with a possible fit at third base floated as a way to address one of New York's lineup weaknesses. The idea would shift Seager off shortstop — where Anthony Volpe is entrenched — and deploy him as a power bat at the hot corner.

Joel Sherman, also of the Post, expressed skepticism. The Yankees already carry significant left-handed power in their lineup, and adding another may create more redundancy than solution. Blue Jays, Red Sox, Dodgers, and Braves have also been mentioned as teams with varying levels of interest.

No deal is close, per all reporting. Any trade still requires Seager's consent, and there is no indication he has signaled willingness to move.

What's Next

The Rangers will continue conversations at the GM Meetings and beyond, but the leverage in this situation belongs to Seager. If he wants to stay in Texas — or simply prefers not to move — no deal happens regardless of how motivated the organization is to free up payroll.

What is clear is that the Rangers are genuinely exploring this. The GM Meetings created the forum; the conversations are real. Whether they lead anywhere depends on a shortstop with full control over his own next chapter deciding that he wants to write it somewhere else.
