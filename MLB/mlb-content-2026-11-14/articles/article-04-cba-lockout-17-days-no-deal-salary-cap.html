CBA Lockout Clock: 17 Days, No Deal in Sight, and Baseball's Winter on the Line

By Ryan Calloway | Ballpark Banter | November 14, 2026

Major League Baseball's collective bargaining agreement expires at 11:59 PM ET on December 1, 2026. Owners are widely expected to lock out players the moment it does. And right now, with 17 days left on the clock, no deal is close.

What freezes the moment a lockout begins: all free agent signings, all trades, all extensions, all minor league contracts. Tarik Skubal. Randy Arozarena. Teruaki Sato. Corey Seager. Everyone. The entire offseason transaction market halts until owners and the MLB Players Association reach a new agreement.

Where the Talks Stand

MLB's latest proposal, issued ahead of the GM Meetings, calls for a $258.2 million salary cap and a $180.2 million floor — with implementation delayed until 2028. The owners framed the delayed start as a concession, a way to give clubs time to adjust without immediate disruption.

The union's response has been consistent since negotiations began: no salary cap, in any form, at any start date. The MLB Players Association has also pushed for earlier free agency eligibility — allowing players to reach the open market sooner than the current service-time system allows — and a higher luxury tax threshold, in the range of $300 million.

The gap between the two sides is not measured in details. It is measured in philosophy. Owners want cost certainty. Players want uncapped earning potential. Those positions do not meet in the middle easily.

Historical Precedent

The last MLB work stoppage began December 2, 2021 — the day after the previous CBA expired — and lasted 99 days. It ended March 10, 2022, with minimal damage to the regular season schedule after both sides reached a last-minute agreement. That lockout is the most recent reference point for what this one could look like.

If this lockout lasts as long, baseball resumes in mid-March 2027. If it runs longer — past 90 days with no agreement — the 2027 regular season could face delays, canceled games, and the revenue losses that come with them. Multiple outlets have cited roughly five percent odds of a deal being reached before December 1.

What's Next

Teams are already freezing trade discussions ahead of the deadline. The 17-day window that remains is when the entire industry will try to compress months of normal offseason activity into a sprint. Skubal, Sato, Arozarena, the QO-declined free agents — all of them need deals before December 1 or they wait out a lockout of unknown duration.

The CBA is not just a backdrop to this offseason. It is the frame that every deal, every signing, and every trade is being built around. And right now, that frame is cracking.
