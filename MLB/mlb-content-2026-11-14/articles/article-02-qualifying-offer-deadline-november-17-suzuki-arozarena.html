Qualifying Offer Deadline November 17: Suzuki, Arozarena, and 6 Others Expected to Decline

By Marcus Cole | Ballpark Banter | November 14, 2026

Three days from now, the baseball offseason officially begins.

Eight players who received $23.15 million qualifying offers earlier this month must decide by 4 PM ET on Tuesday, November 17, whether to accept those one-year deals and return to their current clubs — or decline and enter the open market. All eight are expected to decline, based on historical precedent and industry reporting.

When they do, one of the most compressed free agent windows in recent memory will open. And it will close again in just 14 days, when the collective bargaining agreement expires December 1.

The Eight Players

Here is who received qualifying offers and what they bring to the market:

Seiya Suzuki (Cubs): The outfielder posted a career-best 5.1 WAR in 2026, with 26 home runs and 86 RBI. The Cubs offered him the QO, and the Mariners have been identified as interested in bringing him to Seattle. Chicago is expected to pursue a re-signing if he remains in their price range.

Randy Arozarena (Mariners): The outfielder hit .281/.381/.484 with 26 home runs across 154 games. Seattle offered the QO but has made clear it has no interest in retaining him. Arozarena enters free agency as one of the market's highest-profile outfielders.

David Bednar (Yankees): New York's top closer candidate enters the market after the Yankees issued a QO. His return or departure will shape the Yankees' bullpen construction for 2027.

Jake Bauers (Brewers), Trevor Rogers (Orioles), Ryan Jeffers (Twins), Brandon Lowe (Pirates), Adrian Morejon (Padres): All five are expected to decline and enter free agency, though none carry the same headline profile as Suzuki, Arozarena, or Bednar.

The Historical Context

Qualifying offers rarely get accepted. Of the 157 players who have ever received one, only 18 have accepted — a rate of roughly 11.5 percent. Players at the top of the market almost universally decline, knowing they can exceed the QO value in multi-year deals on the open market.

The complication this year is timing. Once all eight decline on November 17, teams that sign them must forfeit a draft pick — a pick at the end of the first round for revenue-sharing teams, or in the competitive balance round for others. That subtly depresses demand for QO-declined players from smaller-market teams operating under tighter constraints.

What's Next

November 17 is the starting gun. Within hours of the deadline, expect signings, negotiations, and market movement to accelerate. The industry has been in a holding pattern for weeks, waiting for QO decisions to clear before committing to free agents who might return to their original clubs.

But the window is narrow. December 1 looms. Teams that want Arozarena, Suzuki, or any of the other eight will need to move quickly — because once the lockout hits, every transaction freezes until a new collective bargaining agreement is reached.
