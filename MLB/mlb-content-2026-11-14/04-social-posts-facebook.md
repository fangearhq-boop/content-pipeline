### STORY 2: QO Deadline Three Days Away

**Long-Form Post (Window: 10:30 AM ET)**

The qualifying offer clock is ticking — and when it expires on Nov. 17, the free agent market will explode. For about 72 hours.

Eight players received $23.15 million qualifying offers from their teams earlier this month. They have until 4 PM ET on Tuesday, Nov. 17 to accept and return to their current clubs on a one-year deal — or decline and enter the open market.

Here's who we're talking about:
- **Seiya Suzuki** (Cubs) — 26 HR, 86 RBI, career-best 5.1 WAR
- **Randy Arozarena** (Mariners) — 26 HR, .281/.381/.484
- **David Bednar** (Yankees) — the Yankees' top closer candidate
- **Jake Bauers** (Brewers) | **Trevor Rogers** (Orioles) | **Ryan Jeffers** (Twins) | **Brandon Lowe** (Pirates) | **Adrian Morejon** (Padres)

Historically, 18 of 157 players who've received QOs have ever accepted. All 8 of these players are expected to decline and enter free agency.

The catch: once they decline on Nov. 17, there are only 14 days before the CBA expires Dec. 1. That window is when you'll see the first big signings of the offseason.

### STORY 3: Teruaki Sato Posting Window

**Long-Form Post (Window: 12:30 PM ET)**

Teruaki Sato won Japan's Nippon Professional Baseball Triple Crown in 2026. He's 25 years old. He posted a .308/.385/.613 slash line with 39 home runs. And he may become the most sought-after international hitter since Shohei Ohtani. ⚾

Sato was posted by the Orix Buffaloes on Nov. 1. The 45-day negotiating window would normally close Dec. 16. But there's a problem: the MLB lockout, expected to begin Dec. 1, could freeze that window — stopping any deal from being finalized.

The confirmed bidders as of this writing: the Mets, Yankees, Dodgers, Phillies, and Padres. The Rangers have also shown interest. Mets GM David Stearns reportedly flew to Japan to scout him in person — a signal of serious intent.

What's his price? MLBTR projected $80M over four years. Bleacher Report projected $120M over six. Some analysts believe the market will push even higher.

There is a minor knock on Sato: scouts have flagged that his defense at third base may be below MLB average. He could be a DH candidate in the American League, which opens up the Yankees and Rangers as natural fits.

Three weeks to get a deal done. The clock is running. 🕐

### STORY 4: CBA Lockout 17 Days

**Long-Form Post (Window: 3:00 PM ET)**

Baseball's offseason is running against the clock. ⏱️

The CBA — Major League Baseball's collective bargaining agreement — expires at 11:59 PM ET on December 1. When it does, the owners are widely expected to lock out the players, freezing all transactions until a new deal is reached.

Where do talks stand? Not close.

MLB's latest offer: a $258.2 million salary cap and $180.2 million floor, with implementation delayed until 2028 — not immediately. The MLB Players Association's response has been consistent: no salary cap, period. The union also wants earlier free agency and a higher luxury tax threshold (~$300M).

The last lockout started Dec. 2, 2021 and lasted 99 days. It ended March 10, 2022, with minimal damage to the season. If this one drags on longer, the 2027 season could be affected.

What freezes on Dec. 1:
- All free agent signings (Skubal, Arozarena, Suzuki, everyone)
- All trades (Seager, every deadline name)
- Teruaki Sato's posting window (possibly)
- Minor league contracts, extensions, everything

Teams are already freezing trade discussions. The window to do business is 17 days long. ⏰

### STORY 5: Corey Seager Trade Market

**Long-Form Post (Window: 6:00 PM ET)**

The Texas Rangers are willing to trade Corey Seager. There's just one problem: Seager has to agree to it. ⚾

The Athletic's Ken Rosenthal and Will Leitch Grant first reported this week that the Rangers are exploring trading their star shortstop at the GM Meetings. Texas' 2027 payroll target is near $193M, and moving Seager — still owed $155-157.5M over five more years — would create significant flexibility.

Here's the catch: Seager has earned full 10-and-5 no-trade rights after 10 MLB seasons and five with his current team. No deal happens without his blessing.

The fit question is real too. Seager had the worst offensive season of his career in 2026 — a .227 batting average and .736 OPS (career lows). He had elbow surgery after the season. The contract made sense when he signed it; it's starting to look heavy now.

Who's interested? The Yankees have talked to Texas about Seager, with Jon Heyman suggesting a possible fit at third base. Joel Sherman is skeptical — the Yankees already have a lineup with left-handed power. Blue Jays, Red Sox, Dodgers, and Braves have also been mentioned.

No deal is close. And whatever happens, Seager will have the final say.
