### Long-Form Post Pete Crow-Armstrong enters Sunday sitting at 41 home runs and 36 stolen bases. The math is simple and the stakes are enormous. He needs 4 more stolen bases in roughly 14 remaining games to join the most exclusive club in baseball: the 40-40 Club. Six players have done it across the entire history of Major League Baseball — Jose Canseco (1988), Barry Bonds (1996), Alex Rodriguez (1998), Alfonso Soriano (2006), Ronald Acuña Jr. (2023), and Shohei Ohtani (2024). PCA would be the seventh. He would also be the first Cub to accomplish it ever, and the first Cubs player to reach 40 home runs in a single season since Derrek Lee did it in 2005. The question in September is never purely statistical. The Cubs are in a Wild Card race. Managers in pennant chases sometimes pull back the green light for baserunners to avoid injury or outs. Crow-Armstrong will need the Cubs coaching staff to keep trusting him in situations where a steal attempt makes sense — and he'll need the base hits and walks to get there. With 14 games left, this window is real. A run of 4 steals in two weeks isn't automatic, but it's absolutely within reach for one of the most well-rounded offensive players in the National League. ### Image Post Caption Pete Crow-Armstrong: 41 HR. 36 SB. 4 steals from history.

### Long-Form Post Yordan Alvarez is trying to do something that hasn't been done in the American League since Miguel Cabrera in 2012 — win the Triple Crown. Through the first eight months of the 2026 season, Alvarez leads the American League in batting average (approximately .310). His home run total (38) and RBI total (approximately 96) put him within striking distance in both categories — but Junior Caminero of the Tampa Bay Rays hit a walk-off home run on Saturday to stay at 39, one ahead of Alvarez, and the RBI race is similarly tight. The Triple Crown is defined as leading your league in batting average, home runs, and RBI simultaneously at the end of the season. It requires sustained excellence across three completely different statistical measures, and it is extraordinarily rare in the modern game. Cabrera did it in 2012. Carl Yastrzemski did it in 1967. Before Cabrera, you have to go back decades to find another AL winner. Alvarez is already the overwhelming AL MVP favorite — his season would be elite regardless of whether he catches Caminero in HR or pulls away in RBI. But the Triple Crown is the category that would separate his 2026 from merely great seasons and put it into historical territory. 14 games remain. Every plate appearance in the next two weeks carries extra weight. ### Image Post Caption Yordan Alvarez: AL Triple Crown chase. BA (.310) leads. HR (38) and RBI (96) within reach.
