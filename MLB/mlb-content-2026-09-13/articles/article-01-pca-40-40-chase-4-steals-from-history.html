PCA's 40-40 Window: 4 Steals, 14 Games, and the Weight of History

By Jake Torres September 13, 2026

The Math Is Simple

Pete Crow-Armstrong enters Sunday at 41 home runs and 36 stolen bases. He needs 4 more stolen bases in the final 14 games of the 2026 regular season. That is all that stands between him and one of the rarest individual achievements in baseball history.

The 40-40 Club has six members: Jose Canseco (1988), Barry Bonds (1996), Alex Rodriguez (1998), Alfonso Soriano (2006), Ronald Acuña Jr. (2023), and Shohei Ohtani (2024). The gap between those names and the rest of baseball is a testament to how few players combine elite power with elite speed at the level this milestone demands. PCA has a real chance to be the seventh.

What Makes This Specific

The home run side of this equation is already done. PCA hit his 40th on September 6. He is the first Cubs player to reach 40 home runs in a single season since Derrek Lee did it in 2005, and the youngest player in Cubs franchise history to reach the 40-homer mark, passing Ernie Banks. The history pile is already significant.

The steals are the variable. Crow-Armstrong has 36 on the season. He has been consistently aggressive on the bases all year, but September baserunning decisions are not always in a player's hands. The Cubs are in a Wild Card race. Pennant-race managers sometimes dial back the green light to protect baserunners and avoid potential outs that could cost a game. PCA will need his coaching staff to keep trusting him in the right moments — and he'll need to create those moments with the walks and base hits to get there.

Four Steals in 14 Games

Four steals in 14 games works out to roughly one every three and a half games. Over a full season, that pace would produce approximately 42 steals. Crow-Armstrong has been averaging steals at a faster rate than that over the course of 2026. The pace is achievable. The question is whether the opportunity presents itself in a lineup and situation context that allows him to run.

The Cubs play 14 games over the final stretch. The schedule includes games against opponents whose pitchers and catchers vary widely in their ability to control the running game. Crow-Armstrong's baserunning IQ has been one of the quiet stories of his 2026 season — he has not attempted steals recklessly, which is part of why his success rate has stayed at a level that keeps the Cubs comfortable sending him.

What's Next

Every Cubs game for the rest of the regular season is a 40-40 watch. Crow-Armstrong needs one successful steal every 3-4 games to close the deal. He's never done it as a Cub. No Cub ever has. The window is open and the ability is there. The final 14 games will tell the rest of the story.
