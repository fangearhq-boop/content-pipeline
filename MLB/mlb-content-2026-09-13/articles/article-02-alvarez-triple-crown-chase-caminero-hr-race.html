One Behind in Homers, One Step Ahead in History: Alvarez's Triple Crown Run

By Ryan Calloway September 13, 2026

Saturday's Walk-Off Complicates the Picture

Yordan Alvarez entered Sunday leading the American League in batting average at approximately .310. He had 38 home runs and approximately 96 RBI — both figures within striking distance of the leaders. Then on Saturday night, Junior Caminero of the Tampa Bay Rays hit a walk-off home run to give Tampa Bay a 3-1 win over the Astros. Caminero stayed at 39 HR, maintaining his one-home-run lead over Alvarez with roughly 14 games remaining.

The Triple Crown — leading your league in batting average, home runs, and RBI at the end of the season — is one of the rarest achievements in baseball. The last time it was won in the American League was Miguel Cabrera in 2012. Before Cabrera, Carl Yastrzemski did it in 1967. The list of AL Triple Crown winners is extraordinarily short, and Alvarez is positioned to add his name to it if the final two weeks go his way.

Where Each Category Stands

Batting average is the most stable of the three categories for Alvarez. He has held the AL lead for the better part of the second half and his gap over the next closest hitter gives him a reasonable cushion heading into the final stretch. A bad week at the plate could narrow it, but a dramatic collapse is unlikely for a hitter of his caliber.

The home run race is the active subplot. Caminero at 39 versus Alvarez at 38 is close enough that either player could take the lead on any given night. Caminero plays for a Rays team that still has meaningful September games to fill. Alvarez will need to stay hot at the plate and get pitch-to-hit counts in games where the Astros are still competing. The gap is narrow enough that both players finishing with equal HR totals is entirely possible.

RBI totals are harder to pin down precisely because they depend on lineup position and how often runners are on base in front of a hitter. Alvarez hits with power in an Astros lineup that generates opportunities. The category is close but not out of reach.

What the MVP Has Already Secured

Alvarez's candidacy for AL MVP is not in question regardless of the Triple Crown outcome. His .310 average, 38 home runs, and approximately 96 RBI against the quality of competition he has faced in 2026 would be an elite season under any framing. The Triple Crown chase is a chase for something additional — something that would put his 2026 season among the most decorated individual performances in recent AL history.

What's Next

Fourteen games separate Alvarez from either winning or not winning the AL Triple Crown. Every Caminero at-bat in Tampa Bay is now as relevant to Houston fans as Alvarez's own. The RBI race is similarly watchable. Over the next two weeks, the AL Triple Crown will resolve itself one way or another — and Alvarez has put himself in position to be the answer to a trivia question that has stayed dormant since 2012.
