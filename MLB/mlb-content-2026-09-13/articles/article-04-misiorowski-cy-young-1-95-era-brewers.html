1.95 ERA, 19 Wins: The Cy Young Race Is Jacob Misiorowski's to Lose

By Jake Torres September 13, 2026

The Numbers Speak Directly

Jacob Misiorowski leads Major League Baseball with a 1.95 ERA. He is 19-8. He has 236 strikeouts. He throws at or above 100 miles per hour on a consistent basis. He is 24 years old and in his second full season in the big leagues. The NL Cy Young Award market has him at -700 with the next candidate well behind.

There is no honest analysis of the 2026 NL Cy Young race that produces a different front-runner. Misiorowski has been the answer to that question for most of the summer, and his September work has done nothing to change it. The question entering the final stretch of the season is not whether he wins — it is whether his final numbers end up among the truly historic pitching seasons in the award's history.

What a 1.95 ERA in 2026 Actually Means

Modern Major League Baseball produces a lot of runs. The ball is designed to travel. Hitters spend more time in the weight room than at any prior point in the sport's history. Pitchers are pulled earlier than they once were, which can both help (they face lineups once) and limit (fewer innings to accumulate in a win-loss record). In this environment, a starting pitcher finishing a full season with a sub-2.00 ERA is genuinely extraordinary.

Misiorowski has not done it by avoiding contact or running from difficult situations. His 236 strikeouts reflect a pitcher who attacks and dominates. His 19-8 record reflects a pitcher whose team wins when he takes the ball. The combination of ERA, strikeouts, and win total tells the story of a pitcher who is doing everything the position asks of him at an elite level.

Brewers in the Playoff Picture

Every Misiorowski start in September matters twice. He is the primary engine of a Brewers team pushing for postseason positioning. Milwaukee has been one of the more interesting stories in the NL this season — a club that competes through pitching-driven baseball and relies on Misiorowski as the signature name at the top of the rotation.

When he takes the mound in September, the Brewers' playoff positioning shifts with each pitch. The overlap between his Cy Young case and the Brewers' October ambitions is not incidental. He is the reason both conversations are happening.

What's Next

Misiorowski's next start arrives in the coming days. Each outing in September either adds to the historical résumé or creates questions — though at a 1.95 ERA with 14 games remaining, the runway for anyone to overtake him is essentially gone. The vote will happen after the season ends. The winner will be the Milwaukee Brewers pitcher with the best ERA in baseball, the second-most wins in the National League, and one of the highest strikeout totals in recent NL memory.
