Asian Games Softball Super Round Set — Japan and China Unbeaten as Philippines Stun South Korea

By Sarah Mitchell | September 28, 2026

The Opening Round is over, and the picture at the 2026 Asian Games women's softball tournament is exactly as dramatic as the field expected.

Japan and China swept through the preliminary stage without losing a game. Chinese Taipei and the Philippines joined them in the Super Round — and the Philippines got there by eliminating South Korea with a stunning 7-3 comeback win in the final match of the Opening Round. The Super Round at Anjō Sports Park is now underway, with medal matches approaching and the October 3 Final in sight.

The Philippines Upset That Changed Everything

South Korea entered the 2026 Asian Games with a strong WBSC ranking and opened the tournament with a 7-0 win over Thailand. Then China beat them 8-4 — three home runs in that game made it look clinical. South Korea needed a win against the Philippines to stay alive.

They didn't get it. The Philippines' "Blu Girls" trailed 3-0 going into the sixth inning, then scored seven runs to win 7-3. It was the kind of inning that can define a tournament — and it sent South Korea home while the Philippines advanced to medal-round play for the first time.

For the Philippines softball program, just reaching the Super Round is significant. For South Korea, it is a painful exit from a tournament they were expected to survive. The 2026 Asian Games is already more interesting than most people anticipated.

Japan and China: The Teams to Beat

Japan has been the standard-bearer for international softball for over a decade, and their performances in the Opening Round at home in Anjō reinforced that. Ranked No. 1 in the world by the WBSC, they recorded shutout wins in the preliminary stage — the kind of statement performances you expect from a host nation chasing gold.

China has been the more aggressive story. Their 8-4 win over South Korea featured three home runs, a display of power that stands out in a sport where run prevention often dominates. China enters the Super Round ranked No. 8 in the world, but they are not playing like the eighth-best team here. They are a genuine threat to Japan's gold medal.

Chinese Taipei, ranked No. 5 globally, dominated the Philippines 10-1 in five innings during the Opening Round. They are the most polished challenger in this field and have the talent to push Japan in head-to-head play.

What the Super Round Means for LA28

Softball is returning to the Olympic program at the 2028 Los Angeles Games after a 20-year absence following the sport's exclusion after Beijing 2008. That return has reshaped the international softball calendar — every major tournament, every ranking point, every Super Round win now carries implications that extend two years into the future.

For Japan and China, the Asian Games is a critical stage on the road to LA28 seeding. For the Philippines, reaching the Super Round generates ranking points and experience against the top programs in Asia. Every result here matters beyond this week's medal stand.

The WBSC rankings that will shape Olympic qualifying pathways are built from competitions exactly like this one. The teams competing in Anjō right now are not just playing for Asian Games medals — they are playing for position in the global softball order heading into the next Olympic cycle.

What's Next

The Super Round continues through October 2, with Japan, China, Chinese Taipei, and the Philippines all competing in a round-robin format for medal placement. The gold medal Final is scheduled for October 3 at Anjō Sports Park.

The matchup to watch: Japan versus China. The two unbeaten teams from the Opening Round will face each other in the Super Round, and that game could effectively decide the gold medal before the Final is played. For fans tracking the road to LA28, this is the most important international women's softball competition of the fall. The results matter. The standings matter. And the October 3 Final will close out a tournament that has already delivered more drama than most expected.
