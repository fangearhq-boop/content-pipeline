Gasso Lands 2x NFCA All-American Sydney Berzon — Oklahoma's Transfer Portal Rebuild Takes Shape

By Sarah Mitchell | September 28, 2026

Patty Gasso has been through portal cycles before. This one is no different in that sense — players leave, players arrive, the program recalibrates — but the names involved make it worth tracking closely.

Oklahoma lost Tia Milloy and the Zache sisters, Berkley and Riley, to the transfer portal following the 2026 season. Those are not minor departures. Then Gasso went out and added Sydney Berzon from LSU: a two-time NFCA All-American (Second Team in 2024, Third Team in 2023) with one year of eligibility remaining. She also added Macie Harter from Middle Tennessee, who hit .346 with seven home runs, 23 RBI, and 19 stolen bases in 46 starts last season. The rebuild is underway, and it already has an identity.

Berzon Is the Headline

Sydney Berzon earned back-to-back NFCA All-American honors at LSU, which is as rigorous a pitching environment as exists in college softball. LSU pitchers get tested constantly — in the SEC, in tournament play, in environments where every hitter in the opposing lineup is dangerous.

Berzon handled that environment well enough to earn national recognition twice. She has one year of eligibility remaining at Oklahoma, which means the 2027 season is her last competitive opportunity. Players in that situation tend to arrive motivated. Gasso is betting that Berzon's experience, combined with the infrastructure at Oklahoma, will translate to production at a program that has won three national championships.

The priority need heading into the portal was a pitcher. Berzon fills that need at the highest available level. Whether additional portal additions follow — Gasso has suggested the roster is close to where she wants it — remains to be seen. But landing a two-time All-American from a major SEC program is a meaningful response to losing three players.

Gasso's Measured Message

Patty Gasso addressed the transfer portal publicly with comments that were confident without being dismissive. "We definitely are going to take a look-see in the portal to see what we can find for sure," she said. "We don't need a lot, don't need much at all. We'll be looking."

That framing — we'll look, but from a position of strength — is consistent with how Gasso has navigated roster construction throughout her career. Oklahoma doesn't panic. They assess. They target. And they have enough infrastructure in place — recruiting, coaching, facilities — that one portal cycle of losses doesn't destabilize the program.

Texas won back-to-back titles in 2025 and 2026. Oklahoma's response is to add experienced arms like Berzon and versatile contributors like Harter, while trusting the core of the roster that was already there. That is a measured, program-appropriate response from one of the sport's most accomplished coaches.

What's Next

Oklahoma's fall ball slate provides the first look at how the new-look Sooners integrate. Berzon's first actions in crimson and cream will be the most-watched development from a program accustomed to being in the national conversation. Gasso indicated additional portal activity is possible but not urgently needed. The 2027 season is when the results will be measured. For now, the signal from Norman is clear: Oklahoma is not rebuilding. They are reloading — and Berzon is the centerpiece of that effort.
