Mio Goto Throws Perfect Game as Japan Dominates Asian Games Day 5

By Emily Rawlings

October 1, 2026

Mio Goto retired all 18 batters she faced. Nine strikeouts. Sixty-four pitches. Not a single baserunner.

In Japan's 8-0 six-inning rout of Korea on September 30 at the 20th Asian Games, Goto delivered a perfect game — one of the most dominant pitching performances of the entire tournament in Anjō, Aichi, Japan. Combined with an earlier 10-0 run-rule victory over Singapore earlier that day, Japan closed out Day 5 unblemished and sits at 5-0.

The Japanese are not just winning. They're doing it comprehensively.

Two Wins, Two Shutouts

Before Goto's masterpiece against Korea, Saki Miwa had already taken care of business against Singapore. Miwa pitched four shutout innings in a 10-0 run-rule victory, allowing just one hit and limiting the Singaporeans to nothing. Hotaru Tsukamoto and Eri Shimoyama provided the offensive punch for Japan, each going 2-for-2 with two RBIs in that game.

Then came Goto's turn. Working with pinpoint control against Korea in the nightcap, Goto never allowed a baserunner, striking out nine while throwing a remarkably efficient 64 pitches. The final score — 8-0 in six innings — reflected Japan's complete command of the competition.

Race for the Finals

The standings after Day 5 tell the story clearly: Japan and China are both 5-0, and both have clinched spots in the medal-round games. Chinese Taipei, the World No. 5 team, sits at 4-1 and is in strong position to medal as well. Korea (2-3), Hong Kong (1-4), Singapore (0-5), and Thailand (0-5) are all but out of medal contention.

The October 3 finals at Anjō Sports Park carry significance well beyond a gold medal. This tournament is part of the LA28 Olympic qualifying pathway — meaning the teams competing for hardware here are also competing for a road to the Los Angeles Olympics. A Japan-China final would be a preview of a potential Olympic showdown, and a result that could shape the sport's Olympic narrative heading into 2028.

What's Next

The Super Round continues through October 2, with finals scheduled for October 3 at Anjō Sports Park. Japan and China meet in what could be the most consequential matchup of this stage of LA28 softball qualifying.
