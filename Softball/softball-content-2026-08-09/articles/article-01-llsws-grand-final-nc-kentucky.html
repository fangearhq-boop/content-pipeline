Grand Final Sunday: North Carolina vs. Kentucky Battle for the 2026 LLSWS Title

By Sarah Mitchell

August 9, 2026

This is what the whole summer has been building toward. Sunday afternoon at Stallings Stadium in Greenville, North Carolina, North Carolina's Johnston County Little League faces Kentucky's Daviess County Little League for the 2026 Little League Softball World Series championship. First pitch is at 3 PM ET on ABC.

Both teams earned their spots in the hardest way possible — by winning Saturday's bracket championship games and surviving a field that included the best Little League softball programs in the world. They've earned this stage.

North Carolina: Powered by Anna-Lynn Clark

The story of North Carolina's run to the Grand Final begins and ends with pitcher Anna-Lynn Clark. In the Purple Bracket Championship on Saturday, Clark struck out 11 Florida batters in a complete-game shutout, sending North Carolina to the title game with a 4-0 win. It was her third game at this Little League Softball World Series with at least 11 strikeouts. She now leads all tournament pitchers with 35 total strikeouts.

Clark has been dominant from the first day of pool play. She's given North Carolina's offense the luxury of needing only a handful of runs in any given game — the defense knows that if they score, they probably win. On Sunday, that dynamic holds again.

North Carolina enters the Grand Final as the Southeast Region representative and the team chasing history. A win today would give the program its 4th Little League Softball World Series title, joining championships in 2015, 2019, and 2024.

Kentucky: Built on Clutch

Kentucky's path here was defined by one moment. In Saturday's Orange Bracket Championship against New Jersey, the Daviess County Little League trailed with the game on the line. In the fifth inning, Evelyn Chelstrom lined a two-out RBI single to right field, scoring Michaela Knight with the go-ahead run. Kentucky held the 2-1 lead and won the right to play on Sunday.

That's what this Kentucky team does. The Central Region representative has shown throughout the tournament that they don't need blowouts — they need just enough. One-run wins take discipline, pitching, and a belief that you can hold what you have. Kentucky has demonstrated all three.

Against Anna-Lynn Clark and a North Carolina program that has won three championships in the modern era, Kentucky will need another clutch performance. They have shown they're capable of delivering it.

The Matchup

North Carolina comes in as the team with the most dominant pitching at the tournament. Clark's strikeout totals aren't just impressive — they reflect a pitcher who has simply been in a different gear than anyone else in Greenville. Kentucky has had to grind for runs throughout the week, but they know how to protect a lead.

The question on Sunday is whether Kentucky's hitters can make Clark work, extend at-bats, and manufacture enough offense to give their pitcher something to protect. A one-run lead late in a championship game is manageable. Falling behind Clark and needing to score two or three against a team that hasn't given much up all week is a different challenge.

North Carolina's advantage is on the mound. Kentucky's edge is experience with pressure moments — they've already won a one-run elimination game this week.

What's Next

Today's game is a single elimination championship — one team goes home with the title, the other goes home with silver. Watch live at 3 PM ET on ABC at Stallings Stadium in Greenville, North Carolina. The 2026 Little League Softball World Series title will be decided today.
