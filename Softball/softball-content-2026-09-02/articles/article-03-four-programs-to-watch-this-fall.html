Beyond the Big Three: Four Softball Programs Worth Watching This Fall

By Jordan Reeves | September 2, 2026 | I Love Softball

The conversation about the 2027 college softball season starts in the same three places every year: Oklahoma, Texas, and Texas Tech. They deserve that attention. But fall ball is the time to look beyond the obvious — and right now, there are at least four more programs building legitimate claims to 2027 relevance.

SI's fall preview highlighted four teams worth tracking as the 2027 season takes shape. Here's a look at what each brings to the conversation.

Oklahoma State — Five New Transfers, Fresh Identity

Oklahoma State added five transfers this offseason and has a fall slate of eight exhibition games to start integrating them. The additions: pitcher Delainey Everett from Mississippi State, catcher Kennedy Proctor from Utah, and utility players Maddie Hartley (Houston), Payton Kennedy (Ole Miss), and Kendra Lewis (Wisconsin).

That's five new bodies, five new roles to define, and fall camp as the crucible where the 2027 Cowgirls identity starts to form. OSU has been a program on the verge of a deeper postseason run for several years. This offseason's portal additions suggest the staff believes this is the cycle to make the jump.

Duke — 43-17 and Building

Duke went 43-17 in 2026 — a record-setting season for the Blue Devils that ended in the Super Regionals. Their fall schedule features matchups against North Carolina and South Carolina programs, plus a game against the Florida Vibe of the Professional Softball League.

Duke is one of the sport's most interesting growth stories. As an ACC program competing for postseason berths in a conference with powerhouses, they've built genuine credibility over the past few seasons. Their fall matchups against regional programs and a professional opponent give them diverse competition to measure themselves against before February.

Nebraska — The Big Ten Darkhorse

Nebraska doesn't always get the national attention their program warrants, and that's partly by design — the Cornhuskers build quietly and compete hard. Their fall schedule includes a matchup on October 17 at Bowlin Stadium, with more to be announced as the fall calendar fills in.

The Big Ten has become a genuinely competitive softball conference, and Nebraska represents the kind of program that can emerge from that landscape with a March breakthrough that catches the national spotlight off guard. Fall is where that kind of story starts.

Texas — The Program Everyone Else Needs to Beat

Yes, Texas is already part of the top-tier conversation — but their fall context is different from those programs. The Longhorns are defending back-to-back national champions (2025, 2026), chasing an unprecedented three-peat that only UCLA (1988-90) and Oklahoma (2021-23) have ever achieved in the sport's history.

Head coach Mike White has indicated the program will look at the transfer portal again to supplement a returning core that includes six of nine starters and pitcher Teagan Kavan — the first two-time WCWS Most Outstanding Player in history. Texas opens fall ball September 29 at McCombs Field against Murray State, with admission free to the public.

Watching Texas this fall is about understanding how a team carries the weight of being the favorite — and how a coach builds the competitive environment that makes a three-peat possible.

What's Next

Fall exhibitions and scrimmages are what give us the first real look at 2027 rosters in action. D1Softball's Fall Exhibition Central tracks results from programs across the country, providing the earliest glimpse at who's clicking and who needs more time to integrate.

The 2027 championship conversation is not over before it begins. Keep an eye on OSU, Duke, Nebraska, and Texas as they build toward February — because in college softball, April's champion is often determined by what happens on the practice fields in September.
