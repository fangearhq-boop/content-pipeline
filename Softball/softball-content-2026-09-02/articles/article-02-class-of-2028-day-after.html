September 1 Is Done: What the First Day of Class of 2028 Recruiting Looked Like

By Emily Rawlings | September 2, 2026 | I Love Softball

Yesterday was September 1 — the most important date on the college softball recruiting calendar for Class of 2028 prospects. For the first time, NCAA Division I coaches could initiate direct contact with high school juniors in the class: phone calls, text messages, emails, and visit invitations were all permitted starting at midnight.

If you were a top-priority recruit on a major program's board, there's a reasonable chance you heard from a coach before 8 AM.

How September 1 Works

September 1 is the first-contact date for D1 softball programs recruiting Class of 2028 players — those who will graduate high school in the spring of 2028. Before this date, coaches could accept questionnaires and send recruiting materials, but direct communication (calls, texts, emails) and in-person evaluation were both prohibited.

That changes on September 1. The coaches who have been watching film, attending showcases from a distance, and quietly building their target lists for months now have the green light to reach out directly. For programs with urgent needs, September 1 morning means getting on the phone immediately.

The first hour of September 1 morning tells you something. Programs with the highest-priority prospects on their boards make their calls first — signaling to the recruit that she's at the top of the list. How quickly a coach reaches out, and with what level of personal attention and preparation, communicates interest in a way that months of indirect outreach cannot.

What Comes Next — September 9

One week from today is the next critical milestone. On September 9 at 7:00 AM ET, the in-person evaluation window opens for the first time for Class of 2028 recruits.

That means coaches can travel to showcases, travel ball tournaments, and events to watch prospects compete live. From September 9 onward through November 30, coaches across the country will be criss-crossing the country evaluating the 2028 class in person. Top showcases in September carry extra weight this year because they're the first in-person looks coaches get.

For prospects and their families, the key action items right now:

  • Respond to outreach and build relationships with programs that called
  • Ensure upcoming fall schedules, showcase entries, and tournament plans are visible to coaches
  • Understand that September 1 contact is the start of the relationship, not the close — visits and official commitments come later

The Class at a Glance

Extra Inning Softball received 1,226 nominations for their Class of 2028 recognition program — the highest nomination count for any class in the organization's history. That volume signals the breadth and depth of this particular class: coaches across all D1 levels are watching more 2028 players than they have watched any class before.

On3's preview describes the class as featuring "small roster availability with big talent density" — which means top programs with open roster spots will move aggressively, and the best prospects will have multiple high-profile options to evaluate in the months ahead.

What's Next

The fall evaluation period runs through November 30. The combination of phone contact (September 1 onward) and in-person evaluation (September 9 onward) creates the most active stretch of the 2028 recruiting cycle. Verbal commitments from this class will begin to surface in October and November, likely accelerating as top programs lock in their highest-priority targets before the winter.

September 1 was the starting gun. The race is on.
