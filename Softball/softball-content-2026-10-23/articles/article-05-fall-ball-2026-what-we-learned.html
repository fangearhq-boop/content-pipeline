What Fall Ball 2026 Taught Us About the 2027 National Title Race

By Jordan Reeves | October 23, 2026

Fall ball does not award trophies. There are no standings, no bracket, no national championship on the line. What fall ball does — when programs use it right — is reveal things. It shows you roster depth. It exposes weaknesses. It tells you which players have taken a step forward and which programs have the foundation to run deep in June.

We are at the end of fall ball 2026. Here is what it taught us about the national title race that begins in February.

Oklahoma Has Not Lost a Step

Losing a class of players to graduation or the portal is always a question mark entering fall ball. Oklahoma answered that question clearly this autumn: the Sooners have not lost a step, and in some ways they have taken one forward.

Kendall Wells has been the story of every fall game she has played. After 39 home runs and NFCA Freshman of the Year honors in the 2026 spring, she has continued to produce at an elite level in the fall setting — including a home run in Game 4 of the Battle Series just two days ago. Wells is entering her sophomore season as one of the best hitters in college softball, and she is getting better.

Haley Kavan's return for a graduate season gives Oklahoma a security blanket that most programs would trade significant roster pieces to have. Two WCWS Most Outstanding Player awards are not accidents. Kavan elevates the players around her and provides a ceiling that most college pitching staffs cannot reach.

Jocelyn Berzon's arrival from LSU gives coach Patty Gasso a second legitimate ace. That depth — a Kavan-Berzon rotation — is not something teams build around overnight. Oklahoma has it. And with Finlee Williams, the top-ranked outfielder in the 2027 class, joining the program next fall, the Sooners are not in a rebuilding phase. They are in a reinforcing phase.

Florida Has Been the Other Dominant Fall Program

Forty-nine runs in two fall games is not a coincidence. It is a statement about a Florida lineup that is deeper, more powerful, and more confident than it has been in recent years.

Sophia Edwards hitting three home runs in the first two fall games is the kind of production that changes how opposing pitching staffs plan. Six starters returning means the Gators are not learning a new system — they are executing a known one with greater precision. The full pitching staff returning gives head coach Tim Walton the rotation he needs to make a deep postseason run.

Florida's commitment of Kylie Holden in the 2028 class — No. 2 overall by Softball America — shows a program that is not satisfied with the present. The pipeline is active, the roster is strong, and the recent fall performances suggest a team that is peaking at exactly the right time.

Tennessee Is a Legitimate Co-Favorite

Karlyn Pickens and Sage Mardjetko are not a normal 1-2 punch. A staff anchored by a No. 1 AUSL draft pick (194 K, 1.70 ERA) and an NFCA All-American (16-3, 1.26 ERA) is the type of pitching that wins championships. Tennessee has gone at least 3-0 in fall ball and plays its finale tonight, and the staff has been every bit as dominant as advertised.

What makes the 2027 Lady Vols more dangerous than prior Tennessee teams is the addition of Ehren Earleywine as associate head coach and hitting coach. Earleywine comes directly from the Texas staff that won the 2026 national championship. His job is to build an offense that matches what Pickens and Mardjetko do in the circle. If that happens — even partially — Tennessee's ceiling rises significantly.

The Lady Vols are not a dark horse anymore. They are a co-favorite, and fall ball has done nothing but confirm it.

Texas Tech Is the Team Nobody Should Overlook

Texas Tech reached the WCWS final last spring. Their response was to add the best available player in the portal: Kasidi Pickering, who batted .384 with 50 home runs and 169 RBI over three seasons at Oklahoma. Then they hired Jeff Cottrill and Shelby Pendley — a staff combination with WCWS credentials and deep knowledge of Big 12 competition — and added Heavener, Polar, and Gallaway through the portal.

The Red Raiders open Big 12 play on March 12. They are not a program building toward contention. They are already there, and the 2026 fall has shown a roster with genuine national title capability. Sleep on Texas Tech in 2027 at your own risk.

What's Next

Fall ball wraps up across the country over the next two to three weeks. After that, the offseason begins in earnest — portal movement, recruiting visits, and preparation for 2027 spring schedules. The next public look at most of these programs will come in February, when rankings and expectations meet live-game reality. Based on what fall ball has shown, Oklahoma, Florida, Tennessee, and Texas Tech all have strong cases for being the team that cuts down the nets in Oklahoma City next June.
