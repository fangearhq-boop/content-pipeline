Tennessee's Fall Finale Is Tonight — Pickens, Mardjetko Close Out a Dominant Autumn

By Jordan Reeves | October 23, 2026

Tennessee's 2026 fall campaign comes to a close tonight at Sherri Parker Lee Stadium in Knoxville.

The Lady Vols host Lee University at 6:00 PM ET in a free, open-to-the-public game that marks the final fall appearance for one of the most-discussed programs in 2027 national title conversations. Concessions will not be available, but gates open an hour before first pitch. Come early, stay for the whole thing, and catch a program that has spent all fall proving it belongs in the championship picture.

Because here is the thing about this Tennessee team: the 2026 fall has not been a typical fall. It has been a statement.

The Pitching Staff Has Been Everything Advertised

When Tennessee announced the return of Karlyn Pickens and Sage Mardjetko for 2027, the assumption was that the Lady Vols had one of the deepest pitching staffs in program history. Fall ball confirmed it.

Pickens — the No. 1 pick in the AUSL Draft, the best collegiate pitcher in the country by most measures — entered the fall with 194 strikeouts and a 1.70 ERA from her most recent spring campaign. She has done nothing this autumn to suggest that trajectory is changing. Pickens is a generational talent, and Tennessee has her through the spring of 2027.

Mardjetko provides a complement that makes the staff even more formidable. A 16-3 record and a 1.26 ERA earned NFCA All-American recognition, and she has been just as sharp in the fall setting. When a team's No. 2 starter has a 1.26 ERA, its rotation is not a weakness — it is an asset that can carry a team through a postseason run.

Together, Pickens and Mardjetko represent the most dangerous pitching combination Tennessee has put on a fall roster in recent memory. Tonight is the last time to see them together until February.

Earleywine and the Offensive Potential

Head coach Karen Weekly made a significant move this offseason, bringing in Ehren Earleywine as associate head coach and hitting coach. Earleywine comes directly from the Texas program that won the 2026 national championship, where he was part of a staff that developed one of the most productive lineups in college softball.

What that hire signals is clear: Tennessee's pitching was never the problem, and Weekly knows it. The program's next step requires a corresponding offensive upgrade — an attack that matches what Pickens and Mardjetko do in the circle. Earleywine's presence at fall ball has already given the Lady Vols hitters a different look in the batter's box. The results in the spring will be the real measure, but the early returns have been encouraging.

Tennessee went at least 3-0 in fall ball, including a 32-0 win over Tusculum that showed the offense matching the program's pitching ambitions. Tonight's finale against Lee University is one more opportunity to build on that momentum before the offseason begins.

What's Next

Tonight closes Tennessee's 2026 fall schedule — free at Sherri Parker Lee Stadium, first pitch 6:00 PM ET. After tonight, the Lady Vols head into the offseason with recruiting, roster development, and spring preparation ahead. The 2027 season opens in February, and if this fall is any indication, Tennessee will be one of the two or three programs with a genuine shot at the Women's College World Series title. Enjoy the finale while it lasts.
