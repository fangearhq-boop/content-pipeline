Landers Is a Longhorn: Texas Adds No. 1 Recruit as Fall Opener Arrives in Eight Days

By Jordan Reeves | I Love Softball | September 21, 2026

Texas softball is eight days from its fall opener, and the program just became measurably stronger.

The Sept. 18 commitment of Michaela Landers — Softball America's No. 1 overall prospect in the 2028 class — comes to a program already operating at the peak of collegiate softball. The Longhorns are defending national champions (53-12, 2026 WCWS title), their coaching staff won a second consecutive NFCA Staff of Year award, and they return two of the most celebrated players in the sport.

Now they have the top recruit in the country's next class in their pocket, too.

Present and Future, Aligned

What makes the Landers commitment especially meaningful is the context in which it arrives. Texas is not a program rebuilding with future stars. It is a program at the height of its powers using future stars to signal that the height is sustainable.

Isa Torres, the 2026 D1Softball Player of Year who transferred from Florida State — she hit .530 with 16 home runs and 56 RBI — is a Georgetown, Texas native returning to her home state with a national title already on her resume. She will be the centerpiece of Karen White's lineup for the next two seasons.

Jordy Kavan enters her senior year as the only player in college softball history to win the WCWS Most Outstanding Player award twice. That combination — a two-time MOP senior and an All-American-caliber POY transfer — gives Texas a present tense that is nearly impossible to argue with. Landers secures the future tense in the same breath.

The Three-Peat Chase Begins Sept. 29

Texas opens its fall season Sept. 29 against Murray State at McCombs Field. First pitch is set for 6 PM Central. Admission is free.

For fans who want their first look at how Torres and Kavan coexist in burnt orange, how the new additions fit around the returning core, and whether this roster can sustain the standard set by consecutive national championships, Sept. 29 is the starting line.

The stakes of a three-peat are historically unique. Only two programs in college softball history have won three consecutive national titles: UCLA (1988–90) and Oklahoma (2021–23). A third Texas championship in 2027 would place the Longhorns alongside both dynasties as the only programs to accomplish the feat. Karen White has guided the program to its first back-to-back titles. The step from back-to-back to three-peat is where coaching legacies are made.

What's Next

The next eight days are about preparation. Fall ball is not the regular season, but it is the foundation — and every rep Torres takes in the box, every pitch Kavan delivers from the circle, every defensive rotation that clicks into place before Sept. 29 matters. Landers' commitment does not change what happens this fall, but it changes what the next four years of Texas softball look like. That combination of present excellence and confirmed future depth is what makes this program the most watched in the country right now. McCombs Field on Sept. 29. Free admission. Be there.
