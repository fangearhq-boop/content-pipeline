Seven All-Americans, One Team: Texas Tech's Week 3 Is Where Identity Forms

By Sarah Mitchell | I Love Softball | September 21, 2026

No program in Texas Tech softball history has assembled what Gerry Glasco has in Lubbock right now: seven All-Americans on one fall roster, practicing together entering Week 3.

It is an extraordinary collection. And it is beginning to look like more than a collection.

The 2026 Red Raiders went 61-10, won the Big 12 regular season title and Super Regional, and fell one game short of a national championship in the Women's College World Series. Glasco's second year at the helm produced a program-record win total and a WCWS appearance that ended in heartbreak against Texas. Year 2 begins with the same core, reinforced by a transfer class that added three more All-Americans to a roster that was already elite.

Three New Names That Changed the Calculus

Texas Tech's fall 2026 roster features the three most prominent transfer additions in the program's recent history, and each represents a different strength.

Jaysoni Beachum — All-ACC First Team at Florida State — brings one of the most complete offensive profiles of any position player who entered the transfer portal this cycle. Her .406 batting average, 10 home runs and 69 RBI across 2026 are a résumé that made every elite program in the country stand up. She chose Lubbock.

Kasidi Pickering arrived as the consensus No. 2 transfer in the portal after a career at Oklahoma that produced a .384 batting average and 50 home runs. She is an outfielder with the power to reach any gap in any park, and the experience of competing in the sport's biggest moments from her time with the Sooners.

Jayden Heavener spent three seasons at LSU as a left-handed starting pitcher. Her 2026 numbers — 13-9, 2.81 ERA, 128 strikeouts — represent a workhorse arm capable of eating innings and winning at the conference level. In a rotation that already had depth, Heavener adds a genuine second ace option.

What Week 3 Means

In any fall camp, Week 1 is introduction. Week 2 is adjustment. Week 3 is where programs find out who they actually are.

For Texas Tech, that means Beachum, Pickering and Heavener are no longer newcomers — they are participants. The question is not whether the talent is good enough. The question is whether the talent is cohesive enough. Whether seven All-Americans from three different programs can subordinate individual identity to collective purpose. Whether Glasco — who has won Big 12 Coach of Year honors in back-to-back seasons — can build a team in the truest sense of the word out of the sport's most ambitious talent aggregation.

Seven All-Americans. One shared hunger. The 2026 WCWS final loss to Texas is still recent enough to sting — and still fresh enough to motivate every practice in Lubbock between now and next February.

What's Next

Texas Tech's fall schedule will offer the first public window into how this roster performs as a unit. The Red Raiders' 2027 regular season will begin in February, but the foundation is being poured right now. Glasco's program has earned its place among the nation's elite — two consecutive Big 12 regular season titles and a WCWS berth confirm it. What Year 2 ultimately delivers will depend on how well seven All-Americans become one team in a Lubbock fall. Week 3 is where the answer begins to form.
