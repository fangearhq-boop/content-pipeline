The Week That Defined the 2028 Class: Landers to Texas, Schlader to Oklahoma

By Emily Rawlings | I Love Softball | September 21, 2026

In the history of any recruiting class, there are weeks that define it. For the 2028 softball class, this was one of them.

Within days of each other, two of the most coveted prospects in the nation — one a middle infielder, one a pitcher — made their commitments. And in a twist that perfectly captures the stakes of today's recruiting battles, they went to rival programs that were each other's final competition for the other's signature.

Michaela Landers, the No. 1 overall prospect in the 2028 class according to Softball America, chose Texas. Sophia Schlader, the No. 1-ranked pitcher and No. 3 overall player in the class, chose Oklahoma. Two programs, two franchise-altering commitments, one extraordinary week.

Landers to Texas: The No. 1 Overall Recruit Makes Her Call

Michaela Landers grew up less than 30 miles from the University of Texas campus. The Waxahachie, Texas native is a sophomore shortstop and middle infielder who brought historic production to her high school team this year: .639 batting average, 25 home runs — a new Dallas-area record, breaking the 24 set by Florida signee Kennedy Bradley in 2025 — 73 runs scored and 52 extra-base hits across 41 games.

Her skillset reads like a checklist of what every elite program dreams of. Elite arm strength. Lightning-quick reactions at short. True plus-plus range in the field. And at the plate, a rare combination of power and contact that sets her apart from every other prospect in the class. Softball America ranked her No. 1 overall. Line Drive Media placed her at No. 2 in its HOT 100.

She committed to the Longhorns on Sept. 18, choosing Texas over Oklahoma and Florida. For Karen White's program — already featuring 2026 D1Softball Player of Year Isa Torres and two-time WCWS MOP Jordy Kavan — landing Landers signals that Texas's pipeline extends well beyond its current roster.

Schlader to Oklahoma: The No. 1 Pitcher Picks the Sooners

Oklahoma did not have to wait long for its own answer.

Sophia Schlader, out of Waukee Northwest High School in Urbandale, Iowa, announced her commitment to Patty Gasso and the Sooners just days after Landers' Texas decision. The Iowa Gatorade Player of Year for 2025-26 is a two-time state champion and the most dominant young pitcher in the country.

As a sophomore, Schlader went 30-1 with 296 strikeouts in 187 innings of work. She stands 5-foot-8, throws from the right side and offers utility as a two-way player when not in the circle. Schlader is ranked No. 1 among pitchers and No. 3 overall in the 2028 class — and she chose Oklahoma over Texas.

Gasso now has two members of the 2028 class committed, including the class's top pitcher. For a program that already owned the top MIF in Finlee Williams (No. 1 overall) and has secured Saenz as well, Schlader gives Oklahoma a recruiting class that competes on paper with any in recent memory.

The Broader 2028 Landscape After Day 13

Thirteen days into the Sept. 9 in-person window, the 2028 class has produced a wave of commitments unprecedented in modern recruiting history. Tennessee added Reagan Eldred this week. The class has already seen commitments from: Williams (No. 1 overall to OU), Donalson (FSU), Saenz (OU), Gutierrez (TTU), Galindo and Wilson (Tennessee), Losh and Harris (Florida), Myers (South Carolina), and Ridling and Stell (Arizona).

This year's Extra Innings Softball nominations set an all-time record at 1,226. Top uncommitted prospects still to watch: Kylie Holden and Bennett, both capable of producing headlines in the weeks ahead.

What's Next

The 2028 in-person window continues through the fall, and the decisions of remaining elite uncommitted prospects will shape the final class rankings expected in mid-October. Both Landers and Schlader will suit up for their high school programs through the academic year before joining their college programs — but their impact on Texas and Oklahoma softball begins now, in how they shift each program's self-perception and recruiting leverage for every class to follow.
