### STORY 3: Oklahoma — FB Long-Form
**Posting Window:** 11:00 AM ET
**Type:** Long-Form

Time is precious at Oklahoma this week.

Patty Gasso and her two top assistants — son JT Gasso and Jennifer Rocha — are departing for Team USA duties on September 12, just five days away. That means this week is the final full week of fall camp with the complete Sooners coaching staff, and everyone in Norman knows it.

What has fall camp shown so far? Kendall Wells — who shattered the NCAA freshman home run record with 32 bombs last year — is already establishing herself as a sophomore force. She and transfer pitcher Sydney Berzon have been pushing each other in scrimmages, with Wells homering twice off the two-time All-American from LSU.

Berzon brings 486.1 innings of work and a 2.02 ERA from her three years at LSU. Macie Harter (.346 BA from MTSU) adds another dangerous bat. The roster has depth to weather Gasso's 12-day absence.

But the coaching staff wants to be intentional with this final week. Fall opener comes September 30 at Love's Field. Oklahoma needs to be sharp — and with five days to install everything before the staff leaves, every practice rep counts.

### STORY 4: Texas Longhorns — FB Long-Form
**Posting Window:** 1:00 PM ET
**Type:** Long-Form

Something special is building at McCombs Field.

Texas softball is entering Week 3 of fall camp with two of the most decorated players in the game sharing the same dugout for the first time. Isa Torres, the 2026 D1Softball National Player of the Year (.530 BA, 16 HR, 56 RBI at FSU), is beginning to look like a true Longhorn. Teagan Kavan, the only player in WCWS history to win back-to-back Most Outstanding Player awards, is entering her senior season with one thing on her mind: a three-peat.

Only two programs have ever won three consecutive Women's College World Series titles: UCLA (1988–1990) and Oklahoma (2021–2023). Texas is 22 days away from the first step — Sept 29 at home vs Murray State, McCombs Field, 6 PM CT, free to the public.

The chemistry between Torres and Kavan grows every practice. The question isn't whether Texas is a favorite — it's whether this team can reach a level no program has reached in over 30 years.

### STORY 5: Texas Tech — FB Long-Form
**Posting Window:** 3:00 PM ET
**Type:** Long-Form

Seven All-Americans. One roster. Week 3.

That is the reality at Texas Tech softball as Gerry Glasco's program heads deeper into fall camp. Jaysoni Beachum (.406 BA, 10 HR, 69 RBI — All-ACC First Team from FSU), Kasidi Pickering (No. 2 transfer portal, .384 BA, 50 HR from OU), and Jayden Heavener (13-9, 2.81 ERA, 128 Ks from LSU) are the headliners, but none of them have ever shared a field together in a competitive setting until this fall.

That's the great experiment of Glasco's Year 2 in Lubbock — turning an ensemble cast of proven winners into a team with one common identity.

By Week 3, the early signs are encouraging. Leaders are stepping forward. Roles are defining themselves. The Red Raiders who were WCWS runners-up under a different coach in 2026 are building toward something under Glasco that could be even more dangerous in 2027.
