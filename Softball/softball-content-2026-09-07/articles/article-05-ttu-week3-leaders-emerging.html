Texas Tech Softball Week 3: Leaders Are Emerging From Glasco's All-Star Roster

By Emily Rawlings | September 7, 2026

Texas Tech Red Raiders

Seven All-Americans. Three different Power Conference programs of origin. One Week 3 of fall camp in Lubbock.

Gerry Glasco has spent the past two and a half weeks of fall camp asking a fundamental question: can a roster assembled from elite individual talent become a team? Early in Week 3, the answer is beginning to look like yes — and the leaders who will define this group's character are stepping forward.

The headliner trio is well documented: Jaysoni Beachum, who posted a .406 batting average, 10 home runs, and 69 RBI while earning All-ACC First Team honors at Florida State; Kasidi Pickering, the No. 2-ranked transfer portal entry at Oklahoma with a .384 BA and 50 home runs; and Jayden Heavener, the LSU left-hander who went 13-9 with a 2.81 ERA and 128 strikeouts. None of them had competed together before this August.

The Lab Experiment

Glasco's challenge in Year 2 at Texas Tech is not talent acquisition — that is clearly solved. It is integration. Three programs. Three distinct cultures of winning. Three players accustomed to being the centerpiece of their respective rosters.

In a team sport, individual excellence needs an outlet. By Week 3, the Red Raiders are finding it. Beachum's leadership presence in the batter's box has set a tone at the plate. Pickering's power draws defensive attention that creates opportunities for others. Heavener's presence in the circle gives Glasco confidence to build game plans around a proven arm.

But the more important development is the quieter one: who is filling the gaps between the stars? Fall camp is where role players define themselves, where practice intensity reveals depth, where a program's identity shifts from its name players to its collective character.

Glasco's Year 2 Promise

Glasco arrived at Texas Tech in 2026 after serving as head coach at LSU, where his program finished as WCWS runners-up in 2026. He brought with him a recruiting philosophy centered on proven winners and a track record of developing talent at the highest level.

Year 1 was a proof of concept. Year 2 carries expectations. Beachum, Pickering, and Heavener were brought in knowing those expectations — and Week 3 is where they start fulfilling them publicly, one fall rep at a time.

What's Next

Texas Tech continues fall camp through the coming weeks before its fall schedule begins. The Red Raiders' full lineup — seven All-Americans who have never competed together before this fall — is building toward a 2027 regular season that could make Lubbock a legitimate contender for a deep WCWS run. Week 3 is the moment leadership roles are earned, not assigned.
