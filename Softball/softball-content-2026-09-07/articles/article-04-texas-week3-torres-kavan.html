Torres and Kavan Head Into Week 3: The Chemistry Texas Has Been Waiting For

By Sarah Mitchell | September 7, 2026

Texas Longhorns

Three weeks into fall camp, something is taking shape at McCombs Field that has the Texas softball fanbase paying closer attention than any other program in the country. The Isa Torres and Teagan Kavan experiment — pairing two of the sport's most decorated players on the same roster — is entering its most telling phase yet.

Torres brought a resume that would define most careers before ever stepping onto a Texas field: the 2026 D1Softball National Player of the Year award, a .530 batting average, 16 home runs, and 56 RBI across her final season at Florida State. She is a Georgetown, Texas native — and her decision to transfer home carried a weight beyond the stats. This is personal.

Kavan brings something different: history. The senior pitcher is the only player in Women's College World Series history to win consecutive Most Outstanding Player awards, claiming the honor in both 2025 and 2026 while leading Texas to back-to-back national titles.

22 Days to Murray State

The fall opener lands September 29 — a home game at McCombs Field against Murray State College, free for the public, 6:00 PM CT. Every week of fall camp between now and then is about converting individual excellence into team cohesion.

Through Week 3, the signs are encouraging. Torres is adjusting to a new league, a new coaching staff, and a new hitting environment under Mike White. Kavan is absorbing a team that now features one of the most potent offensive weapons in the game sharing the dugout with her. Katie Stewart, who holds the Texas single-season home run record at 31, returns as well.

This is not a roster with one star. It is a roster asking every star to fit within a system — and Week 3 is where the chemistry either locks in or reveals its friction points.

Chasing History

The stakes for 2027 are clear. Only two programs have ever won three consecutive Women's College World Series titles: UCLA won three straight from 1988 to 1990, and Oklahoma repeated the feat from 2021 to 2023. No program has done it in the 30-plus years since those Bruins.

Texas has won two straight. The three-peat is the stated goal. What unfolds in the coming weeks of fall camp — and then in 22 days against Murray State — is the opening chapter of whether this team can write that history.

What's Next

Texas opens fall ball September 29 at McCombs Field (free for the public, 6:00 PM CT). The schedule runs through November 6 at Texas A&M. Week 3 of fall camp this week is the critical window where team identity crystallizes. Watch for Torres's comfort level at the plate and Kavan's in-game sharpness as the primary indicators of where Texas stands heading into the fall schedule.
