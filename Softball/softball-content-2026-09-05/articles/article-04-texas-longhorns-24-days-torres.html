24 Days to Game Day: Torres Arrives in Austin and the Three-Peat Chase Is On

By Jordan Reeves September 5, 2026

Torres Takes the Field in Burnt Orange

Isa Torres went home to Texas, but not to a quiet offseason. The 2026 D1Softball National Player of the Year — .530 batting average, 16 home runs, 56 RBIs at FSU — transferred to the Longhorns this summer. She grew up in Georgetown, Texas, about 30 miles north of Austin. Now she's in her first week of fall practice at McCombs Field, figuring out what life looks like as part of the most successful program in college softball right now.

Texas went 53-12 in 2026 and won the national championship. That was the second straight title. They're back-to-back Women's College World Series champions, and Torres chose to join a program with a target on its back. The question for this week — Week 1 of fall practice — isn't whether she fits. It's what the combination of Torres and Teagan Kavan looks like when they're on the same field together every day.

The Kavan Factor

Teagan Kavan became the first player in WCWS history to win back-to-back Most Outstanding Player awards. Her 2025 and 2026 performances at the national championship level put her in a category occupied by very few players in the sport's history. She's a senior in 2026-27, and this is her last shot at something no pitcher has ever done: three consecutive WCWS MOP awards.

Katie Stewart returns as well, bringing 31 home runs — the Texas school record — to a lineup that already ranked among the most dangerous in the country last spring. Add Torres and the offensive ceiling gets even higher. For opposing pitchers, there's genuinely no break in the Texas lineup from top to bottom. The Longhorns will be favored to win the national title before the first pitch of spring 2027, and that pressure starts in fall camp.

The Three-Peat Conversation

Three consecutive national titles in Division I softball has happened twice. UCLA did it from 1988 to 1990. Oklahoma did it from 2021 to 2023. Texas is now chasing the same history. It's not just hard to sustain talent — it's hard to sustain focus. Championship programs need to find a new hunger each season, and the teams that managed three straight all found different reasons to win each year.

For Texas in 2027, that reason might already be standing in the practice facility. Torres didn't come to Austin to be part of a program that already won. She came to win again. That kind of addition doesn't just improve the roster — it reframes the mentality. Fall camp is where that mentality gets established.

What's Next

Texas opens its fall schedule on Sept. 29 at McCombs Field vs. Murray State at 6 PM CT. The game is free and open to the public. The full fall slate includes Texas State (Oct. 13), Temple College (Oct. 20), McLennan CC (Oct. 27), UTSA (Oct. 29), and a road trip to Texas A&M on Nov. 6. Watch for Torres and Kavan's early fall results as a preview of what the 2027 Longhorns will bring.
