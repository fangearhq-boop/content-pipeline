Week 2 at Texas Tech: Are Beachum, Pickering, and Heavener Starting to Click?

By Sarah Mitchell September 5, 2026

Three Programs, One Locker Room

The résumés are real. Jaysoni Beachum left FSU after hitting .406 with 10 home runs and 69 RBIs in 2026, earning a spot on the All-ACC First Team. Kasidi Pickering left Oklahoma with a .384 career batting average and 50 home runs over three seasons — the No. 2 ranked transfer in the country per Softball America. Jayden Heavener came over from LSU with a 13-9 record and a 2.81 ERA, after winning a Florida 6A state championship in 2024.

Together, they bring FSU, OU, and LSU's culture into the Texas Tech locker room. That's three powerhouse programs with distinct identities, distinct language, distinct ways of competing. Gerry Glasco's job in Year 2 isn't just to put the best players on the field. It's to make those players feel like they belong to something new. Week 2 of fall camp is when that starts to become visible.

The Talent Ceiling Is Already There

Texas Tech was the national runner-up in 2026. They lost to Texas in the WCWS Finals. That stings — but it also establishes a baseline. This program can compete for a national title. Glasco proved that in his first year. Now, with a roster that's arguably deeper and more versatile than last year's, the question isn't whether Texas Tech is a contender. The question is whether they can finish.

Pickering's bat in Lubbock is a legitimate game-changer. She drew 40+ walks in each of her last three seasons at OU, which means pitchers can't nibble around her. Beachum's contact rate — just eight strikeouts all of 2026 — adds a different dimension. And Heavener's lefty arm gives Glasco flexibility in how he builds his staff around the pitching personnel already on campus.

What Chemistry Actually Looks Like

In Week 1 of fall camp, players are still learning where to park, which dining hall to eat at, and who to talk to when something goes wrong. By Week 2, the first layers of actual team trust start to form. Who hits the cutoff man without being told. Who picks up a teammate after an error. Who shows up early to catch in the bullpen without anyone asking.

Those small moments are what Glasco is watching this week. The big-picture talent was never in question. The Red Raiders have the pieces to win a national championship in 2027. Whether they do depends on whether Beachum, Pickering, Heavener, and the rest of this roster can build the kind of identity that carries a team through a 60-game season and a postseason run. Fall camp is when that work starts.

What's Next

Texas Tech's fall schedule hasn't been announced publicly as of this writing. Watch for Glasco's comments on his new additions in upcoming media availability as fall camp continues through September. The Red Raiders will be among the favorites to reach the 2027 WCWS — the question is whether the chemistry that gets built in Lubbock this fall will be enough to get them over the top.
