Tennessee Brings the Fall Ball Home: Pickens, Mardjetko Lead Lady Vols Home Stand

By Jordan Reeves

October 19, 2026

After a Sunday road trip to Murfreesboro for an exhibition at Middle Tennessee State, Tennessee softball is back where it belongs — at Sherri Parker Lee Stadium in Knoxville. The Lady Vols are hosting their third fall weekend of 2026 this week, with Northeast Alabama, Northwest Florida State, and Roane State all making the trip to East Tennessee.

Free admission. A legitimate national title contender. Fall games at one of college softball's best venues. For anyone within driving distance of Knoxville, there is no better option on the calendar this week.

The 1-2 Punch No Program Can Match

The first question anyone asks about Tennessee's 2027 chances leads immediately to the same answer: the pitching staff. Head coach Karen Weekly hasn't hidden her enthusiasm. She's called this the deepest staff in program history — a statement that earns a lot more credibility when you see the names behind it.

Karlyn Pickens is still in Knoxville. The No. 1 overall pick in the 2026 AUSL College Draft, Pickens posted 194 strikeouts and a 1.70 ERA over 148 innings in her final spring season at Tennessee. She won two First Team All-SEC honors and led the Lady Vols back to the Women's College World Series. Pickens didn't have to return — she was already a professional. But she did, and Tennessee is better for it.

Running alongside Pickens is Sage Mardjetko, who went 16-3 with a 1.26 ERA last spring while earning NFCA All-American honors. A program would typically build its entire staff development plan around a pitcher of Mardjetko's caliber. At Tennessee, she's the No. 2 arm.

There is no comparable 1-2 in college softball right now. Oklahoma has talent. Florida has depth. No one has this.

Clarke's Breakout Changes the Offensive Equation

If Tennessee's pitching is the foundation, the offense is quickly becoming the conversation. Sophomore Emma Clarke gave fans a preview earlier this fall with a rare fall ball cycle — home run, triple, two doubles, and six RBI in a 32-0 win over Tusculum. It's the kind of performance that announces a career.

The Lady Vols entered October at 3-0 in fall exhibitions, and Sunday's road trip to MTSU extended that stretch. With the home weekend approaching, Tennessee has an opportunity to continue building momentum in front of its own crowd.

The combination of elite pitching and an emerging offensive weapon in Clarke makes Tennessee's profile this fall genuinely different from prior years. Last spring, the Lady Vols fell to Texas in the WCWS. This year's roster, with a returning core and the additions of fall ball, suggests the gap is narrowing — or closing entirely.

What the Home Weekend Means

Tennessee's home fall games serve a purpose beyond exhibitions. They're an opportunity for coaches to test lineups, evaluate depth, and build chemistry in front of a home environment. Hosting Northeast Alabama, Northwest Florida State, and Roane State gives Weekly a range of competition to work through while keeping the energy at Sherri Parker Lee Stadium high.

For fans, these games offer rare October access to a roster that will be nationally ranked in February. Fall ball doesn't count in the record book, but the evaluations that happen in October shape the decisions that define March and April. Watching Clarke, Pickens, and Mardjetko in fall ball is watching the 2027 national championship conversation happen in real time.

What's Next

Tennessee's fall exhibition schedule continues through October with the home weekend at Sherri Parker Lee Stadium this week, followed by additional games before the program shifts focus to 2027 spring preparations. Admission to fall exhibitions is free. If you're in the Knoxville area, get to the ballpark — you're watching one of the nation's premier programs build toward a title run.
