Jaysoni Beachum Picks Texas Tech: The Portal Chapter Closes, and a New One Opens in Lubbock

By Jordan Reeves

August 1, 2026

After three seasons at Florida State and weeks in the transfer portal, Jaysoni Beachum has made her decision: she's a Texas Tech Lady Raider. The 2024 NFCA Freshman of the Year and 2026 Second Team All-American will bring her career statistics — .375 batting average, 37 home runs, 180 RBI — to Lubbock, completing what transfer portal analysts had ranked as the No. 1 transfer class in the country. For Texas Tech, this is the moment that takes the Lady Raiders from contenders to a genuine national title threat. For Florida State, it's another seismic blow to a program already reeling.

What Texas Tech Is Getting

Beachum is not a project. She is an established star with a three-year track record of elite production at one of the sport's blue-blood programs. As a freshman in 2024, she hit .417 with 16 home runs and 66 RBI — numbers that earned her the NFCA Freshman of the Year Award, the ACC Freshman of the Year Award, and a spot on the national radar that never dimmed. She built on that across two more seasons at FSU, finishing her Seminole career at .375 overall with 37 home runs and 180 RBI.

She was also named to the 2026 All-ACC Championship Team — meaning she was performing at the highest level in one of college softball's toughest conferences right up until the moment she entered the portal.

Texas Tech already had a strong transfer class entering this cycle. The addition of Pickering from Oklahoma gave the Lady Raiders a high-impact bat in the middle of the lineup. Beachum's commitment takes that class to a different tier. National rankings now have Texas Tech at No. 1 in the 2026 transfer portal cycle — and it's not particularly close.

FSU's Summer of Upheaval

For Florida State, Beachum's departure follows a summer that has tested the program's foundation in ways it hasn't experienced in years. First came the news that Isa Torres — the D1Softball 2026 National Player of the Year, .530 batting average, 16 home runs, 56 RBI in 2026 — was transferring to Texas. Then, weeks later, Beachum entered the portal.

Together, Torres and Beachum accounted for 28% of FSU's runs and 35% of the program's home runs in 2026. Losing both players to the portal in the same cycle — not to mention losing them to the same conference (Big 12) — is an almost unprecedented hit for a program of FSU's stature. Head coach Lonni Alameda, who already navigated a difficult 2026 season that included a home regional loss to Stetson and UCF, now faces the most significant roster rebuild of her tenure.

The Recruiting Countdown

With the September 1 recruiting window now 31 days away — the date when Division I coaches can begin officially contacting Class of 2028 prospects — the timing of Beachum's commitment adds another layer to what has been an unusually active summer in the portal. Programs across the country are locked into their 2027 rosters and beginning to think seriously about 2028 recruiting. Texas Tech, freshly armed with Beachum and Pickering, enters that recruiting battle from a position of significant strength.

What's Next

Beachum will join Texas Tech for fall practice and prepare for her senior season in 2027. The Lady Raiders will be one of the most-watched programs in the country as the season approaches — a transfer-class No. 1 ranking creates expectations, and those expectations are going to come with a national title-or-bust standard. For Beachum, the stage is set: one more shot, in a new city, to add a national championship to a resume that's already remarkable.
