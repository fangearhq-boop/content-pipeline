Nebraska's Jordy Bahl Enters Final Season With Unfinished Business After 1-0 Super Regional Loss

By Jordan Reeves | October 5, 2026 | I Love Softball

There are some losses that a program carries. Nebraska softball knows exactly what that feels like. A 1-0 defeat to Tennessee in the Super Regional last May — one run, one game, one margin — cost the Cornhuskers a trip to the Women's College World Series. They finished the 2026 season at 43-15. They were that close.

Now Jordy Bahl is back for her final season, and Nebraska is spending its fall building a roster designed to make sure that this time, the margin goes the other way.

Bahl's Legacy and What It Means This Year

Jordy Bahl has been Nebraska's anchor. Her career at the program has coincided with a period of sustained success — the 43-15 mark in 2026 represents one of the best seasons in program history, and reaching the Super Regional was the result of a year-long performance that demanded respect from every conference and program in the country.

As a senior, Bahl carries a different weight. This is the year that determines legacy for pitchers like her: the ones who have built the program into a contender, who have posted elite numbers, and who now stand one good postseason away from cementing their place permanently in the record books.

The question is not whether Bahl will be excellent. It is whether the team around her will be excellent enough.

Portal Additions Signal Revelle's Intent

Head coach Rhonda Revelle did not sit still. Nebraska added two transfers this offseason to reinforce the roster: Hannah Coor, who came from Oklahoma, and Jesse Farrell, who arrived from UNLV. Both additions address specific needs that the coaching staff identified from the 2026 campaign.

Coor's transfer from Oklahoma is notable — moving from the program that has won the most national titles in recent history signals a deliberate choice to pursue something in Lincoln. Farrell brings a fresh skillset from the Mountain West. Together they represent Revelle's answer to the question that a 1-0 Super Regional loss always creates: what do we need to change?

The Tennessee Rematch Narrative

It is impossible to discuss Nebraska's 2027 season without acknowledging the Tennessee factor. The Lady Vols were the ones who delivered that 1-0 result. The Super Regional loss is the defining event that shapes how Nebraska will prepare, how Bahl will pitch, and how every player in red will approach 2027.

Whether these teams meet in the postseason again is out of Nebraska's control. What is in their control is the quality of their preparation — and fall ball, beginning now, is the first chapter of that response.

What's Next

Nebraska's fall ball schedule continues this month as the Cornhuskers work through their exhibition calendar with an eye toward roster evaluation. Revelle's staff will use these weeks to integrate Coor and Farrell alongside the returning group and begin to determine which combinations give the program the best chance at a deep postseason run in 2027.

Bahl will pitch. Nebraska will build. The Huskers have unfinished business — and they know it.
