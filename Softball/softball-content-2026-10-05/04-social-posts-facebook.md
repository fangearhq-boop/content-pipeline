### STORY 1: Oklahoma Battle Series Game 1 Takeaways

**Long-Form Post (9:00 AM ET)**

⚡ BACK TO BACK. Oklahoma softball showed up Saturday.

2,351 fans packed Love's Field for the first Battle Series scrimmage of the fall. That's a sold-out crowd for a non-scored scrimmage in early October — and what they saw was worth every minute.

Six pitchers each threw three innings. The Sooners put up four home runs. And in the seventh inning, Gabbie Garcia went deep — then Kendall Wells followed her over the fence on the very next at-bat. Back to back.

Patty Gasso said postgame that she was moved to tears by the crowd's energy. After a spring that ended early at the hands of Texas, Oklahoma is using fall ball as a reset — and the fans are clearly ready for the next chapter.

Battle Series Game 2 is Tuesday, October 7 at 6:30 PM CT at Love's Field. Tickets are $10.88 general admission. If you haven't seen this roster yet in person, Tuesday is your next chance. 🌪️

### STORY 3: Nebraska — Jordy Bahl's Final Season

**Long-Form Post (1:00 PM ET)**

🌽 Nebraska softball came this close to the Women's College World Series last spring.

One run. That's all that separated the Cornhuskers from a WCWS berth. They lost to Tennessee 1-0 in the Super Regional — a heartbreak of the highest order for a program that had worked all season to be in that position. They went 43-15 and it still wasn't enough.

Now, Jordy Bahl is back for her senior season. Her final year. And Rhonda Revelle isn't resting — Nebraska added Hannah Coor (who transferred from Oklahoma) and Jesse Farrell (from UNLV) to reload a roster that already knew what it felt like to nearly reach the summit.

The Huskers are going to be motivated in a way that few teams in 2027 will understand. They know what they're chasing. They came within a single run. That doesn't leave you. It stays with you in every fall ball rep, every October practice, every winter workout.

Nebraska's 2027 window is open. Fall ball is where it starts.

### STORY 4: Texas A&M Historic Collapse

**Long-Form Post (3:00 PM ET)**

🐄 Texas A&M softball is playing with something to prove this fall.

It was the most stunning Regional exit in Division I softball history. The Aggies entered the 2026 NCAA Tournament as the No. 1 overall seed — the best record in the entire country — and were eliminated at home in their own Regional by Liberty. It had never happened to a No. 1 overall seed before. Ever. Not once in the history of the sport.

That's what fall ball is about for Texas A&M. It's not just preparation. It's redemption.

On the other side of the SEC, Arkansas head coach Courtney Deifel's Razorbacks are building. The Hogs went 44-14 last spring and didn't stop there — they added Dakota Kennedy (Arizona transfer) and Tianna Bell (California transfer) to a roster that was already dangerous. Arkansas is one of the conference's most underrated programs heading into 2027, and Deifel has them quietly positioning for a deep run.

The SEC is loaded. Fall ball will tell us who's serious.

### STORY 5: Fall Ball Roundup

**Long-Form Post (8:00 PM ET)**

🥎 Fall ball is happening everywhere, and tonight it's time for a quick tour.

FLORIDA STATE: The Seminoles are the only program in the country returning two All-American pitchers — Ashtyn Danley and Jazzy Francik. They opened October 2 against Chipola before postponing Sunday's scheduled game against FAMU (no reschedule date announced). Next up: Troy on October 10. This team will be dangerous in 2027.

PENN STATE: The Nittany Lions launched a multi-team round-robin fall at home, hosting Bucknell, Seton Hill, and East Stroudsburg this past weekend with another midweek game vs. Lock Haven on October 7. The Big Ten is off and running.

UAB: A new era is underway in Birmingham. Megan Curry — hired July 27 as the Blazers' 7th head coach — brings a winning record from Samford (61 wins, SoCon championship appearance) and a fresh approach to a program that's ready for its next chapter. Fall ball opens October 16.

The season never really ends for these programs. October is when the 2027 Longhorns, Volunteers, and Huskers are made. 🥎
