Isa Torres Is a Texas Longhorn: How the 2026 National Player of the Year Changes the Three-Peat Picture

By Emily Rawlings | I Love Softball | August 22, 2026

Texas already won back-to-back national championships. Then they added the 2026 D1Softball National Player of the Year.

Isa Torres spent four years at Florida State building a decorated offensive career, culminating in the sport's highest individual honor in 2026. When she entered the transfer portal, she chose Texas. For a program that won its second straight Women's College World Series title, that kind of acquisition does not happen by accident — it happens because the Longhorns have established themselves as the destination for the best players in the sport.

What Torres Brings

Torres was the most complete offensive player in college softball in 2026 at Florida State. The D1Softball National Player of the Year award reflects production across every offensive dimension — average, power, and run production. At Texas, she joins a lineup built around returning contributors and a program that posted a 49-12 record in 2026 while winning the national title.

The question is not whether Torres can contribute — she absolutely can. The question is what role she plays on a team that already won without her. That is a remarkable thing to be able to ask about an incoming transfer. She will compete for a starting position and produce. In the process, she makes the Longhorns' lineup measurably more difficult to navigate than the one that beat every opponent in Rosemont.

The Supporting Cast

Torres is not the only significant off-season addition to Texas. Catcher Samantha Swan transferred in from Arizona State. Lefty pitcher Samantha Lincoln joined from Texas Tech. The recruiting class adds right-handed pitcher Madi Doty and several other prospects who were among the most decorated in their graduating class.

Anchoring the pitching staff is Teagan Kavan, who made history as the first 2x WCWS Most Outstanding Player before returning for her senior year. Kavan's presence means Texas goes into 2027 with an ace who has already done what no pitcher before her has done in the sport's postseason.

The coaching staff was also refreshed, with head coach Mike White adding replacements for departed assistants who have had an immediate impact in recruiting and player development. The program's infrastructure matches the roster talent.

The Historical Context

Only two programs in modern softball history have won three consecutive WCWS titles. UCLA did it from 1988 to 1990. Oklahoma did it from 2021 to 2023. No other program has accomplished it in the sport's modern era.

Texas is going for it in 2027. And they are doing so with the 2026 D1Softball National Player of the Year on the roster, alongside the first 2x WCWS MOP in history, fresh off a 49-12 season that ended with a second straight national championship.

History is against them by definition — only two programs have ever done this. But the roster they are building is as formidable as any Texas team has ever been. Isa Torres does not just help — she changes the ceiling.

What's Next

Texas softball fall camp is underway with 24 players and 11 new additions, including Torres, Swan, Lincoln, and the incoming recruiting class. The 2027 regular season is months away, but the Longhorns' bid for a three-peat — which would be only the third in college softball history — begins in fall workouts.
