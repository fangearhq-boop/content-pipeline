Series 3 Teams Are Set: AUSL Draft Complete, Week Three Opens Sunday

By Sarah Mitchell | I Love Softball | August 22, 2026

The AUSL All-Star Cup Series 3 draft is complete. Captains Kayla Kowalik, Keilani Ricketts, Danieca Coffey, and Devyn Netz assembled their rosters Friday at Parkway Bank Sports Complex in Rosemont, Illinois — and with teams finalized, the individual title race has entered its most consequential chapter.

Series 3 opens Sunday, August 24. Series 4, the finale, concludes August 30. Between now and then, eight days of competition will determine who becomes the 2026 AUSL All-Star Cup individual champion.

Where the Individual Standings Race Stands

Kayla Kowalik enters Series 3 in first place on the individual leaderboard. Her lead was built across the first two series through consistent production and a performance level that kept her ahead of a competitive field.

The closest challengers are Sierra Romero and Emma Lemley. Romero had the most dramatic Series 2 of anyone in the field — climbing +42 individual points to reach No. 9 on the overall standings. That kind of surge is exactly the type of performance that can reset a race. Lemley added +26 points in Series 2, maintaining her position as one of the contenders capable of putting pressure on Kowalik in the final week.

With the individual champion crowned after Series 4, both Romero and Lemley need strong individual statistical performances in Series 3 games while Kowalik has a below-average series. Both conditions have to be true simultaneously — which is the nature of the AUSL format and what makes it compelling to watch.

What the Draft Means for the Race

In the AUSL All-Star Cup, team assignment matters for individual points accumulation. Points flow from statistical performance and from team wins. A player who lands on a competitive team has more opportunities to generate the wins — and the wins generate points that compound over a series.

For Romero, who surged in Series 2, the draft outcome shapes whether she can repeat or exceed that performance in Series 3. Landing on a team capable of winning games gives her the runway she needs. For Lemley, the same logic applies.

Kowalik, as a captain, was on the other side of the equation — building a team designed to win while simultaneously being aware that which rivals she selected (and which she left for other captains to claim) carries standings implications. That dual-purpose decision-making is one of the most interesting strategic elements of the All-Star Cup format.

How to Watch Series 3

All AUSL All-Star Cup games are broadcast nationally on ESPN, MLB Network, and CBS Sports Network. Series 3 games begin Sunday, August 24, at Parkway Bank Sports Complex in Rosemont, Illinois.

The All-Star Cup features 45 of the AUSL's top players competing across four series over four weekends. Teams are redrafted before each series, meaning the rosters that take the field Sunday are entirely new — former teammates become rivals, and former rivals line up side-by-side.

For fans who have been tracking the individual standings race, every game from here forward is meaningful. Statistical lines, win totals, and individual contributions all feed into the final standings calculation that will crown a champion after Series 4 on August 30.
