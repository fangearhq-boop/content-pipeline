Jaysoni Beachum Enters the Transfer Portal: FSU's Rebuild Just Got Much Harder

By Jordan Reeves

July 30, 2026

Florida State's 2026 offseason just got significantly more complicated. Less than two weeks after Isa Torres — the D1Softball National Player of the Year — announced she was leaving for Texas, FSU has now lost another cornerstone piece. Jaysoni Beachum, the 2024 NFCA Freshman of the Year and 2026 Second Team All-American, has entered the transfer portal.

The numbers Beachum posted over three seasons in Tallahassee tell the story of how significant this departure is: a .375 batting average, 37 home runs, and 180 RBIs. She was one of the most productive outfielders in Florida State's recent history — a player programs would typically build their offense around for years.

The Scope of FSU's Portal Losses

Combined with Torres's exit, the scale of what Florida State is losing becomes staggering. The Torres departure alone accounted for 28% of FSU's runs, 35% of the program's home runs, and 29% of its hits from the 2026 season. Add Beachum's production to that calculation and head coach Lonni Alameda is looking at a reconstruction project — not just a roster refresh.

Alameda already faced criticism after a 2026 home regional loss to Stetson and UCF, a result that underscored program vulnerabilities even before the portal departures began. Now, heading into the 2027 season, FSU will need to identify new offensive pillars fast. The transfer portal closed June 22 for Division I, which limits the immediate options for rebuilding through the portal for this cycle.

Who Benefits from Beachum's Move

For programs looking to add immediate offensive firepower, Beachum is one of the most valuable names available. A Second Team All-American with a .375 career average and elite power numbers brings instant credibility to any lineup. She joins a portal cycle that also features Tori Edwards (LSU, .457 BA, 11 HR, .777 SLG in 2026) as another high-impact available name.

The September 1 recruiting window is 32 days away — that's when Division I coaches can make direct contact with prospective transfers for the 2027 cycle. Programs across the country are positioning themselves now. Power programs with remaining roster flexibility will be the frontrunners for Beachum's commitment.

The Bigger Picture

FSU's situation is not unique in the current environment. The Mountain West and Pac-12 restructuring is driving heavy portal movement across the sport, as 21 programs face conference realignment ahead of 2026–27. Roster reconstruction is a league-wide challenge — but few programs have lost as much offensive production in a single offseason as the Seminoles.

The 2027 season will be a pivotal test for Lonni Alameda's program identity. Can she rebuild around what remains and recruit at the level FSU's history demands? That answer will start taking shape this fall.

What's Next

Watch Beachum's recruitment closely in August and September as she settles on a destination. The programs that land her will be instant contenders in 2027. For FSU, the focus now turns to identifying the next wave of talent that can restore the offensive identity the portal has stripped away. The September 1 window opens in 32 days — that's when the recruiting picture starts to crystallize.
