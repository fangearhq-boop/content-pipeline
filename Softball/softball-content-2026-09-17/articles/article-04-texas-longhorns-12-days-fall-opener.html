12 Days to McCombs Field: Isa Torres, Hannah Kavan and Texas Softball's Three-Peat Chase Begins Sept. 29

By Emily Rawlings | I Love Softball | September 17, 2026

The back-to-back Women's College World Series champions host Murray State on September 29 — and the first public look at this roster carries the weight of history.

Twelve days separate Texas softball fans from the first public look at a team that could rewrite the record books. On September 29 at McCombs Field, the Longhorns host Murray State in the fall opener — 6 PM CT, free admission, and the unofficial start of a season that carries three-peat potential and the most anticipated roster in Austin in years.

The headline addition is Isa Torres, the 2026 D1Softball National Player of the Year who arrives from Florida State with career numbers that demand immediate attention: .530 batting average, 16 home runs, 56 RBI. She grew up 30 miles south of Austin in Georgetown, Texas — and came home in the most complete way possible.

Torres Meets Kavan

The pairing of Torres and Hannah Kavan gives Texas one of the most accomplished offensive combinations in college softball history. Kavan holds a distinction that no one else in the sport can claim: she is the only two-time Women's College World Series Most Outstanding Player in history. Back-to-back national championships, back-to-back MOP awards. For her senior season, Kavan steps into a lineup that is now more dangerous than it has ever been.

Torres and Kavan represent two entirely different offensive profiles — Torres with elite contact and run production, Kavan with the postseason pedigree and clutch performance track record that defines programs. Head coach Mike White, who earned the NFCA Coaching Staff of the Year award for the second consecutive season, now has both of them on the same roster.

The Three-Peat Context

Consecutive national championships are rare. Three consecutive championships are the exclusive territory of two programs in the sport's modern history. The UCLA Bruins won three straight from 1988 to 1990. The Oklahoma Sooners won three straight from 2021 to 2023. Those are the only two programs to accomplish it in the NCAA era.

Texas finished 53-12 in the 2026 season — back-to-back Women's College World Series champions with the talent base and coaching infrastructure to compete again in 2027. Whether that adds up to a third straight title depends on what White and his staff build in the coming months, but the conversation is legitimate and the roster construction supports it.

The Full Fall Schedule

Texas has a six-game fall schedule that gives fans multiple opportunities to see this roster before the 2027 regular season begins:

The opener is September 29 against Murray State at McCombs Field (6 PM CT, free admission). Texas State follows on October 13, then Temple on October 20 and McLennan on October 27. UTSA comes to McCombs on October 29 before the fall concludes on November 6 at Texas A&M's Davis Diamond (7 PM CT) — a road closer that figures to draw significant attention given the rivalry stakes.

Six games, multiple looks at the Torres-Kavan pairing, and a chance for White's staff to evaluate depth and rotation options before the 2027 campaign opens in earnest.

What the Sept. 29 Opener Means

Fall exhibition games rarely carry the weight that September 29 at McCombs will carry this year. This is the first time Texas fans see Torres in burnt orange. It is the first time they see what Kavan looks like in her final college season. It is the first window into whether this roster can sustain the standard the Longhorns have set over the last two seasons.

Free admission means the crowd will reflect genuine demand, not ticket prices. McCombs Field is one of college softball's premier venues, and a fall opener of this magnitude figures to draw a packed house. Twelve days away. Mark the calendar.

What's Next

The countdown to September 29 runs through 12 more days of fall camp preparation. Torres and the full Texas roster are practicing now, building chemistry and working through the details that White's staff will need to answer before Murray State arrives. The fall schedule extends through November 6 at Davis Diamond, giving fans and coaches alike a sustained evaluation window. The three-peat chase is not a headline yet — it becomes one on the first pitch of a real game. That first pitch is 12 days away.
