Class of 2028 Recruiting: Day 9 of the In-Person Window and the Commit Wave Rolls On

By Sarah Mitchell | I Love Softball | September 17, 2026

Nine days into the in-person contact window, programs across five conferences continue to add commitments daily — and the end is not yet in sight.

Nine days in, and the Class of 2028 in-person contact window shows no sign of slowing. When the NCAA opened the in-person period on September 9 at 7 AM ET, the softball recruiting world expected a wave. What arrived was closer to a sustained current — a daily flow of commitments across the SEC, ACC, Big 12, Pac-12 and beyond that has produced one of the earliest and most active recruiting cycles in recent memory.

With 1,226 Extra Innings Softball nominations making this the largest Class of 2028 field on record, the talent pool is deep enough to support the volume. Programs are not just moving fast — they are making choices among an unusually large and accomplished group of young players.

The Commit List at Day 9

The top two spots in the class are already decided. Finlee Williams, the No. 1 overall prospect, committed to Oklahoma. Peyton Ushery, No. 2 in the Line Drive Media rankings, is headed to Georgia. Below them, a significant second tier has taken shape.

Paityn Donalson, a two-way player with a .693 batting average, 11 home runs and 50 RBI in her prep career, committed to Florida State. Braylyn Saenz added another OU commitment at No. 41 in the class. Star Gutierrez became Texas Tech's first Class of 2028 commit, choosing the outfield spot in Lubbock. Sophia Galindo and Nevaeh Wilson joined Tennessee — bringing U-18 Women's National Team experience to the Volunteers. Kate Losh, a pitcher with two-way ability, and catcher Ryann Harris both chose Florida. Siena Myers (catcher) committed to South Carolina. And Arizona added an in-state tandem with Ridling and Stell.

That is more than a dozen commitments from programs with national title aspirations, and Day 9 is not the end of this window — not by a long stretch.

What's Still Out There

The in-person window extends through mid-October, giving programs several more weeks of active evaluation and recruitment. The first class rankings update from the major outlets is expected mid-October, which will provide the first structured look at how the Class of 2028 is shaping up relative to itself and to historical classes.

Several high-profile uncommitted prospects remain — names like Michaela Landers, Kylie Holden and Sophia Schlader, all five-star caliber players who have received attention from virtually every top program. When those decisions come, they will define the top of the class and reshape the recruiting landscape for the programs involved.

What This Class Means

The size and pace of the Class of 2028 reflects structural changes in how programs recruit — earlier evaluation, faster relationship-building and a compressed timeline from first contact to commitment. The 1,226 EIS nominations record is not just a number; it is a measure of how widely coaches cast their nets and how seriously the pipeline is being developed nationwide.

For the programs adding commits right now, the decisions made in September and October will shape their rosters for 2030 and beyond. The window is open, the talent is there, and the commit wave is still rolling.

What's Next

The Class of 2028 in-person contact window continues daily through mid-October. Programs are active every day, and commitments are arriving at a steady pace. The next major milestone is the first class rankings update in mid-October, which will provide the first structured comparison of this class against national standards. For fans tracking the pipeline, the next few weeks are among the most important of the recruiting calendar. Keep watching.
