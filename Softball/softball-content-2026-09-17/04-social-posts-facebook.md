### STORY 1: USA WNT — Group Oklahoma Recap

**Long-Form Post**
USA SOFTBALL IS GOING TO AUSTRALIA. 🇺🇸🥎

Five days. Five wins. And on Wednesday afternoon at Devon Park in Oklahoma City, Team USA punched their ticket to the 2027 WBSC Women's Softball World Cup Finals in Redcliffe, Queensland with a dominant 7-0 run-rule victory over China.

The hero of the day was Maya Brady, who hit THREE home runs and drove in five runs. The decisive blow came in the sixth inning, when Brady launched a 276-foot two-run shot to deep right-center field that triggered the run-ahead rule and ended the game six innings early.

On the mound, Gabbie Garcia was simply untouchable. Garcia retired all nine batters she faced through the sixth inning, striking out six. USA's defense and pitching was airtight throughout Group Oklahoma — they went unbeaten from Day 1 through the bracket.

Head coach Patty Gasso and her USA staff (including JT Gasso and Rocha) are back in Norman today, returning after the tournament concluded Wednesday evening. And what a homecoming it is: TODAY is the first full-staff practice for Oklahoma softball since Gasso departed for Team USA duties on Sept. 12.

The 2027 Finals in Redcliffe will serve as a key qualifier on the road to the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics. USA has earned their place at that table. Group Oklahoma is done. Redcliffe is next.

### STORY 4: Texas Longhorns — 12 Days to Sept. 29

**Long-Form Post**
Twelve days. That's all that stands between college softball fans and the first public look at Isa Torres in burnt orange.

Texas (53-12, back-to-back WCWS champions) hosts Murray State at McCombs Field on Sept. 29 — 6 PM CT, free admission, and what figures to be one of the most watched fall exhibition games in program history. Torres, who won the 2026 D1Softball National Player of the Year award at FSU (.530 BA, 16 HR, 56 RBI) and grew up 30 miles south of Austin in Georgetown, TX, has come home in the best way possible.

She'll be paired with Hannah Kavan, the only two-time Women's College World Series Most Outstanding Player in history, for a senior season that has three-peat potential. That's rare company: only the UCLA Bruins (1988-90) and the Oklahoma Sooners (2021-23) have ever won three consecutive national championships.

Head coach Mike White, who won the NFCA Coaching Staff of the Year award for the second consecutive season, has a full fall schedule lined up: Murray State (Sept. 29), Texas State (Oct. 13), Temple (Oct. 20), McLennan (Oct. 27), UTSA (Oct. 29), and the final at Texas A&M's Davis Diamond on Nov. 6. 

The countdown is on. 12 days. Free admission. McCombs Field. You won't want to miss it. #HookEm

### STORY 5: Texas Tech Red Raiders — Glasco Year 2

**Long-Form Post**
Seven All-Americans. One fall camp. Week 2 of Tim Glasco's second year at Texas Tech.

For the first time in program history, all seven of Tech's All-American-caliber players are practicing together under the same coaching staff. Nicole Beachum (.406 BA, 10 HR, 69 RBI, All-ACC 1st Team from FSU), Jayda Pickering (No. 2 transfer portal player, .384 BA, 50 HR from Oklahoma), and Haley Heavener (LHP, 13-9, 2.81 ERA, 128 K from LSU) headline a group that also includes Terry, Williams, Pannell, Polar, and Burns.

The Red Raiders were WCWS runner-up in 2026, falling to Texas in the championship series. With a coaching staff in Year 2 and a roster that has now had a full offseason to absorb Glasco's system, expectations are high entering the 2027 season.

The chemistry being built this fall — combining three distinct program cultures from FSU, Oklahoma and LSU — is what TTU is betting its national title run on.
