The Calls Start Today: Inside NCAA Softball's 2028 Recruiting Contact Window

By Emily Rawlings | October 17, 2026 | I Love Softball

Somewhere right now, a head coach is picking up the phone and calling a 15-year-old who has dreamed about this moment for years. That's what October 17 means in college softball — the opening of the 2028 direct contact window, the 48-hour sprint that shapes programs for years.

Starting today and running through Sunday, Division I coaches can call class-of-2028 prospects directly for the first time. No more indirect communication, no more recruiting questionnaires and indirect signals. Just a coach, a player, and a direct conversation about what the next chapter of a career could look like.

The Programs Already Ahead

Several programs enter this weekend with significant momentum in the 2028 class. Tennessee holds the No. 4 nationally ranked class, having already secured a commitment from Reagan Eldred and building around a program that has established itself as a perennial WCWS contender under Karen Weekly.

Oklahoma brings five commits into the weekend, including pitcher Sophia Schlader. The Sooners' track record speaks for itself — reigning national champions with a roster that recruits understand is a pipeline to the highest levels of the sport.

Florida has already secured Kylie Holden, the No. 2-ranked 2028 prospect nationally, a versatile multi-infield player with tools that project well at the college level. The Gators also have Evans committed and enter the contact window with Tim Walton's staff ready to push for more.

Texas has the No. 1-ranked 2028 prospect in the country — Michaela Landers, per Softball America — already committed to the Longhorns program. Florida State landed twins Keira and Kelsey Luderer in the 2028 class, adding depth to a program that has been consistently competitive in national recruiting.

What the Contact Window Means

Before the contact window, NCAA recruiting rules limit how coaches can communicate with prospects. Questionnaires, camp evaluations, and indirect communication channels dominate the relationship-building phase. The contact window changes that — suddenly, the conversations are real.

For uncommitted top-50 prospects, the next 48 hours are both exciting and overwhelming. Multiple programs will be calling. Coaches will be making their best cases. The difference between a program that wins a commitment and one that doesn't often comes down to relationships built over the preceding months — and now those relationships get a direct test.

The players being called this weekend are freshmen and sophomores in high school who won't set foot on a college campus as enrolled students for another three or four years. But the decisions made this weekend will have real consequences for every program involved. A commitment from the No. 1 national prospect is a recruiting class-defining moment. A missed call is an opportunity that doesn't come back.

What's Next

Watch for commitment announcements over this weekend and into next week. The 48-hour window opens conversations that don't always close quickly — a player who receives a call today might commit tomorrow, or might take months to decide. But the contact period is when the momentum shifts, and the programs that come out of this weekend with new commitments will have tangible advantages in shaping the class of 2028.

The class won't matriculate until the fall of 2028, but the work happening this weekend will shape what Tennessee, Oklahoma, Florida, Texas, and every other program in the country looks like two years from now. The calls start today. The future of college softball is being decided right now.
