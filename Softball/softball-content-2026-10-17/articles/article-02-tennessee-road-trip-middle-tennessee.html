Lady Vols Hit the Road: Tennessee's Only Fall Trip Tests a 3-0 Squad at Middle Tennessee

By Jordan Reeves | October 17, 2026 | I Love Softball

Tennessee softball is 3-0 in fall ball, and the wins have been convincing. The 19-1 run-rule victory over Carson-Newman was the kind of statement game that gets made when talent meets preparation. But Tennessee hasn't left Knoxville yet this fall. Saturday, they do.

The Lady Vols make their only fall road trip Saturday when they travel to Murfreesboro for a 3 PM ET game at Middle Tennessee — the one away-from-home test on the fall schedule before the program turns its full attention toward February. It's a game that sounds routine. It probably isn't.

Why the Road Game Matters

Fall ball serves a different purpose than regular-season games. Coaches use it to evaluate depth, test younger players in live situations, and work on the pieces of the roster that don't get proven until competition arrives. Home games against familiar regional opponents answer some questions. A road game asks different ones.

How does the lineup perform outside of Lindsey Nelson Stadium? How do the pitchers settle in on an opponent's field? Does the defense stay sharp when the routine changes? These are the kinds of questions that a trip to Murfreesboro can answer that a game in Knoxville simply cannot.

Head coach Karen Weekly has said this is the deepest pitching staff she has assembled in her career at Tennessee — a program she has led since 1999. That's a statement worth examining under road conditions, not just at home. Karlyn Pickens and Sage Mardjetko anchor a rotation that few programs in the country can match at any level, but the question of depth matters most in March and April, when the schedule gets harder and the staff gets leaned on.

The Staff That Makes Tennessee a Contender

Pickens enters the game as one of the most decorated active players in the sport. Four career no-hitters. Two First Team All-SEC selections. The No. 1 overall pick in the 2026 Athletes Unlimited Softball League draft by the Carolina Blaze. She has one more season in Knoxville to add to a legacy that is already extraordinary.

Mardjetko is the counterpart who makes the rotation elite rather than just good. A 16-3 record, 1.26 ERA, and 171 strikeouts last season — numbers that earned her NFCA First Team All-American honors. In a lot of programs, Mardjetko is the ace. At Tennessee, she's the No. 2 starter, and that depth is exactly what Weekly is talking about.

Behind them, Erin Nuwer, Kailey Plumlee, transfer Peyton Hardenburger, and transfer Maddi Rutan give the Lady Vols options that extend well into any game or any week of the season. The road game Saturday will give Weekly another data point on how those pieces fit together outside of home conditions.

What's Next

After Middle Tennessee on Saturday, Tennessee returns home for the rescheduled Roane State game on October 21 (6 PM), then Lee University on October 23. The fall schedule wraps up before the program heads into the winter prep period ahead of the 2027 season opener in February.

The Lady Vols are building toward something real. The pitching staff is historically deep. The returning lineup includes All-SEC outfielder Alannah Leach. The 2028 recruiting class is already ranked No. 4 nationally. Saturday's road trip is one more piece of the foundation. By the time the season begins, Tennessee expects to be one of the teams everyone else is trying to beat.
