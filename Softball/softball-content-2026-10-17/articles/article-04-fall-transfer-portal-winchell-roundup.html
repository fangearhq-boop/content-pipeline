Winchell to LSU, Heavener to Texas Tech: Ranking the Fall Portal's Biggest Moves

By Emily Rawlings | October 17, 2026 | I Love Softball

The fall transfer portal window has reshaped multiple rosters across the SEC and Big 12, and the moves being made now will echo through the 2027 season. Over 137 entries have hit the portal this fall, and while not every name will land in a high-profile destination, the headliners have already made significant impacts.

The biggest name on the board is already off it. Mickey Winchell — a Notre Dame outfielder who hit .335 with 14 stolen bases in her Fighting Irish career — is headed to Baton Rouge. The LSU commitment changes the Bayou Tigers' outfield picture and adds a dimension the program has been looking for: a contact bat with elite speed who can run the bases and cover ground in the outfield.

Winchell's Value to LSU

A .335 batting average at Notre Dame, combined with 14 stolen bases, tells a complete story about what Winchell brings to a lineup. She's not a power bat — she's the kind of player who puts the ball in play consistently, gives the team speed at the top of the order, and creates problems on the bases that force defensive decisions.

LSU enters 2027 with a nucleus that can compete in the SEC, and Winchell addresses one of the areas where the program needed help: outfield depth with a legitimate contact presence. The combination of her bat and her legs gives the Tigers a multi-dimensional threat that changes how opposing pitchers have to approach the top of the order.

The Big 12 Reshapes Itself

Texas Tech's fall portal haul deserves significant attention. Under new coaches Cottrill and Pendley — Cottrill brought a 41-7 record in Big 12 play from his previous program — the Red Raiders built what is being rated as the No. 1 fall portal class in the country. Heavener's move to Lubbock is a key piece of that class. The Texas Tech rebuild is happening faster than expected.

Oklahoma's additions of Harter, Hansen, and Gregus continue the Sooners' pattern of using the portal to maintain depth behind one of the most talented rosters in the sport. Oklahoma doesn't need the portal to compete — they just entered the fall as reigning national champions — but the program understands that the margin between good and great gets maintained through exactly this kind of depth building.

The Wildcard: Kasidi Pickering

The most unresolved name on the portal board this fall is Kasidi Pickering. The high-end player entered the portal following the WCWS and has been connected to multiple programs, including OU and Texas Tech. Where Pickering lands will be one of the more consequential portal decisions of the fall — the kind of move that reshapes a program's ceiling immediately.

The other notable move in the SEC was Kailey Plumlee — a left-handed pitcher from Tennessee — making her way to Alabama. The Crimson Tide program added a capable arm to a pitching staff that is looking to take a step forward in 2027. Tennessee, which has extraordinary pitching depth with Pickens and Mardjetko, could absorb the departure. Alabama gained meaningful depth.

What's Next

The fall portal window remains open, and more moves are expected before programs settle their rosters for the winter. The names that haven't found landing spots yet — including Pickering — will likely resolve in the coming weeks as coaches finish evaluating their fall rosters and identify the specific needs the transfer class can address.

The programs that manage the portal most effectively tend to win in April. LSU's Winchell addition, Texas Tech's class, and Oklahoma's depth moves all represent programs doing the work now that pays off in the spring. The 137-plus portal entries this fall haven't all found homes yet. The next few weeks will tell us who the real winners are.
