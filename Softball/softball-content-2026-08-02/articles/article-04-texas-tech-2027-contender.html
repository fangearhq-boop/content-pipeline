Texas Tech Went to the WCWS Finals. Now They Have Jaysoni Beachum.

By Sarah Mitchell

August 2, 2026

Before you contextualize Jaysoni Beachum's commitment to Texas Tech as a program-on-the-rise story, consider what the Lady Raiders just did in Oklahoma City: they went to the 2026 Women's College World Series finals. That is not a program ascending toward the conversation. That is a program already in it — competing on the sport's biggest stage, in the bracket's final game, with everything on the line.

Now that program is adding one of the best players available in the transfer portal. The combination of what Texas Tech accomplished in 2026 and what Beachum brings heading into 2027 is a formula that should have every program in the country paying attention.

What Beachum Brings

Jaysoni Beachum's 2026 season at Florida State: .406 batting average, 10 home runs, 69 RBI. Those numbers came in a year when FSU's program was navigating significant transition — and Beachum still put up those stats. That is not a player putting up numbers in a favorable environment. That is a player being the environment.

The credentials span multiple seasons. She was the 2024 NFCA National Freshman of the Year — the ACC Freshman of the Year, the Softball America Freshman of the Year. In 2026, she earned Second-Team All-American honors. She has been one of the most impactful players in the country since she arrived at FSU, and she leaves as one of the program's most productive hitters of the modern era.

Texas Tech gets a player who can hit for average, for power, and who drives in runs at an elite rate. In the transfer portal era, that combination does not wait long on the market. The Lady Raiders moved fast, and it shows in their result.

The No. 1 Transfer Class

Beachum was not the only major portal addition for Texas Tech this cycle. The Lady Raiders also added Pickering from Oklahoma, giving them a two-player haul that national transfer class rankings have rated No. 1 in the country.

The broader portal context: 135 players entered the 2026-27 softball transfer portal, and 87 percent of them have already committed to new programs. The market is nearly closed. Texas Tech navigated it better than anyone.

For Florida State, the departure of Beachum — along with Isa Torres earlier in the cycle — represents a substantial reshaping of the program's roster. FSU's rebuild under head coach Lonni Alameda is in full force. Texas Tech's gain is a direct reflection of what FSU lost in a single portal window.

The 2027 Case

Programs that reach the WCWS finals and then add the No. 1 transfer class nationally tend to arrive in the preseason ranked in the top five. That is the reasonable expectation for Texas Tech heading into 2027. The Lady Raiders have the experience of competing in Oklahoma City in the finals, the returning core that got them there, and now the portal reinforcements that could push them over the top.

The September 1 recruiting window — when college coaches can begin contacting Class of 2028 prospects — is 30 days away. The Lady Raiders will be recruiting from a position of strength when those calls go out. They just went to the WCWS finals. They just landed the No. 1 transfer class. That is a pitch that sells itself.

What's Next

Texas Tech heads into fall practice as one of the programs to watch for the 2027 season. Preseason polls will position them near the top of the national rankings. For the transfer portal picture across the sport, the September 1 window is the next major milestone — watch for early signing period activity in November that will begin to shape the 2027 class of freshmen joining programs like Texas Tech's loaded returning group.
