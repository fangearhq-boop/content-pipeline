The Little League Softball World Series Begins Today in Greenville

By Emily Rawlings

August 2, 2026

After last night's opening ceremony at Stallings Stadium — the parade of champions, the first pitches by AUSL stars Amanda Lorenz and Kinzie Hansen-McKinzie, the music and the crowd — the waiting is over. The 2026 Little League Softball World Series begins today with four games on ESPN2, all free to the public.

Twelve teams have earned the right to be in Greenville, North Carolina. Eight U.S. regional champions and four international champions — and every one of them arrived here by beating a bracket full of teams that wanted to be here just as badly. Today is the first day those players get to find out what happens when the best meet the best at Stallings Stadium at Elm Street Park.

The Bracket Structure

The 2026 LLSWS uses a double-elimination format with two six-team brackets: Purple and Orange. Every team gets at least two chances before elimination — but only the bracket champions advance to the championship round. With twelve teams and double-elimination, the margin for error is real but not wide.

For the first time in tournament history, all 22 LLSWS games air on ESPN family channels — primarily ESPN2. No cable blackouts, no streaming-only games buried behind a paywall. Every at-bat of this tournament is on television, which means every player on every team gets to show what they can do in front of a national audience.

Today's Schedule

Four games open the tournament today at Stallings Stadium. All four are on ESPN2. All four are free admission for fans in Greenville.

  • 10 AM ET — Purple bracket: Northwest (Snohomish, WA) vs. Southeast (Lake Mary, FL)
  • 1 PM ET — Orange bracket: Mid-Atlantic (Toms River, NJ) vs. Asia-Pacific (Kinki Softball LL, Japan)
  • 4 PM ET — Orange bracket: West (Live Oak, CA) vs. Central (Daviess County, KY)
  • 7 PM ET — Purple bracket: Canada (OnField LL, Repentigny, QC) vs. North Carolina (Johnston County LL, Clayton, NC)

The morning game between Northwest and Southeast opens with two regional powers. The Pacific Northwest region out of Snohomish, Washington brings strong youth softball tradition, while Lake Mary, Florida represents the Southeast with programs that have consistently produced championship-caliber squads. The 1 PM matchup between the Mid-Atlantic's Toms River, New Jersey entry and the Asia-Pacific team from Kinki Softball Little League in Japan is one of the most intriguing of the day — international competition at the LLSWS has grown in quality each year.

The night cap — Canada versus North Carolina — gives the home crowd a late reason to stay. Johnston County Little League out of Clayton, North Carolina enters as one of the regional favorites, and playing in front of a home-state crowd at 7 PM ET on ESPN2 is a significant advantage. Canada, represented by OnField LL out of Repentigny, Quebec, is the wild card — the Canadian program has been a regular LLSWS qualifier, and they travel well.

What to Watch For

Double-elimination means every game counts, but the first round has a specific rhythm: teams entering 0-0 with no scouting, no film, nothing but their preparation and what happens in real time on the field. The teams that handle first-game nerves and adjust quickly tend to build momentum through the bracket. The teams that don't can find themselves in the losers' bracket after just one game.

With all 22 games on ESPN, there is also the element of visibility. Players in the LLSWS range in age but are at a stage where standout performances in a nationally televised tournament create lifelong memories. For softball families across the country, the LLSWS is as much about that — the joy of competition at the highest level — as it is about the championship.

What's Next

The tournament runs August 2 through August 9 at Stallings Stadium in Greenville, NC. Games continue daily on ESPN2 with free admission throughout. The bracket will take shape quickly in the first two days — by Tuesday, the early contenders will be clear.
