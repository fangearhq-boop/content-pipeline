Bre Warren Ends Her Virginia Tech Eligibility Fight — What It Means for 850 Athletes Still in the Battle

By Jordan Reeves | I Love Softball | November 23, 2026

Bre Warren's eligibility fight is over. The Virginia Tech outfielder and South Carolina graduate transfer will not seek a permanent injunction against the NCAA, according to Ground News. Warren's state court request for a preliminary injunction was denied, and she has decided to end her pursuit of a sixth year of collegiate softball.

She was on the Hokies' roster this season. She just never got to play.

Warren's case was one of the most visible individual eligibility fights of a year defined by them. She sued the NCAA in Montgomery County Circuit Court after the governing body denied her waiver request for an extra year of competition. Her case was filed, argued, and ultimately lost — the court said no, and Warren is stepping back.

The Case in Context

Warren's fight unfolded in the shadow of a larger legal landscape. The Tenth Circuit Court of Appeals reversed the federal five-year rule injunction in the Wisne v. NCAA case in early November — a ruling that closed the most effective federal pathway for the class of 2022. That ruling sent individual athletes scrambling to state courts, which had been granting relief at a higher rate than federal courts in eligibility disputes.

Warren pursued that state court path. It did not produce the outcome she was fighting for. The court denied the preliminary injunction — the first and most critical legal threshold — and she chose not to escalate further. That decision is both practical and deeply personal. The process of fighting a legal battle against the NCAA while also serving on a roster, managing coursework, and waiting for decisions is exhausting in ways that outsiders rarely account for.

What Her Case Illustrated

Warren's eligibility dispute highlighted the gap between what the NCAA's rules allow and what individual athletes experience as fair. She completed her undergraduate degree at South Carolina, transferred to Virginia Tech in pursuit of graduate study and an additional year of competition, and was denied the waiver that would have made that last season possible.

Her case also illustrated the asymmetry of the state court strategy. While state courts have granted relief at higher rates than federal courts, those rulings are inconsistent — dependent on jurisdiction, judge, and the specific facts of each case. What works in one state may not work in another. Warren's jurisdiction did not produce the result her case needed.

850 Athletes Still Fighting

Warren's decision to end her fight is her choice, and it deserves to be treated as such. But the broader legal context continues. More than 200 cases are still active in state courts, involving more than 850 athletes. These are largely class of 2022 players — athletes who lost a year of eligibility during the COVID-19 disruption and have been fighting ever since to recover it.

The NCAA is pursuing Congressional legislation that would preempt state court cases and establish a uniform federal standard for eligibility disputes. That legislative effort is ongoing. LSU athletes Paytn Monticelli and Jalia Lassiter are among a 40-plus athlete group that has continued pursuing relief through the courts. For those athletes, Warren's case ending is one data point, not a signal to stop.

What's Next

Warren's case is closed. The larger fight over NCAA eligibility rules is not. Congressional action remains the most viable path to a consistent resolution — one that doesn't require each athlete to file individually, navigate local court systems, and fight the NCAA with their own resources. Until that legislation moves or fails, the state court strategy will continue, case by case. Warren chose to step back from her piece of it. For the 850 athletes still in the fight, the December portal opening in eight days represents the most immediate practical relief valve — moving to a new program and competing isn't the same as winning the legal argument, but for athletes who can use that portal, it's the option available right now.
