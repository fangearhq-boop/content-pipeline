Tennessee's Last Public Fall Event: Free Intersquad Oct. 29 Features the SEC's Deepest Pitching Staff

By Sarah Mitchell | October 25, 2026 | I Love Softball

If you want to see the Tennessee Lady Vols before the 2027 season, this Thursday is your last chance. The program's final public fall event is an intersquad scrimmage on October 29 at 5 PM ET at Sherri Parker Lee Stadium in Knoxville — free admission for anyone who wants to attend.

It's a low-key event on paper. In practice, it's the last live look at what may be the deepest pitching staff in college softball before the offseason closes.

What Weekly Said After the Finale

Following Tennessee's 14-1 fall ball finale win over Lee University on Oct. 23, head coach Karen Weekly gave an honest assessment: the competition level this fall hasn't been where she'd prefer it. It's a frank comment that tells you something important about how the program operates.

Tennessee won 14-1 and Weekly still felt the schedule didn't challenge the roster the way she wanted. That's not a complaint — it's a coaching standard. When you run a program that competes for national championships on an annual basis, a 14-1 win over a lower-level opponent doesn't give you the diagnostic information you need. You need competitive at-bats. You need your pitchers facing line-ups that can actually hurt them.

The public intersquad on Oct. 29 gives the staff a chance to simulate that competition internally. It also gives fans one more look before the long break until February.

The Pitching Trident

The reason Tennessee's staff generates as much attention as it does comes down to three names: Karlyn Pickens, Sage Mardjetko, and Bailee Shelton.

Pickens needs no introduction. The No. 1 overall pick in the AUSL Draft posted 194 strikeouts with a 1.70 ERA in 2026. She is one of the most dominant pitchers in college softball history at this stage of her career, and she returns for 2027 with experience, maturity, and velocity that has only grown since she arrived in Knoxville.

Mardjetko is the wins machine. Her 2026 line — 16-3, 1.26 ERA — speaks for itself. She is the kind of back-end starter who wins the games your ace doesn't pitch, which is how championship programs are built. You can't win a Women's College World Series with one great pitcher. You need two. Tennessee has two.

Then there's Shelton. In her first fall finale appearance against Lee University, the freshman struck out 7 batters in 3.0 innings of work, allowing just one hit. Her hard-hit rate allowed was the best on the Tennessee staff in fall ball — over 50 percent of batted balls off her came back soft. For a freshman seeing live action for the first time at this level, those are not numbers you manufacture. That's real.

Three pitchers. Three different profiles. Combined, they represent a rotation that no offense in the country will find comfortable to face over a three-game series.

Why This Intersquad Matters

Internal intersquads give coaches what fall games against lower-level competition can't: the combination of familiarity (these hitters know these pitchers) and quality (these pitchers are the best on the roster). The hitters know what's coming. The pitchers know the hitters' tendencies. The competitive level within one of the country's best rosters mirrors the kind of pressure you face in March and May better than a 14-1 win over a Division II program.

For fans attending, the intersquad offers a chance to see the Vols work in a relaxed but genuinely competitive environment — the pitchers will be throwing to win, and the hitters will be trying to square them up. That's more informative than most fall games.

What's Next

The free public intersquad is Thursday, October 29 at 5 PM ET at Sherri Parker Lee Stadium in Knoxville. After that, Tennessee goes into winter mode until preseason opens ahead of the 2027 campaign.

With Pickens, Mardjetko, and Shelton all returning, the Volunteers will enter next season as one of the programs opponents least want to face come tournament time. Thursday's intersquad is the last chance to see it before it begins in earnest.
