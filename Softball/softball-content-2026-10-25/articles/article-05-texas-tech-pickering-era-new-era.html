The Pickering Era Begins — Texas Tech's Fall Debut Shows a Program Ready to Compete

By Sarah Mitchell | October 25, 2026 | I Love Softball

Texas Tech closed their fall softball schedule this weekend, and what new head coach Kasey Cottrill saw over those games was the beginning of something the Red Raiders haven't had before: a roster capable of competing at the highest level in college softball.

The centerpiece of this transformation is Kasidi Pickering — the most decorated transfer in Texas Tech softball history. In three seasons at Oklahoma, Pickering posted a .384 batting average, hit 50 home runs, and drove in 169 runs. She signed with the Red Raiders in June 2026, becoming the anchor around which Cottrill is building everything else.

The Portal Class That Changed Everything

Pickering isn't the only piece. Texas Tech assembled what on3.com and others have ranked as the No. 1 transfer portal class in program history. The class includes Beachum (an FSU transfer who brings high-level experience from a program that went 52-1 in fall ball), along with Heavener, Polar, and Gallaway — each of whom arrives with significant collegiate experience from programs that compete nationally.

Collectively, this group changes the talent ceiling at Texas Tech. Previous Red Raiders rosters have been competitive at the conference level, but the WCWS has been out of reach. With this class surrounding an offensive centerpiece in Pickering and a returning foundation that already includes program home run record-holder Jaelynn Williams (27 HRs in 2026), the ceiling has moved.

Cottrill and Pendley: The Coaching Reset

The hiring of Kasey Cottrill as head coach, with Jacy Pendley joining as associate head coach, was the event that set this roster construction in motion. Coaching hires either attract portal talent or they don't — and the No. 1 portal class in program history is the clearest possible signal that Cottrill earned the trust of players who had options.

Cottrill and Pendley both have experience in high-level college softball environments, and the fall ball period has given them their first full evaluation of the roster in competitive action. By all accounts, the integration has gone smoothly — getting players from different programs to buy into a shared identity and system is the hardest part of a portal-heavy rebuild, and the fact that all five key transfers are still in Lubbock heading into winter is meaningful.

Williams Is Still Here Too

In the excitement over the portal class, it's easy to overlook the player who set the program home run record last season. Jaelynn Williams hit 27 home runs in 2026 — a Texas Tech single-season record — and returns for 2027. Williams and Pickering in the same lineup, behind a rotation that has added depth from the portal, gives the Red Raiders something opponents haven't had to prepare for when scouting a Texas Tech team.

Big 12 play opens March 12. The conference includes Oklahoma, Texas, Florida, and Oklahoma State — a gauntlet that will immediately test whether this roster translates from fall potential to spring performance.

What's Next

With fall ball complete, Texas Tech enters winter training with a clear identity: Cottrill and Pendley are the standard-setters, Pickering is the offensive anchor, Williams is the record-holder, and the portal class provides the depth needed to compete deep into May.

The 2027 Red Raiders are not a long-term project. They are built to contend now. Big 12 play opens March 12 — and the West Texas contingent is planning to make noise.
