25 Days Away: Texas Longhorns in the National Spotlight as Fall Camp Builds

By Sarah Mitchell

September 4, 2026

Twenty-five days until Texas opens fall ball at Red & Charline McCombs Field — and the national media is already paying attention. SI this week named the Longhorns one of four college softball programs to watch this fall, citing the combination of returning talent and championship expectations. Yahoo Sports published a profile on pitcher Teagan Kavan, calling her simply "unbeatable."

The headlines are earned. Texas is the two-time defending national champion, pursuing something only two programs in the history of the sport have accomplished: three consecutive national titles. UCLA did it from 1988 to 1990. Oklahoma did it from 2021 to 2023. Texas enters the 2027 season with the roster to make history.

Kavan's Senior Season

Teagan Kavan enters her senior season as the only player in Women's College World Series history to win the Most Outstanding Player award in back-to-back years. The Yahoo Sports feature traced her rise through the WCWS, detailing the numbers — 4-1 in starts, two saves, 33⅓ innings pitched, just 16 hits and 7 runs allowed across both championship runs combined — and the mentality that drives her. Head coach Mike White has described her as the kind of player who simply rises when the moment gets biggest.

Katie Stewart also returns for her senior campaign, providing the lineup with another veteran anchor. And transfer Isa Torres — the 2026 D1Softball National Player of the Year from Florida State — is now on campus in Austin, adding an elite bat to a roster that already knew how to win.

The Fall Schedule

Texas opens its fall slate Sept. 29 against Murray State at McCombs Field — free admission, 6 PM Central. The fall schedule closes Nov. 6 at Texas A&M. White has indicated he will continue monitoring the transfer portal, though with six of nine starters returning and Torres now in the mix, the roster has the foundation it needs.

SI's "4 Programs to Watch This Fall" framing puts Texas in company with Nebraska, Oklahoma State, and Duke — programs that media and coaches are identifying as the ones to track as fall camp results start filtering through. For Texas, the question isn't whether they'll be good. It's whether they can be historically great.

What's Next

Texas opens fall ball Sept. 29 against Murray State at Red & Charline McCombs Field (6 PM CT, free admission). The fall schedule closes Nov. 6 at Texas A&M. Follow the Longhorns through fall camp as Kavan, Stewart, Torres, and the returning core begin building the team that will chase the program's third consecutive national championship in 2027.
