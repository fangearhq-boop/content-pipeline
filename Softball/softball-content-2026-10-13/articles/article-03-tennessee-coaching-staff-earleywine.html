Tennessee's Coaching Staff Takes Shape: Earleywine Brings Championship Blueprint from Texas

By Sarah Mitchell | October 13, 2026 | I Love Softball

When Craig Snider and Stephanie Sanders departed Tennessee softball this past spring to become co-head coaches at Auburn, it left Karen Weekly with a significant hole in her staff going into one of the most important offseasons in Lady Vols history.

Weekly's answer came in June: she hired Ehren Earleywine as associate head coach and hitting coach.

It was not a placeholder hire. Earleywine arrives in Knoxville with more than two decades of coaching experience, a 14-year stretch as a head coach, and most recently a tenure as associate head coach at Texas — where he helped build the offensive approach that powered the 2026 NCAA champion Longhorns. Earleywine has been inside a championship program while it was winning. That is not something you can teach from a playbook.

What the Hire Means

Tennessee returns nine starters for the 2027 season, including senior infielder Karley Shelton, who transferred from South Carolina. The talent is already in place. What Weekly needed was someone who could maximize it — a hitting coach who could develop an offensive identity capable of competing with the sport's elite programs deep into the postseason.

Earleywine fits that profile. His Texas tenure coincided with the Longhorns assembling the roster that won a national title. He understands what championship-level preparation looks like, what the difference is between a good offensive day in February and a winning offensive approach in June.

The Lady Vols have been a consistent top-10 program under Karen Weekly for years. They have all the ingredients to make the next step. A hitting coach with a championship fingerprint is one more piece added to that case.

Putting the Staff in Context

Snider and Sanders were well-regarded at Tennessee before departing. Their move to Auburn as co-head coaches is a reflection of the preparation they received under Weekly — and a sign of how well Tennessee's staff has been developing coaches as well as players.

The replacement hire, however, may represent an upgrade in experience for the specific role of hitting coach. Earleywine's tenure as a head coach — 14 seasons running his own program — gives him a perspective that pure assistants sometimes lack: he has seen how hitting philosophy, lineup construction, and at-bat approach interact at the highest levels of collegiate competition.

For Tennessee's 2027 players, he is not just a drill instructor. He is someone who has built offenses from scratch and watched them win.

What's Next

Tennessee's fall schedule includes home ball games at Sherri Parker Lee Stadium, with several games open to the public. The Lady Vols are building quietly — no major transfer portal splash, no recruiting circus — but the pieces are there for a deep 2027 postseason run. With the coaching staff now set, Karen Weekly's focus shifts entirely to preparation for February.

Rocky Top is watching. And with Earleywine now on staff, the rest of the SEC should be too.
