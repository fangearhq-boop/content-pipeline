Torres and Kavan Together for the First Time: Texas Hosts Texas State Tonight

By Jordan Reeves | October 13, 2026 | I Love Softball

It is the most anticipated pairing in college softball, and tonight fans finally get a real look at it.

Texas softball hosts Texas State at McCombs Field in Austin at 6 PM CT on Tuesday, October 13. Admission is free. The Longhorns are in the second game of their six-game fall schedule, and for the first time in a competitive setting, Isa Torres and Teagan Kavan will share the same lineup.

These are not just good players. They are two of the most accomplished players in the sport — and the Longhorns landed both of them.

The Torres Factor

Isa Torres arrived at Texas from Florida State carrying one of the most impressive statistical lines in recent memory. In 2026, she batted .530 with a .609 on-base percentage and slugged .980 with 16 home runs and 56 RBIs. D1Softball named her Player of the Year. She is, in a word, relentless — a hitter who forces pitchers to be perfect and punishes them when they are not.

The decision to transfer to Texas was not made lightly. Torres chose the Longhorns knowing she was walking into a program that had just won the 2026 national championship. The expectation level is different. The spotlight is brighter. Her first test in burnt orange comes tonight.

What Kavan Brings

Teagan Kavan is a two-time Women's College World Series Most Outstanding Player. She has stood in the biggest moments the sport offers and delivered. Her presence transforms how opposing pitching staffs prepare — game-planning against Kavan is a week-long project, and when Torres is hitting behind (or in front of) her, the problem becomes exponentially harder.

The Texas coaches have experimented with lineup construction through fall workouts. Seeing how Torres and Kavan interact in the order — who bats second, who bats cleanup, how pitchers try to navigate around them — is one of the fall season's most compelling subplots.

The Bigger Picture

Texas opened its fall schedule with a 28-1 blowout of Murray State. Tonight's opponent, Texas State, provides a stiffer test. The Bobcats are not a pushover program, and their pitching staff will give the Longhorns something to work against.

The date on everyone's calendar in Austin is November 6 — when the Longhorns travel to Texas A&M for the rivalry game that closes out the fall schedule. Coaches are using every game between now and then to dial in a rotation and finalize lineup decisions that will carry into February.

What's Next

Texas has four more fall games after tonight, culminating with the Nov. 6 road trip to College Station. The regular season begins in February 2027. The question is not whether this Texas team is talented enough to compete for a national title. The question is whether Torres and Kavan together can reach the heights their individual résumés suggest — and tonight is one of the first data points toward that answer.

McCombs Field. 6 PM CT. Free admission. Be there.
