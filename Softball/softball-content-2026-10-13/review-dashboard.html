Robot Umpires Coming to Softball — ABS Challenge System Approved for 2027
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post The biggest rules change coming to NCAA softball since the mercy rule — and it's happening in 2027. The NCAA has approved an Automated Ball-Strike (ABS) challenge system for all NCAA levels starting next season. The system is confirmed for baseball and moving to softball on an experimental basis. The Sun Belt Conference is already in, announcing an expanded partnership with Trackman to bring ball-strike technology to their parks. Here's how it works: Teams can challenge certain ball-strike calls by the home-plate umpire when the appropriate tracking technology is installed. If the challenge succeeds, the call gets overturned. If it fails, you lose a challenge. At the SEC's 2026 baseball tournament, 105 calls were challenged — and about 56 percent were overturned. That's more than half of challenged calls reversed by technology. What does this mean for softball? ✅ The check-swing call — one of the sport's most disputed — becomes challengeable. ✅ Close pitches on the black get objective review. ✅ The game's integrity improves. ⚠️ Not every park will have it immediately — installation costs are a barrier. ⚠️ Managing challenge strategy mid-game adds another decision layer for coaches. This is the future of the sport arriving in real time. What do you think — is this good for softball? ### Image Post Caption Robot umpires are coming to NCAA softball in 2027. The ABS challenge system is approved. The check-swing debate just got a ticking clock. ⏰
