9 Days to September 1: How Coaches Build Their Call Lists — and How You Get On One

By Sarah Mitchell | I Love Softball | August 23, 2026

Nine days from now, Division I softball coaches across the country will pick up their phones and make calls they have been planning for years. September 1, 2026 is the date when D1 coaches can contact Class of 2028 recruits directly — by phone, text, and email — for the first time.

For recruits and their families, it helps to understand what is happening on the other end of that call. Coaches are not calling randomly. They have lists. Those lists are ordered. And the process of building those lists is further along than most recruits realize.

How a Call List Gets Built

At most programs, a priority recruiting list is built through a combination of evaluation camps, showcase tournaments, film review, and informal communications. The list reflects which prospects the coaching staff has spent the most time evaluating, the positions they need to fill in future recruiting classes, and the players they believe fit their program's culture and competitive level.

Most programs target somewhere between 8 and 12 priority recruits for first-hour contact on September 1. Those are the players who will receive a call within the first 60 minutes of the window opening. For a prospect to land on that short list, the coaches have typically been watching them for 12 to 24 months, seen them in person at least once or twice, and have already decided that this recruit is someone they want to pursue seriously.

Being called in the first hour is not accidental. It reflects where a recruit stands on a program's board.

What the Next 9 Days Are For

The recruiting list is largely set. This is not the time for dramatic gestures or completely new outreach to programs you haven't engaged with in months. What these last nine days are for is confirmation of intent.

Recruits who have been building relationships with their top programs should use this week to send a brief, sincere message confirming that those programs are still a priority and that they are looking forward to talking September 1. A message of that kind — genuine, not performative — can reinforce a recruit's standing on a board where slots are actively being prioritized right now.

For families who have not yet had the internal conversation about which programs are truly the top priorities and why, that conversation should happen this week. Not on September 1. Not at midnight. This week — while there is still time to approach the first call with clarity.

The September 9 Window

September 1 is the first contact date, but it is not the only important date on the recruiting calendar this fall. On September 9 at 7:00 AM ET, the in-person contact window opens. Coaches can attend games. Official visits and unofficial visits become possible. For many recruits, the experience of an official visit to a campus will feel more tangible and decisive than any phone call.

The combination of September 1 calls and September 9 visits creates a compressed recruitment window that can move very quickly. Recruits who prepare deliberately for both dates — with clear priorities, informed questions, and family alignment — will navigate that window with more confidence than those who try to process everything in the moment.

What's Next

September 1 is nine days away. The call list at your top programs is already built. The work now is confirmation — making sure the programs you care about know you care about them, and making sure your family is aligned on priorities before the first call comes in. On September 9 at 7:00 AM ET, in-person contact opens. The recruitment window arrives fast. Be ready for it.
