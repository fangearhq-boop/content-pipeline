Isa Torres Is Coming Home — and Texas Is Already Better for It

By Sarah Mitchell | I Love Softball | August 23, 2026

Isa Torres grew up in Georgetown, Texas. She spent three years building one of the most decorated careers in recent collegiate softball history at Florida State. And on June 18, she signed with the University of Texas and came home.

The Longhorns — already the defending back-to-back national champions — were not a program that needed help. And yet, the addition of the 2026 D1Softball National Player of the Year makes them demonstrably stronger than the team that won the last title. That is a remarkable thing to be able to say about any defending champion.

What Torres Did at FSU in 2026

Torres's 2026 season at Florida State was statistically exceptional. She hit .530 — a number that placed her among the elite offensive performances in the country. She scored 78 runs, hit 21 doubles, 7 triples, and 16 home runs, and drove in 56. She broke FSU's single-season records in batting average, runs scored, slugging percentage (.978), and on-base percentage (.591).

The awards that followed reflected everything her statistics suggested: D1Softball National Player of the Year, NFCA First-Team All-American, Gold Glove Award, ACC Player of the Year, and ACC Defensive Player of the Year. Torres was not just the best hitter available in the transfer portal. She was arguably the most decorated offensive player in college softball in 2026.

How She Fits at Texas

Torres will compete at shortstop, where she'll work alongside Vivianna Martinez. The position has the depth to support competition — and having a Gold Glove-winning defensive shortstop competing at that spot raises the floor of what Texas can do in the field.

In the lineup, the impact is also significant in a less obvious way. Katie Stewart set the Texas school record with 31 home runs in 2026. Adding Torres — a player of that offensive caliber — to the lineup above or below Stewart creates protection that opposing pitchers cannot safely navigate around. A lineup featuring both Stewart and Torres is not something any pitching staff can simply choose one side to attack.

The Longhorns' pitching staff remains anchored by Teagan Kavan, the first player in WCWS history to win Most Outstanding Player twice, who returns for her senior year. Samantha Swan (catcher, Arizona State) and Samantha Lincoln (lefty pitcher, Texas Tech) complete the portal class.

The Three-Peat Context

Only two programs in college softball history have won three consecutive Women's College World Series titles: UCLA, which won in 1988, 1989, and 1990, and Oklahoma, which won in 2021, 2022, and 2023. Texas is attempting to join that group in 2027.

The argument that the 2027 Longhorns are better than the team that just won two straight titles is not a stretch. Torres is a year removed from her best college season. Kavan is back for one final year. Fall camp is underway. And a Georgetown, Texas kid is home, playing for the school in her state, with a chance to win a ring.

What's Next

Texas opens fall camp in August with Torres, Kavan, Swan, Lincoln, and a seven-player incoming freshman class. The 2027 season begins in the spring. The three-peat target is set. Only UCLA and Oklahoma have accomplished what Texas is pursuing — and the Longhorns, with Isa Torres back in Texas, have the talent to make a serious run at it.
