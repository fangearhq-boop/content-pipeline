Mid-Major Programs Find Their Lane in the Class of 2027 Landscape

By Sarah Mitchell

November 19, 2026

The Class of 2027 headlines belong to Oklahoma, LSU, and Tennessee. The top 10 is a familiar mix of SEC blue-bloods and newly ascendant Big 12 programs. But D1Softball ranked 50 signing classes — and what the full list reveals is a college softball landscape that is growing deeper in ways the top-line rankings don't capture.

Georgia Tech came in at No. 32. That placement tells a larger story about where the sport is heading.

What Georgia Tech's No. 32 Ranking Means

Five years ago, a No. 32 D1Softball ranking would have meant very little for a program outside the SEC or Pac-12 power structure. The gap between programs at that level and programs in the top 10 was enormous. The talent simply didn't flow in that direction.

That gap has closed. NIL has changed the value proposition for recruits in ways that are only now becoming visible in class rankings. A five-star athlete can now weigh an NIL arrangement from Georgia Tech against a scholarship offer from Florida and make a real financial and competitive comparison. For recruits who want to start immediately, play close to home, or compete in a major media market, mid-major programs are offering real advantages.

The Transfer Portal as an Equalizer

The transfer portal has also reshaped the competitive landscape for programs outside the traditional power conferences. Programs no longer have to build exclusively through recruiting cycles. A well-timed portal class can leapfrog years of development and add immediate impact players who have already proven they can compete at the highest level of the sport.

This is particularly true in the Sun Belt and ACC, where programs have used the portal intelligently to supplement signed classes and shore up positional needs. The flexibility that the portal provides — combined with NIL resources — has given non-SEC programs tools they never had in prior eras.

Regional Recruiting Is Maturing

Perhaps the most underappreciated trend in the Class of 2027 landscape is the maturation of regional recruiting. Programs in the Sun Belt and ACC that once lost their best in-state recruits to SEC schools are keeping more talent at home. The appeal of playing close to family, in familiar facilities, in a program that is building real momentum, has become a genuine competitive factor.

This doesn't mean the SEC dominates less. Oklahoma's No. 1 D1Softball class and LSU's No. 1 Softball America class demonstrate that the elite programs are still elite. But the gap between No. 10 and No. 30 in this class is narrower than it has been in years.

What's Next

January preseason polls will reflect the top 10 that everyone already knows. But the fuller picture of the Class of 2027's impact — including what programs like Georgia Tech and others in the mid-major tier do with their incoming classes — won't be visible until spring. Watch for breakout programs in conference play. The sport is spreading, the talent is deeper, and the 2027 season will be better for it.
