The Texas Tech Effect — How One Program Changed the Big 12's Power Structure

By Jordan Reeves

November 19, 2026

Two years ago, Texas Tech was part of the Big 12 conversation. Not the center of it. A team to watch, perhaps. Not a team that made every other coach in the conference adjust their recruiting strategy.

That is no longer the case. And the person responsible is Jaime Glasco, now entering his second full year leading the program.

The Class That Changed the Conference

Softball America named Texas Tech's 2026-27 transfer class No. 1 in the country. Nationally. Not in the Big 12. Not among non-SEC programs. First among every Division I softball program in the country.

The centerpiece is Kasidi Pickering. Her numbers at Oklahoma — .384 batting average, 50 home runs, 169 RBIs — are not the product of a hitter who padded statistics against weak opponents. She produced those numbers in the Big 12, against the best pitching in college softball. When she arrives at Texas Tech for the spring, opposing pitchers will game-plan around her from day one.

Jocelyn Gentry joins as the first five-star freshman in Texas Tech program history. That alone would be a headline for most programs. For Texas Tech, in a signing class that also includes the best transfer class in the country, it is a secondary story.

What This Means for the Big 12

The Big 12 in 2027 may be the most competitive conference in college softball history. Oklahoma enters with the No. 1 D1Softball signing class — Goose Hutchens enrolled early, Finlee Williams and Riley Hilliard are five-stars, and the program returns its offensive core. Texas is building toward national prominence and has the No. 1 recruit in the Class of 2028 already committed. Arizona went 15-2 in the fall under first-year coach Caitlin Lowe, with Big 12 DPOY Regan Shockey returning.

Now add Texas Tech, with the No. 1 transfer class nationally and a lineup that will feature one of the most feared hitters the conference has ever seen.

The Big 12 could realistically send three programs to the Women's College World Series quarterfinals in 2027. That would represent a historic shift in softball's power balance — a conference once dominated entirely by SEC programs asserting itself as an equal.

The Ripple Effect

Glasco's success has forced every Big 12 program to elevate its game. Oklahoma has responded by locking down its recruiting pipeline more aggressively. Arizona accelerated its portal strategy. Texas invested more heavily in NIL infrastructure. The floor of the Big 12 rose because one program raised the ceiling.

That is what transformational coaching does. It doesn't just improve one program. It makes the entire conference harder to navigate.

What's Next

The Big 12 season opens March 12. Texas Tech's first Big 12 game of 2027 will be one of the most watched regular-season matchups in conference history. When Glasco's loaded roster takes the field — Pickering in the lineup, the No. 1 transfer class assembled — the entire sport will be watching to see if the Texas Tech effect translates to wins in the spring.
