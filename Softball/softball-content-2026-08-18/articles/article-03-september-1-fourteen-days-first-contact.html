14 Days to September 1: What Class of 2028 Recruits Should Expect When the Phone Finally Rings

By Sarah Mitchell | August 18, 2026

I Love Softball / FanGear HQ

The countdown is now in single digits of business days.

Fourteen days from today — September 1 — marks a pivotal moment for every Class of 2028 softball recruit in the country. That is the date when Division I coaches can pick up the phone and call, text, or request an off-campus meeting with a junior-year recruit for the very first time. It is the moment the recruiting process moves from controlled communication into real conversation.

For families who have been navigating the early stages of the recruiting calendar, understanding what happens on that day — and how to read what unfolds — can make a significant difference in how the process goes.

What Changes on September 1

Since June 15, Division I coaches have been permitted to send printed recruiting materials and some forms of electronic communication to Class of 2028 recruits. They could not call, text in a personal conversation, or arrange an off-campus meeting to evaluate a player directly.

September 1 removes those restrictions. Starting on that date, coaches can call or text recruits directly, arrange off-campus visits to watch them play, and have substantive recruiting conversations. For recruits who have been receiving questionnaires and recruiting flyers all summer, the first real phone call from a coaching staff is a qualitatively different moment.

How Timing Reveals Priority

One of the most important things families can learn from September 1 is where a recruit stands on a program's priority list — based entirely on when the call comes.

Programs that call in the first days of September are signaling that a recruit is a top target. These programs have had the recruit identified for months, have studied film, and are prioritizing that relationship from the earliest possible moment. A call in early September from a program a recruit has mutual interest in is one of the clearest signals in the recruiting process.

Programs that call in October or November are typically still in evaluation mode, watching a recruit's junior season before committing significant attention. That is not necessarily negative — it may simply reflect the volume of players those programs are tracking — but it means the prospect is likely not the top target.

Programs that do not call at all through the fall are almost certainly not pursuing that recruit in a meaningful way.

Preparing for September 1

Coaches have had their call lists built for weeks. They know which players they want to reach first, and they will be organized on September 1. Recruits and families can prepare by having a clear sense of which programs matter most to them, keeping their schedule accessible so they can take first calls promptly, and having a brief, focused response ready when a coach asks about their interest.

Film from late-summer showcases and August tournaments is already in coaches' hands. The evaluation work largely happened before September 1. The call is the activation of decisions coaches have already made about which recruits they want to pursue.

What's Next

September 1 typically launches a wave of commitment announcements as recruits who receive first calls from their top programs move quickly to verbally commit. Watch the commitment tracker at Extra Inning Softball and other recruiting services for the Class of 2028 activity that follows. The next four weeks after September 1 are among the most active in the entire softball recruiting calendar for a junior class.
