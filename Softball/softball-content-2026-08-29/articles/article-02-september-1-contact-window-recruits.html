3 Days to September 1: What Class of 2028 Recruits Need to Know Right Now

By Emily Rawlings

August 29, 2026

For the Class of 2028 and their families, the calendar says August 29. But the only date that matters is three days away.

September 1 is the official opening of the Division I contact window for current junior-year softball recruits — the day that NCAA rules allow college coaches to initiate calls, texts, and official communication with prospective student-athletes for the first time. It is the single most important date in the D1 softball recruiting calendar, and preparation in these final 72 hours can make a real difference.

What Changes on September 1

Before September 1 of a prospect's junior year, Division I coaches cannot initiate contact. They can evaluate athletes at showcases, camps, and games — and prospects can reach out to coaches on their own — but the phone cannot ring from a coaching staff before that date.

When September 1 arrives, everything opens. Coaches can call, text, and email. They can invite prospects on official visits. They can meet with athletes and families in person. And the programs that have been watching your athlete all year are ready to move.

The first hour after midnight on September 1 is the most competitive window in recruiting. Top programs prioritize their boards and make their most important calls early. Athletes who are accessible, prepared, and responsive in that first hour signal to coaches that they are serious — and serious coaches notice.

The September 9 In-Person Window

September 1 opens phone and digital contact, but the in-person contact window — when coaches can visit prospects at their schools or attend their events — opens on September 9 at 7 AM ET. This phase of recruiting is particularly significant for mid-cycle athletes who are still finalizing their school lists and want to be evaluated up close before committing.

Families should have a target list of programs in place and be ready to facilitate in-person contact if and when it comes. Coaches who travel to see a prospect are communicating something important: this athlete is a priority.

Preparation Checklist

For athletes and families in these final days, the most important things to have ready are an updated highlight film, a current list of academic stats and contact information, and a clear communication plan for September 1. Know which coaches you want to hear from most — and be ready to respond when the phone rings.

The athletes who have done the showcase work, built the film library, and reached out proactively to coaching staffs over the spring and summer are the ones who will hear from programs first. September 1 rewards preparation.

What's Next

September 1 is three days away. September 9 brings the in-person contact window. Over the coming weeks, the recruiting landscape for the Class of 2028 will shift rapidly as schools extend offers and athletes begin narrowing their lists. For prospects who have not yet identified their top programs, now is the time to finalize that research. When the phones start ringing on September 1, the athletes who know exactly where they want to play will be in the best position to make the right decision.
