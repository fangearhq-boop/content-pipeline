Texas Longhorns Eye the Three-Peat: Kavan's Historic Return Fuels 2027 Buzz

By Emily Rawlings

August 29, 2026

Texas has back-to-back Women's College World Series championships. Teagan Kavan is coming back. And the three-peat conversation — once premature — is now unavoidable.

The Longhorns swept Texas Tech in the 2026 WCWS championship round to claim their second consecutive national title under head coach Mike White. In doing so, they set up one of the most compelling storylines the sport has produced in years: a senior Kavan season, six returning starters, and a historical three-peat that only two programs in softball history have achieved.

The Player Nobody Has Done Before

Teagan Kavan is the only player in Women's College World Series history to be named Most Outstanding Player twice. She won the award in 2025 and repeated in 2026 — a feat unprecedented in the tournament's history.

A junior during the 2026 season, Kavan returns for her senior year in 2027 as the most decorated active pitcher in college softball. Her 2026 numbers reinforced what the 2025 campaign established: she is a generational talent who performs at her best on the sport's biggest stages.

The presence of a two-time MOP returning for one final college season is not something programs get to build around often. Texas gets that luxury next spring.

Six of Nine Starting Spots Return

Kavan is not the only reason the Longhorns project as the 2027 preseason frontrunner. Texas brings back six of nine starters from the 2026 championship team — a foundation of experienced, proven production that most programs would envy at the start of a rebuild, let alone heading into a potential three-peat.

The combination of an elite returning pitcher and a deep, veteran lineup gives White's program a ceiling that few programs can match on paper before a single fall practice is held. Outfielder Katie Stewart, who set the Texas single-season home run record with 31 in 2025, is among the key returning contributors.

The Company Texas Is Trying to Join

Only two programs in softball history have won three or more consecutive national championships. UCLA won three in a row during their dynasty runs in the 1980s. Oklahoma claimed multiple three-title stretches during their dominant years. Both programs required decades to build those runs.

Texas is positioned to do it in three years. That kind of concentrated excellence is historically rare — and it reflects the program-building work White has done since taking over in Austin.

What's Next

The 2027 college softball season opens in February. Preseason polls will begin circulating in the fall, and Texas figures to be No. 1 or No. 2 in every major publication's ranking. The Longhorns have Kavan for one more season — and a chance to cement a dynasty that very few programs in the sport's history have matched. The three-peat chase begins in February. 🤘
