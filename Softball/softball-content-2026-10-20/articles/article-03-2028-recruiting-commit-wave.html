2028 Commit Wave Rolls On: Texas Leads, But Florida and Tennessee Are Closing Fast

By Sarah Mitchell | I Love Softball | October 20, 2026

The two-day contact window for the Class of 2028 closed Sunday. The commitments are just getting started.

With coaches now having completed their first direct in-person evaluations of 2028 prospects, the commit wave that typically follows a contact period is in full motion. Programs that spent October 17-18 calling and evaluating eighth- and ninth-graders across the country are now waiting — and increasingly receiving — verbal commitments as recruits process what they heard and decide where they want to spend their college career years from now.

Line Drive Media reported that the softball industry surpassed 1,000 total commits since September 1 as of its mid-October update. The Class of 2028 is shaping up as one of the most actively recruited in recent memory. Here's where the top programs stand.

Texas — Still Leading the Class

Texas entered the contact window as the class frontrunner and nothing since has changed that. The Longhorns' class is anchored by Michaela Landers — the No. 1 infielder in the 2028 class — alongside Ainsley Morvan, who posted 22 home runs and committed to Texas in October. The Longhorns have built their class heavily from the Texas Bombers Gold program, one of the nation's premier travel organizations, with Abdullah and Schellhammer also in the mix. Head coach Mike White has made home-state recruiting a priority, and the 2028 class is the clearest expression of that philosophy yet.

Florida — Holden Leads a Deep Group

The Gators are building a class that could challenge Texas for the top ranking. Five-star middle infielder Kylie Holden — ranked No. 2 overall in the 2028 class by Softball America — committed to Florida in early October. She's joined by five-star catcher Ryann Harris, five-star two-way player Kate Losh, and four-star catcher Brooklyn Evans. The depth and versatility of Florida's class reflects a program that has learned from its recruiting losses in prior cycles and committed to winning on the 2028 trail early.

Tennessee — Five Commits and Counting

The Lady Vols have parlayed their 2026 WCWS success into serious 2028 momentum. Five players have committed to Tennessee since September 1, including Caelyn Campos at No. 4 overall and right-handed pitcher Megan Kuiper from Illinois. Head coach Karen Weekly's program has developed a reputation for developing elite pitchers — a pitch that clearly resonates with recruits — and the 2028 class reflects that identity.

Oklahoma and Oklahoma State

Oklahoma enters the wave with five commits, continuing to build after the coaching staff's contact window push. Oklahoma State landed its first 2028 building block last week when top-100 infielder Peyton Cleary of Liberty North High School in Missouri committed to head coach Kenny Gajewski's program. Cleary chose the Cowgirls after a late-August visit to Stillwater and becomes OSU's foundation piece for the class.

What's Next

The next 48-72 hours will likely see the biggest cluster of 2028 commitments yet, as recruits who had their final face-to-face evaluations over the weekend process their visits and make decisions. Programs that made strong impressions during the contact window will see commitments flow. Those that didn't will need to wait until the spring showcase circuit reopens the conversation.

For the Class of 2028 as a whole, the emerging picture is a class that will continue to take shape through the fall and into early 2027 — with the contact window having set the stage for everything that follows.
