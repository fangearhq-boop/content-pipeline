The Early Recruiting Machine: How College Softball Changed in Two Years

By Jordan Reeves | I Love Softball | October 20, 2026

Oklahoma State just received its first commitment from the Class of 2028. The prospect is a top-100 infielder from Missouri who plays for her state's premier travel ball program. She's in eighth grade.

That sentence, a few years ago, would have been remarkable. Today, it's Tuesday's news.

Since September 1, more than 1,000 softball players have committed to college programs across the country. The October 17-18 contact window — the first window in which Division I coaches could contact 2028 prospects directly — closed Sunday, and the commit wave that follows is still building. In a sport that has moved faster than almost any other in college athletics toward earlier and earlier recruiting, the Class of 2028 is arriving right on schedule.

How It Happened

The acceleration of softball recruiting has multiple causes, but the most significant is the increasing competitiveness at the top of the sport. As programs like Oklahoma, Texas, Florida, Tennessee, and others have built rosters that compete for national titles year after year, the arms race for top talent has pushed recruiting timelines earlier. If a program misses on a top-100 prospect in the class of 2028, there's no guarantee a comparable player will be available in 2029.

NCAA rule changes have adjusted the contact calendar — when coaches can reach out, visit, and evaluate prospective students — but the fundamental pressure doesn't come from rules. It comes from competition. Programs that want to win recruit when the talent is available, not when a calendar says it's convenient.

What It Means for Players and Families

For families of talented young players, the early recruiting era creates real pressure alongside real opportunity. The exposure circuit — travel teams, summer showcases, fall tournaments — has become a year-round commitment. Missing a key showcase can mean missing a coach's evaluation. Missing an evaluation can mean missing a scholarship offer. The window feels narrow even when a player is 13 years old.

Programs have responded by developing deeper pipelines into the travel ball world. Relationships with elite programs like Texas Bombers Gold, the organization behind several of Texas's top 2028 commits, are as important to recruiting success as any single visit or camp. Coaches who build those relationships early — and maintain them through years of roster transition — consistently outrecruit programs that don't.

The Programs Winning the Early Game

In the Class of 2028, Texas sits in the lead — a class anchored by the No. 1 infielder in the country and built heavily from in-state travel ball pipelines. Florida has assembled one of the most complete early classes, led by the No. 2 overall prospect. Tennessee has five commits and a pitching identity that sells itself. Oklahoma has continued to build from its position of sustained excellence.

These programs aren't winning the recruiting race by accident. They're winning it through relationships, resources, and a willingness to be present in a prospect's life years before she puts on a college uniform.

What's Next

The Class of 2028 contact window has closed, but the conversation hasn't. Commits will continue rolling in through the fall and into 2027, shaped by the evaluations coaches made last weekend. By the time this class arrives on campus in the fall of 2028, some of these programs will have been in the conversation with their signees for nearly four years.

Whether that's the right approach for a player's development — or for the sport's culture — is a legitimate debate. But the numbers tell the story: more than 1,000 commits since September 1, and counting. The early recruiting era in college softball is fully here.
