Tennessee's Fall Ball Finale: Lady Vols Enter Final Week with Eyes on 2027 WCWS

By Emily Rawlings | I Love Softball | October 20, 2026

Tennessee's fall ball season has one game remaining. This Friday, October 23, the Lady Vols host Lee University at Sherri Parker Lee Stadium in Knoxville — the final chapter of a fall campaign that has done everything a fall schedule is supposed to do: build chemistry, sharpen fundamentals, and leave the coaching staff — and its fanbase — excited about what's coming in spring.

Head coach Karen Weekly has not been shy about her assessment of what she's seen. She has described this as the deepest pitching staff in program history. That's a high bar at a program that has developed elite arms for decades, but the numbers make the case compelling.

The Pitching Staff That Makes It Work

Karlyn Pickens enters the fall fresh off being named the No. 1 overall pick in the Athletes Unlimited Softball League Draft — a recognition of what she accomplished in 2026: 194 strikeouts and a 1.70 ERA. She's not just the ace; she's arguably the best pitcher in college softball heading into 2027.

Behind her, Sage Mardjetko finished 2026 at 16-3 with a 1.26 ERA, earning NFCA All-American honors. A staff's No. 2 pitcher with those numbers would be a No. 1 on most programs in the country. The combination of Pickens and Mardjetko gives Tennessee an elite pitching tandem that compares favorably with any in the sport's history.

The fall has given both pitchers live reps, new looks at opposing lineups, and continued development time ahead of a 2027 spring schedule that will come loaded with expectations.

Offense to Match

The pitching gets the headlines, but Tennessee's fall showed an offense with real teeth. Emma Clarke delivered the fall's signature moment — a cycle in the Lady Vols' dominant win over Tusculum, hitting a home run, triple, two doubles, and driving in six runs in a single game. If Clarke carries that production into the spring, the Lady Vols have a lineup that won't need to rely entirely on its pitching staff to win games.

Other returners and newcomers have had the opportunity to prove themselves in fall competition, and Weekly has indicated satisfaction with the progress across the roster.

What's Next

Friday's finale against Lee University at Sherri Parker Lee Stadium closes the book on fall 2026. After that, Tennessee enters its winter preparation period — conditioning, skill development, and the kind of internal competition that shapes roster depth for the long season ahead.

The Lady Vols head into winter with a target on their back and the talent to justify it. A pitching staff this deep, an offense with this potential, and a head coach with Weekly's championship track record makes Tennessee one of the clearest WCWS favorites heading into 2027. The fall has done its job. Now comes the spring.
