The Early Recruiting Machine — How College Softball Changed in Two Years
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Oklahoma State just got its first commitment from the Class of 2028. The prospect — Peyton Cleary, a top-100 infielder from Liberty North High School in Missouri — is in eighth grade. That's the reality of college softball recruiting in 2026. Since September 1, more than 1,000 players across the country have committed to college programs. The October 17-18 contact window — which allowed coaches to call prospects directly for the first time — was just the most recent marker in an acceleration that has been building for years. How did we get here? A combination of factors: increased roster competition pushing programs to recruit earlier, rule changes that opened up earlier contact, and a cultural shift among elite programs that has made the "get them early" philosophy standard practice. Programs at Texas, Oklahoma, Florida, and Tennessee now regularly sign players who are still learning to drive. For families of talented players, this creates both opportunity and pressure. The exposure circuit — travel ball, showcases, camps — has become a year-round operation. It's a system that rewards early development and early decisions. Whether that's good for the sport long-term is a conversation worth having. For now, the numbers speak for themselves: 1,000+ commits, and counting. ### Image Post Caption 🏆 1,000+ softball commits since September 1. Eighth-graders committing to Power conferences. The early recruiting era in college softball is fully here — and it's reshaping the sport. 🥎
