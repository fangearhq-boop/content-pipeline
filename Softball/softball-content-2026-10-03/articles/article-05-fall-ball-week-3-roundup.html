Fall Ball Week 3: Texas A&M Rolls, Texas Tech Debuts Star Duo, FSU Starts Over

By Emily Rawlings October 3, 2026

The Fall Ball Calendar Is Full

We are three weeks into fall ball, and the picture developing across the country is both exciting and unsettled. Programs have absorbed their transfer classes, introduced their freshmen to game speed, and started to figure out lineups and rotation depth. Some answers are emerging. Others will take weeks more. Here is where things stand as Week 3 gets underway.

Texas A&M Opens With a Statement

The Aggies got their fall off to the loudest start of any program in the country. Texas A&M opened on September 30 with an 18-3 win over Murray State at Davis Diamond. It was a statement before a single September game had been played at most programs. A&M enters 2027 with one of the more talented pitching staffs in the SEC and a lineup built to challenge for the conference title. Running up 18 runs in a fall opener tells rival coaches that the offense is not lagging behind the pitching.

The Aggies have consistently been positioned as an SEC program capable of making a deep postseason run. Their fall start suggests the integration of new personnel is going smoothly.

Texas Tech Debuts the Canady-Beachum Partnership

The most anticipated fall game in the Big 12 is happening today in Lubbock. Texas Tech is hosting West Texas A&M as Jaysoni Beachum and NiJaree Canady take the field as teammates for the first time. Beachum is the No. 1 ranked corner infielder from the 2026 transfer portal. Canady went 35-4 with a 0.98 ERA at Stanford last spring before transferring. The Lady Raiders already had back-to-back Big 12 regular season titles before either player arrived.

The combination they form together is what the entire Big 12 is watching. Oklahoma has been the conference's standard for years. Texas Tech has assembled a legitimate challenger. Whether that challenger is ready for a national title run will take a full spring to determine, but fall is where the foundation either holds or requires renovation.

Florida State Starts Fresh

In Tallahassee, Florida State is opening fall ball against Chipola College with a different kind of challenge: proving the program is not just Jaysoni Beachum. The Seminoles are the defending ACC champions. They have the coaching staff, the recruiting pipeline, and the program infrastructure to remain in the national title conversation. What they do not have is the production Beachum generated for three years in the corner infield.

FSU's fall is less about proving dominance and more about identifying who steps into expanded roles. The Seminoles do not lack talent. The question is which of their returning contributors can absorb what Beachum produced at the plate. Fall games against Chipola and upcoming opponents provide the first evidence on that question.

The Portal Keeps Moving

More than 250 players remain active in the 2026-27 transfer portal cycle. That number means the landscape is still shifting. Some of those transfers will land at programs in time for fall ball. Others will commit in November and December, arriving in the spring with limited practice time before the season opens. The programs managing that integration well — using fall to build chemistry before late arrivals disrupt it — are the ones best positioned for June.

Louisiana is among the programs building quietly on the margins. The Ragin' Cajuns open fall on the road at McNeese on October 9, then return home for several fall dates. The Sun Belt has had a strong run in recent seasons, and Louisiana has been part of that story.

What's Next

The next two weeks of fall ball will produce more clarity. Program identities will sharpen, lineup battles will partially resolve, and coaches will have a better sense of what they have heading into winter conditioning. The teams that emerge from fall ball with clean integrations and confident rosters have a meaningful advantage when February arrives. Check back each week as the picture develops.
