Jaysoni Beachum Suits Up for Texas Tech — The Lady Raiders' New Era Begins

By Emily Rawlings October 3, 2026

The Most Watched Fall Game in the Big 12

Jaysoni Beachum plays her first game in a Texas Tech uniform today. The Lady Raiders are hosting West Texas A&M at Tracy Sellers Field in Lubbock, and whatever your rooting interest, this is the fall game worth watching. Beachum is the No. 1 ranked corner infielder in the 2026 transfer portal cycle, the most talked-about player to change programs this offseason, and now she belongs to a program that already had NiJaree Canady.

That combination took time to become real. It is real today.

What She Brings to Lubbock

Beachum spent three seasons at Florida State, and by any measure, they were exceptional. She hit .406 with 10 home runs and 69 RBIs in 2026. Over her career at FSU, she posted a .375 batting average with 37 home runs and 180 RBIs. She earned All-American honors twice and won the 2024 National Freshman of the Year award in her first college season. She was the engine of FSU's offense during three seasons that included ACC regular season titles and deep postseason runs.

She committed to Texas Tech on June 26, 2026, choosing Lubbock after a recruitment cycle that included a well-publicized visit to Florida, complete with photos in a Gators uniform. SEC fans had reasons to believe she might be heading to Gainesville. She picked the Big 12 instead.

Joining Canady

The reason Beachum's destination matters so much is who was already in Lubbock. NiJaree Canady transferred to Texas Tech from Stanford after the 2026 spring season. Canady went 35-4 with a 0.98 ERA in 2026 for the Cardinal, one of the most dominant single-season pitching performances in recent college softball history. She is the most coveted transfer the Lady Raiders have ever landed.

Texas Tech had already won back-to-back Big 12 regular season titles before either player arrived. Adding Canady in the circle and Beachum in the corner infield simultaneously does not just make the Lady Raiders better. It changes the ceiling of what this program can be. The two had been on opposite sides of conference games. Starting today, they are teammates.

What Texas Tech Has Built

It is worth stepping back to appreciate what head coach Jenna Knust has assembled. The Lady Raiders entered this fall as back-to-back Big 12 regular season champions with their deepest roster in program history. Seven transfers joined the program this offseason alongside Canady and Beachum, giving Texas Tech one of the most experienced rosters in the country. That kind of depth does not guarantee anything in spring, but it changes how coaches can manage a 60-game schedule and survive injuries and slumps.

For the Big 12, the implications are significant. Oklahoma remains the measuring stick in this conference and nationally, but Texas Tech has assembled the pieces to push them. Arizona, Oklahoma State, and others will spend all fall and winter figuring out how to slow down a lineup with Beachum in it backed by Canady in the circle.

What's Next

Today's fall game against West Texas A&M is the first of several fall appearances before the Lady Raiders head into winter preparation. The real test comes in February when Big 12 opponents with full game film can design plans against Canady and scheme to limit Beachum's at-bats. Fall ball results are not predictive of spring outcomes, but chemistry, comfort, and cohesion all start somewhere. They start today, in Lubbock, against real competition. For anyone watching the national title race begin to take shape, this is the game to circle.
