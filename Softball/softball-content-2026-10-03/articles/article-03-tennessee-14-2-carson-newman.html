Barnhart Goes Yard Twice in Third Career Game as Tennessee Rolls 14-2

By Jordan Reeves October 3, 2026

A Freshman Announces Herself

Meredith Barnhart had played two collegiate games before Friday night. She had not yet established a career arc, a reputation, or a track record at this level. By the time Tennessee finished its 14-2 win over Carson-Newman at Sherri Parker Lee Stadium, that had changed. Barnhart hit two home runs and drove in five runs in her third college appearance, delivering the kind of performance that makes coaches and rival recruiting coordinators look up from their scorebooks and take notes.

Tennessee won going away. Seven runs in the first inning removed any doubt about the outcome early. But Barnhart's night was the story.

Nuwer's Night in the Circle

Sophomore Erin Nuwer was equally impressive, though the attention fell on Barnhart's bat. Nuwer threw four scoreless innings, struck out seven batters, and allowed just three baserunners. At one point she retired 15 consecutive batters. It was the kind of outing that reassures a fanbase already confident in the pitching staff that the No. 2 option is rounding into form alongside ace Karlyn Mardjetko.

Tennessee's pitching depth has been a program strength for years. Nuwer's development into a reliable second starter gives head coach Karen Weekly flexibility in how she manages a national title run, when shortening starts or going to a second arm in a tight game can change outcomes.

The Numbers Are Not a Fluke

Tennessee is now 3-0 in fall ball and has outscored opponents 39-4 across three games. The sequence: Walters State lost 15-1, Lipscomb lost 10-1, and Carson-Newman lost 14-2. That is not a soft schedule producing inflated numbers. That is a program with nine returning starters operating at a high level from the first fall game, with a freshman class that is ahead of schedule.

Fall ball results do not translate directly to spring performance, but the margin matters. A team that is clicking in October, integrating new players cleanly, and showing no obvious holes is different from one still working out its lineup construction and rotation management. Tennessee looks like the former.

What Barnhart Adds

Tennessee already has one of the deepest offensive lineups in the country returning for 2027. Nine starters are back. Adding a legitimate home run threat in the freshman class is the kind of addition that pushes a contender into a different conversation. Barnhart is three games into her career and already looks like someone who will hit in the middle of a lineup that competes for national titles.

Two home runs in game three does not guarantee anything about a 60-game spring season. But it generates expectations, and in Knoxville, the expectations for the 2027 Lady Vols were already significant. Barnhart just made them larger.

What's Next

Tennessee continues fall ball with several more scheduled appearances before the program turns its attention to winter preparation and preseason work. The Lady Vols will enter the 2027 season as a national title contender by any reasonable projection, with Mardjetko and Nuwer in the circle, nine returning starters in the field, and now a freshman class with Barnhart at its center. For programs trying to close the gap with Tennessee, Friday night was not a reassuring evening.
