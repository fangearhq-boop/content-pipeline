Tennessee's Emma Clarke Hits for the Cycle in Fall Ball

By Emily Rawlings | October 10, 2026 | I Love Softball

Emma Clarke does not do things halfway. The Tennessee sophomore hit for the cycle in fall ball this week — a single, a double, a triple, and a home run — and drove in six runs as the Lady Vols dismantled Tusculum, 32-0. It is a rare achievement in any competitive setting. In fall ball, where rosters are shuffled and the schedule is compressed, it still demands something most players simply do not have: the ability to put quality contact to all parts of the field at will.

Clarke had 14 home runs in 2026 as a freshman. She was one of three Lady Vols to eclipse 10 long balls, joining Alannah Leach (13) and Gabby Leach (10). That trio is back for 2027, and now Clarke is showing she can do more than hit for power — she can hit for everything.

A Lineup Built for October

Tennessee has been to four Women's College World Series semifinals in five years. The 2027 roster could be the program's most complete top-to-bottom lineup in that stretch.

The pitching staff returns both Peyton Mardjetko (16-3, 1.26 ERA, 171 strikeouts in 2026) and Madison Nuwer (15-1, 1.01 ERA). Together they combined for 31 wins and eight shutouts last season. That is a staff that can carry a team through a regional, a super regional, and deep in Oklahoma City.

Behind them, the position players are built for run production. Briley Knight hit .394 in 2026. Clarke, the younger Leach, and Alannah Leach form an offense that forces pitchers to make a quality pitch every single at-bat. Fall ball is the testing ground for all of it — the refinements, the reads, the adjustments. Clarke's cycle is a reminder that this lineup is not just returning; it is improving.

What Fall Ball Actually Tells You

Coaches and scouts who watch fall ball are not there for the scores. They are there for the details — pitch recognition, footwork in the field, how hitters approach two-strike counts, how pitchers locate when they have nothing to prove. A 32-0 result does not carry much diagnostic weight. The cycle does.

Hitting for the cycle means going to left-center for the opposite-field double, pulling the ball for the home run, running out a legged single to the right side, and finding a gap on three legs for the triple. Clarke did all of it in the same game. That is a complete hitting performance, and it suggests her junior year approach — if the development arc continues — will be even more dangerous than her already-remarkable freshman numbers.

What's Next

Tennessee continues its fall schedule over the coming weeks. Head coach Karen Weekly's staff is also in transition, with assistants Craig Snider and Stephanie Sanders departing at the end of the season. But the nucleus of the program — the roster Weekly built — is firmly in place. If Clarke keeps hitting like this, Tennessee in 2027 is not a preseason darling. It is the real thing.
