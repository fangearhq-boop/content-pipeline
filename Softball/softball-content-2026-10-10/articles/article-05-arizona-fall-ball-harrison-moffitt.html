Arizona Fall Ball: Newcomers Harrison and Moffitt Make Early Impressions

By Jordan Reeves | October 10, 2026 | I Love Softball

Arizona softball opened its 2026 fall camp this week, and a pair of newcomers made sure the program's first intrasquad scrimmage was worth watching. Pitcher Ryley Harrison and position player Elizabeth Moffitt both drew positive early reviews in the Wildcats' Red-Blue scrimmage — the kind of performance that makes fall ball feel like a real preview of what is to come.

Harrison went five innings allowing four hits and two walks. It was a controlled debut under competitive game conditions, a strong first impression for a newcomer who will be expected to contribute in 2027. Moffitt, meanwhile, showed the tools that made her a recruiting target and gave Arizona fans reason to watch her closely going forward.

Competition in the Desert

The Blue team came back in the top of the seventh inning to win the Red-Blue scrimmage. Even in an intrasquad format — where the opponent is your own teammate — competitive fire matters. A late comeback in fall ball signals a roster that does not give up, and for a program still establishing its identity under current staff leadership, that mental edge is a building block.

Arizona finished 2026 at 30-20 and was a Big West co-coach of the year recipient for head coach Panita Thanatharn in her first full season. The Wildcats have the pieces to improve, and fall ball is the opportunity to find out how those pieces fit together. Harrison and Moffitt are two of the newer additions to that puzzle.

A Program to Watch in the Pac-12 Era

Arizona has the brand and tradition to compete at the national level. The 2027 season will be a critical one for a program that wants to push back into the Women's College World Series conversation. Fall ball results — like this week's scrimmage — give coaches data and give fans early glimpses of what might be possible.

Harrison's controlled outing is the kind of debut that builds a pitcher's confidence heading into a full fall schedule. Moffitt's early impression gives the lineup another name to track. Neither story is finished — they are just beginning. But in a sport where programs are built one season at a time, beginnings matter.
