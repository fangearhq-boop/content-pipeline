Tennessee's Fall Finale Is Tomorrow — Pickens and Mardjetko Close Out a Dominant Autumn

By Sarah Mitchell | October 22, 2026

Thursday is the last day of fall softball for the Tennessee Lady Vols, and if this autumn has been any indication of what 2027 holds, the rest of the college softball world should be paying close attention.

Tennessee hosts Lee University on October 23 at Sherri Parker Lee Stadium in Knoxville. Admission is free. The game is part of the program's eight-game fall schedule, all played at home or on the road for free — head coach Karen Weekly's commitment to making fall ball accessible to the fanbase that has supported this program so passionately.

A Fall Defined by Dominant Pitching

Any discussion of Tennessee softball in the fall of 2026 begins with Karlyn Pickens and Sage Mardjetko — two pitchers who have combined to give the Lady Vols the deepest rotation in college softball.

Pickens was the No. 1 overall pick in the 2026 AUSL Draft — the first draft of the new professional softball league — and she chose to return for a fifth year of eligibility rather than turn professional immediately. Her spring numbers justified the attention: 194 strikeouts and a 1.70 ERA across a full season. Those are not good numbers. Those are historically elite numbers, and Pickens will enter 2027 as arguably the best pitcher in the country.

Mardjetko went 16-3 with a 1.26 ERA last spring and earned NFCA All-American recognition. In any other program, she would be the clear headliner. At Tennessee, she is the second arm in the rotation — a depth position that most programs would love to fill with a player half as accomplished.

Weekly has called this combination the deepest pitching staff in Tennessee program history. That is not hyperbole. It is a fair characterization.

The New Hitting Coach Changes Everything

Tennessee added Ehren Earleywine as associate head coach and hitting coach in June 2026, after the departures of assistant coaches Snider and Sanders — who left for Auburn as co-head coaches. Earleywine brings over 20 years of coaching experience, including a 14-year tenure as a head coach and most recently a role at 2026 national champion Texas.

The hire matters because Tennessee's 2026 season, despite excellent pitching, left questions about offensive production. Earleywine's specific mandate is to upgrade the Lady Vols' hitting approach, and having been part of a program that won the national title in his final season, he brings a championship-tested system to Knoxville.

The Fall Record and What It Means

Tennessee entered the fall finale having posted at least three wins in fall exhibitions, including a dominant 32-0 victory over Tusculum in which sophomore Emma Clarke hit for the cycle. The results confirm what the roster composition already suggested: this team is ready to compete for a national title in 2027.

Thursday's finale against Lee University closes the book on fall. The Lady Vols will begin full winter preparations in November, with a spring schedule that will include the full SEC slate and postseason competition that will determine whether this group's talent translates into a championship run.

What's Next

Tennessee's fall finale is Thursday, October 23, at Sherri Parker Lee Stadium — free admission, time TBD. Winter workouts and recruiting visits will follow before the 2027 spring schedule is announced. Watch for the Lady Vols in the early preseason Top-10 polls; the roster more than justifies it.
