Johnston County Heads Home as LLSWS Champions — Anna-Lynn Clark's Historic Tournament

By Emily Rawlings

August 12, 2026

Johnston County Little League is heading home to Clayton, North Carolina as Little League Softball World Series champions. The program shut out Owensboro, Kentucky 2-0 on Sunday at Stallings Stadium in Greenville, NC, delivering North Carolina its fourth LLSWS title and making Johnston County the first district to represent the state and walk away with the championship in the same year.

The result had as much to do with one pitcher's arm as anything else. Anna-Lynn Clark was extraordinary from the opening day of the tournament to the final pitch of the championship game — and her two-day finish to the LLSWS may be the most dominant stretch of pitching the event has produced in years.

Back-to-Back Shutouts, 24 Hours Apart

On Saturday, Clark threw a complete-game shutout in the semifinal — 109 pitches, 11 strikeouts, zero runs. Less than 24 hours later, she walked back to the circle for the championship game and did it again.

Against Owensboro in the final, Clark worked through 93 pitches and limited Kentucky to 3 hits and no runs, striking out 6 more batters. The physical and mental challenge of throwing consecutive shutout games at the youth world series — one night after a 109-pitch effort — is something the Johnston County pitcher made look almost routine.

Over the entire 2026 LLSWS tournament, Clark struck out 41 batters. No other pitcher in the field came close to that number.

How the Championship Came Together

Belle Cox provided the game's offensive spark, singling to drive in the first run of the championship in the bottom of the second inning. Johnston County added a second run an inning later on a Kentucky error. Two runs was all Clark would ever need.

The 12-team LLSWS field saw teams from across the country compete in Greenville throughout the tournament week. North Carolina came in as the host-region representative and never trailed in the championship game from the moment Cox's single broke the scoreless tie.

North Carolina's History at the LLSWS

This was North Carolina's fourth Little League Softball World Series title, joining championships in 2015, 2019, and 2024. No other state west of the Appalachians has kept pace with North Carolina's run in the LLSWS over the past decade. Texas (14 titles), Florida (8), and California (5) lead the all-time standings, but North Carolina's four titles since 2015 represent the most by any state over that span.

What makes the 2026 championship particularly notable is that Johnston County — representing the area around Clayton, NC — was participating in the LLSWS for the first time. For a program making its first appearance at the World Series to win the whole thing, behind a performance like Anna-Lynn Clark's, is the kind of story that Little League softball championships are built for.

What's Next

Johnston County returns home to Clayton as 2026 LLSWS champions. For Anna-Lynn Clark and the rest of the roster, celebrations in the community will follow. North Carolina's softball pipeline remains among the nation's strongest, and programs across the state will be watching to see how this championship class develops through the youth and high school ranks in the years ahead.
