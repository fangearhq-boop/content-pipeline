Week Two: Isa Torres and Teagan Kavan Are Getting Into the Real Work in Austin

By Sarah Mitchell September 6, 2026

Past the Honeymoon Phase

Week 1 of fall practice is for first impressions. Week 2 is for real work. Isa Torres — the 2026 D1Softball Player of the Year who batted .530 with 16 home runs and 56 RBIs at Florida State — enters her second week of fall practice at Texas, and the dynamic has shifted from "getting to know you" to "figuring out how we do this together."

That mostly means live reps with Teagan Kavan. The two-time WCWS Most Outstanding Player is entering her senior season in burnt orange, already the most decorated pitcher in Women's College World Series history in terms of that particular honor. Now she shares a practice field, a film room, and a dugout with the best hitter in the sport. The question the rest of college softball is asking is whether that combination turns into something that can chase a third straight national title.

Why Week 2 Is Different

The first week of fall camp is logistics and discovery. New players figure out where everything is, what the practice culture feels like, how the coaching staff communicates. Torres came from Georgetown, Texas originally and described the move to Austin as a homecoming — but even a homecoming requires adjustment in a new program.

Week 2 is different because the reps become meaningful. Torres and Kavan are in live situations now — Torres in the box while Kavan is working, or vice versa on the defensive side. The chemistry between the best hitter and the best pitcher on the same team isn't built in a conversation; it's built in hundreds of practice at-bats where they both learn each other's tendencies. That process is underway right now in Austin.

The Three-Peat Context

Texas is 23 days from its fall opener against Murray State at McCombs Field (Sept. 29, 6 PM CT, free and open to fans). The three-peat is the backdrop. Back-to-back national championships — two seasons with a combined record of 53-12 — puts the Longhorns in rarefied air historically. Only UCLA (1988-90) and Oklahoma (2021-23) have ever won three consecutive national titles in college softball. Both of those dynasty programs had a defining player who was the constant. UCLA had Lisa Fernandez. Oklahoma had Jocelyn Alo, then Tiare Jennings. Texas has Kavan.

The addition of Torres doesn't just add a bat to the lineup. It adds the best hitter in the sport to a team that was already winning at the highest level. If the chemistry builds the way Texas expects it to in fall camp, the argument for the Longhorns as the most dangerous team in college softball for 2027 becomes very short. They were already the favorites heading into this fall. Torres makes the argument almost unnecessary to have.

What's Next

Texas plays its first fall game on Sept. 29 against Murray State at McCombs Field (6 PM CT, free). The fall schedule runs through early November with five home games and one road game at Texas A&M. Week 2 of fall camp is the first real look at the Torres-Kavan partnership in live reps. By Sept. 29, the public gets its first look at what this Texas team is becoming.
