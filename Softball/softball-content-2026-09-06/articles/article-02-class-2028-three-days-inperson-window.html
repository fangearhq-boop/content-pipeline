72 Hours: What the Final Push to the In-Person Window Looks Like in 2028 Recruiting

By Emily Rawlings September 6, 2026

The Final 72 Hours

Seventy-two hours. That's how much time separates the Class of 2028 from the most important phase of the recruiting calendar so far. On Sept. 9 at 7 AM ET, D1 coaches can visit prospects in person for the first time — showing up at schools, homes, and practice facilities across the country. Five days of phone and text contact have built the foundation. Now it gets face-to-face.

The contact period opened Sept. 1 and the first major commit was already on the board within hours: Peyton Ushery, ranked No. 2 in the Line Drive Media HOT 100, chose Georgia on the opening day. She's the bellwether for what's coming. Programs that have been carefully managing their communication since Sept. 1 are now working through their final logistics — flights, school calendars, family schedules — to make Sept. 9 count.

What Programs Are Doing Right Now

The 72 hours before the in-person window is some of the most strategically active time in a coaching staff's calendar. Head coaches and assistant coaches divide up their target boards. Travel is booked. Some programs have identified two or three prospects they want to visit on Sept. 9 itself — which means tight scheduling and clear priority ordering. The teams that have the most to gain from Sept. 9 are the ones that have been most active on the phone since Sept. 1.

For prospects, this final push tends to produce a wave of commitment announcements. Recruits who have been leaning toward a program for months finally get that in-person moment — and it tends to accelerate decisions. The verbal offer can come on a home visit. The handshake moment happens in a kitchen or a living room, not over FaceTime. Some of the biggest 2028 commits in this class will be announced in the 96 hours after Sept. 9 opens.

A Class Built for This Moment

The Class of 2028 has been described as historically deep since the Extra Inning Softball nominations came in — 1,226 submissions, a record for the platform. There are 16 five-star athletes in the latest rankings. Top prospects like Michaela Landers, Kylie Holden, and Sophia Schlader have been responding to coaches at a volume that is unlike any prior class at this stage of the cycle. This isn't a thin class getting outsized attention. The talent is real, and the programs chasing it know it.

Ushery's Georgia commitment is the first signal of where this class is headed. The in-person window will produce the next wave. Programs with geographic advantages, campus culture fits, and strong existing relationships with prospects' club coaches are the ones most likely to close in September.

What's Next

The in-person contact window opens Sept. 9 at 7 AM ET. That's when D1 coaches can legally visit prospects at their schools and homes for the first time in the 2028 cycle. Expect a wave of commitment announcements in the days that follow — particularly for the top 50 prospects who have been narrowing their lists during the contact period. Michaela Landers, Kylie Holden, and Sophia Schlader are among the names to watch most closely in the coming week.
