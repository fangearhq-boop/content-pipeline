Three Weeks In: Leadership Is Emerging at Texas Tech — and That's Everything

By Emily Rawlings September 6, 2026

The Week That Reveals the Team

In fall camp, Week 1 is adjustment. Week 2 is calibration. By Week 3, you start to see the team that's actually going to show up in February. The natural leaders reveal themselves — not in what they say in team meetings, but in how they play when the competition is real and the coaches aren't reminding them of every detail. That's where Texas Tech is right now.

Three weeks into fall camp, Jaysoni Beachum, Kasidi Pickering, and Jayden Heavener are past the honeymoon of arriving in Lubbock. They know the practice schedule. They know the coaching voices. They've seen each other in enough live situations to have real opinions about each other's games. And now the real question — whether three cultures from three powerhouse programs can become one team — is starting to get answered in real reps.

The Three Players Who Matter Most

Beachum (from FSU) hit .406 with 10 home runs and 69 RBIs in her final season with the Seminoles and was a two-time All-American. As a senior corner infielder, she's the most natural candidate to be the voice of this offense. Pickering (from Oklahoma) is the No. 2-ranked transfer in the portal per Softball America, with 50 career home runs in the Big 12 and a .384 career batting average. The left-handed bat brings a different dimension to the lineup — and the experience of coming from the program that has been on the other side of a WCWS final from Texas Tech.

Heavener (from LSU) is the left-handed starting pitcher who went 13-9 with a 2.81 ERA and 128 strikeouts in 139.1 innings. She was named to the NFCA All-Region Team and earned a Florida state championship ring before arriving in Lubbock. A lefty with that ERA at LSU is a legitimate top-of-the-rotation arm in the Big 12. How she adjusts to a new conference, a new catcher's signs, and a new pitching coach is the fall camp storyline for the pitching staff.

Glasco's Year 2 Equation

Gerry Glasco is now in his second year as Texas Tech's head coach. Year 1 produced a WCWS runner-up finish — the talent was always there. Year 2 is the harder test for any program built on transfers. Last year's team had to learn Glasco's system. This year's team has to learn Glasco's system AND absorb multiple new high-profile additions AND maintain the standard of a WCWS finalist. That's three different demands happening simultaneously.

The distinction that Week 3 makes visible is which players are moving from "learning the system" to "operating in the system." The ones who are operating — not thinking, just playing — are the ones who are going to lead. For Texas Tech to take the next step from runner-up to champion, someone on this roster has to become the player who sets the standard that everyone else follows. Week 3 is when that person starts to come into view.

What's Next

Texas Tech's fall schedule will move into scrimmage and exhibition phases in the coming weeks. The WCWS runner-up from 2026 has the talent to win a national title in 2027 — Beachum, Pickering, Heavener, and several returning pieces from that runner-up squad make the case. But the identity question is the one that fall camp answers. By the time the Red Raiders play their first official spring game, Week 3's chemistry signals will have had months to develop into something real.
