Texas Tech Got Beachum. Florida Got Edwards. The 2027 Race Has Legitimate Competition.

By Emily Rawlings

August 4, 2026

Two programs entered the 2026 off-season already considered elite. Two programs used the transfer portal to get measurably better. When the 2027 season opens, Texas Tech and Florida will both look fundamentally different than they did when 2026 ended — and both programs believe that difference is the one that wins a national championship.

Texas Tech's Case

Texas Tech finished 2026 as national runners-up, reaching the Women's College World Series Finals. That result alone made them a serious program. Then the portal window opened, and they added Jaysoni Beachum.

Beachum comes from Florida State with a 2026 line of .406 batting average, 10 home runs, and 69 RBI. She also carries the 2024 NFCA National Freshman of the Year award and 2026 Second-Team All-American credentials. She is, by any objective measure, one of the top positional players available in the portal this cycle. Texas Tech also added Karlyn Pickering from Oklahoma, giving the Red Raiders two headline-level additions to an already deep roster.

The result is a transfer class that was ranked No. 1 nationally, and a Texas Tech program that now has seven current or former All-Americans. That is not a rebuild. That is an assembly.

Florida's Case

Florida didn't need a Tori Edwards. The Gators were already one of the most talented programs in the country. They added her anyway.

Edwards transferred from LSU, where her redshirt freshman season produced a single-season slugging percentage record for the program: .802, with 18 home runs. In 2026, playing in a more demanding environment, she hit .284 with 12 home runs and 43 RBI. Sports Illustrated ranked her as the No. 2 available transfer prospect in the portal. Florida winning that recruitment — over programs that also wanted her — says something about where the Gators sit in the national hierarchy.

The Florida roster that Edwards walks into already has experienced pitching, multiple returning All-American-caliber players, and a coaching staff that regularly develops and signs the top position players in the country. What Edwards adds is a middle-of-the-order bat with demonstrated power production and a track record of elite performance from day one at the college level.

Who Else Is in the Conversation

Naming Texas Tech and Florida as the two programs most changed by the portal is not the same as saying only two programs matter in 2027. Oklahoma has its own returning core and remains a consistent WCWS presence. Tennessee, Alabama, and Oklahoma State have all recruited well through both high school signing classes and the portal. The preseason polls that come out this fall will likely reflect a group of five to eight teams with legitimate national title cases.

What Texas Tech and Florida have done is ensure they are both in that group — and both upgraded themselves enough to make the case that they deserve to be near the top of it.

What's Next

Fall practice begins in the coming weeks for most programs. The first preseason polls typically publish ahead of fall scrimmages and are refined in late October and November. September 1 — 28 days away — opens the recruiting window for the Class of 2028, giving coaches and prospects another layer of roster-building to manage simultaneously. Texas Tech and Florida have done their most impactful work for 2027 already. What happens between now and opening day in February is about refinement, development, and preparation.
