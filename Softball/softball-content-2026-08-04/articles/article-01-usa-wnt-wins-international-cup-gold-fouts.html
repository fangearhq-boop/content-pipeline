Montana Fouts Was Nearly Unhittable. The USA WNT Wins International Cup Gold.

By Emily Rawlings

August 4, 2026

The 2026 USA Softball International Cup ended Sunday at Devon Park in Oklahoma City with the USA Women's National Team holding a gold medal and Montana Fouts having delivered one of the most dominant pitching performances of the entire tournament. The WNT beat No. 1 Japan 2-0. Fouts carried a no-hitter into the seventh inning — the only baserunner Japan would manage all game was a leadoff single to begin the seventh — and then the game was over.

What Fouts Did

Pitching a near-perfect game against the world's No. 1-ranked team, in a gold medal final, is not a small thing. That was the assignment, and Fouts delivered. Through six innings, Japan did not have a baserunner. The leadoff single to start the seventh was the only crack in an otherwise clean performance. Fouts finished it from there, and the USA WNT ended the tournament without allowing Japan to score on the day.

The gold medal game was a fitting conclusion to how the WNT had played all week. They went 3-0 in pool play, their only real test being a 10-7 comeback win over No. 4 Canada — a game they trailed 5-0 at one point. When the bracket called for a near-perfect outing in the final, Fouts gave them one.

The Path to Sunday

The WNT and Japan were the two unbeaten teams entering the semifinals. On the WNT's side of the bracket: they faced the USA Women's Elite Team in the semifinal and won 6-2. On the other side, Japan came from behind to beat Canada 6-5 in the bottom of the seventh inning — a come-from-behind win that secured Japan's spot in the gold medal final and sent Canada to the bronze medal game.

The Elite Team, which finished pool play 2-1 (their only loss a 9-2 result to Japan in the final group game), played Canada in the bronze medal game. The two USA squads finishing on separate sides of the podium while both in the medal round is exactly the kind of outcome this tournament format is designed to produce — genuine competition at the top level, with depth across both rosters proving itself on the way down to the final two teams.

What This Tournament Means for USA Softball

The International Cup is a high-level international competition but is separate from the official World Cup qualifying pathway. The real next step for the USA program is the WBSC Women's Softball World Cup Group Stage, specifically the Group C event scheduled for September 12-16 in Oklahoma City. That is the event where USA competes for direct entry into the 2027 Women's Softball World Cup Finals — which, in turn, functions as the first Olympic qualification event on the road to LA28.

What the International Cup provides, and provided this year, is high-stakes international competition in an elimination format against genuine world-class opponents — Japan, Canada, China, Australia, Chinese Taipei, and American Samoa were all in the field. Going 4-0 and winning gold, with a near-perfect pitching performance in the final, is a useful indicator of form heading into the September qualifying window.

What's Next

For the USA Women's National Team, the focus turns quickly to September. The WBSC Women's Softball World Cup Group C competition runs September 12-16 in Oklahoma City — the same Devon Park venue where the International Cup just concluded. Two top finishers from each of three worldwide Group Stage events, plus wildcards, advance to the 2027 World Cup Finals in Redcliffe, Australia in April. That is the event where the road to LA28 begins in earnest.

The Little League Softball World Series continues through August 9 in Greenville, North Carolina. The 2026 Asian Games, where softball is a competitive event, begin September 26 in Aichi-Nagoya, Japan. The international softball calendar is full through the fall, and Sunday's gold medal at Devon Park was the right way to begin it.
