Seven All-Americans, Zero Practices Together: Texas Tech's Fall Camp Is Almost Here

By Sarah Mitchell

August 11, 2026

The Texas Tech softball roster for 2027 is complete. Seven current or former All-Americans. A pitcher who posted a 2.81 ERA in 139 innings at LSU. A coaching staff that has reached back-to-back Women's College World Series Finals.

None of the new players have been on the same practice field at the same time yet. That changes when fall semester opens — which, at this point, is a matter of days.

The Roster on Paper

The talent assembled in Lubbock this summer is without precedent in Texas Tech softball history. Head coach Gerry Glasco — whose contract runs through 2030 after the program reached back-to-back WCWS Finals in 2025 and 2026 — brought in three of the most sought-after portal players of the 2026 cycle:

Jaysoni Beachum arrived from Florida State, where she hit .406 with a .549 on-base percentage, .671 slugging percentage, 10 home runs, and 69 RBI in 2026. A two-time All-American and 2024 NFCA Freshman of the Year, Beachum is one of the premier corner infielders in the country.

Kasidi Pickering transferred from Oklahoma, where she was a national champion. Her 2026 line: .370 average, 20 home runs, 61 RBI, .806 slugging percentage, .502 on-base percentage. The No. 2-ranked transfer in the portal per Softball America.

Pitcher Jayden Heavener came from LSU: 13-9 record, 2.81 ERA, 128 strikeouts, NFCA All-Region honors in 2026. She joins a rotation built around returning Red Raiders in what is now a legitimate two-ace candidate situation.

Add Kaitlyn Terry, Mia Williams, Taylor Pannell, Jasmyn Burns, and Moriah Polar — the returning veterans who bring All-American credentials of their own — and the 2027 Red Raiders roster reads like a preseason watch list for every individual award in the sport.

What Fall Camp Has to Answer

A collection of elite individuals does not automatically become a team. Glasco knows this. His players know this. They've played for different programs, in different offensive systems, with different bullpen hierarchies and lineup philosophies. Fall camp is where seven stars start the process of learning how to play together — who takes which at-bat in which lineup spot, how the pitching staff divides roles when both Heavener and the returning arms are healthy, how the defense aligns when you have four or five players capable of starting in any given position.

This is what preseason camp is for. It's not glamorous. It doesn't generate rankings. But championships come from the fall, not the spring — from the practices where roles get defined and trust gets built between players who have never shared a dugout before.

Texas Tech's fall camp has every reason to be closely watched. When seven All-Americans share a practice field for the first time, something worth paying attention to is happening.

What's Next

Fall semester opens at Texas Tech in the coming weeks, and with it comes the first shared practice for this unprecedented roster. Fall scrimmages will follow. The first real data on how this team performs together — not on paper, but against opponents — arrives later this fall.

Preseason rankings for 2027 will be released later in the fall and winter, and Texas Tech will almost certainly appear near the top of every list. Whether they live up to the billing starts now, in the weight room and on the practice field in Lubbock.

The roster is built. Fall camp answers the only remaining question.
