Johnston County Is Going Home: What NC's Fourth LLSWS Title Means for the Sport

By Sarah Mitchell

August 11, 2026

The Little League Softball World Series is over, and Johnston County is heading home to Clayton, North Carolina as four-time champions.

Watch parties filled Johnston County's community spaces on Sunday afternoon while the 2-0 championship win over Kentucky played out at Stallings Stadium in Greenville. A celebration is being organized for when the team arrives back home. Coach Ben Catarozzoli captured the moment after the final out: "Can't take that feeling away, they've worked so hard. We told them from day one if they continue to work the way they did and believe in each other, that we could accomplish this."

They accomplished it. Again.

Anna-Lynn Clark's Place in LLSWS History

When this tournament is discussed years from now, it will be discussed through the lens of what Anna-Lynn Clark did in Greenville.

Clark pitched 20 of Johnston County's 24 tournament innings. She allowed only 3 earned runs across the entire event. She struck out 41 batters — the most of any pitcher in the field, by a significant margin. She had three separate games of 10 or more strikeouts: a complete-game shutout against Lake Mary with 13 strikeouts, an 11-strikeout shutout in the semifinal against Florida (109 pitches), and then Sunday's championship performance — 93 pitches, 6 strikeouts, 3 hits allowed, not a single Kentucky runner advancing past second base in the final five innings of the game.

There is no useful frame of reference for that level of dominance at this level of tournament. She didn't just pitch well. She pitched at a level that made the rest of the field look like it was competing in a different weight class.

North Carolina's Championship DNA

Sunday's title is North Carolina's fourth Little League Softball World Series championship. The year list — 2015, 2019, 2024, 2026 — tells a story beyond just winning. North Carolina has won four titles in the last 12 years. Only Texas (14 championships since the tournament launched in 1974), Florida (8), and California (5) have won more. Four titles in 12 years puts North Carolina in a program conversation that very few states in LLSWS history can access.

Johnston County Little League is based in Clayton, NC — a community that has now produced a team capable of competing at the LLSWS level at the highest possible outcome. That doesn't happen by accident. It happens because something is being built in the program culture — and Sunday's result is the proof.

What's Next

For the players of Johnston County's 2026 championship team, the summer ends at the top. They return to Clayton as champions, welcomed by a community that watched every out from hundreds of miles away. Coach Catarozzoli and his staff will process what this run meant — and then, eventually, the work begins again.

The LLSWS returns to Greenville in 2027. The teams that compete will chase something this Johnston County team left behind: a standard. Anna-Lynn Clark set one. The championship set another. Whoever runs the 2027 gauntlet starts by measuring themselves against what happened here.

Johnston County goes home as champions. That's the one thing no one can take away.
