21 Days: The Class of 2028's September 1 Recruiting Window Is Almost Here

By Jordan Reeves

August 11, 2026

Twenty-one days.

On September 1, at midnight, the NCAA Division I softball recruiting landscape changes for the Class of 2028. Coaches can call. They can text. They can email. They can invite prospects for visits. For months — years, really — the rules have prevented that direct contact. On September 1, it all opens at once.

This is the moment the Class of 2028 has been building toward. And by every available measure, this is the most-scouted, most-discussed recruiting class the sport has ever produced.

Why September 1 Matters

The NCAA's recruiting calendar for Division I softball prohibits coaches from initiating direct contact with prospects before September 1 of their junior year. Before that date, coaches can only respond to contact an athlete initiates — they can watch at showcases, evaluate film, build their internal boards, and finalize their call lists. They cannot pick up the phone and call a recruit.

That changes at midnight on September 1. And the first call matters.

When a program calls a prospect on or around September 1, it's communicating priority. Coaches know how many slots they have. They know what positions they need. They've ranked their targets. The prospect who gets a call in the first hours of September 1 knows something significant about where they stand with that program — and where that program stands on their list.

For a class of 1,226 nominated prospects, the phones will be ringing from multiple directions simultaneously. Twenty-one days from now.

The 1,226 Nominations

Extra Inning Softball — the premier national recruiting evaluation service for women's softball — received 1,226 nominations for their Class of 2028 recognition program. That's the highest nomination total in the organization's history for any recruiting class. The EIS Extra Elite 100 position rankings across four positions (pitchers, catchers, infielders, outfielders) have already been published, and coaches have been using those evaluations all summer.

A record nomination total doesn't just reflect one exceptionally talented class. It reflects a sport that has grown its talent base significantly over the last several years, with more players competing at high levels earlier, more showcase opportunities, and more college programs taking Class of 2028 evaluations seriously before the contact window opened.

The Class of 2028 will be the most heavily competed-for recruiting class in the sport's history when September 1 arrives. The programs that built their boards carefully and efficiently are about to get their reward for that work.

What's Next

For Class of 2028 athletes and their families, the next three weeks matter. Update video from any late-summer exposure events. Keep recruiting profiles current. Make sure coaches who have shown interest know you're available and ready for that September 1 conversation.

And then wait for the phone to ring.

Twenty-one days. September 1 is close enough to plan for, and important enough that every day between now and then counts. The class that earned 1,226 nominations is about to find out which programs were paying the most attention.
