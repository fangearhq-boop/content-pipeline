### STORY 1: LLSWS 2026 — Johnston County Homecoming

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 9:00 AM ET

They're going home.

Johnston County Little League is heading back to Clayton, North Carolina as the 2026 Little League Softball World Series champions — and the town is ready for them. Watch parties packed Johnston County during Sunday's 2-0 championship win, and a celebration is being organized for the team's return.

Coach Ben Catarozzoli said it best after the final out: "Can't take that feeling away, they've worked so hard. We told them from day one if they continue to work the way they did and believe in each other, that we could accomplish this."

That belief produced one of the most dominant tournament runs in LLSWS history.

Anna-Lynn Clark pitched 20 of North Carolina's 24 tournament innings. She allowed only 3 earned runs across the entire event. She struck out 41 batters — the most of any pitcher in the field, by a considerable margin. She had three separate games of 10 or more strikeouts. And when the championship game arrived, she threw 93 pitches, allowed 3 hits, struck out 6, and didn't let a Kentucky runner past second base in the final five innings.

There's no meaningful way to grade that performance higher than it deserves. That was dominance.

North Carolina now has four LLSWS titles: 2015, 2019, 2024, 2026. Only Texas (14 titles), Florida (8), and California (5) have more. Johnston County earned their place in that conversation — and they earned it in the most convincing way possible.

Welcome home, champions.

### STORY 2: AUSL All-Star Cup — Series 1 Complete

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 11:00 AM ET

Series 1 of the 2026 AUSL All-Star Cup is complete. Three games, three captains, some outstanding individual performances — and now the competition resets for Series 2 with a major addition: Kayla Kowalik is in.

Here's how Series 1 played out:

Game 1 (Aug 8): Team Garcia won. Sami Williams delivered a 2-RBI double in the third inning for the decisive blow. Williams earned the first All-Star Cup MVP of her career — along with MVP-2 honors going to Karlyn Pickens and MVP-3 to Sharlize Palacios.

Game 2 (Aug 9): Team Corrick won 4-3. Megan Grant hit a grand slam as the key play.

Game 3 / Series 1 Finale (Aug 10, ESPNU): Team Ricketts won 2-0 over Team Garcia to close the round-robin.

Now it resets. The top three individual point earners from Series 1 become the Series 2 captains and draft entirely new teams. Individual points carry over — no one starts fresh. The momentum established in Series 1 follows you into Series 2.

And Kowalik — the 2025 All-Star Cup champion who batted .486 and accumulated 1,590 individual points to run away with last year's title — walks in for Series 2 with nothing on her ledger yet. She sat out Series 1 entirely. The early leaders now have a target.

The AUSL All-Star Cup runs through August 30 with four total series. Things are just getting started.

### STORY 3: September 1 — 21 Days Out

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 1:00 PM ET

Twenty-one days.

September 1 arrives in three weeks, and when it does, every Division I softball coach in the country can pick up the phone and call a Class of 2028 recruit directly for the first time.

Before September 1, coaches are bound by strict NCAA contact rules: they can only respond to athlete-initiated communication. They can watch. They can attend showcases. They can finalize their boards. They cannot call, text, or email a prospect unprompted. Three weeks from now, that changes at midnight.

What that first call means — and when it comes — matters. Programs that call on or around September 1 are communicating priority status. The timing tells a prospect where they stand. And for a class this well-scouted, that call list has been built and refined for months.

Extra Inning Softball received 1,226 nominations for their Class of 2028 recognition program — the highest in the organization's history for any recruiting class. The Extra Elite 100 position rankings (pitchers, catchers, infielders, outfielders) have already been published. Coaches have been evaluating from those lists all summer.

If your Class of 2028 athlete is waiting for September 1 — you're in the most-nominated class the sport has ever produced. Twenty-one days.

### STORY 4: Texas Tech 2027

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 3:00 PM ET

Gerry Glasco has put together seven current or former All-Americans on one Texas Tech softball roster. None of them have practiced together yet. Fall camp is about to change that.

The 2027 Red Raiders roster reads like an all-star ballot: Jaysoni Beachum (two-time All-American, .406/.549/.671 at FSU in 2026), Kasidi Pickering (.370/.502/.806, 20 HR, 61 RBI — national champion at Oklahoma), Kaitlyn Terry, Mia Williams, Taylor Pannell, Moriah Polar, and Jasmyn Burns. Add pitcher Jayden Heavener (13-9, 2.81 ERA, 128 Ks from LSU) and you have a rotation and lineup that looks, on paper, built to end the program's WCWS championship drought.

The question that fall camp answers: can seven elite players become one team?

This has never been assembled before in Lubbock. Coach Glasco, who earned a contract extension through 2030 after back-to-back Women's College World Series Final appearances in 2025 and 2026, now faces the integration challenge that no recruiting class solves on its own. Seven stars don't automatically share the same instincts. They haven't been through a practice, a fall scrimmage, a team meeting together.

Fall semester opens soon. With it comes the first shared field — and the first real data on whether this roster becomes the team everyone expects it to be.

The talent is exceptional. The chemistry starts from zero.

### STORY 5: USA WNT — 32 Days to OKC

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 5:30 PM ET

Thirty-two days.

The USA Women's National Team opened the 2026 International Cup at Devon Park in Oklahoma City on July 30. They went 5-0 through the event. They outscored opponents in every direction. They won gold.

On September 12 — 32 days from today — they go back.

The WBSC Softball Women's World Cup Group Stage returns to Oklahoma City, and the USA returns to the same venue where they just proved what they're capable of. Head coach Patty Gasso leads a 16-player roster that includes Rachel Garcia, Dejah Mulipola, and an array of athletes from the 2025 World Games gold medal squad. The program has been building this depth for years. The results show it.

The group stage opponents are set: Mexico (No. 7) in the September 12 opener, followed by Netherlands (No. 6), China (No. 8), New Zealand (No. 23), and South Africa (No. 36) through the round-robin.

These aren't just points in a tournament. Group Stage results feed directly into 2027 World Cup Finals qualification in Redcliffe, Australia — which feeds the LA28 Olympic pipeline. Every game in Oklahoma City is a step toward something bigger.

USA softball heads back to OKC in 32 days. With gold already earned at this same park, they know what winning here feels like.
