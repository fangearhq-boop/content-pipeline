The Tenth Circuit Heard Arguments Three Weeks Ago — Here's What Its Silence Means for Softball

By Sarah Mitchell | November 2, 2026 | I Love Softball

On October 13, 2026, a three-judge panel of the Tenth Circuit Court of Appeals heard oral arguments in the case challenging the NCAA's 5-for-5 eligibility rule. That was 20 days ago. No ruling has been issued. With National Signing Day arriving on November 11 and programs finalizing 2027 roster plans across the country, the court's silence has become its own kind of statement — one that coaches, recruits, and compliance offices are trying to interpret.

Where the Case Stands

The procedural timeline matters for understanding the current moment. The NCAA adopted the 5-for-5 rule on June 23, 2026, requiring athletes to complete five years of eligibility within five calendar years. A federal district court issued a preliminary injunction blocking the rule on July 31. The Tenth Circuit issued a 2-1 stay of that injunction on August 21, which put the rule back in effect while the appeal is pending.

The oral arguments on October 13 were on the merits — whether the stay should become a permanent ruling. After arguments, the panel took the matter under advisement. That is where it stands today.

What Appellate Courts Do After Arguments

Appellate panels do not rule from the bench. After oral arguments, the judges deliberate internally, circulate draft opinions, and revise before issuing a final ruling. This process can take weeks or months depending on the complexity of the case, the judges' workloads, and whether there is disagreement among the panel. Twenty days is well within the normal range for a case of this complexity.

The 2-1 vote on the stay in August suggests that at least one judge had reservations about the NCAA's position. Whether that translates into a dissent on the merits, a partial ruling, or a full affirmation of the rule's legality is impossible to predict from the outside.

The Practical Effect: Operate as If the Rule Is Law

While the court deliberates, the stay remains in force. This means the 5-for-5 rule is being enforced. Programs must operate under the assumption that athletes who have completed five years of eligibility within five calendar years are done playing. There is no guidance to ignore the rule until the court rules otherwise.

The complication is that a ruling against the NCAA could arrive at any moment — before Signing Day, after it, or months from now. Programs that have already built their 2027 rosters under the 5-for-5 framework could find that framework overturned. It is a genuine operational challenge with no clean solution.

The Patchwork Problem

Beyond the federal appellate case, at least 26 lawsuits have been filed in state and county courts across the country. In Texas, a McLennan County court issued an order enabling 39 named plaintiffs to regain their eligibility and transfer portal access, regardless of the federal stay. This means there are athletes in different states operating under different legal frameworks — a patchwork that compliance offices are struggling to navigate.

The NCAA has tried to maintain a unified national standard through the Tenth Circuit litigation. But state courts are harder to control, and programs in states with active litigation are making decisions their counterparts elsewhere are not.

What's Next

The Tenth Circuit will rule when it rules. Signing Day proceeds on November 11 regardless. Programs will make their best decisions with the information available, and recruits will sign with the understanding that roster math may shift before they ever set foot on campus. For coaches navigating this environment, the court's silence is uncomfortable but manageable. The alternative — planning for a ruling that hasn't come — is paralysis, and no one can afford that with November 11 nine days away.
