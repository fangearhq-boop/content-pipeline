Texas Tech Softball: No. 1 Portal Class, Kasidi Pickering, and Nine Days to Change Everything

By Sarah Mitchell | November 2, 2026 | I Love Softball

Nine days from now, National Signing Day will officially open the gates for programs to formally sign their 2027 recruiting classes. Across college softball, there is no program more compelling to watch than Texas Tech. Head coach Gerry Glasco has assembled the No. 1 portal class in the country and added one of the most decorated hitters in recent memory in Kasidi Pickering. What happens on November 11 could complete the transformation of the Red Raiders from rising program into legitimate national title contender.

What Pickering Brings to Lubbock

Pickering spent three seasons at Oklahoma, where she hit .384 with 50 home runs and 169 RBI. Those are not numbers that need context — they are numbers that define careers. During her time in Norman, she played alongside Rylie Kavan and Kendall Wells on one of the most talented rosters in program history. She chose to transfer to Texas Tech this summer, and her decision immediately reframed the conversation around the Red Raiders.

Pickering plays outfield and brings a left-handed bat that generates elite power. At Texas Tech, she becomes the kind of hitter that opposing pitchers build game plans around. In a program still building its national reputation, that kind of proven offensive production is transformational.

The No. 1 Portal Class

Pickering was the centerpiece but not the whole story. Texas Tech assembled a portal class that ranked No. 1 nationally, adding depth across multiple positions. Glasco, who has a well-established reputation as a recruiter after his time at LSU and Georgia, used his connections throughout the SEC and Big 12 to identify and land pieces that fit the Red Raiders' system.

Texas Tech competes in the Big 12, where they share a conference with Oklahoma, Texas, and Arizona — all programs with recent WCWS appearances or championships. Building a roster that can compete at that level requires not just star power but depth, and the portal haul addresses both.

The Signing Day Equation

November 11 represents the opportunity to add the next class — the 2028 high school freshmen and transfers who will arrive in the summer of 2027. For Texas Tech, a strong signing day would signal that the program's rise is attracting top recruits at a level that can sustain itself beyond the portal. The best programs recruit both ways: landing established talent through transfers while also developing incoming freshmen into the stars of tomorrow.

One complicating factor across the sport is the 5-for-5 eligibility litigation. The NCAA's rule requiring athletes to complete five years of eligibility within five calendar years is currently in effect under a Tenth Circuit stay, but the court has not ruled on the merits since October 13 oral arguments. Programs must make scholarship decisions without knowing whether additional fifth-year players will become available, which complicates roster math at every program, including Texas Tech.

What's Next

Texas Tech's November 11 signing class will be announced alongside those of programs across the country. For a program in Glasco's position, the week of November 11 is the equivalent of a March Madness bracket reveal — the moment when months of recruiting work becomes public. With Pickering leading the portal class and a strong fall behind the team, the Red Raiders enter the signing period as one of the most-watched programs in the sport. Nine days will reveal what 2027 looks like in Lubbock.
