EE100 Southeast Hitters Hotlist — Class of 2028 Day 2 Breaks Down the SEC's Next Wave

By Jordan Reeves | November 2, 2026 | I Love Softball

Extra Inning Softball's Class of 2028 regional hitters rollout continues today with the Southeast Top 100, the second of eight regional lists scheduled to drop between November 1 and November 6. If yesterday's Northeast release was the opening act, today's is the main event. The Southeast is, by every measure, the most competitive recruiting region in college softball, feeding programs across the SEC and Big 12 with the athletes who will be competing for national championships in four years.

Why the Southeast Matters Most

The Southeast region as defined by Extra Inning Softball encompasses Georgia, Florida, Alabama, Tennessee, Mississippi, and the Carolinas — the heart of SEC softball country. More top-25 national recruits come out of this region than any other, and the 2028 class is no different. Programs from Florida, Tennessee, Alabama, and Georgia have traditionally dominated regional recruiting, turning their home-state pipeline into sustained national title contention.

For the Class of 2028, the stakes are especially high. With 1,200-plus nominations making this the largest class in Extra Inning Softball history, the Southeast list is expected to be deep at every position. The hitters who crack the regional Top 100 today represent the programs that will be competing in Norman, Tucson, and Oklahoma City in the 2031 Women's College World Series.

The Bigger Picture: National Rankings Take Shape

The regional hitters rollout does not operate in isolation. Extra Inning Softball's Extra Elite 100 — the top overall ranking for the class — was released earlier this fall in its positional categories. No. 1 overall prospect Avery Landers, an infielder committed to Texas, leads the class nationally. No. 2 Ellie Holden is committed to Florida, and No. 3 Mia Schlader — an infielder with a 70-plus mph riseball — is bound for Oklahoma.

The regional hotlists serve a different purpose than the EE100. While the national rankings identify the absolute top of the class, the regional lists surface talent that may have flown under the national radar. Programs in the Southeast know this better than anyone — some of the best hitters to ever wear SEC uniforms first built their reputations on regional hotlists before climbing into national conversations.

After today's Southeast release, the schedule continues with Midwest on November 3, Southwest on November 4, Mountain on November 5, and West Coast on November 6. The national Top 25 hitters lists drop November 7 and 8, with the Pitcher-Hitter Duo Top 25 closing the rollout on November 9.

What Programs Are Watching For

For coaches who have already extended offers to the 2028 class, today's release is as much about validation as discovery. Seeing a committed recruit appear on the Southeast Top 100 — especially high in the list — strengthens confidence in a class that won't officially sign until November 2027. For programs still competing for top regional prospects, the list is a roadmap for the next twelve months of in-home visits, campus trips, and official visit planning.

The 2028 class is drawing early attention because it follows what many evaluators consider the deepest class in recent memory. The 1,200-plus nominations suggest a wide base of talent, and the Southeast's contribution to that total is expected to be as large as ever.

What's Next

The Class of 2028 regional hitters rollout continues through November 6, with national lists to follow. Programs and fans can track the full schedule on extrainningsoftball.com. For the current crop of college sophomores and juniors, these lists help set expectations about how the next recruiting cycle will unfold. One more year of rankings, then official visits, then the November 2027 signing window. The process starts with lists like today's Southeast Top 100 — and today, the Southeast's best hitters get their moment.
