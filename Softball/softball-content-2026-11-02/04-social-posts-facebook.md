#### Long-Form Post -- 3:00 PM ET

```
Here's where the NCAA's 5-for-5 eligibility case stands right now — and why it still matters as Signing Day approaches.

On October 13, the Tenth Circuit Court of Appeals heard oral arguments on whether the NCAA's 5-for-5 rule — requiring athletes to complete five years of eligibility within five calendar years — is constitutional. That was 20 days ago.

No ruling has been issued.

This is not unusual for an appellate court. Federal circuits can take weeks or months to issue opinions after arguments. What matters is what's in place in the meantime: the Tenth Circuit's stay from August 21 remains in effect, which means the 5-for-5 rule is currently being enforced.

The real-world impact is straightforward. Programs building their 2027 rosters must operate as if the rule is law, even though a ruling could change everything. Recruits signing on November 11 are committing to programs that may have different roster shapes by January.

At least 26 lawsuits have been filed in state and county courts across the country challenging the rule. In Texas, a McLennan County court already allowed 39 players to regain eligibility.

The court will rule when it rules. Until then, softball coaches are making scholarship decisions in the dark.
```
