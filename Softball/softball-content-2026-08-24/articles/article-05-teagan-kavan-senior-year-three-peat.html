Teagan Kavan's Senior Year: The Weight of History Starts Now

By Jordan Reeves | I Love Softball | August 24, 2026

Teagan Kavan returns to Austin for her senior year carrying an achievement no pitcher in Women's College World Series history has ever held: two consecutive Most Outstanding Player awards. She won the first in 2025, leading Texas to its first national title. She won the second in 2026, leading the Longhorns to a back-to-back championship and a 49-12 season. Now she enters fall camp as the anchor of a program that believes a third straight championship — a three-peat that only two teams in the sport's history have ever accomplished — is within reach.

The Historical Context

Winning three consecutive WCWS titles requires a kind of sustained excellence that only two programs have achieved. UCLA won three straight from 1988 to 1990 under a different era of the sport. Oklahoma won three straight from 2021 to 2023, the most recent dynasty in college softball history. Texas is now entering the season with back-to-back titles and a roster that may be stronger than the one that won the last two.

Kavan's role in both championships was central. In 2025, she took the ball in the biggest moments and delivered. In 2026, she did it again — and earned the MOP recognition that validated what coaches, players, and fans already knew. No one in WCWS history had been named Most Outstanding Player in back-to-back years before Kavan did it. She enters her senior season as the undisputed anchor of the program.

The Roster Around Her

The case that Texas enters 2027 stronger than when it won in 2026 begins with one name: Isa Torres.

Torres is from Georgetown, Texas. She played the 2026 season at Florida State, where she hit .530 — breaking FSU's single-season records in batting average, slugging (.978), and on-base percentage (.591). She scored 78 runs, hit 16 home runs, and drove in 56. She won the 2026 D1Softball National Player of the Year award, the NFCA First-Team All-American honor, the ACC Player of the Year, the ACC Defensive Player of the Year, and the Gold Glove. And then she came home — transferring to Texas on June 18.

Torres will compete at shortstop alongside Vivianna Martinez, adding a historically productive offensive bat at a position that was already strong. She provides lineup protection for Katie Stewart, who set the Texas school record with 31 home runs in 2026. The combination of Torres in the infield and Stewart in the lineup creates a run-scoring environment that opponents will have no easy answer for.

The pitching additions reinforce what Kavan anchors. Samantha Lincoln, a left-handed pitcher from Texas Tech, provides a different look in the circle. Samantha Swan, a catcher from Arizona State, strengthens the defensive battery behind Kavan and the staff.

What's Next

Fall camp is underway in Austin. The three-peat chase does not start in Tallahassee or Norman or Tuscaloosa — it starts now, in fall practice, before any opponent sees this roster. Kavan's senior year has begun. The weight of history is part of the deal. But for a pitcher who has carried that weight twice already and won both times, this fall is simply the beginning of the next chapter in a run that very few programs in college softball have ever attempted — and almost none have completed.
