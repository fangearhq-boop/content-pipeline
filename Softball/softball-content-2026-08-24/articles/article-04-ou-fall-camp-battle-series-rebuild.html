Inside OU's Fall Camp: The Battle Series Is How Gasso Rebuilds From the Inside Out

By Emily Rawlings | I Love Softball | August 24, 2026

Oklahoma softball is in the middle of one of its more complex fall camps in recent memory. The roster has been rebuilt. The familiar names from the WCWS run are largely gone — Kasidi Pickering transferred to Texas Tech, Tia Milloy is no longer in the program. Four of the biggest remaining names — Alyssa Brito, Jayda Coleman, Tiare Jennings, and Kelly Maxwell — are away competing with the USA WNT through September 16. And yet Patty Gasso is already running competitive evaluation through the structure that defines how Oklahoma falls: the Battle Series.

Six intrasquad scrimmages. Aggregate scoring tracked across all games. Competition built into the calendar from day one.

What the Battle Series Does

The Battle Series is not practice with a scoreboard tacked on. Gasso uses the intrasquad format to simulate game situations — the kind of competitive pressure where players are fighting for lineup spots, pitching roles, and defensive assignments rather than just getting reps. Aggregate scoring across six scrimmages means a single dominant performance by one player doesn't automatically determine anything; consistency across the series matters.

For a roster with as many new faces as this one has, the Battle Series accelerates the evaluation timeline. It's the fastest way to find out which additions can contribute immediately and which returning players have elevated their games over the offseason.

The New Additions Being Evaluated

Sydney Berzon is the headline pitching addition. The two-time All-American from LSU brings elite experience to a pitching staff that lost significant production after the WCWS miss. Her arrival changes what OU's pitching rotation can look like in 2027.

Macie Harter — transfer from Middle Tennessee State with a .346 batting average, 7 home runs, and 23 RBIs — provides a proven offensive presence at a position of need. Both Berzon and Harter were active in the transfer portal window and both represent high-ceiling additions for a program trying to return to the WCWS after its first absence since 2015.

Ori Mailo, the No. 4 overall prospect in the 2027 class, enrolled early and is already on campus participating in fall camp. Her presence as an infielder gives Gasso an additional competitive option in defensive alignment decisions. Payton Westra, ranked No. 2 overall in the 2027 class as an outfielder, is expected to join the program ahead of the spring season.

Building Without the Four Sooners

One of the unusual dynamics of OU's fall camp is that four of the roster's most accomplished players aren't there yet. Brito, Coleman, Jennings, and Maxwell are with the USA WNT competing at the WBSC World Cup Group Oklahoma stage through September 16. Their absence creates an extended opportunity for the new faces and younger players to define themselves before the full roster assembles.

This dynamic is familiar ground for Gasso. Building identity in a fall camp before the senior players arrive is a challenge, but it's also an opportunity — players who compete well in September establish themselves before October's exhibition schedule begins.

What's Next

Oklahoma's fall exhibition schedule opens October 4 and runs through November 9. By the time the exhibitions begin, Gasso will have completed the Battle Series, the WNT players will have returned, and the 2027 roster picture will be substantially clearer. The program that went to its first WCWS miss since 2015 has rebuilt aggressively. Fall is where that rebuild begins competing — and where Gasso finds out who she has.
