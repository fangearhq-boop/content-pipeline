### STORY 2: September 1 Countdown — 8 Days

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 11:00 AM ET

Eight days to September 1 — and here's the part of the recruiting calendar that not enough people talk about.

September 1 is when D1 coaches can call, text, and email Class of 2028 recruits for the first time. That's the headline date. But eight days later — September 9 at 7:00 AM ET — the in-person contact window opens.

That second date changes everything.

From September 9, coaches can show up at your games, your practices, and your events. They can sit in the stands and watch you play. Official visits and unofficial visits become permitted. For many recruits — especially those whose programs haven't been calling as much as expected — the September 9 experience of seeing a coach in the stands is the moment that tells them exactly where they stand.

Here's what that means for the next eight days: you have two preparation jobs. Job one: know which programs you want to hear from on September 1, and make sure they know you're expecting that call. A brief check-in message right now — "I'm looking forward to talking September 1, this is a priority program for me" — reinforces your standing on a board where order matters.

Job two: update your film through the most recent games, and make sure your schedule for September 9 onward is public. Coaches who are evaluating you for the in-person window need to know where to find you.

Eight days. Two windows. One recruiting class.

### STORY 4: Oklahoma Softball — The Battle Series Begins

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 1:00 PM ET

If you want to understand how Patty Gasso evaluates her rebuilt roster, look at the fall structure.

Oklahoma's fall camp is built around the Battle Series — six intrasquad scrimmages played throughout the fall with aggregate scoring tracked across all games. It's not just practice. It's competition with consequences. Players compete for lineup spots, pitching roles, and defensive assignments in game-like settings from the first day of fall.

For a roster that looks as different as this one — Sydney Berzon (two-time All-American pitcher from LSU) and Macie Harter (.346 BA transfer from MTSU) as headline additions, alongside Ori Mailo (No. 4 overall 2027 commit already on campus) and Payton Westra (No. 2 overall 2027 commit) — the Battle Series is the first real sorting mechanism.

There's a complicating factor: four of Oklahoma's most accomplished players — Alyssa Brito, Jayda Coleman, Tiare Jennings, and Kelly Maxwell — are away representing the USA WNT at the WBSC World Cup Group Stage through September 16. Fall camp begins without them.

That means the new additions and younger players get an extended first look in competitive settings before the full roster assembles. For players like Berzon and Mailo, this is an opportunity to establish their roles early.

The fall exhibition schedule opens October 4 and runs through November 9. By the time it ends, Gasso will know what she has.

### STORY 5: Texas Softball — Kavan's Final Chapter

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 8:00 PM ET

No player in Women's College World Series history has won Most Outstanding Player twice.

Teagan Kavan has done it in back-to-back years. She's a senior. And she's back at Texas to try to do something only two programs in the history of the sport have done: win three consecutive national championships.

The company is exclusive. UCLA won three straight from 1988 to 1990. Oklahoma won three straight from 2021 to 2023. Texas is trying to join that list.

The roster around Kavan heading into 2027 is arguably the most complete it's been during the Longhorns' back-to-back run. Isa Torres — the 2026 D1Softball National Player of the Year — transferred from Florida State, where she hit .530 with 16 home runs and 78 runs in a single season. She broke FSU's single-season records in batting average, slugging, and on-base percentage. She grew up in Georgetown, Texas. Coming to Austin, for Torres, was coming home.

Katie Stewart set the Texas school record with 31 home runs in 2026. She provides another dangerous bat in a lineup now anchored by Torres. Samantha Swan (catcher, ASU) and Samantha Lincoln (lefty pitcher, TTU) round out the additions.

Fall camp is underway. The three-peat chase has started.
