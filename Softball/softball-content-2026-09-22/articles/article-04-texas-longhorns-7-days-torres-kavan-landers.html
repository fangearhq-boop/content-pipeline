7 Days Out: Torres and Kavan Lead Texas's Three-Peat Bid at McCombs

By Jordan Reeves September 22, 2026

The Roster That Could Do Something Historic

One week from today, Texas softball opens fall ball at McCombs Field. The opponent is Murray State. The game is Sept. 29 at 6 PM CT and admission is free. What will not be free is the attention this roster commands, because the Longhorns are assembling a program that does not come around often.

Texas won back-to-back Women's College World Series titles in 2025 and 2026. The 2026 team went 53-12. A third straight title would put the Longhorns alongside UCLA (1988-90) and Oklahoma (2021-23) as the only programs in history to win three consecutive national championships. Mike White was named NFCA National Coaching Staff of the Year for the second straight season. The standard at Texas is not just to compete. It is to win everything.

Torres and Kavan: The Pairing That Makes This Real

Isa Torres is home. The shortstop from Georgetown, Texas hit .530 with 16 home runs and 56 RBI at Florida State in 2026, winning the D1Softball Player of the Year award. She is the best player in college softball coming off last season. Choosing Texas was a homecoming. The motivation to win a title at home may be the biggest advantage White has this fall.

Teagan Kavan returns for her senior season. She is the only player in Women's College World Series history to win the Most Outstanding Player award twice. Two WCWS titles, two MOP awards. If Texas wins a third title, Kavan's legacy as the greatest player in the tournament's history gets cemented. She knows exactly what is at stake.

Together, Torres and Kavan form the kind of tandem that programs build five-year plans around. Both are on campus now. The fall games will be the first chance to see how they fit.

Landers Commits: The Future Matches the Present

This week brought news that Michaela Landers, Softball America's No. 1 overall prospect in the 2028 class, committed to Texas. The Waxahachie, TX native hit .639 with 25 home runs in 2026 for her high school team. She is two years away from playing at McCombs, but her commitment signals that the pipeline matches the product on the field right now.

White is not just winning today. He is building a program that expects to contend every year. Landers arriving in 2028 while the current roster chases a three-peat in 2027 is exactly the kind of momentum a dynasty sustains.

What's Next

Sept. 29 at McCombs Field vs. Murray State is the first test. Fans who want a free look at this roster should circle that date. After that, Texas plays Texas State (Oct. 13), Temple (Oct. 20), McLennan (Oct. 27), UTSA (Oct. 29), and finishes the fall at Texas A&M's Davis Diamond (Nov. 6). By November, White will know exactly what this team is and what it needs heading into the spring of a three-peat chase.
