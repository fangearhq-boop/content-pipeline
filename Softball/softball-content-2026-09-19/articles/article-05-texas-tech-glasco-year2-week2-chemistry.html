Glasco Year 2, Week 2: Texas Tech's Chemistry Lab Is Open and the Results Are Coming

By Sarah Mitchell | I Love Softball | September 19, 2026

Seven All-Americans, three program cultures, and one mission — Week 2 of Tim Glasco's second fall camp at Texas Tech is the real test of whether all that talent can become a team.

The talent is in Lubbock. It has been there since June, when the transfers arrived and the rosters were finalized. Week 2 of fall camp is when the talent has to become a team — and in Tim Glasco's chemistry lab, the results of that process are starting to come into view.

Texas Tech enters the second week of fall camp with a roster headlined by seven All-Americans competing together under one coaching staff for the first time. For a program that was the 2026 WCWS runner-up, the question was never whether the pieces were good enough. The question is whether they fit.

Three Programs, One Team

The integration challenge at Texas Tech in 2026-27 is genuinely unprecedented in college softball. Three elite programs — Florida State, Oklahoma and LSU — each contributed marquee players to Glasco's roster. Each of those programs has its own culture, its own language, its own way of playing the game.

Nicole Beachum arrived from FSU carrying two All-American honors and the 2022 National Freshman of the Year title. She hit .406 with 10 home runs and 69 RBIs in 2026, posting a .549 on-base percentage with 54 walks and just eight strikeouts. That is the profile of a complete hitter — one shaped over multiple years in Tallahassee's system.

Kasidi Pickering made the trip from Oklahoma, where she was ranked No. 2 overall among all transfer portal participants by Softball America. She hit .384 with 50 home runs at OU and drew at least 40 walks in each of her last three collegiate seasons — a level of disciplined power that makes her one of the most complete hitters in the country.

Haley Heavener arrived from LSU, where she posted a 13-9 record with a 2.81 ERA and 128 strikeouts. The left-handed pitcher represents the kind of versatile, innings-eating presence that Glasco can build a rotation around. Her LSU pedigree — and her 280 total strikeouts across two seasons in Baton Rouge — brings a different kind of competitive edge to the Lubbock program.

The Full Seven

Beyond Beachum, Pickering and Heavener, the Red Raiders' seven-All-American group includes Kaitlyn Terry, Mia Williams, Taylor Pannell and Moriah Polar — each bringing their own credentials and history to the program. The fact that all seven are practicing together for the first time in program history is itself a story. Making that collection of individual excellence into something greater than the sum of its parts is Glasco's challenge heading into his second year.

Year 2 carries a distinct advantage over Year 1: Glasco's culture, vocabulary and expectations are no longer new. The players who were in Lubbock last fall have already learned the system. The newcomers are learning from veterans who can translate. That second-year acceleration is the intangible that separates Year 2 from Year 1 — and it is part of why expectation around Texas Tech in 2027 is so high.

Runner-Up Hunger

Texas Tech was the 2026 WCWS runner-up after falling to Texas in the championship series. That loss does not define the program — but it fuels it. Every drill in fall camp, every chemistry-building session in Week 2, carries the memory of Oklahoma City and the unfinished business that memory represents. Programs that reach the final and fall have a choice: rebuild or reload. Glasco chose to reload, and then some.

What's Next

Texas Tech's fall schedule will begin in the coming weeks, providing the first external test of the chemistry being built in Lubbock. The results of fall camp will shape expectations heading into a 2027 season that arrives with national title ambitions after last year's runner-up finish. Week 2 is the moment where the work starts to show — and in Lubbock, they are watching closely for what the lab produces.
