10 Days: Texas Longhorns Build Toward Sept. 29 With Torres, Kavan and a Three-Peat in Sight

By Jordan Reeves | I Love Softball | September 19, 2026

The back-to-back defending champions are 10 days from the most-anticipated fall opener in college softball, led by the Torres–Kavan partnership and a program chasing history.

Ten days. Double digits, but just barely. And for college softball fans who have been waiting all summer to see Isa Torres in burnt orange, the count cannot move fast enough.

Texas — 53-12, back-to-back Women's College World Series champions — opens its 2026 fall schedule against Murray State on September 29 at McCombs Field. Game time is 6 PM CT. Admission is free. What is not free is what it would mean if the Longhorns do what they are chasing: a third consecutive national title that would place them alongside the only two programs in softball history to have ever done it.

The Torres–Kavan Partnership

The headlining story of this fall and the entire 2027 Texas season is the pairing of Isa Torres and Hannah Kavan in the same lineup. These are two of the most accomplished players in the sport, united for the first time, in pursuit of something historic.

Torres won the 2026 D1Softball National Player of the Year award at Florida State, where she hit .530 with 16 home runs and 56 RBIs. She grew up in Georgetown, Texas — 30 miles south of Austin — making the decision to transfer to Texas something beyond just a roster move. It was a homecoming.

Kavan is already a legend. The only player in Women's College World Series history to win the Most Outstanding Player award twice, Kavan returns for her senior season having already achieved more at the WCWS than most players dream of. Paired with Torres at the top of the Texas lineup, the Longhorns have a 1-2 combination that opposing pitchers will spend the entire off-season scheming around.

The Three-Peat Context

In the entire history of NCAA Division I softball, only two programs have ever won three consecutive national championships: the UCLA Bruins (1988-90) and the Oklahoma Sooners (2021-23). Texas won in 2025. Texas won in 2026. September 29 is the first public moment of the season that ends, or does not end, with a third trophy.

The pressure of that context does not seem to bother head coach Mike White, who won the NFCA Coaching Staff of Year award for the second consecutive season. White has built a program that handles expectations as part of the job description — and this fall's roster gives him every reason to be confident heading into the spring.

The Fall Calendar

Texas will play six games in its 2026 fall schedule. After the Sept. 29 opener against Murray State at McCombs, the Longhorns face Texas State on Oct. 13, Temple on Oct. 20, McLennan on Oct. 27, UTSA on Oct. 29, and conclude against Texas A&M at Davis Diamond on November 6 at 7 PM CT. The fall slate is as much about evaluation and integration as results — but every game is a data point for what Texas will look like when the 2027 regular season begins.

What's Next

Texas opens fall play on September 29 against Murray State at McCombs Field — free admission, 6 PM CT. For fans who want to see the Torres–Kavan partnership in a game setting for the first time, this is the moment. The six-game fall schedule runs through November 6, giving White and his staff ample opportunity to evaluate depth, develop chemistry and build the foundation for what they hope will be a historic 2027 campaign.
