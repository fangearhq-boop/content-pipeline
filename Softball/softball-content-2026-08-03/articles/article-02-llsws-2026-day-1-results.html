Southeast Ran Away From the Start. Asia-Pacific Survived the Weather. Day 1 Is Done.

By Emily Rawlings

August 3, 2026

The 2026 Little League Softball World Series got its first two results on Sunday at Stallings Stadium in Greenville, North Carolina — and both games told you something about what this tournament is going to look like over the next week.

Southeast Goes Off in the First Two Innings

Lake Mary, Florida — representing the Southeast — left no doubt in Game 1. The Floridians opened the scoring early and kept pushing, putting up 10 runs across the first two innings against Snohomish, Washington's Northwest squad. By the final out, the scoreboard read 14-0. When a team wins a World Series opener by 14 runs, there's no ambiguity about what happened.

Brynn Fieser and Callie Ousley led the offensive damage, each driving in three runs for the Southeast. Pitcher Chloe Cheek handled the rest: four innings pitched, three strikeouts, two hits, one walk, and zero runs allowed. Cheek was never in trouble. The Southeast came in ready to win and did exactly that.

Northwest's team from Snohomish, Washington earned their spot here through regional qualifying. Sunday's opener was tough, but the double-elimination format means this isn't over. A loss in the first game is survivable. What the Northwest team does next is the question.

Asia-Pacific Fights Through the Weather and the Mid-Atlantic

Game 2 between Asia-Pacific and Mid-Atlantic was a different kind of challenge. A weather delay interrupted the action mid-game, breaking the rhythm for both teams and testing their ability to refocus once the skies cleared. Asia-Pacific — representing Sangō, Japan — navigated the stop better. They came away with the win over Toms River, New Jersey's Mid-Atlantic squad.

The weather that affected Game 2 at Stallings Stadium also prompted schedule adjustments for Monday's games. Tournament officials adapted the Day 2 slate to account for the disruptions, so the schedule for today may look different from what was originally posted. The full adjusted schedule will be available through Little League's official channels and the ESPN broadcast listing.

What the Double-Elimination Format Means for Day 1 Losers

Both Snohomish, Washington (Northwest) and Toms River, New Jersey (Mid-Atlantic) dropped their opening games, but they're not out of the tournament. The LLSWS uses a double-elimination format within two brackets — Purple and Orange — and a loss in the first round drops a team into the losers' bracket, not out of contention.

In a world series format, early game results set bracket paths but rarely determine champions in the first two days. Northwest and Mid-Atlantic will need to string together wins in the losers' bracket to stay alive. That's a harder road — but it's a road that's still open.

Day 2 Action Continues Today

Stallings Stadium at Elm Street Park in Greenville, North Carolina hosts a full adjusted slate today, August 3. Sunday's rain also forced the postponement of Game 4 — Canada vs. North Carolina — which was originally scheduled for 7:00 PM ET Sunday but has been moved to 9:00 AM ET Monday. That game kicks off today's action at Stallings Stadium before the rest of the Day 2 schedule unfolds.

All games remain on the ESPN family of networks — and free admission continues for all fans at Stallings Stadium. For a tournament featuring 12 teams competing for a world championship, that kind of access is something worth noting. The tournament runs through August 9. There is still a lot of softball left to play at Stallings Stadium.
