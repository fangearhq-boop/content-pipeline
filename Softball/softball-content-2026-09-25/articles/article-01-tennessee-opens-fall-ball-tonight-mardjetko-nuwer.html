Tennessee Opens Fall Ball Tonight — Mardjetko, Nuwer Step Back Into Game Mode

By Emily Rawlings | September 25, 2026

The waiting is over.

Tonight at 6 PM CT, Tennessee opens its 2026 fall softball schedule against Walters State at Sherri Parker Lee Stadium — and for the first time since the 2026 Women's College World Series, Sage Mardjetko and Erin Nuwer will be in game action together. Admission is free. The gates open an hour before first pitch. No concessions, but that shouldn't stop anyone from making the trip to Knoxville.

What These Two Did Together Last Spring

Mardjetko was one of the best pitchers in college softball in 2026. She finished 16-3 with a 1.26 ERA and 171 strikeouts, earning NFCA First Team All-America honors in the process. She was reliable, dominant, and consistent throughout a full season — exactly what a program needs from its ace.

Nuwer was the quieter half of the duo, but no less effective. Her 2026 line: 15-1 with a 1.01 ERA and 90 strikeouts. Together, the pair combined for 31 wins and 8 shutouts. That production from two pitchers — both returning — is the kind of starting point that sends programs into the preseason ranked in the top five.

Both are back for 2027. Both return to the mound tonight for the first real evaluation of where they are.

Nine Starters Coming Back

The pitching is the headline, but the Lady Vols aren't a pitching-only team heading into 2027. Tennessee returns nine starters from the 2026 WCWS squad. Sophia Knight, who led all UT players with a .394 batting average last season, is back. So are Emma Clarke (14 home runs), Alannah Leach (13 home runs), and Gabby Leach (10 home runs). That's four of the team's most productive offensive contributors returning to a lineup that made life difficult for opposing pitchers all spring.

Fall ball is typically not the place to draw sweeping conclusions about a program. The competition is lighter. The rosters are experimental. Stats don't count. But for a team with nine returning starters and the most accomplished returning pitching duo in the country, this fall isn't about questions — it's about confirmation.

What the Fall Schedule Looks Like

After tonight's opener against Walters State, Tennessee hosts Lipscomb on September 27 and Carson-Newman on October 2. The Lady Vols have seven home games scheduled at Sherri Parker Lee Stadium through October 23, with one road game at Murfreesboro on October 18 against Middle Tennessee. It's a full fall slate that gives the staff a genuine evaluation window before the spring season begins in February.

The programs watching Tennessee most closely — Texas, Oklahoma, UCLA, and Florida — are all doing the same thing right now: running their new rosters out in fall games and figuring out what they have. For Tennessee, the starting point is stronger than almost everyone else's. Nine starters. Two of the best pitchers in the country. The framework of a top-five program in place.

What's Next

Tonight's game is the opening chapter of a fall that could shape expectations for the entire 2027 season. If Mardjetko and Nuwer look like themselves early — and there's no reason to think they won't — Tennessee will enter winter ranked near the top of every preseason poll. The Lady Vols host Lipscomb on Sunday and Carson-Newman the following Thursday. The picture will get clearer with each game. For now, it starts tonight at 6 PM CT. Free admission. First pitch in Knoxville.
