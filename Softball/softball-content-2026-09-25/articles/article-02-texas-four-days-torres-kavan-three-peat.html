Texas Longhorns Softball — Four Days Until Torres and Kavan Take the Field Together

By Sarah Mitchell | September 25, 2026

In four days, two of the most accomplished softball players in the country will share a dugout for the first time in a game that matters.

Isa Torres and Teagan Kavan take the field together on Monday when Texas opens its 2026 fall ball slate against Murray State at McCombs Field. First pitch at 6 PM CT. Free admission. For Texas fans, it's the first real look at a roster built to do what only two programs in history have ever done.

The Player of the Year Comes Home

Torres spent the 2026 season at Florida State, where she was the D1Softball National Player of the Year. She hit .530 with 16 home runs and 56 RBI. Those are the numbers of a generational player — and she's a Georgetown, Texas native who transferred to the Longhorns for her senior season. This is a homecoming in every sense of the word.

She joins a program that won the last two national championships. That's not a transition, it's an alignment of elite talent with an elite program at the exact right moment.

Kavan's Place in History

Kavan is entering her senior season as the only player in Women's College World Series history to win back-to-back Most Outstanding Player awards. She did it in 2025. She did it again in 2026. Softball America named her the No. 1 player on its Top 100 list for the 2026 season. There are no higher individual accolades available to a college softball player, and she's collected them consecutively.

When Torres and Kavan share a lineup, opposing coaches will have no clean answer. Two of the most decorated players in the country, at the same program, at the same time.

The Three-Peat Target

It has happened exactly twice in the history of college softball. UCLA won three consecutive national titles from 1988 to 1990. Oklahoma did it from 2021 to 2023. Texas is trying to become the third program to accomplish it.

The path runs through the toughest schedule in the country. The 2027 Longhorns face Tennessee, Alabama, and Arkansas on back-to-back-to-back SEC weekends in the spring. The margin for error is thin. The additions of Samantha Swan (a senior catcher from Arizona State) and Samantha Lincoln (a junior left-handed pitcher from Texas Tech) give the roster additional depth around the core, but the work won't be easy.

It's never supposed to be easy. That's the point.

What's Next

Monday's opener against Murray State is the starting point. McCombs Field, 6 PM CT, no cost at the gate. After that, Texas works through a six-game fall schedule before the real preparation for 2027 begins. The roster is in place. The coaching staff is intact. Torres and Kavan are on the same team. Three-peats don't happen often. Monday is when the attempt begins in earnest.
