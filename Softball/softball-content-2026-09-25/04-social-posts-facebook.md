### STORY 2: Texas Longhorns — 4 Days to Opener

#### FB Long-Form Post

Four days from right now, Isa Torres will walk onto McCombs Field as a Texas Longhorn for the first time in a game.

Torres was the D1Softball National Player of the Year in 2026. She hit .530 with 16 home runs and 56 RBI while at Florida State. She's a Georgetown, Texas native — this is truly a homecoming. And she's joining the defending back-to-back national champions.

Her teammate will be Teagan Kavan, a senior who is literally the only player in Women's College World Series history to win back-to-back Most Outstanding Player awards. Two in a row. Never been done before or since. Kavan and Torres together could be the most decorated 1-2 combination in the country this spring.

Texas is chasing a three-peat. It has happened only twice in college softball history — UCLA in 1988-90 and Oklahoma in 2021-23. The Longhorns face a brutal SEC gauntlet in 2027 (Tennessee, Alabama, Arkansas on consecutive weekends), but they're built to weather it.

Fall opener: Monday, September 29. McCombs Field. 6 PM CT. Free admission. Come see what this roster looks like.

### STORY 4: Asian Games Softball Opens Tomorrow in Japan

#### FB Long-Form Post

Tomorrow, the 2026 Asian Games softball competition opens in Anjō, Japan — and for fans of the international game, this is a must-watch event.

Eight nations will compete at Anjō Sports Park Softball Ground, with the tournament running through October 3. Japan enters as the world's No. 1 ranked team and is the heavy favorite on home soil. Chinese Taipei (No. 5 globally) and China (No. 8) represent the most credible challengers to Japan's dominance.

The Philippines (No. 13) is one of the most compelling stories — Southeast Asian softball has grown significantly in recent years, and the Philippines' presence at the Asian Games is a reflection of that development. South Korea (No. 31), Singapore (No. 22), Hong Kong (No. 32), and Thailand (No. 44) round out the field.

The stakes extend beyond a gold medal: the Asian Games carry LA28 Olympic qualification pathway implications, which means every game matters for teams looking to build points toward Los Angeles 2028.

Japan last won the Olympic gold medal in Tokyo (2020 Games). They'll be attempting to use the Asian Games as a springboard for that ultimate goal.

Gold medal game: October 3.
