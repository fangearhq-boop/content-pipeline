It's Game Day at McCombs Field: Isa Torres and Teagan Kavan Debut Together as Texas Opens Fall Ball

By Emily Rawlings | September 29, 2026

The offseason is over. Tonight, Isa Torres and Teagan Kavan play softball together for the first time.

Texas opens its 2026 fall schedule against Murray State College at McCombs Field in Austin — Tuesday, September 29, 6:00 PM CT, free admission. It is the first public appearance for the two-time defending national champions and the first chance for softball fans to see the Longhorns' most anticipated transfer pairing play in the same orange uniform.

There are fall openers across the country this week. This one is different.

Why Tonight Matters

Isa Torres hit .530 last season at Florida State — a number that requires a second read to fully register. She was named the 2026 national Player of the Year, the best hitter in college softball, and she chose to transfer to Texas.

Teagan Kavan won the Women's College World Series Most Outstanding Player award in both 2025 and 2026. No player in WCWS history had ever won it twice before Kavan did it back-to-back. She returns for another year in Austin.

These two players have never been on the same team. Tonight changes that. For any softball fan who has been tracking this offseason, the Murray State game is appointment viewing — not for the opponent, but for the first glimpse of what the 2027 Texas lineup could look like when it matters.

The Three-Peat Is the Goal

Texas won back-to-back Women's College World Series titles in 2025 and 2026, sweeping Texas Tech in the 2026 Championship Series on June 3-4. Now they are pursuing something that only two programs in the history of college softball have accomplished: three consecutive national championships.

UCLA won it from 1988-90. Oklahoma won three straight from 2021-23. No one has done it since — and the sport has become deeper, more competitive, and more transfer-portal-driven in the years since the Sooners completed their run. For Texas to join that list, they would need to navigate a field that will be coming for them from opening day of the 2027 season.

The coaching staff knows it. The players know it. Tonight's fall opener is the first step in that chase — even if Murray State is not the measuring stick for where this team is heading.

Fall Ball Details

Texas has six games on its 2026 fall schedule. Tonight's opener at McCombs Field is the first. The atmosphere will be familiar — McCombs Field seats over 3,000 fans, and a free September game in Austin is a draw for more than just the hardcore faithful.

Fall ball games are technically exhibitions, but the coaching staff uses them to evaluate lineups, develop pitching depth, and let new players get comfortable in the system before spring competition begins. For a roster that has added Torres alongside the returning championship core, every fall rep counts as orientation.

The Longhorns do not get extra time with their new players. Tonight is how you start building the chemistry that wins championships in June.

What's Next

After tonight's opener, Texas continues its fall schedule through late October. The games are low-stakes in the standings and high-stakes in the building of a potential three-peat team.

The first impression matters. How Torres hits in burnt orange, how Kavan commands the at-bats around her, how the defense aligns — every detail from tonight's fall opener will be dissected by a softball community that has been waiting all summer for this. Show up to McCombs Field if you're in Austin. Tune in on the Texas Athletics stream if you're not. The three-peat chase starts tonight at 6 PM CT.
