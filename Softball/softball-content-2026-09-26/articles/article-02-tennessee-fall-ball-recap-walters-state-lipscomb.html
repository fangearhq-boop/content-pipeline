Tennessee Opened Fall Ball Thursday — Mardjetko and Nuwer Are Back, Lipscomb Comes Sunday

By Emily Rawlings | September 26, 2026

It took all of one game for the wait to officially end.

Tennessee opened its 2026 fall softball schedule Thursday evening against Walters State at Sherri Parker Lee Stadium — and for the first time since the 2026 Women's College World Series, Sage Mardjetko and Erin Nuwer were back in game action together. For Lady Vols fans who have been waiting since late June to see what this program looks like heading into 2027, Thursday night was the first piece of real evidence.

There will be more to come quickly. Lipscomb visits Sherri Parker Lee Stadium on Sunday, and Tennessee's full eight-game fall slate gives the coaching staff a steady evaluation window across the next two months.

The Pitching Tandem Everyone's Watching

In a fall ball environment, headlines are typically reserved for high-profile recruits and transfers getting their first live reps. But Tennessee's fall story this year is anchored by two returning veterans who have already proven they belong at the highest level of college softball.

Mardjetko posted a 16-3 record with a 1.26 ERA and 171 strikeouts in 2026, earning NFCA First Team All-America honors. Nuwer's line was nearly as good: 15-1 with a 1.01 ERA and 90 strikeouts. Together, they combined for 31 wins and 8 shutouts — a production figure that would be the envy of virtually every program in the country. Both are back for 2027. Both pitched Thursday. And both will see plenty of action through October.

The question every Tennessee observer wants answered over the fall: where are they, physically and mechanically, after a full offseason? Fall ball is not the place to draw permanent conclusions — competition is lighter, pressure is lower, and the rosters are different. But it is the first data point, and for a pitching tandem that significant, even a small data point matters.

Nine Starters Give the Lady Vols a Deep Foundation

The pitching duo is the headliner, but this isn't a Tennessee team built around two arms alone. The Lady Vols bring back nine starters from the 2026 WCWS squad — a number that reflects the kind of roster continuity that makes programs dangerous year after year.

Sophia Knight (.394 batting average in 2026) anchors the offense. Emma Clarke (14 home runs) adds power production. Alannah Leach and Gabby Leach give Tennessee two more returning hitters with WCWS experience. The lineup that made the College World Series isn't starting from scratch — it's adding to an existing foundation.

Fall ball gives those nine starters something they couldn't get in the offseason: actual game reps against actual pitching, in an actual stadium. The rust is coming off in September, which means it should be largely gone by the time the 2027 regular season starts in February.

What's Ahead This Fall

After Sunday's Lipscomb game, Tennessee hosts Carson-Newman on October 2, Northeast Alabama on October 9, and Northwest Florida State College on October 10. The lone road game comes October 18 at Middle Tennessee in Murfreesboro. The full slate wraps up in late October.

It's a schedule designed for evaluation, not for marquee matchups. But the program that finished in the top eight at the College World Series, with a returning pitching staff this strong, doesn't need marquee competition in September to make a statement. They just need to look like themselves. Thursday night, and Sunday's Lipscomb game, are the beginning of that process.

What's Next

Sunday, September 27 — Lipscomb at Sherri Parker Lee Stadium. Admission is free. If Tennessee's fall opener was the first chapter, Sunday is the second. Watch Mardjetko and Nuwer continue to find their rhythm, and watch whether the returning lineup picks up where it left off at the WCWS. The 2027 national championship chase starts with these fall games, and for the Lady Vols, the early evidence suggests the foundation is solid.
