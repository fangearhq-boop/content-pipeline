Texas Is Building the Roster for a Three-Peat — Torres, Lincoln, and Bordi Are the New Pieces

By Jordan Reeves | September 26, 2026

Two national championships in two years.

For most programs, that would be the destination. For Texas softball under head coach Mike White, it's starting to feel like the foundation of something larger. Three consecutive national championships — matching UCLA's 1988-90 run and Oklahoma's 2021-23 dynasty — is the target. And the Longhorns are assembling the pieces to chase it seriously.

The fall opener is Tuesday, September 29 at McCombs Field against Murray State. 6 PM CT. Free admission. By then, the newest additions to the Texas roster will be stepping onto the field together for the first time.

The Transfer Portal Additions That Change the Equation

Mike White's stated philosophy is straightforward: build from within. The Longhorns' back-to-back titles were constructed primarily through recruiting and player development, not portal dependence. But White also knows that the transfer portal, used strategically, can add layers that recruiting alone doesn't provide.

The most significant addition: Isa Torres, the infielder who came over from Florida State with a .530 batting average in 2026 and the D1Softball National Player of the Year award on her resume. Torres is the type of player who changes lineup dynamics — a proven offensive weapon who gives opposing pitchers another problem they have to solve. Paired with a lineup that already won back-to-back titles, she becomes a multiplier.

Samantha Lincoln, a pitcher from Texas Tech, gives the rotation depth that any championship program needs. The 2026 run required reliability across multiple games at the Women's College World Series, and adding another capable arm to the mix only strengthens Texas' position when the postseason arrives.

Also in the mix: Sophia Bordi, a redshirt freshman pitcher who transferred from Oklahoma and was ineligible during the 2026 season. She becomes available for 2027 — another body in a rotation that figures to be one of the deepest in the country.

The Foundation: Kavan and the Core

The portal additions matter, but they don't overshadow what Texas already has. Teagan Kavan — the two-time WCWS Most Outstanding Player, the only player in the history of the award to win it twice — is back. She anchors everything the Longhorns do in the circle. Whatever depth Lincoln and Bordi provide, Kavan is the horse.

The offensive core that won two consecutive national championships also returns. White's "build from within" mantra has produced a culture and a skill base that doesn't disappear between seasons. The portal additions are additions — they're not replacements for what Texas already has in place.

The Historical Context

Only twice in NCAA softball history has a program won three consecutive national championships. UCLA did it from 1988 to 1990 — a different era, a different game. Oklahoma did it from 2021 to 2023, the most recent dynasty in the sport. Texas has now won two straight. The question that will define the 2027 season: can the Longhorns be the third program to reach three?

The competition is aware of the target on Texas' back. Programs across the country spent the offseason evaluating what they needed to compete with the reigning champions. Tennessee returned two elite pitchers and nine starters. Alabama brought back 13 from a WCWS semifinal team. Oklahoma rebuilt its roster around new standouts. The path to a three-peat will not be easy.

But Texas has the pieces. And they're adding more.

What's Next

Tuesday, September 29. McCombs Field. Murray State. 6 PM CT. Free. That's where the three-peat chase officially begins — in a fall exhibition, with a full semester of competition ahead before the 2027 season starts in February. Torres, Lincoln, and Kavan on the same field together for the first time. White's strategy on full display. Three-peat or bust — and right now, all the evidence suggests they're building for both.
