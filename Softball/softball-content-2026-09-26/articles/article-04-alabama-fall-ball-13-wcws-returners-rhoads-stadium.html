Alabama Returns 13 From Its 2026 WCWS Semifinal Squad — Crimson Tide Open Fall Ball in October

By Sarah Mitchell | September 26, 2026

Alabama is not starting over.

When the Crimson Tide open fall ball at Rhoads Stadium in October, they'll do it with 13 athletes returning from the squad that reached the 2026 Women's College World Series semifinals. In a sport where transfer portal attrition, graduation losses, and eligibility gaps can shrink a roster significantly from one season to the next, that retention number is extraordinary — and it's the clearest signal that Alabama expects to contend at the highest level in 2027.

The All-Americans Who Are Coming Back

The headline returners are two NFCA All-Americans who played major roles in Alabama's 2026 postseason run: Jocelyn Briski and Brooke Wells. Both earned All-America recognition based on their 2026 production. Both are back in Tuscaloosa this fall, getting ready to build on what they accomplished last spring.

Also returning: Vic Moten and Kaitlyn Pallozzi, two freshmen All-Americans who made immediate impacts in their first seasons at Alabama. For both players, 2027 represents the opportunity to build on a strong debut — with a full season of college softball experience now behind them, the expectation is a significant leap in production and consistency.

Four standout contributors returning, 13 total athletes back from a WCWS semifinal team. That's not a roster that needs to find itself. That's a roster that knows what it's capable of and needs to take the next step.

A Fall Schedule Built for Evaluation

Alabama's fall slate is set for October and November, with the majority of games at Rhoads Stadium. The schedule includes five fall ball dates, with the marquee matchup being a home game against Auburn. In-state rivalry games carry their own energy even in a fall setting, and a Rhoads Stadium showdown between Alabama and Auburn — two programs with significant Big 12 expectations — is exactly the kind of measuring-stick opportunity the coaching staff wants in the fall.

Fall ball for Alabama isn't about proving anything to the national media. It's about integrating the 13 returners with any new additions, developing the depth of the roster, and giving the coaching staff enough game footage to understand exactly what kind of team they have before February arrives.

How Close Did They Get in 2026?

Alabama reached the Women's College World Series semifinals in 2026 — one win away from the championship game. The Crimson Tide came as far as a program can come without winning it all. That semifinal exit creates a specific kind of hunger that programs either channel productively or struggle to move past.

The 13 returners suggest Alabama is choosing the former. These are players who experienced the best and most painful kind of near-miss — reaching Oklahoma City, winning games, and falling one step short. They return to Tuscaloosa with the unfinished business that has historically been the fuel for championship runs.

What's Next

Alabama's fall schedule opens in October at Rhoads Stadium. The Auburn game is the one to watch. After that, the Crimson Tide will spend November working through the final details of 2027 preparation — roster decisions, rotation assignments, and lineup configuration. For a program with 13 returners from a WCWS semifinal team, the work this fall isn't about building from scratch. It's about refining a program that's already close. One step closer. Roll Tide.
