Asian Games Women's Softball Opens in Anjō — Japan, Eight Nations Compete for LA28 Points

By Sarah Mitchell | September 26, 2026

The biggest international softball event of the fall is underway.

Women's softball at the 2026 Asian Games officially opened Saturday at Anjō Sports Park in Anjō, Japan — the start of an eight-day competition that will run through October 3. Eight nations are on the field. The finals are one week away. And for a sport preparing for its return to the Olympic stage at the 2028 Los Angeles Games, these matches carry weight beyond the medal stand.

Eight Nations, Eight Stories

The 2026 Asian Games women's softball field is the broadest the continent has fielded in years. Japan enters as the No. 1-ranked team in the world by the World Baseball Softball Confederation — and as host nation, they carry the dual pressure of expectation and home-crowd energy. Chinese Taipei, ranked No. 5 globally, is the most capable challenger to Japan's dominance. China (No. 8) has been building steadily and entered the competition as a legitimate contender for a podium spot.

The rest of the field — South Korea, the Philippines (No. 13), Hong Kong, Singapore, and Thailand — represents the breadth of softball's growth across Asia. For programs like the Philippines, competing against Japan and Chinese Taipei in the preliminary round is its own form of progress. International tournaments like this one are how the sport builds depth globally, one competitive match at a time.

Why the Stakes Are Higher Than Usual

Softball is returning to the Olympic program at the 2028 Los Angeles Games after a twelve-year absence following the sport's exclusion after Beijing 2008. That return has energized every corner of the international softball community, and the Asian Games is one of the key stages on the road to LA.

For Asian nations, establishing competitive standing in events like this one matters. The WBSC world rankings that determine tournament seeding and, eventually, Olympic qualification pathways, are shaped by performances at major international competitions. Every game Japan wins in Anjō matters. Every upset Chinese Taipei or the Philippines can manufacture matters.

The stakes, in other words, are not just regional. They are part of a longer road leading to a Los Angeles softball stadium in the summer of 2028.

The Schedule and What to Watch

Preliminary round play continues through October 2, with teams competing in a round-robin format. The Finals are scheduled for October 3. For fans tracking the competition from outside Japan, the WBSC website and the official 2026 Asian Games coverage channels are the best places to follow results in real time.

Japan is the team to beat. They have been the gold standard for international softball for more than a decade, and their home-field advantage in Anjō adds another layer. The most interesting storyline to watch: whether Chinese Taipei can match them across a full tournament, or whether any of the emerging nations — the Philippines in particular — can pull an upset in the preliminary round that reshapes the bracket heading into finals week.

What's Next

The 2026 Asian Games softball competition runs through October 3. Preliminary round results will shape the Finals bracket, and the gold medal match on October 3 will be the most high-profile international softball game of the fall season. For American fans accustomed to the college game, this tournament offers a window into the international landscape that will define the road to LA28 over the next two years. Follow along. The global game is growing, and this week in Anjō is part of that story.
