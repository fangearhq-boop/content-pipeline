### STORY 2: September 1 Countdown — 4 Days

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 11:00 AM ET

Four days.

For Class of 2028 recruits, September 1 is the date everything changes. On that morning, D1 coaches are allowed to call, text, and email prospects directly for the first time. The phones start ringing before 8 AM in many cases. The first hour of September 1 has historically been one of the most telling signals of which programs are serious about a recruit.

Right now — with four days left — there's still time to influence where you land on those lists. Coaches who watched your summer film and followed your summer season are finalizing their call-order priorities. Updates matter. Responding to messages matters. Being reachable and clearly interested in the programs you want matters.

What September 1 looks like in practice:
• Phone calls start early — before 8 AM for top priorities in many programs
• The first-hour list is usually 8-12 athletes per program
• After the first hour, coaches work through a secondary list
• The September 9 in-person window opens at 7:00 AM ET — official campus visits and in-person meetings become allowed

Four days. Use them.

### STORY 3: USA WNT — 15 Days to Group Oklahoma

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 3:00 PM ET

Fifteen days.

That's how long until the first pitch of the WBSC Women's Softball World Cup Group Stage in Oklahoma City, and the USA Softball WNT heading to Devon Park has a specific kind of team to offer — Olympic experience alongside World Games gold, veterans alongside newcomers.

Rachel Garcia and Dejah Mulipola both wear the rings from 2020. They've been in the biggest games at the international level. Seven more players on this roster helped win the 2025 World Games gold medal. That combination of proven experience is what the coaching staff is building around.

The group stakes are significant. USA (No. 2 in the world) opens against Mexico (No. 7) on September 12. The Netherlands (No. 6) game will likely decide the top seed in Group Oklahoma City. China (No. 8), New Zealand (No. 23), and South Africa (No. 36) round out the six-team field.

Only the top two of six advance to the 2027 WBSC Women's Softball World Cup Finals in Redcliffe, Queensland — a key stop on the LA28 Olympic qualification pathway.

Four Sooners (Brito, Coleman, Jennings, Maxwell) will represent Oklahoma on this roster. The whole softball world will be watching September 12-16 in OKC.

### STORY 4: Oklahoma — Gasso Coaching USA WNT

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 1:00 PM ET

This fall at Oklahoma is unlike any fall Patty Gasso has navigated.

When the WBSC Women's Softball World Cup Group Stage tips off September 12 in Oklahoma City, four of OU's best players — Alyssa Brito, Jayda Coleman, Tiare Jennings, and Kelly Maxwell — will be there competing for Team USA. That part is familiar. What's new this year: Patty Gasso, JT Gasso, and Jennifer Rocha are all serving on the USA Softball coaching staff for the event.

That means Oklahoma's entire top coaching infrastructure will be in OKC at the same time its four players are.

Back in Norman, the fall camp continues. Sydney Berzon — a two-time All-American from LSU (486.1 career IP, 2.02 ERA, 406 strikeouts) — is integrating with the group. So is Macie Harter, who hit .346 with seven home runs and 23 RBI at Middle Tennessee State. The first Battle Series scrimmage this fall drew 2,351 fans to Love's Field. Public events begin October 3.

Gasso is building two rosters at once this fall — one with the WNT four and one without. That's data no other coaching staff in the country will have before the 2027 season begins.
