Six Questions Softball Fans Will Have Answered by Christmas

By Jordan Reeves

November 20, 2026

The December transfer portal opens in 11 days. The state courts are still active. The January preseason polls are six weeks away. Between now and Christmas, college softball's most consequential questions will get answers. Here are six of them.

1. Who Wins the December Portal?

At least four programs enter the December window with a critical need: Arizona needs a pitcher (lost D1Softball National Player of the Year Mariah Torres to Texas Tech). Oklahoma needs rotation depth. Ole Miss needs a frontline arm to push into the postseason. Auburn needs pitching to convert an offensive class into a complete roster.

One of these programs will win the December portal. It will almost certainly be the one that did the most off-the-clock relationship building in October and November — because the first commitments in any portal window go to programs that had conversations before the portal opened. Watch the first 48 hours after December 1 for the clearest signal of who built the most pipeline.

2. Does Arizona Land the Pitcher They Need?

Caitlin Lowe's program went 15-2 in the fall. Big 12 Defensive Player of the Year Regan Shockey returns. The offense is capable. The hole is at pitcher, and it is a significant one. Arizona without elite pitching has a ceiling that falls well short of their talent level in every other area.

The Wildcats are likely to be the most aggressive team in the December portal, and they have the resources and facilities to make a compelling case. But they're also competing against established programs for a small pool of available arms. The answer to this question determines whether Arizona is a Big 12 contender or a Big 12 champion in 2027.

3. Who Claims No. 1 in the January Preseason Polls?

D1Softball has Oklahoma first in its signing class rankings. Softball America has LSU first — a historic first for the Tigers. Neither ranking is a preseason poll, and the January preseason polls will factor in portal additions, returning rosters, and schedule strength in ways that signing class rankings don't.

December portal movement will substantially influence those January polls. Oklahoma with an elite rotation addition is probably the consensus No. 1. LSU with a complete roster as-is is the strongest challenger. Tennessee's nine returning starters could earn a top-three slot if coaches' polls weight experience over recruiting rankings. The January announcement will reset the entire conversation.

4. Do the State Courts Produce 5-for-5 Relief?

The Tenth Circuit ruled against the 5-for-5 athletes on November 6, ending the nationwide preliminary injunction that had given 850-plus athletes an extra year of eligibility under federal protection. The ruling was a significant victory for the NCAA — but it did not end the legal battle. More than 200 state-court cases continue across multiple jurisdictions, with varying theories of liability and different standards for preliminary injunctions.

A favorable state-court ruling before year-end could reopen eligibility for Class of 2022 players in that jurisdiction. If a large state — particularly one with multiple Power programs — produces a favorable ruling, the competitive implications could be substantial. Watch for state-court activity in early December, particularly in jurisdictions where active litigation has the most players represented.

5. Who Is the First Big Name to Enter the December Portal?

Every portal window produces at least one surprise entrant — a player expected to stay at their program who tests the market instead. The December window in softball is typically smaller than the summer window, but 5-for-5 pressure and House settlement roster caps have created unusual circumstances at programs that cannot guarantee playing time to every athlete on the roster.

Programs under 5-for-5 pressure — particularly those that rostered but could not play affected athletes — are the most likely source of unexpected December entrants. When those athletes receive clarity from a court ruling or from their program about their 2027 status, some will choose the portal over staying put. The first big name to enter December 1 or 2 could change the entire conversation.

6. Can a Mid-Major Pull Off a Portal Surprise?

Georgia Tech ranked No. 32 in D1Softball's 2027 class — the highest mid-major ranking in the survey and a signal that the gap between Power programs and rising challengers is narrowing. NIL, portal access, and improved facilities have made mid-major programs legitimate landing spots for top-50 recruits for the first time in the sport's history.

The December portal could accelerate that trend. A top-50 transfer from an SEC or Big 12 program landing at a mid-major creates a headline that changes perception. It signals that the talent advantage the established programs have relied on is not permanent. If a Georgia Tech, a Washington, or a Connecticut lands a recognizable name in December, it will be the most important signal of the portal era's democratizing effect on the sport.

What's Next

The answers start arriving December 1. The portal opens at midnight. The first commitments come within hours. The state courts could rule any day before Christmas. The January preseason polls begin dropping in the first weeks of the new year. By December 25, every question on this list has at least a partial answer — and college softball's 2027 picture will be sharper than it has been all offseason. Eleven days until the action starts.
