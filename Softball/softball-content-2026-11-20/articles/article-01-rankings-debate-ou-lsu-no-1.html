D1Softball Says Oklahoma. Softball America Says LSU. Who Has the No. 1 Class in 2027?

By Emily Rawlings

November 20, 2026

Two of college softball's most respected recruiting services released their final 2027 signing class rankings after National Signing Day on November 11, and they reached different conclusions at the top. D1Softball ranked Oklahoma No. 1 in its post-signing-day standings. Softball America — posting its first-ever No. 1 for LSU — gave the Tigers the top spot nationally.

Both services are credible. Both have Oklahoma and LSU in their top two. The gap between the services isn't about bad information — it's about what they value. And for fans trying to figure out who the most dangerous program in the country will be in 2027, the answer is: it may be both.

How D1Softball Sees It

D1Softball's post-signing-day ranking places Oklahoma at No. 1, followed by LSU at No. 2, then Oregon, Auburn, and Stanford. The service emphasizes class depth and the number of elite-level recruits across all positions. Oklahoma's class fits that profile precisely.

The Sooners' haul includes five-star outfielder Finlee Williams — the No. 1-ranked outfielder in the 2027 class and the No. 3 overall prospect in D1Softball's rankings — alongside four-star catcher Riley Hilliard (No. 11 overall, No. 2 catcher) and infielder-utility standout Goose Hutchens, who reclassified and is already enrolled on campus. Texas A&M rounds out the top 10 in D1Softball's list.

The case for Oklahoma at No. 1 comes down to this: when you combine an elite recruiting class with a fully intact returning roster — including Jilian Wells (39 home runs, NFCA Freshman of the Year), Grace Kavan (two-time WCWS MVP), and 11 returning contributors — the program's overall talent level is unmatched entering 2027.

How Softball America Sees It

Softball America's ranking has LSU at No. 1 — a first in program history for the Tigers. The service places Florida second, with Auburn, Stanford, and Oklahoma rounding out the top five. Its evaluation process weights individual player ratings more heavily than class depth, which is why LSU's collection of elite individual prospects lands them at the top of this ranking.

Beth Torina's 2027 class is led by Sydney Gonglik, a five-star utility player ranked No. 1 overall by Softball America. The class also includes pitcher Macie Bryant (No. 22 SA), four-star prospects Jewel Cooper (No. 29) and Bryli Seifert (No. 32), and portal additions of twins Mackenzie and Madison Pickens alongside Notre Dame outfielder Michey Winchell. When those portal additions are factored in, LSU's composite 2027 roster may be the deepest in the country position by position.

What the Difference Tells You

The divergence in rankings reflects a real philosophical divide in how to evaluate a recruiting class. D1Softball's model rewards programs that build from the top of a class down through depth picks. Softball America's model rewards programs whose highest-rated individual prospects are elite by an absolute standard, regardless of class size.

Neither is wrong. Both are measuring something real. Oklahoma has the more complete class. LSU has the higher concentration of top-50 talent. If you believe the best teams are built from elite individuals, LSU is No. 1. If you believe championships come from depth and returning experience, Oklahoma is No. 1.

The Programs Watching From Below

Florida earned the No. 2 spot in Softball America's rankings for the second consecutive year. Texas A&M jumped into the top 10 of both services with seven top-100 prospects. Oregon continues to build in the Pac-12-turned-Big-12 era. Arizona State earned a top-10 slot in both rankings. The national talent distribution is broader than it has been in years — meaning even programs outside the top five are building rosters capable of deep postseason runs.

What's Next

The January preseason polls will stake the first real position in the 2027 rankings debate. If Oklahoma's December portal additions fill the pitching depth gap — the one area where the class remains incomplete — the Sooners become a clear consensus No. 1. If LSU adds portal pieces on top of an already-elite class, the Tigers may be the first team since the portal era's onset to hold that spot wire-to-wire. For now, the debate is exactly what college softball should want heading into December: two elite programs, two legitimate cases, and six months until Oklahoma City settles it.
