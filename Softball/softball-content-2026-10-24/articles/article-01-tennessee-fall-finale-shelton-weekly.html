Tennessee Closes Fall With 14-1 Win — Shelton Strikes Out 7 in Dominant Finale

By Sarah Mitchell | October 24, 2026 | I Love Softball

Tennessee wrapped up its 2026 fall schedule Thursday night with a 14-1 victory over Lee University at Sherri Parker Lee Stadium, capping an exhibition run that showed exactly why the Volunteers enter 2027 as one of the nation's most dangerous teams.

The evening's biggest headline wasn't the final score — it was freshman Bailee Shelton's coming-out performance on the mound. In 3.0 innings of work, Shelton allowed just one hit while striking out seven batters. She worked all quadrants of the zone with poise that belied her freshman status, earning praise from the coaching staff for her ability to change speeds and locate pitches against hitters she had never faced in live competition before.

Shelton also posted the best hard-hit ball rate allowed of any Tennessee pitcher this fall — over 50 percent of balls in play were hit softly. That's the kind of metric that gets pitching coaches excited.

Weekly's Honest Assessment

Head coach Karen Weekly was candid after the game, acknowledging that the competition Thursday didn't provide the resistance she'd prefer at this point in the fall schedule. It's an honest coaching comment that means more than it might first appear — when your standard for competition is high enough that a 14-1 win feels insufficient, your program is operating at the right level.

Weekly noted she wishes the fall slate had included more competitive pitching matchups to better evaluate hitters. The team's final public intersquad is set for Thursday, October 29 at 5 PM ET at Sherri Parker Lee Stadium, and that session will be free and open to the public — a chance for fans to see the Vols in a more controlled competitive environment before the long break until spring.

The Rotation That Makes Tennessee Terrifying

Tennessee enters 2027 with what is arguably the deepest pitching staff in the SEC. Jenna Pickens — coming off a season where she struck out 194 batters with a 1.70 ERA — remains the anchor. Ashley Mardjetko went 16-3 with a 1.26 ERA in 2026 and returns as one of the most reliable starters in college softball. Now add Shelton, who showed Thursday night that she's ready to contribute at the highest level.

That combination — a proven strikeout artist, a wins machine with historically low ERA, and a freshman with genuine upside — is the kind of rotation depth that coaches spend years trying to build. Tennessee has it right now.

The fall schedule also included notable exhibitions against Tusculum (32-0 Tennessee win, where sophomore Emma Clarke hit for the cycle), Roane State, and MTSU. Thursday was the final competitive game before winter training takes over.

What's Next

For fans who want one more look at the Vols before the offseason, Thursday, October 29 at 5 PM ET is the date to circle. The final intersquad is free and open to the public at Sherri Parker Lee Stadium in Knoxville.

After that, Tennessee goes dark until preseason practices begin ahead of the 2027 season opener. With Pickens, Mardjetko, and Shelton all returning to a program that perennially competes for SEC titles and Women's College World Series appearances, the Vols will once again be among the teams mentioned in the national title conversation from Day 1.
