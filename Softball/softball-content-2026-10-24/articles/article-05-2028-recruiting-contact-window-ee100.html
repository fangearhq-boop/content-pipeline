2028 Recruiting: Contact Window Open, Rankings Continue — Where the Race Stands Now

By Emily Rawlings | October 24, 2026 | I Love Softball

College softball's 2028 recruiting class is shaping up as the largest and most competitive in the history of the sport — and the race to land the best players is fully underway. The contact period for the Class of 2028 (current high school juniors) is open, meaning Division I coaches can actively recruit these players right now. With over 1,200 nominations submitted to Extra Elite 100, this class has already established a historic baseline.

The next major ranking milestone arrives Monday October 27, when EE100 releases its Pitchers 201-300 rankings. For pitching prospects in that range, Monday's list could be the defining moment of their recruiting cycle — a ranking that changes which schools call and how urgently.

Where the Top Programs Stand

The recruiting landscape for 2028 is already taking shape at the top. Here is where the major programs stand heading into the final stretch of this fall's contact window:

Texas leads the race for class supremacy. The Longhorns hold the No. 1 overall prospect in Michaela Landers, an infielder whose commitment to Texas was one of the most talked-about moments of the recruiting cycle. Texas recently added its fourth commit in Ainsley Morvan, another infielder who brings significant pop to the class.

Florida countered with No. 2 overall Kylie Holden, a catcher who committed to the Gators and brings one of the most rare skill sets in the class — a power bat with elite defensive tools behind the plate.

Oklahoma has locked in five commits, with No. 3 overall Schlader as the headline get. Five commits this early in the cycle for OU signals that the Sooners are not planning to slow down their recruiting pace just because they have a deep 2026 and 2027 roster.

Tennessee has matched OU with five commits. The most recent addition is four-star pitcher Megan Kuiper from Woodstock, Georgia, who gives the Vols another high-ceiling arm to develop alongside the elite staff already on campus. Earlier commit Megan Campos is ranked No. 4 overall in the class.

Florida State landed a notable two-for-one when twins Keira and Kelsey Luderer both committed to Tallahassee. The Luderer sisters bring versatility and the kind of family story that resonates with recruiting classes.

Why 1,200-Plus Nominations Matters

Extra Elite 100 nominations are not self-nominations — they come from coaches, scouts, and evaluators who have seen these players in person. When that number crosses 1,200 for a single class, it reflects something real: youth softball participation is at an all-time high, and the depth of talent being developed at the youth level is feeding into the best recruiting class the sport has ever seen.

The challenge for programs is identifying the diamonds in that volume. The EE100 ranking process — releasing groups in tiers throughout the calendar — is how the sport's scouting community communicates which prospects have risen to the top of that field.

What's Next

Pitchers 201-300 drops Monday October 27. After that, the EE100 process continues its rollout toward the full list. The contact window remains open for the 2028 class through the rest of their junior year, meaning commit announcements should continue to flow from October through the spring.

For any fan tracking where the sport's talent is heading, the 2028 class is the story of the next recruiting cycle. The class ranking picture should clarify significantly once more EE100 tiers are published. Until then, Monday is the next date to watch.
