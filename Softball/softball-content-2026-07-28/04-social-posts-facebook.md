### STORY 1: Jadelyn Allchin — AUSL Championship Series MVP

**POST S1-FB-LONGFORM** | 9:00 AM ET
When Jadelyn Allchin steps up in big moments, the Utah Talons win championships. And after two straight AUSL titles, that's no longer a coincidence — it's a pattern.

Allchin was named the 2026 AUSL Championship Series MVP after a dominant performance in the Talons' series-clinching 6-1 Game 2 victory over the Chicago Bandits. She hit a two-run home run to right field in the very first inning to set the tone, then came back in the fourth inning with an RBI double to extend Utah's lead to 4-0. Three RBI in the clinching game. Six of Utah's eight total runs across the entire two-game sweep were driven in by her.

"The whole series, I think we woke up and we were just ready to go," Allchin said after the game. "The big thing that I thought today was like heart. I think we brought a lot of heart to the past two games. Knowing that showing up with heart for ourselves, for our teammates, like showing up for the game in general, is what I think got us through it all."

That kind of leadership is exactly what championship teams are built on.

The Talons also had a deep UCLA connection on this roster — five former Bruins were part of the championship team, a testament to the pipeline that UCLA softball has built and the talent level that now flows into the professional game.

Utah is now the first team in AUSL history to win back-to-back championships. Coach Cindy Ball-Malone is the first coach to accomplish that feat. And Jadelyn Allchin is a two-time champion who keeps proving she belongs on the biggest stage.

What a run. 🏆 #ILoveSoftball #UtahTalons #AUSL

### STORY 2: Isa Torres Transfers to Texas

**POST S2-FB-LONGFORM** | 11:00 AM ET
Texas already won the 2026 national championship. And then they went and added the best player in the country.

Florida State shortstop Isa Torres — D1Softball's 2026 National Player of the Year — signed with the Longhorns on June 18, making her one of the most anticipated transfers in recent college softball history. And when you look at what she did in her final season with FSU, it's not hard to see why.

Torres hit .530 this past spring (third-best in the nation), slugged .978, and posted a .591 on-base percentage. She drove in 56 runs, scored 78 times, and hit 16 home runs — all while setting Florida State single-season records in batting average, runs, slugging, and on-base percentage. She played the shortstop position at .989 fielding percentage.

The awards followed: D1Softball National Player of the Year, NFCA 1st-Team All-American, NFCA Gold Glove, ACC Player of the Year, ACC Defensive Player of the Year.

Oh, and she's from Georgetown, Texas — so she's coming home.

She's joining a Texas program that not only won the 2026 Women's College World Series but also added former Arizona State catcher Samantha Swan and former Texas Tech lefty Samantha Lincoln in the transfer portal. The Longhorns are building something that looks more like a dynasty every day.

September 1 — the opening of the Class of 2028 recruiting window — is just 35 days away. But the 2027 roster? Already looks scary good.

### STORY 4: 2026 Transfer Portal Class Rankings

**POST S4-FB-LONGFORM** | 3:00 PM ET
The 2026 softball transfer portal class rankings are in — and Texas Tech has claimed the No. 1 spot in the country.

According to rankings from Softball America and SI.com, the Red Raiders put together the most impressive portal class of the offseason, headlined by a trio of experienced hitters. Kasidi Pickering — the OU junior outfielder who was one of the Sooners' most reliable contributors — leads the group. She's joined by Jaysoni Beachum, the former FSU All-ACC talent who hit .406 with 10 home runs and 69 RBI in her final season with the Seminoles, and another experienced bat in the lineup. Texas Tech also addressed the pitching staff with two arms joining behind ace Kaitlyn Terry.

Oregon came in at No. 2, Oklahoma at No. 4, and Texas A&M at No. 7 in the national rankings.

The timing matters too. The NCAA contact period — during which coaches can make in-person contact with prospects — ends July 31, just three days from now. The September 1 recruiting window, which opens the early contact period for the Class of 2028, is 35 days away. The calendar is turning, the portal is mostly closed, and programs are now finalizing their rosters for the 2026-27 season.

### STORY 5: AUSL 2026 Season by the Numbers

**POST S5-FB-LONGFORM** | 5:30 PM ET
The 2026 AUSL season is officially in the books. And when you step back and look at what this league accomplished in just its second year of existence, the numbers are remarkable.

Viewership grew 181% over 2025. The single-game attendance record was set on June 21 when 404,000 viewers tuned in to watch the OKC Spark take on the Portland Cascade. And on July 25, professional softball was broadcast on network television for the first time in history — Game 1 of the AUSL Championship on ABC.

On the field, the records fell. Montana Fouts shattered four AUSL single-season records in one season: innings pitched (62.1), strikeouts (63), wins (7), and complete games (7). Maya Brady — in her first AUSL season — set the league's single-season home run record with 11, surpassing the previous mark of 9 set by Softball in 2025, doing it in her team's elimination loss for some extra bittersweet drama. Jadelyn Allchin closed out the season as Championship Series MVP, adding to a list of accolades for one of the sport's most complete players.

And the Utah Talons became the first team in AUSL history to win back-to-back championships. Head coach Cindy Ball-Malone is now the first coach to accomplish that feat.

The league enters its third season in 2027 with more momentum, more visibility, and more talent than ever before. Professional softball is here to stay.
