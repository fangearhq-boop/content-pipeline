AUSL 2026 Season by the Numbers: Records, Viewership Milestones, and What Year 3 Holds

By Emily Rawlings

July 28, 2026

The 2026 AUSL season is complete. The Utah Talons are back-to-back champions, the records have been shattered, and the numbers tell a story of a league that is still only beginning to grow.

In just its second full season, the AUSL accomplished things that seemed impossible when the league launched: a championship broadcast on network television, a viewership record, and a statistical season that rewrote the record books. Here is a look at the year that was.

The Viewership Story

The headline number from the 2026 season is simple: viewership increased 181% over 2025. For a league in only its second year of existence, that growth rate is extraordinary — a sign that the product on the field is connecting with an audience that is actively growing.

The peak moment came on June 21, when a matchup between the OKC Spark and the Portland Cascade drew 404,000 viewers, setting a new AUSL single-game viewership record. The league had gone from a promising startup to a genuine sports television property in the span of one year.

And then came July 25 — when Game 1 of the AUSL Championship between Utah and Chicago aired on ABC, becoming the first professional softball game ever broadcast on a major network. Whatever the final viewership numbers were for that broadcast, the symbolic significance is enormous: softball had arrived on the same platform as every other major American sport.

The Individual Records

Montana Fouts had one of the most historically dominant pitching seasons in professional softball history. The Utah Talons ace finished 7-4 with a 2.36 ERA, 63 strikeouts, seven complete games, and 62.1 innings pitched — breaking four AUSL single-season records in a single year. On June 18, she set the single-game strikeout record with 12 Ks against the Texas Volts. Opponents batted just .200 against her all season. She was named AUSL Pitcher of the Year and finished the season by throwing a complete game in the championship clincher.

Maya Brady wrote a different kind of record. The OKC Spark outfielder — the No. 1 overall pick in the AUSL Expansion Draft — finished the 2026 season with 11 home runs, setting the AUSL single-season record. She surpassed the previous mark of nine, set by Amber Zerkle in 2025. Brady accomplished it in her team's elimination loss — the kind of bittersweet milestone that sticks in the memory of any season.

The Championship

The Utah Talons swept the Chicago Bandits in two games at Davis Diamond on the campus of Texas A&M in College Station, Texas. Game 1 was a 2-1 Talons win behind Georgina Corrick's complete game. Game 2 was a 6-1 Utah victory with Montana Fouts on the mound from start to finish.

Jadelyn Allchin won Championship Series MVP after driving in six of Utah's eight runs across the series. Head coach Cindy Ball-Malone became the first coach in AUSL history to win back-to-back titles. The Talons are now the standard — the first dynasty in the league's short history.

What's Next

The AUSL enters its third season in 2027 with more momentum than it has ever carried. The first-ever network television broadcast proved that the audience for professional softball exists at scale. The viewership numbers prove it is growing. The quality of play — a record-setting Fouts, a record-setting Brady, a Talons dynasty — proves it is worth watching.

For the Utah Talons, the target on their back gets bigger. For the Chicago Bandits, the Sami Williams-led offense and Lexi Kilfoyl in the circle will push to become the team that ends the dynasty. For Portland, OKC, Carolina, and the rest of the league, the message from 2026 is clear: the level of competition is rising every year.

Year 3 of the AUSL cannot come soon enough.
