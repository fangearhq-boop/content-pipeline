2026 Transfer Portal Class Rankings: Texas Tech Leads the Nation as Contact Period Nears Its End

By Sarah Mitchell

July 28, 2026

The 2026 softball transfer portal cycle is winding down — and when the final rankings are in, Texas Tech stands at the top.

Softball America and SI.com released their rankings of the nation's best transfer portal classes, and the Red Raiders claimed the No. 1 spot, ahead of Oregon at No. 2, Oklahoma at No. 4, and Texas A&M at No. 7. Texas Tech assembled a class that addresses every position of need while adding proven All-American talent to a program already built around ace Kaitlyn Terry.

Texas Tech's Class: Proven Hitters, Depth on the Mound

The headline addition for Texas Tech is Kasidi Pickering, the former Oklahoma outfielder who spent three seasons contributing at one of the most demanding programs in the country. Pickering brings big-game experience from the SEC and a track record of performing in conference competition — exactly what Texas Tech needed to add to the middle of its lineup.

Alongside Pickering comes Jaysoni Beachum, the former Florida State outfielder who earned All-ACC recognition behind one of the most impressive statistical seasons in FSU history: a .406 batting average, 10 home runs, and 69 RBI. Those numbers made Beachum one of the most coveted position players in the portal, and Texas Tech won the competition to land her.

The Red Raiders also addressed the pitching staff, adding two arms who will compete for roles behind the established Terry. The combination of experienced hitters at the top of the lineup and pitching depth behind an ace is a blueprint for a postseason run.

The Rest of the Top Classes

Oregon's No. 2 ranking reflects the Ducks' consistent ability to identify and attract transfer talent — a program that has rebuilt itself through the portal as much as through traditional recruiting. Oklahoma's No. 4 class added two strong arms from SEC programs, addressing their most pressing need after a productive offensive year. Texas A&M at No. 7 continues to build its depth across the board, adding portal pieces that complement a recruiting class that has ranked among the nation's best for several consecutive years.

The Calendar Is Closing

The NCAA contact period — the stretch of the calendar when coaches can make in-person contact with prospects — ends July 31. That means college coaches have just three more days to be present at tournaments, camps, and showcases before the communication restrictions tighten.

The September 1 recruiting window, which opens the early contact period for the Class of 2028, is 35 days away. Programs that have been building their boards at PGF Nationals, on the road at club events, and through video evaluations all summer are now entering the phase where conversations will formalize into commitments and offers.

For the transfer portal cycle itself, the 2026-27 window is largely closed. Most decisions have been made, most commitments are signed, and programs are finalizing the rosters they will take into fall practice. Texas Tech, with the nation's top-ranked class, enters fall with more talent than perhaps any program outside of the defending national champion.

What's Next

Fall practice begins in August for most programs, putting the 2026 transfer portal class in front of their new teammates for the first time. The work of integration — installing systems, building chemistry, establishing roles — begins immediately.

Texas Tech's class will be one of the most closely watched groups in college softball this fall. If Pickering, Beachum, and the rest of the portal additions live up to their rankings, the Red Raiders have the depth to make a serious run in 2027. For programs that finished just below — Oregon, Oklahoma, and others — the gap is narrow enough to close with the right development and the right breaks.

The portal is over. The work is just beginning.
