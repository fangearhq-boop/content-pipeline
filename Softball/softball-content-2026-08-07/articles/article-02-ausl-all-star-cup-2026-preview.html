The AUSL All-Star Cup Starts Tomorrow — Here's What Makes This Format Different

By Emily Rawlings

August 7, 2026

The 2026 AUSL All-Star Cup begins Saturday in Rosemont, Illinois, and it's worth understanding why this event is unlike any other competition on the professional softball calendar.

Forty-five players. Twelve games. Running through August 30 at Parkway Bank Sports Complex. And a format designed to make every individual performance matter — because there is no team winner.

How the Redraft Format Works

The All-Star Cup uses the Athletes Unlimited format that the AUSL has built its season around: players are organized into teams that are completely redrafted before each of four series. That means the player who pitched against you in Series 1 may be your teammate in Series 2. Every game is different. Every lineup is different. And the athletes know that the individual performance tracked by the AU scoring system is the only constant.

At the end of the event, no team is crowned. An individual champion is named based on cumulative points across all 12 games. It's a format that rewards consistency, adaptability, and the kind of all-around value that gets overlooked when team outcomes dominate the story.

Who's In the Field

The 45-player roster was built from the six AUSL regular-season teams. Heading into the draft, the breakdown by club is:

  • Chicago Bandits: 9 players
  • Carolina Blaze: 9 players
  • Utah Talons (2026 AUSL Champions): 9 players
  • Oklahoma City Spark: 8 players
  • Portland Cascade: 6 players
  • Texas Volts: 6 players

Eight players joined through a public fan vote: Reese Atwood, Rylie Boone, Alyssa Brito, Bri Ellis, Jayda Coleman, Megan Grant, Karlyn Pickens, and Jordan Woolery. The fan-elected group adds names fans across the sport specifically wanted to see in this event.

The Storylines to Watch

The defending AUSL champion Utah Talons enter this event with nine players. Back-to-back AUSL championships mean team success, but the All-Star Cup strips that away and asks individual players to perform without the organizational structure that made the Talons successful. Jadelyn Allchin, the 2026 AUSL regular-season MVP, is among those in the field.

Karlyn Pickens, the AUSL's most recognizable pitcher, was one of the fan-elected players chosen to compete. Pickens set the NCAA pitching speed record at 79.4 mph earlier this season and has been one of the most-watched players in the professional game. In an individual-format event, her performance across the four series will be a focal point.

Where to Watch

All 12 All-Star Cup games air nationally across ESPN platforms, MLB Network, and CBS Sports Network. Coverage begins Saturday, August 8, with the event running through August 30.

What's Next

Series 1 begins Saturday at Parkway Bank Sports Complex in Rosemont, Illinois. After four series with complete roster redraws between each, the individual champion will be crowned on August 30. Full schedule and roster information is available at theausl.com.
