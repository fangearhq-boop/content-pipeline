September 1 Is 26 Days Away — and the Class of 2028 Is the Biggest Ever Evaluated

By Jordan Reeves

August 6, 2026

September 1 is 26 days from today. For Division I softball coaches, that is the date the recruiting calendar flips — the first day they can initiate direct contact with Class of 2028 prospects who have not yet committed. For those prospects, it is the day they find out which programs have been watching them most closely all summer.

The Biggest Class Ever Evaluated

Extra Inning Softball has processed 1,226 nominations for the Class of 2028 — the most in their company's history. That number reflects the scale of the 2026 summer evaluation circuit. PGF Nationals, major showcase tournaments, the Little League Softball World Series, the summer exposure season — all of it fed into a database of evaluated players larger than any previous class. More prospects went through more events this summer than at any point before. More coaches were watching.

The result is a coaching staff across the country that has more data than ever before: more film clips, more game logs, more side-by-side comparisons of prospects within the same class. They have spent August reviewing it. September 1 is when they act on it.

How the Window Works

Before September 1 of a prospect's junior year, Division I coaches cannot initiate contact. They can receive questionnaires sent by prospects. They can attend camps and showcases and evaluate publicly available film. But they cannot call, text, or email first. The prospect can reach out; the coach cannot reach back until the window opens.

On September 1, that changes. Coaches can call. They can email. They can send a text saying: we've been watching you, and we want to talk. The first outreach from a program is a signal. It tells a prospect where she ranks in that staff's priorities — not because programs say so, but because coaches make their most-wanted calls first.

What the Next 26 Days Look Like

Coaches are finalizing call lists now. The film has been reviewed. The showcase stats have been logged. Internal conversations about who to prioritize on September 1 morning are already happening. For the Class of 2028 prospects who had strong summers — particularly those who stood out at PGF Nationals or produced at major events — the calls are coming. The question is which programs are on the other end.

For prospects who underperformed at their biggest showcases this summer, the window to influence September 1 priority lists has narrowed, but it has not closed. Fall showcases still exist. Coaches still attend them. But the September 1 call order is being set right now, and the summer film is the primary input.

What's Next

September 1, 2026 is a Wednesday. Twenty-six days from today. For the record Class of 2028 prospects who went through this summer's evaluation gauntlet, the waiting is nearly over.
