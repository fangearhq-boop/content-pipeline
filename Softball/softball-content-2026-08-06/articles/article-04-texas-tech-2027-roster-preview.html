Texas Tech 2027: What the Full Roster Looks Like — and What It Would Take to Win It All

By Sarah Mitchell

August 6, 2026

For five days, the coverage of Texas Tech's transfer class came in installments — one player at a time. Now the portal window has settled and all the commitments are in. Here is what the 2027 Red Raiders roster looks like when you step back and see the whole picture.

What's Coming In

Three transfers define this class, and each fills a specific need:

Jaysoni Beachum (from Florida State): The 2024 NFCA Freshman of the Year and 2026 Second-Team All-American. She hit .406 with 10 home runs and 69 RBI at Florida State before entering the portal. She is an elite contact hitter who can drive the ball — exactly the type of bat that carries lineups in elimination games.

Kasidi Pickering (from Oklahoma): Three seasons as a Sooner, career average of .384, 50 home runs, 169 RBI in 186 games. In 2026, she hit .370 with 20 home runs and 61 RBI. She was a 2024 national champion and earned WCWS All-Tournament recognition. She has one year of eligibility remaining and will bring power that Texas Tech has not had in the same lineup as Beachum before.

Jayden Heavener (from LSU): The pitching addition that changes the conversation. She went 13-9 with a 2.81 ERA in 2026 at Louisiana State and has two full years of eligibility remaining. Pitching depth — specifically, what happens after the No. 1 arm in a long tournament week — had been the gap between Texas Tech and a national title. Heavener fills it.

What's Returning

Texas Tech reached the 2026 Women's College World Series Finals. This was not a rebuilding program — it was an elite team that came one step short of a national title. The core that made that run is still in place: the coaching staff, the returning roster pieces, the program infrastructure. The transfer class layers onto a team that already knew how to win at the highest level.

The Question That Remains

On paper, this roster is complete — offense, pitching depth, experience, and championship pedigree across multiple players. But on paper is not in the batter's box and not on the mound in the seventh inning of a WCWS elimination game. These players have never shared a dugout. They have never gone through fall practice together, never built the trust that makes a team something greater than a collection of individual stats.

Fall camp begins in a few weeks. That is when the real work starts: chemistry, roles, the adjustment of fitting into a new system. If that goes well, Texas Tech 2027 could be the most complete team in the country.

What's Next

Fall camp and the early fall schedule will be the first real indicator of how this roster integrates. Texas Tech's first opportunity to show what the 2027 edition looks like comes when games start. Watch the fall slate for early signals — and watch September recruiting announcements for any additional pieces the program might add before the season begins.
