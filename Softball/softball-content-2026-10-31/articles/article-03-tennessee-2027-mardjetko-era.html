Tennessee Softball 2027: Sage Mardjetko Leads the Post-Pickens Era

By Sarah Mitchell | October 31, 2026 | I Love Softball

Karlyn Pickens is not coming back. The most decorated pitcher in recent Tennessee history exhausted her NCAA eligibility following the 2026 season, was drafted No. 1 overall in the Athletes Unlimited Softball League, and is now a student assistant with the Lady Vols program. Any fan still expecting to see her in the circle next February needs to update the picture. But here is what the updated picture looks like — and why it is not as alarming as the headline might suggest.

Mardjetko as the Ace

Sage Mardjetko was a first-team All-American for a reason. Her 2026 season line — 16-3, 1.26 ERA, 171 strikeouts — would be the unquestioned ace résumé on virtually every program in the country. At Tennessee, it was the supporting act to Pickens. In 2027, Mardjetko gets the ball in the biggest games, and the argument for her as a legitimate national Cy Young candidate is not a stretch.

She returns as a senior, meaning 2027 is her final year in Knoxville. That creates urgency and focus on both sides. Mardjetko knows what she has in front of her, and the program will need her to deliver the kind of anchor performances that define postseason runs.

Nuwer, Shelton, and the Depth Behind the Ace

Erin Nuwer recorded four shutouts as a 2026 freshman and appeared in 18 games. She returns as a junior, which means the curve of development that typically carries pitchers from their freshman year to their peak years is right on schedule for a meaningful 2027 role. Karen Weekly has the ability to turn to Nuwer in a real moment and trust that the pitcher has the experience to handle it.

Bailee Shelton, still a freshman, showed up in a fall intersquad with a three-inning, seven-strikeout showing that confirmed she belongs in the conversation as a legitimate depth piece. A freshman class ranked in the top six nationally by both D1Softball and On3 adds more arms to a staff that is deeper than it appears at the headline level.

Weekly addressed the rotation directly after fall ball wrapped, calling pitching depth "the biggest question mark of the fall, but one she feels good about." That is not a ringing endorsement, but it is not panic either. It is the honest assessment of a coach who knows exactly where her program sits and what it needs to do.

What Tennessee Lost and What It Kept

The loss of Pickens is real. She was a 194-strikeout arm with a 1.70 ERA who made opposing hitters look uncomfortable from the first pitch. That kind of presence does not simply transfer to the next name on the depth chart. But the Lady Vols still have a legitimate ace in Mardjetko, a proven secondary option in Nuwer, and a recruiting class that gives the program ammunition for the years after 2027.

Tennessee beat Lee University 14-1 in its fall finale, which is not a scouting report on the pitching staff, but it is a data point. The Lady Vols showed up, executed, and ended fall on a dominant note. That is the tone Weekly wanted heading into winter preparation.

What's Next

The 2027 season will test whether Tennessee's pitching depth translates from fall ball confidence into March and April performance. The SEC is as deep as it has been in years, Florida, OU, and Texas Tech all have legitimate national title aspirations, and the margin for error in a postseason run is always thin. But the Lady Vols have the pieces. Mardjetko leading, Nuwer backing her up, and a freshman class growing into the rotation behind them — that is a program with options. How Weekly uses them will define Tennessee's 2027 season.
