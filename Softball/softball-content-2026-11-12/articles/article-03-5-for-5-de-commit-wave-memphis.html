Memphis Pulls Offers as 5-for-5 Fallout Hits Signing Day Commits

By Emily Rawlings | November 12, 2026 | I Love Softball

Kentucky head coach Rachel Lawson warned months ago that the NCAA's 5-for-5 eligibility rule would create "casualties." The day after Signing Day, her prediction arrived.

Memphis softball pulled scholarship offers from three committed Class of 2027 athletes: Cassidy Ott, Kinley Patten, and Isabell Gonzales. All three announced their situations publicly on social media. Cassidy Ott said she had been "involuntarily de-committed from the University of Memphis due to the new 5-year eligibility rule." For athletes who had built their college plans around these commitments, the week that was supposed to celebrate their futures became something else entirely.

The Roster Math Behind the Decision

The 5-for-5 rule — formally an age-based eligibility model adopted by the NCAA Division I Cabinet in June 2026 — gives athletes up to five years of eligibility within a continuous five-year window. When veteran players exercise that fifth year of eligibility, they remain on the roster. The House v. NCAA settlement simultaneously caps Division I softball rosters at 25 players.

The math is straightforward and brutal. A program retaining multiple fifth-year players enters the offseason with fewer open spots for incoming recruits. When those spots disappear, some committed freshmen lose their offers. Memphis found itself in that situation and chose to retain returning players over honoring its commitments to incoming high school athletes.

The Tenth Circuit Ruling Changed the Calculus

The timing of the roster crunch was sharpened by the Tenth Circuit's November 6 ruling, which reversed and vacated the nationwide preliminary injunction in Wisne v. NCAA. That injunction, originally granted July 31 and stayed August 21, had given programs reason to hope that the 5-for-5 rule might be struck down before Signing Day. When the Tenth Circuit vacated it, the rule became the definitive framework for roster planning.

Programs that had committed Class of 2027 athletes under the assumption that the injunction might hold — or that fifth-year veterans might not all return — now faced the reckoning. For Memphis, the numbers did not add up. The result was three athletes losing their futures at that program the week those futures were supposed to be confirmed.

Approximately 200 state-court cases involving individual athletes continue across the country. For athletes in those cases, roster uncertainty persists even after the federal ruling.

Memphis Is Not Alone

Memphis may be the first program to report de-commitments publicly, but the roster pressure it faces is not unique. Any program retaining multiple fifth-year players and trying to maintain a full incoming class is working with the same math. Lawson's warning — delivered well before Signing Day — was not about Memphis specifically. It was about the structural reality of the new eligibility landscape.

The athletes who lost their offers — Ott, Patten, and Gonzales — are not the only ones in this position. As more programs finalize their rosters in the coming days and weeks, additional de-commitments are possible. The recruits most vulnerable are those at programs with heavy fifth-year participation and limited flexibility in the 25-player cap.

What's Next

Cassidy Ott, Kinley Patten, and Isabell Gonzales are now in the recruiting market at the start of a signing window that runs through August 1, 2027. Other programs with open spots — created by their own departures or roster management decisions — could reach out. The December contact window for Class of 2028 recruits is the next scheduled recruiting milestone, but the more pressing story will be what programs come forward to offer these de-committed athletes a new home. The 5-for-5 situation will continue to evolve as more programs report their final roster configurations.
