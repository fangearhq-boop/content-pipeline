Five Programs That Could Win the 2027 Women's College World Series

By Jordan Reeves | November 14, 2026 | I Love Softball

Fall ball is in the rearview mirror. The signing period has closed. And the five programs most likely to compete for a national championship in 2027 are coming into focus.

No preseason rankings are out yet — those come in January. But between fall ball results, signing day hauls, and returning talent, the picture is clear enough to draw. Here are five programs that could cut down the net in Oklahoma City in June.

No. 1 Contender: Oklahoma

The Sooners are D1Softball's No. 1 class and the consensus favorite to enter 2027 as the nation's top-ranked program. NFCA Freshman of the Year Kendall Wells (39 HR in her debut season) and two-time WCWS Most Outstanding Player Rylie Kavan return as the most decorated duo in college softball. Add five-star signees Finlee Williams and Riley Hilliard, and Patty Gasso has a full house before spring practice begins.

Oklahoma's path to the WCWS runs through the Big 12, which is deeper than ever. Texas Tech and Arizona aren't going away. But the Sooners have the talent ceiling to win it all — and Gasso has done it before.

No. 2 Contender: LSU

Beth Torina secured Softball America's No. 1 class — the first in LSU program history — by landing five-star pitcher Sydney Gonglick, five-star infielder Bryli Seifert, and portal additions Mackenzie and Madison Pickens. The SEC placed six programs in Softball America's top 10 on Signing Day, and LSU led the way.

The Tigers were not a WCWS contender in recent years. This class changes that conversation. If Gonglick develops as advertised and the Pickens twins contribute immediately out of the portal, LSU becomes a problem for the entire SEC in 2027.

No. 3 Contender: Florida

The Gators went 4-0 in fall ball and outscored opponents by more than 60 runs. Tori Edwards — who transferred from LSU and hit four home runs in four fall games — gives Florida an offensive centerpiece to pair with six returning starters. The No. 2 class nationally (Taylor Jennings at No. 9 overall, Taegan Lermann at No. 19) adds immediate depth.

Tim Walton's program has been to the WCWS and knows how to get back. The 2027 roster looks like one of his most complete.

No. 4 Contender: Tennessee

Karen Weekly's contract extension through 2030 sent a clear message: Tennessee is building, not transitioning. The Lady Vols went 3-0 in fall ball and return pitcher Hannah Mardjetko (16-3, 1.26 ERA), who will be a senior in 2027 and a legitimate WCWS MVP candidate. Signing Day additions Emma Pynes (No. 55 overall) and Evelynn Swartzlander (No. 61) provide rotation depth.

Tennessee has arguably the best pitching staff in the SEC — and pitching wins championships.

No. 5 Contender: Texas Tech

The Red Raiders may be the most intriguing wildcard in the field. No. 1 portal class nationally. Kasidi Pickering (.384 BA, 50 HR, 169 RBI in three seasons at Oklahoma) arrives as the most decorated transfer in the portal class. Hollynn Gentry — the No. 11 overall recruit and the first five-star freshman in Texas Tech program history — joins her behind the plate. Big 12 play opens March 12.

Glenn Moore Glasco's program has the pieces to surprise the field. If Pickering's bat translates immediately, Texas Tech should be in every conversation.

What's Next

Preseason rankings from D1Softball, USA TODAY, and ESPN arrive in January. Spring schedules will be released over the next several weeks. The 2027 Women's College World Series is scheduled for Oklahoma City, June 1–9. Five programs are built to be there. Only one cuts down the net.
