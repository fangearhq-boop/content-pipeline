Tennessee Fall Intersquad Recap: Pickens, Mardjetko, and Shelton Put the SEC on Notice

By Emily Rawlings | October 30, 2026 | I Love Softball

Tennessee softball held its final public fall event Wednesday evening at Sherri Parker Lee Stadium — a free intersquad that gave fans and scouts a last look at a pitching staff that could be the most formidable in the SEC heading into 2027. Head coach Karen Weekly has been vocal throughout the fall about wanting tougher competition for her team. Based on what Karlyn Pickens, Kelly Mardjetko, and freshman Bailee Shelton have shown, the challenge for opposing offenses is obvious.

Pickens Remains Elite

Karlyn Pickens posted 194 strikeouts and a 1.70 ERA during the 2026 season, numbers that placed her among the best pitchers in college softball. Pickens and Mardjetko were both named Softball America Second Team All-Americans in May 2026, a recognition of what has been a two-ace rotation unlike anything most programs can put together.

Pickens' ability to miss bats at a high rate while maintaining efficiency in the circle makes her a matchup problem for any lineup. Wednesday's intersquad was the last public look before she returns to competition-level preparation for February.

Mardjetko's Consistency Is Underrated

While Pickens draws more national attention, Kelly Mardjetko's 16-3 record and 1.26 ERA in 2026 may be the most underappreciated stat line in women's college softball. A 1.26 ERA across a full season is the kind of number that wins conference Pitcher of the Year awards in most leagues. In Tennessee's rotation, it makes her the second option.

The dynamic Pickens-Mardjetko rotation gives Weekly a luxury few coaches have: the ability to deploy two genuine aces in the same series. It neutralizes opponent preparation, keeps pitch counts manageable, and allows Tennessee to be aggressive in postseason matchups without overextending either arm.

Shelton Arrives as a Freshman to Watch

The most intriguing development of the fall has been freshman Bailee Shelton's emergence. Shelton posted 3 innings and 7 strikeouts in her fall finale performance — a display that suggested she is not a project but a genuine contributor from the start.

Adding a third arm of that caliber to an already elite rotation deepens Tennessee's pitching resources significantly. A freshman who can strike out batters at that rate in fall competition is the kind of player who earns postseason innings early in her career. Karen Weekly has something to look forward to there.

What's Next

Tennessee's fall is now complete. The Lady Vols will not play publicly again until the 2027 spring schedule opens in February. Weekly and her staff will spend the offseason preparing Pickens, Mardjetko, and Shelton for a full spring workload and sharpening a lineup built to compete for SEC titles. For opposing coaches scouting this rotation on tape, Wednesday's intersquad at Sherri Parker Lee Stadium was a preview of the problem they will face in 2027. It is a formidable one.
