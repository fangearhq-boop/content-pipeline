Arizona Softball Closes Fall at Hillenbrand: Shockey, 10 Letterwinners Set Stage for 2027

By Sarah Mitchell | October 30, 2026 | I Love Softball

Arizona softball wraps up its fall schedule this weekend at Rita Hillenbrand Memorial Stadium in Tucson, closing out the first fall ball period under new head coach Caitlin Lowe. The Wildcats return 10 letterwinners to the 2027 roster, headlined by Big 12 Defensive Player of the Year Regan Shockey — a combination of returning experience and fresh leadership that has the program pointed in a promising direction.

Lowe's First Fall as Head Coach

Caitlin Lowe inherits a program with strong bones. Arizona has built a tradition of competitive softball, and the Big 12 membership adds a layer of urgency and expectation that comes with playing in one of the country's premier conferences. This fall has been Lowe's extended evaluation period — a chance to assess personnel, install her systems, and get a feel for the culture she is building.

Closing the fall schedule today and tomorrow at Hillenbrand Stadium marks the end of that evaluation phase. What Lowe has seen gives her a clear picture of what she is working with heading into the spring. The presence of 10 returning letterwinners means she is not starting from scratch. Experience is already in place.

Shockey Anchors the Defense

Regan Shockey's selection as the Big 12 Defensive Player of the Year places her among the best fielders in the conference. Her return for the 2027 season gives Lowe an anchor in the infield — the kind of proven defensive presence that stabilizes a roster and allows coaching staff to build around a known quantity.

In a league as competitive as the Big 12, defensive excellence at key positions is often what separates contenders from the rest of the field. Shockey's presence means Arizona has that standard already set at one critical spot on the diamond.

Ten Letterwinners Provide a Foundation

Returning 10 letterwinners to the 2027 roster is a significant asset for any program entering a new coaching era. These are players who have been through the grind of a Big 12 season, who know what it takes to compete at this level, and who bring institutional knowledge that a first-year staff cannot manufacture from scratch.

For Lowe, this group represents the bridge between the previous era and the culture she is building. The fall has given her a chance to evaluate each of them in competition, identify her starters and rotation depth, and begin laying the foundation for a 2027 campaign that will be watched closely across the Big 12.

What's Next

After this weekend's final fall games at Hillenbrand Stadium, Arizona softball goes into its offseason preparation period before the 2027 season begins in February. Lowe will spend the coming months finalizing her roster, building her coaching approach around the experience she has assembled, and preparing Shockey and the rest of the returning letterwinners for a spring run in the Big 12. The expectations in Tucson are building. This fall was the first chapter.
