5-for-5 Eligibility Litigation Reaches the Tenth Circuit: What It Means for College Softball

By Sarah Mitchell | October 30, 2026 | I Love Softball

College softball coaches have had one eye on a federal courthouse in Colorado this fall. The NCAA's Five-for-Five eligibility rule — adopted June 23, 2026 — is now under review by the Tenth Circuit Court of Appeals following a challenge brought by student-athletes from the high school class of 2022. The outcome could affect roster construction at programs across the country heading into 2027 and beyond.

What the 5-for-5 Rule Does

The NCAA's Five-for-Five rule grants athletes five years of eligibility, calculated from the date they enroll or the academic year following their 19th birthday — whichever comes first. The rule was designed to provide clarity and consistency in the wake of years of COVID-related eligibility extensions and court-ordered exceptions that left the eligibility landscape difficult to manage for both athletes and programs.

For softball specifically, the rule affects players from the class of 2022 who have been competing under various extended eligibility interpretations. The question before the courts is whether the NCAA's application of the rule is fair to those athletes.

The Legal Timeline

The case is Wisne v. NCAA, filed in Colorado federal court. On July 31, 2026, Judge Charlotte N. Sweeney granted a preliminary injunction for class of 2022 athletes, ruling that the NCAA's enforcement of the Five-for-Five rule unfairly limited their ability to compete and earn revenue from NIL and revenue-sharing arrangements. The court clarified that its ruling did not extend to enjoin NCAA roster limits, transfer rules, or other related regulations.

The NCAA appealed and sought a stay. On August 21, 2026, the Tenth Circuit granted that stay pending the full appeal. Arguments before the Tenth Circuit were heard on October 13, 2026. A ruling from the appellate court could come at any time, and both sides are watching closely.

The Softball Roster Implications

If the NCAA ultimately loses the appeal and the preliminary injunction is upheld, softball programs would be permitted to add former players from the class of 2022, provided their rosters remain within applicable limits. For programs that lost players to graduation or transfer under the current eligibility framework, this could represent an opportunity to bring back experienced contributors.

The practical impact would vary widely by program. Teams that maintained relationships with class of 2022 players — and whose athletes wish to return — would benefit most. Programs that have already fully committed their roster spots to incoming recruits and transfers would have less flexibility to act. In either case, the decision-making window between a ruling and the start of the 2027 season would be compressed.

What's Next

The Tenth Circuit heard arguments on October 13 and is now deliberating. Related state-level lawsuits challenging the NCAA's eligibility rules are also pending in courts across the country, adding to the uncertainty. Congress has been discussing federal legislation that could resolve the eligibility question through statute rather than litigation, though no bill has advanced to a vote. For softball coaches, athletic directors, and players who might be affected, the waiting is the hardest part. A ruling before the start of the 2027 recruiting calendar would give programs the most time to plan. Every week without a decision narrows that window further.
