Defending Champs Texas Open Fall Ball Sept. 29 — Free Admission at McCombs Field

By Emily Rawlings | September 23, 2026

The back-to-back Women's College World Series champions are returning to the field this week — and Texas fans can get their first look at the 2027 Longhorns for free.

Texas softball opens its 2026 fall schedule on Tuesday, September 29 at Red and Charline McCombs Field, hosting Murray State College at 6 p.m. CT. All home fall games are free and open to the public, giving fans a no-cost opportunity to watch the defending national champions begin building toward a potential three-peat.

Coming Off a Championship Season

It's hard to overstate what Texas accomplished in 2026. The Longhorns finished the season 53-12 and swept Texas Tech in the Championship Series on June 3-4 — winning 7-3 and then 4-1 to claim their second consecutive WCWS title. For perspective, back-to-back WCWS championships are an extraordinarily rare achievement in college softball.

Now comes the harder question: can they do it a third time?

Fall ball is where the answer begins to take shape. The fall schedule gives coaches time to evaluate returning contributors, integrate transfers and freshmen, and start developing the combinations that will define the 2027 team's identity.

Full Fall Schedule

Texas has six fall games on the books, with five at McCombs Field and one road trip to College Station:

  • Sept. 29 — Murray State College (6 p.m. CT, McCombs Field)
  • Oct. 13 — Texas State (6 p.m. CT, McCombs Field)
  • Oct. 20 — Temple College (6 p.m. CT, McCombs Field)
  • Oct. 27 — McLennan Community College (6 p.m. CT, McCombs Field)
  • Oct. 29 — UTSA (6 p.m. CT, McCombs Field)
  • Nov. 6 — at Texas A&M (7 p.m. CT, Davis Diamond)

All home games are free. If you're in Austin, there's no excuse to miss a chance to see the defending champs up close.

A Three-Peat in Sight

The concept of a three-peat in college softball is rare territory. Texas has established itself as the gold standard of the sport through consecutive championships, and fall ball 2026 is the first step toward finding out if this group has what it takes to make history again.

For the Longhorn faithful, the anticipation is real. For opposing programs watching from afar, the sight of Texas getting back to work this fall is a reminder that the target on their back isn't going anywhere.

What's Next

Texas opens fall competition Tuesday, September 29. The Longhorns' full regular-season schedule for 2027 has not yet been announced, but the SEC slate will be revealed in the coming months. All eyes will be on McCombs Field this fall as the three-peat conversation officially begins.
