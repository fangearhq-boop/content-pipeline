Johnston County Is a Champion: North Carolina Wins 4th LLSWS Title With 2-0 Shutout Over Kentucky

By Emily Rawlings

August 10, 2026

Johnston County, North Carolina is bringing home a championship.

North Carolina's Johnston County Little League defeated Kentucky's Daviess County Little League 2-0 in the 2026 Little League Softball World Series Grand Final on Sunday afternoon at Stallings Stadium in Greenville, North Carolina. The title is North Carolina's fourth in the modern era — following championships in 2015, 2019, and 2024 — and it was delivered by a pitcher who was simply in a different category than everyone else in the field all week.

Anna-Lynn Clark's Championship Performance

Anna-Lynn Clark has been the story of the 2026 LLSWS from the first round of pool play, and Sunday's championship game was her statement. In 93 pitches, she threw a complete-game shutout: 3 hits allowed, 6 strikeouts, and not a single Kentucky runner reaching second base in the final five innings of the contest. Kentucky had its chances early. After the third inning, Clark closed the door.

Across the entire tournament, Clark was the field's most dominant force by the numbers that matter. She recorded 41 total strikeouts — the highest total of any pitcher in the field. She pitched 20 of Johnston County's 24 tournament innings and allowed only 3 runs across the entire week. She had three separate outings of 10 or more strikeouts, including an 11-strikeout shutout in Saturday's semifinal against Florida. What she did in Greenville over the course of a week is the kind of performance the Little League Softball World Series will be measuring future tournaments against.

How the Scoring Unfolded

North Carolina's offense did exactly what it needed to do against a Kentucky team that had won games by keeping scores low all week. In the second inning, Belle Cox lined an RBI single that scored Scarlett Cerda from third base — Johnston County led 1-0, and it felt like more given who was in the circle.

The second run came in the third inning on a Kentucky error. That was it. Two runs, two scoring plays, and Clark handled everything else. In a championship game defined by pitching and defense, North Carolina's formula was clean: score first, score again, and hand Clark a lead to protect. She made it look easier than it was.

North Carolina in LLSWS History

Sunday's title gives North Carolina four Little League Softball World Series championships. The state now trails only Texas (14 titles), Florida (8), and California (5) in the all-time standings since the tournament launched in 1974. Four titles, all earned in the last twelve years, all representing a program that has become one of the sport's most consistent forces at the youth level.

Johnston County's run in 2026 was not just championship-quality — it was dominant in the way that rare teams are dominant. They didn't just win the tournament. They outclassed it.

What's Next

Johnston County's players and coaches return to Clayton, North Carolina as Little League Softball World Series champions. For Anna-Lynn Clark and her teammates, the summer ends at the top. The LLSWS will take a year off before returning to Greenville in 2027, and whoever puts together the next run will be measured against the standard this North Carolina team set. That's the best compliment anyone can offer.
