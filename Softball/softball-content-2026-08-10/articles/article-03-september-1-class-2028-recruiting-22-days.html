22 Days: The Most-Nominated Class in Softball History Waits for September 1

By Sarah Mitchell

August 10, 2026

Twenty-two days from today, at midnight on September 1, NCAA Division I softball coaches across the country will be permitted to pick up the phone and call a Class of 2028 recruit for the first time.

That moment — the first direct call from a Power 4 program to a high school sophomore who has spent years working toward it — is 22 days away. Every recruit and every program knows it's coming. The question is just what happens when it does.

The Rule and What It Changes

Softball operates on a strict September 1 contact calendar for Division I recruiting. Before that date, D1 coaches cannot initiate contact with Class of 2028 prospects. They can attend games. They can send recruiting questionnaires once a recruit turns 13. They can respond if an athlete reaches out first. But the phone calls, the text messages, the emails, the visit invitations — none of that is permitted until the clock strikes midnight on September 1 of a prospect's sophomore year.

When that date arrives, everything changes simultaneously. Every program sends its first outreach at the same moment. Athletes who have been waiting for this window to open will receive multiple contacts in the first 24 to 48 hours. What a recruit does with that moment — and how prepared they are when it arrives — can shape the next four years of their athletic and academic life.

A Record-Setting Class

The Class of 2028 is drawing more attention than any recruiting class before it. Extra Inning Softball, the sport's leading recruiting and prospect coverage platform, has received 1,226 nominations for their Class of 2028 recognition program — the highest total in the organization's history for any single recruiting class. The volume reflects how serious programs and families have become about early identification, and how many genuinely talented athletes are in this particular group.

For coaches, that means more evaluation work and tighter competition for commitments in the window ahead. For prospects, it means the landscape is crowded but the opportunities are real — programs are looking, and they're looking at a lot of players simultaneously.

What the Next Three Weeks Mean

The 22 days before September 1 are not idle time. They are the last window for prospects to make sure everything is in place. Film updates from any late-summer exposure events matter — coaches may reference them in first calls. Academic records should be current in any recruiting database profiles. If a coach has attended a showcase or game this summer, a brief athlete-initiated follow-up message before September 1 is appropriate and can help keep the program engaged heading into the contact window.

When September 1 arrives, programs with the best prospect lists and the clearest evaluations will move fastest. Those are the calls that come early on September 1. Being visible and prepared in the days before the window opens is how prospects position themselves to be at the top of those lists.

What's Next

September 1, 2026 is 22 days away. When the date arrives, the full recruitment process opens. For Class of 2028 athletes and their families, the work done between now and then determines how those first conversations go. Stay visible. Stay prepared. The phones ring in 22 days.
