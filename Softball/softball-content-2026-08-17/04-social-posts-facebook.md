### STORY 2: Oklahoma Softball — First WCWS Miss Since 2015

**FB Long-Form:**
Eleven years is a long time to be at the Women's College World Series.

For Oklahoma, that streak just ended. The Sooners fell in the Super Regional to Mississippi State and will not be in Oklahoma City in 2027. It's the first time since 2015 that Patty Gasso's program has missed the WCWS.

This isn't a program collapse — it's a reset. But it's a real one.

The transfer portal took some key pieces this offseason. Berkley Zache, Riley Zache, and Tia Milloy all departed. The void at pitcher is the biggest need. Gasso has already addressed part of it, adding RHP Miali Guachino from Ole Miss. Top 2026 recruit Payton Westra enrolled, giving the Sooners a building block at the plate.

Gasso's public message was direct: "We definitely are going to take a look-see in the portal to see what we can find."

Outfielders Kai Minor and Ella Parker are locked in returning roles. The nucleus is there. The ace is the missing piece.

This version of OU won't be an underdog in 2027. But they have something to prove that most recent OU teams haven't had to face: the other programs are no longer afraid.

Can Gasso get them back to OKC in one year?

### STORY 3: September 1 — 15 Days (Class of 2028)

**FB Long-Form:**
Fifteen days.

That's how long the Class of 2028 has to wait before the single most important day of their recruiting journey.

September 1 is when D1 coaches can call, text, and arrange face-to-face meetings with Class of 2028 prospects for the very first time. Since June 15 of their sophomore year, coaches have been limited to printed materials and certain digital communications. No live calls. No direct conversations.

On September 1, all of that changes — simultaneously, across every D1 program in the country.

Here's something most families don't know: Extra Inning Softball's Class of 2028 rankings deadline is also September 1. Nominations close for pitchers, catchers, infielders, and outfielders — and the rankings drop after that. This means the last two weeks of August are a crucial window to finalize a player's profile, film, and showcase results before the rankings landscape sets.

The coaches who call first are signaling one thing: this is a priority. The programs with the most organized call lists are the ones who've been evaluating this class all summer.

If you have a 2028 player, this is the week to be sure everything is in order. Not September 1 morning. Now.

Fifteen days. The phones are about to ring.

### STORY 4: USA WNT — 26 Days to World Cup, Group C Preview

**FB Long-Form:**
Twenty-six days until USA Softball opens the WBSC Women's Softball World Cup Group Stage in Oklahoma City.

The group has been set for a while. But with 26 days to go, it's worth looking at who stands between USA and Brisbane.

**USA's Group C opponents, ranked by WBSC:**
- Netherlands — No. 6 in the world
- Mexico — No. 7
- China — No. 8
- New Zealand — No. 23
- South Africa — No. 36

The Netherlands, Mexico, and China are genuine tests. These aren't games USA will sleepwalk through. The top two teams from the group advance to the 2027 World Championship in Brisbane, Australia — everything else is going home.

The 16-player USA roster breaks down this way by conference: SEC (10 players), Big 12 (3), Big Ten (3). Oklahoma leads all schools with four members on the roster: Alyssa Brito, Jayda Coleman, Tiare Jennings, and Kelly Maxwell.

This group also carries two members of the 2020 Olympic team (Rachel Garcia and Dejah Mulipola) and seven players from the 2025 World Games gold medal squad.

Devon Park, OKC. September 12–16. The next chapter of USA Softball starts in less than four weeks.

### STORY 5: Texas Longhorns — 11 New Additions, Competition Begins

**FB Long-Form:**
Texas is defending two national championships heading into 2027. And head coach Mike White just gave the clearest signal yet of what this season is going to look like.

"Of the 24 players on our roster, 11 are new additions, so that means a lot of competition within the team for starting positions this year."

Eleven new players. In a program that just won back-to-back WCWS titles.

That's not a caution sign — that's the price of being a destination program. Every portal player who left for Texas came to compete. Every freshman chose Texas over a shorter path to playing time somewhere else. They knew what they were getting into.

Teagan Kavan enters her senior year as the most decorated active player in college softball. She is the only person in history to win back-to-back WCWS Most Outstanding Player awards. That standard becomes the ceiling for everyone competing to start next to her.

Only two programs have ever won three consecutive national championships: UCLA (1988–90) and Oklahoma (2021–23). Texas has one shot at joining that list.

Rick Fremin and Amber Freeman are on board. The staff is complete. The roster is full. Now the competition begins.
