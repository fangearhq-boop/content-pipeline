Fifteen Days to September 1 — The Recruiting Clock Is Almost Out of Time

By Emily Rawlings | I Love Softball | August 17, 2026

Fifteen days. That is what separates the Class of 2028 from the moment that reshapes everything in their recruiting journey.

September 1, 2026 is the date D1 coaches can make live contact with Class of 2028 prospects for the first time. No more printed mailers. No more one-way digital communications. On September 1, the phone rings, the texts go out, and the conversations that determine where hundreds of high school juniors will play college softball begin.

For the families who have been living in the pre-September-1 world — watching coaches watch their players at showcases, receiving interest signals but no calls, reading between the lines of every camp invitation — the next fifteen days may feel both long and very short.

What Coaches Are Doing Right Now

Here is what most families never see: D1 coaches have had their September 1 call lists built for weeks. The evaluation period from June through August is not just for watching. It is for ranking. Programs sort their targets, establish which calls to make in the first hour of September 1, which conversations to have in the first week, and which prospects are in the second tier of priority.

A player who has been in a coach's database since a tournament last spring is in a different position than a player who only showed up on a coach's radar at a late-summer showcase. The evaluation machine has been running all summer. On September 1, it shifts from observation to action.

The players who receive calls early on September 1 are receiving a signal: this program considers you a priority recruit. That signal matters even when no formal offer is on the table.

The EIS Deadline

There is a second clock running alongside the NCAA calendar. Extra Inning Softball's Class of 2028 ranking nominations also close on September 1. Nominations for pitchers, catchers, infielders, and outfielders are being accepted now, with the rankings released after the deadline passes.

For a player in the 2028 class, appearing in the EIS rankings is both a measure of national standing and a recruiting tool. Coaches who follow the rankings use them as a cross-reference against their own internal evaluations. A player ranked highly by EIS, who coaches have also seen in person, becomes a priority faster than a player who exists only in a regional recruiting circuit.

The nomination deadline and the NCAA contact window opening on the same date is not a coincidence — September 1 is when the Class of 2028 conversation begins publicly in earnest.

What to Do with Fifteen Days Left

For athletes and families approaching September 1, the work that matters most right now is not high-volume or complicated. It is specific. Film should be current — including moments from this summer's showcases and tournaments. A highlight video that stops in February does not tell coaches what the player looks like in August. Contact information and communication with target programs should be confirmed and clean.

September 1 will generate a wave of conversations, offers, and decisions. The players positioned best going into that wave are the ones who spent August doing the quiet, focused work that most recruiting guides don't mention: keeping their profiles updated, staying visible at events, and understanding which programs have shown consistent interest versus a one-time camp invite.

What's Next

September 1 arrives in fifteen days. When the contact window opens, D1 coaches across the country will make their first direct calls to Class of 2028 recruits. Following September 1, watch for a wave of public commitment announcements as conversations that have been building all summer become official. The Class of 2028 cycle officially moves into its most active phase.
