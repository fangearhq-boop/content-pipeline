Series 3 Captains Are Set — Now Kowalik Has to Defend Her Lead

By Jordan Reeves | I Love Softball | August 17, 2026

The Series 3 captains for the AUSL All-Star Cup are official. Keilani Ricketts, Danieca Coffey, and Devyn Netz will each lead a team when competition resumes August 24–26 at Parkway Bank Sports Complex in Rosemont, Illinois. The team draft is scheduled for Friday, August 22.

Missing from that captain list: Kayla Kowalik. And yet she enters Series 3 in a stronger position than any of the three captains. Kowalik has won the Game MVP in every contest she has played through the first two series — four games, four MVPs. Her individual points total of 370 (220 MVP points plus 150 stat points) represents a lead that the rest of the field is now chasing without the advantage of calling the shots on a team.

Three Captains, One Points Leader

The dynamic heading into Series 3 is straightforward on the surface: Ricketts, Coffey, and Netz each build their rosters through the August 22 draft, and then the competition begins. But Kowalik's presence — as a non-captain with the individual race lead — adds a layer that makes every game more interesting than it looks on paper.

Kowalik won the 2025 AUSL All-Star Cup with a final total of 1,590 individual points. She is the defending champion in the individual competition, and through two series in 2026, she has done nothing to suggest that is changing.

Ricketts is no stranger to the captain role — she held it in Series 2 as well. Coffey and Netz are stepping into the captain seat for the first time in this cycle. All three bring different team-building philosophies to the August 22 draft, and those choices will shape how Series 3 plays out on the field.

The Romero Factor

If Series 2 told us one thing, it's that the individual leaderboard can shift fast.

Sierra Romero made the biggest jump of any player in Series 2, climbing 42 spots in the individual standings to reach No. 9 overall. Her two-game point total of 410 was the second-highest of anyone who played in Series 2. In five at-bats, she produced: a sacrifice bunt, a single, two doubles, and a home run. That kind of output turns a name near the bottom of the leaderboard into a genuine threat.

Emma Lemley also made a substantial leap — up 26 spots — after throwing four scoreless innings and earning MVP 3 recognition. Korbe Otis moved as well. The message from Series 2 is that any given series can fundamentally reshape the standings.

That is the pressure Kowalik carries into Series 3. She leads, but she leads in a format where four days of competition can turn the leaderboard upside down.

What Happens on August 22

The draft on August 22 is when the Series 3 rosters get built. Ricketts, Coffey, and Netz will each select from the 45-player pool, constructing teams that they believe can win games and accumulate points for their own individual tallies.

Where Kowalik lands — which captain selects her, and who ends up as her teammates — shapes her Series 3 outcome in a way that being the race leader cannot protect against. The team dynamics matter. The matchup combinations matter. Every game in a three-team format produces point differentials that can close or widen the overall gap.

Kowalik has handled those variables better than anyone so far in 2026. The question for Series 3 is whether three new captains building against her changes that.

What's Next

Team rosters will be finalized through the August 22 draft at Parkway Bank Sports Complex in Rosemont, Illinois. Series 3 games run August 24–26. The individual champion will be crowned August 30, at the conclusion of Series 4. Check the AUSL's official channels for real-time draft updates and final rosters ahead of the August 24 opener.
