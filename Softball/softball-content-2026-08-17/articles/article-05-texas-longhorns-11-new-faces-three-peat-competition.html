Texas Softball Has 11 New Faces and One Goal — The Three-Peat Nobody Has Done in 33 Years

By Sarah Mitchell | I Love Softball | August 17, 2026

Mike White did not sugarcoat the challenge ahead. Looking at the 24-player Texas softball roster entering the fall, he put the number out plainly: "Of the 24 players on our roster, 11 are new additions, so that means a lot of competition within the team for starting positions this year."

Eleven of twenty-four. Nearly half the roster is new to the Longhorns program. In most places, that statistic raises questions. At Texas, it is the direct result of what the program has become — a destination for the best players in the transfer portal and the top names in every recruiting class.

What Competition Looks Like From Inside

The returning Longhorns know what the new additions are walking into. Texas went 49-12 in 2026, won the SEC Tournament, and captured the national championship — the second consecutive title for a program that had never won one before 2025. Every player who competed in those runs knows what a WCWS champion looks like from the inside.

The eleven new players coming in are not joining a rebuild. They are joining a program that has set the standard in women's college softball for two straight years. Whether those new additions raise the ceiling or simply compete for spots on a team already built to win is the question White and his staff will answer in the fall.

With Rick Fremin and Amber Freeman now in place alongside White — a full staff with combined experience running programs and developing hitters at the highest levels — the coaching infrastructure to evaluate that competition is stronger than it has ever been in Austin.

Kavan's Senior Year

Running through all of it is Teagan Kavan, who enters her senior season carrying something no one has ever carried before. She is the only player in the history of college softball to win back-to-back Women's College World Series Most Outstanding Player awards. Two championships. Two MOPs. One more season to write the ending to that story.

For the eleven new additions learning the program, Kavan is the standard. For the veterans who won with her, she is the anchor. The internal competition White described exists with Kavan at the center — not competing for her spot, but setting the tone that defines what competing for every other spot looks like.

The History That Waits at the End

Only two programs in the history of women's college softball have won three consecutive national championships. UCLA accomplished it first, winning from 1988 to 1990. Oklahoma matched it decades later, winning from 2021 to 2023. No one else.

Texas has a legitimate shot at joining that list. The program has the coaching, the roster depth, the facility investment, and the recruiting footprint to sustain the level of play required over a third consecutive season. The 11 new additions are a sign of that footprint, not a concern about it.

What stands between Texas and history is six months of competition, starting with fall ball and running through the 2027 WCWS in Oklahoma City. The Longhorns have been to that destination two years in a row. White's message is clear: getting back a third time will require everyone to earn their spot.

What's Next

Texas softball fall practice will begin in the coming weeks. The 2027 regular season opens in February. Keep an eye on the Longhorns' fall scrimmage schedule for early looks at how the roster competition takes shape. Kavan's senior season begins with the program's best chance at a place in history that has only been reached twice before.
