Eleven Years, Then a Loss — How Oklahoma Softball Rebuilds After Its First WCWS Miss Since 2015

By Sarah Mitchell | I Love Softball | August 17, 2026

For eleven consecutive seasons, Patty Gasso took Oklahoma to the Women's College World Series. The streak ran from 2016 through 2026, producing three national championships and establishing the Sooners as the standard every other program was chasing.

Then Mississippi State ended it in the Super Regional. A Game 3 loss sent the Sooners home and ended the longest active WCWS streak in the country. For the first time since 2015, Oklahoma will not be in Oklahoma City next June.

The Portal Takes Priority

Gasso did not shy away from the situation when she addressed it publicly. Her response was direct: "We definitely are going to take a look-see in the portal to see what we can find."

The offseason departures created real needs. Berkley Zache (pitcher), Riley Zache (catcher/infielder), and Tia Milloy (utility) all left through the transfer portal. The biggest hole is at the top of the rotation. Oklahoma's staff produced results, but 2027 demands an ace — a pitcher around whom a national-championship push can be built.

The program has already made one move to address it. Right-hander Miali Guachino, formerly at Ole Miss, committed to Oklahoma. She gives the Sooners a proven collegiate arm, but the search for rotation depth is not finished.

What's Already in Place

The rebuild is real, but it is not starting from scratch. Outfielders Kai Minor and Ella Parker return as locked-in contributors. The offensive core is intact in ways that matter.

More importantly for the long-term picture: Payton Westra has enrolled. Westra was the No. 2 overall recruit in the Class of 2026, a playmaker at the plate whose commitment to OU signaled that the program's recruiting footprint has not shrunk. Top-tier prospects are still choosing Norman. That matters as much as any portal addition when projecting where this program lands in 2027.

Gasso has rebuilt before. The Sooners went through a 2015–2016 transition period and emerged to win three national championships in the following decade. The institutional knowledge of what it takes — the facilities, the staff depth, the recruiting connections — does not disappear with one Super Regional loss.

The Climate Has Changed

What is different in 2026 is the landscape. Texas is the two-time defending national champion. UCLA is rebuilding under new direction. Nebraska made a WCWS run. The SEC continues to load the field every June. Programs that spent years chasing Oklahoma are now setting the pace.

The Sooners enter 2027 without the streak, without the aura of inevitability, and with a real roster need to fill. For any other program, that would be a manageable rebuild. For Oklahoma, it is the kind of challenge Gasso has not faced in more than a decade.

The portal window and the fall recruiting cycle will define what version of OU shows up next spring. What Gasso said is accurate: the portal is the fastest path back. The question is which arm commits to Norman before the December signing period.

What's Next

Oklahoma's fall practice will begin in the coming weeks, giving Gasso and her staff a first look at the new-look roster with Guachino, Westra, and returning players. Transfer portal activity in the pitcher market typically accelerates after the conclusion of fall ball. Watch for additional additions through December as the 2027 roster picture comes into focus.
