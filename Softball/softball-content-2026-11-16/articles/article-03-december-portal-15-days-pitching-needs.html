The December Transfer Portal Opens in 15 Days — Here's Which Programs Need Pitching Most

By Sarah Mitchell | November 16, 2026 | I Love Softball

The Division I softball transfer portal opens December 1 and closes December 15. Under normal circumstances, the December window is quieter than the summer window that follows the season. This year is not normal. The House v. NCAA settlement's 25-player roster cap is in force, the Tenth Circuit reversed the federal 5-for-5 injunction on November 6, and multiple programs enter December with roster holes they cannot survive without filling.

The most critical need in this window is pitching. Here are the three programs with the most urgent rotation problems heading into December 1.

Arizona — Replacing an Irreplaceable Arm

No program lost more in the portal than Arizona when Marissa Torres — D1Softball's National Player of the Year — transferred to Texas Tech. Torres was the best pitcher in the country and the centerpiece of a program that was building toward a Big 12 debut. First-year head coach Caitlin Lowe, who took over in the offseason, now enters December knowing that Arizona's ability to compete in a loaded Big 12 depends significantly on what she can find in the portal.

The good news for Lowe: Arizona's defensive foundation remains elite. Big 12 Defensive Player of the Year Regan Shockey returns. Ten letterwinners are on the roster. The fall closed on a 15-2 win over UNLV. What Arizona needs is a frontline arm capable of bearing a heavy workload in a 56-game regular season and beyond. The December window is the first realistic opportunity to address it.

FSU — Rebuilding the Pitching Staff After Two Key Departures

Florida State lost two cornerstones to the portal this offseason: Torres and Jaysoni Beachum, who both transferred out after the 2026 season. FSU opened fall ball 19-5 against Chipola — an encouraging sign — but the Seminoles enter December in a rebuild mode that extends to the circle. The character of head coach Lonni Alameda's program and the program's history of recruiting elite pitching provide reasons for optimism, but the roster needs reinforcement before the spring schedule begins.

Ole Miss — SEC Rotation Demands Elite Depth

The Southeastern Conference grind demands pitching depth that most programs outside the SEC rarely encounter. Ole Miss, which went through significant roster turnover this offseason, enters December needing a frontline arm that can compete in a league where every weekend series features power hitters. The December window offers a narrower pool than the summer portal, but programs that act quickly within the first 24 to 48 hours often secure the best available talent.

The 5-for-5 Wildcard

The Tenth Circuit's November 6 reversal of the federal 5-for-5 injunction adds an unpredictable element to the December portal. Athletes who qualify for a fifth year but cannot practice or play under the current enforcement of the rule may choose the portal over holding a roster spot under the House settlement's 25-player cap. For programs in need of experience — including pitching — a fifth-year athlete who can compete is a potentially valuable December addition. Watch for unexpected names in the first 72 hours after the portal opens.

What's Next

December 1 is fifteen days away. Programs that have already identified their targets are in permissible contact within recruiting rules. The 15-day window moves fast — the best available players rarely last more than a week without multiple offers. Arizona, FSU, and Ole Miss are not the only programs searching for pitching, but they are the ones with the most to gain — or lose — in the next three weeks.

The spring seasons of multiple programs may be decided not on the field, but in the December portal tracker.
