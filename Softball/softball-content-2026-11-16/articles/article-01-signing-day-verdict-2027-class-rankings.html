Signing Day Verdict: Who Won the 2027 College Softball Recruiting Race?

By Emily Rawlings | November 16, 2026 | I Love Softball

Five days after Signing Day, the final picture on the 2027 college softball recruiting class is set. D1Softball ranks Oklahoma No. 1 nationally. Softball America — ranking the same class by a different methodology — places LSU at the top for the first time in program history. Both assessments are defensible. Both reflect something true about the landscape of college softball heading into 2027.

What is not debatable: five programs assembled elite classes, and those five programs will enter January's preseason polls as the favorites to reach the Women's College World Series in Oklahoma City.

Oklahoma: The D1Softball No. 1

Patty Gasso's 2027 class is the one D1Softball evaluators trust most. The headliner is Julianna "Goose" Hutchens — the No. 1 overall recruit in the 2027 class — who reclassified and is already enrolled in Norman. Five-star outfielder Finlee Williams and five-star catcher Riley Hilliard round out the top-end talent in an incoming freshman group that complements a returning roster that features prolific veterans. D1Softball's evaluation process leans on in-person assessment and roster depth, and by that measure, Oklahoma's combination of elite incoming talent and experienced returners is the class of the class.

LSU: First No. 1 in Program History

Softball America placed the Tigers at the top — a first for the program. Beth Torina's six-player class is built around pitching depth in a way that addresses the Tigers' most urgent roster need. Five-star utility/pitcher Sydney Gonglik (No. 1 overall, Softball America) and four-star pitcher Macie Bryant (No. 22 overall) give Torina two elite arms arriving simultaneously. Add four-star catcher Jewel Cooper (No. 29), four-star infielder Bryli Seifert (No. 32), and four-star utility Lillie Gaudio and infielder Jillian Wimmer, and LSU has a class that the SEC will be discussing for years.

Texas Tech: No. 1 Transfer Class

Softball America's No. 1 transfer class ranking goes to Texas Tech — which also signed the first five-star freshman in program history in Jocelyn Gentry. The transfer haul includes NFCA First-Team All-American Kasidi Pickering, who posted a .384 batting average, 50 home runs, and 169 RBI across three seasons at Oklahoma before moving to Lubbock. Eric Glasco's program made perhaps the loudest single statement of any team in the country on and around Signing Day.

Florida and Tennessee: The Class Depths Behind the Elite

Florida finished No. 2 in Softball America's class rankings — its second consecutive year in the top two. The Gators return six starters from a team that went 4-0 in fall action, and the incoming class adds proven depth to a program already positioned to compete for a WCWS berth. Tennessee's class ranked No. 5-6 nationally and pairs with one of the deepest returning pitching staffs in college softball history. Senior Kelsey Mardjetko (16-3, 1.26 ERA) and junior Nuwer (4 shutouts) return atop a staff that head coach Karen Weekly — who signed a contract extension through 2030 — has built into an elite unit.

What's Next

The January preseason polls — from D1Softball, USA TODAY, and ESPN — will be the first measure of media consensus on how these classes translate into 2027 expectations. The SEC placed six programs in Softball America's top 10; the Big 12 sent multiple programs into the top tier as well. That overlap at the top of the rankings means the WCWS field in June 2027 may be drawn almost entirely from two conferences.

No program won Signing Day without earning it. Oklahoma, LSU, Texas Tech, Florida, and Tennessee all have legitimate arguments. The 2027 season will be the final judge.
