Tennessee's 2027 Pitching Staff Is the SEC's Deepest — And Possibly the Country's

By Jordan Reeves | November 16, 2026 | I Love Softball

When Karen Weekly finalizes her 2027 pitching staff, she will look at a rotation that no other program in the country has quietly assembled. Senior Kelsey Mardjetko, who went 16-3 with a 1.26 ERA in 2026, returns for what amounts to a farewell campaign. Junior Nuwer — who posted four shutouts in 2026 — is ready to become a true No. 2 arm on a staff that doesn't require her to be a No. 1. And freshman Shelton, who struck out seven batters in just three innings of limited fall action, is the kind of arm programs recruit once a decade.

Tennessee's pitching staff is not one pitcher. It is three pitchers with different skill sets and meaningful postseason experience, all on the same roster at the same time. Weekly's contract extension through 2030 suggests the program is treating 2027 as a window, not a transition year.

Mardjetko: One Last Shot at Oklahoma City

Kelsey Mardjetko's 2026 season was one of the best individual pitching campaigns in the SEC. A 16-3 record and a 1.26 ERA are numbers that belong in the conversation for any pitching award. She returns as a senior with the motivation of a program that expects more than a postseason appearance — it expects a deep run. How Tennessee uses Mardjetko through the first half of the season, before the SEC schedule fully arrives, may determine how much she has left in the WCWS field.

Nuwer and Shelton: The Depth No One Else Has

What makes Tennessee's staff genuinely dangerous is what happens when Mardjetko isn't pitching. Nuwer's four shutouts in 2026 demonstrate the ability to carry a complete game against quality opposition. That is not a reliever's profile — that is a pitcher who can win a postseason game on short rest. Shelton's fall debut (3 IP, 7 K) generated significant attention from people who track pitching development closely. A true freshman posting those numbers in live fall action suggests a pitcher who does not need time to adjust to the college game.

The combination of Mardjetko, Nuwer, and Shelton gives Tennessee a three-arm rotation that most staffs don't have with two arms.

The Lineup Is the Question

Tennessee's 2026 season demonstrated that a great pitching staff is a necessary but not sufficient condition for a WCWS title. The Vols' incoming recruiting class — Pynes (No. 55 overall), Swartzlander (No. 61 overall) — adds depth without dramatically reshaping the offensive identity. The challenge for Weekly heading into 2027 is building a lineup capable of supporting the kind of run differential a staff like Mardjetko-Nuwer-Shelton can produce.

Programs that reach the WCWS and win it typically carry both a dominant staff and an offense that scores in clusters. Tennessee has half that equation locked. The winter will tell us whether the other half is coming.

What's Next

Tennessee's 2027 schedule has not been released publicly. The program has historically scheduled a mix of invitational events in February and an SEC schedule that opens in March. The preseason polls — expected in January from D1Softball, USA TODAY, and ESPN — will give the first media consensus on where Tennessee's rotation ranks relative to the other title contenders.

The case for Tennessee as the team to beat in 2027 begins in the circle. And based on what fell camp showed, the case is a strong one.
