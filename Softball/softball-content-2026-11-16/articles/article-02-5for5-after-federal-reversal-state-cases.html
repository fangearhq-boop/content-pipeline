5-for-5 After the Federal Reversal: What Happens to the 200+ State Cases?

By Jordan Reeves | November 16, 2026 | I Love Softball

The federal safety net for Class of 2022 athletes is gone. On November 6, the Tenth Circuit Court of Appeals reversed the preliminary injunction in Wisne v. NCAA, vacating the lower court's order that had temporarily protected affected athletes from the NCAA's age-based eligibility rule. The court found the district court had erred in how it defined the market the athletes said was restrained.

What remains: approximately 200 state-court cases nationwide, still active on separate legal tracks. For the athletes caught between the federal reversal, the House v. NCAA settlement's 25-player roster cap, and the December 1 transfer portal window, three paths exist — and none of them are clean.

The Legal Timeline, Condensed

The original district court injunction in Wisne came on July 31. The Tenth Circuit stayed it August 21, and oral arguments on the merits were heard October 13. The November 6 reversal followed. Under the ruling, the NCAA's 5-for-5 rule — which ties eligibility to an age-based calculation for athletes who first enrolled in college in fall 2022 — applies as written to the class the case covered. The case does not affect athletes who first enroll in fall 2027 or later.

NCAA officials described the situation as one of continuing "chaos" even after the ruling. That language is not an overstatement. The federal ruling answers one question — will the federal injunction hold — but leaves every state case open and every affected athlete's situation unresolved.

Three Paths for Affected Athletes

Athletes currently on rosters under the 5-for-5 umbrella who cannot practice or play face a decision before the December portal window opens. The options:

Path 1: Stay on the roster and wait for a state court. This is the highest-risk, highest-reward option. A favorable state court ruling before the spring season would restore eligibility. But it requires a program willing to hold a roster spot under the House settlement's 25-player cap — a spot that cannot be filled with an active contributor in the meantime. Kentucky head coach Rachel Lawson has described situations like this as producing "casualties." She's not wrong.

Path 2: Enter the December portal. This is the most practical option for many athletes. The window opens December 1 and closes December 15. Entering the portal as a free agent does not prejudge a state court outcome — an athlete can still benefit from a ruling later — but it does start the clock on finding a new program before spring rosters lock. Programs in need of pitching or positional depth will be watching the December portal carefully for unexpected names.

Path 3: Accept the year is lost. Some athletes may have exhausted their realistic legal options or face program constraints that make the other paths impractical. For this group, the federal reversal is effectively the end of the road.

The Double Squeeze

The collision between the House settlement's 25-player cap and 5-for-5 creates what analysts have called a "double squeeze." A program that holds a roster spot for an unplayable athlete is sacrificing cap space it could use for a December portal addition or a healthy returner. Programs that entered the fall with borderline roster sizes may find themselves unable to honor the return of 5-for-5 athletes even if they want to — the math simply doesn't work under the cap.

This is the scenario Lawson was warning about. The legal fight has produced an outcome where there are no good choices — only choices with different costs.

What's Next

The state-court landscape is the story to watch through the winter. With approximately 200 active cases across multiple jurisdictions, any single ruling that goes in the athletes' favor could create a new wave of eligibility restorals — but only in that state, and only for the athletes covered by that case. No national resolution is expected before December.

The December 1 portal window will be the first real indicator of how many 5-for-5 athletes choose to pursue the practical path. Watch the portal tracker carefully — the names entering December may tell us more about the state of this legal fight than any court docket ever could.
