Tenth Circuit Overturns 5-for-5 Injunction — NCAA Wins Key Eligibility Ruling Ahead of Signing Day

By Emily Rawlings | November 10, 2026 | I Love Softball

The Tenth Circuit Court of Appeals issued a ruling on Friday that could reshape college softball's 2027 roster landscape. In a decision that reversed and vacated the nationwide preliminary injunction in Wisne v. NCAA, the appeals court found that the Colorado district court had improperly defined the relevant labor market when issuing the original injunction last July. The ruling, issued five days before National Signing Day, gives programs clarity they have been waiting months to receive.

What the Court Decided

The panel, led by Judge Carolyn McHugh, concluded that the district court's all-inclusive definition of the collegiate athletic labor market was unsupported by the evidence. The original injunction, issued by Judge Charlotte Sweeney on July 31, had allowed Class of 2022 Division I athletes to seek a fifth season of competition under the argument that the NCAA's 5-for-5 eligibility rule violated antitrust law.

The Tenth Circuit paused that injunction on August 21 with a 2-1 stay pending appeal. Friday's ruling goes further: the court reversed and vacated the injunction entirely. That means the 5-for-5 rule — which limits athletes to five total years of competition, with no more than four at the same division level — is now in effect nationwide through the federal courts.

NCAA chief legal officer Scott Bearby called the decision a vindication. "Injunctions like the one entered by the district court have created chaos for the 2026-27 season," he said in a statement.

What It Does Not Resolve

The Tenth Circuit's ruling applies to the preliminary injunction only. It does not definitively end the broader legal challenge to the NCAA's eligibility framework. The case can continue at the district court level, and the plaintiffs may seek further review.

More significantly, the federal ruling does not affect the roughly 200 separate lawsuits pending in state courts, involving hundreds of athletes who pursued their own eligibility challenges. Some of those state-court cases resulted in individual injunctions that allowed players to compete under the 5-for-5 rule — a situation the NCAA's Bearby characterized as the source of the "chaos." Those cases continue on their own timelines.

The Signing Day Dimension

The timing of Friday's ruling is not incidental. Programs have been navigating 2027 roster planning under a cloud of legal uncertainty since the original injunction was issued in July. With Signing Day falling on November 11, coaches have had to plan for two scenarios: one in which certain players retained fifth-year eligibility and one in which they did not.

That uncertainty is now reduced, though not entirely eliminated. The state-court cases mean that some individual athletes may still compete under a local injunction. But the nationwide federal injunction is gone, and programs can proceed with Signing Day on firmer legal ground.

For programs like Tennessee, which dealt with roster complications throughout fall ball, and Texas Tech, which built its No. 1 portal class in part around 2027 planning, the ruling provides a cleaner path forward.

What's Next

The plaintiffs in Wisne v. NCAA have several options: seek review from the full Tenth Circuit en banc, petition the U.S. Supreme Court for certiorari, or return to the district court on the merits of their antitrust claim. None of those options would restore the nationwide injunction quickly, if at all. Meanwhile, state-court litigation continues, and individual athletes may continue to have court-issued protections in specific jurisdictions.

For college softball broadly, the more immediate next step is Signing Day. Programs across the country will sign their 2027 classes on November 11 knowing the federal eligibility landscape is clearer than it has been since summer. The legal story is not over, but for now, the NCAA holds the upper hand.
