Texas Tech's Portal Revolution — Pickering Signs Tomorrow, and the Red Raiders Are Building Toward Oklahoma City

By Jordan Reeves | November 10, 2026 | I Love Softball

Tomorrow, Kasidi Pickering signs her athletics aid agreement with Texas Tech. The numbers she brings with her — .384 career batting average, 50 home runs, 169 RBI over three seasons at Oklahoma — represent the most consequential transfer in Red Raiders softball history. And she is not coming alone.

How Gerry Glasco Built the No. 1 Portal Class

Gerry Glasco arrived at Texas Tech from Louisiana, where he built a program that made multiple WCWS appearances and became one of the most respected coaching staffs in the sport. His first full recruiting class at TTU was constructed almost entirely around the transfer portal — a deliberate strategy for a program that needed to accelerate its development timeline without waiting three to four years for high school recruits to mature.

The result is the No. 1 portal class nationally, by multiple recruiting services' rankings. Pickering headlined the class. Around her, Glasco added players across the lineup to support her impact at the plate and fill gaps left by program departures. The class was assembled during the spring and summer portal windows, finalized before the fall semester, and becomes official tomorrow.

What Pickering Means for the Red Raiders

Kasidi Pickering's career at Oklahoma was defined by her bat. Across three seasons, she hit .384 with 50 home runs and 169 RBI — numbers that place her among the most productive offensive players in the Big 12 over that span. She is a right-handed corner bat who hits for both average and power, a combination that has become increasingly rare in the sport as programs lean more heavily on speed and contact over raw power production.

For Texas Tech, Pickering provides something the program has not had in recent history: a known commodity at the middle of the lineup. Opponents will have to game-plan for her. That attention creates opportunities for everyone around her.

The 2027 Picture

Texas Tech opens Big 12 play on March 12. The Red Raiders will face some of the same programs that watched Pickering in a different uniform for three years. That includes Oklahoma, Texas, and others who know exactly what she can do. Glasco's challenge is to build a system around her that plays to her strengths while developing the supporting pieces into a cohesive unit over the winter months.

Several analysts have named Texas Tech as a dark-horse contender for the 2027 Women's College World Series. That projection rests almost entirely on how well the new roster gels in the first half of the spring season. If it comes together, the Red Raiders have the talent to make a run at Oklahoma City. If it does not, the parts will still be there to build on for 2028.

The 2028 piece matters too: Glasco has also added 2028 high school commitments this fall, signaling that the portal-first strategy is a bridge to a sustainable pipeline, not a one-year gamble.

What's Next

Tomorrow's Signing Day is the formal start of the Texas Tech transformation under Glasco. Pickering signs. The portal class is official. The spring schedule will begin revealing how the roster translates from paper to field. For Red Raiders fans who have watched programs like Texas and OU build generational dynasties, this is a moment that feels different. The building blocks are in Lubbock. The question is whether March comes fast enough.
