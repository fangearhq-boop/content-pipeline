### STORY 1: Tenth Circuit Overturns 5-for-5 Injunction

#### Long-Form Post -- 9:00 AM ET

```
The Tenth Circuit issued its ruling on Friday, and it was a decisive win for the NCAA.

The federal appeals court reversed and vacated the nationwide preliminary injunction in Wisne v. NCAA — the ruling that had opened a potential fifth year of eligibility for Class of 2022 Division I athletes. The panel found that the Colorado district court's broad labor-market definition was unsupported by the evidence.

What does this mean? The 5-for-5 rule — which limits athletes to five years total of competition, with a maximum of four at the same division level — is now in effect nationwide through the federal courts. The stay the Tenth Circuit granted in August has become a full reversal.

The timing is significant. Signing Day is tomorrow, November 11. Programs across the country can now finalize 2027 rosters without waiting for a potential federal reversal. The NCAA's Scott Bearby said the lower court's injunction had "created chaos for the 2026-27 season." That chaos is not fully over — roughly 200 state-court cases involving hundreds of athletes continue — but the federal door has closed.

For programs like Tennessee, which dealt with roster complications throughout fall ball, the ruling offers a cleaner picture heading into the spring.
```
