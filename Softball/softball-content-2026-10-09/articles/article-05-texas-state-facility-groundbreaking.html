Texas State Breaks Ground on $16.5M Softball and Baseball Facility

By Sarah Mitchell | October 9, 2026 | I Love Softball

Texas State softball is building toward something bigger — and this fall, that building is happening literally. The university broke ground this month on a $16.5 million improvement project for its baseball and softball programs, funded in large part by an $11 million gift from Eric and Candice Hays.

Phase 1 of the project is expected to wrap up by June 2027, meaning the Bobcats will step into a transformed facility as they head into the heart of the Sun Belt Conference season next year. For a mid-major program looking to close the gap with wealthier competition, the investment is a statement of intent.

The Size of the Gift Matters

An $11 million private donation is significant by any standard in college athletics, but it is especially meaningful for a Sun Belt program competing against Power Five schools with substantially larger athletic budgets. The Hays gift signals that Texas State softball has donors willing to bet on the program's future in a real way.

Facility upgrades in college softball have a direct competitive impact. Recruits choose programs based in part on the quality of the training environment they are walking into. A modern, well-funded facility can shift the conversation for a program trying to attract top talent against more historically prominent programs.

The Broader Context: College Softball Is Growing

Texas State's groundbreaking is part of a broader trend of institutions investing heavily in softball infrastructure. From power programs like Oklahoma and Texas commissioning major stadium renovations to mid-major programs making transformative gifts, college softball is attracting real capital. The sport's growing visibility — driven by strong attendance, increasing media coverage, and a pipeline of elite talent — is making these investments easier to justify.

The Texas State project is a local example of a national story: softball programs at every level are building for the future, and the sport is responding with the talent and fan engagement to justify every dollar spent.

What's Next

Phase 1 of the Texas State project is expected to be complete by June 2027, giving the program the first full offseason in a new facility before the 2028 season. For the Bobcats, it is the foundation of something bigger. For the sport, it is one more data point showing where college softball is heading.
