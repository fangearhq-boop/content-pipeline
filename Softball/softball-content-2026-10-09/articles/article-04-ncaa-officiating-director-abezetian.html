NCAA Names Marty Abezetian Softball Officiating Director in Key Hire

By Jordan Reeves | October 9, 2026 | I Love Softball

If you have ever wanted better officiating at the highest level of college softball, October 5 brought a genuinely encouraging development. The NCAA announced that Marty Abezetian has been named Director of Training and Video Review for softball umpires — a role built specifically to improve officiating consistency and develop the next generation of collegiate umpires.

Abezetian is not a newcomer to this work. He brings 17 seasons of collegiate softball umpiring experience, including work at the Women's College World Series in 2025. His history with the NCAA's officiating infrastructure runs even deeper: he previously served six years as the softball video services coordinator, then became the first-ever director of training and video review before this return to the role.

Why This Hire Matters

Umpiring quality is one of the consistent pressure points in high-stakes college softball. As the game grows — in fan interest, media attention, and on-field talent — the scrutiny on officiating has grown alongside it. Abezetian's appointment signals that the NCAA is investing in the infrastructure to keep pace.

The training and video review role sits at the intersection of instruction and accountability. It is not just about teaching umpires the rulebook — it is about using game film to identify patterns, address specific call types that generate controversy, and build consistency across a national pool of officials who have to make split-second decisions at the highest level of play.

With 17 seasons of field experience and a proven track record building the role itself, Abezetian is well-positioned to move the needle.

What's Next

Abezetian's work will unfold largely out of the public eye — the best officiating development is invisible on game day. But for fans and coaches who care about consistent rule enforcement at the college level, the hire is a meaningful step. Expect the impact to be gradual and cumulative, with the 2027 season and beyond as the real test of whether the program delivers.
