14 Days: 5-for-5 Athletes Must Decide Before the December Portal Closes

By Emily Rawlings | November 17, 2026 | I Love Softball

Fourteen days. That is how long athletes affected by the NCAA's 5-for-5 rule have to make one of the most consequential decisions of their college softball careers: enter the December transfer portal, continue fighting in state court, or accept that this year may be lost.

The Tenth Circuit reversed the preliminary injunction in Wisne v. NCAA on November 6. The federal class action protection that had been shielding Class of 2022 athletes from the age-based eligibility rule is gone. Athletes who had been practicing, competing, and planning their fifth year on the belief that federal courts would protect them are now subject to the rule as written — and the December portal opens in 14 days.

What the Reversal Means in Practice

Under the 5-for-5 rule, college athletes must have enrolled in college no later than the academic year after their 19th birthday to receive a fifth year of eligibility. For Class of 2022 athletes who turned 20 before enrollment, that fifth year is now gone under federal law — unless state courts intervene.

Approximately 200 state court cases nationwide continue. Kentucky head coach Rachel Lawson called the resulting situation one with "casualties," a word that captures what these legal proceedings mean in human terms. These are real athletes with real scholarships, real coaches who recruited them, and real seasons in the balance.

No state court ruling is expected before December 1. That means the portal is the most immediate legal path forward for most affected athletes.

The Roster Cap Complicates Everything

The situation is not simply about individual athletes. The House v. NCAA settlement imposed a 25-player roster cap on Division I programs. Programs carrying athletes who can be rostered under 5-for-5 but cannot practice or play in games are holding roster spots that could otherwise go to contributors.

That math forces conversations between coaches and athletes that neither party wants to have. A coach who built a roster assuming a fifth-year athlete would practice and play full-time now faces a binary choice: hold the spot for a player who cannot contribute, or free it for someone who can. The human cost of that decision falls entirely on the affected athlete.

Programs that are most exposed are those that relied on multiple 5-for-5 athletes to fill rotation or lineup depth. A single roster slot held for a non-contributor is manageable. Multiple slots held simultaneously creates a competitive disadvantage that affects recruiting for 2027 and beyond.

What Happens on December 1

When the portal opens on December 1, affected athletes have until December 15 to submit their names. Any Division I softball player who enters the portal is immediately eligible to be recruited by other programs. The window is narrow — fifteen days — and there is no extension.

Programs actively seeking portal additions — including Arizona, FSU, and Ole Miss, all of which have identified pitching as a priority — will be making contact within permissible recruiting rules as soon as the window opens. The demand for quality portal transfers is high. The supply of athletes whose situations have changed due to 5-for-5 enforcement adds unexpected names to the available pool.

For athletes still fighting in state court, the portal decision is not necessarily final. Some state court cases may allow relief even after a player has entered the portal. But the legal landscape is complex and program-specific. The safest path for most athletes is to enter the portal and secure a landing spot while the litigation continues.

What's Next

The next 14 days are the most critical period of the offseason for dozens of athletes whose careers were built around an expectation that federal courts would protect them. That protection is gone. The portal opens December 1 and closes December 15. After that, the next regular window does not open until June.

Rachel Lawson's word — "casualties" — is not hyperbole. It is an accurate description of what the Tenth Circuit reversal means for athletes who did everything right, followed the rules as they understood them, and are now navigating a system that changed on them. The December portal is the last exit before the year is lost. And that exit opens in 14 days.
