Your 2027 College Softball Offseason Calendar: What Happens Between Now and Opening Day

By Sarah Mitchell | November 17, 2026 | I Love Softball

Signing Day was November 11. The class rankings followed. The debate about who won the recruiting season is well underway. And yet, for most fans, the next three months look like a gap — time between now and when it actually matters.

It is not a gap. The offseason from now through early spring is the period when the 2027 season gets built. Here is the calendar every college softball fan should be tracking.

December 1-15: Transfer Portal

The Division I softball transfer portal opens December 1 and closes December 15. This is the critical window where programs add the missing pieces they could not address in the summer cycle or on Signing Day.

This year's December window carries unusual weight. The 5-for-5 enforcement after the Tenth Circuit's November 6 reversal in Wisne v. NCAA means affected athletes who have not yet secured their situation are now weighing portal entry as the most immediate option available. Additional movement from that category adds names to the portal that were not expected to appear.

Programs to watch: Arizona (needs pitching after losing D1Softball National POY Marissa Torres to Texas Tech), FSU (lost both Torres and Jaysoni Beachum in the summer), and Ole Miss (rotation question marks entering December). The demand for quality pitching is high. The window is narrow. By December 15, the rosters that will take the field in February will be largely set.

January: Preseason Polls and Schedule Releases

January is when the season narrative gets its first formal framework. The NFCA, D1Softball, USA TODAY, and ESPN/USA Softball each release preseason Top 25 polls that reflect voter assessments of returning rosters, transfer additions, and recruiting class strength.

The 2027 polls will be the most closely watched in years. Oklahoma and LSU split the No. 1 class ranking between D1Softball and Softball America respectively, and both programs have legitimate arguments for the preseason top spot. Tennessee's returning pitching staff — led by senior Kelsey Mardjetko (16-3, 1.26 ERA) — may convince pitching-focused voters to put the Vols at the top instead.

Conference schedules also drop in January, giving fans the first look at when the marquee matchups occur. Big 12 fans will be particularly eager to see when Texas Tech (Kasidi Pickering's first Big 12 games), Oklahoma, Arizona, and Texas meet each other. Those head-to-head games will shape NCAA Tournament seeding discussions before March is finished.

Mid-January to Early February: Practices Begin

Programs open full practice around mid-January, moving from intersquads and fall schedules to full roster preparation for the spring season. This is where fall promise gets tested against the full competitive environment — new transfers meet the returning core, freshmen face varsity pitching for the first time, and spring lineups take shape.

The programs with the largest roster changes — Texas Tech (Pickering plus the No. 1 transfer class), LSU (six-player signing day class with two five-stars), and programs with multiple December portal additions — will be most watched during this period. How quickly new pieces integrate often predicts early-season results.

February: Season Opens

The 2027 season typically opens in mid-to-late February with neutral-site tournaments and early non-conference games. The first rankings of the season drop within days of the first games, and the debates that began in November get their first evidence.

Big 12 play for Texas Tech opens March 12 — the first time the reconstituted conference meets in full force with all its elite programs at peak roster strength. That March 12 date is circled on every Big 12 fan's calendar.

What's Next

Ninety days from now, the 2027 college softball season will be underway. The offseason moves between now and opening day — December portal additions, January poll placements, early season results — will shape the NCAA Tournament bracket, national seeding decisions, and ultimately the WCWS field in Oklahoma City.

Signing Day was last week. It feels like the beginning. The calendar says so, and it's right: for the programs competing at the highest level in 2027, the work has already started. Stay tuned.
