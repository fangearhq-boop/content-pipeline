Oklahoma vs. LSU: Who Gets the January Preseason No. 1 Vote?

By Sarah Mitchell | November 17, 2026 | I Love Softball

Signing Day answered one question and created another. D1Softball ranked Oklahoma No. 1 in the 2027 class. Softball America ranked LSU No. 1 — the first time in program history. Both are defensible. Neither is final. The vote that actually sets the narrative for the season is the one that comes in January: the NFCA preseason poll, D1Softball's first poll, USA TODAY, and ESPN/USA Softball's collegiate Top 25.

That vote is now three months away. And the debate about which program deserves the spot at the top has never been more competitive.

Oklahoma's Case: Veterans, Recruits, and a Freshman Already in Norman

Patty Gasso's argument for No. 1 starts with what she is returning. Kelsi Wells hit 39 home runs last season and was named the NFCA Freshman of the Year. Jocelyn Kavan was the Women's College World Series Most Outstanding Player — twice. Those two form a returning core that most programs in the country cannot match.

Then there is Julianna "Goose" Hutchens. Ranked No. 1 overall in the 2027 class before reclassifying, Hutchens is already enrolled at Oklahoma. She is a freshman with the talent evaluators considered the best in a class — and she is practicing this fall in Norman while other No. 1 recruits are still in high school.

D1Softball's evaluators trust Oklahoma's combination of elite returning talent and high-ceiling incoming players more than any other program in the country. Five-star outfielder Finlee Williams and five-star catcher Riley Hilliard round out what D1Softball calls the No. 1 class. The Sooners' case for a January preseason No. 1 is built on proven production and elite additions.

LSU's Case: Historic Class, Elite Pitching Depth

Beth Torina's program did something in November 2026 it has never done before: claimed the No. 1 class ranking from Softball America. That matters not just as a headline but as evidence of a program arriving at a new tier of national recruiting competitiveness.

The class is led by five-star pitcher/utility Sydney Gonglik, who Softball America rates as the No. 1 overall player. Four-star pitcher Macie Bryant joins her to give Torina an elite pitching duo arriving simultaneously. Add four-star catcher Jewel Cooper (No. 29), four-star infielder Bryli Seifert (No. 32), and four-star utility Lillie Gaudio and infielder Jillian Wimmer, and LSU's class addresses the program's most critical roster needs.

Softball America's methodology weights different factors than D1Softball's, and by those standards, the Tigers have assembled the best group of incoming players in the country. When preseason voters rank teams in January, LSU's historic class will be a centerpiece of the Tigers' argument for No. 1.

Tennessee's Wild Card: The Pitching Staff That Might Flip the Conversation

Neither Oklahoma nor LSU is guaranteed the January No. 1 spot. Tennessee is quietly building the most dangerous argument in college softball — a returning pitching staff that may be the best in the sport.

Senior Kelsey Mardjetko was 16-3 with a 1.26 ERA last season. Junior pitcher Nuwer threw four shutouts. True freshman Shelton showed 3 innings and 7 strikeouts in limited fall action before anyone had seen her at the college level. Head coach Karen Weekly has a contract extension through 2030 and arguably her deepest staff ever.

Media voters who prioritize pitching depth over recruiting rankings could make Tennessee the January No. 1 — a result that would surprise almost no one who has watched what Weekly has built in Knoxville.

What's Next

The January preseason polls will not settle the debate permanently. They will restart it with new information — class additions confirmed, returning rosters assessed, and postseason expectations translated into voter rankings. By the time the first pitch is thrown in February, the conversation about No. 1 will have changed three more times.

What won't change is the standard Signing Day set: this may be the deepest collection of elite programs in college softball history entering a single season. Oklahoma, LSU, Tennessee, Texas Tech, and Florida all have legitimate arguments for the top five. The preseason poll is the first verdict. It will not be the last.
