Caitlin Lowe's First Big Test: Finding an Ace in the December Portal

By Jordan Reeves | November 17, 2026 | I Love Softball

Arizona closed its fall slate 15-2. Big 12 Defensive Player of the Year Regan Shockey is coming back. Ten letterwinners return to a program that played its first season under new head coach Caitlin Lowe with remarkable competitive results. By almost every measure, Arizona is in position to compete at the highest level in a loaded Big 12 in 2027.

There is one problem. The best pitcher who wore an Arizona uniform last season is now in Lubbock.

The Torres Departure Changed Everything

Marissa Torres was the D1Softball National Player of the Year. When she entered the transfer portal in the summer and landed at Texas Tech, she took with her Arizona's ace, its identity in the circle, and the competitive centerpiece around which Lowe's staff had been building. Losing an ordinary pitcher in the portal is a setback. Losing the national POY is a program-reshaping event.

Arizona responded in the fall transfer cycle by adding four players, including former Iowa pitcher Jalen Adams and players with connections to the Oklahoma system. Those additions gave the Wildcats depth and versatility. They did not replace what Torres provided at the front of the rotation.

Caitlin Lowe knows what it takes to win at the highest level. She was a national champion player. She has built a staff around her. And she will tell you plainly what her program needs: a frontline pitcher who can carry a rotation into June.

What December Offers

The December transfer portal opens on December 1 and runs through December 15. For Arizona, it is the first real opportunity to address the program's most critical roster need. Other programs are also prioritizing pitching — FSU lost both Marissa Torres (via trade to TTU) and standout contributor Jaysoni Beachum in the summer cycle; Ole Miss enters December with rotation questions; the demand for quality pitching in the portal has never been higher.

Lowe's program has competitive advantages in this market. The Big 12 is ascending, and pitchers who want to play for a conference title have reasons to consider Tucson. Regan Shockey and the returning letterwinners make Arizona an attractive destination. The program's academic environment and Lowe's reputation as a former player who understands pitcher development both matter to prospects evaluating fits.

The competition for the same names is fierce. Texas Tech already acquired Torres. Programs that also need pitching are making the same calls to the same agents. Arizona must be aggressive, specific, and creative in how it approaches December.

The Stakes for Lowe's First Spring

Caitlin Lowe's debut as a college head coach will be evaluated partly on what happens in December. A program's ability to identify and acquire the right transfer at the right position is now as important a coaching skill as in-game strategy or game-day preparation.

If Lowe adds a frontline pitcher through the December portal, Arizona enters spring with a realistic path to the Big 12 tournament, the NCAA Tournament, and potentially the WCWS. The 15-2 fall record proves the talent is there. The Wildcats can hit, play defense, and compete with elite programs.

If the December window produces nothing, Arizona's ceiling drops. Not because the program lacks talent, but because a rotation without an ace — in a conference that now includes Texas Tech with Kasidi Pickering and a loaded OU lineup — is a rotation that will be tested beyond its limits by April.

What's Next

The portal opens December 1. Lowe and her staff have been building relationships within permissible recruiting rules since the summer. The names on Arizona's target list are not public, but the need is clear: a pitcher who can be the No. 1 arm on a team competing in the most competitive conference in college softball.

Lowe was hired to restore Arizona to national prominence. Her first fall built the foundation. December is where she starts building the ceiling. The portal is open in two weeks. Watch Tucson closely.
