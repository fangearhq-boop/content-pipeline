The Pickering Effect: Why Texas Tech Is a 2027 WCWS Title Contender

Emily Rawlings | November 17, 2026 | I Love Softball

Three seasons. Three hundred eighty-four batting average. Fifty home runs. One hundred sixty-nine runs batted in. NFCA First-Team All-American.

Those are Kasidi Pickering's numbers at Oklahoma. They are now Texas Tech's problem to solve for opposing pitchers — and the foundation of the most anticipated roster addition any Big 12 program has made in years.

What Pickering Brings to Lubbock

Kasidi Pickering's numbers at Oklahoma are generational. A .384 career batting average across three seasons is elite contact hitting. Fifty home runs is the kind of power that changes how opposing coaches build a game plan — not just for her plate appearances, but for every lineup spot around her. When a hitter forces pitch-around decisions in a college softball lineup, the batters before and after her benefit from better counts, more fastballs, and more opportunities to do damage.

At Texas Tech, Pickering slots into a lineup that already won against elite competition. The Red Raiders' summer transfer class added Bradi Gallaway, Jayden Heavener, and Moriah Polar — complementary players who can hit, field, and compete at the Big 12 level. Pickering is not being asked to carry a struggling lineup. She is being added to a good team that is now being asked to become a great one.

The Class That Surrounds Her

Softball America ranked Texas Tech's transfer class No. 1 nationally. That ranking reflects not just Pickering, but the totality of what Eric Glasco built in the portal in 2026. No single transfer haul assembled a more complete roster upgrade than what Texas Tech accomplished.

Then there is Jocelyn Gentry — the first five-star freshman in Texas Tech program history. Gentry's arrival signals that the Red Raiders are now recruiting at a level previously reserved for programs like Oklahoma, LSU, and Florida. When a program recruits its first five-star athlete, it changes the conversation at every kitchen table in the country for future prospects. Lubbock is now a destination.

Glasco enters his second full season at the program having already reshaped the roster twice through the portal. The 2026 campaign showed that the foundational culture is there — the WCWS experience proved they could compete with anyone. The 2027 roster is built to do more than compete.

Big 12 Play Starts March 12

Texas Tech opens Big 12 conference play on March 12. The first month of Big 12 games will likely include matchups against Oklahoma, Arizona, and Texas — three programs with their own elite-level arguments for WCWS contention.

No previous version of the Big 12 — the original or the reconstituted version that added Arizona and others — has had four programs with legitimate WCWS title expectations entering the same season. Oklahoma has Wells and Kavan and the No. 1 D1Softball class. Arizona has Shockey and Lowe and the best fall record in the conference. Texas is perennially dangerous. And Texas Tech has Pickering, the No. 1 transfer class, and a head coach building something that did not exist in Lubbock five years ago.

The team that emerges from those Big 12 matchups in March and April will almost certainly be a top-five national seed. The team that wins the Big 12 tournament might be the team that holds up the WCWS trophy in June.

What's Next

Texas Tech's December portal window is less urgent than Arizona's or FSU's — Glasco built his roster in the summer and on Signing Day. What comes next for the Red Raiders is managing expectations, maintaining chemistry as a roster with significant new faces comes together in the spring, and proving in conference play that the numbers on paper translate to wins in the Big 12 standings.

Kasidi Pickering hit 50 home runs at Oklahoma. If she produces at anything close to that level in Lubbock, Texas Tech will not just be a national title contender. They will be the team everyone else is building a game plan around. The Pickering era at Texas Tech begins March 12. Watch closely.
