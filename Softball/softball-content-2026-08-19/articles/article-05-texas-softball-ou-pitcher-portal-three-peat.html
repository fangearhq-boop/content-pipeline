Texas Softball Adds Former Oklahoma Pitcher Through Portal, Builds Toward Historic Three-Peat

By Emily Rawlings

August 19, 2026

The Texas-Oklahoma rivalry extended into the transfer portal this offseason, and the Longhorns came out on the winning end of another exchange.

Texas softball has added a former Oklahoma pitcher through the transfer portal, continuing a summer of additions that has positioned the back-to-back national champions as one of the most roster-complete programs in the country heading into fall camp. With 11 new additions to a 24-player squad, fall practice is giving head coach Mike White his first real look at how this group comes together ahead of a 2027 season with historic stakes.

Portal Dynamics Between the Programs

Oklahoma and Texas have been on opposite ends of the portal this offseason. While the Sooners navigated notable departures — including Kasidi Pickering's move to Texas Tech — Texas has been adding players at nearly every position. The addition of a former OU pitcher represents both a roster gain and a symbolic one: the program that ended Oklahoma's WCWS streak continues to build from the same talent pool.

That competition for talent is likely to continue. Both programs need pitching depth for a full college softball season, and the portal has made it easier to find it quickly. Texas's ability to attract a former OU arm reflects the Longhorns' standing as one of the most desirable destinations in the sport after consecutive national titles.

The Roster Taking Shape

Texas enters fall camp with Teagan Kavan as its anchor. Kavan became the first two-time Most Outstanding Player in Women's College World Series history when she led Texas to the 2026 national title — repeating the feat she accomplished in 2025. Her return for her senior season gives the Longhorns an ace whose resume already belongs in the sport's record books.

The coaching staff is complete. Fremin joins as associate head coach, bringing a decade of head coaching experience from SLU. Freeman handles hitting. The staff construction reflects White's preference for experienced assistants who have led programs of their own.

Texas went 49-12 in 2026. The 11 new additions bring depth at multiple positions and reflect a commitment to not just defending the title but chasing something no program has done since Oklahoma's dynasty ended in 2023.

The Historical Target

Three consecutive Women's College World Series titles have been won exactly twice in the history of the sport. UCLA did it from 1988 to 1990. Oklahoma did it from 2021 to 2023. No program has ever won four straight.

Texas is attempting to become the third program to win three in a row. The difficulty of that task is evident in the history: programs that win national titles often lose key contributors to the AUSL, to graduation, and to the portal. Sustaining the kind of roster quality and team cohesion required to win the WCWS is genuinely hard even when you are the defending champion.

The portal additions, including the former OU pitcher, are Texas's answer to that challenge. Do not let attrition accumulate — address it immediately and build depth in the offseason.

What's Next

Fall camp is underway in Austin. Watch for player reports and practice updates through September as White's staff evaluates the full 24-player group. Oklahoma's Battle Series events begin October 3, and Texas is expected to participate in fall competition as well. The first formal picture of the 2027 Longhorns will emerge by the end of October. The three-peat chase is real — the build to make it possible starts now.
