September 1 Is 13 Days Away: What Class of 2028 Softball Recruits Need Ready Now

By Jordan Reeves

August 19, 2026

Thirteen days from now, Division I softball coaches can pick up the phone and call Class of 2028 recruits directly for the first time. If you are a player or a family waiting for that call, the question is not whether you are ready to play softball. The question is whether you are ready for the recruiting process that is about to begin.

September 1 is the first day of the official D1 contact window for Class of 2028 athletes — meaning coaches can call, text, and make off-campus visits beginning that morning. With a record 1,226 athletes nominated in the Extra Inning Softball Class of 2028 rankings, the competition for roster spots is intense. The programs that will offer are already ready. Are you?

Have Your Film Ready

The single most important thing a recruit can do before September 1 is ensure that current, representative film is easily accessible. Not a highlight reel from two summers ago. Not a clip you have to email on request. A link that a coach can open in thirty seconds and see who you are as a player right now.

Coaches are calling on September 1 with specific evaluation questions already formed. They have often watched film informally, reached out to travel ball coaches, and attended showcases in the months before the contact window opened. The call is not an introduction — it is a conversation that builds on information they already have. If your film does not reflect your current skill level, you are starting the conversation at a disadvantage.

Rank Your Schools Before the Phone Rings

The best recruiting outcomes happen when athletes have done the internal work before the external pressure begins. Make a list of the programs you genuinely want to attend. Be honest about academics, location, playing time, and fit — not just reputation.

When a coach from one of your top schools calls on September 1, you want to be able to say, with confidence, that their program is a priority for you. When a coach from a program that was not on your list calls, you want to be able to respond with gratitude and clarity, without a conversation that leads anywhere it should not.

Know the list before the calls arrive.

Set a Family Decision Framework

One of the most common mistakes in the Class of 2028 recruiting cycle is the commitment made out of excitement rather than evaluation. September 1 is an emotional day. Coaches are enthusiastic, offers can feel like validation, and the pressure to commit quickly — because "the spot won't be there forever" — is real whether it comes explicitly or implicitly.

Before the window opens, sit down with your family and answer these questions: How long are we willing to take before committing? What are our non-negotiables on academics, culture, and playing time? What information do we need before we are ready to say yes? What does an official visit look like in our process?

Having those answers in place before September 1 means you can engage with every call thoughtfully instead of reactively.

Understand What Call Timing Means

Not every call on September 1 carries the same weight. Programs that call in the first hour of September 1 are sending a clear message: you are a top priority. Programs that call in the first week are still signaling strong interest. Programs that reach out in October are interested but likely filling secondary spots on their board.

This is not about judging programs — it is about understanding where you stand and making decisions accordingly. A program that calls you in October might still be the right fit. But knowing where you are on their list is information you should have when you are evaluating offers.

September 1, 2026 is 13 days away. Between now and then, finalize your film, confirm your school list, and have the family framework conversation.
