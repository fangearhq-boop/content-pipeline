19 Days: What September 1 Means for Class of 2028 Softball Recruits

By Emily Rawlings

August 13, 2026

For every Class of 2028 softball player who has spent the summer at showcases, in coaches' film queues, and in the inbox of every Division I program they've dreamed about, the countdown has been simple: September 1. That date — now 19 days away — is when the door opens. D1 coaches can make direct phone calls for the first time. Texts. Emails. The full weight of the recruiting process lands all at once.

The Class of 2028 is heading into that window as the most-watched recruiting class in Extra Inning Softball's history. A record 1,226 athletes received nominations — the highest total for any class the service has tracked. The Extra Elite 100 position rankings are already published. Programs have been building their boards since spring showcases. They're ready to call. The question is whether recruits are ready to receive.

What Changes on September 1

Prior to September 1, NCAA Division I coaches face strict limits on how they can reach Class of 2028 athletes. Written materials and certain electronic communications are permitted — but direct phone calls, text messages, and official in-person contacts are off the table until that date hits. Coaches can watch athletes at summer showcases and make evaluations, but they cannot follow up with a direct recruiting pitch.

September 1 removes all of those restrictions simultaneously. The moment midnight passes, coaches can call recruits they've been watching all summer. Official visits can be scheduled. For athletes who have already built relationships through camps, email exchanges, and third-party coaches, that first direct call from a program is confirmation: they've been on your board this whole time.

A Record Class Heading Into the Window

The record 1,226 EIS nominations for the Class of 2028 reflect both the size of the sport and the intensity with which programs are evaluating it. Extra Inning Softball's nomination system has historically been an accurate predictor of recruiting traffic — more nominations means more programs are doing formal evaluations earlier, which tends to accelerate the timeline for top prospects to receive offers.

For 2028 recruits near the top of the Extra Elite 100 position rankings, offers may already be informal — and September 1 converts those conversations from hopeful to official. For athletes further down the board, the first week of September is often when their names start moving across program lists as coaches fill gaps on their rosters.

How to Use the Next 19 Days

Recruits who haven't already done so should ensure their recruiting profiles are updated with current film — ideally from a recent summer showcase — along with academic information, current metrics, and a fall schedule that shows coaches when and where they can be evaluated in person. Programs receiving 200-plus prospect emails on September 1 will prioritize athletes whose information is ready to act on.

The days between now and September 1 are also a final window to send follow-up emails or video messages to programs that have shown interest without a formal commitment. A brief, specific note — "I watched your fall schedule and I'll be at the October showcase in your region" — is the kind of touchpoint that keeps a recruit's name in front of a coaching staff when the contact window opens.
