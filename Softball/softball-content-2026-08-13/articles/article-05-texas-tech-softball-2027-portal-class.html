Texas Tech Softball Is Building Again — and the 2027 Roster Is Already Taking Shape

By Emily Rawlings

August 13, 2026

Bailey Lindemuth entered the transfer portal after the 2026 season. In Lubbock, that kind of roster news would have felt like a crisis two years ago. Under Gerry Glasco, it's just the offseason. The Texas Tech head coach guided the Red Raiders to their first-ever Women's College World Series appearance in 2026, where they lost to Texas in the final. Now, with that same core returning and another round of transfer portal additions already reportedly in place, the 2027 off-season is looking like a continuation of the build — not a rebuilding moment.

What Returns for 2027

The core of the 2026 team that reached the WCWS Finals is intact. NiJaree Canady — the 2026 AUSL Draft No. 2 pick and one of the most dominant pitchers in the country — enters her senior year. Kasidi Pickering, who transferred from Oklahoma with .370 career batting average and 50 career home runs, is back. Natalie Heavener, the LSU transfer who posted a 2.81 ERA across 139.1 innings in 2026, returns in the rotation.

Those three players represent the kind of established production that most programs can only recruit toward. Texas Tech has them on the same roster heading into 2027, making them a preseason top-five program before fall camps have even opened.

The Portal Class for 2027

Reports indicate Glasco has already secured four significant transfer portal additions for the 2027 roster, though the specific names have not yet been publicly confirmed. After the 2026 portal class was ranked No. 1 nationally — a class that included Pickering, Heavener, and other key contributors to the WCWS run — the expectation is that Glasco's second portal class will be built with equal ambition.

The framework for Texas Tech's recruitment is clear by now: target players coming from programs with winning culture, players with positions of need, and players who have faced adversity in the portal process and responded. That formula built a WCWS finalist roster in one calendar year. What it builds in two is the open question.

Glasco's Unprecedented First Year

What Gerry Glasco accomplished in 2026 has no real parallel in recent college softball history. A first-year head coach at a program without a WCWS Finals tradition guided that program to the national championship game, losing to the sport's most dominant team in back-to-back title Texas. No first-year coach at Texas Tech had ever come close to that result.

That success changes the recruiting calculus significantly. The players Texas Tech can target in the Class of 2028 — or in the portal — will be players who watched the 2026 WCWS and saw a Red Raiders program capable of competing at the highest level. The program credibility Glasco built in Year 1 is an asset that compounds into Year 2 and beyond.

What's Next

Texas Tech softball heads into fall camp as one of the preseason favorites for the 2027 national championship. The specific names of the program's 2027 portal additions are expected to be announced publicly in the coming weeks.
