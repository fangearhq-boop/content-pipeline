Texas Has Back-to-Back WCWS Titles. Now Teagan Kavan and the Longhorns Are Chasing History.

By Jordan Reeves

August 13, 2026

The 2026 Women's College World Series ended in June with Texas sweeping Texas Tech in the final, 4-1 in Game 2, at Devon Park in Oklahoma City. It was the same opponent, the same venue, and nearly the same result as 2025. Back-to-back national champions. The first rematch final in the sport in 22 years — and Texas won it again.

At the center of it, as she has been for two consecutive championship runs, was Teagan Kavan. The junior pitcher — now entering her senior season — won the Women's College World Series Most Outstanding Player award for the second consecutive year, becoming the first player in the tournament's history to win back-to-back MOPs. Not the first pitcher. Not the first in the last decade. The first ever.

What Kavan Means to This Program

The MOP distinction matters because the WCWS is the sport's most pressure-compressed environment. Single elimination from the bracket stage, best-of-three in the finals, against the best lineups the country produces. Kavan has now pitched in — and won — two of those championship series. She threw a complete-game shutout in the 2025 WCWS final and carried that form into 2026, leading the Longhorns through a 49-12 season (16-8 in SEC play) to another title.

As a senior in 2027, Kavan's final collegiate season will be among the most closely watched in the sport's history. The questions she'll hear all spring — can she win a third? Can Texas become the first program to win three consecutive WCWS titles? — are the kinds of questions that define legacies. She's already in the conversation for the best WCWS pitcher of her generation.

Mike White's Standard

Head coach Mike White has built something in Austin that has genuinely changed what people believe is possible in college softball. The roster construction, the recruiting, and the in-season management that produced two consecutive championships against increasingly difficult fields — with Texas Tech specifically making the jump to a WCWS finalist — required execution at every level.

White's program enters 2027 as the established target. Every program in the country will have circled the Longhorns on their schedule. Every elite recruit will have had discussions with Texas. The standard is set. Maintaining it — and pushing beyond it — is the challenge that defines dynasty-building in any sport.

What a Three-Peat Would Mean

Three consecutive national championships would be historic in NCAA softball. The sport's depth, transfer portal activity, and rising talent from programs like Texas Tech, Oklahoma, and UCLA make it difficult enough to win once. Winning twice is rare. Winning three times in a row would require Kavan to sustain her peak performance through a final season, a roster that can support her, and a bracket draw that doesn't put the Longhorns in an impossible position early.

None of that is guaranteed. What is guaranteed is that the 2027 conversation around Texas softball will start from a premise that would have seemed unlikely five years ago: the Longhorns are the defending champions, they have the best pitcher in the sport, and they have done this before.

What's Next

Texas softball enters fall camp as the defending back-to-back national champions. The 2027 Women's College World Series will be held at Devon Park in Oklahoma City in June 2027. Preseason rankings, when released later this fall, will establish Texas as the team to beat. Follow the Longhorns' 2027 campaign at texaslonghorns.com and at ncaa.com/softball.
