10th Circuit Grants NCAA Stay: Class of 2022 Fifth-Year Eligibility Door Closes — For Now

By Sarah Mitchell | October 16, 2026 | I Love Softball

The fifth-year eligibility window for Class of 2022 college softball players has closed again — at least for now.

On August 21, the 10th Circuit Court of Appeals voted 2-1 to grant the NCAA an emergency stay in Wisne et al. v. NCAA, placing Judge Charlotte Sweeney's class-wide preliminary injunction on hold while the full appeal is decided. The stay means the NCAA's age-based eligibility rules — which prohibit athletes older than 24 from competing — are back in force for Class of 2022 athletes who had believed they were eligible to return for a fifth season.

What the Stay Means Practically

When Judge Sweeney issued her preliminary injunction earlier this summer, it briefly opened a door. Class of 2022 athletes who had exhausted their eligibility in the 2025-26 season were told they might have a path back to a fifth year. Some athletes re-enrolled. Some programs added them back to rosters.

The 10th Circuit's stay slams that door shut — at least pending the appeal. Per the NCAA, Class of 2022 athletes who had returned under the injunction are now ineligible again. The NCAA has stated that it will not penalize programs that added those athletes back to rosters in good faith while the injunction was in place, which limits the institutional damage but does not resolve the individual athletes' situation.

The case remains active. An expedited appeal is underway in the 10th Circuit, though no ruling date has been announced. Until the appellate court decides, the stay remains in effect.

A Narrow Exception in Tennessee

The national stay does not necessarily represent the final word everywhere. A state court in Tennessee denied the NCAA's request for a stay in a separate state-level lawsuit — a parallel case that may offer a narrow avenue for some Class of 2022 athletes in that state to pursue eligibility through the state courts.

This is a complex, multi-front legal landscape. Softball players and programs navigating these questions should consult with their compliance departments and legal counsel rather than acting on any single piece of reporting. The situation continues to evolve.

The Bigger Context

The Wisne v. NCAA case is part of a broader wave of post-House settlement litigation reshaping college athletics. Judge Sweeney's August 2 clarification had already narrowed the injunction — it did not override transfer rules, roster caps, or revenue-sharing provisions under the House settlement framework. The stay narrows it further, at least temporarily.

For softball specifically, this eligibility saga has been one of the most consequential legal stories since the COVID waiver years. Programs that thought they were getting veterans back are now operating with the pre-injunction roster reality. The coaches, athletes, and compliance officers who spent weeks navigating these decisions are now waiting again.

What's Next

The 10th Circuit's expedited appeal is the next major development to watch. A ruling in favor of the plaintiffs would reinstate the injunction and potentially re-open eligibility. A ruling in favor of the NCAA would end the injunction entirely. Either outcome will have immediate implications for programs that have been planning around this uncertainty.

We will continue tracking the appeal as it moves through the courts. In the meantime: the door is closed, the NCAA has said programs will not be penalized for good-faith actions, and the legal fight continues.
