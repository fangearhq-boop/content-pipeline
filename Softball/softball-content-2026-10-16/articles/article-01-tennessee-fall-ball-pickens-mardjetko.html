Tennessee Softball Is 3-0 in Fall Ball — and Karlyn Pickens and Sage Mardjetko Are Just the Start

By Jordan Reeves | October 16, 2026 | I Love Softball

It has been a quiet October in Knoxville — no major headlines, no drama, just Tennessee softball going 3-0 in fall ball and head coach Karen Weekly quietly declaring this the deepest pitching staff she has ever assembled in her career with the Lady Vols.

That's not a throwaway line. Karen Weekly has been coaching Tennessee softball since 1999. She has built programs that have gone to the Women's College World Series, developed pitchers who have played professionally, and run one of the most consistent operations in the SEC. When she says deepest, she means it.

The Top of the Rotation

Karlyn Pickens is the name everyone knows. The senior ace owns four career no-hitters, is a two-time First Team All-SEC selection, and was the No. 1 overall pick by the Carolina Blaze in the 2026 Athletes Unlimited Softball League draft. She is entering what figures to be her final season in Knoxville, and there is every reason to believe she intends to go out on her terms.

Sage Mardjetko is Pickens' equal in terms of impact. Last season, Mardjetko went 16-3 with a 1.26 ERA and 171 strikeouts — numbers that earned her NFCA First Team All-American honors and cemented her as one of the best pitchers in the country. She is the definition of a staff ace, and the fact that she pitches behind Pickens at Tennessee says something about the depth of this program.

Behind them: Erin Nuwer, Kailey Plumlee, and transfer Peyton Hardenburger round out a rotation with options at every level of a lineup. Maddi Rutan, a transfer who drew early fall attention, adds even more depth. Weekly can go five pitchers deep on any given night and not feel like she is reaching.

The Fall Record Tells a Story

Tennessee is 3-0, with the most notable result being a 19-1 run-rule win over Carson-Newman. A schedule disruption — weather forced the cancellation of an October 10 game against Roane State, which was rescheduled to October 21 — has not slowed momentum.

The Lady Vols' next fall game is October 18 at Middle Tennessee (3 PM ET), followed by the rescheduled Roane State matchup on October 21. All fall games are free and open to the public.

The returning piece to watch on offense is All-SEC outfielder Alannah Leach, who brings her twin sister Gabby Leach alongside her this season. Transfers Sophia Knight and Mackenzie Butt have drawn fall attention for their offensive contributions. The one notable absence is starting second baseman Ella Dodge, who transferred to Florida State in the offseason — a loss the coaching staff has been working to fill.

What's Next

October 18 at MTSU is the next test, followed by the rescheduled Roane State game on October 21. But the real test begins in February, when Tennessee steps into a 2027 SEC schedule loaded with familiar rivals and a new set of expectations.

This is a program that knows what it takes to compete at the highest level. Pickens and Mardjetko give Tennessee a pitching advantage against nearly every team in the country. If the offense clicks — and the early fall numbers suggest it will — the Lady Vols are one of the legitimate national title contenders heading into 2027. The October quiet is just the setup. Watch what happens in February.
