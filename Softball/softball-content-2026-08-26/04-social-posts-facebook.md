### STORY 1: AUSL All-Star Cup — Series 3 Complete, Klingler Leads

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 9:00 AM ET

Series 3 is in the books — and the individual race just had its biggest shift yet.

This is significant: Kayla Kowalik entered Series 3 in first place at 860 points. After three series, Klingler has overtaken her. The athlete who had led all summer no longer holds the top spot heading into the final week.

Amber Fiser and Reese Atwood each made major jumps during Series 3 — Fiser climbed 13 places, Atwood moved up 14 — both now sitting inside the top 15.

Series 4 begins Friday, August 28 at 7 PM ET on CBS Sports Network. The individual champion is crowned August 30. Four days. Three captains. One title.

### STORY 2: September 1 Countdown — 6 Days

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 11:00 AM ET

Six days to September 1. Here's what that means.

The preparation phase is over. The Class of 2028 recruits who are going to receive first-hour calls on September 1 have already been identified by D1 coaches. Those call-order lists were finalized last week or this week — the programs with the most serious interest in a recruit have already ranked her internally.

What can you still do in six days? You can confirm your interest. A short, clear message to the programs at the top of your list — "I'm looking forward to hearing from you September 1, this program is a priority for me" — is still meaningful. It doesn't change your ranking on a list that's already set, but it reinforces your commitment in the moment right before the phone starts ringing.

What you cannot do is manufacture interest that was never there, or make a program care about you with a single message they weren't already excited about. The real work happened earlier — in your junior film, your summer showcase performances, your camp visits, your consistent communication since June.

September 9 at 7:00 AM ET is the in-person contact window. That's when D1 coaches can show up at your high school or watch you at a fall tournament in person for the first time.

Here's what September 1 actually looks like: The phone starts ringing before 12:01 AM in some states. Within the first hour, the recruiters who are most serious about you will have tried to reach you. By noon, most programs will have worked through their priority list. By the end of the day, the landscape of your recruiting will look very different than it did on August 31.

Six days. Know your programs. Be ready to answer.

### STORY 3: USA WNT — 17 Days, Garcia and Mulipola as Veteran Anchors

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 3:00 PM ET

Seventeen days until USA Softball opens Group Oklahoma at Devon Park in Oklahoma City — and experience is going to matter.

Rachel Garcia and Dejah Mulipola are the two 2020 U.S. Olympians on the 16-player World Cup roster. These are not nominal roster spots. These are athletes who competed on the biggest stage international softball has — who have played in knockout-round situations where one bad at-bat or one shaky inning can end a medal chase.

The 2026 WBSC Women's Softball World Cup Group Stage runs September 12-16 in Oklahoma City. Six nations compete round-robin. Only the top two advance to the 2027 Finals in Redcliffe, Queensland — the direct path to LA28.

The group: USA (No. 2 in the world), Netherlands (No. 6), Mexico (No. 7), China (No. 8), New Zealand (No. 23), South Africa (No. 36).

USA opens against Mexico on September 12. The Netherlands plays later in the group stage. These are the two matchups that will define whether USA clinches the top seed or faces a tighter path to the top two. Mexico (No. 7) is athletic and pitch-first. The Netherlands (No. 6) is technically disciplined and has competed at the top of international softball for decades.

With Garcia and Mulipola as experience anchors alongside seven World Games gold medalists and four Sooners (Brito, Coleman, Jennings, Maxwell), this roster has both proven talent and competitive depth. The question in Group Oklahoma won't be whether USA belongs in the top two. It will be how they handle the pressure moments.

Seventeen days.

### STORY 4: Oklahoma — First Battle Series Scrimmage Is Done

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 1:00 PM ET

Oklahoma has had its first public look at the 2026-27 roster — and the crowd that showed up Friday night wants more.

The Sooners held their first Battle Series intrasquad scrimmage at Love's Field in Norman, drawing 2,351 fans for an early glimpse at a roster that has been rebuilt around significant transfer additions and a highly ranked freshman class. LSU transfer Sydney Berzon — a two-time All-American with a career 2.02 ERA and 406 strikeouts across 486.1 innings with the Tigers — took the mound in a Sooners uniform for the first time in front of a live crowd.

That's the headline for a team that missed the Women's College World Series in 2026 for the first time since 2015: the ace has arrived, and she can play.

The Battle Series format runs six intrasquad scrimmages with aggregate scoring, giving Patty Gasso a competitive internal environment to evaluate depth and establish roles before the regular season. Four of OU's key returners — Alyssa Brito, Jayda Coleman, Tiare Jennings, and Kelly Maxwell — are still away with the USA Women's National Team through September 16. That means the Battle Series is also a formative period for the players who are on campus right now.

Macie Harter (.346 BA, 7 HR, 23 RBI from Middle Tennessee State) is also part of this group — working her way into the lineup alongside the existing returners and the incoming freshman class that includes No. 2 overall commit Payton Westra and No. 4 overall commit Ori Mailo, both already on campus.

The first public Battle Series events open October 3 at Love's Field ($10 general admission). If 2,351 people showed up for an intrasquad scrimmage, the fall schedule is going to be worth watching.

### STORY 5: Teagan Kavan — 500 Career Ks, Two MVPs, One More Season

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 8:00 PM ET

In April, Teagan Kavan struck out her 500th career batter against Oklahoma. The milestone was almost lost in the noise of a Texas team that had already locked up a postseason berth and was rolling toward another national title run.

That's how good this has been.

Kavan ended the 2026 season with 260 strikeouts (a career high), a 2.33 ERA, and 228.2 innings pitched. She went 4-1 in the Women's College World Series with two saves and 33.1 innings in Oklahoma City. Texas went 49-12. They won the national championship. For the second year in a row, Kavan walked away with WCWS Most Outstanding Player honors — the first pitcher in WCWS history to win the award twice.

Now she's back in Austin for her senior season. Fall camp is underway. The home opener against Texas State is September 5.

The roster beside her is better. D1Softball National Player of the Year Isa Torres transferred in from Florida State, where she hit .530 this past spring. Katie Stewart — who set the Texas home run record with 31 this season — returns. The pieces are there for something historic.

Three consecutive national championships. Only two programs have ever done it: UCLA from 1988 to 1990, and Oklahoma from 2021 to 2023. Texas is trying to become the third.

Kavan is the pitcher at the center of that bid. Five hundred career strikeouts, two MVP awards, and a senior season that hasn't started yet.
