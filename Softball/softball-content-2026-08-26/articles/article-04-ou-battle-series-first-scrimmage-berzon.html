OU's First Battle Series Scrimmage Is Done. Here Are Three Takeaways.

By Sarah Mitchell | I Love Softball | August 26, 2026

Oklahoma held its first Battle Series intrasquad scrimmage Friday night at Love's Field in Norman, drawing 2,351 fans for an early look at a roster that has been rebuilt with significant transfer talent alongside returning players. LSU transfer Sydney Berzon — a two-time All-American with a career 2.02 ERA across 486 innings — took the mound in a Sooners uniform in front of a live crowd for the first time. It was one data point in a fall that will produce many more, but it was a meaningful one. Here are three things that stood out.

1. Berzon Is Already Working

Sydney Berzon's arrival was one of the biggest news stories of the offseason for Oklahoma softball. A two-time All-American from LSU with a career 2.02 ERA and 406 strikeouts across 486.1 innings is not a portal addition that needs evaluation — she has already proven herself at the highest level of college softball. But proving that she can mesh with a new program, command a new system, and perform in front of a new crowd takes time. Friday was step one.

Berzon's participation in the Battle Series scrimmage means the integration is underway in a competitive environment. Gasso has always used fall competition to evaluate and build roles, not just condition players. Berzon being on the mound in the first scrimmage signals she is being treated as a frontline contributor from the start — not eased in, but inserted into the competitive mix immediately.

2. The Lineup Still Has Gaps

Four of Oklahoma's most prominent players — Alyssa Brito, Jayda Coleman, Tiare Jennings, and Kelly Maxwell — are away with the USA Women's National Team through September 16. Their absence from fall camp creates both a challenge and an opportunity. The challenge is that the internal competition is happening without all of the pieces present. The opportunity is that players lower on the depth chart get extended looks in a competitive environment before the starters return.

Macie Harter — who hit .346 with 7 home runs and 23 RBIs at Middle Tennessee State before transferring to OU — is one of the players in this group. So is the 2027 incoming class, which includes No. 2 overall recruit Payton Westra and No. 4 overall recruit Ori Mailo. Both are already on campus. Both are competing for playing time in the Battle Series format before Brito, Coleman, Jennings, and Maxwell return from the national team.

3. The Crowd Shows This Team Has an Audience

Two thousand, three hundred and fifty-one people showed up for an intrasquad scrimmage in late August. That is not a small number for an event with no opponent, no stakes, and no television audience. It tells you something about where Oklahoma softball stands in the program's community after missing the WCWS last year for the first time since 2015.

The fan base showed up Friday because they want to see what the rebuild looks like. They wanted to see Berzon. They wanted to see the new class. They wanted to see Gasso's team take the field with competitive intent. That level of engagement — sustained even through a difficult season and into a new fall — is something programs work years to build. OU has it. The first Battle Series scrimmage confirmed that the audience is still there.

What's Next

Oklahoma's Battle Series continues through fall camp. Public Battle Series events at Love's Field begin October 3 (general admission $10). The four Sooners away with the USA Women's National Team — Brito, Coleman, Jennings, and Maxwell — return after September 16. The 2026-27 regular season tips off in the spring.
