Kavan Has 500 Career Ks and Two WCWS MVPs. Now She Wants Three Championships.

By Emily Rawlings | I Love Softball | August 26, 2026

In April 2026, Teagan Kavan struck out her 500th career batter against Oklahoma — a milestone that arrived in the middle of a season that would end, as her previous one had, with a national championship. Kavan went 4-1 in the 2026 Women's College World Series. She threw 33.1 innings. She earned WCWS Most Outstanding Player honors for the second consecutive year — the first pitcher in tournament history to win the award twice. Texas went 49-12. They won it all again.

Now fall camp is underway in Austin, the home opener against Texas State is scheduled for September 5, and Kavan enters her senior season with a clear mission: make it three in a row.

What the Numbers Say About Her Career

Kavan's 2026 season was her best statistically. She finished with a 2.33 ERA, 260 strikeouts — a career high — and 228.2 innings pitched. Over four years at Texas, she has accumulated 500-plus career strikeouts while anchoring a staff that has won back-to-back national championships. The combination of workload, efficiency, and postseason production places her among the most accomplished pitchers in the history of the program.

The 2x WCWS MOP is the headline statistic — because it is the only one of its kind. No pitcher in the 46-year history of the Women's College World Series had ever won Most Outstanding Player twice before Kavan. She holds that distinction alone.

The Roster Around Her Got Better

The most notable offseason addition to Texas's roster is Isa Torres, the 2026 D1Softball National Player of the Year. Torres transferred from Florida State, where she hit .530 this past spring — one of the highest single-season batting averages ever recorded at the Division I level. She is a Gold Glove outfielder who hit 16 home runs and drove in 56 runs as the nation's best offensive player. She is now at Texas.

Returning alongside Kavan and Torres is Katie Stewart, who set the Texas home run record with 31 this past season. The Longhorns go into fall camp with a roster that was already a national champion and added arguably the most decorated offensive transfer in the sport's recent history.

The Historical Context of the Three-Peat

Only two programs have ever won three consecutive NCAA Division I softball national championships. UCLA did it from 1988 to 1990. Oklahoma did it from 2021 to 2023. Texas, with championships in 2025 and 2026, is now attempting to become the third.

The challenge of the three-peat in softball is rooted in the same dynamics that make it rare in any sport: key players graduate, the field improves in response to success, the tournament format allows upsets, and the mental weight of defending a title is different from the hunger of chasing one. Texas will arrive in 2027 as the team everyone is hunting.

Kavan understands this. She has been part of Texas softball long enough to know what it means to be the program everyone wants to beat. She also has two national titles to draw from — and a senior season to prove she still belongs at the top.
