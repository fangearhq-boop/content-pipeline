6 Days to September 1: What Happens When the Phone Starts Ringing

By Emily Rawlings | I Love Softball | August 26, 2026

Six days from now, September 1 arrives — and the Class of 2028 recruiting window officially opens. For the first time, Division I coaches can call, text, and email the high school juniors they have been watching all summer. Some recruits will have their phones ring before the sun comes up. Others will wait hours. Some will not hear from their top school on September 1 at all. What happens on that day — and how to make sense of it — is worth understanding before the moment arrives.

What September 1 Actually Means

Until September 1, Division I coaches have been permitted to send materials and some electronic communications to rising 2028 juniors, but they cannot call, text, or appear at a high school practice. September 1 removes those restrictions for phone and digital contact. That shift changes everything about the pace and tone of the recruiting process.

Programs that have been building relationships through questionnaires, camp invitations, and limited electronic communication for months can now have direct conversations. Coaches can call from their personal phones. Assistants can follow up the same day. The timeline moves from weeks to hours.

September 9 at 7:00 AM Eastern Time is when in-person contact opens. That is the date when Division I coaches can show up at a high school practice, watch a fall tournament, or arrange an off-campus meeting. September 1 is the verbal and digital window. September 9 is the in-person window.

The Call-Order List Is Already Set

Programs with serious interest in a recruit have already ranked their prospects internally. Coaches have a priority list — typically eight to twelve recruits for the first-hour calls — and they work through it systematically from midnight or from the moment the coaching staff starts their day on September 1.

The position on that list was determined by everything that happened before now: junior film quality, summer showcase performance, camp attendance, consistency of communication, and demonstrated academic readiness. A recruit who has had ongoing contact with a program since the spring and checked in consistently will be higher on that list than one who went quiet after a showcase.

Is there anything to do in six days that changes a position on the list? Not fundamentally. But confirming interest through a brief, direct message still has value. Something like: "I'm looking forward to hearing from you September 1 — this program is one of my priorities." It does not rearrange a list that is already finalized. What it does is put a name at the front of a recruiter's mind in the final days before the phone lines open.

How to Handle September 1 When It Arrives

Be ready to have real conversations. Coaches who call on September 1 are calling because they want to make an impression early — the first program to reach out signals genuine, active interest. Have the key information ready: your class schedule for the upcoming semester, your fall tournament schedule, and any planned campus visits. Being prepared to discuss these concretely makes the first call more productive for both sides.

Do not measure a program's interest by when it calls. Some programs systematically work through every recruit on their list on September 1. Others call in waves over the first week. A call on September 3 from a program that had you in its top priority list all summer does not mean less than a midnight call from a program that had you lower on the board. Context matters.

What's Next

September 1, 2026 is six days away. September 9 at 7:00 AM ET is when in-person contact opens. Class of 2028 recruits: confirm your priorities, prepare for direct conversations, and know that the programs most interested in you have been thinking about this call for months. Be ready to answer.
