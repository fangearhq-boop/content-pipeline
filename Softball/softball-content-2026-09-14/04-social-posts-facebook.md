Three wins in three games. That's the story of Team USA at the WBSC Women's Softball World Cup Group Stage in Oklahoma City, and the best part? The tournament is just getting started.

### STORY 2: Oklahoma Sooners Fall Camp

**Long-Form Post (11:00 AM ET)**
⚡ GASSO COMES HOME MONDAY — AND THE SOONERS WILL BE READY

Patty Gasso, JT Gasso, and Amanda Rocha have been coaching Team USA at the WBSC World Cup Group Stage in Oklahoma City this week. After the Group Stage playoffs wrap on September 16, they head straight back to Norman.

That means the Sooners — who have been holding down fort during fall camp — are about to get their head coach back.

And what a fall camp it's been. Kendall Wells, the NFCA Freshman of the Year and 2026 SEC Freshman of the Year, set the NCAA freshman home run record (32) in her first year and has been the centerpiece of Oklahoma's fall workouts. Sydney Berzon, the LSU transfer with a 2.02 ERA and 406 career strikeouts, has been competing against her all fall — including scrimmages where Wells homered off Berzon twice.

Macie Harter (.346 BA at MTSU) rounds out a pitching staff that could be elite.

The fall opener is September 30 vs. Tarleton State — just 16 days away.

### STORY 4: Texas Longhorns

**Long-Form Post (3:00 PM ET)**
🤘 DEFENDING CHAMPIONS — AND THEY STILL HAVE EVERYTHING

Texas went 53-12 in 2026. They swept Texas Tech in the Women's College World Series. They won their second straight WCWS title and earned the NFCA Staff of the Year honor for the second year in a row.

Now, 15 days before their fall opener (September 29 vs. Murray State at McCombs Field), the Longhorns are quietly building what could be the most dangerous roster in college softball.

The headliner is Reese Torres — the 2026 D1Softball Player of the Year. Torres transferred from FSU (.530 BA, 16 HR, 56 RBI) and is a Georgetown, Texas native, coming home. Playing for the team that just won it all.

Then there's Jayda Kavan, entering her senior year. Kavan is the only player in WCWS history to win the Most Outstanding Player award TWICE. She's chasing something only two programs have ever accomplished: a three-peat national championship. UCLA did it in 1988-90. Oklahoma did it in 2021-23.

Texas is going for a three-peat in 2027.

The fall opener is free admission. September 29 at McCombs Field. 6PM CT. First look at what this team can do.

### STORY 5: 4 Programs to Watch This Fall

**Long-Form Post (8:00 PM ET)**
👀 4 FALL PROGRAMS YOU NEED TO BE WATCHING

Every fall, the race for the 2027 national championship quietly begins while most of college softball is focused on recruiting. Here are four programs that are going to matter next year — and are building something special RIGHT NOW in fall camp:

🔴 **Nebraska** — The Huskers went to the WCWS in 2026 (losing to Texas in the semifinals) and are primed for a deep run in 2027. Their fall schedule includes exhibitions against Oklahoma State and they're trending as a legitimate national title contender.

⚫️ **Oklahoma State** — The Cowgirls lost to Nebraska in the Lincoln Super Regional, but they came back this offseason and added five high-impact transfers (Mississippi State, Utah, Houston, Ole Miss, Wisconsin). They're hosting eight fall exhibitions and landed their first 2028 pledge.

🔵 **Duke** — One of the surprising dark horse picks for 2027. Duke has been quietly building toward a WCWS-caliber program and is generating real buzz heading into fall camp.

🔴 **Texas A&M** — The Aggies came in a big way in the Class of 2028 recruiting cycle, pulling in multiple Top 30 prospects. With talent stacking up, A&M is a name to watch as the 2027 season approaches.

The best part? Fall camp is public and free at most of these programs. Go watch.
