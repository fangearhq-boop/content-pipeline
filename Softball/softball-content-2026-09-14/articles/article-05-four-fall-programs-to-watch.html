Four College Softball Programs to Watch This Fall: Nebraska, OSU, Duke, and Texas A&M

By Emily Rawlings | September 14, 2026

The conversation around college softball in September tends to center on Texas, Oklahoma, and the other programs that just won — or nearly won — the WCWS. That makes sense. But the fall is also when the next wave of contenders begins to separate itself from the field.

Here are four programs outside the familiar power-brand conversation that are generating real buzz this fall and are worth watching closely as the 2027 season approaches.

Nebraska — WCWS Muscle Memory Is Real

The Huskers went to Oklahoma City in 2026 and made a run in the Women's College World Series before falling to Texas in the semifinals. That's not a program to dismiss. Nebraska leaves the WCWS having experienced the atmosphere, the stage, and the pressure — and returns with most of that core for 2027.

Nebraska is hosting fall exhibitions this fall, including a matchup with Oklahoma State, and is trending as a genuine national title contender for 2027. Programs that make WCWS semifinal appearances tend to take a major step the following year. The Huskers fit the profile.

Oklahoma State — Rebuilding With Intention

The Cowgirls had a difficult ending in 2026, falling to Nebraska in the Lincoln Super Regional. But the program didn't sit still. Oklahoma State added five transfers in the offseason: pitcher Delainey Everett from Mississippi State, catcher Kennedy Proctor from Utah, utility player Maddie Hartley from Houston, pitcher/utility Payton Kennedy from Ole Miss, and pitcher/utility Kendra Lewis from Wisconsin.

That's a meaningful infusion of talent at key positions. OSU is hosting eight fall exhibitions and already landed its first 2028 pledge in top-100 infielder Peyton Cleary from Liberty North High School in Missouri. The Cowgirls are building, not retreating.

Duke — The Dark Horse Nobody Expected

Duke has emerged as one of the more intriguing dark horse picks for 2027. The Blue Devils have been quietly assembling a roster and developing a culture capable of making national noise. For a program that doesn't often appear in WCWS bracket projections, the buzz around Duke this fall is unusually loud.

Keeping an eye on Duke's fall results and any significant recruiting news between now and December will be worth the effort. Dark horses tend to make their presence known in the fall before shocking people in March.

Texas A&M — The Class of 2028 Haul Is a Statement

Texas A&M didn't make the biggest splash in the 2026 postseason, but the Aggies swung for the fences in the 2028 recruiting class — landing multiple Top 30 prospects during the early weeks of the September contact window. Programs that can consistently attract elite talent before they've built a WCWS résumé are doing something right.

A&M's recruiting momentum, combined with fall camp results, makes the Aggies a name worth monitoring throughout the fall. The SEC has room for another elite program, and College Station is making moves to claim that space.

What's Next

Fall exhibition schedules are largely public and free at most programs. The fastest way to gauge where these four teams actually are — not where analysts think they are — is to watch them play in October. Nebraska, Oklahoma State, Duke, and Texas A&M all have something to prove this fall. The 2027 race is quietly already underway.
