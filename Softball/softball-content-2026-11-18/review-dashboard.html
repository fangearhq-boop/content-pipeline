Tenth Circuit Rules for NCAA in Wisne — Fifth Year Closed for Class of 2022
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post The legal fight over college softball's fifth-year eligibility just reached a major turning point. The Tenth Circuit Court of Appeals reversed and vacated the nationwide preliminary injunction in *Wisne v. NCAA*, handing the NCAA a significant victory in the 5-for-5 eligibility litigation. The court found that the plaintiffs — class-of-2022 athletes who sought a fifth year of competition under COVID-era eligibility rules — failed to demonstrate a likelihood of success on their antitrust argument. Specifically, the panel rejected the plaintiffs' definition of the relevant labor market. For softball players who had been counting on a fifth season, this ruling closes what many had considered the likeliest path to eligibility. But the story isn't over. Approximately 200 concurrent lawsuits filed in state courts still involve up to 850 athletes across all sports, and a new 46-athlete group has already filed a fresh suit seeking an immediate preliminary injunction. Plaintiffs in the Wisne case can also petition for en banc rehearing from the full Tenth Circuit. What does this mean for softball programs? The December transfer portal window opens in just 13 days (December 1–15). Programs that had budgeted roster spots for returning class-of-2022 players now need to reassess — and fast. The portal has become the primary relief valve. We'll continue tracking this. The legal calendar is moving quickly. ### Image Post Caption The Tenth Circuit ruled for the NCAA in the 5-for-5 case. Class-of-2022 softball players face a harder road to a fifth year. 200+ state cases remain. December portal opens December 1. #CollegeSoftball
