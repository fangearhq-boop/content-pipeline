Oklahoma State Cowgirls Sign Seven on Signing Day — Five Four-Stars Join Stillwater

By Emily Rawlings | I Love Softball | November 18, 2026

Oklahoma State softball had a productive National Signing Day. The Cowgirls signed seven players on November 11, 2026, with five of the seven carrying four-star ratings from recruiting services. It is one of the program's most talent-dense signing day classes in recent memory, and it arrives at a moment when the Big 12 landscape is shifting significantly.

Among the notable commitments: pitcher Ava Salcedo, a prospect from Washington High School who had previously been committed to Grand Canyon University before choosing Oklahoma State. Flips like Salcedo's signal that OSU is competing at a higher level for recruits and winning those battles.

Five Four-Stars Lead the Class

Quantity matters, but the quality of this class is the real headline. Five four-star prospects in a single signing class represents a significant step for a program that has been building steadily under head coach Kenny Gajewski. Four-star recruits in softball are prospects who projects have drawn interest from multiple Power programs and demonstrated above-average potential to contribute at the college level.

Oklahoma State finished 2026 with a competitive Big 12 record, and the Cowgirls made the NCAA tournament. The question heading into 2027 is whether the program can push past that ceiling — take a regional, make a super regional, return to Stillwater for the postseason with something to show for the talent being assembled.

Big 12 Opportunity

Oklahoma and Texas have departed for the SEC, leaving the Big 12 without its two most historically dominant softball programs. That creates real opportunity. The teams that built their programs in the shadow of the Sooners and Longhorns now have a path to conference titles that did not exist three years ago.

Oklahoma State, Texas Tech, Baylor, and Kansas State are among the programs most positioned to benefit. Texas Tech has made perhaps the loudest statement this offseason with the No. 1 transfer class nationally and the addition of Kasidi Pickering. But OSU's Signing Day class signals they intend to compete through recruiting, not just the portal.

Building through high school signees provides more structural stability — four-year contributors who develop within your program and understand its culture. For programs trying to establish a new Big 12 identity, that foundation matters.

Salcedo's Flip

Ava Salcedo's decision to leave her Grand Canyon commitment and sign with Oklahoma State is a small but telling data point. Players who flip commitments late in the cycle have usually weighed their options carefully and seen something at the new program that changes the calculation.

Pitching is always at a premium in college softball. A pitcher who chooses Stillwater over a program like Grand Canyon — which has shown growth in its own right — is making a statement about where she sees her development heading. Salcedo's presence strengthens what could be a deep pitching staff heading into 2027.

What's Next

The December transfer portal window (December 1–15) will provide OSU's next opportunity to add experienced players to complement the incoming signing class. For Cowgirl fans watching the program's trajectory, the story to follow this winter is whether Gajewski uses the portal to add a veteran piece — a pitcher, a middle-of-the-order hitter — to anchor the class of signees.

Spring practice typically begins in mid-January. By the time the Cowgirls play their first game of 2027, the new additions will have had a few weeks to settle in. The Big 12 opens March 12, and Oklahoma State's upgraded roster will be tested quickly.

For a program that has spent years building toward a conference championship window, the class of 2027 is evidence that window is approaching.
