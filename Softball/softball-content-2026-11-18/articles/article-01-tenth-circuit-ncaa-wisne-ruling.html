Tenth Circuit Rules for NCAA in Wisne — Fifth Year Door Closes for Class of 2022

By Sarah Mitchell | I Love Softball | November 18, 2026

The Tenth Circuit Court of Appeals handed the NCAA a major victory in the ongoing 5-for-5 eligibility litigation, reversing and vacating the nationwide preliminary injunction in Wisne v. NCAA. For class-of-2022 softball players who had anticipated a fifth season, the ruling represents the most significant legal setback since the case began.

The three-judge panel ruled that the plaintiffs — athletes seeking an extra year of competition under COVID-era eligibility provisions — failed to demonstrate a likelihood of success on the merits of their antitrust claims. The court specifically rejected the plaintiffs' definition of the relevant labor market, the cornerstone of their legal theory.

What the Court Decided

At issue was whether the NCAA's limitation on athlete eligibility to four competitive seasons constituted an unlawful restraint of trade. The plaintiffs argued that the market for college athletic labor was distinct and that restricting fifth-year eligibility harmed competition within that market.

The Tenth Circuit disagreed. The panel found the market definition insufficient to support the antitrust theory, which is typically fatal to such a claim. Without a properly defined relevant market, the antitrust case cannot proceed.

NCAA chief legal officer Scott Bearby acknowledged the court's decision, while noting that the wave of eligibility injunctions had "created chaos for the 2026-27 season" across college sports.

The Fight Is Not Over

The Tenth Circuit ruling does not end the broader eligibility litigation. Approximately 200 concurrent lawsuits filed in state courts still involve up to 850 athletes across college sports, including softball. These cases operate under different legal frameworks and are not automatically resolved by the federal ruling.

A new group of 46 athletes has already filed a fresh federal lawsuit seeking an immediate preliminary injunction that would grant them eligibility to compete this season. The plaintiffs in Wisne can also petition for en banc rehearing — a review by the full Tenth Circuit — though that process is uncertain and not guaranteed to succeed.

Legal analysts at Sportico noted the Tenth Circuit decision was "a decisive, published opinion" that will carry significant persuasive weight in other federal courts, even if it does not directly bind state proceedings.

Softball-Specific Implications

For programs managing rosters heading into 2027, the ruling arrives at a critical moment. The December transfer portal window — the primary off-season mechanism for roster movement — opens in just 13 days on December 1.

Programs that had planned to carry class-of-2022 athletes under the assumption of a fifth-year injunction must now reassess. Some of those players may enter the portal themselves, seeking programs that want their experience regardless of the legal outcome. Others may pursue individual state-court injunctions, a slower and less certain path.

Rachel Lawson, former Tennessee head coach and a vocal observer of the eligibility landscape, had previously described the litigation uncertainty as producing "casualties" — programs and players making decisions based on a legal outcome that remained unresolved. That uncertainty has now shifted in the NCAA's favor, though it has not fully resolved.

The 25-player roster cap established under the House settlement continues to apply, compounding the planning challenge. Programs operating near capacity cannot simply add affected athletes even if a future court ruling reopens the door.

What's Next

The immediate timeline for 5-for-5 developments includes:

  • Potential en banc rehearing petition in the Tenth Circuit (timeline: weeks to months)
  • Ongoing state-court cases involving 850+ athletes — no unified ruling date
  • The new 46-athlete federal suit and its preliminary injunction request (hearing date TBD)
  • December 1–15 transfer portal window — the most direct near-term roster relief valve

For softball fans and program followers, the December portal will tell a significant story about how teams absorbed this ruling. Watch for veteran players entering the portal as their fifth-year pathway narrows. The offseason is moving fast.
