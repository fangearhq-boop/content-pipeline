Florida State Softball Signs Highest-Rated Recruit in Program History — Five-Star Pitcher Addison DeRoche

By Jordan Reeves | I Love Softball | November 18, 2026

Florida State softball has set a new bar for itself. On National Signing Day, the Seminoles signed pitcher Addison DeRoche, the No. 5 player nationally in the 2027 recruiting class — the highest-rated prospect in FSU softball program history. It is a recruitment milestone that reflects how much the program's national profile has grown.

DeRoche's signing was accompanied by four-star catcher Layna Ayala, giving FSU a talent boost at two critical positions on a single day. The Seminoles' overall 2027 class ranks No. 13 nationally, their strongest class ranking to date.

Breaking the Program Ceiling

Signing a five-star pitcher ranked No. 5 nationally is not something Florida State has done before. That fact alone says something about the trajectory head coach Lonni Alameda has established. Programs that have consistently reached super regionals and College World Series appearances attract the top players — and FSU's sustained postseason presence has made Tallahassee a destination for elite pitching talent.

DeRoche's addition addresses a need that matters most in college softball: top-of-rotation pitching depth. Teams that win the Women's College World Series almost always have multiple legitimate starters who can compete on consecutive days. Florida State has been close to that threshold in recent seasons, and DeRoche's development could be what pushes the Seminoles over it.

The Full Class Picture

DeRoche and Ayala are the headliners, but the No. 13 national class ranking reflects depth beyond just those two signings. Florida State's 2027 class is built to contribute at multiple positions.

Earlier this fall, the program also added Tennessee infielder Ella Dodge through the transfer portal. Dodge brings ACC experience and starts immediately, filling a gap in the infield. The combination of Signing Day additions and portal strategy gives the Seminoles a more complete picture of their 2027 roster than programs relying solely on one pathway.

For comparison, FSU's 2026 class ranked No. 13 nationally with five signees. Matching that ranking with the addition of a historic No. 1 program recruit suggests the Seminoles are building a foundation that future recruiting classes can build on.

The Case for FSU in 2027

Florida State was 2026 NCAA tournament participant with legitimate postseason capability. The 2027 roster, anchored by DeRoche and Ayala in the incoming class alongside returning veterans, is built to push deeper in the bracket.

The ACC continues to produce multiple teams capable of deep postseason runs — Florida State, Virginia Tech, North Carolina, and Notre Dame have all been relevant in recent seasons. DeRoche's signing elevates FSU within that competitive landscape.

Catcher is one of the most demanding and highest-impact positions in softball. A four-star catcher like Ayala who can handle a staff that includes a prospect of DeRoche's caliber provides the kind of synergy that coaches specifically recruit for — a battery that grows together through a career.

What's Next

Florida State will head into the December portal window with a clear identity: a program capable of signing the best high school pitchers in the country. If Alameda and her staff use the portal to add a veteran hitter or outfield presence — the complement to an elite pitching staff — the Seminoles have all the pieces for a deep 2027 postseason run.

Watch DeRoche's spring development closely. Five-star pitchers who arrive at FSU with that level of recruiting cache tend to be on fast tracks — and her progress in the spring of 2027 will tell a story about whether the Noles have a legitimate WCWS ace or someone still a year away from that level.

Either way, National Signing Day 2026 was Florida State's best day in program recruiting history. That matters.
