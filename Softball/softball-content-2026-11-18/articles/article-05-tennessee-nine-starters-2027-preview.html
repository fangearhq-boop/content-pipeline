Tennessee Returns Nine Starters for 2027 — Lady Vols' Fall Ball Points to SEC Title Contention

By Emily Rawlings | I Love Softball | November 18, 2026

In an era when the transfer portal empties rosters every spring and programs rebuild chemistry from scratch, Tennessee is doing something rare heading into 2027: returning nine starters. That kind of continuity — experienced players who understand a system, trust each other, and have competed together — is one of the most undervalued advantages in college softball.

Tennessee finished 2026 at 49-12. The Lady Vols are not a program rebuilding. They are a program reloading with almost everyone they need already in place.

Mardjetko and the Pitching Staff

The name that every opponent will circle on their scouting reports is Sage Mardjetko. The NFCA First Team All-American went 16-3 with a 1.26 ERA and 171 strikeouts in 2026. Those are numbers that belong in conversations about the best pitchers in the country — and she is coming back.

Mardjetko's 1.26 ERA is elite. For context, ERA benchmarks in college softball generally put anything below 2.00 as outstanding and below 1.50 as elite. At 1.26, Mardjetko was one of the most dominant pitchers in the sport in 2026. Having that anchor return for 2027 is not a minor advantage — it is the difference between a team that might make a super regional and a team that expects to.

Pitcher Erin Nuwer also returns, giving the Lady Vols genuine two-starter depth in the circle. Rotational depth matters enormously in a 56-game regular season and postseason, where starters can face the same lineup twice in a weekend series.

Sophia Knight and the Lineup

Sophia Knight's .394 batting average led the team in 2026, and she returns as the focal point of an offense that now brings back nine starters total. That means the core of Tennessee's lineup — not just Knight — returns with another year of chemistry and development.

Multi-year starting experience in college softball is increasingly rare. When teams lose three or four starters to the portal each spring, they spend the first months of the following season rebuilding. Tennessee's nine returning starters skip that phase entirely. By March, when the SEC schedule begins, the Lady Vols will be a known quantity — experienced, cohesive, and difficult to surprise.

The SEC Landscape

Tennessee will compete in a conference that includes Alabama, LSU (now with the No. 1 recruiting class), Oklahoma (which joined the SEC), and Florida. That is a genuinely difficult environment. But returning nine starters is a significant equalizer.

Alabama finished 2026 at 56-9 and ranked No. 3 nationally. They are the standard in the SEC. Tennessee at 49-12 and No. 4 is not far behind — and with nine returning starters, the gap between the two programs in 2027 may be smaller than the final standings suggested.

The Lady Vols' fall ball schedule — eight games, including a road game at Middle Tennessee — concluded with the roster largely intact and signs of continuity that the coaching staff will build on through the winter.

What's Next

Tennessee will enter January preseason polls as a Top 10 team. Expect the Lady Vols to be ranked in the top five in several services, with Mardjetko drawing national attention as a preseason Player of the Year candidate.

The spring schedule will reveal whether the nine returning starters take another step together or plateau. Programs at this level do not stand still — they either advance or fall behind against the recruiting and development programs that compete at the top of the SEC. For the Lady Vols, the foundation is there. What they build on it in 2027 starts now.

Sage Mardjetko. Sophia Knight. Nine starters. Tennessee is ready to compete for a national title.
