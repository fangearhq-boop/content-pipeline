LSU Goes All-In: Signs Portal Twins Pickens and Outfielder Winchell to Complete No. 1 Class

By Sarah Mitchell | I Love Softball | November 18, 2026

Beth Torina was not finished after securing the No. 1-ranked signing class in LSU softball history. In the days following National Signing Day, the Tigers' head coach added portal twins Mackenzie and Madison Pickens to the roster, then signed Notre Dame outfielder Michey Winchell — a transfer who will be a senior with the Tigers in 2027.

The additions complete what is shaping up as the most ambitious roster construction in LSU softball history, combining the top-ranked high school class with targeted portal acquisitions at key positions of need.

The Pickens Twins: A Rare Portal Acquisition

Signing twin pitchers in a single portal move is unusual in college softball. Mackenzie and Madison Pickens join a LSU pitching group that will be built around the No. 1 recruiting class's pitchers — Maci Bryant and Sydney Gonglick — giving Torina rotation options she did not have heading into the offseason.

Pitching depth separates programs that survive a super regional from programs that fall short. Having four credible starters heading into a May-June run is not a luxury — it is a postseason requirement. The Pickens twins' addition directly addresses that need, even before spring workouts reveal individual development arcs.

Winchell Brings Immediate Experience

Michey Winchell arrives from Notre Dame as a senior — a player with four years of ACC experience who can step into an outfield role and produce from day one. In the portal era, this type of addition has become essential: balancing elite youth with proven veteran contributors who understand what it takes to compete at the highest level of college softball.

Winchell brings outfield depth to a lineup that returns five-star infielder Bryli Seifert and four-star catcher Jewel Cooper from the signing class, alongside returning veterans. Her ACC pedigree means LSU will not be breaking in an inexperienced outfielder during a season where the expectations are legitimate WCWS contention.

The Export Side

No roster build happens in isolation. LSU has also seen transfers leave. Reports indicate the program has been a net exporter of portal players this cycle, with more outgoing than incoming at some points in the fall. That is not unusual for a program with the No. 1 incoming class — roster management under the 25-player House settlement cap requires difficult decisions about who stays and who seeks opportunities elsewhere.

What matters is the net result. Torina's approach — sign the elite class, add targeted veterans, manage departures selectively — reflects a philosophy that prioritizes balance over volume. Rather than filling every available portal spot, LSU targeted specific positional needs and filled them with high-impact players.

What 2027 Could Look Like

The emerging 2027 LSU roster combines:

  • The nation's No. 1-ranked signing class (six signees including five-star Bryli Seifert, four-star Jewel Cooper, pitchers Bryant and Gonglick)
  • Portal additions at pitching depth (Twins Pickens) and outfield (Winchell)
  • Returning veterans from a 2026 squad that competed in the SEC

Building a WCWS-caliber roster requires all three layers. Programs that win on pure recruiting talent without veteran experience often hit walls in postseason pressure situations. Programs that rely exclusively on experienced portal additions lack the ceiling for multi-year excellence. LSU's 2027 construction targets both.

What's Next

The December transfer portal (December 1–15) closes LSU's primary off-season roster construction window. By mid-December, the Tigers should have a near-final picture of their 2027 roster. Spring practice begins in mid-January, and the SEC schedule opens in March.

Beth Torina has built something in Baton Rouge that did not exist before: a first-ever No. 1 ranking in program history combined with the kind of portal acquisitions that veteran programs rely on. Whether the 2027 Tigers can deliver on that potential is the question that matters — and they will have every resource needed to find the answer.
