Inside OU's Fall Camp: How the Transfer Class Is Integrating With the Sooners

By Sarah Mitchell | I Love Softball | August 25, 2026

Oklahoma softball's fall camp is underway, and it looks different from any fall Patty Gasso has run in recent memory. The Sooners missed the Women's College World Series in 2026 for the first time since 2015. The roster that shows up for the 2026-27 season has been rebuilt, and the integration work happening in Norman right now will determine whether the pieces form a team that gets back to Oklahoma City in June.

The most prominent new pieces are Sydney Berzon and Macie Harter — two of the most accomplished transfer additions in college softball this cycle. Neither arrived at OU to fill a backup role. Berzon is expected to anchor the pitching staff. Harter provides a bat that hit .346 with 7 home runs and 23 RBIs at Middle Tennessee State. The question is not whether they can play. It's how quickly they become Sooners — which is something different from simply being on the roster.

What Berzon Brings

Sydney Berzon was a two-time All-American at LSU. Over three college seasons, she went 52-25 with a 2.02 ERA and 406 career strikeouts. She was named to the Softball America Top 50 Watch List. She arrives at OU as the answer to a pitching staff that lost its top arm after last season and struggled to find consistency in 2026.

What she's learning in fall camp is the OU system — Gasso's expectations, the defensive alignment behind her, how communication works on the field with a new catcher and a new infield. These are not things Berzon has to be taught from scratch, but they are things that require reps and repetition before they become second nature. The fall Battle Series — public intrasquad events beginning October 3 — will be the first opportunity for Sooner fans to see how that integration looks.

Building Around the Absences

Fall camp at OU is also happening without four of its most recognizable names. Alyssa Brito, Jayda Coleman, Tiare Jennings, and Kelly Maxwell are all competing for USA Softball at the WBSC Women's Softball World Cup Group Stage in Oklahoma City, which runs through September 16. They won't be back in Norman until the fall schedule is already several weeks old.

That changes what fall camp looks like. The players who are on the field in August are building habits and building culture without the program's WNT contingent. In some respects, that's an opportunity: younger players and new additions get earlier reps, the coaching staff gets a clearer read on depth, and everyone involved learns the system before the most prominent returners arrive.

When Brito, Coleman, Jennings, and Maxwell return after September 16, they'll be joining a team that has already been practicing together for weeks. The integration challenge runs in both directions.

The Freshman Class and 2027 Commits

Gasso has described Oklahoma's incoming class as "special." Already on campus are No. 4 overall 2027 commit Ori Mailo and No. 2 overall commit Payton Westra. These are not development players — they're expected contributors from early in their careers, recruited during a cycle when OU needed to show it could still attract elite talent despite the 2026 WCWS miss.

How the 2027 class integrates alongside a transfer class and a WNT contingent returning mid-fall will be one of the defining stories of Oklahoma's program heading into the season. The Battle Series gives the coaching staff six formal evaluation points before November. That's the visible product of what's being built right now.

What's Next

Oklahoma's fall exhibition schedule runs October 4 through November 9. The public Battle Series intrasquad events begin October 3 at Love's Field. Alyssa Brito, Jayda Coleman, Tiare Jennings, and Kelly Maxwell return from USA WNT duty after September 16. The full roster integration — transfers, freshmen, and returning Sooners — will be visible for the first time at the Battle Series events this fall.
