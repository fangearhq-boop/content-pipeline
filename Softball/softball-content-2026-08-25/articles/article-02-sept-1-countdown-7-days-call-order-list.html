7 Days to September 1: How D1 Coaches Build the Call-Order List Right Now

By Emily Rawlings | I Love Softball | August 25, 2026

Seven days from today, September 1 arrives — the date when D1 softball coaches can call, text, and email Class of 2028 recruits for the first time. For many prospects, it's the moment they've been anticipating for months. But here's what most recruits don't see: the decision about who gets a first-hour call on September 1 is being made right now, in the week before the window opens.

D1 coaches work from call-order lists — a prioritized ranking of the recruits they're most actively pursuing. The eight to twelve names at the top of that list receive calls in the first hour. Everything else follows. The first hour of September 1 is the clearest signal a program sends about where a recruit stands.

What Goes Into the List

The factors that determine call order are not mysterious. Programs weigh a recruit's fit for the roster they're building — position, graduation year, skill level, and academic standing. But those factors have been largely settled for weeks. What moves a recruit up or down in the final days before September 1 is recent communication.

Coaches talk to each other, compare notes with assistants, and take stock of which recruits have shown consistent, ongoing interest. A recruit who emailed a month ago and then went quiet sends one signal. A recruit who checked in recently — even briefly — sends another. In a week where coaches are making final prioritization decisions, visible interest matters.

This doesn't mean a recruiter should overload programs with messages. A single, clear note is enough: what you're excited about in the program, that September 1 is a date you're looking forward to, and that this school is a genuine priority. Brief, direct, genuine. That's what moves a recruit into the first hour.

The September 9 Context

September 1 is not the only date that matters. September 9 at 7:00 AM ET is when the in-person contact window opens — coaches can show up at games, practices, and camps for the first time. For recruits who are still evaluating programs or waiting to see which coaches show up to watch them play, September 9 is often the more consequential date.

Recruits should think about both windows. The phone call on September 1 tells you which programs are interested. The coach who shows up on September 9 tells you which programs are serious. They're not always the same programs.

The nine days between September 1 and September 9 are not a break. They're when relationships get built — when recruits take unofficial visits, when coaches schedule time in the field, when commitment conversations begin in earnest. Recruits who are prepared for both windows put themselves in a much stronger position than those who prepare only for the phone call.

What to Do This Week

Before September 1 arrives, Class of 2028 recruits should have current film accessible to every program on their priority list. Not the best highlight package from two years ago — current film, from the most recent games of the summer. Coaches making final call-order decisions are thinking about the player they'll recruit to campus in 2028, not the player from last season.

Along with updated film, recruits benefit from having a clear, short list of which programs they want to hear from in the first hour. Being prepared — knowing what you'll say when a coach calls, knowing what questions you want to ask — turns what can be an overwhelming September 1 into a productive one.

Seven days. The window is almost open.

What's Next

September 1 arrives one week from today. D1 coaches across the country will begin direct contact with Class of 2028 recruits for the first time. September 9 at 7:00 AM ET opens the in-person contact window. Recruits who prepare for both dates — and who show programs they're ready — will be in the best position when the calls start coming in.
