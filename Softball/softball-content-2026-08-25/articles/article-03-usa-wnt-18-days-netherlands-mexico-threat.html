18 Days Out: Why the Netherlands and Mexico Are the Real Test in Group Oklahoma

By Jordan Reeves | I Love Softball | August 25, 2026

Eighteen days separate the 2026 WBSC Women's Softball World Cup Group Stage in Oklahoma City from the calendar page we're on today. USA Softball opens September 12 at Devon Park — a venue in a city the program knows well, against a field that includes two nations capable of producing the kind of result that reshapes a group stage in a single afternoon.

The format is unforgiving: six nations, round-robin play, and only the top two advance to the 2027 WBSC Women's Softball World Cup Finals in Redcliffe, Queensland, Australia — the direct qualifier for the LA28 Olympics. USA is ranked No. 2 in the world. The expectation is clear. So are the risks.

The Netherlands — A Genuine Threat

The Netherlands enters Group Oklahoma ranked No. 6 in the world. That ranking reflects a program that has been consistently competitive at the international level for more than a decade, producing elite players who compete in the AUSL and other professional leagues. Dutch softball is not simply physical athleticism — it is technically refined, consistently coached, and deeply competitive in tight games.

For USA, the Netherlands represents the kind of opponent where a lack of early sharpness can lead to a long, close game. The Netherlands does not give away runs. Their pitching is composed, their lineup disciplined, and their coaching staff experienced in international tournament play. A Group Oklahoma game against the Netherlands is not an exhibition — it is a test against a team that is there to win.

Mexico — USA's Opening Day Opponent

USA opens against Mexico (No. 7 in the world) on September 12 in the final game of the day. Mexico's ranking undersells their competitive danger. They are physically athletic, aggressive on the bases, and pitch-first in a way that can keep games close deep into the late innings. Mexico has pushed USA in recent regional play, and in a tournament setting where momentum matters, a close opener against Mexico can set an uncomfortable tone for the rest of the group stage.

This is why the opener matters. A clean, commanding win against Mexico on September 12 sends the right message to the rest of the field. A narrow escape — or worse, a loss — changes the group stage calculus immediately.

The USA Roster

The 16-player USA WNT roster for Group Oklahoma is one of the strongest assembled in recent cycles. It includes Olympic veterans Rachel Garcia and Dejah Mulipola, seven members of the 2025 World Games gold medal squad, and ten players from the 2025 USA Down Under Series. The roster draws from powerhouse programs including UCLA, Oklahoma, Tennessee, Texas, and Alabama — players who know big games in front of hostile crowds.

OU leads all programs with four players on the roster: Alyssa Brito, Jayda Coleman, Tiare Jennings, and Kelly Maxwell. Devon Park in Oklahoma City is a venue they know better than almost any other players in the group. That familiarity is not trivial — international softball in a venue tied to your college career carries a different energy than competing on neutral ground.

What's Next

Group Oklahoma runs September 12-16 at Devon Park in Oklahoma City. USA opens against Mexico (No. 7) on September 12. The top two of six advance to the 2027 WBSC Women's Softball World Cup Finals in Redcliffe, Queensland, Australia — the direct qualifier for the LA28 Olympics. The Netherlands and Mexico are the teams worth watching. Eighteen days until the games begin.
