Kavan's 500th Strikeout and What Comes Next: A Three-Peat Bid in Her Senior Season

By Emily Rawlings | I Love Softball | August 25, 2026

In April 2026, Teagan Kavan recorded her 500th career strikeout. The milestone came against Oklahoma — the program that has represented the other side of the biggest moments of her college career — and it arrived in a season that ended with Kavan doing what no pitcher in Women's College World Series history had done before: winning Most Outstanding Player for the second consecutive year.

Fall camp at Texas is kicking off. Kavan is back. And the Longhorns are chasing something only two programs have ever accomplished.

What the 2026 Season Looked Like

Kavan's 2026 numbers deserve to be understood in full. She finished with a 2.33 ERA, 260 strikeouts — a career high — and 228.2 innings pitched, also a career high. In the WCWS itself, she went 4-1 with two saves across 33.1 innings. Texas finished 49-12 and claimed its second consecutive national championship.

For a pitcher who came to Austin with high expectations, the reality has exceeded them. The 500th career strikeout is a benchmark that reflects consistency across three seasons at a high level, not a single dominant stretch. Kavan has been this good for a long time. The milestones are arriving because she earned them one game at a time.

The Roster Around Her

Kavan is returning to a team that didn't get worse in the offseason — it got better. D1Softball National Player of the Year Isa Torres transferred from Florida State, where she hit .530 in 2026, to join the Longhorns. Torres grew up in Georgetown, Texas, which adds a homecoming narrative to what is already a compelling player profile. Katie Stewart, who set the Texas program record with 31 home runs in 2026, returns. Catcher Samantha Swan joined from Arizona State. Lefty pitcher Samantha Lincoln arrived from Texas Tech.

Coach Mike White has spoken publicly about wanting to reduce reliance on Kavan by deepening the pitching rotation. The additions suggest he's serious about it. If Lincoln can give Texas quality innings alongside Kavan, the Longhorns' ability to navigate a long postseason run improves meaningfully.

The Historical Weight

Three consecutive national championships is not something that happens in college softball. Only two programs have done it: UCLA from 1988 to 1990, and Oklahoma from 2021 to 2023. For Texas — a program that won its first title in 2025 and its second in 2026 — the bid for three in a row places it in historically significant territory from the very first pitch of the season.

Every Longhorn on the current roster is aware of what they're chasing. Fall camp, which opens with a home exhibition against Texas State on September 5, is the first step in a process that ends in June. Kavan has won at the end of that process twice. She knows what it takes.

What's Next

Texas's fall home opener against Texas State is September 5 at 2:30 p.m. CT. The Longhorns will continue building through the fall exhibition schedule as they prepare for a 2026-27 season that opens with the highest of expectations. Kavan leads a pitching staff that now has real depth. The three-peat bid is officially underway.
