Oklahoma Has the No. 1 Class — But One Question Still Needs Answering Before Signing Day

By Sarah Mitchell | November 26, 2026 | I Love Softball

Oklahoma's Class of 2027 is, by most objective measures, the best assembled high school recruiting class in college softball this cycle.

No. 1 overall prospect Julianna "Goose" Hutchens is already enrolled in Norman, beginning her Sooner career ahead of schedule. Outfielder Finlee Williams — one of the top position players in the class — is committed. So is catcher Riley Hilliard, ranked No. 2 at her position nationally, and shortstop Emma Reynolds, a 4-star talent. D1Softball has ranked the class No. 1 in the country.

By every traditional marker, Oklahoma is winning this recruiting cycle. And yet, heading into National Signing Day on December 2, the Sooners still have an answer they need.

The One Missing Piece

What Oklahoma does not have, entering Signing Day, is an elite pitcher to anchor the 2027 rotation.

The class is loaded with position talent. But the Sooners have not yet publicly added a frontline arm capable of carrying them through an SEC schedule that now includes the sport's most competitive programs. Oklahoma transitioned to the SEC prior to the 2026 season, and the demands on a rotation in that conference are unforgiving.

Before the Thanksgiving blackout started November 25, the Sooners hosted pitcher prospects Vivian Knott and Aspen Boulware. Both are top-tier arms being recruited by multiple programs. Whether either one commits in the December 2-4 signing window — or whether they opt to wait and use the portal after December 1 — is the defining question of OU's offseason.

Signing Day or the Portal?

The timing this year creates a genuine choice for elite pitching prospects: sign with a school through the HS process on December 2-4, or enter the transfer portal on December 1 and choose from multiple scholarship offers. It is a leverage question that particularly sophisticated recruits — and their advisors — are navigating carefully.

For Oklahoma, that means two separate lanes are open. The Sooners can try to close a high school pitcher on Signing Day, and they can simultaneously work the portal for a veteran arm. The dead period (December 8-12) is a complicating factor for both lanes.

Either way, the answer should come this week.

What a Finished Class Would Mean

An Oklahoma class that adds a frontline pitcher — through any avenue — would be the best single-program recruiting haul in the sport this cycle, by a wide margin. The position talent is already in place. A rotation ace on top of it would give the Sooners a roster built to contend in their second full SEC season and make a serious run at the Women's College World Series.

Without one, the class is excellent but incomplete. And incomplete classes, in the modern transfer portal era, have a way of being addressed quickly — just not always on the program's preferred timeline.

What's Next

The Thanksgiving blackout clears November 29. Contact resumes November 30. The December portal opens December 1, and National Signing Day begins December 2. Oklahoma's staff is expected to pursue pitching through both channels simultaneously. The dead period runs December 8-12. National preseason polls drop in January, at which point the Sooners' class will be fully evaluated — pitching ace included or not.
