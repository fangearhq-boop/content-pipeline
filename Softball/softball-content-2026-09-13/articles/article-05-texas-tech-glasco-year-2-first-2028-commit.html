Glasco Year 2: Texas Tech Lands First 2028 Commit as Fall Camp Takes Shape

By Jordan Reeves September 13, 2026

Runner-Up, Then What?

Texas Tech finished as the 2026 WCWS runner-up after falling to Texas in the championship series. The Red Raiders went into June as one of the hottest teams in the bracket and ran up against a Longhorns program that had been the gold standard of the sport for two years running. Being swept doesn't erase the run it took to get there.

Gerry Glasco enters Year 2 with a clearer picture of what he has and what he still needs to build. His roster has three of the most decorated transfer additions of the off-season: Jaysoni Beachum (.406 BA, 10 HR, 69 RBI from Florida State), Haley Pickering (No. 2 transfer portal player at .384 BA with 50 career home runs from Oklahoma), and Jayden Heavener (LHP, 13-9, 2.81 ERA, 128 strikeouts from LSU). Add in six other All-Americans and the depth of this roster is real on paper.

Fall Camp and the Chemistry Question

Seven All-Americans practicing together for the first time is not the same as seven All-Americans playing well together. That is the question Glasco and his staff are working through right now in fall camp. Beachum, Pickering, and Heavener all come from winning programs with established cultures. Merging three different sets of habits, routines, and expectations into one cohesive team identity takes time.

The fall schedule is the laboratory. Glasco has said publicly that building chemistry and finding lineup roles are the priorities this fall. The talent ceiling is obvious to anyone who follows the sport. What is less obvious — and what fall scrimmages will start to reveal — is how quickly this group develops the trust that championship teams need in tight games.

The 2028 Class Gets Its First Commit

On Sunday, outfielder Star Gutierrez became the first verbal commitment in Texas Tech's Class of 2028. It is an early signal of how Glasco's recruiting pitch is landing with the next generation of prospects. Programs that finished as WCWS runners-up historically see a significant lift in recruiting interest the following cycle, and the Red Raiders appear to be capturing that momentum.

Gutierrez is one commitment, not a complete class. But getting the first one in before fall camp is even two weeks old says something about the program's current standing. Recruits at that stage are watching programs that feel like they're going somewhere. The runner-up finish in June is the clearest proof point Glasco has that TTU is exactly that kind of program.

What's Next

Texas Tech's fall schedule is underway. The games don't count but the reps matter. By the time fall camp wraps in November, Glasco will have a clearer read on which version of this lineup is going to step onto the field in February. If the chemistry pieces fall into place, this roster has the talent to compete for a national title in 2027 — and the Class of 2028 will be watching every step of the way.
