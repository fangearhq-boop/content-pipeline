NFCA Honors, Back-to-Back Titles, and 16 Days to Fall Ball: Texas Is Ready

By Emily Rawlings September 13, 2026

The Hardware Keeps Coming

The NFCA named Mike White's Texas Longhorns coaching staff the ATEC/NFCA National Coaching Staff of the Year for the second consecutive year in June, recognizing a 53-12 season that ended with the Longhorns sweeping Texas Tech in the WCWS Championship. Two programs in the country have ever gone back-to-back with that award. White's staff is one of them, twice in a row now.

The case for the honor isn't hard to make. Texas won 53 games. They swept a Texas Tech team loaded with seven All-Americans in the title series. And they did it while integrating new personnel, managing expectations, and staying ahead of the curve on every front. Fall 2026 starts with that staff intact, plus a few new pieces in the building who haven't yet worn burnt orange in a real game.

Torres and Kavan: The Players at the Center

Isa Torres is the player everyone wants to see. The 2026 D1Softball National Player of the Year hit .530 with 16 home runs and 56 RBI at Florida State before transferring to Austin. She is from Georgetown, Texas, which means she spent years watching the program she's now joining. The fall opener in 16 days will be the first chance for McCombs Field fans to see her in uniform.

Hannah Kavan is the other half of this story. She is the only two-time Women's College World Series Most Outstanding Player in the history of the tournament — a fact that gets easier to say out loud as she prepares for her senior year. Kavan as a senior, with Torres behind her in the lineup, changes what opposing pitching staffs have to plan for. The third title would put Texas in a three-person conversation with UCLA (1988-90) and Oklahoma (2021-23) for the most consecutive WCWS championships ever.

September 29 at McCombs Field

The fall opener is free. Sept. 29 at Red and Charline McCombs Field, 6 PM CT, Texas vs. Murray State. Five of the six fall games are at McCombs. The only road trip is the Nov. 6 closer at Texas A&M.

White has already said the fall slate is about getting live at-bats for Torres, evaluating new arms, and finding out who steps into the roles left by last year's departures. But anyone walking into McCombs on Sept. 29 will not be watching a team trying to figure itself out. They will be watching a program that has won two straight national championships and is already thinking about a third.

What's Next

Texas opens fall ball on Sept. 29. The schedule runs six games through Nov. 6. The roster answer that matters most — how Torres integrates with Kavan and the holdover core — starts getting answered the moment the first pitch is thrown. If the fall looks anything like the last two springs, opponents have plenty to worry about before February.
