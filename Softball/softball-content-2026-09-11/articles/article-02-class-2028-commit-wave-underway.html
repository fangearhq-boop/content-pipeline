Class of 2028 Commit Wave Is Officially On

By Emily Rawlings | September 11, 2026

Recruiting

Two days into the Class of 2028 in-person contact window, and the commit wave that recruiting analysts predicted is arriving right on schedule.

The window opened September 9 at 7:00 AM ET — the first moment D1 coaches could show up at a prospect's school, home, or practice field. Within 48 hours, the verbal commitments started rolling in from programs across the SEC, ACC, and beyond. Tennessee landed Sophia Galindo and Nevaeh Wilson. LSU signed five-star Joley Bennett. Oklahoma hosted Sophia Schlader and Caelyn Campos on campus this week. As of today, three dozen Class of 2028 juniors have committed to Division I programs — and that number is climbing by the day.

A Different Kind of Recruiting Cycle

The 2028 class has a different feel than recent cycles, and On3's recruiting team captured it well: this is a small-rosters, big-talent cycle. Programs have fewer scholarship spots available than in years past, which means every offer carries genuine intent. A coach who shows up in person this week is not just keeping options open — they are telling a prospect: you are someone we want.

That selectivity has raised the stakes for prospects and programs alike. The 1,226 Extra Inning Softball nominations this cycle set a record, meaning more players than ever were being evaluated through the process. But record nominations also mean more competition for a limited number of spots.

Middle infielders and catchers headline the class. Two-way players and a strong pitching pool round out the depth. Programs that can identify and land the right shortstop or catcher in the next few weeks will have a significant advantage heading into the 2028 season.

Who Set the Tone

Peyton Ushery didn't wait for the in-person window. The No. 2-ranked prospect in the HOT 100 committed to Georgia on September 1, the same day the communication window opened. Her commitment established the class's early identity — elite talent making decisions early, forcing other programs to accelerate their timelines.

That move — and others like it — compresses the calendar for coaches who prefer to develop relationships gradually. Programs that were planning a slow build through the fall are now scrambling to confirm their top targets before the best players are gone.

What Prospects Should Know

For Class of 2028 players still in the process, the next few weeks matter more than any other stretch of the recruiting calendar. An in-person visit from a coach is the clearest signal available. A campus visit invitation is the next step.

Official visits can begin in October. Programs that are serious about a prospect are already scheduling those trips. If a coach has reached out and offered since September 1, the in-person follow-up this week or next is the bridge between interest and commitment.

The window stays open through the fall. But the best spots at the best programs fill fast. The wave is moving now.

What's Next

Expect the commit count to grow significantly over the next two to three weeks as in-person evaluations convert to offers and offers convert to verbals. Official visit windows open in October, and programs will use those trips to close on their top targets.
