### STORY 1: USA WNT — ONE DAY to Devon Park

**FB Long-Form (9:00 AM)**
Tomorrow night, the moment softball fans have been waiting for all summer arrives.

The USA Women's National Team opens the WBSC Women's Softball World Cup Group Oklahoma at Devon Park in Oklahoma City — and their first opponent is Mexico (No. 7 in the world) at 7:30 PM ET on September 12.

Head coach Patty Gasso departed Norman today to lead Team USA in what could be the most important six days of the LA28 Olympic cycle. The top two teams from this six-nation round-robin advance to the 2027 Finals in Redcliffe, Queensland — the gateway to Los Angeles 2028.

The roster reads like an All-Star lineup. Rachel Garcia and Dejah Mulipola carry Olympic gold experience from 2020. Seven players on the roster won gold at the 2025 World Games. Alyssa Brito, Jayda Coleman, Tiare Jennings, and Kelly Maxwell represent Oklahoma in force. Head coach Gasso, assistant JT Gasso, and Jennifer Rocha round out the bench.

The path through Group Oklahoma: USA needs to navigate Mexico (No. 7), then the bigger test — Netherlands (No. 6). Win that round-robin bout and USA controls the No. 1 seed. The September 16 play-off bracket gives everyone a second chance, but first place avoids the repechage route entirely.

USA went 5-0 at the International Cup at Devon Park. The venue is familiar. The mission is clear.

Tomorrow we find out if they're ready.

### STORY 2: Class of 2028 — Commit Wave Underway

**FB Long-Form (11:00 AM)**
Two days into the Class of 2028 in-person contact window, and the commit wave is officially picking up speed.

The September 9 window opening — the moment when D1 coaches could walk up to a prospect's school, home field, or front door for the very first time — triggered exactly what recruiting analysts predicted: a surge of verbal commitments from the country's top 2028 talent.

Tennessee was among the first to strike, landing both Sophia Galindo and Nevaeh Wilson. LSU added five-star Joley Bennett to its growing 2028 class. Oklahoma hosted Sophia Schlader and Caelyn Campos on campus this week. More announcements are expected in the days ahead.

The cycle has a different feel from previous years. On3's analysis points to small roster spots and big talent — programs are being selective. With 1,226 Extra Inning Softball nominations in the record books, most prospects went through the entire contact season before coaches ever showed up in person. The ones getting offers now know they are genuinely wanted.

Peyton Ushery, who committed to Georgia on September 1 as the No. 2-ranked prospect in the HOT 100, set the tone for the class. Three dozen juniors are now committed, and that number will grow significantly in the weeks ahead as official visits begin filling up fall calendars.

If you're a Class of 2028 prospect — this is your week. Coaches aren't calling just to check in. They're showing up.

### STORY 3: Oklahoma — Gasso Departs for WNT

**FB Long-Form (1:00 PM)**
For weeks, Patty Gasso has been running two operations at once — building Oklahoma's 2027 roster in Norman and preparing to lead the United States Women's National Team in Oklahoma City.

Today, one of those operations takes over completely.

Gasso left Norman to join Team USA at Devon Park for Group Oklahoma (September 12–16). She brings with her JT Gasso and Jennifer Rocha as assistants. Four of her own players — Alyssa Brito, Jayda Coleman, Tiare Jennings, and Kelly Maxwell — will take the field as Sooners wearing USA jerseys.

For OU, the associate coaching staff takes the wheel for the duration of the tournament. And the team they've built is ready.

Kendall Wells has already shown what she's capable of — two home runs off Sydney Berzon in fall scrimmages. Berzon, the LSU transfer who posted a 486.1-inning, 2.02 ERA, 406-strikeout career at LSU across two All-American seasons, is integrating smoothly. Macie Harter, who hit .346 at Middle Tennessee, rounds out the new additions.

The fall schedule runs Sept. 30 through Nov. 4, all at Love's Field. First opponent: Tarleton State. Nineteen days away.

Gasso will be back in Norman by September 17. Until then, the foundation she and her staff built should hold just fine.

### STORY 4: Texas Longhorns — Week 5 Fall Camp

**FB Long-Form (3:00 PM)**
Eighteen days from now, Isa Torres and Teagan Kavan step into McCombs Field together for the first time as Texas Longhorns in a live game setting.

Week 5 of Texas fall camp has the feel of execution over introduction. Torres, who arrived from FSU with the 2026 D1Softball Player of the Year award (.530 BA, 16 home runs, 56 RBIs), is no longer the new face — she's becoming the identity. Kavan, coming off back-to-back WCWS MVP trophies (the only player in history to accomplish that), is entering her senior season with something to chase that has only been chased twice before.

Only UCLA (1988–90) and Oklahoma (2021–23) have won three consecutive Women's College World Series championships. Texas is trying to join that list.

The September 29 opener against Murray State at McCombs Field (6 PM CT, free admission) will be the first public look at how this two-headed attack fits together. The fall schedule closes November 6 at Texas A&M.

Texas already released its 2027 schedule earlier this week, a sign the program is thinking well beyond this fall. But right now, it's about the reps, the reads, and getting 18 more days of work before real games begin.

### STORY 5: Texas Tech — Week 5 Fall Camp

**FB Long-Form (5:30 PM)**
Five weeks into fall camp, and Gerry Glasco is getting his first real read on what this Texas Tech roster can be.

The headliners need no introduction. Jaysoni Beachum hit .406 with 10 home runs and 69 RBIs at Florida State while earning All-ACC First Team honors. Kasidi Pickering — the No. 2 transfer in the country — hit .384 with 50 home runs at Oklahoma. Jayden Heavener went 13-9 with a 2.81 ERA and 128 strikeouts at LSU. Add in four more All-Americans and you have seven total on one roster.

What Glasco is building at Texas Tech isn't just a collection of individual talent — it's an experiment in chemistry. These players came from different programs, different cultures, different systems. FSU's approach doesn't look like LSU's. OU's isn't the same as either.

Week 5 is where camps start to settle. The adjustment phase gives way to personality. Leaders emerge not because they have to, but because they earn it in reps.

The 2027 Red Raiders were WCWS runners-up last season under Glasco at LSU. Now he's doing it with his own program, his own transfer class ranked No. 1 nationally. The potential is there. The work continues.
