The Big 12 Is No Longer Oklahoma's Conference

By Sarah Mitchell

November 24, 2026

For a generation of college softball fans, the Big 12 had one team at the top and everyone else trying to stay close. Oklahoma was the conference. The Sooners recruited nationally, produced All-Americans at every position, and went to Oklahoma City so often that the Women's College World Series felt like a home event.

That dynamic is changing — and the numbers prove it.

Arizona State's 2027 signing class is ranked No. 8 nationally by D1Softball and No. 10 by Softball America, making it the highest-ranked recruiting class in the Big 12 not named Oklahoma. For a program that has been building toward this moment under consistent investment in roster construction and facilities, reaching consensus top-10 status is not just a milestone. It is a statement about where Arizona State is going.

Three Programs at the Top

Add up what the Big 12 is bringing into 2027 and the picture is striking.

Oklahoma holds the No. 1 national recruiting class according to D1Softball, anchored by a returning roster that includes Jordy Bearce — 39 home runs, NFCA Freshman of the Year in 2026 — and Karli Kavan, who has won the WCWS Most Outstanding Player award twice. The Sooners added No. 1 overall prospect Goose Hutchens, already enrolled. Oklahoma lost significant pieces in Kasidi Pickering and others, but the class restocking suggests the program is not in a rebuilding mode. It is reloading.

Texas Tech holds the No. 1 transfer class nationally per Softball America. That list is headlined by Pickering herself — .384 batting average, 50 home runs, 169 RBI over her career at Oklahoma — plus a full complement of portal additions under coach Jenna Cottrill and pitching coordinator Ashley Pendley. Texas Tech was already a WCWS finalist. With this roster, they are a legitimate championship contender.

And now Arizona State sits at No. 8 nationally, the highest in the conference outside Norman. Big 12 play opens March 12. The conference tournament is going to be extraordinary.

What It Means for the Title Picture

The 2027 Women's College World Series will likely feature a Big 12 program in the championship game. The question is whether it is one of these three — or whether the SEC's depth advantage proves decisive one more time.

D1Softball's current top 10 has six SEC programs: Oklahoma at No. 1, LSU at No. 2, Auburn at No. 4, Stanford at No. 5, Florida at No. 6, Tennessee at No. 9. Three Pac-era programs round out the list. The SEC is as deep as ever.

But depth cuts both ways. When three Big 12 programs are genuinely elite, they can share the postseason load in a way that one dominant team never could. Big 12 teams play each other tough every week of conference play. By the time the postseason arrives, they will have been tested against elite competition in ways that some SEC schedules — where only six programs are truly elite — may not fully replicate.

What's Next

The Big 12 opens conference play March 12. Arizona State, Oklahoma, and Texas Tech will be among the most watched programs from opening day. January's first preseason polls will offer an early verdict on where the national media ranks the conference's top programs. Watch for Arizona State's spring additions — including any portal moves in December — that could push the Sun Devils even higher in the preseason rankings.
