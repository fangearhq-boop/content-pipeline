5-for-5 and the House Settlement: College Softball's Double Roster Squeeze Explained

By Emily Rawlings | November 15, 2026 | I Love Softball

College softball programs are navigating the most complicated roster environment in the sport's history. Two separate legal and policy developments are colliding at the worst possible time — right before the December transfer portal window and the start of spring practice planning.

Understanding both rules, and how they interact, is essential for any fan trying to follow where players end up this winter.

The House Settlement: 25-Player Cap

The House v. NCAA settlement, finalized this year, established a 25-player roster cap for Division I softball programs. This cap is now in effect. Programs that previously carried significantly larger rosters — particularly power-five programs with 28 to 32 scholarship athletes — must trim down.

For athletes currently enrolled, the cap can mean difficult conversations about scholarship renewals. For recruits and transfers, it means fewer available slots than existed in previous cycles. Programs at the cap cannot simply add a portal transfer without first opening a roster spot.

The 5-for-5 Rule: More Athletes, Same Spots

At the same time, the NCAA adopted the "5-for-5" eligibility model, which would allow athletes from the class of 2022 — whose early collegiate careers were disrupted by the COVID-19 pandemic — to return for a fifth year of competition. The rule would increase the total number of eligible athletes competing in Division I softball.

But the rule is currently on hold. A federal judge in Colorado issued a preliminary injunction in the case Wisne et al. v. NCAA — blocking the full implementation of the rule while litigation continues. The Tenth Circuit Court of Appeals heard oral arguments on October 13. No merits ruling has been issued.

Under the current injunction, programs may roster 5-for-5 eligible athletes — but those athletes cannot practice with the team or compete in games until the litigation resolves. As Kentucky head coach Rachel Lawson said earlier this season, the situation has real "casualties." Memphis de-committed three recruited players on Signing Day because the roster squeeze became untenable.

The Interaction: A Catch-22 for Programs

The two rules together create an impossible situation for many programs. The cap limits how many athletes can be on the roster. The 5-for-5 injunction means eligible athletes who are rostered cannot help the team compete. Programs must decide whether to hold a scholarship slot for an athlete who cannot play — at the cost of a December portal addition who could.

Approximately 200 state-court cases are still active nationwide. No resolution is expected before December. Programs are planning 2027 rosters without knowing the final legal outcome — a situation that will not be fully resolved before spring practice begins.

What's Next

The Tenth Circuit's ruling is the key event to watch. If the court upholds the NCAA's position and lifts the injunction, 5-for-5 athletes could potentially practice and compete in 2027 — but would still count against the 25-player cap. If the court sides with the plaintiffs and strikes down the rule entirely, programs can plan with certainty under the existing cap.

No timeline has been announced for the ruling. For now, college softball fans should expect continued uncertainty — and continued roster movement — through at least the spring of 2027.
