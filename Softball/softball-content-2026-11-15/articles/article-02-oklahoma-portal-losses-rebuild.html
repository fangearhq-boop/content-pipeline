Oklahoma's Transfer Portal Losses: What Patty Gasso Is Working With Heading Into December

By Emily Rawlings | November 15, 2026 | I Love Softball

It has been a painful offseason on the transfer portal front for Oklahoma softball. Four players have departed since the spring: outfielder Kasidi Pickering, utility player Tia Milloy, pitcher Berkley Zache, and utility player Riley Zache. The most significant loss — by a wide margin — is Pickering.

Pickering finished her three seasons in Norman with a .384 batting average, 50 home runs, and 169 RBIs in 189 games. She was a 2025 NFCA First-Team All-American and a 2024 Women's College World Series All-Tournament Team selection. She entered the transfer portal on June 8, 2026, and officially signed with Texas Tech on June 19-20. For the Red Raiders, it is a transformative acquisition. For Oklahoma, it is the program's biggest transfer departure since the portal became a central tool in college softball roster building.

What Oklahoma Adds

Gasso has not been passive in response. Transfer additions Macie Harter (from Middle Tennessee State) and Adi Hansen (from College of Southern Idaho) have joined the program. Neither arrives with Pickering's profile, but both fill roster depth needs at a time when the Sooners' incoming freshman class carries the program's ceiling.

That freshman class remains a primary reason for optimism. D1Softball ranks it No. 1 nationally heading into 2027, led by five-star outfielder Finlee Williams and five-star catcher Riley Hilliard. And Goose Hutchens — originally the top recruit in the 2027 class nationally — reclassified to 2026 and enrolled at Oklahoma this fall. She is already in the building.

Then there is what Oklahoma brings back. Kendall Wells, the NFCA Freshman of the Year, hit 39 home runs in her debut season. Rylie Kavan, a two-time WCWS Most Outstanding Player, returns. The Sooners' returning production in the lineup is as good as anyone's in the country.

The December Portal and What Comes Next

Gasso has been direct about the team's plans. The December transfer portal window — commonly cited as opening December 1 and closing December 15 — is Oklahoma's next opportunity to address roster gaps, particularly in pitching depth after Berkley Zache's departure.

Programs that lose All-American-caliber players in the portal do not simply replace them one-for-one. The December market will be competitive, with programs across the country shopping for similar needs. What Oklahoma can offer — a No. 1-ranked facility, an established pathway to the WCWS, and Wells and Kavan in the lineup — remains among the most compelling pitches in college softball.

Patty Gasso has rebuilt after portal losses before. This is not new territory for her.

What's Next

The December transfer portal window is 16 days away. OU's immediate needs are pitching — Berkley Zache's departure leaves a gap in the rotation that Gasso will look to address. Preseason rankings in January will provide the first real outside assessment of where Oklahoma sits in the 2027 power structure despite this offseason's losses.

No program has more experience than Oklahoma at turning portal chaos into postseason success. The Sooners may not be the favorites on paper this December. But they will be in the conversation by the time January rankings arrive.
