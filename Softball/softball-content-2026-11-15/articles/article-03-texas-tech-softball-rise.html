Texas Tech's Softball Rise: How the Red Raiders Became a Big 12 Power

By Jordan Reeves | November 15, 2026 | I Love Softball

Three offseasons ago, Texas Tech softball was a Big 12 program fighting for recognition in a conference dominated by Oklahoma and Texas. This fall, the Red Raiders hold the No. 1 transfer class in college softball nationally, the first five-star freshman in program history, and an NFCA First-Team All-American transfer from Oklahoma on their roster. The rise is not an accident.

The centerpiece is Kasidi Pickering. The former Oklahoma outfielder — who batted .384 with 50 home runs and 169 RBIs over three seasons in Norman — was one of the most coveted players in the June 2026 transfer portal. She signed with Texas Tech on June 19-20, and her arrival signals a program that has moved past "building phase" and into "competing phase."

No. 1 Transfer Class and the NIL Blueprint

Pickering's signing coincides with a broader recruiting push that has produced results. Texas Tech's transfer class ranks No. 1 nationally per Softball America — a ranking that reflects not just Pickering, but the depth of the program's portal haul this cycle. An investigation by Sports Illustrated documented how an NIL strategy at Texas Tech has enabled the program to offer compensation structures that attract talent from traditional powerhouses in ways that were not viable before the NIL era.

The result is a roster that, on paper, should compete for the Big 12 title on its first day of conference play — March 12 — and potentially far beyond it.

Jocelyn Gentry and the Freshman Factor

The transfer haul is only part of the story. Jocelyn Gentry, the incoming freshman, became the first five-star recruit in Texas Tech softball program history. Her signing signals that the Red Raiders are no longer an afterthought on the recruiting trail — they are a legitimate destination for the highest-rated prospects.

When you combine a No. 1 transfer class with the program's first five-star freshman and an NFCA All-American from a rival program, you are not describing a team on the rise. You are describing a team that has already arrived.

What's Next

Texas Tech opens Big 12 play on March 12, 2027. Preseason rankings — expected from D1Softball, USA TODAY, and ESPN in January — will provide the first formal assessment of where the Red Raiders sit relative to Oklahoma, LSU, Florida, and Tennessee in the national power structure.

The honest answer today: they belong in that conversation. How high they rank in January depends on how the December portal window shapes their final roster.

Lubbock is no longer a sleeper. The only question now is how big the ceiling actually is.
