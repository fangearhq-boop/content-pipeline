### STORY 4: December Transfer Portal Window — What You Need to Know

#### Long-Form Post — 3:00 PM ET

The softball transfer portal opens December 1.

If you've been waiting to find out where your favorite players land — this is the window you've been waiting for.

Here's what you need to know:

📅 **Window:** December 1-15, 2026
🏠 **Roster cap:** 25 players (House v. NCAA settlement in effect)
⚖️ **5-for-5 rule:** Athletes can be rostered but cannot practice or play while litigation continues in the Tenth Circuit

That combination — more eligible players than ever, fewer roster spots than ever — means every move in this window counts double.

The teams most likely to be active buyers: Oklahoma (needs pitching depth after portal losses), Arizona (new staff, roster rebuilding), FSU (lost All-Americans to transfer), Ole Miss (significant departures this summer).

Programs sitting on loaded rosters face harder choices: do you bring in a 5-for-5 athlete who can't play? Do you hold a spot for the spring window?

There's no clear answer. But in 16 days, the market opens — and programs have to decide.

### STORY 5: 5-for-5 and House Settlement: College Softball's Double Roster Squeeze

#### Long-Form Post — 8:00 PM ET

Right now, college softball programs are trying to plan 2027 rosters while two separate legal situations are unresolved.

The House v. NCAA settlement created a 25-player roster cap. That means programs that had 30+ players now have to cut athletes or not renew scholarships.

The 5-for-5 eligibility rule (currently under injunction in the Tenth Circuit) would allow athletes from the class of 2022 — who had their seasons disrupted by COVID — to return for a fifth year. Some of those athletes want to come back. Programs want them back. But under the current injunction, they can be rostered… but cannot practice or play in games.

So you get a Catch-22: carry a scholarship athlete who can't play, or don't honor their return and face the optics of turning away a deserving player.

Kentucky head coach Rachel Lawson called it a situation with "casualties." Memphis already de-committed three recruits on Signing Day because of the roster squeeze. The court heard oral arguments on October 13. No ruling has been issued.

Approximately 200 state-court cases nationwide are still active. No resolution is expected before December.

Programs are doing the best they can in an impossible situation. The athletes caught in the middle deserve better.
