The In-Person Era Begins: Class of 2028 Contact Window Opens Today

By Sarah Mitchell | September 9, 2026

Recruiting

Today at 7:00 AM Eastern Time, the recruiting world changed for the Class of 2028.

The in-person contact window opened this morning for Division I softball coaches, meaning for the first time in this recruiting cycle, a coach can show up at your school, drive to your practice facility, and sit at your kitchen table. The phone calls and texts that defined the first eight days of September contact are still happening — but today they get paired with faces, handshakes, and presence.

That distinction matters more than it might appear. In-person contact is where ambiguity disappears. A coach who texts you every day and a coach who drives four hours to stand in your gym on the first morning they're legally allowed to — those two coaches are not sending the same message.

The Class That Set a Record

The Class of 2028 enters this contact window having already set a milestone: 1,226 prospects have been nominated in the Extra Innings Softball system, the largest pool in the database's history. The talent depth in this class is real, and coaches have been planning their in-person priority lists for weeks.

September 1 opened verbal and electronic contact for the first time — coaches could call, text, and FaceTime. Peyton Ushery answered that opening by committing to Georgia on day one, becoming the first major Division I commitment of the Class of 2028 and marking her place as the No. 2 prospect in Line Drive Media's HOT 100. Since then, the cycle has been building toward today.

What In-Person Contact Actually Looks Like

Under NCAA rules, Division I coaches can now contact Class of 2028 prospects at any time during a contact period — and the window that opened this morning is the first true contact period of this recruiting cycle. That means coaches can attend games, practices, and showcases. They can visit your school and speak with your coaches. They can come to your home.

The first 60 to 90 minutes after 7:00 AM are traditionally the most active stretch of any new contact window. Coaches who have been tracking prospects for months prioritize their top targets immediately. The conversations that happen in that first window often determine whether an in-person visit follows in the coming days, and whether that visit produces a commitment announcement.

For prospects who have not yet had that priority conversation with their target school, today is the day to read the room carefully. How quickly does a coach reach out? Do they reference scheduling an in-person visit? Do they ask when you're available? The answers tell you where you stand.

The Commit Wave Begins

Ushery's September 1 commitment to Georgia was the starting gun for this class. The coaches who have been building relationships over the phone since then now have the tool that closes commitments: the in-person visit. Expect the first wave of Class of 2028 announcements over the next several days as coaches convert their deepest relationships into commitments.

Programs with strong early momentum — those who identified their top targets early, built consistent contact since the calendar opened in September, and were ready to move the moment the in-person window allowed it — are likely to see the first wave of their class fall into place this week.

What's Next

The Class of 2028 in-person contact window is now open, and it will remain open through the full contact period. Watch for commitment announcements over the next several days as coaches make their first in-person pitches to top targets. The first wave of official visit scheduling begins today as well — those visits typically happen in early fall for junior-year prospects, with the highest-priority targets often locking in their schools before spring season begins.

The recruiting class that set a nomination record is now open for business. Today is day one of the in-person era for 2028.
