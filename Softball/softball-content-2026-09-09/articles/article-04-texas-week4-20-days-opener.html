Week 4: Torres and Kavan Are Past the Adjustment Phase

By Sarah Mitchell | September 9, 2026

Texas Longhorns

Twenty days to the fall opener. Week 4 begins in Austin. And the adjustment period for Texas softball's most-watched partnership is over.

Isa Torres joined the Longhorns from Florida State as the 2026 D1Softball National Player of the Year — .530 batting average, 16 home runs, 56 RBI in her final season in Tallahassee. Those numbers explain why the transfer drew national attention. What they don't measure is what Week 4 actually captures: the version of Torres that isn't figuring out her surroundings anymore.

That version — fully acclimated, reading her new teammates' tendencies, trusting the system around her — is what Teagan Kavan gets to hit behind in 2027. And that context changes what you're watching when you see the Longhorns' fall camp.

What Kavan Brings to This Partnership

Kavan enters her senior season as a two-time WCWS Most Outstanding Player — the only player in the tournament's history to earn that designation in consecutive years. Back-to-back MOP awards don't describe an athlete who happens to perform well. They describe an athlete who rises specifically in the highest-stakes environment the college game offers.

Torres and Kavan together in the same lineup creates a problem that opposing coaching staffs will spend considerable time trying to solve before March. Pitch around one, and you face the other. Adjust your pitching plan, and one of them makes you pay. Week 4 is when that dynamic starts to develop from individual excellence into genuine lineup threat.

The Three-Peat Context

Only two programs in WCWS history have won three consecutive national championships: UCLA (1988–1990) and Oklahoma (2021–2023). Texas stands at back-to-back. What the Longhorns do in 2027 will either put them in that company or confirm that the three-peat is the hardest accomplishment in college softball.

Head Coach Mike White rebuilt the Texas program into a national powerhouse. He knew that sustaining it required additions, and Torres is the most significant transfer addition in the country. Combined with Kavan returning for her senior season and a roster that held onto key contributors, the Longhorns enter this fall with the pieces to make a genuine run at history.

Twenty days from now, against Murray State at McCombs Field in a free public setting, the story begins to unfold.

What's Next

Texas opens its fall schedule Monday, September 29, against Murray State at McCombs Field at 6:00 PM CT. The game is free and open to the public. The fall schedule runs through November 6, with the closing contest at Texas A&M's Davis Diamond. The 2027 regular season begins in February.

Week 4 is the beginning of the stretch run toward that opener. Twenty days. The chemistry is building.
