### STORY 2: USA WNT — FB Long-Form
**Posting Window:** 8:00 PM ET
**Type:** Long-Form

Three days. That's all that's left before USA steps onto the Devon Park field for real.

The WBSC Women's Softball World Cup Group Oklahoma opens Thursday, September 12, at OGE Energy Field in Oklahoma City — and USA enters as the field's most prepared team for one simple reason: they've done this before, on this field.

USA went 5-0 at the International Cup at Devon Park earlier this year. Same venue. Same crowd. Same home-state energy. The only thing that changes on Thursday is that both teams in every matchup understand exactly what's at stake.

Head Coach Patty Gasso leads a 16-player roster that is not being assembled in September — it has been built over years. Rachel Garcia and Dejah Mulipola return from the 2020 Olympic gold. Seven players hold 2025 World Games gold medals. Ten made the Down Under Series trip. This is a team that has been forged through international competition, not just named to it.

Opening night: USA vs. No. 7 Mexico at 7:30 PM (Opening Ceremony at 6:30 PM). The round-robin runs through September 15, with the top-seed battle coming against No. 6 Netherlands. Top two teams advance directly to the 2027 Finals in Redcliffe, Queensland — the first formal qualification rung for the LA28 Olympic Games. Third and fourth enter the Repechage.

Three days. Devon Park is ready. 🇺🇸

### STORY 3: Oklahoma — FB Long-Form
**Posting Window:** 1:00 PM ET
**Type:** Long-Form

Three days. That's how long the Sooners have with their complete coaching staff before Patty Gasso, JT Gasso, and Jennifer Rocha board a flight to Oklahoma City for Team USA duties.

Gasso departs September 12 for the WBSC Women's Softball World Cup Group Oklahoma, where she'll lead the USA coaching staff through September 16. The fall schedule resumes September 30 with the opener against Tarleton State at Love's Field — 21 days away.

What Gasso is building into these final three days: every competitive rep she can find.

Kendall Wells, the sophomore who reset the NCAA freshman home run record with 32 bombs last year, has carried that energy directly into fall camp. She homered twice off Sydney Berzon in fall scrimmages — and Berzon doesn't give up easy ones. The LSU two-time All-American (486.1 IP, 2.02 ERA, 406 Ks) is learning the Sooner system while pushing her new teammates to levels they wouldn't reach against lesser competition.

Macie Harter (.346 BA, 7 HR, 23 RBI at MTSU) adds a proven bat to a lineup that was already loaded. Three days of fall camp with this staff won't feel like three days. #OUSoftball 🥎

**Type:** Long-Form

Twenty days.

That's what Texas has before the Sept. 29 fall opener against Murray State at McCombs Field. And as Week 4 of fall camp begins in Austin, one thing has become clear: the adjustment phase is over.

Isa Torres, the 2026 D1Softball National Player of the Year (.530 BA, 16 HR, 56 RBI at FSU), spent her first three weeks learning a new environment. New teammates. New coaching staff. A new city, even if "home" was always somewhere nearby for the Georgetown, Texas native. Week 4 is different. Week 4 is execution.

Teagan Kavan — two-time WCWS Most Outstanding Player, the only player in the tournament's history to win back-to-back — doesn't need to prove anything to anyone. What Week 4 builds for her is the version of herself that shares a lineup with the most dangerous bat in college softball. The chemistry between two elite players is not automatic. It's earned through reps. And three weeks of those reps are turning into something real.

Twenty days. Sept. 29. McCombs Field. Free. Public. The start of the three-peat chase.

Only UCLA (1988–1990) and Oklahoma (2021–2023) have ever won three straight WCWS. Week 4 is the next step. 🤘 #HookEm

### STORY 5: Texas Tech — FB Long-Form
**Posting Window:** 5:30 PM ET
**Type:** Long-Form

Week 4 in Lubbock, and the compound is setting.

Three weeks ago, Gerry Glasco had a roster full of extraordinary résumés and almost no shared practice reps. Seven All-Americans from three programs — Jaysoni Beachum (.406 BA, 10 HR, 69 RBI, All-ACC First Team from FSU), Kasidi Pickering (No. 2 transfer portal ranking, .384 career BA, 50 home runs from OU), and Jayden Heavener (13-9, 2.81 ERA, 128 Ks from LSU) leading the group — had built their reputations in different systems, in different conferences, with different teammates.

Week 4 is where the science experiment stops being theoretical.

Leaders are emerging. Roles are locking in not because of what someone did at their previous school, but because of what they're doing in Lubbock right now. Beachum is setting the tone at the plate. Pickering's power is orienting the lineup. Heavener is establishing herself as the anchor of the pitching staff. Glasco, in his second year building this program from a WCWS runner-up pedigree at LSU, knows this phase well.

When seven All-Americans stop being seven individual competitors and start being one team — that's when Texas Tech becomes dangerous. Week 4 says that moment is arriving. #WreckEm 🥎
