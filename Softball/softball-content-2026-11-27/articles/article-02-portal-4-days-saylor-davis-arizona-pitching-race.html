Portal in 4 Days: Saylor Davis, Arizona's Pitching Search, and the Race That Starts Sunday

By Sarah Mitchell | November 27, 2026 | I Love Softball

Four days. That is what stands between Arizona softball and the December transfer portal window.

When that window opens on December 1, the most urgent pitching market in college softball opens with it. The Wildcats lost D1Softball Player of the Year Mariah Torres to Texas Tech in June and have spent five months without a proven ace capable of carrying the rotation through a postseason run. The portal is their path to solving it — but the window is narrow, the competition is real, and the clock starts Sunday.

Saylor Davis: The Most Anticipated Day 1 Entry

Oklahoma State ace pitcher Saylor Davis is widely expected to enter the December portal on Day 1. Davis has three years of eligibility remaining, making her one of the most valuable arms available in any portal window this cycle. She has been coached at OSU under head coach Kenny Gajewski's program and developed into one of the premier pitchers in the Big 12.

Three years of eligibility is the key factor. Most high-end portal arms entering December have one or two seasons remaining. A pitcher with three years of eligibility represents not just an immediate upgrade but a building block for a program's rotation through 2029.

Arizona is widely viewed as the most likely destination, but Davis's market extends well beyond Tucson.

Why Arizona Is So Desperate

The numbers tell the story. Arizona closed the fall at 15-2 — a record that reflects genuine talent across the roster. Regan Shockey, the Big 12 Defensive Player of the Year, anchors the infield. Head coach Caitlin Lowe, in her first full season, has a roster capable of making a deep postseason run. The hole is singular and specific: the front of the rotation.

Torres was not just a good pitcher. She was D1Softball's national Player of the Year — the top individual award in the sport. When she left for Texas Tech in the June portal, she did not just take her 2026 production with her. She took the blueprint for how Arizona was going to win.

No program in Division I softball entered the fall portal window more desperate for elite pitching than the Wildcats. That desperation does not change with the December window. If anything, it intensifies. Failing to land a proven arm in December leaves Arizona hoping for a 15-2 fall squad to somehow survive a March-to-June postseason run without an ace.

The Competition for Every Available Arm

Arizona is not alone in the market. Ole Miss is hunting portal pitching after a successful 2026 season that ended short of its goals. Auburn, with its top-10 class and high-end position player depth, needs the same thing every SEC program needs: a frontline arm capable of stopping a hot opponent's lineup in a Super Regional or WCWS game.

The compressed nature of the December window intensifies that competition. When the portal opens December 1, every program that has identified a target will be calling simultaneously. The dead period runs December 8-12 — that is a seven-day effective window from portal opening to the last day that coaches can communicate with potential transfers before communications must stop.

Seven days to find, contact, and close a deal on the most valuable commodity in college softball.

What's Next

The December transfer portal window opens December 1 and runs through December 15. The dead period runs December 8-12, creating a seven-day effective window for programs to contact and secure portal commitments. Arizona, Ole Miss, and Auburn are among the programs most urgently seeking elite pitching. Oklahoma State ace Saylor Davis — with three years of eligibility remaining — is expected to enter on Day 1. Programs are using the remaining days of the Thanksgiving recruiting blackout (which ends November 29) to finalize their December portal strategies.
