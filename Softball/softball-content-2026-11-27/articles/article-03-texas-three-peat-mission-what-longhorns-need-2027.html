Texas's Three-Peat Mission: What the Back-to-Back Champions Need for 2027

By Emily Rawlings | November 27, 2026 | I Love Softball

No program in the modern era has won three consecutive national championships in Division I softball.

Texas now has the chance to be the first.

The Longhorns won back-to-back Women's College World Series titles in 2025 and 2026, defeating Texas Tech 4-1 in the championship series both years. It was the first back-to-back finals rematch in 22 years, and it made Texas the fifth program in history to win consecutive national titles. Head coach Patty Gasso had built dynasties at Oklahoma. Now, at the other end of the rivalry, her program is chasing history of its own.

But the team that might win a third title does not yet exist. It has to be rebuilt.

What Texas Lost

Three significant contributors departed via the transfer portal this offseason. Samantha Swan, Samantha Lincoln, and Isa Torres all left the program after the 2026 championship. Combined, they represented a substantial portion of the production and experience that carried Texas through consecutive title runs.

Torres, in particular, was a June portal departure that raised questions about the program's depth heading into 2027. After winning the WCWS for the second time, losing a key contributor to the portal underscored the volatility of roster construction in the NIL era — even for the defending national champions.

The roster does not rebuild itself. Texas has to be active.

What Texas Added

On National Signing Day, November 11, the Longhorns announced the signing of three high school prospects: Lolo Gamble, Brooklyn Hagerty, and Emree Massaad. The class is ranked No. 12 nationally by D1Softball and No. 18 by Softball America — a solid incoming group, though not the top-ranked class in the country.

These are not replacement-level players. Gamble, Hagerty, and Massaad are ranked prospects who chose a defending national champion program over other major offers. The pipeline at Texas remains strong, and the recent back-to-back titles make recruiting easier, not harder.

But three high school prospects do not fill the specific holes left by three departed veterans in a single offseason. The December portal is where Texas completes its roster — if it needs to.

The Case for Texas

Winning two consecutive national titles in the modern era requires more than talent. It requires continuity, depth, and a coaching staff that can reload without rebuilding. Patty Gasso built exactly that model at Oklahoma across a decade of dominance, and she is applying the same framework in Austin.

Texas enters 2027 as the defending champion — not the consensus favorite. Oklahoma, LSU, Tennessee, and Florida are all receiving heavy preseason consideration. That may actually benefit the Longhorns. Flying under the radar, slightly overlooked, playing with the institutional confidence that back-to-back titles provide — that combination has carried Texas through two consecutive championship runs already.

No program has won three in a row in the modern era. If Texas can address its roster needs in December and emerge from the portal window with the right pieces, they will enter January as a legitimate contender for history.

What's Next

The December transfer portal opens December 1 and runs through December 15, with a dead period from December 8-12 that creates a seven-day effective window. Texas's activity in the portal will determine whether the program can fully replace its departed veterans before spring practice begins. National preseason polls drop in January — by that point, Texas's fully assembled 2027 roster will face its first formal ranking under the weight of back-to-back title expectations. No program has won three consecutive national titles in the modern era. That chase begins in December.
