### STORY 1: Thanksgiving Blackout Day 3

#### Long-Form Post

Today is Day 3 of the NCAA Division I softball Thanksgiving recruiting blackout.

From November 25-29, every Division I softball coach in the country is prohibited from contacting any recruit — no calls, no texts, no emails, no DMs. It's a complete communication freeze that applies to prospects from every class year, not just 2027.

Happy Thanksgiving to all the coaches spending the holiday reviewing film and finalizing paperwork for conversations they're not allowed to have yet.

Contact resumes Saturday, November 29. Twenty-four hours later, the December transfer portal window opens. The sprint begins Sunday, December 1.

### STORY 2: Portal 4 Days Out

#### Long-Form Post

Four days. That's how long Arizona has to wait before the December transfer portal opens — and the race to find the program's next ace begins.

In June, the Wildcats lost Mariah Torres — the D1Softball Player of the Year — to Texas Tech. Arizona closed its fall slate at 15-2, but without a frontline pitcher capable of carrying a rotation through the postseason, the question isn't whether this team is talented enough to make a run. It's whether they can get one.

Enter Oklahoma State pitcher Saylor Davis. With three years of eligibility remaining, Davis is widely expected to enter the December portal on Day 1 — December 1. Arizona is considered the most likely destination, but Ole Miss and Auburn are actively recruiting the same pool of portal pitchers.

The dead period runs December 8-12. That means teams have roughly seven effective days from portal opening to close a deal before communication shuts down again.

Seven days. One critical need. Multiple programs competing for a small pool of elite arms.

### STORY 3: Texas Three-Peat

#### Long-Form Post

Texas is the back-to-back national champion. The Longhorns defeated Texas Tech 4-1 in both the 2025 and 2026 Women's College World Series championship series — the first back-to-back rematch finals in 22 years. Texas is now one of only five programs in history to win consecutive national titles.

But the team that wins a third straight will look different from the one that just won two.

The Longhorns lost three significant contributors to the transfer portal this offseason: Samantha Swan, Samantha Lincoln, and Isa Torres all departed. Texas replaced them on November 11 with three highly rated high school prospects — Lolo Gamble, Brooklyn Hagerty, and Emree Massaad — a class ranked No. 12 nationally by D1Softball and No. 18 by Softball America.

The question isn't talent. It's depth, continuity, and whether the December portal gives head coach Patty Gasso another lever to pull.

No program has won three consecutive national titles in the modern era. Texas has two months to find out whether they can be the first.

### STORY 4: January Rankings Battle

#### Long-Form Post

January is when college softball's preseason argument gets settled on paper — at least temporarily.

Right now, there are two polls and two different answers at the top. D1Softball has Oklahoma at No. 1, and the case is strong: Julianna "Goose" Hutchens enrolled, Finlee Williams and Riley Hilliard signed, Paige Kavan returning as a two-time WCWS MOP, and Jayda Coleman Wells coming back after hitting 39 home runs. The Sooners' class is as deep as any in the country.

Softball America has LSU at No. 1 — the first time in program history. Beth Torina's Tigers signed five-star utility player Sydney Gonglik and pitcher Macie Bryant to a class that brought in the Pickens twins from the portal.

Then there's Tennessee, which returns nine starters behind Sage Mardjetko (16-3, 1.26 ERA) — the most intact rotation in the SEC. And Florida, ranked No. 2 by Softball America, has never won a national title but added Tori Edwards from LSU.

December portal moves will shift all of these rankings before January polls drop. The team that makes the best use of the next two weeks may enter the new year holding the top spot.

### STORY 5: 5-for-5 Enters December

#### Long-Form Post

The Tenth Circuit reversed the federal Wisne v. NCAA injunction earlier this month. But the legal battle over the NCAA's 5-for-5 eligibility rule is far from over.

More than 200 state court cases covering over 850 athletes are still active. State courts have been granting injunctions independently of the federal ruling, creating a patchwork system where athletes in some states can compete under court protection while athletes in others cannot. Programs in states with active injunctions are carrying athletes on their rosters who may or may not be eligible under NCAA rules — depending entirely on where they play.

Congressional legislation has been discussed as a path toward a uniform national standard, but nothing has been enacted.

As programs finalize their rosters heading into 2027, the uncertainty isn't going away. Some coaching staffs are planning with two different roster scenarios depending on how their state's cases resolve in December.

The most confusing eligibility situation in college sports history is heading into another month without resolution.
