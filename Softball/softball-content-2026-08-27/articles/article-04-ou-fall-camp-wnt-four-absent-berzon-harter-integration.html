Gasso Is Building Two Rosters at Once: OU Fall Camp Without the WNT Four

By Jordan Reeves | I Love Softball | August 27, 2026

Patty Gasso is running two fall camps at the same time — and only one of them is in Norman. While Alyssa Brito, Jayda Coleman, Tiare Jennings, and Kelly Maxwell represent the United States in Oklahoma City with the Women's National Team through September 16, the Sooners practicing at Love's Field are building the kind of chemistry that falls apart when four starters are absent. Gasso is watching it all unfold — and she is learning things about this team that no one else will see until the season matters.

The Transfer Integration Is Already Happening

Sydney Berzon did not come to Oklahoma to wait. The NFCA Second Team All-American from LSU — rated the No. 1 available pitcher in the transfer portal when she committed — brings a resume built over multiple seasons at one of the sport's elite programs. In four years at LSU: 486.1 innings pitched, a 2.02 ERA, 406 strikeouts, and a 52-25 record. She earned All-American recognition in back-to-back seasons (Second Team 2024, Third Team 2023). She is a proven commodity. She needs only to learn a new system, a new catcher's cadence, and a new pitching coach's preferences.

In the first Battle Series scrimmage at Love's Field — which drew 2,351 fans — Berzon participated alongside the group currently in Norman. The early returns on her integration are the kind of data point Gasso evaluates quietly. A pitcher with Berzon's profile does not need a coaching staff to manufacture confidence. The adjustments are technical, not philosophical.

Macie Harter is doing the same in the outfield. Harter hit .346 at Middle Tennessee State with 7 home runs, 15 doubles, 4 triples, 23 RBI, and 37 walks — a stat line that reflects both bat-to-ball skill and an exceptionally disciplined approach at the plate. She tied a program record in walks. Those are not numbers that disappear in a new conference. They are the product of a hitter who understands the strike zone.

What Gasso Is Watching Right Now

When four starters are gone, the players who remain tell you things about depth that you cannot see in a full-roster practice. Who adjusts? Who leads in a lineup that is missing familiar names? Who performs when the competition is redistributed across a shorter bench?

These are not abstract questions at Oklahoma. The Sooners have a reputation built on competing at the highest level, and after missing the WCWS in 2025 for the first time since 2015, the pressure in Norman this fall is real. Gasso is not rebuilding — she is reloading. But reloading with transfers requires a specific kind of evaluation that you can only do in fall camp, when the stakes are low enough to experiment and high enough to matter.

September 16, the WNT event window closes in Oklahoma City, and Brito, Coleman, Jennings, and Maxwell come back to Norman. That is when Gasso gets to see the full picture — what changes when the four returners slot back in, what Berzon and Harter look like alongside the players who have been away. No other coaching staff in the country will have seen both versions of this roster before the 2027 season opens.
