16 Days to Group Oklahoma: Why Every Game Matters for USA Softball

By Emily Rawlings | I Love Softball | August 27, 2026

Sixteen days until the first pitch at Devon Park in Oklahoma City. The WBSC Women's Softball World Cup Group Stage runs September 12-16, and the format is ruthlessly simple: six nations play round-robin, and only the top two advance to the 2027 Finals in Redcliffe, Queensland — the gateway to LA28. Finishing third goes home. Every game in Group Oklahoma has direct consequences.

The Opener: USA vs. Mexico (September 12)

USA (No. 2 in the world) opens against Mexico (No. 7) on September 12. This is not a warmup. Mexico holds the No. 7 world ranking for a reason, and their ability to compete at the WBSC level has been consistent across multiple recent events. USA has strong historical results against Mexico, but in a round-robin format where every result matters for advancement positioning, a loss on opening day reshuffles the math for everything that follows.

USA would still have opportunities to recover — the format is six games per team over five days — but entering the Mexico game with anything less than full preparation is a risk the coaching staff will not take. Rachel Garcia and Dejah Mulipola, the two 2020 U.S. Olympians on the 16-player roster, bring pressure-situation experience that is directly relevant to a game where both teams know what a Group Oklahoma win means.

The Top-Seed Battle: USA vs. Netherlands

The Netherlands (No. 6) is the game that determines seeding. The Dutch have competed at the top level of international softball for decades — technically disciplined, with pitching depth that makes one-run games genuinely dangerous. If both USA and the Netherlands win their games against the rest of the group, the direct result between them will determine whether USA exits as the first or second seed.

In a tournament setting where semifinal matchups are determined by seeding, finishing first in Group Oklahoma matters. The path to the 2027 Finals in Redcliffe is not just about getting there — it is about entering the eight-team field in the strongest position possible.

China (No. 8) sits just below the Netherlands and should not be dismissed. China performs above their ranking in group stages consistently. A focused China team in a must-advance situation is a different team than their world ranking implies.

The Roster Built for This

The USA roster for Group Oklahoma has layers. Garcia and Mulipola provide Olympic-stage experience. The 16-player group also includes seven athletes from the 2025 World Games gold medal squad and four Sooners — Alyssa Brito, Jayda Coleman, Tiare Jennings, and Kelly Maxwell — who train and play at Devon Park in Norman. Home-field familiarity is not irrelevant at this level of competition. These four players know the turf, the lights, and the crowd dynamic.

New Zealand (No. 23) and South Africa (No. 36) round out the six-team group. Both are legitimate World Cup participants. Neither is a guaranteed win — round-robin upsets happen — but both represent the easier part of USA's schedule in terms of world ranking differential.

What's Next

The WBSC Women's Softball World Cup Group Stage takes place September 12-16 at Devon Park in Oklahoma City. USA opens against Mexico (No. 7) on September 12. Top 2 of 6 advance to the 2027 Finals in Redcliffe, Queensland, Australia. Full schedule and rosters at wbsc.org and usasoftball.com.
