Texas Tech Is Preseason No. 1. NiJaree Canady and 105 Combined Transfer HRs Explain Why.

By Sarah Mitchell | I Love Softball | August 27, 2026

Texas Tech enters the 2027 season as a preseason No. 1 in a major poll. If you know what the Red Raiders added this offseason, the ranking is not a surprise — it is a logical conclusion from a roster construction that looks intentionally designed to compete for a national title from the season's first weekend.

The Foundation: NiJaree Canady

Canady anchors the Texas Tech pitching staff, and that matters more than any other single roster factor in college softball. A true ace defines a program's ceiling — not the offensive firepower, not the depth in the field, not the coaching adjustments. The ace determines how many times your team can win a low-scoring game in March, in conference play, and in the postseason when every run is earned. Canady has been one of the elite arms in the sport across multiple seasons, and she gives the Red Raiders a built-in Cy Young conversation every time she takes the circle.

Programs with aces of Canady's caliber tend to be competitive in every game they play. Programs without that kind of anchor — even ones that can hit — are always one bad pitching day from a loss that damages their profile. Texas Tech, with Canady on the mound, does not have that ceiling.

The Transfer Class: 105 Combined Home Runs

The offense that surrounds Canady was built through the transfer portal, and the numbers are striking. Seven transfers came in during the 2026-27 cycle. Combined, they hit 105 home runs at their previous schools. For context, the Red Raiders produced 60 home runs as a team in a prior season — this single transfer class nearly doubles that output on paper.

The additions: Mia Williams from Florida, Taylor Pannell from Tennessee, Jasmyn Burn from Ohio State, Kaitlyn Terry from UCLA, Lagi Quiroga from Cal, and Jackie Lis and Desirae Spearman from Southern Illinois. That is power from multiple Power conferences on one side — programs that regularly host regional weekends and compete in the Women's College World Series — and consistent production from mid-major programs known for developing hitters on the other.

The mix is not accidental. Power hitters from high-competition environments bring park-adjusted production. Players from programs like Southern Illinois have often faced elite pitching at MAC tournaments and conference play — they are not underdeveloped prospects. The blend reflects a roster-building philosophy that values proven numbers over raw potential.

The Preseason No. 1 Context

Preseason polls are imperfect. Programs ranked No. 1 in August lose to unranked opponents in February. Programs left out of preseason top-25 ballots win national championships in May. The sport does not reward preseason positioning — it rewards performance in elimination games in Oklahoma City.

What makes Texas Tech's preseason case more compelling than most is the layering. They have a true ace. They have a renovated lineup. They are in the Big 12, which provides the conference depth to be battle-tested before the postseason begins. Programs that can check all three of those boxes simultaneously do not fall out of top-25 conversations easily.

The Red Raiders will play enough games against ranked competition before March is over to validate or complicate the preseason billing. That process is part of what makes the 2027 season worth following from day one.

What's Next
