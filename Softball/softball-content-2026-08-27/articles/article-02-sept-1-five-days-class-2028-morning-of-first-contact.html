5 Days to September 1: Here's What the Morning of First Contact Actually Looks Like

By Sarah Mitchell | I Love Softball | August 27, 2026

Five days. On September 1, the phones open for the Class of 2028 — the first day NCAA Division I coaches can call, text, or email recruits directly. If you are a current high school junior in that class, this is the moment the recruiting calendar has been building toward. Here is what the morning actually looks like, so you are not caught off guard when it arrives.

The First Hour Is Everything

September 1 does not start at 9 AM. In the Eastern time zone, coaches who are serious about a recruit begin making calls before the school day begins. Programs with a clear No. 1 target on their list have often planned this call for weeks — they know what they will say, they know their talking points, and they are not waiting to see how other programs move first.

The first hour of September 1 is the most information-dense window of the entire recruitment process. By 8 or 9 AM, every program that has you at the top of their priority list will have attempted to reach you. The recruits who pick up — and who are prepared to have a real conversation — make the best impression in that window.

By midday, most programs will have worked through their top tier and moved on to the second group — recruits they are genuinely interested in but were not certain enough about to prioritize first thing in the morning. By the end of the day, the landscape of your recruiting looks completely different than it did on August 31.

What the Call Volume Tells You

The first-hour call list was finalized by your top programs earlier this week, if not last week. If a program at the top of your list has been in consistent contact through the camp-and-evaluation period, you likely already have a sense of where you stand with them. September 1 confirms it.

If a program you care about has not called by noon, that is information. Not a final verdict — programs have different processes, different time zones, different staff structures — but it tells you where you sit relative to their internal priority ranking. Programs do not accidentally forget to call the recruits they most want to sign.

By contrast, if you receive calls from three programs you had ranked lower on your own list and silence from a program you had prioritized, that is also information worth processing before the day is over. Recruitments have surprised both coaches and recruits in the September 1 window before. What you learn that morning is worth sitting with before you commit to a direction.

September 9: The Next Window

September 1 opens phone and digital communication. September 9 at 7:00 AM ET is the in-person contact window — the first date when D1 coaches can appear at your high school, watch you at a fall tournament in person, or host you on campus for an official visit. The two dates are separate tools in the recruiting process.

Some programs use September 1 calls to establish communication and then prioritize September 9 in-person evaluations to make their final decisions. Others make scholarship offers over the phone or by text on September 1 itself. Knowing which type of program you are dealing with helps you understand the speed of the process.

What You Can Still Do in Five Days

The call-order lists are already set. The real work — film, showcase performance, camp visits, communication since June — happened before this week. But in five days, one thing still matters: confirming your interest. A brief message to the programs you care most about — "I'm looking forward to hearing from you September 1, this program is a priority for me" — puts your name in front of the coach the day before the window opens. It does not change where you rank on a finalized list, but it reinforces your commitment at exactly the moment when coaches are thinking about who to call first.

Be ready to answer on September 1. Know your priorities before the phone rings. The morning is almost here.
