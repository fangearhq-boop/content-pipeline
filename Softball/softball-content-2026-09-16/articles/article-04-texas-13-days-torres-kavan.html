13 Days: Torres and Kavan Are Building Something Historic in Austin

By Jordan Reeves | I Love Softball | September 16, 2026

Texas's most anticipated fall opener in program history is 13 days away — and the players chasing a three-peat are running out of time to prepare.

The countdown inside the Texas softball program is not just to a fall opener. It is to the first public glimpse of what might become the most celebrated roster in the history of the sport. On September 29, at 6 PM CT at McCombs Field, Texas opens its fall schedule against Murray State. Admission is free. The audience will be enormous. And Isa Torres and Hannah Kavan will be sharing the same dugout in burnt orange for the first time anyone outside the program has seen.

Thirteen days. The most watched clock in college softball right now is ticking in Austin.

Two Players, One Goal

Isa Torres does not require much introduction to anyone who followed the 2026 season. The D1Softball National Player of the Year. The First Team All-American with a .530 batting average, 16 home runs and 56 RBI at Florida State. The Gold Glove recipient in center field. She is from Georgetown, Texas — about 30 miles north of Austin — and when she chose to transfer to Texas, she called it coming home. Head coach Mike White and his staff landed the best player in college softball. Now they get to put her in the lineup.

Hannah Kavan needs no additional credentials. She is the only player in Women's College World Series history to win the Most Outstanding Player award twice — doing it consecutively, in 2025 and 2026. She is a senior now, and she enters this season with one objective that no one in college softball has ever achieved: three consecutive national championships. A three-peat.

The Historical Weight

What Texas is attempting has been done only twice in NCAA softball history. UCLA won three consecutive national championships from 1988 through 1990. Oklahoma did the same from 2021 through 2023. Those are the only two programs to ever accomplish it. Texas won in 2025. Texas won in 2026. The Longhorns finished that season 53-12 and are entering 2027 with the most complete roster in the country — headlined by a two-time POY from last year's national champion and a returning two-time WCWS MOP.

Mike White, whose staff earned the NFCA Coaching Staff of the Year award for the second consecutive year, is not treating this fall as a transition period. It is a runway. The chemistry being built right now between Torres, Kavan and their teammates will be what carries this team into March.

What's Next

The fall schedule is short but pointed: Murray State on September 29, Texas State on October 13 and Temple on October 20. Three games to set a foundation. Three games to let a historically talented roster show how it fits together. The first one — September 29, free, McCombs Field, 6 PM CT — will be the most-watched fall game in Texas softball history. If you are anywhere near Austin that evening, you will want to be there.
