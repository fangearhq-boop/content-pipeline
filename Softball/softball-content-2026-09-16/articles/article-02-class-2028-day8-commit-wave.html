Class of 2028 Commit Wave Enters Day 8 With No Signs of Slowing

By Sarah Mitchell | I Love Softball | September 16, 2026

Eight days into the in-person contact window, the 2028 recruiting class is shaping up to be historically deep — and the commitments keep coming.

The in-person contact window for the Class of 2028 opened September 9 at 7 AM ET, and eight days later, the softball recruiting world is moving fast. Programs across the SEC, ACC, Pac-12 and Big 12 have been hosting campus visits, sitting in living rooms and making the calls that turn interest into commitment. The wave is building — and it is not slowing down.

Since the window opened, more than a dozen named commitments have come from top programs. The list keeps growing, and coaches say they expect activity to remain high well into October as the first class rankings update approaches and the remaining uncommitted top-100 prospects weigh their options.

Who Has Committed So Far

The top of the class has already taken shape. Finlee Williams, the No. 1-ranked prospect in the 2028 class by Line Drive Media, committed to Oklahoma. Peyton Ushery (No. 2) chose Georgia. Florida State landed Paityn Donalson, a two-way player who posted a .693 batting average with 11 home runs and 50 RBI — numbers that generated nationwide attention. Oklahoma also added Braylyn Saenz. Texas Tech landed Star Gutierrez, a standout outfielder who became the Red Raiders' first commit in the class.

Tennessee brought in Sophia Galindo and Nevaeh Wilson, both of whom had previously drawn attention from USA Softball under-18 pipeline coaches. Florida landed Kate Losh, a pitcher and two-way player, and catcher Ryann Harris. South Carolina added Siena Myers (C). Arizona, meanwhile, landed two in-state prospects — Ridling and Stell — in back-to-back days to signal their early presence in the class.

A Class Built on Historic Depth

The raw numbers speak to how loaded this group is. Extra Inning Softball received 1,226 nominations for the Class of 2028 — the most nominations in the record books for any class in the publication's history. That volume reflects not just the increased visibility of the sport post-LA28 announcement, but also the genuine breadth of talent at the 14-and-15 year-old level across the country.

Recruiting analysts have pointed to the strength of the pitching pool in particular. Multiple five-star arms are still uncommitted, including names that appeared in USA Softball's U-18 pipeline camps this summer. The catching position is also notably deep, with Myers (South Carolina), Harris (Florida) and several others who have not yet committed among the most coveted players at the position nationally.

What's Next

The in-person window remains open, and programs are still executing their call lists. Recruiting analysts expect a surge in announcements through late September and early October as more campus visits conclude and players make their decisions before the academic year gets fully underway. The first major class rankings update is expected in mid-October, which will reset the national picture and likely trigger another round of activity as programs reassess their target lists. For fans tracking the Class of 2028, the best advice is simple: keep watching. The window is open and the wave is rolling.
