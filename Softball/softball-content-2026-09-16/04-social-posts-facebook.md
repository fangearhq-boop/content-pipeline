### STORY 1: USA WNT — Group Oklahoma Playoff Day

**Long-Form Post — 9:00 AM ET**
🇺🇸 IT ALL COMES DOWN TO TODAY 🇺🇸

Group Oklahoma at Devon Park ends Wednesday. And it ends with everything on the line.

The WBSC Women's Softball World Cup Group Oklahoma City has been building to this moment for five days. Today, the playoff bracket runs three sessions — 1 PM, 4 PM and 7 PM ET — and when it's all over, two nations will have earned direct berths to the 2027 WBSC Women's Softball World Cup Finals in Redcliffe, Queensland.

Those Finals are the primary qualifier for the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics. Today is not just about a group stage. It is about who makes it to LA28.

USA enters the bracket as the top seed after a dominant 4-0 group stage run that included run-rule wins over Mexico and Brazil (17-0), demonstrating why they are the world's most dangerous softball nation. China is also in the field. So are the Netherlands — a team that showed real competitive grit through the round-robin.

The bracket is simple: the No. 1 and No. 2 seeds clash at 1 PM ET, with the winner earning their direct Redcliffe berth. The No. 3 and No. 4 seeds meet at 4 PM ET, and the winner of that game faces the loser of the 1 PM match in a 7 PM Repechage to claim the second spot.

Gasso, JT Gasso and Rocha — who have been coaching Team USA through the entire Group Oklahoma event — are expected to return to Norman after today's bracket concludes. The coaching staff and their four Sooners (Brito, Coleman, Jennings, Maxwell) plus Tokyo 2020 silver medalists Garcia and Mulipola have been a dominant force at Devon Park all week.

Two tickets to Redcliffe. One city. Today.

### STORY 4: Texas Longhorns — 13 Days to Sept. 29

**Long-Form Post — 3:00 PM ET**
🤘 13 DAYS UNTIL HISTORY STEPS ONTO MCCOMBS FIELD 🤘

September 29. Murray State. McCombs Field. 6 PM CT. Free.

That is the moment Texas softball fans have been circling on their calendars since the spring. Because September 29 is the first public chance to see Isa Torres and Hannah Kavan together in burnt orange.

Isa Torres: 2026 D1Softball National Player of the Year. .530 batting average. 16 home runs. 56 RBI. First Team All-American. Gold Glove. All from a season at Florida State — before she chose to come home to Texas. She grew up in Georgetown, TX, about 30 miles from Austin. She is a Longhorn now. And she is that good.

Hannah Kavan: Senior. Two-time WCWS Most Outstanding Player — 2025 and 2026. The only player in the history of the Women's College World Series to win that award twice. She is chasing something no one has chased in decades.

A three-peat.

Only two programs in NCAA softball history have won three consecutive national championships: UCLA (1988, 1989, 1990) and Oklahoma (2021, 2022, 2023). Texas won in 2025. Texas won in 2026. If the Longhorns win in 2027, they join the most exclusive list in college softball — with Torres and Kavan at the center of it.

Head coach Mike White, whose staff earned the NFCA Coaching Staff of the Year award for the second consecutive season, is building toward that.

13 days. Three games: Murray State (Sept. 29), Texas State (Oct. 13), Temple (Oct. 20).

The most anticipated fall in Austin. We cannot wait. 🤘
