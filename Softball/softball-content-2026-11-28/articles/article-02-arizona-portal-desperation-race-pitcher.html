Arizona's Desperation Race: 72 Hours to Fix a Rotation

By Emily Rawlings

November 28, 2026

The December transfer portal opens in 72 hours, and no program in college softball enters it with more urgency than Arizona.

When Mariah Torres — the D1Softball Player of the Year — entered the summer portal and transferred to Texas Tech, the Wildcats lost more than their best pitcher. They lost their identity. Torres was the kind of arm that makes rosters around her better: a proven, dominant presence who could carry a rotation through a March grind, a conference tournament, and a regional. When she left for Lubbock in June, Arizona's staff knew December was coming, and December had to deliver.

It hasn't happened yet. And the clock is running down.

The Most Contested Market in the Sport

Arizona is not the only program that needs pitching. It is, by most accounts, the most desperate one — but the December portal market for frontline arms is exceptionally competitive.

Programs including Ole Miss, Auburn, and Texas A&M are expected to be active on Day 1 of the Dec. 1 portal window. Each has rotation questions heading into 2027. Each has NIL resources and recruiting infrastructure to make serious offers to top portal arms. For Arizona, that means the Wildcats aren't just racing to find a pitcher. They're racing to find one before three or four other programs do.

Oklahoma State's Saylor Davis is widely considered the most anticipated Day 1 portal entry. She carries three years of eligibility, which is exactly what programs looking for a long-term anchor need. If she enters on Dec. 1 as expected, the calls will come immediately — and from every direction.

The Window Problem

Beyond competition, Arizona faces a structural challenge: time.

The Dec. 1 portal opening is followed by a dead period from Dec. 8-12. That means every program — including Arizona — has roughly seven effective days to recruit portal entrants before in-person contact is prohibited again. Official visits, campus tours, and in-person meetings all go on pause during the dead period.

For a pitcher who enters the portal on Dec. 1, that means about a week of active recruitment before programs are forced to step back. The programs that move fastest — with concrete scholarship offers, roster conversations, and genuine relationship-building — will win portal targets in that window. The ones that delay or wait to see who else commits will fall behind.

Head Coach Caitlin Lowe has built Arizona back into a consistent regional host. The Wildcats have the facilities, the brand, and the academic profile to attract top portal players. What they need this December is a pitcher who can walk into the circle in February and compete at the top of the Pac-12.

What Arizona Needs

The ideal portal addition for Arizona is a frontline starter with at least two years of eligibility and a proven history of handling a full conference schedule. The most important quality isn't raw stuff — it's durability. Programs that go deep in the postseason need pitchers who can throw four games in four days at a regional and an extra-inning super regional game on top of that.

Torres provided that. Finding her equivalent in a December portal window, against active competition from programs with similar resources, is not simple.

But Arizona has no choice but to try. The alternative — going into 2027 with the same rotation question they had in October — is not acceptable for a program with their expectations.

What's Next

The transfer portal opens Tuesday, Dec. 1. Arizona is expected to be active from the first hour, targeting pitchers with high-eligibility profiles. With the dead period (Dec. 8-12) creating a compressed shopping window, Wildcat fans should expect movement — announcements, official visits, scholarship offers — in the first 48 to 72 hours.
