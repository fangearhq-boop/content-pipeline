Signing Day Is 4 Days Away: The Top Uncommitted 2027 Targets Programs Are Chasing

By Jordan Reeves

November 28, 2026

The fall signing window opens Wednesday, Dec. 2 — four days from now — and while most of the elite 2027 class has already committed verbally to programs around the country, a handful of top-50 prospects remain unsigned. Those are the players who will define the final class rankings and potentially hand a program a game-changing addition heading into 2027.

Some of the sport's biggest names are still in play. And the programs chasing them are doing everything they can in the final 36 hours of the blackout to be ready when contact resumes Monday morning.

The Pitcher Everyone Wants

The most discussed uncommitted recruit in the 2027 class is pitcher Vivian Knott, ranked No. 7 overall by Softball America. Knott has taken multiple campus visits, including a stop at Oklahoma, and has yet to announce where she'll sign.

At a position where supply never meets demand, Knott represents something rare: a top-10 overall recruit who can fundamentally alter a program's ceiling. Oklahoma's interest is well-documented — the Sooners have hosted her and need exactly what she offers. But she's not choosing between bad options. Multiple elite programs have been in communication, and the Signing Day window may finally force a decision.

Programs that can offer a proven development track record — documented by statistics, alumni in the NFCA Coaching Honor Rolls, players who improved their ERA by 0.50+ in their first season — have an edge over ones that simply offer money. Knott has the ability and the market value to pick for fit.

The Catcher Situation

Beyond pitching, there are top-tier catchers and infielders in the 2027 class still weighing their options. While Riley Hilliard's commitment to Oklahoma has been confirmed, teams with open catcher spots are actively pursuing comparable prospects in the class.

Catching is a position where the supply is thin every year. Teams that miss on their primary target in December will face the same competition again in February's signing window, with a smaller pool of available options. The programs that close in the next four days will have an enormous competitive advantage heading into 2027.

What Happens to Rankings After Dec. 4

Recruiting rankings in college softball are fluid throughout the cycle, but the fall signing window typically triggers a significant reshuffling. Programs that add one or two top-25 prospects in the Dec. 2-4 window can jump three to five spots in class rankings overnight. Programs that miss their primary targets can drop just as quickly.

Oklahoma enters Signing Day at No. 1 nationally — but that ranking is built on committed position players. If a program like Tennessee or Florida locks up a top pitcher in the Dec. 2-4 window, the gap between No. 1 and No. 2 could narrow significantly heading into the final February signing period.

For uncommitted prospects, the leverage is real. The sport is watching. The decisions made over the next four days will be discussed all winter.

The Second Bite: February 3

It's worth noting for recruits who aren't ready to sign this week: the second signing window opens Feb. 3, 2027. Programs will continue building their classes in the meantime, and some prospects who want more time, more visits, or simply more information may hold off until February.

But the Dec. 2-4 window is typically the more active of the two, and the programs that need to fill specific spots — particularly pitching — will be making their most aggressive pushes this week. For elite uncommitted prospects, the next four days are the ones that determine where they'll spend the next four years.
