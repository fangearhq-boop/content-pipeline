Utah Talons Win Game 2, Claim Back-to-Back AUSL Championships

By Sarah Mitchell | I Love Softball | July 26, 2026

The Utah Talons are the 2026 AUSL champions. Again.

Utah defeated the Chicago Bandits in Game 2 of the AUSL Championship Series on Sunday at Davis Diamond in College Station, Texas, completing a sweep and becoming the first team in AUSL history to win back-to-back championships. The Talons also swept the Bandits in the 2025 inaugural championship series — winning Game 1 three-to-one and Game 2 one-to-nothing on a Montana Fouts complete-game shutout. Now they have done it a second time, across back-to-back seasons, against the same opponent.

Professional softball has its first dynasty.

How Utah Built This Sweep

The Talons won five of their six regular-season meetings against the Bandits in 2026 and earned the No. 1 seed. While Chicago had to survive a grueling eleven-inning play-in game against Portland Cascade on Thursday — a 6-5 walk-off victory that required extra-inning pitching and a taxed bullpen — Utah waited with five days of rest heading into the championship.

Game 1 went exactly as the Talons scripted it. Georgina Corrick pitched a complete game, allowing just 4 hits and 1 earned run, striking out 5 and walking nobody. She retired 15 of the final 16 batters she faced. Sahvanna Jaquish delivered the go-ahead sacrifice fly in the bottom of the 6th. Final score: Utah 2, Chicago 1. The Bandits' ace Lexi Kilfoyl started and took the loss.

With the bullpen fresh and a series lead in hand, Montana Fouts — the 2026 AUSL Pitcher of the Year — took the ball for Game 2. She entered Sunday's start with four AUSL single-season records: innings pitched (61.1), strikeouts (63), wins (7), and complete games (7). The Talons needed one more from their best pitcher. They got it.

The Bandits Had No Answer

Chicago arrived at this series with legitimate credentials. Lexi Kilfoyl finished the regular season at 6-0. Skylar Wallace became the first qualified player in AUSL history to finish a full season with a .500 batting average. The Bandits knocked out the Portland Cascade in an eleven-inning thriller to even earn a spot in the championship.

But the rest disadvantage was real. And against Utah's pitching — the deepest and most dominant staff in the AUSL — it showed. The Talons led the league in ERA (2.67), strikeouts (143), and complete games (16). They allowed the fewest home runs (17) and fewest earned runs (66) of any team in the six-team league. Pitching depth wins championships. Utah has it. No other team in the AUSL comes close.

What This Means for the Sport

Two seasons. Two sweeps. One opponent both times. The Utah Talons have established something that professional softball has never seen before: a repeating champion, a program that sustains excellence across multiple seasons, a roster that delivers in the moments that matter most.

Hannah Flippen — the 2025 AUSL Defensive Player of the Year — anchors a defense that holds leads. Fouts leads a pitching staff that rarely allows games to get away. The Talons won 4 of 5 against Chicago in the regular season. They swept both times in the postseason.

The first professional softball dynasty is Utah's. The question heading into the third AUSL season is no longer whether any team can compete at Utah's level. The question is whether any team can get close enough to make it interesting.

What's Next

The AUSL's third season is scheduled to begin in 2027. The Utah Talons enter as two-time defending champions — the only back-to-back titleholder in league history. The Chicago Bandits, who have appeared in both championship series, will return as the league's other top contender. The Portland Cascade, in their inaugural 2026 season, advanced to the play-in game and will return as a legitimate threat. The AUSL also has room for expansion as professional softball continues to grow on the strength of its ESPN and ABC broadcast deals.
