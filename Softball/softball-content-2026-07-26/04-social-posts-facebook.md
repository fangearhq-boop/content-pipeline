### STORY 1: AUSL Championship — Utah Talons Win Game 2, Back-to-Back Champions

#### Long-Form Post — 5:30 PM ET

It's done. The Utah Talons are back-to-back AUSL champions.

Utah defeated the Chicago Bandits in Game 2 on Sunday afternoon at Davis Diamond, completing the sweep and becoming the first team in AUSL history to win back-to-back championships. The Talons also swept the Bandits in 2025 to win the inaugural AUSL title.

This is what the start of a dynasty looks like.

Georgina Corrick won Game 1 on Saturday — a complete-game gem, 2-1, retiring 15 of the final 16 batters she faced. Montana Fouts stepped onto the mound for Game 2 — carrying a Pitcher of the Year award, four AUSL single-season records, and the weight of a franchise moment.

The Bandits never had the lead. They went through an eleven-inning play-in game Thursday night against Portland Cascade. They came in on less rest, with a taxed bullpen, against the No. 1 seed in professional softball.

Two years. Two sweeps. Utah built something that nobody else in this league has come close to matching. They win with their pitching. They win with their defense — Hannah Flippen is the best defensive player in the sport. They win with a roster that was constructed to compete and has delivered every time the stakes were highest.

The AUSL enters its third season with one question that matters above everything else: can anyone stop the Utah Talons?

### STORY 2: Georgina Corrick — The Game 1 Gem That Started a Dynasty

#### Long-Form Post — 9:00 AM ET

Georgina Corrick was not the name everyone was watching. That was fine with her.

Montana Fouts had the Pitcher of the Year award. The four AUSL records. The single-game strikeout record. The narrative of the entire season. Fouts was the person the Utah Talons were built around — at least in the public's mind.

Corrick was the one who started Game 1 of the AUSL Championship Series on Saturday at Davis Diamond.

Final line: 4 hits. 1 earned run. 5 strikeouts. 0 walks. Complete game. She retired 15 of the final 16 batters she faced. Sahvanna Jaquish delivered the go-ahead sacrifice fly in the bottom of the 6th. Lexi Kilfoyl — the Bandits' ace, who went 6-0 this season and saved the play-in game from the bullpen — started and took the loss.

Talons 2, Bandits 1. Series lead in hand. Bullpen intact for Game 2.

That was the plan. Corrick executed it exactly.

This is the part of a championship that doesn't get celebrated the way it should. Fouts gets the award. Corrick set the table. They both go home as champions.

### STORY 3: Montana Fouts — 2026 AUSL Pitcher of the Year

#### Long-Form Post — 11:00 AM ET

Four AUSL single-season records. One award that covers all of them.

Montana Fouts is the 2026 AUSL Pitcher of the Year. She earned it by doing something no pitcher in the league has done in its two-year history — she dominated a full season across every measurable category at once.

The numbers: 7-4 record. 2.36 ERA. 61.1 innings pitched. 63 strikeouts. 7 wins. 7 complete games. All four of those last categories are new AUSL single-season records. She also holds the single-game strikeout record — 12, against the Texas Volts.

The Utah Talons, as a team, led the league in ERA (2.67), strikeouts (143), and complete games (16). They allowed the fewest home runs (17) and fewest earned runs (66) in the league. That starts at the front of the rotation. That starts with Fouts.

She started Game 2 of the AUSL Championship Series today on Sunday. Win or lose, her fingerprints are on everything the Talons have accomplished.

Two seasons into professional softball. The sport's best pitcher is still getting better.

### STORY 4: Transfer Portal — Alabama Softball Adds Three Marquee Pieces

#### Long-Form Post — 3:00 PM ET

Alabama softball used the transfer portal to build one of the most imposing rosters in the SEC.

Three additions. Three conferences. Three proven producers:

Jaysoni Beachum (3B, from Florida State) — 2026 First Team All-ACC. .406 batting average. 10 home runs. 69 RBI. She was one of the best third basemen in the country in 2026.

Tori Edwards (1B/UTIL, from LSU) — 12 home runs. 45 RBI. 1.019 OPS. Power from the left side, experience in the SEC's toughest lineup.

Karlee Ford (C, from Iowa State) — Second Team All-Big 12. .345 batting average. 14 doubles. 10 home runs. 49 RBI. Elite defensive catcher who can hit.

The 2026-27 transfer portal window opened June 8. Alabama made its moves. The question now is what they do with a lineup that looks like this heading into the season.

September 1 — when Division I coaches can make direct contact with the Class of 2028 for the first time — is 36 days away. In college softball, a program's transfer activity tells recruits as much as anything else about whether a staff is committed to winning. Alabama just sent a message.

### STORY 5: PGF Nationals Week 2 Begins

#### Long-Form Post — 8:00 PM ET

PGF Nationals isn't over — it's just starting the next chapter.

Week 2 begins today in Southern California. The 14U, 12U, and 10U divisions take center stage through August 2. This is the part of PGF Nationals where the youngest players in the country get their national stage — and where coaches from every D1 program are watching even harder than they were for the older age groups.

Before looking ahead, here's what Week 1 produced:

Over 300 programs from 34 states competed in Week 1. The 14U and younger divisions draw a similar field. This is the largest youth softball tournament in the country.

September 1 — when Division I coaches can directly contact the Class of 2028 — is 36 days away. Every player on every field this week is on somebody's list.
