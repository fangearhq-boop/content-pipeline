### STORY 2: North Carolina Wins 2026 LLSWS

**FB Long-Form:**
The 2026 Little League Softball World Series champion is North Carolina. 🏆

Johnston County Little League (Clayton, NC) defeated Kentucky 2-0 in the championship game at Stallings Stadium in Greenville. It took exactly one run — and one pitcher absolutely refusing to let anyone score.

Anna-Lynn Clark's tournament-closing stretch:

Friday (semifinal vs. Florida): 109 pitches. 11 strikeouts. Shutout.
Saturday (championship vs. Kentucky): 93 pitches. 6 strikeouts. 3 hits. Shutout.

Back-to-back shutouts on back-to-back days to close the tournament. That's not just good pitching — that's elite mental resilience under championship pressure.

Belle Cox drove in the winning run with an RBI single in the bottom of the 2nd inning — the same spot she opened scoring the game before. Routines matter, even in a championship setting.

Congratulations to Johnston County, to Anna-Lynn Clark, to Belle Cox, and to the entire North Carolina squad. This is what softball looks like when everything clicks. 🥎

### STORY 3: September 1 — 16 Days

**FB Long-Form:**
Sixteen days. That's what stands between the Class of 2028 and the moment that changes everything.

September 1 is the first day D1 coaches can call, text, and arrange in-person meetings with Class of 2028 recruits. Right now — since June 15 — coaches have been limited to printed materials and certain electronic communications. No live phone calls. No direct conversations.

On September 1, all of that changes. The phones will ring.

For families navigating this process for the first time, the noise can be overwhelming. The key things to know:

• You don't have to commit on September 1. The window is opening, not closing.
• Coaches calling is not the same as a firm offer — evaluate the full picture.
• It's okay to tell a coach "I need time to think about this."
• The player makes the decision, not the pressure.

The Class of 2028 has been waiting a long time for this. Sixteen more days.

### STORY 4: USA WNT — 27 Days

**FB Long-Form:**
Twenty-seven days until USA Softball opens the WBSC Women's Softball World Cup Group Stage in Oklahoma City.

And here's what makes this one feel different: four of the 16 athletes on the USA roster — Alyssa Brito, Jayda Coleman, Tiare Jennings, and Kelly Maxwell — all played their college softball under Patty Gasso at Oklahoma.

They're going back to OKC. Different uniforms, same city, same drive.

The group stage runs September 12-16 at Devon Park. USA will face Netherlands (No. 6), Mexico (No. 7), China (No. 8), Brazil, and New Zealand. The top two teams in the group advance to Brisbane, Australia for the World Cup final rounds in April 2027.

OU leads all schools with four players on the roster. UCLA has three. Kentucky and Tennessee each have two.

That Gasso pipeline isn't just about national championships — it's about building players who represent the sport at its highest level.

27 days. Who are you watching?

### STORY 5: Texas Longhorns — Staff Complete

**FB Long-Form:**
Mike White has his staff. And the 2027 Texas softball season hasn't started yet.

The Longhorns — back-to-back national champions in 2025 and 2026 — are adding Rick Fremin as Associate Head Coach and Amber Freeman as hitting coach as they chase a historic three-peat.

Fremin is not a typical assistant. He spent 10 years as the head coach at Southeastern Louisiana, with 21 years of head coaching experience total before stepping into the AHC role at Texas. That's a different kind of presence in the staff room — a coach who has run a program and knows what that pressure feels like.

Freeman comes from Arizona, where the Wildcats went 85-31 in her two seasons coaching hitting and working with catchers.

The departures of Ehren Earleywine and Kristen Zaleski left real voids. Fremin and Freeman are the answers.

And then there's Hailey Kavan — entering her senior year as the first two-time WCWS Most Outstanding Player in tournament history. No one has ever won consecutive WCWS MOPs. She did.

Only UCLA (1988-1990) and Oklahoma (2021-2023) have ever won three consecutive national titles. Texas is trying to join that group.

The staff is assembled. The mission is clear.
