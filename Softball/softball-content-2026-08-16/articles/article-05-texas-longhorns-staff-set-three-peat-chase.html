Texas Softball's Staff Is Complete — Now the Three-Peat Chase Can Begin

By Emily Rawlings | I Love Softball | August 16, 2026

Mike White has his staff. Rick Fremin is the new Associate Head Coach. Amber Freeman is the new hitting coach. The departures of Ehren Earleywine and Kristen Zaleski have been answered. And now, the University of Texas softball program turns its full attention to the question that has been building since the final out of the 2026 Women's College World Series: can they win three in a row?

Only two programs in the history of the sport have accomplished that. UCLA won consecutive national championships in 1988, 1989, and 1990. Oklahoma did it in 2021, 2022, and 2023. Texas is trying to join that list. The 2027 season is when that attempt begins in earnest.

What Fremin Brings

Rick Fremin is not a typical assistant coach hire. He comes to Texas having spent 21 years as a head coach, the last 10 leading Southeastern Louisiana. When a program like Texas adds a coach with that much head coaching experience at the associate head coach level, the message is intentional: this staff is built to handle pressure, and to think like a coaching staff that has been through the grind of running a program.

Fremin's arrival matters not just for what he knows about softball, but for what he knows about managing people and moments. In a program chasing history, having an AHC who has been a head coach before is a different kind of resource than a staff built entirely of coordinators and assistants who have never had to make the final call.

Freeman's Impact in the Batter's Box

Amber Freeman joins from Arizona, where she served as the hitting coach and also worked with the catchers. Her Arizona teams went 85-31 during her two seasons, and the offensive profile she built there was what attracted Texas's attention. In a program where maintaining elite offensive output year over year is a prerequisite for contending, adding a hitting coach with that track record is a priority move.

Freeman also takes on the defensive workload with the catchers — a dual-role responsibility that will be important as Texas evaluates its depth at a position that requires both offensive production and defensive leadership.

Kavan's Senior Year

Into all of this context steps Hailey Kavan, entering what is already one of the most anticipated senior seasons in recent college softball history. Kavan is the only player ever to win back-to-back WCWS Most Outstanding Player awards. She claimed the honor in 2025 and again in 2026, and there is no comparable standard in the tournament's history for what she has already done.

Her senior year is the centerpiece of the 2027 Texas story. If the Longhorns win a third consecutive national title, Kavan will almost certainly be at the center of it. If they fall short, her legacy as a two-time MOP on consecutive champions still puts her in the conversation with any pitcher in the sport's history.

What's Next

Texas will open fall practice in the coming weeks as the roster and staff finalize preparations for the 2027 season. Watch for preseason rankings to begin taking shape in the fall, with the Longhorns expected to be a top-five program heading into what would be a historic three-peat bid.
