Tennessee's Coaching Search: Who Replaces Snider and Sanders?

By Jordan Reeves | October 11, 2026 | I Love Softball

Tennessee softball is searching for new coaches. Hitting coach Craig Snider and assistant Stephanie Sanders are both departing after the 2026 season, according to D1Softball's coaching carousel. As of Sunday, no replacements have been announced by head coach Karen Weekly or the Tennessee athletics department.

The departures come at a complicated time. Tennessee has reached the Women's College World Series in back-to-back seasons — 2025 and 2026 — and is widely regarded as one of the elite programs in college softball. Maintaining that standard requires not just players but coaches, and rebuilding a staff mid-cycle adds pressure to an offseason that is already complicated by the ongoing search for roster reinforcements.

Snider's Background

Snider is not just any assistant coach. He served as Texas Tech's head coach for two seasons before Karen Weekly brought him to Knoxville in June 2024 as the program's hitting coach. A former head coach handling a position staff role is a significant piece — someone who understands both the technical side of hitting and the broader demands of running a program. His work helped a Tennessee offense featuring Emma Clarke, who hit 14 home runs as a freshman in 2026, along with Abbey Leach (13 HR) and Grace Leach (10 HR).

The Search Ahead

The coaching marketplace moves quickly in October. Schools that lose assistants to other head-coaching jobs, or to programs willing to pay more, often find themselves scrambling to fill holes before the November early signing period. For Tennessee, the priorities are clear: the program needs a hitting coach with a track record of developing power hitters and a second assistant who can contribute to the daily practice environment at an elite level.

No names have publicly surfaced as candidates. The NFCA's transactions page and D1Softball's carousel will be the first places to look as the search progresses. Weekly has built Tennessee into a program that attracts coaching talent — the question is how quickly the right candidates emerge and whether the Lady Vols can close before rival programs lock up the best available coaches.

What's Next

Watch for Tennessee coaching announcements in the coming two to three weeks as the fall carousel accelerates. Programs typically want their full staffs in place before fall ball concludes and before the early signing period opens in November. For the Lady Vols, every day without a hire is a day that recruiting conversations are slightly more uncertain — and in college softball, uncertainty is a luxury top programs cannot afford.
