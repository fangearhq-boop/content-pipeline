Torres and Kavan Together at Last: Texas Fall Ball Begins the Championship Defense

By Sarah Mitchell | October 11, 2026 | I Love Softball

The defending national champions are back at work in Austin, and this fall they are doing something that did not happen during their championship run — they are practicing together for the first time. Isa Torres, the D1Softball Player of the Year who transferred from Florida State, and Teagan Kavan, a two-time Women's College World Series Most Outstanding Player, are now Longhorns together. Texas fall ball just got a whole lot more interesting.

Torres arrived in Austin after one of the most electrifying seasons any college softball player has ever had. At Florida State in 2026, she posted a .530 batting average, hit 16 home runs, and drove in 56 runs in 54 games. She also called Georgetown, Texas home — meaning when the Longhorns came calling, she was already a fan. Head coach Mike White now has to fit her into a lineup built around an existing championship core, and the question of whether she plays shortstop or second base remains open heading into the fall slate.

Kavan's Second WCWS MVP — and What Comes Next

Teagan Kavan accomplished something only a small group of pitchers can claim: back-to-back WCWS Most Outstanding Player awards. In June, she led Texas to its first national championship, cementing herself as the best pitcher in college softball at this moment. Now a junior, Kavan enters fall ball with the weight of expectation on her right arm — and no shortage of motivation.

Together, Torres and Kavan represent the spine of a Texas team that has real three-peat ambitions. Only two programs in NCAA softball history have won three consecutive national championships: UCLA from 1988 to 1990 and Oklahoma from 2021 to 2023. The Longhorns are a long way from joining that company, but fall ball is where the foundation gets built.

The Fall Schedule

Texas opened its fall slate on September 29 against Murray State. The Longhorns host Texas State on Tuesday, October 13, before adding games against Temple College, McLennan, and UTSA through the end of October. The marquee event on the fall calendar is a November 6 road trip to face Texas A&M — the kind of rivalry matchup that creates early-season moments the fan base will be talking about for months.

All home fall games at UT are free and open to the public. For fans in Austin who want an early look at the Torres–Kavan era, this fall is the opportunity.

What's Next

Texas hosts Texas State on Tuesday, October 13, in the next installment of fall ball at home. After that, the Longhorns continue building through October before the Texas A&M game on November 6 closes the fall slate. Every rep in the fall is part of the championship defense — and with Torres and Kavan finally in the same dugout, Texas enters 2027 as the team everyone else has to chase.
