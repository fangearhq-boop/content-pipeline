**Posting Window:** 11:00 AM ET
**Type:** Long-Form

The clock in Norman has two countdowns running simultaneously.

Four days until Patty Gasso, JT Gasso, and Jennifer Rocha head to Oklahoma City to coach Team USA in the WBSC Women's Softball World Cup Group Oklahoma. And 22 days until the Sooners' fall opener against Tarleton State at Love's Field on September 30.

That four-day window — the final stretch of fall camp with a complete coaching staff — is precious.

Macie Harter (.346 BA from MTSU) adds another dangerous bat to a lineup that already had enough weapons.

The Sooners have the talent to weather Gasso's 12-day absence. The question is what this final complete week of coaching imprints on them. In Norman, every rep of these four days counts.

### STORY 4: Texas Longhorns — FB Long-Form
**Posting Window:** 1:00 PM ET
**Type:** Long-Form

Twenty-one days.

That's how long Texas has until the Sept. 29 fall opener against Murray State at McCombs Field. And as Week 3 of fall camp winds down in Austin, what the Longhorns are building is becoming visible to anyone paying attention.

Isa Torres, the 2026 D1Softball National Player of the Year (.530 BA, 16 HR, 56 RBI at FSU), is entering her third week of practice in burnt orange. The adjustment period is over. Now it's about execution. About trust. About the kind of chemistry that only develops through daily reps over time.

Teagan Kavan, entering her senior year as a two-time WCWS Most Outstanding Player — the only player in the event's history to win the award back-to-back — doesn't need to learn how to be great. She's already proven it. What Week 3 is building is the version of Texas softball where Kavan and Torres become something greater together than either was apart.

Three-peat. It has only happened twice in WCWS history: UCLA (1988–1990) and Oklahoma (2021–2023). Texas is 21 days from the first step. The fall opener is free, public, and waiting.
