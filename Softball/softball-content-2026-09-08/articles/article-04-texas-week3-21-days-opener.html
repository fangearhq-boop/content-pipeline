Torres, Kavan, and 21 Days: Texas Softball's Three-Peat Blueprint Is Taking Shape

By Jordan Reeves | September 8, 2026

Texas Longhorns

Twenty-one days until the fall opener. Three weeks of practice behind them. And at McCombs Field in Austin, what Isa Torres and Teagan Kavan are building week by week is becoming something you can describe in concrete terms rather than aspirational ones.

Torres, the 2026 D1Softball National Player of the Year, arrived at Texas carrying numbers that don't need context: .530 batting average, 16 home runs, 56 RBIs at Florida State. She came home to Georgetown, Texas and drove to Austin to play for a program that already had everything except the offensive piece she represents. Week 3 of fall practice is where the adjustment period ends and the Texas version of Torres begins.

Kavan's Legacy in the Making

Teagan Kavan is the only player in Women's College World Series history to win the Most Outstanding Player award in consecutive seasons. Back-to-back. The first player ever. That is the standard she set over the past two seasons — and entering her senior year, the standard she carries into every practice.

The question for this Texas program was never whether Kavan was good enough to lead a three-peat charge. It was whether the roster around her had the depth to get back to Oklahoma City and win it again. Torres answers that question with a .530 batting average and a National Player of the Year trophy.

The Historical Weight of Three in a Row

Two programs in the history of college softball have won three consecutive Women's College World Series titles: UCLA (1988–1990) and Oklahoma (2021–2023). That is it. That is the entire list. Every other dynasty has stopped at two.

Texas is two seasons removed from back-to-back championships. The fall opener on September 29 — Murray State, McCombs Field, 6:00 PM CT, free and open to the public — is the first official step in the three-peat calendar. Twenty-one days from now, the Longhorns get their first look at what this season can become.

The historical company at the top of the three-peat list is rarefied. UCLA. Oklahoma. The Longhorns want to be next.

Week 3 Is Where Chemistry Becomes Visible

The first two weeks of fall practice are introductions. Players learn the system, the tempo, the expectations. By Week 3, those introductions are over. What you see in Week 3 is the team as it actually functions — not as it presents on paper.

What Texas is showing in Week 3 is a group that has absorbed the arrival of significant new talent without losing the identity that produced back-to-back titles. Torres is learning what it means to be a Longhorn. Kavan is learning what it means to have a partner at the top of the order. Both are discovering that the version of themselves that plays together might be better than either version that played apart.

What's Next

Texas opens its fall schedule September 29 vs. Murray State at McCombs Field, 6:00 PM CT. The game is free and open to the public. The fall schedule closes November 6 at Texas A&M. Kavan's senior season — and the three-peat bid — begins in 21 days.
