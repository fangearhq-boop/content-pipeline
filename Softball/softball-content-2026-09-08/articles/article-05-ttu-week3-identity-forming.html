Texas Tech Softball's Week 3: When Seven All-Americans Become One Team

By Sarah Mitchell | September 8, 2026

Texas Tech Red Raiders

The recruiting résumés at Texas Tech softball read like a highlight reel from three different All-American ceremonies. Jaysoni Beachum (.406 BA, 10 HR, 69 RBI — All-ACC First Team from Florida State). Kasidi Pickering (No. 2 transfer portal ranking, .384 career batting average, 50 home runs — from Oklahoma). Jayden Heavener (13-9, 2.81 ERA, 128 strikeouts — from LSU). Add in four more All-Americans scattered through the roster and you have seven decorated players who, until August, had never shared a dugout in any competitive setting.

Week 3 is where that changes. Or rather, Week 3 is where it has been changing — and the change is becoming visible.

Glasco's Year 2 Blueprint

Gerry Glasco arrived at Texas Tech to run exactly this kind of experiment. Before accepting the Lubbock head coaching position, he was the architect of LSU's WCWS runner-up season in 2026 — a program built around a similar philosophy of assembling elite individual talent and forging collective identity.

Year 1 was the foundation. Systems, culture, vocabulary. Year 2 is where the ceiling raises. And with the talent Glasco has assembled in Lubbock — specifically the additions of Beachum, Pickering, and Heavener from three of the sport's most successful programs — the ceiling entering 2027 is genuinely hard to measure.

The key challenge of any all-star roster is organizational. Elite players carry their own habits, their own expectations, their own read on what leadership looks like. Synthesizing that into a shared identity takes time — and takes coaching. Glasco is three weeks into that process, and by Week 3, the results are encouraging.

What the Lab Is Producing

Beachum brings a power-and-average combination that few college players can match. Her .406 batting average from FSU isn't a fluke — it reflects an elite approach at the plate combined with genuine gap power. Pickering adds home run punch (50 career at OU) and the competitive fire that comes from being the No. 2-ranked player in the transfer portal. And Heavener, the LSU left-hander, gives Glasco a proven ace who has faced top competition and delivered: 13-9, 2.81 ERA, 128 strikeouts in a season where LSU competed for the national title.

None of them needed to prove themselves to be recruited. But proving yourself to each other — that is the Week 3 work. And the early signals suggest the work is paying off.

What's Next

Texas Tech's fall schedule will be announced in the coming weeks. Glasco's program enters its second full fall camp with the most talent-dense roster in program history. The 2027 season will begin with the Red Raiders among the sport's most closely watched programs. What happens between now and then in fall practice determines how ready they are when it counts.
