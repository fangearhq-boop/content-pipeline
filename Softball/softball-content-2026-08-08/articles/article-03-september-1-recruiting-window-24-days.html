Twenty-Four Days: The Class of 2028 Countdown Reaches Its Final Stretch

By Jordan Reeves

August 8, 2026

September 1 is 24 days away. For coaches across NCAA Division I softball, that number means something specific: it is the date when the recruiting contact window for the Class of 2028 opens. On that day, a coach can pick up the phone and call a high school junior for the first time. Until then, they cannot initiate a single phone call, text message, or email to a prospective recruit in that class.

The rule is absolute. The window is not a gradual process. It opens all at once — at midnight on September 1, calls begin, and the race to reach the top prospects first becomes the competition that shapes recruiting classes for years.

Why This Class Has Generated Record Attention

Extra Inning Softball, the leading recruiting information service for the sport, has processed 1,226 nominations for Class of 2028 recognition — the highest number for any class in the company's history. The number reflects both the growth of the sport and the intensity of the recruiting conversation happening at every level of youth softball right now.

Class of 2028 prospects are current high school juniors in the fall of 2026. They have been playing travel softball for years, some since age 10, and many have already attended recruiting camps, unofficial visits, and tournaments specifically designed to put them in front of college coaches. The pre-contact relationship-building that precedes the September 1 window is legal and extensive. What is not legal, before September 1, is any direct initiation of contact by a D1 coach to a prospect in this class.

That restriction lifts in 24 days.

What September 1 Looks Like in Practice

Programs at every level of D1 softball — from national championship contenders to mid-major programs building toward a breakthrough — have built their Class of 2028 lists over the past two years. Coaches know which pitchers they want. They know which position players fit their system. They know which prospects have the academic profile, the geographic flexibility, and the competitive mindset to succeed at their program.

They have been watching. They have been attending tournaments. They have communicated through the indirect channels the NCAA allows before the contact window opens. And on September 1, all of that preparation turns into action simultaneously. A top prospect in this class could receive calls from a dozen programs within the first hour of September 1. The programs that reach the right person first, with the most compelling message, will shape which players commit earliest — and early commitments, increasingly, define the top of every recruiting class.

For Prospects and Families

The 24-day window before September 1 is also the last stretch of quiet for Class of 2028 recruits who have identified themselves as top targets. Travel schedules, unofficial visits, and any communication the recruit initiates (rather than the coach) are still permissible. What changes on September 1 is not just permission — it is volume. The landscape shifts entirely for the most sought-after players in this class on that single date.

What's Next

September 1 falls on a Tuesday this year, and the first wave of Class of 2028 early commitment announcements typically follows within days or weeks of the contact window opening. For coaches, the goal is to get in, make the case, and secure commitments before the competition does. For families, September 1 marks the beginning of the most active phase of the college recruiting process. The countdown is at 24 days.
