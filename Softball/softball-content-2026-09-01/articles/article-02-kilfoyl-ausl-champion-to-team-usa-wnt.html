From AUSL Champion to Team USA: Kilfoyl's Season Isn't Over

By Emily Rawlings

September 1, 2026

Less than 24 hours after being crowned the 2026 AUSL All-Star Cup individual champion, Lexi Kilfoyl's season has already moved to its next chapter.

In 11 days, Kilfoyl will be in Oklahoma City — not as the All-Star Cup's top finisher, but as a member of the 16-player USA Softball Women's National Team roster for the 2026 WBSC Women's Softball World Cup Group Stage. The event runs September 12-16 at Devon Park in Oklahoma City.

For Kilfoyl, the transition from the AUSL All-Star Cup to international play is a testament to what professional softball has become. The same players who compete against each other in the AUSL spend the off-window representing their country. The competition is relentless, and the best players don't get much of a break.

What She Just Did

The 2026 AUSL All-Star Cup crown wasn't easily won. Kilfoyl entered the Series 4 finale on August 30 trailing Baylee Klingler by 24 points in the individual standings — a gap that made the championship look out of reach entering the final game of the season.

What followed was one of the more compelling finishes in the four-year history of the AUSL All-Star Cup. Team Kilfoyl defeated Team Klingler 4-3, with Sami Williams delivering a clutch performance that carried the night. The individual points tallied in Kilfoyl's favor when the final out was recorded, and the comeback was complete.

Kilfoyl finished the 2026 All-Star Cup with three Game MVP awards — the most of any player in the league across the entire competition. That sustained excellence over four series was what made her an individual title contender even when the standings didn't reflect her closeness to the top.

AUSL to WNT: A Familiar Pipeline

Kilfoyl is far from alone in making this transition. The USA WNT roster for Group Oklahoma includes multiple players who competed in the AUSL All-Star Cup this summer. Valerie Garcia and Dejah Mulipola — two members of the 2020 U.S. Olympic Team — are among the roster's anchors. The pipeline between the AUSL and the national team is one of the clearest demonstrations of what the professional league has accomplished in terms of player development and competition level.

Head coach Patty Gasso, who also coaches at Oklahoma, will lead Team USA beginning September 12. The compressed schedule — from the final AUSL series to international competition in less than two weeks — reflects the density of the elite softball calendar at the end of the summer.

What's Next

Kilfoyl and Team USA open Group Oklahoma against No. 7 Mexico on September 12. The group also includes No. 6 Netherlands, No. 8 China, No. 23 New Zealand, and No. 36 South Africa. The top two teams from the six-team group advance to the World Cup Finals in Redcliffe, Queensland, Australia (April 5-11, 2027) — an event that serves as a direct qualifier for the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics. For Kilfoyl, the road from the AUSL All-Star Cup to a potential LA28 berth passes through Oklahoma City in the next 11 days.
