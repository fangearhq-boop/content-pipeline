28 Days Out: Texas 2027 Fall Opener Launches the Three-Peat Chase

By Emily Rawlings

September 1, 2026

In 28 days, Texas softball opens its fall season at McCombs Field. In 28 days, the three-peat chase officially begins.

The Longhorns take the field September 29 against Murray State (6 PM CT, free admission) as the back-to-back national champions and one of the most watched programs in college softball. Head coach Mike White's team has won the last two Women's College World Series. A third consecutive title would put Texas alongside the only two programs that have ever done it: UCLA, which won from 1988 to 1990, and Oklahoma, which won from 2021 to 2023.

Twenty-eight days from now, the first step toward that history takes place at McCombs Field against Murray State. It's just a fall opener. It also marks the start of something that hasn't been done in over three decades at the Power conference level.

What Returns

The foundation of the 2027 Texas roster is the group that just won two national championships. Six of the nine starters from the 2026 title team return, giving White a starting point that most programs would envy heading into a fall camp.

The anchor of the returning group is Teagan Kavan. The senior pitcher is the only two-time WCWS Most Outstanding Player in history — she won the award in 2025 and again in 2026, a distinction no player had achieved in the history of the tournament. Having Kavan back for her final season gives Texas a genuine ace around whom to build a championship run.

What Comes Next

White confirmed this fall that Texas will re-enter the transfer portal to fill gaps left by graduation losses. The current 2027 roster is already taking shape around the returning core, with new additions to the class announced in recent weeks. The fall schedule provides the coaching staff an extended look at the full group — starters, transfers, and freshmen — before the spring season begins.

Today is September 1 — the first day D1 coaches can contact Class of 2028 recruits. Texas, as a back-to-back national champion with a two-time WCWS MOP on the roster, is positioned to be in early conversations with the nation's top young players. The recruiting pitch writes itself.

The fall schedule closes November 6 at Texas A&M's Davis Diamond, giving the Longhorns a lengthy evaluation window before the 2027 campaign begins in earnest.

The Historical Context

Three consecutive national championships in college softball is not merely difficult — it is historically rare. Of the dozens of programs that have won the Women's College World Series since the tournament's modern era, only UCLA and Oklahoma have ever managed back-to-back-to-back titles. The gap between Oklahoma's third consecutive championship in 2023 and today is three years. Before Texas can get there, the Longhorns have to navigate a full 2027 season against a field that will be studying their roster and scheme all spring.

That journey starts in 28 days. Murray State at McCombs Field. Free admission. Kavan on the mound. The three-peat chase is on.
