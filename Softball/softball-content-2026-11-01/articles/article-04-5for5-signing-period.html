NCAA 5-for-5 Litigation Hits 26 Cases — With Early Signing Period 12 Days Away

By Sarah Mitchell | November 1, 2026 | I Love Softball

The NCAA's 5-for-5 eligibility rule — which requires athletes to complete five years of eligibility within five calendar years — has generated a legal battle unlike almost anything in college sports history. As of an Associated Press report on October 16, at least 26 separate cases have been filed in state and county courts across the country, each seeking to push back against a rule that the NCAA adopted in June 2026. With softball's early signing period opening on November 13 — twelve days away — programs and recruits are being forced into major decisions without legal clarity.

Where the Federal Case Stands

The federal case is anchored by Wisne v. NCAA, which produced a district court preliminary injunction on July 31, 2026, blocking the 5-for-5 rule from taking effect. The NCAA appealed to the Tenth Circuit Court of Appeals, which issued a 2-1 stay of that injunction on August 21 — meaning the rule was reinstated while the appeal is heard in full.

Oral arguments were held on October 13. The Tenth Circuit has not issued a merits ruling as of November 1. The stay remains in place, and under the current state of the law, the 5-for-5 rule is in effect: athletes whose eligibility clock has expired cannot use a fifth year.

The State Court Patchwork

While the federal case works through the appeals process, a parallel system of state court litigation has created a patchwork of outcomes. A McLennan County district court in Texas already enabled 39 named plaintiffs to regain their eligibility and access to the transfer portal, per October reporting. That result applies to those specific athletes — not to the broader Class of 2022 population that the federal case sought to protect.

The AP reported 26 cases filed as of October 16, and the number is likely higher now. Each case represents a different jurisdiction, different legal arguments, and different plaintiffs — meaning the effective rules vary based on where a player and their institution are located. For an NCAA that has long sought uniform national standards, this is exactly the kind of outcome that most threatened the rule's implementation from the start.

The Signing Period Problem

Softball's early signing period opens November 13. For 2027 recruits — players who are currently juniors in high school and have been making their college decisions throughout this fall — the uncertainty created by the 5-for-5 litigation has direct consequences.

Programs that expected to have scholarship space tied up by fifth-year players suddenly discovered that space might become available, or might not, depending on what a court decides in the next two weeks. Recruits who committed to a program expecting a certain roster composition now face the possibility that the arithmetic changes. And coaches who need to make offers before November 13 are doing so without knowing exactly how many scholarships they have to work with.

Several programs reportedly pulled offers from 2027 recruits earlier in the fall, citing the scholarship uncertainty created by the eligibility rule's unpredictable status. That is an extraordinary step — pulling an offer is a significant relationship decision — but it reflects the genuine complexity of roster planning in this environment.

What's Next

The Tenth Circuit could issue a ruling before the November 13 early signing period, though there is no guarantee of timing on court decisions. Programs that have adapted to operate under uncertainty — planning parallel rosters for both outcomes — are best positioned to navigate what comes next. For recruits signing on November 13, the best advice is to sign with the program and coaching staff that fit best, regardless of the eligibility litigation. What happens in court will happen; the relationships and fit that define a college softball experience should drive the decision.
