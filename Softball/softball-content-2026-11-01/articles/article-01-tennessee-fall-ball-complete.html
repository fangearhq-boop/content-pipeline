Tennessee Softball Closes Fall Ball — What the Lady Vols Showed Us Before Winter

By Sarah Mitchell | November 1, 2026 | I Love Softball

Tennessee softball has finished its 2026 fall schedule against outside competition, and the Lady Vols spent the final day of October and the first of November doing exactly what Karen Weekly's programs have always done — answering questions on the mound. A 14-1 win over Lee University in the fall finale closed the books on a fall slate that clarified the most important storyline heading into 2027: the post-Karlyn Pickens era does not require a stepdown in expectations.

The Fall Finale Said Everything

Beating Lee University 14-1 is not the kind of result that generates national headlines, but the details mattered. Freshmen Elsa Morrison and Emma Clarke both hit home runs in the game, adding power depth to a roster that had already shown strong returning production at the plate. Freshman Bailee Shelton continued the consistent fall that made Weekly's staff optimistic about the offense beyond the veterans. These were not marginal contributors — they were players putting themselves into the 2027 lineup conversation.

Only private intersquad sessions remain before the winter shutdown. Weekly confirmed that the fall's outside competition is complete: "We're going to intersquad a couple of times. That'll be our last official night of fall ball." When Tennessee goes dark for winter, it does so with a clear picture of what this roster can do.

The Mardjetko-Nuwer Question

Weekly has been candid throughout the fall about where the 2027 Lady Vols have work to do. She named the pitching staff as "the biggest question mark of the fall — but one I feel good about," and the nuance in that phrasing matters. She is not worried. She is realistic about what it takes to replace a pitcher of Karlyn Pickens' caliber.

Pickens finished her Tennessee career with 194 strikeouts and a 1.70 ERA in 2026, was named the No. 1 pick in the AUSL draft, and departed as one of the most decorated pitchers in program history. She does not return for 2027.

In her place stands Sage Mardjetko, who is not a placeholder. The incoming senior went 16-3 with a 1.26 ERA in 2026 and earned NFCA First Team All-America honors — numbers that would be a top-of-the-rotation standard at nearly any program in the country. Erin Nuwer, who threw four shutouts as a freshman last season, returns as a junior with game experience most sophomore pitchers cannot claim.

The question is not whether Tennessee has pitching. It does. The question is whether this staff can operate under SEC-level pressure with the same consistency Pickens provided. Weekly's fall assessment suggests she believes the answer is yes.

Depth at the Plate

If the pitching narrative is about managing the Pickens departure, the offense narrative is about adding to what was already a capable lineup. Morrison and Clarke showing home-run power in the fall finale is the kind of freshman contribution that turns a competitive team into a dangerous one. Shelton's consistency as a contact bat provides a different look, and her fall production earned consistent mentions from the Tennessee staff.

The returning veteran core, paired with this freshman class — ranked No. 5 nationally by D1Softball and No. 6 by Softball America — gives the Lady Vols multiple lineup combinations to deploy against different pitching matchups.

What's Next

Tennessee softball will complete intersquad sessions before shutting down for winter. The 2027 spring schedule will open in February, with the SEC portion the true measure of where this team stands nationally. For now, what the fall provided is clarity: this program is not rebuilding. It is reloading, with an ace in Mardjetko, a deep pitching staff, power at the plate from its incoming freshmen, and a head coach who has won at this level for two decades. The 2027 Lady Vols are not sneaking up on anyone.
