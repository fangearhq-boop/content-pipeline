NCAA Softball Recruiting Blackout Begins Today: What It Means for Programs and Prospects

By Sarah Mitchell | November 25, 2026 | I Love Softball

Starting today and running through November 29, every Division I softball coach in the country just went silent.

The NCAA's annual recruiting blackout period — a five-day stretch during which coaches cannot call, text, email, or make any contact of any kind with recruits — is in effect. No visits. No phone calls. No DMs. Zero contact with any prospect in any class year. The phones have stopped ringing, and they will not ring again until November 30.

Yesterday Was the Last Day

November 24 was the final day of unrestricted contact — the last opportunity for coaches to follow up on official visits, check in with uncommitted prospects, and make a final push before the freeze. Programs that have been hosting top targets in recent weeks burned every last hour of contact time before midnight.

For programs still chasing commitments in their 2027 high school classes or laying the groundwork for December portal moves, the next 24 hours are going to feel very long.

What Is Restricted

During a recruiting shutdown, the prohibition is total. NCAA Division I bylaws bar coaches from initiating or accepting contacts, evaluations, campus visits, unofficial visits, and all forms of correspondence, including telephone calls. High school coaches and players can still talk with one another, but the moment it involves a college coaching staff, the conversation must wait.

The shutdown applies to all prospects — not just the class of 2027. Coaches cannot contact currently enrolled transfers or players in the portal during this window, complicating December portal strategy for programs trying to identify and build relationships with transfer targets before the December 1 open date.

The Calendar After the Blackout

The sequence that follows makes the next two weeks one of the most significant stretches of the entire recruiting calendar:

November 30: Unrestricted contact resumes. Coaches have one day to reach back out to uncommitted recruits and re-engage with transfer targets before the portal opens.

December 1: The transfer portal window opens (through December 15). Players can formally enter the portal, triggering official contact from programs. This is when the market moves.

December 8-12: A dead period restricts in-person recruiting and official visits, compressing the effective recruiting window inside the portal period itself.

December 15: The portal window closes. Programs that have not secured their targets must wait until the summer window.

What to Watch During the Blackout

Recruits are free to communicate with one another and can still announce commitments through social media during the blackout — coaches just cannot initiate those announcements. Programs watching for high school prospects to self-report visits or commitment decisions may still see activity on the recruiting trail even as coaches stay silent on their end.

For transfer portal strategy, programs like Arizona — which is in urgent need of a pitching ace after losing D1Softball Player of the Year Mariah Torres to Texas Tech — are waiting with their phones ready for November 30.

What's Next

The blackout lifts November 30. Coaches can resume all contact with recruits, and December 1 marks the official start of the transfer portal window. Oklahoma State pitcher Saylor Davis, who has three years of eligibility remaining, is expected to be among the first significant names into the portal when it opens. For programs still building their 2027 rosters, the December window is the last significant roster-reshaping opportunity before spring practice begins.
