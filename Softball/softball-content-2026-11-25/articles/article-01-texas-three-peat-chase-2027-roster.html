Texas Chasing Historic 3-Peat: How the Longhorns Are Building a 2027 Dynasty Roster

By Jordan Reeves | November 25, 2026 | I Love Softball

Two national championships in a row. It is not supposed to be this easy.

Texas softball won the 2025 Women's College World Series for the program's first national title. Then they came back and did it again in 2026, cementing themselves as the sport's dominant force. Now, with the offseason in full swing, the Longhorns are doing something that would make dynasties nervous: they are building the roster to win it three straight.

No program in the modern Division I era has won three consecutive national championships. Texas is trying.

The Returners: Kavan, Stewart, and a Senior Foundation

The backbone of the 2027 Texas roster returns. Teagan Kavan and Katie Stewart, two of the most decorated players in program history, are both expected back for their senior seasons. Their experience leading the Longhorns through consecutive championship runs gives Texas a continuity advantage that money cannot buy.

That said, the losses are real. Kaiah Altmeyer, a hero in multiple WCWS games, is gone — leaving second base open. Stewart is also the only returning catcher with experience behind the plate, putting additional pressure on the incoming class to provide depth.

Portal Reinforcements

Coach Mike White and his staff have been active in the transfer portal. Texas secured catcher Samantha Swan from Arizona State, bringing immediate depth and competition at a position of need. Pitcher Samantha Lincoln arrives from Texas Tech, adding to a rotation that must stay elite in the SEC. Infielder Isa Torres, formerly of Florida State, fills the gap left by Altmeyer at the infield corners.

These are not filler additions. They are starting-caliber players joining a defending champion — a signal that elite transfer targets want to come to Texas because Texas wins.

The Class of 2027

Texas's high school signing class addresses the program's most pressing needs. Catcher Lolo Gamble and outfielder Emree Massaad headline the position players. On the mound, the Longhorns signed pitcher Brooklynn Hagerty and, perhaps most excitingly, Madi Doty — described widely as one of the top pitching prospects in the nation entering the 2027 class.

Doty alone gives Texas a future ace to build around beyond this season. Combined with Lincoln's transfer arrival, the rotation depth is real.

The Challenge of Repeating — and Then Some

Winning back-to-back titles is extraordinary. Winning three in a row requires not just talent but a program culture that sustains excellence through roster turnover, heightened expectations, and a sport where every opponent has circled you on the schedule.

Texas has the talent. They have the culture. They have the transfers and the recruits to reload. What they cannot know yet is whether the baseball gods — or in this case the softball gods — will cooperate when it matters in June.

Official preseason polls will not drop until January 2027. But there is little doubt where the Longhorns will land when they do.

What's Next

Texas has until November 30 before contact with remaining recruits resumes following the NCAA recruiting blackout (Nov 25-29). The December transfer portal window (Dec 1-15) offers additional roster-building opportunities. Spring preseason practice typically begins in mid-January, with the 2027 SEC schedule kicking off in mid-March. The Women's College World Series returns to Oklahoma City in June.

Three-peat attempts do not come along often. For softball fans — in Texas or anywhere else — this is a season to watch from Day 1.
