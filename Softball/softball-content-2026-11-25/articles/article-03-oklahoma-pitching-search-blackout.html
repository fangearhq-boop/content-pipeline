Oklahoma Has the No. 1 Class in the Country. Now the Sooners Need a Pitcher.

By Emily Rawlings | November 25, 2026 | I Love Softball

When it comes to the 2027 class, Oklahoma has built something truly impressive. Julianna "Goose" Hutchens — the No. 1 overall recruit nationally per Softball America — is already enrolled in Norman. Finlee Williams, the top-rated outfielder in the class, is committed. No. 2 catcher Riley Hilliard is committed. Four-star shortstop Emma Reynolds is committed.

By almost every metric, the Sooners have assembled the No. 1 recruiting class in the country. And yet, conversations among the softball faithful keep circling back to the same question: where is the pitcher?

What Patty Gasso Built — and What She Still Needs

Gasso has built Oklahoma into a perennial national championship contender. The Sooners' offense is elite. With Hutchens and Williams anchoring the class, and Brooke Wells returning (the 2026 National Freshman of the Year with 39 home runs), the lineup has the kind of firepower that wins WCWS championships.

The pitching picture is less settled. Oklahoma's current class has the hitting to outscore almost anyone. But in a seven-game WCWS run, elite pitching is what separates national champions from teams that flame out in the semifinals.

Hosting Before the Blackout

Reports indicate that Oklahoma hosted pitcher Vivian Knott and held a visit with Aspen Boulware — widely considered a top-ranked national recruit — in the days before today's recruiting shutdown went into effect. Neither has announced a commitment to Oklahoma, and the blackout (November 25-29) means no contact is permitted until November 30 at the earliest.

That timing matters. Any commitment announcements during the blackout would come at a prospect's initiative, not from continued recruiting pressure by coaches. The Sooners have made their pitch. Now they wait.

The December Portal as a Parallel Option

High school pitchers are not the only path. The December transfer portal window (Dec 1-15) opens in six days and brings with it a fresh pool of experienced arms. Oklahoma State pitcher Saylor Davis — who has three years of eligibility remaining — is expected to enter the portal on Day 1. Davis would immediately address the Sooners' most glaring need.

Oklahoma is not the only program in the market. Arizona, which lost D1Softball Player of the Year Mariah Torres to Texas Tech last summer, is aggressively hunting pitching. Ole Miss and Auburn are also competing for the same names. The portal window moves quickly.

What's Next

The recruiting blackout lifts November 30, giving Oklahoma coaches one day to re-engage with high school targets before December 1. The transfer portal window opens that same morning and runs through December 15. Patty Gasso and her staff will have multiple fronts to work simultaneously: high school pitching prospects and portal arms competing for the same roster spot. If Oklahoma can find its ace — in either market — the No. 1 class becomes a genuine 3-peat threat alongside Texas. If the pitching search comes up short, the storyline writes itself.
