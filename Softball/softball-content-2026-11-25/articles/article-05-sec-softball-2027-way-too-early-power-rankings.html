Way-Too-Early 2027 SEC Softball Power Rankings: Who Runs the Nation's Toughest Conference?

By Sarah Mitchell | November 25, 2026 | I Love Softball

The 2026 season ended months ago. The portal has not opened yet. Official preseason polls will not drop until January. And yet, the softball world is already asking the question that matters most heading into next year: who runs the SEC in 2027?

Early power rankings from Yahoo Sports offer a first look at the SEC's pecking order, with some familiar names at the top — and one very interesting new entrant.

No. 2 Alabama — The Crimson Tide Target Their Own Three-Peat of WCWS Runs

Patrick Murphy returns a team that reached the WCWS semifinals in 2026 and brings back two of its most dangerous pieces in pitcher Jocelyn Briski and outfielder Brooke Wells. Alabama has been a consistent top-five program in the Murphy era, and nothing about their 2027 roster suggests that changes. Early rankings have Alabama sitting at No. 2 in the SEC — a position they will defend with an experienced core.

No. 3 Oklahoma — The New SEC School With the No. 1 Class

In their first full year as an SEC member, the Sooners are already projected as a top-three conference team. Oklahoma's No. 1-ranked 2027 recruiting class — led by enrolled star Julianna "Goose" Hutchens and three additional elite commits — provides instant credibility. Coach Patty Gasso has built one of the sport's enduring dynasties. Early projections have her program No. 3 in a loaded conference before the first pitch of 2027. The only question is pitching.

No. 4 Tennessee — The Deep Rotation and Nine Returning Starters

Perhaps the most underrated team in these early rankings is Tennessee. The Lady Vols return nine starters from a 2026 squad that went 49-12 — an extraordinary number in the portal era. NFCA First Team All-American Sage Mardjetko (16-3, 1.26 ERA, 171 strikeouts) returns to the circle. Two first-team All-SEC honorees anchor the lineup, including Alannah Leach. Coach Karen Weekly extended through 2030 in a signal of program stability.

Tennessee is ranked No. 4 in this early SEC preview, but that number undersells how dangerous a returning nine-starter lineup with an ace pitcher can be. If Mardjetko replicates her 2026 form, the Lady Vols will push for a conference title.

No. 5 Florida — Annual Contender Reloading After Super Regional Exit

The Gators fell at home to eventual national runner-up Texas Tech in their Super Regional. Florida returns to the portal and recruiting markets as it always does — with resources and a tradition that attracts elite talent. Watch for the Gators to be aggressive in December to address pitching depth.

No. 9 LSU — The No. 1 Class That Has Something to Prove

Beth Torina signed the No. 1 recruiting class in LSU history on November 11 — a class that includes five-star pitcher Sydney Gonglick, five-star catcher Jewel Cooper, and multiple four-star prospects. The Tigers also added portal transfers including Mackenzie and Madison Pickens and Notre Dame's Michey Winchell. Despite this, early power rankings have LSU sitting outside the top five at No. 9 in the SEC.

The gap between the No. 1 recruiting class and a No. 9 power ranking tells a story about roster uncertainty at this stage of the offseason. With January preseason polls fast approaching, LSU will have every opportunity to prove the rankings wrong.

What's Next

These early rankings are a conversation starter, not a verdict. Official preseason polls from D1Softball, Softball America, and the coaches' poll will begin dropping in January, giving the first real consensus on 2027 projections. Between now and then, the December transfer portal window (Dec 1-15) will reshape rosters significantly. Any team that lands Saylor Davis or another elite arm will see its ranking move. The 2027 SEC title race is wide open — and it starts right now.
