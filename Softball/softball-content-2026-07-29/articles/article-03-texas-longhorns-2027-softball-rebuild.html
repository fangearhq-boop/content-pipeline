Texas Softball's 2027 Makeover: How One Transfer Window Changed Everything

By Jordan Reeves | July 29, 2026

Isa Torres headlines a massive Texas portal class — and the flip side of the coin is a Florida State program staring at its most challenging rebuild in years.

When Isa Torres posted her commitment photo in burnt orange and wrote "I'm coming home," it wasn't just a player announcement. It was a declaration that Texas softball is coming for everyone.

Torres, the 2026 D1Softball National Player of the Year, hit .530 at Florida State this past season — an FSU single-season record. She scored 78 runs (another FSU record), posted a .591 on-base percentage (FSU record), a .978 slugging percentage (FSU record), and set the NCAA record for consecutive hits at 16. She won the ACC Player of the Year, the ACC Defensive Player of the Year, and was a unanimous First Team All-American. Now she's heading to coach Mike White's program in Austin with one year of eligibility remaining.

Torres is a Georgetown, Texas native. She is, quite literally, coming home.

Texas Didn't Stop at Torres

Coach White used the June portal window to reshape his roster at multiple positions. Texas added catcher Samantha Swan from Arizona State and pitcher Samantha Lincoln from Texas Tech alongside Torres.

The combination gives the Longhorns their strongest transfer class in recent memory. Torres at shortstop — arguably the game's most important defensive position — anchors an infield that suddenly looks capable of competing at the national level. Lincoln adds pitching depth that can change the dynamic of a weekend series. Swan brings experience behind the plate from a Power Four program.

September 1 is 33 days away — the next NCAA recruiting portal window. If Texas isn't already in the conversation for top programs nationally, they will be by the time that window closes.

The Other Side: FSU's Daunting Rebuild

For every team that gains in the transfer portal, another faces the math on the other side. For Florida State, the math is brutal.

Torres and Jaysoni Beachum — who transferred to Texas Tech — together accounted for 28 percent of FSU's runs, 29 percent of hits, 35 percent of home runs, 30 percent of RBI, and 32 percent of walks in 2026. That's not depth leaving. That's a program's identity walking out the door.

Head coach Lonni Alameda also lost Mimi Gooden and Addie DeLong to the portal, making the 2026 offseason one of the most disruptive in recent FSU history. The context is even more difficult given how 2026 ended — FSU lost at home in regionals to Stetson and UCF, a stinging exit that accelerated the portal departures.

A Program at a Crossroads

FSU softball has been one of the most consistent programs in the country under Alameda's tenure. The Seminoles have been a regular NCAA Tournament presence, and Tallahassee has produced All-Americans at every position. But losing your best hitter (.530 BA), your second-best hitter (Beachum, .406 BA at FSU before transferring), your catcher, and your leadoff contributor in the same window is a generational setback — regardless of how good your program has been.

Alameda is a proven builder. She will find answers in the fall. But this is a rebuild, not a reload — and everyone in college softball knows it.

What's Next

The next NCAA transfer portal window opens September 1 — just 33 days away. That window will determine whether Texas adds to its class, whether FSU can bring in enough talent to stabilize, and which other programs make major moves before 2027 practice begins. For Longhorns fans, the future looks very bright. For FSU, the work starts now.
