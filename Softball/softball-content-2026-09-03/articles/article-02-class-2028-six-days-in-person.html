Six Days: What the Class of 2028's In-Person Contact Window Means for Recruits

By Emily Rawlings

September 3, 2026

The phones have been ringing since Sept. 1. Now comes the next escalation.

Six days from today — on Sept. 9 at 7:00 AM ET — the in-person contact window opens for the Class of 2028. College coaches will be allowed to visit recruits at their schools and homes, transforming what has been a wave of calls and messages into face-to-face conversations that can change the trajectory of a young athlete's future.

For the 1,226 prospects nominated in Extra Inning Softball's record-setting Class of 2028 rankings, the in-person window is the moment a recruiter's interest becomes something tangible. It's one thing to get a phone call. It's another to look a coach in the eye.

The Two-Phase System and What Sept. 9 Unlocks

Under the current NCAA recruiting calendar, the Class of 2028 entered its first official phase on Sept. 1, when coaches gained the ability to call, email, and direct-message prospects. That window brought a flood of first contact from programs across the country as coaches began working through their priority lists.

Sept. 9 at 7 AM ET is different. The in-person contact window allows coaches to visit a prospect's school during official school hours — watching film, meeting with families, and sitting across the table from the student-athletes themselves. Under the NCAA's 5-5 recruiting rules, this type of direct engagement is limited to a recruit's junior and senior years, making September of junior year the first opportunity coaches have had since these athletes were in middle school.

For elite prospects, the in-person visit often functions as a closing conversation — a final push that separates an offer from every other program sending texts and making calls. The programs that show up first, and show up in person, frequently have a recruiting edge.

A Deep and Talented Class

The Class of 2028 earned its record nomination count for good reason. Evaluators point to a class headlined by elite middle infielders and catchers — positions where depth often separates good programs from great ones. Top prospects include Michaela Landers, Kylie Holden, and Sophia Schlader, while a deep pitching pool gives coaches several directions to pursue.

Programs with limited roster space will have to be selective. The in-person window is where that selectivity becomes visible — the coaches who show up are telling recruits exactly where they stand.

What's Next

The in-person contact window opens Sept. 9 at 7:00 AM ET for all Class of 2028 prospects. Recruits should prepare by having their film updated, their academic profile ready to discuss, and a clear sense of what they're looking for in a program. The weeks following Sept. 9 will bring the first wave of official in-person visits — and likely, the first verbal commits from the class as programs work to lock in their top targets. Keep an eye on Extra Inning Softball, Softball America, and On3 for early commit announcements.
