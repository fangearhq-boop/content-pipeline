Seven All-Americans, One Question: Can Texas Tech's Chemistry Match Its Talent?

By Emily Rawlings

September 3, 2026

On paper, Texas Tech may have the most talented roster in college softball heading into 2027. That's not a debate — it's arithmetic.

Count the All-Americans practicing at Texas Tech this fall: Jaysoni Beachum. Kasidi Pickering. Jayden Heavener. Terry. Williams. Pannell. Burns. That's seven players who earned All-American honors at their previous programs, all in Lubbock at the same time, all putting on Red Raiders gear for the first time. Second-year head coach Gerry Glasco assembled this group piece by piece through the transfer portal after a 2026 season that saw Texas Tech reach the WCWS Finals.

Talent was never going to be the problem. Chemistry is the question fall camp was designed to answer.

The New Arrivals

The headliner of the transfer class is Jaysoni Beachum, who arrives from Florida State having batted .406 with 10 home runs and 69 RBIs in 2026, earning All-ACC First Team honors and NFCA First Team All-Region recognition. Over her career, Beachum has hit .375 with 37 home runs and 180 RBIs — numbers that made her one of the most coveted players in the transfer portal.

Alongside her is Kasidi Pickering, rated the No. 2 transfer in the portal by Softball America. The former Oklahoma Sooner brings a .384 career batting average, 50 career home runs, and 169 career RBIs — and a 2024 national championship ring from her time in Norman. She's the kind of veteran presence that teams win titles with.

On the mound, Jayden Heavener gives Glasco a proven left-handed arm from LSU, where she went 13-9 with a 2.81 ERA in 2026. Additional transfers Moriah Polar (Purdue), Bradi Gallaway (Louisiana Tech, .317 BA, 11 HR), and Hailey Maestretti (Utah) fill out a roster that already existed at a high level.

The Chemistry Question

Every player in this group has been a star somewhere else. Every one of them is accustomed to being the player their team builds around. The task of fall camp is not developing talent — it's reorganizing egos, building trust, and finding the right roles for players who haven't shared a dugout before.

Glasco, in his second year at Texas Tech, has been through this kind of roster assembly once before. He knows what the early weeks of practice look like when talented players are still figuring each other out. He also knows that programs don't become champions from rosters — they become champions from teams.

The difference between the 2026 Texas Tech squad that reached the WCWS Finals and the 2027 version is experience. Players like Pickering who've already won a national championship know exactly what it takes to get there. That experience could accelerate the chemistry timeline.

What's Next

Texas Tech's fall camp continues through October and November as Glasco integrates his new roster. The Red Raiders are expected to be among the preseason favorites heading into 2027 based on roster construction alone. The real evaluation begins when teams start playing live — how Beachum, Pickering, Heavener, and their All-American teammates perform in competitive situations, under pressure, together, will determine whether this talent translates into a national title. Watch for fall scrimmage results and early chemistry signals over the next two months.
