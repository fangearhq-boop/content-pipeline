Three-Peat Pursuit: Texas Softball's Fall Opener Is 26 Days Away

By Sarah Mitchell

September 3, 2026

The defending back-to-back national champions open fall ball in 26 days. And when Texas takes the field at McCombs Field on Sept. 29 against Murray State — free admission, 6 PM Central — it won't just be a fall scrimmage. It will be the beginning of a three-peat chase that virtually no program in history has completed.

Head coach Mike White returns six of nine starters from a squad that went 49-12 and won consecutive national titles. The group includes senior pitcher Teagan Kavan, who owns a distinction that no one else in Women's College World Series history can claim: two consecutive Most Outstanding Player awards. Back-to-back. The only one who has ever done it.

"She's the best competitor I've ever coached," White said of Kavan in the offseason. Entering her final year in burnt orange, Kavan is pitching toward a legacy that already belongs in the record books.

How Rare Is Three in a Row?

The history of three consecutive national championships in women's college softball is very short. Only two programs have done it: UCLA, which won from 1988 to 1990 under the Bruins' dynasty, and Oklahoma, which rattled off three straight from 2021 to 2023 under Patty Gasso. No one else.

Texas won it in 2025. Texas won it again in 2026. Now the Longhorns stand at the doorstep of history. Getting through that door requires repeating as champions in one of the most competitive landscapes in the sport's history.

The good news for Texas: they have the horses. Six starters back, including Kavan, gives White a veteran foundation that doesn't have to be rebuilt from scratch. The transfer portal remains active — White has indicated he will continue monitoring it — but the core of this team is already proven at the highest level.

The Fall Schedule

Texas opens its fall slate Sept. 29 vs. Murray State at McCombs Field (6 PM CT, free admission) and closes the fall Nov. 6 at Texas A&M. All home fall games at McCombs Field are open to the public at no cost, giving fans their first look at the 2027 Longhorns before the spring season begins.

White and his staff will use the fall to experiment with lineups, evaluate depth, and give younger players a taste of game action. For a program with championship expectations, fall camp is less about development and more about refinement.

What's Next

Texas opens fall camp games Sept. 29 against Murray State at McCombs Field (6 PM CT, free). The Longhorns close out the fall on Nov. 6 at Texas A&M. Spring 2027 and the national championship chase officially begin in February. For those chasing the third-peat storyline, the first measuring stick comes this fall — how does a team that already knows how to win look when it's preparing to do it again?
