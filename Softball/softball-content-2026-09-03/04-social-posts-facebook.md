### STORY 1: USA WNT — Nine Days to Group Oklahoma

#### Long-Form Post
Nine days from now, Team USA takes the field at Devon Park in Oklahoma City for the most important international tournament of the year.

The WBSC Women's Softball World Cup Group Stage opens Sept. 12, and the United States draws No. 7 Mexico in the night game — 7:30 PM local time — to open the tournament. The Netherlands (No. 6) looms as the top-seed decider later in the round-robin.

What's at stake: only two of six teams advance to the 2027 World Cup Finals in Redcliffe, Queensland, Australia (April 5-11), which serves as a key qualifier on the road to LA28. The six-team field features Brazil, China, Mexico, Netherlands, New Zealand, and hosts USA.

Patty Gasso leads the coaching staff. The 16-player roster includes seven World Games gold medalists, 2020 Olympians Valerie Garcia and Dejah Mulipola, and four Sooners — Alyssa Brito, Jayda Coleman, Tiare Jennings, and Kelly Maxwell.

This is the same Devon Park venue where USA went undefeated (5-0) en route to International Cup gold just weeks ago. Home turf. Familiar venue. Everything to play for.

Nine days. Let's go, USA. 🥎🇺🇸

### STORY 2: Class of 2028 — Six Days to In-Person Window

#### Long-Form Post
The phones started ringing on September 1. Now the next milestone is six days away.

On September 9 at 7 AM ET, the in-person contact window opens for the Class of 2028. College coaches can visit recruits at their schools, their homes — anywhere they're allowed to be. For many of the 1,226 prospects nominated by Extra Inning Softball (a record), this will be the moment a recruiting interest becomes something real and tangible.

The Class of 2028 is shaping up to be one of the best in recent years. Middle infielders and catchers headline the talent pool, with top prospects including Michaela Landers, Kylie Holden, and Sophia Schlader. A deep pitching pool rounds out what evaluators are calling one of the deepest classes in years.

Under the NCAA's 5-5 recruiting rules, coaches can now communicate during a recruit's junior and senior years — meaning Sept. 9 launches roughly two full years of active recruiting. For elite prospects, the in-person visit could set the tone for every interaction that follows.

Six days. If you're a Class of 2028 prospect, your recruiting journey is officially entering its most exciting phase. 🥎

### STORY 3: Oklahoma Fall Camp — Wells and the New-Look Sooners

#### Long-Form Post
The most dominant freshman in college softball history is back — and she's just getting started.

Kendall Wells shattered the NCAA single-season freshman home run record in 2026, blasting 39 home runs while batting .358 with 88 RBIs and posting a 1.028 slugging percentage. She won the NFCA Freshman of the Year award, the SEC Freshman of the Year, and the Johnny Bench Award. Now she returns for year two at Oklahoma.

That's the good news. The complicated news: Patty Gasso departs in nine days for her Team USA coaching role at the WBSC Group Oklahoma event (Sept. 12-16). The Sooners have only two returning pitchers — sophomore Audrey Lowry (who flashed 2.2 shutout innings with 2 strikeouts in a recent fall scrimmage) and Deal. The rest of the staff must be built through transfers.

Transfer Sydney Berzon (2x All-American at LSU, 486.1 career innings, 2.02 ERA, 406 strikeouts) arrives as the headliner. Macie Harter (.346 BA, MTSU) adds depth at the plate. With 12 returning players and 9 newcomers, Gasso and her staff have six weeks of fall ball to piece it together before she leaves for Oklahoma City.

Fall opener: Sept. 30 at Love's Field against USAO. Free admission.

This is what a championship rebuild looks like. 🥎

### STORY 4: Texas Longhorns — 26 Days to Fall Opener

#### Long-Form Post
The back-to-back national champions are 26 days from their first fall game.

Texas opens the fall slate Sept. 29 against Murray State at McCombs Field — free admission, 6 PM Central. Head coach Mike White has six of nine starters returning from the squad that went 49-12 and won consecutive national titles.

At the center of it all is pitcher Teagan Kavan, who enters her senior season as the only player in Women's College World Series history to win the Most Outstanding Player award in back-to-back years. She's the standard-bearer for a program chasing something only two teams in history have ever accomplished.

The last programs to win three straight national championships: UCLA (1988-90) and Oklahoma (2021-23). Texas is trying to join that list.

White has indicated he'll continue monitoring the transfer portal, and with 6 starters back and a roster that knows how to win, the Longhorns may not need much. Fall camp is where the next chapter begins.

26 days. 🥎🤘

### STORY 5: Texas Tech — Can Seven All-Americans Become a Championship Team?

#### Long-Form Post
Seven All-Americans. One roster. One question.

Gerry Glasco has assembled one of the most decorated rosters in college softball history at Texas Tech: Jaysoni Beachum (.406 BA, 10 HR, 69 RBI in 2026; All-ACC First Team), Kasidi Pickering (No. 2 transfer in the portal per Softball America, .384 career BA, 50 career HRs), Jayden Heavener (LHP from LSU, 13-9, 2.81 ERA), and four more All-Americans — Terry, Williams, Pannell, and Burns — now practicing together for the first time.

The Red Raiders were WCWS runner-up in 2026. They're not rebuilding — they're reloading. Additional transfers Moriah Polar (Purdue), Bradi Gallaway (Louisiana Tech), and Hailey Maestretti (Utah) add even more talent to the mix.

But history has shown that talent doesn't win championships by itself. Chemistry does. That's the question Glasco is answering in fall camp: can seven players who all came from different programs and different cultures figure out how to be a team?

Early signs from fall workouts suggest the talent is very real. The chemistry is the work of September, October, and November. The answers will start coming by spring.

Year two under Glasco. Seven All-Americans. One shot. 🥎
