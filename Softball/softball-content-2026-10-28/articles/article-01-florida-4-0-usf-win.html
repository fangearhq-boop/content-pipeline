Florida Softball Goes 4-0 in Fall With 11-3 Victory Over South Florida

By Jordan Reeves | October 28, 2026 | I Love Softball

Florida softball kept rolling Tuesday night, beating South Florida 11-3 at Katie Seashole Pressly Stadium to move to 4-0 in fall play. The Gators have now outscored opponents by more than 65 runs across four fall games, cementing their status as the most dominant team in college softball this fall.

It is a remarkable run for a program that was already among the favorites to contend for a national title in 2027. The USF result adds another chapter to what has been one of the most dominant fall schedules any Florida softball team has put together.

Four Games, No Resistance

The Gators opened their fall schedule with a 20-0 win over Santa Fe College on October 16, then followed it the very next day with a 29-1 dismantling of Carson-Newman. After a win over UNF, Florida continued to roll Tuesday with an 11-3 win over USF.

The pattern is clear: Florida is not just winning. They are winning convincingly, against programs that should provide at least some resistance. When a team as loaded as Florida makes two-inning work of opponents it expects to beat, it signals genuine readiness for a run at the Women's College World Series.

Sophia Edwards has been the offensive highlight of Florida's fall. The transfer from LSU has homered three times in fall action, giving the Gators a legitimate middle-of-the-order presence to complement the returners they already had. Edwards was one of the most coveted names in the spring portal window, and her performance so far validates the decision to bring her to Gainesville.

A Roster Built for March and Beyond

What separates Florida from a team simply taking advantage of weaker fall competition is the depth of what they are returning. At least six starters from the 2026 roster come back for 2027, meaning the Gators are not being built from scratch — they are being sharpened.

Add to that a recruiting class that ranked No. 2 nationally by both Softball America and D1Softball, and the picture becomes even clearer. Freshman pitcher Caroline Stanton — the top-ranked recruit in the class, per Softball America — enrolled early and is redshirting this fall, preserving a year of eligibility while she develops. That means Florida's best piece from the 2027 class has not even taken the field yet.

For fall purposes, the Gators are operating with more than enough firepower to handle what is in front of them. For 2027 purposes, the ceiling on this roster is higher than what the current fall competition can reveal.

What This Means for 2027

The case for Florida as the preseason No. 1 or No. 2 team in the country next spring is straightforward. Six-plus returning starters, a top-two recruiting class, a proven transfer in Edwards, and a redshirt freshman who was the top recruit in her class — that is a combination very few programs can match.

The fall has done what it was supposed to do: give the coaching staff game reps against real competition while keeping the injury risk low, integrating new faces with returning core pieces, and building the kind of confidence that carries into February and March. Florida has accomplished all of that at an unusually high level.

What's Next

Florida's fall schedule is complete. The Gators are 4-0 and will now shift to winter conditioning and spring preparation. Their 2027 season schedule will be released in the coming months.

No team in fall ball has looked better. When the 2027 preseason polls come out, Florida will almost certainly be inside the top five — and based on what this fall has shown, that ranking will likely be well-deserved.
