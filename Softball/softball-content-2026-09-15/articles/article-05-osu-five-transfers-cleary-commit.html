Oklahoma State Goes All-In: Five Transfers and First 2028 Commit Signal Cowgirls' Rebuild Is Over

By Emily Rawlings | I Love Softball | September 15, 2026

Kenny Gajewski and Oklahoma State are done waiting. Five portal additions, a top-100 2028 pledge and eight fall exhibitions tell you everything you need to know about OSU's intentions heading into 2027.

There are programs that rebuild slowly, and then there are programs that look at an early postseason exit and decide to blow the doors off the offseason. Oklahoma State softball has chosen the latter. After falling to Nebraska in the Lincoln Super Regional to end the 2026 season with a No. 13 national seed, head coach Kenny Gajewski went to work — and the results are impossible to ignore.

Five transfer portal additions. A top-100 commitment from the Class of 2028. Eight fall exhibitions to sort it all out. The Cowgirls are not rebuilding. They are reloading, and fall camp 2026 is where it all begins.

Five New Faces, Five Different Programs

Oklahoma State added five transfer players this offseason, each from a different program and each filling a specific roster need. The group includes pitcher Delainey Everett from Mississippi State, catcher Kennedy Proctor from Utah, and three versatile utility players: Maddie Hartley from Houston, Payton Kennedy from Ole Miss and Kendra Lewis from Wisconsin.

The diversity of their origins tells a story about what Gajewski was looking for: not one archetype, but a variety of experiences, skill sets and cultural backgrounds that can be molded into something new. That is the challenge of building through the portal — getting five people who have never played together to function as a cohesive unit. Fall camp is where that process starts in earnest.

Peyton Cleary: The First 2028 Signal

Alongside the transfer haul, Oklahoma State made its first 2028 commitment with the arrival of Peyton Cleary, a four-star shortstop from Liberty North High School in Missouri. Cleary is a top-100 prospect nationally, and her decision to choose OSU over other competing programs — after an official visit to Stillwater in late August — sends a message that Gajewski's vision for this program is landing with the next generation of players.

Cleary's profile checks every box. She is a high-IQ infielder with a strong arm, proven production at the high school level and a commitment to the kind of two-way development that college coaches prize. That she is the first 2028 pledge rather than a later addition suggests OSU identified her early and worked hard to win the recruitment.

The Fall Calendar

Oklahoma State is scheduled for eight fall exhibitions, giving Gajewski and his staff ample time to evaluate the new additions alongside the returning core. The exhibitions serve as low-stakes, high-information opportunities — a chance to see how Everett's stuff plays in a Cowgirl uniform, how Proctor calls a game behind the plate and how quickly the utility players settle into defined roles.

SI.com named OSU one of the "4 Programs to Watch This Fall," citing the sheer volume of change on the roster and the intrigue surrounding how Gajewski puts it all together. The ranking is deserved.

What's Next

The question for Oklahoma State is not whether the pieces are there — they clearly are. The question is whether this group can develop the identity and trust needed to make a serious postseason run in 2027. The Cowgirls have the talent, the coaching and now the recruiting momentum. Fall camp is the laboratory. By the time the 2027 season begins, this roster should have its answers. For now, Stillwater is watching — and so is the rest of the sport.
