2028 Recruiting Day 6: Arizona, South Carolina and Florida Add to the Commit Wave

By Emily Rawlings | I Love Softball | September 15, 2026

Six days into the in-person window and the commitments keep rolling in — from Tucson to Columbia to Gainesville, no one is standing still.

The September 9 in-person contact window opened six days ago for the Class of 2028, and the wave of commitments it triggered has not lost a single bit of momentum. On Tuesday, Arizona added two in-state pledges, South Carolina landed a catcher and Florida continued building what is shaping up as one of the most stacked 2028 recruiting classes in the nation.

This is what the most anticipated recruiting cycle in recent memory looks like in real time.

Tuesday's Headlines

Arizona locked up two in-state prospects — identified as Ridling and Stell — giving the Wildcats an early regional foothold in a class where every program is fighting for limited spots. The Pac-12 presence in this recruiting cycle has been quieter than the SEC and ACC, making Arizona's early moves noteworthy.

South Carolina added catcher Siena Myers, a highly regarded prospect who brings defensive skill and bat ability behind the plate. Catchers with the full-package profile are among the most coveted positions in softball recruiting, and the Gamecocks securing one early in the window is a significant get.

Florida continued its run by formally adding pitcher and two-way star Kate Losh and catcher Ryann Harris. The Gators have now locked in multiple pieces on both sides of the ball in just the first week of the window.

The Class So Far

The top two overall prospects in the 2028 class are already off the board. Finlee Williams (No. 1 overall), a versatile infielder, committed to Oklahoma. Peyton Ushery (No. 2 overall), a five-star outfielder from Lakeland, Florida, chose Georgia. Both announcements came in the first days of the in-person window and reset expectations for how fast the top of the class would move.

The full list of known commitments continues to grow. Florida State landed Paityn Donalson, a two-way standout who hit .693 with 11 home runs, 50 RBI and 35 stolen bases as a sophomore at Lakeland Christian High School in Florida. Oklahoma also added infielder Braylyn Saenz (ranked No. 41, 13th at the position). Tennessee secured Sophia Galindo and Nevaeh Wilson, both of whom have U-18 Women's National Team ties. Texas Tech received its first 2028 pledge from outfielder Star Gutierrez.

The backdrop to all of this: a record 1,226 Extra Inning Softball nominations for the Class of 2028 — the most in the history of the rankings. This is the deepest early class the sport has ever seen.

Why This Week Matters

The in-person window matters because it gives coaches the chance to host prospects on campus, walk them through facilities, and make the personal connection that most families require before a commitment. Prospects who have been on phone calls since September 1 — and building relationships since they were in eighth grade — can now sit in a locker room, speak with current players and get a feel for the culture.

Programs that have been building relationships for the past year or two are now reaping those rewards. And programs that waited to cultivate those relationships are finding themselves behind.

What's Next

Day 7 arrives Wednesday with more announcements expected across the country. The window stays open through the fall tournament season, meaning commits will continue to trickle in through November. Class rankings will see their first major update in mid-October, which could reshuffle how several top programs are perceived in this cycle. For now, the only thing certain is that the Class of 2028 is going to be very, very good — and it is moving faster than any class before it.
