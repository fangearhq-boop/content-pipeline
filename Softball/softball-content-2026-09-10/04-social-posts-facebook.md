### STORY 1: USA WNT — FB Long-Form
**Posting Window:** 8:00 PM ET
**Type:** Long-Form

Two days.

The WBSC Women's Softball World Cup Group Oklahoma opens Thursday at Devon Park — OGE Energy Field in Oklahoma City — and for Team USA, these final 48 hours aren't about preparation. The preparation is done. These two days are about focus.

USA enters as the field's most proven team at this venue. They went 5-0 at the International Cup at Devon Park earlier this year, competing in the same stadium against overlapping competition. The venue isn't intimidating. The stakes are.

Top two finishers in this six-nation round-robin advance directly to the 2027 WBSC Women's Softball World Cup Finals in Redcliffe, Queensland — the first meaningful qualification rung on the road to the LA28 Olympics. The team that wins this group controls their Olympic destiny. The teams that finish third and fourth enter the Repechage.

Head Coach Patty Gasso leads a 16-player roster that has been assembled through years of international competition, not drafted in September. Rachel Garcia and Dejah Mulipola won Olympic gold in 2020. Seven players are 2025 World Games gold medalists. Ten made the Down Under Series. This is a team that has done hard things in front of big crowds.

Opening night: USA vs. No. 7 Mexico, Thursday, 7:30 PM. The round-robin runs through September 15. The Netherlands (No. 6) will be the defining match for top-seed positioning.

Two days. 🇺🇸

### STORY 2: Class of 2028 — FB Long-Form
**Posting Window:** 9:00 AM ET
**Type:** Long-Form

Day 2 of in-person recruiting for the Class of 2028 is underway — and the results are starting to show.

Yesterday morning at 7:00 AM ET, Division I coaches were permitted for the first time to make in-person contact with Class of 2028 prospects: to walk into schools, show up at practices, and sit at kitchen tables. It was the first time this recruiting cycle moved from the phone to the face.

The results were immediate. Oklahoma committed Braylyn Saenz (INF, Moore, Oklahoma — No. 41 nationally) as the program's first 2028 commit. Tennessee landed Sophia Galindo and Nevaeh Wilson, with Wilson bringing U-18 National Team experience. LSU flipped a five-star in Joley Bennett. Programs that had been building relationships since September 1 started cashing those relationships in as soon as coaches could appear in person.

Today — Day 2 — is where the follow-up momentum lives. The visits that happened yesterday are being processed into decisions. Programs that had deep phone relationships are now calling with offers to come see the campus in person this week. The official visit calendar is starting to get populated.

This is the class with 1,226 Extra Innings Softball nominations — a record. Peyton Ushery started the commit wave on September 1 when she chose Georgia. Now the rest of the class follows, and the coaches who showed up first on Day 1 are the ones seeing the early results. 🥎

### STORY 3: Oklahoma — FB Long-Form
**Posting Window:** 1:00 PM ET
**Type:** Long-Form

Two days. That's what's left on the clock before Patty Gasso packs her bag for Team USA duties.

Oklahoma's fall camp has been building toward an opener on September 30, and these are the final two days with the complete coaching staff — Gasso, JT Gasso, and Jennifer Rocha — all on campus at Love's Field before the WNT group stage pulls three of the program's most experienced voices to Oklahoma City for five days.

This year's camp carries a genuine storyline in the pitching staff. Sydney Berzon, who transferred from LSU as one of the most decorated arms in the country — 486.1 career innings, 2.02 ERA, 406 strikeouts, 2x All-American — signed professionally with the Texas Volts in the AUSL in June. The door she would have walked through as OU's staff ace is now open. Who steps through it is the defining question of this fall camp.

Kendall Wells doesn't need an answer on pitching to make her presence felt. Entering her junior year, Wells carries back-to-back records: 32 HR as a freshman (NCAA record for the class) followed by 39 HR as a sophomore (OU and SEC records). She is the offense. The fall camp question is who matches her on the mound.

Twenty days to September 30. Two days with the full staff. The answers that come out of Love's Field this week set the tone for March. #OUSoftball

### STORY 4: Texas Longhorns — FB Long-Form
**Posting Window:** 5:30 PM ET
**Type:** Long-Form

Nineteen days.

Back-to-back national champions don't usually spend Week 4 of fall camp figuring out who they are. Texas knows who they are. The question entering the fall was whether the new pieces fit — specifically, whether Isa Torres and Teagan Kavan would build the kind of chemistry that wins championships.

The answer, four weeks in, is yes.

Torres transferred from Florida State carrying the 2026 D1Softball National Player of the Year award — .530 batting average, 16 home runs, 56 RBI. She is not a prospect coming in to learn the program. She is a champion joining a championship program. Kavan is entering her senior year having already earned the WCWS Most Outstanding Player award in back-to-back seasons — the only player in the award's history to do so.

When those two players are in the same lineup, the math for opposing pitchers doesn't work.

September 29 vs. Murray State at McCombs Field. Six PM CT. Free, open to the public. It's the first look at what this program can be in 2027 — and with 19 days until the opener, Texas is already playing like a team that knows exactly how this season ends. #HookEm

### STORY 5: Texas Tech — FB Long-Form
**Posting Window:** 5:30 PM ET
**Type:** Long-Form

Week 4 in Lubbock. And the question that has hung over this program all fall is starting to get answered.

Texas Tech has seven current or former All-Americans on its 2027 roster. None of them had practiced together before August. The first three weeks of fall camp were about orientation — learning each other's timing, understanding each other's tendencies, figuring out what this team actually is.

Week 4 is when that question starts getting answered.

Jaysoni Beachum (FSU, .406 BA, 10 HR, 69 RBI) and Kasidi Pickering (OU, No. 2 transfer, .384 BA, 50 HR career) give the Red Raiders an offense that can compete with anyone in the Big 12. Jayden Heavener (LSU, LHP, 13-9, 2.81 ERA, 128 Ks) gives them the arm to stay in every game. What Glasco is building in his second year is the team he always intended TTU to be.

They lost to Texas in last June's Championship Series. That memory lives in the building. The motivation to close the gap — or erase it entirely — is the compound's heartbeat heading into Week 4.

The identity isn't forming anymore. It's formed. #WreckEm #Softball
