Day 2: The Class of 2028 In-Person Window Is Already Producing Results

By Emily Rawlings | September 10, 2026

Recruiting / Class of 2028

Day 1 of in-person recruiting for the Class of 2028 ended yesterday. Day 2 begins with programs already holding the commitments they went out to earn.

When the in-person contact window opened at 7:00 AM ET on September 9, Division I coaches were permitted for the first time to appear in person before Class of 2028 prospects — at schools, at practice facilities, and at home. For a class of 1,226 nominated prospects, that meant dozens of programs making their most important connections face to face, within hours of the window opening.

The results arrived fast.

Day 1 Commitments

Oklahoma secured the first commitment of its 2028 class: Braylyn Saenz, a middle infielder from Moore, Oklahoma, ranked No. 41 nationally. It was OU's signal that their in-person priority list was ready the moment the window opened, and Saenz — a home-state recruit — was at the top of it.

Tennessee landed two commitments on Day 1: Sophia Galindo and Nevaeh Wilson. Wilson brings U-18 Women's National Team experience to Knoxville, making her one of the more decorated early commits in the class. LSU's five-star addition, Joley Bennett, continued the Tigers' momentum in a class that figures to be a deep one for them. Sophia Schlader and Caelyn Campos — both ranked five-stars — made visits to Norman, adding to OU's recruiting footprint on the first day of the window.

Florida State's commitment thread for 2028 remained active, with Paityn Donalson from Lakeland Christian drawing attention.

Why This Class Moves Fast

The Class of 2028 is the largest in modern recruiting history with 1,226 Extra Innings Softball nominations. But the pace of early commitments has exceeded even that scale. Recruiting sources noted more than three dozen juniors had already committed within the first week of the September 1 verbal contact opening — a pace described as faster than typical cycles.

The combination of a record-setting class size and a competitive pool of programs that identified their targets early has compressed the timeline. Coaches who were most organized heading into September 9 are already seeing results by September 10.

Peyton Ushery set the tone on September 1 when she chose Georgia — the No. 2 prospect in Line Drive Media's HOT 100 becoming the first major D1 commit of the class. The programs watching her commitment understood what the first in-person contact day would mean: organized programs that had built relationships over the phone now had the most powerful tool in recruiting. They could show up.

What Day 2 Looks Like

Day 2 of the in-person window is where the follow-through happens. The coaches who were in the field yesterday are now following up — converting Day 1 conversations into official visit invitations, finalizing scholarship offer timelines, and turning the most promising in-person first impressions into decisions.

Programs that were most aggressive on Day 1 are already ahead. For prospects who received visits yesterday but haven't announced: the next 24 to 72 hours is typically when the decision crystalizes.

What's Next

The in-person contact window remains open through the full contact period, and the early wave of commitments will continue to build through the fall. Official visits for junior-year prospects are now schedulable, and the programs locking those in earliest are the ones most likely to close their top targets before the spring season begins.

The class that set a nomination record is now producing commitments to match. Day 2 is just getting started.
