Week 4 at Texas: Back-to-Back Champions Are 19 Days From Proving It Again

By Sarah Mitchell | September 10, 2026

Texas Longhorns Softball

Nineteen days. September 29 at McCombs Field, Murray State, 6:00 PM CT — free, open to the public. The first chance for anyone outside the Texas program to see what the back-to-back national champions look like heading into a season where the three-peat has never been done outside of two programs in the history of college softball.

Inside that building, Week 4 of fall camp is underway. And the question that opened the fall — will Isa Torres and Teagan Kavan be able to build the chemistry that defines championship offenses? — has an answer.

They can.

What Torres Brings

Torres transferred from Florida State carrying the 2026 D1Softball National Player of the Year award: .530 batting average, 16 home runs, 56 RBI. Those numbers do not belong to a player who needs to find her footing at a new program. They belong to a player who is ready to play championship softball wherever she lands — and she landed in Austin.

She is from Georgetown, Texas. She is, in the most direct way possible, coming home. That combination — elite talent returning to her home state with a national championship award — is the kind of addition that changes a program's ceiling.

Kavan as the Standard

Teagan Kavan is entering her senior year as the only player in college softball history to win the WCWS Most Outstanding Player award in back-to-back seasons. That achievement does not happen by accident. It happens because Kavan is the kind of player who performs her best when the stage is largest, who has been through the NCAA Tournament's final weekend twice, and who knows exactly what it takes to win a national championship.

Torres and Kavan in the same lineup, four weeks into building their partnership, are not figuring out timing anymore. They are executing.

The Three-Peat Context

Only two programs in college softball history have won three consecutive WCWS titles: UCLA from 1988 to 1990, and Oklahoma from 2021 to 2023. Texas is entering the 2027 season as the defending back-to-back champion with the personnel to extend that history.

No fall camp guarantees a national title. But fall camp is where the foundation gets built, and what Texas is laying down in Week 4 — with Torres and Kavan and Katie Stewart on the same field, working through the same repetitions — looks like a foundation that could carry a program through March and into June.

What's Next

The fall schedule runs from September 29 through November 6, closing at Texas A&M. The public can see this team for the first time in 19 days at McCombs Field — no cost, no reservation required. What Texas has been building over the past four weeks, the opener is the first window to see it from the outside.

Week 4 means the transition from introduction to execution is complete. The Longhorns are not figuring out who they are anymore. They know.
