Texas Softball: 11 Days Until Torres and Kavan Take the Field Together for the First Time

By Sarah Mitchell | I Love Softball | September 18, 2026

Back-to-back WCWS champions host Murray State on September 29 in a fall opener that has become the most anticipated exhibition game in college softball this season.

Eleven days. That number has taken on a particular weight in college softball circles this September.

On September 29, Texas hosts Murray State at McCombs Field in Austin — 6 PM CT, free admission. It is a fall exhibition. The standings mean nothing. And yet the softball world will be watching in a way that few fall games have ever demanded attention. Because for the first time, Isa Torres and Hannah Kavan will be wearing burnt orange and taking the field together in a game.

Texas (53-12, 2026 WCWS champions) arrives at this fall opener as the team defending back-to-back national championships — and chasing a place in history that only two programs have ever reached.

Torres: The Transfer Who Came Home

Isa Torres is, by any measure, the most consequential transfer of the 2026 portal cycle. She won the 2026 D1Softball National Player of the Year award at Florida State, hitting .530 with 16 home runs and 56 RBIs — a offensive line that made her the unquestioned best position player in the country. She grew up in Georgetown, Texas, 30 miles south of Austin. Coming home was always part of the equation; the question was when.

The answer is now. Torres is deep into fall camp integration at Texas, a Longhorn in every sense of the word. Head coach Mike White, who won the NFCA Coaching Staff of the Year award for the second consecutive season, has spoken about building the offense around her ability to hit for both average and power — a rare combination at the highest level of college softball.

Kavan: The Senior Season With History on the Line

Hannah Kavan is the only two-time Women's College World Series Most Outstanding Player in history. Full stop. That distinction — achieved in consecutive years at Texas — places her in a category of her own in the record books.

Her senior year in 2027 carries the weight of that legacy. The three-peat bid is the most discussed storyline in college softball, and Kavan is at its center. Two pitching performances that defined national championship runs; one final season to add a chapter that has never been written in the sport's history.

The pairing of Torres and Kavan — two seniors, two elite credentials, one shared mission — is the narrative that will define Texas softball from now through the final out of the 2027 Women's College World Series.

The Three-Peat and Its Historical Weight

Only two programs in Division I softball history have won three consecutive national championships: the UCLA Bruins (1988, 1989, 1990) and the Oklahoma Sooners (2021, 2022, 2023). Texas would become the third.

White and his staff are not shying away from that context. The full fall schedule — Murray State (Sept. 29), Texas State (Oct. 13), Temple (Oct. 20), McLennan (Oct. 27), UTSA (Oct. 29), Texas A&M at Davis Diamond (Nov. 6) — is designed to put the roster through competitive scenarios before the spring season begins. Six games, six opponents, six data points for a coaching staff building toward an unprecedented run.

What's Next

September 29. McCombs Field. 6 PM CT. Free admission. The fall opener is 11 days away, and for all its exhibition status, it will be one of the most closely watched collegiate softball games of the season. It is the first public look at Isa Torres in burnt orange. It is the beginning of Hannah Kavan's farewell campaign. It is Game 1 of the three-peat chase.

Eleven days. Texas is ready.
