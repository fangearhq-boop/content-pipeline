Class of 2028 Softball Recruiting: Day 10 of In-Person Window, Commit Wave Shows No Sign of Stopping

By Jordan Reeves | I Love Softball | September 18, 2026

Ten days into the NCAA in-person contact window, the Class of 2028 is one of the most active early recruiting cycles in recent memory — and the pace has not slowed.

Ten days. The NCAA in-person contact window for the Class of 2028 opened on September 9 at 7 AM ET, and coaches and prospects have been moving at full speed ever since. Today is Day 10, and if anything, the activity level has increased.

With 1,226 Extra Innings Softball nominations — a record for the Class of 2028 — the talent pool is the largest it has ever been at this stage of a recruiting cycle. Programs are competing fiercely for top players, and the commit list is growing across every major conference.

The Commit List at Day 10

The top of the class was established early and has held firm. Finlee Williams, the No. 1 overall prospect in the Class of 2028, committed to Oklahoma. Peyton Ushery, ranked No. 2, chose Georgia. Those two commitments set the tone for what was to come.

From there, the wave accelerated. Paityn Donalson, a two-way standout from Lakeland, Florida (.693 BA, 11 HR, 50 RBI, 35 SB), committed to Florida State. Oklahoma added No. 4 Braylyn Saenz. Texas Tech secured Star Gutierrez — the first major Big 12 commitment in the class. Tennessee landed Sophia Galindo and Nevaeh Wilson from the United States Under-18 Women's National Team pipeline. Florida secured pitcher Kate Losh and catcher Ryann Harris in the same window. South Carolina added infielder Siena Myers. Arizona gained the in-state duo of Ridling and Stell.

The breadth of these commitments — spread across the SEC, ACC, Big 12 and Pac-12 — speaks to the depth of the talent in this cycle. Every major conference is well represented, and programs that have built recruiting infrastructure over years are now reaping the results.

Still Uncommitted: The Names to Watch

Despite the volume of commits so far, several elite prospects remain uncommitted, and the competition for them is fierce. Michaela Landers, Kylie Holden and Sophia Schlader are among the most closely watched uncommitted players in the class. Oklahoma has hosted Schlader and Caelyn Campos for visits. LSU continues to pursue Joley Bennett, the five-star pitcher who represents one of the most coveted arms in the class.

As the window moves deeper into October, the next tier of elite prospects will narrow their lists and make decisions. Programs that have already landed top-10 talent are not slowing down — the window rewards the persistent.

The Record That Puts It in Context

The 1,226 Extra Innings Softball nominations for the Class of 2028 is a record. That number — the largest field of ranked prospects ever assembled for a single class — reflects both the growth of the sport at the youth level and the increasing sophistication of the recruiting infrastructure that identifies and evaluates talent.

For coaches, it means more options. For prospects, it means more opportunity. For fans following the cycle, it means more names to learn — and a recruiting story that is going to run well into the fall and winter before this class is anywhere close to fully shaped.

What's Next

The NCAA in-person contact window runs through mid-October, and the first class rankings update from the major recruiting services is expected around that same time. The next milestone for the Class of 2028 is the initial ranking — a moment that will crystallize the current landscape, spark conversation about programs on the rise and identify the remaining uncommitted elite.

Day 10 is not a plateau. It is a checkpoint in the middle of a sustained sprint. The Class of 2028 is going to look significantly different by the time Halloween arrives.
