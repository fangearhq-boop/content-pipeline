Kavan Is Back for One More Year — and Texas Is Already the Team to Beat in 2027

By Sarah Mitchell

August 14, 2026

There is no debate about the standard in college softball heading into the 2026-27 season. Texas is the back-to-back Women's College World Series champion. Teagan Kavan is the first player in WCWS history to win consecutive Most Outstanding Player awards. And she is entering her senior year.

The Longhorns defeated Texas Tech 4-1 in Game 2 of the 2026 WCWS Championship Series in June, sweeping the Red Raiders for the second consecutive year. It was the same matchup, the same result, and the same pitcher walking away with the MOP trophy. Head coach Mike White has turned Texas into a program that doesn't just reach the finals — it wins them.

What Makes the Three-Peat Conversation Real

Kavan's senior year is the centerpiece of everything that follows. She was the best pitcher in college softball in 2025. She was the best pitcher in college softball in 2026. Her 2026 WCWS performance — 30 strikeouts across 33.1 innings in Oklahoma City — was the final, emphatic statement of a two-year run without peer at the collegiate level.

Three-peats in college softball are not unprecedented — Oklahoma won three consecutive national titles from 2016 to 2018 — but they are rare, and they require a combination of talent, roster depth, and sustained execution over a 60-plus game season. Texas has shown it can do all three. The question entering 2027 is not whether the Longhorns are capable. It's whether anyone can stop them.

A Freshman Class Built for the Long Run

Texas added a top-four recruiting class nationally for 2026-27 that includes seven incoming freshmen who will be on campus this fall. The class features multiple former gold medalists at the WBSC U-15 and U-18 World Cups and was rated among the program's strongest recruiting cycles in recent memory.

The blend of Kavan's senior-year excellence with a deep incoming class is precisely the combination that sustained dominant programs in prior eras. For the 2027 season, the Longhorns will have both an elite returning ace and significant new talent arriving simultaneously.

The Target on Their Back

Every preseason poll released ahead of the 2027 season will begin with Texas at or near the top. Every program in the country knows what it needs to beat to win a national title. That is both pressure and motivation — and it's a dynamic Kavan and this program have navigated before.

Texas entered 2025 as the defending champion and won again. They entered 2026 as the two-time defending champion and won again. The argument for a three-peat is not that no one will try to stop them. It's that no one has figured out how.

What's Next

Texas fall camp is expected to begin in late August, giving the Longhorns the first look at how the 2026-27 roster comes together. Preseason polls — typically released in late October or November — will be the first official measure of how the rest of the sport views the three-peat chase.
