Texas Tech 2027: Glasco Builds Without Canady — and the Red Raiders Still Have a Case

By Jordan Reeves

August 14, 2026

The most significant offseason development for Texas Tech softball is not who arrived — it is who left. NiJaree Canady, the ace who helped Gerry Glasco reach the Women's College World Series in his first season as a college head coach, went professional. She was selected No. 2 overall by the Texas Volts in the 2026 AUSL draft and will not return to Lubbock for a senior year.

What Glasco has left to build with is still formidable. It is also a genuine rebuild of the pitching role that defined the 2026 Red Raiders. Replacing Canady — one of the most dominant strikeout pitchers in the sport — is the central challenge of Texas Tech's 2027 offseason.

What Remains

The offense isn't going anywhere. Kasidi Pickering — the transfer from Oklahoma who brought a .384 career batting average and 50 career home runs to Lubbock — is returning for 2027. Pickering was one of the most sought-after transfer portal players last offseason, and her production in the lineup gives Texas Tech one of the most dangerous bats in the Big 12.

On the mound, Natalie Heavener — the LSU transfer who posted a 2.81 ERA in her first season with the program — is back. She and the additional portal pitching additions Glasco has reportedly secured will need to collectively replace what Canady produced.

Glasco's Portfolio

The case for Texas Tech in 2027 starts with what Glasco has already done. He arrived at Lubbock as a first-year college head coach and guided the program to its first Big 12 regular-season title and its first Women's College World Series appearance in the same season. That is not a fluke. That is a coach who knows how to build rosters, develop pitchers, and win in high-pressure moments.

The portal class that produced the 2026 WCWS run was rated No. 1 nationally. The 2027 portal class — which reportedly includes four significant additions on top of the returning core — is tracking toward similar recognition. Whether those additions can fill the Canady-shaped hole is the question fall camp will begin to answer.

The Question That Defines Their Ceiling

In the modern era of college softball, the programs that sustain at the highest level have one thing in common: they find aces. Canady was an ace. Teagan Kavan at Texas is an ace. The programs at the very top of the sport are defined by who is throwing the ball in the biggest moments.

Texas Tech's ceiling in 2027 will be determined by who emerges as the answer in the circle. Heavener is the returning option with the most established track record. The portal additions Glasco has added could produce someone who steps into the role Canady vacated. Fall camp — when all of these players are together for the first time — is where the 2027 picture begins to come into focus.

What's Next
