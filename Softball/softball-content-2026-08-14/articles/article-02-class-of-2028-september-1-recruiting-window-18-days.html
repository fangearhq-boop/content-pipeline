18 Days to September 1: What the D1 First-Contact Window Means for the Class of 2028

By Jordan Reeves

August 14, 2026

On September 1, 2026 — eighteen days from today — NCAA Division I college coaches can make direct phone, text, and email contact with Class of 2028 softball recruits for the first time. The call lists are built. The scouting is complete. The moment the softball recruiting calendar has pointed toward all summer is now less than three weeks away.

The Class of 2028 generated a record 1,226 nominations through Extra Inning Softball — the most for any recruiting class in EIS history. The Extra Elite 100 position rankings are already published. Programs at every level of the sport have evaluated prospects at summer showcases and tournaments, and coaches have spent the final stretch before September 1 finalizing their contact priorities.

What Changes on September 1

Before September 1, Division I programs have been permitted to send printed materials and certain electronic communications to Class of 2028 prospects. What is strictly prohibited until that date: phone calls, text messages, in-person off-campus contacts, and official or unofficial visits that originate from a coach's initiative.

On September 1, all of that opens simultaneously. A coach can pick up the phone and call a 2028 recruit for the first time. Texts flow. Email conversations become two-way in real time. Official visits — in which the program pays for the recruit's travel to campus — become permissible to arrange.

For the most heavily recruited athletes in the class, the first 24 hours after midnight on September 1 can produce dozens of calls from programs at the Power Five level and beyond. The window does not last — coaches compete intensely in the early days to establish relationships before their rivals do.

Why the Class of 2028 Is Being Watched So Closely

The record 1,226 EIS nominations for the Class of 2028 reflect a broader trend: elite travel softball has grown the pipeline of identifiable talent at younger and younger ages. The Extra Elite 100 — which ranks the 100 best position-by-position prospects in the class — has already generated significant attention from programs building toward the 2028 recruiting cycle.

For programs like Oklahoma, Texas, UCLA, Tennessee, and Florida, the Class of 2028 is part of a multi-year roster projection. Many coaching staffs have been in contact via allowable written communication for weeks, building familiarity ahead of the calls that will come September 1.

For the Recruits: What to Do in the Next 18 Days

The period before September 1 is not passive time. Recruits who are prepared when the phone rings will be in the best position to navigate the wave of calls. Updated game film — particularly from summer events — is one of the most valuable assets a 2028 prospect can have ready. Coaches who call on September 1 will almost immediately ask where to find the most current footage.

Academic preparation matters equally. D1 programs evaluate academic profiles alongside athletic ones, and coaches who call September 1 will have questions about coursework, GPA, and standardized test timelines. Having those answers ready signals that the recruit is managing their process seriously.

What's Next

September 1 arrives in 18 days. The countdown continues daily. Following the contact window opening, the next major milestones are early commitment announcements from top-ranked prospects as programs compete to secure verbal commitments from the class's most sought-after players.
