### STORY 1: AUSL All-Star Cup — Series 3 Captains Set

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 9:00 AM ET

The 2026 AUSL All-Star Cup has its Series 3 captains.

Kayla Kowalik — the defending All-Star Cup champion who returned for Series 2 and immediately took over the individual points lead — will captain one of four teams when Series 3 opens August 24. Joining her as Series 3 captains: Keilani Ricketts, Danieca Coffey, and Devyn Netz.

Four teams. Four captains. This is a step up from the three-team format used in Series 1 and Series 2.

And the individual race? It's Kowalik's to lose. She entered the leaderboard cold — unavailable for Series 1, came back for Series 2, led her team to an unbeaten record, and now holds the No. 1 spot on the individual points board with two series remaining.

The surprise movers from Series 2: rookies Emma Lemley (rose 26 spots) and Korbe Otis (up 22 spots to No. 10 overall). The veterans dominate, but the young players are making noise.

Sami Williams — Series 1 MVP — is still in this race. Two series remain and anything can happen.

Series 3 opens August 24 at 3:00 PM ET at Parkway Bank Sports Complex in Rosemont, Illinois. All games on ESPN+, MLB Network, and CBS Sports Network. 🥎

### STORY 3: Texas Longhorns — Defending Champions

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 1:00 PM ET

There is no debate about who the best team in college softball is right now.

Texas. Back-to-back Women's College World Series champions. And entering 2027 with the best player in the sport for at least one more season.

Teagan Kavan is a senior. And she is the first player in the history of the Women's College World Series — 70-plus years of the event — to win consecutive Most Outstanding Player awards. She won it in 2025. She won it again in 2026. First ever.

The Longhorns beat Texas Tech in the WCWS final both years. Head coach Mike White has built something that doesn't just win — it dominates the moment when the sport's biggest stage arrives.

The 2027 season won't start officially until February. But the three-peat chase starts right now, in the conversations happening in opposing programs' offices. Because everyone already knows what the target looks like.

### STORY 5: Texas Tech 2027 — Building Without Canady

#### Long-Form Post
**Window:** 3:00 PM ET

The biggest question in Texas Tech softball for 2027 is not who's arriving. It's who isn't coming back.

NiJaree Canady is now a professional. The Texas Tech ace — who helped pitch Gerry Glasco's program to its first-ever Big 12 regular-season title and its first Women's College World Series appearance — was selected No. 2 overall in the 2026 AUSL draft by the Texas Volts. She's not returning.

Glasco's challenge in year two: replace one of the most dominant pitchers in college softball.

What remains is still formidable. Kasidi Pickering — the Oklahoma transfer with a .384 career batting average and 50 career home runs — is back. Natalie Heavener — the LSU transfer who posted a 2.81 ERA — is back. The program reportedly added four portal pieces for 2027 (names pending confirmation). The recruiting pipeline that produced the No. 1-rated transfer class last offseason is still active.

But Canady is the hole. What the Red Raiders build around that absence — and who steps up in the circle — will define whether 2026 was a launching pad or a peak.

Fall camp starts soon. The answer begins there. 🥎 #TexasTech
