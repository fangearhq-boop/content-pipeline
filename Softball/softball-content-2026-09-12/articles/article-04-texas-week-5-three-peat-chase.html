Texas in Week 5: Torres, Kavan, and the Weight of a Three-Peat Chase

By Jordan Reeves | September 12, 2026

Texas Longhorns

The back-to-back Women's College World Series champions are in Week 5 of fall camp, and the adjustment period is over. Everything Texas does now is about execution — refining the details that separate a very good team from a historically great one.

Seventeen days separate the Longhorns from their September 29 fall opener against Murray State at McCombs Field (6 PM CT, free admission). That game is not the finish line — it is the first measurement. The real deadline is in Oklahoma City in late May 2027.

The Dynamic Duo

The two players Texas is building its identity around could not be more different in their paths to this moment. Isa Torres — the 2026 D1Softball Player of the Year — hit .530 with 16 home runs and 56 RBI at Florida State before transferring to Austin. She is the most decorated transfer portal acquisition in the sport this cycle. Teagan Kavan, a two-time WCWS Most Outstanding Player, is entering her senior season having already won two national championships with the Longhorns. She does not need to prove anything; she wants to win again.

Together, Torres and Kavan give Texas a lineup combination that opposing pitching staffs have to game-plan around in every at-bat. Week 5 is about making sure that combination runs on instinct rather than thought — about building the chemistry that turns individual talent into a championship-quality offense.

The History They're Chasing

Two programs have three-peated in the modern era of college softball. UCLA did it from 1988 to 1990. Oklahoma did it from 2021 to 2023. Every other dynasty has eventually stopped at two consecutive titles. Texas is attempting to become the third.

The 2027 schedule, released September 8, gives the Longhorns their first look at the road ahead. Every game is a step toward defending what they built in 2025 and 2026 — the sweep of Texas Tech in the 2026 national championship series still fresh in the memory of everyone in the program.

What's Next

Texas opens its fall schedule September 29 against Murray State at McCombs Field — 6 PM CT, free to the public. It is the first opportunity to see Torres in a Texas uniform in game conditions and the first look at how this roster has developed through five weeks of practice. Watch for the offense and pitching staff alignment as the program enters the final 17 days of prep work before the fall games begin.
