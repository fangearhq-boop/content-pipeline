Class of 2028 Commit Wave Hits Full Speed on Day 3

By Sarah Mitchell | September 12, 2026

Recruiting

Three days since the in-person contact window opened on September 9, and the Class of 2028 recruiting landscape is moving fast. Major programs have already secured top-ten prospects, campus visits are in full swing, and the pace of commitments is accelerating rather than slowing down.

Oklahoma landed the biggest get yet: Finlee Williams, the No. 1 overall prospect in the class per Softball America, committed to the Sooners. For head coach Patty Gasso — who is currently in Oklahoma City coaching Team USA but whose recruiting staff has been working non-stop — securing the top prospect in the country in the first days of the window is exactly how you build a dynasty.

The Commits Keep Coming

Tennessee's Karen Weekly added two key commitments this week. Sophia Galindo brings elite athleticism at the middle infield, and Nevaeh Wilson — a shortstop and utility player who is a member of the U-18 Women's National Team — gives the Lady Vols a player with international-level experience already on her résumé.

LSU secured five-star Joley Bennett, adding another impact arm to a pitching program that is consistently among the nation's best. Georgia's class, meanwhile, already has its cornerstone: No. 2 prospect Peyton Ushery committed to the Bulldogs on September 1, giving head coach Tony Baldwin a foundational piece at the top of the class.

More than three dozen Class of 2028 prospects are now committed across the country — and the window has only been open three days. This cycle began with a record 1,226 EIS nominations, the most in the history of the program. The depth of talent available is matching the record demand.

Why This Class Feels Different

Every recruiting class gets described as exceptional. This one has the numbers to back it up. The 1,226 EIS nominations represent a record for the classification. The top prospects are committing to Power Five programs before the ink is dry on their first week of contact. And the geographic spread — from California to Florida to the Midwest — suggests this isn't a regional bubble; it's a national class.

For coaches recruiting today, the decisions made in September 2026 will shape rosters for the 2028 and 2029 NCAA seasons. The programs that move first tend to set the tone for what's possible. Oklahoma and Tennessee have made their moves. The rest of the country is responding.

What's Next

The in-person contact window continues, and the commit wave shows no signs of slowing. Watch for additional top-25 names to commit in the next 7–10 days as campus visit trips wrap up and coaches close on prospects they've been recruiting since middle school.
