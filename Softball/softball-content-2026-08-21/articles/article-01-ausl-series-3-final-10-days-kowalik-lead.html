Draft Is Tomorrow: Can Anyone Close the Gap on Kowalik in the Final 10 Days?

By Emily Rawlings | I Love Softball | August 21, 2026

Tomorrow's AUSL All-Star Cup Series 3 draft at Parkway Bank Sports Complex in Rosemont, Illinois arrives at a pivotal moment: the individual title race is entering its final chapter, and the gap between Kayla Kowalik at No. 1 and the rest of the field is real — but not insurmountable.

With Series 3 opening Sunday, August 24, and the individual champion being crowned after Series 4 on August 30, there are roughly 10 days of competitive play remaining. That is enough time for a surge. Sierra Romero, who climbed an extraordinary +42 points in Series 2 to reach No. 9 on the individual standings, knows this better than anyone. Emma Lemley, who added +26 points in the same series, is also in the conversation.

The question is whether the players who need to close the gap have enough runway to do it.

What Tomorrow's Draft Means for Non-Captains

In the AUSL All-Star Cup format, the draft is not just a roster-building exercise — it shapes which players land on teams most likely to generate wins, and team wins generate individual points. For a player like Romero who surged in Series 2, landing on a competitive team in Series 3 is critical. A strong team means more games won, more points generated, and more opportunities for individual statistical contributions.

Captains Keilani Ricketts, Danieca Coffey, and Devyn Netz make the picks tomorrow. Each captain faces their own strategic calculus: build the most competitive team, or account for the individual standings implications of which players they select and which rivals they allow to land elsewhere.

For Romero and Lemley, the draft outcome is partially out of their control. They perform well, they make themselves valuable — and then they see where they land.

Kowalik's Position and What It Would Take to Overtake Her

Kowalik enters Series 3 atop the individual standings. That lead was built through consistent performance across the first two series, including periods where the rest of the field was still finding its footing. In a four-series format, the players who are productive early tend to hold their advantages — the math of cumulative points favors those who started strong.

For Romero to overtake her, the +42 surge from Series 2 would need to be repeated or exceeded. A single strong series can close a gap. Two series of sustained excellence could flip the standings entirely. But Kowalik is not standing still — she will be competing in Series 3 and generating her own individual points along the way.

The realistic scenario for a Romero or Lemley challenge: dominant individual statistical performances in Series 3 games while Kowalik has a below-average series. Both conditions have to be true simultaneously. The AUSL format makes that possible — but it is far from guaranteed.

The Broadcast Schedule and How to Follow Along

All AUSL All-Star Cup games through the conclusion of Series 4 on August 30 are televised on ESPN, MLB Network, and CBS Sports Network. For fans tracking the individual standings race, the scoreboard and stat lines from every game matter — because in this league, the individual points are not just a subplot. They are the main event for the players who have been grinding all summer.

The draft happens tomorrow. The games begin Sunday. And the individual champion — whoever that turns out to be — will be decided in the final points tally after Series 4.

What's Next

The Series 3 draft takes place Friday, August 22, at Parkway Bank Sports Complex in Rosemont, Illinois. Series 3 games open Sunday, August 24. Series 4 — the final series — concludes August 30, when the individual champion will be crowned. For fans watching the standings race, every game this weekend counts.
