The Rarest Achievement in College Softball: Why a Texas Three-Peat Would Be Historic

By Jordan Reeves | I Love Softball | August 21, 2026

Fall camp is underway in Austin, and the Texas Longhorns are chasing something that has only been accomplished twice in the history of Division I college softball — and only by two programs that set the standard for sustained excellence at their peaks.

A WCWS three-peat. Back-to-back-to-back Women's College World Series championships. The rarest team achievement in the sport.

Texas won it in 2025. Texas won it again in 2026, finishing 49-12 and sending Teagan Kavan into the record books as the first pitcher in WCWS history to win the Most Outstanding Player award twice. Now, with fall camp in full swing and 24 players on roster — 11 of them new additions — the question shifts from can they do it again to how rare would it actually be if they did.

The Only Two Programs That Did It

In the decades of modern Division I softball competition, only two programs have ever won three consecutive WCWS titles.

UCLA did it first: 1988, 1989, 1990. Three straight titles in the era when the Bruins were establishing what sustained college softball dominance could look like.

Oklahoma did it second — and most recently — from 2021 through 2023. Patty Gasso's program at its absolute peak, winning three straight national championships in a run that redefined expectations for what a top program could achieve.

That is the full list. Every other program that has won back-to-back championships has fallen short of the third. The portal era, the transfer rules, the depth of competition across the sport — all of it works against sustained championships at this level. Three straight is not just difficult. It is genuinely extraordinary.

What Texas Has Going For It

The Longhorns' case starts and ends with Teagan Kavan. The senior pitcher — the only two-time WCWS Most Outstanding Player in the history of the event — anchors a pitching staff that includes an expanded supporting cast. A former Oklahoma pitcher, added via the transfer portal this offseason, joins a group that also includes the complementary pieces built around Kavan in consecutive championship runs.

The roster has 11 new additions in a 24-player group, which means significant change — but change that Longhorns head coach Mike White has managed carefully. Texas has been here before: the program rebuilt around Kavan after the 2025 title and came back to win again in 2026. The process of integrating new talent while maintaining championship culture is something the program now has genuine experience managing.

Kavan's presence alone elevates the ceiling. When the best pitcher in the sport is on your roster for a third consecutive WCWS run, the odds of any given game going in your direction increase substantially.

What History Says About the Challenge

The modern college softball landscape makes three-peats structurally difficult. Transfer portal activity means rosters change dramatically year to year. Programs that struggled in previous years can close the gap quickly through targeted portal moves — as Oklahoma is attempting right now, and as Texas Tech is pursuing with its own significant transfer class.

The WCWS format itself is a single-elimination bracket at its final stages, which means any given day — any given opponent, any given matchup — can end a dynasty's run. The 2025 and 2026 Texas runs were not smooth straight-line progressions to the title. They were grind-it-out championship runs that required execution under pressure at every stage.

That is what the third would require as well. A full season of sustained excellence, followed by winning five games in Oklahoma City in June.

What's Next

Texas fall camp is underway now with 24 players and 11 new additions. The 2027 season opens in late January or February. If the Longhorns make it to Oklahoma City and win, they join UCLA and Oklahoma as the only programs in the modern era to win three straight Women's College World Series titles. Fall camp is where the foundation gets built.
