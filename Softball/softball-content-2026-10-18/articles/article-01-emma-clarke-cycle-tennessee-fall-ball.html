Tennessee Sophomore Emma Clarke Hits for the Cycle in 32-0 Fall Win

By Sarah Mitchell

October 18, 2026

Fall ball is supposed to be about reps, adjustments, and keeping players healthy. Nobody told Emma Clarke.

The Tennessee sophomore hit for the cycle — a home run, a triple, and two doubles — in the Lady Vols' 32-0 exhibition win over Tusculum, adding six RBI to a performance that had coaches and fans buzzing. It's the kind of showing that puts a name on everyone's radar well before February.

"That's a rare thing at any level," said one observer watching the Lady Vols' fall sessions. Hitting for the cycle requires hitting for all four types of extra-base hit in a single game — a challenge even for the most complete hitters in the sport.

The Lady Vols Start Fall 3-0

Clarke's performance highlighted a fall ball stretch that has Tennessee at 3-0 in exhibitions. Head coach Karen Weekly has used the fall to test combinations and build depth, and early returns suggest the Lady Vols are ahead of schedule entering 2027 preparations.

Today, Tennessee hits the road for an exhibition at Middle Tennessee State (Blue Raider Softball Field in Murfreesboro), with first pitch set for 2 p.m. Central / 3 p.m. Eastern. Admission is free, giving fans in the Murfreesboro area a chance to see this roster firsthand.

The Deepest Pitching Staff in Program History

While Clarke's bat has generated headlines, Tennessee's pitching staff may be the real story of 2027. Karen Weekly has called it the deepest staff in program history — a claim that becomes easy to believe when you look at the numbers.

Karlyn Pickens is back. The No. 1 overall pick in the 2026 AUSL College Draft — who won the Tennessee Sports Hall of Fame Female Amateur Athlete of the Year award — posted 148 innings pitched, 194 strikeouts, and a 1.70 ERA in 2026 while leading the Lady Vols to the Women's College World Series. She's one of the most dominant pitchers in the country and she's not done yet.

Running alongside Pickens is Mardjetko, who went 16-3 with a 1.26 ERA and earned NFCA All-American honors in 2026. A 1-2 punch of that caliber is extraordinary in college softball. Most programs would be fortunate to have one of them.

What Tennessee Is Building

Last spring, Tennessee fell to Texas in the Women's College World Series — a result that stung, but one that also left a roster largely intact and hungry. The addition of Emma Clarke's breakout performance to a returning core of elite pitching suggests Weekly's program isn't just recovering. It's ascending.

Fall ball results are exhibitions — they don't count toward the official record — but they tell you something about a team's identity and depth. A 32-0 win featuring a sophomore hitting for the cycle tells you a great deal.

What's Next

Tennessee is at MTSU today (2 PM CT / 3 PM ET) at Blue Raider Softball Field, free admission. The Lady Vols will continue their fall exhibition schedule through October and November before shifting focus to 2027 spring preparations. With Pickens, Mardjetko, Clarke, and a full returning roster, the early signs suggest Tennessee will be a major factor in the national championship conversation next spring.
