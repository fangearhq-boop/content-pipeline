LSU Lands Winchell and Twin Sisters Pickens to Complete 2027 Portal Class

By Emily Rawlings

October 18, 2026

Beth Torina has rebuilt programs before. She's doing it again.

LSU softball's 2027 transfer portal class is now taking its final shape, featuring a graduate transfer who was one of the top outfielders in the ACC last season and twin sisters who each played at different SEC and ACC programs before choosing the same destination: Baton Rouge.

Together, they represent a significant answer to one of the bigger questions facing the Tigers this offseason — how do you replace what you lost?

Mickey Winchell: Notre Dame's Best to Tiger Park

Mickey Winchell arrives from Notre Dame as a graduate transfer, bringing the credentials of one of the best outfielders in the ACC last season. The 5-foot-9 California native from Livermore batted .335, led the Irish with 14 stolen bases, and recorded 59 hits in 2026.

Her primary job at LSU: fill the void left by Jalia Lassiter, who graduated after serving as the anchor of the Tiger outfield. Winchell's combination of contact hitting and elite speed in center field makes her a natural fit for that role.

LSU's roster page lists her wearing No. 9 as a graduate student, meaning 2027 will be her final season of eligibility. She has a chance to go out in style at one of the most electric softball venues in the country.

The Pickens Twins: A Story Unlike Any Other

Madison Pickens and Mackenzie Pickens are twins who played at different programs — Madison at Louisville as an outfielder, Mackenzie at Ole Miss as an infielder — and both chose to spend their next chapter together at LSU.

The Tiger Rag broke the story of LSU's "Recruiting Doubleheader," noting that landing both sisters via the portal in the same cycle was an unusual and compelling move by Torina's staff. It's the kind of human-interest story that resonates beyond box scores: two sisters, two different paths, same destination.

On the field, the twins give LSU versatility across the outfield and infield. Torina now has options in multiple positions without needing to rely exclusively on the returning roster or the incoming high school class.

Managing the Losses

No roster overhaul happens without challenges. LSU's most significant loss was sophomore ace Jayden Heavener, who transferred to Texas Tech — a notable blow to the 2027 pitching staff. Torina will need to replace that production from a combination of returning arms and the seven incoming high school signees who join the program next spring.

One significant development from the portal process: Kylee Edwards, a starting infielder who had initially entered the transfer portal, ultimately withdrew her name and stayed at LSU. Her decision to return provides stability and continuity on the infield, and it speaks to Torina's ability to retain talent even during periods of roster turnover.

What's Next

LSU opened their fall ball schedule at Tiger Park in October, with the Purple-Gold World Series — the program's marquee fall showcase — set for November 17-19. Torina's complete 2027 roster, combining Winchell, the Pickens twins, returning contributors like Edwards, and seven high school signees, will be on display throughout the fall. If the pieces come together, the Tigers could be a serious SEC contender come spring.
