Texas Softball's Three-Peat Roster Is Coming Into Focus at Fall Camp

By Emily Rawlings | I Love Softball | August 20, 2026

Fall camp is underway in Austin, and the Longhorns are assembling the roster they will carry into their most ambitious chase yet: three consecutive Women's College World Series titles. If they pull it off, Texas will join an extraordinarily short list — just UCLA (1988–90) and Oklahoma (2021–23) have ever won three straight WCWS championships.

The talent base to attempt it is taking shape. Twenty-four players. Eleven new additions. And Teagan Kavan back at the top of the pitching staff, carrying the unique distinction of being the first player ever to win WCWS Most Outstanding Player honors twice.

The Roster's New Look

Eleven new faces in a 24-player roster is a significant turnover for a defending national champion. The additions come from multiple pathways — traditional signing classes and the transfer portal — including a former Oklahoma pitcher who brings Big 12 experience and a built-in understanding of facing top-tier competition.

For head coach Mike White and pitching coach Donna Fremin, the integration task is real. A team that has won back-to-back WCWS titles has a culture and expectation base that new players must quickly absorb. The advantage is that when a program has won that consistently, new arrivals generally want to learn and adapt — the culture sells itself.

Alongside Kavan on the mound, pitchers Fremin and Freeman give Texas a multi-dimensional staff that does not rely on a single ace. In 2026, the Longhorns went 49-12 — a mark that reflected both their offensive depth and the staff's ability to piece together quality starts across an NCAA tournament run.

Kavan: The Anchor of Everything

Teagan Kavan's return changes the math of any Texas season projection. She is the first player in Women's College World Series history to win Most Outstanding Player in back-to-back years — a distinction that reflects not just statistical performance but the ability to rise in the highest-pressure setting the sport offers.

Returning a two-time WCWS MOP as your ace means that every team Texas faces in an NCAA regional, super regional, or Oklahoma City knows what is coming. The challenge for opponents is that knowing Kavan is dominant and stopping her are very different problems.

The Historical Weight

The framing matters here. Three straight WCWS titles is something that has happened only twice in the history of the sport. UCLA did it in the late 1980s, a different era of softball with different competitive dynamics. Oklahoma did it from 2021 to 2023, recently enough that the players and coaches who accomplished it are still in the sport — and some are now competing against the Longhorns.

Texas's two-year run (2025–26) was already historic. The third would be something else entirely — a legitimizing argument for the program as one of the all-time great dynasties in collegiate athletics. That is the ceiling they are chasing in 2027, starting with the work being done in Austin right now.

What's Next

Fall camp continues through the coming weeks as Texas finalizes its roster and prepares for the 2026–27 academic year. Any last-minute transfer portal additions must be completed before the August 31 deadline. Preseason rankings for the 2027 season are typically published in December or January. The 2027 WCWS is scheduled for Oklahoma City in June 2027.
