The AUSL Series 3 Draft Is Tomorrow — Here's What Each Captain Is Playing For

By Sarah Mitchell | I Love Softball | August 20, 2026

The Athletes Unlimited Softball League (AUSL) All-Star Cup Series 3 draft arrives tomorrow, and for the three players stepping up as captains, the choices they make at Parkway Bank Sports Complex in Rosemont, Illinois, will carry a weight that goes far beyond team wins.

Captains Ricketts, Coffey, and Netz will select their rosters for Series 3, which tips off Sunday, August 24. But in the AUSL's unique format, every draft pick is a strategic decision with two distinct dimensions: building a team capable of winning matches and managing the individual point standings race that will crown the All-Star Cup champion after Series 4 on August 30.

The Individual Race Changes Everything

That dual-purpose nature is what makes the draft so fascinating for serious fans of the sport. In most formats, you draft to win. In the AUSL All-Star Cup, you draft to win — and you draft with one eye on the scoreboard that decides who takes home the individual championship.

Heading into tomorrow's draft, Kowalik holds the No. 1 position in the individual standings. Romero surged with a +42 point swing in Series 2. Lemley has been climbing steadily, picking up +26 points in the most recent series.

For a captain at the top of the standings, there is a compelling case for surrounding yourself with players who help you score individual points — because team wins generate points, and so do individual statistical performances. But there is also a case for managing rivals: draft a competitor high and you control when and how much they play, at least theoretically.

Three Captains, Three Different Situations

Ricketts, Coffey, and Netz each enter the draft with different needs. A captain leading the individual standings has the luxury of drafting for maximum team performance, knowing that winning games is the fastest path to individual points. A captain sitting in second or third approaches the board differently — they may look for opportunities to create separation or to disrupt the leader's momentum.

The AUSL All-Star Cup format is specifically designed to generate these layered decisions. Teams are rebuilt every series. No roster carries over. That constant reshuffling keeps the competition fresh, ensures that players who are performing well get noticed and selected, and forces captains to make high-stakes choices under time pressure on draft day.

Series 3 and the Road to the Championship

With Series 3 set to run August 24 through roughly August 28, and Series 4 — the final series — concluding August 30, the window for any player to make their move in the standings is rapidly closing.

The individual champion will be determined by cumulative point totals across all four series. That means tomorrow's draft is not just a roster-building exercise. It is the penultimate opportunity for captains and non-captains alike to position themselves for the title.

Players who are not captains also have a voice in the process — they can express preferences, and the draft order and captain selections reflect the competitive dynamics that define this league's appeal. Every player in the pool is a pro. Every player is competing.

What's Next

The Series 3 draft happens tomorrow, Thursday, August 22, at Parkway Bank Sports Complex in Rosemont, Illinois. Series 3 games begin Sunday, August 24. The final series — Series 4 — closes out August 30, when the individual champion will be crowned.

For fans who have been following the individual standings race, tomorrow is appointment viewing. The decisions made at the draft board will shape not just who wins the most team matches in Series 3, but who stands tallest when the AUSL All-Star Cup ends in less than two weeks.
