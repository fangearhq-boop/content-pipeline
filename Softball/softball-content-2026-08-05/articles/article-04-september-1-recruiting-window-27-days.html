September 1 Is 27 Days Away. The Summer Film Is Done. Coaches Are Building Their Call Lists Now.

By Sarah Mitchell

August 5, 2026

September 1, 2026 is 27 days away. That is the date Division I coaches can pick up the phone and call a Class of 2028 recruit directly for the first time — the moment the most consequential window in softball recruiting opens for the incoming junior class.

The summer showcase season is over. PGF Nationals closed. The major events are done. The Little League Softball World Series is ongoing, but for Class of 2028 prospects who competed in this summer's recruiting circuits, the evaluation window has closed. Everything that matters on film is already on film.

What Coaches Are Doing Right Now

Between now and September 1, Division I coaches are reviewing summer footage, cross-referencing showcase statistics, and finalizing their priority lists for the Class of 2028. They have spent the summer watching — at showcases, at tournaments, at PGF Nationals, at camp settings. Now they are deciding who gets the first call.

A coach who calls a recruit at 8 AM on September 1 is communicating something specific: you are a priority. Programs have limited roster spots. Every call has a sequence. The order in which coaches make those calls is a direct expression of where a recruit stands in that program's evaluation.

Before September 1, coaches may send written or electronic materials but cannot initiate phone calls, text messages, or arrange in-person recruiting meetings. The communication window that opens September 1 changes everything.

What This Means for Class of 2028 Prospects

The Class of 2028 is entering its junior year of high school. For prospects who had a strong summer on the showcase circuit — who hit at PGF Nationals, who got attention at major recruiting events, who stood out in front of coaches at high-visibility tournaments — the next 27 days are a waiting period before the phones ring.

For prospects who feel uncertain about their standing: coaches adjust. September and fall showcases still matter. Programs have needs that shift. But the most valuable first-call list — the one built from summer observation — is being finalized now.

What prospects can control: being ready when the calls come. Having clear answers about academics, eligibility, position preferences, and timeline. Coaches on September 1 are evaluating fit as much as talent. A recruit who knows what she wants and communicates it clearly stands out from one who is still figuring it out.

The Bigger Recruiting Picture

The 2026-27 portal cycle already closed its biggest windows, with programs like Texas Tech, Florida, and others making major additions. The September 1 window for the Class of 2028 is a different conversation — early prep, long timelines, and first impressions. Programs who land the best 2028 recruits in the September 1 window often have a head start on the national title conversations two years from now.

The National Letter of Intent day for the Class of 2028 is still more than a year away. September 1 is not a commitment deadline — it is the start of an official conversation. But those first conversations set the tone for everything that follows.

What's Next

The countdown continues. September 1 arrives in 27 days. The fall high school season begins around the same time. September showcases give programs a final evaluation window before the calendar turns to winter preparation. For Class of 2028 prospects, the fall is when the conversations become real — coaches can call, can schedule visits, can talk specifically about roster needs and opportunities. The summer was about being seen. The fall is about being chosen.
