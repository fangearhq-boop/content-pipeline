The Philippines "Blu Girls" Haven't Medaled at the Asian Games Since 1998. That Changes in Aichi-Nagoya.

By Emily Rawlings

August 5, 2026

The Philippines national softball team is going to the 2026 Asian Games in Aichi-Nagoya with a specific goal that has eluded them for nearly three decades: a medal. The "Blu Girls" finished fourth at the 2014, 2018, and 2022 Asian Games — three consecutive tournaments, three fourth-place finishes, no hardware. They are ranked No. 13 in the world and 4th in Asia. Japan enters as the host nation and the world's No. 1 team. The Philippines has reason to believe this time is different.

The Lima Moment

In July, the Philippines competed in the WBSC Women's Softball World Cup Group Lima qualifier in Lima, Peru — a qualifying event for the 2027 Women's Softball World Cup Finals. They faced Puerto Rico, the world's No. 3-ranked team, with their tournament life on the line.

They won 5-4 on a walk-off. An upset, by almost any calculation — the Philippines beating a top-three program in a tournament that mattered. The result did not send the Philippines to the 2027 Finals (Japan and Puerto Rico advanced from Group Lima), but it sent a message about where this program stands and what it is capable of in a high-stakes elimination situation.

The Manila Times covered the Blu Girls' Asian Games preparation on August 4, noting the win over Puerto Rico as a confidence builder entering the September stretch.

The Asian Games Landscape

Women's softball has been a medal sport at the Asian Games since 1990. Japan has dominated the event as both the host nation and the sport's top power in Asia. The 2026 edition runs September 26 through October 3 at Anjō Sports Park Softball Ground in Aichi-Nagoya, with eight nations competing. Japan enters as defending champion and overwhelming favorite — playing on home soil in front of their home crowd against a field that knows how dangerous they are.

That is the mountain. The Philippines has been at the base of that mountain three times and left without a medal each time.

Why This Time Is Different

The Philippines' case for a medal at Aichi-Nagoya rests on a combination of development and demonstrated results. They are 4th in Asia — which means bronze is a realistic target even before the field is evaluated game by game. The Lima walk-off over Puerto Rico is evidence that the program can execute under pressure. Their preparation this summer has been active and intensive.

A first Asian Games medal since 1998 would be a significant moment for Philippine softball — for the players currently on the squad, for the programs that developed them, and for the visibility of the sport in a country where it has a real but underrecognized base. The team is preparing for that moment. Aichi-Nagoya is 52 days away.

What's Next

The Asian Games 2026 run September 19 through October 4, with softball competition September 26–October 3 at Anjō Sports Park Softball Ground in Aichi-Nagoya. Eight teams compete: Japan, China, Chinese Taipei, Philippines, South Korea, Hong Kong, Singapore, and Thailand. Japan enters as host and No. 1. The Philippines enters as the team with the most to prove — and, after Lima, some evidence they can do it.
