Five Days Away: Torres, Kavan and the Texas Longhorns' Three-Peat Countdown

By Sarah Mitchell | September 24, 2026

The most anticipated matchup in college softball this fall isn't a rivalry game or a postseason showdown. It's a free admission game on a Tuesday afternoon in Austin.

Five days from now — Tuesday, September 29 at 6 PM CT — Isa Torres and Teagan Kavan will take the field together at McCombs Field in burnt orange for the very first time. The opponent is Murray State, and the result will barely matter. What will matter is what fans and coaches see: two of the most accomplished players in college softball history, finally on the same team, beginning the chase for something almost no program has ever done.

Two Transcendent Talents, One Lineup

Torres arrived at Texas this offseason as the 2026 D1Softball National Player of the Year. In her final season at Florida State, she hit .530 with 16 home runs and 56 RBI — numbers that defined her as the best hitter in the sport. She grew up in Georgetown, Texas, and her move to Austin feels inevitable in retrospect: the state's best player, coming home to the defending champions.

Kavan's case is unique in a different way. She won the WCWS Most Outstanding Player award in consecutive years — 2025 and 2026. That has never been done before in the history of the Women's College World Series. She's back for another season, and Texas will ask her to anchor a lineup that already has Torres at the top.

Put those two players in the same order card, and you have a top-of-the-lineup pairing that opposing pitchers will lose sleep over.

The Three-Peat That Almost Never Happens

Texas finished 53-12 last season and claimed the 2026 WCWS title to go back-to-back with their 2025 championship. Now they're pursuing something that only two programs in the history of the sport have achieved: three consecutive national titles.

UCLA did it from 1988 to 1990. Oklahoma did it from 2021 to 2023. That's it. That's the entire list. Every other program that has tried to run three straight has fallen short at some point. The physical, mental, and recruiting demands of sustaining that level across three full seasons are enormous.

Texas believes they have the roster to do it. Coach Mike White earned the NFCA Staff of the Year award for the second consecutive season — a rare honor that reflects both the program's on-field results and its ability to recruit and develop at the highest level. With Torres and Kavan leading the way, the pieces are in place.

What's Next

Tuesday's opener is the first of Texas's fall schedule, which also includes games against Texas State (Oct. 13), Temple (Oct. 20), McLennan (Oct. 27), UTSA (Oct. 29), and Texas A&M at Davis Diamond (Nov. 6, 7 PM CT). The fall won't settle any big questions — those answers come in February — but it will give fans their first look at this version of Texas softball: the one trying to make history. All games are free. Get to McCombs Field while you can.
