Defending Champions Building Something Special: Texas Fall Ball and the Torres-Kavan Partnership

By Emily Rawlings | October 14, 2026 | I Love Softball

The Longhorns are the defending Women's College World Series champions. They also have arguably the most compelling roster construction in college softball — a lineup that pairs the D1Softball Player of the Year with the two-time WCWS Most Outstanding Player in the same batting order.

Isa Torres and Teagan Kavan are at Texas now, and fall ball is giving the sport its first extended look at what they can become together.

The Transfer That Changed Everything

Torres arrived from Florida State as the consensus best transfer in the portal. Her 2026 season at FSU was remarkable: .530 batting average, 16 home runs, and the D1Softball Player of the Year award. She was the kind of hitter programs build offenses around. She chose to build around a program that was already winning everything.

Kavan's story is equally compelling. She won back-to-back Women's College World Series Most Outstanding Player awards — twice being the best player on the biggest stage in the sport. Her transfer to Texas means the defending champions now have a lineup that contains both last year's championship difference-maker and the nation's best transfer talent.

The chemistry question is the only one left to answer. Fall ball is where that answer starts to take shape.

What Fall Ball Has Shown

Texas opened its fall schedule with a dominant 28-1 win over Murray State — an early statement of offensive intent. The margin was not a warning about Murray State; it was a preview of what this lineup can do when it is fully assembled and firing.

The Longhorns' fall schedule includes six total games. The competitive tests grow as the schedule progresses, culminating in a November 6 matchup at Texas A&M — the kind of in-state rivalry game that carries significance even in the fall.

For head coach Mike White and his staff, fall ball is about more than wins and losses. It is about lineup construction, role definition, and the small adjustments that turn individual talent into team execution. Torres and Kavan can both hit. The question is how they protect each other, how pitchers have to navigate a lineup that features both, and what the rest of the batting order becomes when built around them.

The 2027 Stakes

Texas is not just trying to have a good season in 2027. They are trying to repeat as national champions — something that has become increasingly rare in college softball's era of competitive parity. The SEC is brutal. Oklahoma enters the conference for the first time. Georgia, Alabama, and Tennessee are all loaded. The path to Oklahoma City is hard.

But if the Torres-Kavan partnership develops into something as good as it looks on paper, Texas has the offensive ceiling to get there. Two elite hitters protecting each other in the middle of the order creates lineup problems that very few pitching staffs are equipped to solve.

Fall ball is not the destination. It is the proving ground. What happens in these exhibition games — what adjustments get made, what chemistry gets built — is going to be the foundation of the 2027 national championship run.

What's Next

Texas has more fall games ahead before February opens the competitive season. Each game is another chance to answer the questions that championship runs are built on: how does this lineup hold up against quality pitching? What does this team look like when things are not going perfectly? How do the new pieces fit with the returning veterans?

For now, the answers are promising. A 28-1 opener. Two of the best players in college softball in the same dugout. A staff that has proven it knows how to win in Oklahoma City.

The 2027 Longhorns are worth watching all the way to June.
