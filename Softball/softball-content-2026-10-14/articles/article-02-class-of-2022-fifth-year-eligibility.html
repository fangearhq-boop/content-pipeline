Class of 2022 Athletes Win Fifth Year: What the 5-for-5 Ruling Means for College Softball

By Emily Rawlings | October 14, 2026 | I Love Softball

College softball is entering unfamiliar territory — again.

A federal court injunction is giving Class of 2022 athletes the possibility of a fifth season in 2026-27, upending rosters that coaches thought were finalized months ago. The ruling comes from U.S. District Judge Charlotte Sweeney of the District of Colorado in a case called Wisne v. NCAA — and its impact on softball programs is just starting to become clear.

What the Court Ruled

On July 31, 2026, Judge Sweeney granted a preliminary injunction blocking the NCAA's 5-for-5 eligibility model as applied to the Class of 2022. The NCAA had adopted the 5-for-5 framework on June 23, 2026, granting fifth-year eligibility to athletes in certain classes — but explicitly excluding Class of 2022 athletes who exhausted their fourth season of eligibility in spring 2026.

The plaintiffs argued that exclusion violated Section 1 of the Sherman Act — antitrust law. Judge Sweeney agreed, at least enough to issue a preliminary injunction. That injunction means the NCAA cannot enforce its 5-for-5 model in a way that prevents Class of 2022 athletes from returning for a fifth year.

The NCAA is appealing to the Tenth Circuit and asked Judge Sweeney to issue an administrative stay of the injunction while the appeal proceeds. She denied that request. The injunction stands.

What It Means for Softball

For college softball, the practical impact is significant. Any Class of 2022 player — meaning athletes who began their college careers in fall 2022 — who played their fourth season of eligibility in 2025-26 could potentially petition their program for a fifth year of competition in 2026-27.

There are important limitations. SI's coverage of the ruling noted that Class of 2022 players most likely need to still be enrolled at their school to qualify. Transfer eligibility under this specific injunction is an unsettled question. Programs may also face scholarship constraints and roster limits that complicate bringing athletes back even if they are legally eligible.

But the door is open — and coaches are paying attention. A fifth-year veteran pitcher or a senior-level utility player who said goodbye at the 2026 WCWS might have one more season available to them. The phone calls between programs and athletes are already happening.

The Larger Context

This is not the first time the courts have reshuffled the eligibility deck in college athletics. COVID waivers created extra-year eligibility for the Class of 2020 and 2021. The result was roster overcrowding, scholarship pressure, and an era of roster instability that coaches are still navigating.

The 5-for-5 litigation is adding another chapter to that story. More than 30 lawsuits have been filed against the NCAA on eligibility issues, and multiple courts have weighed in differently. Some injunctions have been stayed on appeal; others have not. The landscape is genuinely uncertain.

The NCAA is not done fighting. Its appeal to the Tenth Circuit is ongoing. A different appellate court stayed a similar wide-sweeping injunction in a different case earlier this summer. The Wisne injunction may not survive the appeals process — but until it is overturned, it has legal force.

What's Next

Coaches and athletic departments are reviewing their rosters now. The athletes most likely to return are those already enrolled at their programs — veteran players who can step back in without a transfer process. Programs with roster depth may have fewer options; programs that lost key contributors to graduation may have meaningful opportunities to bring them back.

The 2026-27 season begins in February. Rosters must be finalized well before then. The next few weeks — as the Tenth Circuit appeal proceeds and athletic departments work through the legal fine print — will determine exactly how transformative this ruling is for college softball programs across the country.
