2028 Recruiting Snapshot: Tennessee's No. 4 Prospect and the Race Before the Evaluation Window

By Jordan Reeves | October 14, 2026 | I Love Softball

Three days from now, the October 17-18 evaluation window opens for the 2028 recruiting class — and some programs are already miles ahead.

Tennessee, Oklahoma, and Florida have each built early commitments that put them among the early leaders in what analysts are calling one of the most talent-dense 2028 classes in recent memory. By Sunday, a wave of new announcements from top-ranked prospects could reshape the leaderboard entirely.

Tennessee's Big Haul

Karen Weekly's rebuilt Tennessee staff — anchored by new associate head coach Ehren Earleywine, who joined this past June from Texas's championship staff — has already landed five 2028 commits. The headline addition is the No. 4 ranked player in the national class, a recruit whose commitment signals that Tennessee's rebuilding process is producing results well beyond just scheming and depth.

Weekly's program was in a period of transition this offseason after the departures of longtime assistants Craig Snider and Stephanie Sanders, who left for co-head coaching roles at Auburn. But the early 2028 class is a sign that the Lady Vols' program identity — and their ability to attract elite recruits — remains intact.

Oklahoma Stays on the Pace

Oklahoma has five 2028 commits of its own, keeping pace with the programs that historically dominate early recruiting cycles. Grace Tonga, an outfielder who committed in September, and Sophia Schlader, who followed shortly after, are among the five.

The Sooners are making their 2026-27 transition to the SEC while simultaneously recruiting for 2028. The combination of Patty Gasso's coaching reputation and the move to the nation's strongest conference has not slowed their recruiting momentum — if anything, the SEC entry date seems to be accelerating interest from top prospects who want to play in that environment.

Florida's Top-Ranked Commits

Florida has secured two of the highest-ranked players in the 2028 class. Kylie Holden, the No. 2 overall prospect and a five-star middle infielder, is committed to the Gators. Brooklyn Evans, a catcher, has also chosen Florida.

Tim Walton's program is already a top contender for the 2027 WCWS, and with the foundation of the 2028 class taking shape, the Gators are building for sustained success. Holden's commitment at No. 2 overall is a statement recruit — the kind of player who elevates every team around her and often determines a program's ceiling for years.

The Evaluation Window and What Comes Next

The October 17-18 evaluation period allows college coaches to attend open and closed scholastic and non-scholastic events. Coaches can see prospects play, make impressions on their staffs and families, and build the relationships that turn into commitments in the weeks that follow.

LSU, Clemson, and Oklahoma State have also added 2028 commits in recent weeks. Other programs are active. The gap between the early leaders and the rest of the recruiting landscape can close quickly once evaluation events create new visibility for uncommitted prospects.

By this time next week, the 2028 commitment list will look substantially different than it does today.

What's Next

The 2028 class will take shape throughout the next 12-18 months as evaluation events and contact periods bring prospects and programs together. The programs with the strongest early commitments — Tennessee, Oklahoma, Florida — have a foundation to build on. The programs still looking to add will be among the most active this weekend.

College softball recruiting has never moved faster or earlier. The players committing to play in 2028 are doing so as high school freshmen and sophomores. The sport's future is being sorted out right now, three years before it steps on the field for real.
