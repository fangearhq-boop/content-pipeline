Softball Was on ABC Today. Here's What That Means.

By Emily Rawlings | I Love Softball | July 25, 2026

Today, Game 1 of the AUSL Championship Series aired on ABC at 1 PM ET. Utah Talons 3, Chicago Bandits 1. Davis Diamond, College Station, Texas. Sold-out stadium. Montana Fouts on the mound. First professional softball game ever broadcast on U.S. network television.

Whatever the viewership number is when it gets reported in the next few days — it is now the number every future broadcast gets measured against. That is what Saturday created.

What Network Television Actually Means

Cable and streaming reach opted-in audiences. When you tune into ESPN or ESPN+, you are choosing to watch sports. Network television is different. ABC reaches households with antennas, basic cable subscribers, and families who have it on in the background on Saturday afternoon. It puts a product in front of people who did not necessarily seek it out.

That is the growth mechanism professional sports use to build new audiences. NFL games on broadcast television built fans who had never been to a game. College football's Saturday afternoon dominance on ABC built a generation of viewers. When a new league gets that placement — even for one game — it is making the case to a different category of fan than its core base.

The AUSL is two years old. Getting Game 1 of its championship on ABC is the kind of broadcast milestone that organizations spend years working toward. It happened today.

The Building Blocks Were Already There

The AUSL's viewership grew 181 percent from 2025 to 2026. That number came before Saturday's broadcast. The league was already demonstrating that there was an audience for professional softball. Davis Diamond — the premium facility on the Texas A&M campus, with a sold-out house for all three potential championship games — gave the broadcast a legitimate venue that looked and sounded like major-sport programming.

Montana Fouts is the kind of pitcher that gives a new viewer a single performance to anchor around. You see her pitch for nine innings, you understand what dominance looks like in this sport, and you come back for Game 2 on ESPN. That is the conversion the broadcast was designed to produce.

The Next Question Is What Comes After

One game on ABC does not transform a sport overnight. The number that matters most is what happens with Game 2 viewership — whether the audience that found Saturday's game on ABC carries over to Sunday on ESPN. A bounce in Game 2 viewership would tell the AUSL that the broadcast exposure did its job.

The sport's growth in youth participation has been sustained for over a decade. The college game has been consistently strong. The AUSL is the professional extension of that pipeline. Today's broadcast is the moment the pipeline connected directly to network television.

The Talons won 3-1. The series continues Sunday. The sport is different today than it was yesterday.

What's Next

Game 2 of the 2026 AUSL Championship Series is Sunday, July 26, at 2 PM ET on ESPN at Davis Diamond, College Station, Texas. The Utah Talons lead the series 1-0. A Utah win gives them the back-to-back championship. Game 1 viewership data from ABC will be reported by ESPN in the days ahead and will set the benchmark for every future professional softball broadcast.
