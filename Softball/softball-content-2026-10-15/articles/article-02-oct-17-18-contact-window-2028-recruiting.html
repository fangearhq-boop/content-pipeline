The Oct. 17-18 Contact Window: What It Means for the 2028 Recruiting Class

By Sarah Mitchell | October 15, 2026 | I Love Softball

Saturday morning, college softball coaches across the country board flights, load up cars, and head for high school diamonds and travel ball facilities. The NCAA's fall evaluation and contact window opens October 17-18 — and for the 2028 recruiting class, this weekend may be the most significant two days of the cycle so far.

The 2028 class is already considered one of the most talent-dense in recent memory. Multiple programs have built strong early foundations. But the best uncommitted prospects are still out there, and this is the weekend when coaches get to make their cases in person.

What the Contact Window Actually Means

During the evaluation and contact period, coaches are permitted to travel off campus to observe recruits and engage in in-person contact with them and their families. It's a significant shift from most of the fall calendar, during which coaches are limited to campus visits initiated by the recruit and phone or written communication.

For 2028 prospects still working through their decisions, the window is when programs get to make a personal impression — the coach sitting across from the family at the kitchen table, or watching a prospect at a fall tournament and connecting afterward.

Where the Top Programs Stand

Oklahoma enters the window with five commits in the 2028 class, including Sophia Schlader — listed as the No. 3 nationally ranked player and the class's top pitcher — along with outfielder Grace Tonga and infielder Kate McCartney among others. Patty Gasso's program has been dominant in recruiting for years, and the 2028 class continues that pattern.

Tennessee has also built aggressively, landing five commits including Caelyn Campos, the No. 4 ranked player in the class. Karen Weekly's staff is in the middle of a significant rebuild, and the 2028 class reflects both recruiting firepower and a long-term vision for Tennessee's return to national contention.

Texas — defending WCWS champions — has four commits including top-ranked Michaela Landers, now bolstered by outfielder Zoey Abdullah's commitment this week. Florida entered the window with No. 2 overall Kylie Holden and Brooklyn Evans already committed.

What Happens Next

Watch for commitment announcements this weekend and into early next week. The personal contact of the evaluation period often accelerates decisions, particularly for prospects who have been in communication with programs but haven't yet committed.

The 2028 class's top uncommitted players have leverage — they have their pick of programs. The programs that come in with the best pitch, the clearest path to playing time, and the most compelling vision for 2028 will be the ones that close.

For fans of the sport, the next two days are worth following. Big commitments have a way of clustering around windows like this one.

What's Next

The contact window runs October 17-18. Commitment announcements typically follow throughout the week. Check D1Softball, On3, and Softball America for the latest updates on the 2028 class as the weekend unfolds.

After this window, the next major recruiting milestone for the 2028 class is the start of official visits in the spring. Programs that land their targets this weekend will have more flexibility to address remaining needs then.
