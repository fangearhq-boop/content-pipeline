The 5-for-5 Fight Hits a Crossroads: What Dec. 1 Means for 850 Softball Athletes

By Jordan Reeves | I Love Softball | November 21, 2026

The NCAA won a significant legal battle earlier this month when the Tenth Circuit Court of Appeals reversed and vacated the federal injunction in Wisne v. NCAA. The federal shield that had temporarily protected affected athletes in Colorado is gone. The NCAA's 5-for-5 eligibility rule is now back in full effect in federal court.

But the fight is not over. Not even close.

More than 200 state court cases remain active across the country, covering an estimated 850 athletes who have been fighting for continued eligibility under the 5-for-5 framework. State courts operate completely independently of the Tenth Circuit — and those cases have not been dismissed.

What the Tenth Circuit Ruling Actually Means

The Wisne case was a federal antitrust challenge to the NCAA's age-based eligibility rule. When the Tenth Circuit reversed the injunction, it handed the NCAA a win on the antitrust argument — specifically that the plaintiffs had failed to properly define the relevant market.

That's an important legal distinction. The Tenth Circuit didn't say the 5-for-5 rule is good policy. It said the specific legal theory the plaintiffs used wasn't strong enough to justify a federal injunction. En banc rehearing of the Tenth Circuit's decision is possible but rarely granted — it requires a majority of the court to agree to hear the case again.

So where does that leave affected athletes? Dependent on state courts, which are still active and have produced a range of injunctions and stays across multiple jurisdictions.

December Adds Pressure

The timing matters. The December transfer portal opens Dec. 1 — the same date looming as a psychological pressure point for athletes in legal limbo. Athletes who have been waiting for a court ruling that extends their eligibility now face a choice: enter the portal and find a new program, or stay and continue fighting.

The 25-player House settlement roster cap is still in effect, which limits how many transfers any program can absorb. That cap has made coaches cautious about adding portal athletes who might face eligibility challenges later. For affected athletes, the uncertainty is exhausting — and December doesn't offer a clean answer.

What's Next

The state court landscape is the most likely source of near-term relief for affected athletes. Several state cases have produced temporary injunctions that remain in effect. Those cases are proceeding on their own timelines, independent of what the Tenth Circuit did in Wisne.

December 1 through December 15 — the portal window — will be a revealing period. Some athletes in legal limbo will make the practical decision to enter the portal rather than continue fighting. Others will stay put. Programs will navigate the roster cap while trying to serve athletes caught in the middle.

There is no clean resolution coming before January. But the legal fight for 850 athletes is very much alive — and December is not the end of their story.
