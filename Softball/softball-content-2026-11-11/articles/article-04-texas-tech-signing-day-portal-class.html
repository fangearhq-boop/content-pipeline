Texas Tech's Signing Day — Portal Class Meets High School Class

By Sarah Mitchell | November 11, 2026 | I Love Softball

Texas Tech has never entered a Signing Day like this one. For the first time in program history, the Red Raiders arrive on November 11 as one of the most watched programs in college softball — not because of a tradition of success, but because of what head coach Gerry Glasco has built in a single offseason.

The Portal Class That Started Everything

The foundation was laid in the summer. On June 19, 2026, Texas Tech announced the signing of Kasidi Pickering, an All-American transfer from Oklahoma who spent three seasons in Norman hitting .384 with 50 home runs and 169 RBI over 189 games. Pickering was the second-ranked transfer in the country by Softball America and carried a full season of eligibility to Lubbock.

She was not alone. The portal class assembled by Glasco also included Bradi Gallaway, Jayden Heavener, and Moriah Polar. Multiple recruiting services ranked it the No. 1 transfer portal class in the country. For a program that reached the Women's College World Series championship series in each of the past two seasons under Glasco, it was a statement about expectations.

What Today Adds

The portal class handles the present. Today's high school signings handle the future — and shape the depth behind Pickering and company in 2027. Glasco's ability to recruit from the high school side will determine whether Texas Tech can sustain this level of competition beyond one season's portal haul.

The Red Raiders also benefit from a geographic advantage: Texas is the deepest state in the country for softball talent, and the program's improved national profile makes in-state recruiting easier than it was in previous years. What Texas Tech signs today in the high school class signals whether Glasco can hold onto homegrown Texas talent that might otherwise leave for Austin or College Station.

The Big 12 and the WCWS

The Big 12 softball schedule opens March 12, 2027. Texas Tech will face a conference that includes Texas (defending national champion), Oklahoma (preseason top-five), and a full slate of programs that have been building toward this same window. The Red Raiders have never been here before as a favorite to compete for the conference title.

That's the ceiling. It is not guaranteed. But it is real in a way it has never been for this program.

What's Next

Signing Day class rankings from recruiting services will be updated over the coming days. For Texas Tech specifically, the roster is effectively set once today's high school signings are complete — the portal class is in place and the high school class completes the picture. The spring schedule release and the first Big 12 conference games in March are the next milestones. If Pickering and the portal class translate on the field the way scouts expect, the Red Raiders could arrive in Oklahoma City in June as a legitimate contender.
