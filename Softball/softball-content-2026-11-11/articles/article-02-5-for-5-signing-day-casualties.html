5-for-5 Creates Signing Day Casualties — Roster Squeeze Forces De-Commits

By Emily Rawlings | November 11, 2026 | I Love Softball

Kentucky head coach Rachel Lawson said it plainly months ago: "There's going to be casualties." On National Signing Day 2026, that warning has arrived. The Tenth Circuit's November 6 reversal of the 5-for-5 nationwide injunction is reaching Class of 2027 recruits in the most direct way possible — through de-commits and pulled scholarship offers on the day those recruits expected to sign.

What the Rule Does

The age-based 5-for-5 eligibility model, passed by the Division I Cabinet in June 2026, allows all Division I student-athletes up to five years of competition, provided they enroll in college no later than the academic year after their 19th birthday. The practical effect is a maximum roster size that many programs had been managing around — planning for some athletes to age out or exhaust eligibility and open scholarship slots.

When a preliminary injunction allowed Class of 2022 athletes to seek a potential fifth year, programs faced an unexpected roster crunch. Some already gave commitments to incoming recruits under the assumption that those extra spots would open. When the Tenth Circuit reversed the injunction on November 6, those spots reopened — but only after some programs had already adjusted their planning in ways that left incoming recruits exposed.

Memphis and the Cassidy Ott Case

The most visible casualty came from Memphis. Committed Class of 2027 recruit Cassidy Ott publicly announced on social media that she was "involuntarily de-committed from the University of Memphis due to the new 5-year eligibility rule." Ott's statement put a human name on a problem that had been playing out quietly across the country.

Memphis pulled offers from multiple committed recruits as the program navigated the roster math created by the rule change. The school was not alone — just the most publicly reported example of a broader trend that coaches had been warning about all fall.

The Coaches Who Saw It Coming

Lawson was not the only coach who anticipated this outcome. Multiple SEC head coaches raised concerns after the June rule passage that the combination of the age-based model and existing roster caps would force programs to choose between honoring obligations to current players and honoring commitments to incoming recruits. In some cases, incoming recruits lost.

The situation is complicated by what the federal ruling did not resolve. Approximately 200 state-court cases involving hundreds of athletes with potential eligibility claims under state law continue. Programs operating in states with active litigation still face some roster uncertainty, even though the federal injunction is gone.

What Recruits Should Know

For athletes who lost commitments before today, options remain. The transfer portal is active, and programs that created unexpected roster openings through de-commits may turn around and have spots to fill. The signing window runs through August 1, 2027, so there is time — but the initial Signing Day opportunity is gone for anyone who was de-committed.

For athletes who are signing today without incident, the landscape is as stable as it has been all fall. The federal ruling clarified the nationwide picture. What remains unresolved is the state-court litigation, which could affect individual athletes depending on jurisdiction.

What's Next

The state-court cases continue on their own timeline, with no coordinated resolution date. The next scheduled federal development is the Tenth Circuit's merits ruling on the full Wisne v. NCAA case, which follows the preliminary injunction reversal. Additional de-commits may surface in the coming days as programs finalize their 2027 roster math. For recruits who signed today without complication, the next step is arrival on campus next fall. For those affected by the roster squeeze, the portal and December contact window offer a path forward.
