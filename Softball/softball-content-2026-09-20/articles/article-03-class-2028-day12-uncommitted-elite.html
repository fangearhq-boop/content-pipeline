Class of 2028 Day 12: Three Dozen Locked In, Elite Names Still on the Board

By Jordan Reeves | I Love Softball | September 20, 2026

The September 9 in-person contact window is 12 days old and the commit wave has filled a remarkable number of roster spots. But the most coveted prospects in the Class of 2028 are still deciding.

The numbers are impressive. Three dozen committed in 12 days. A class record 1,226 Extra Innings Software nominations. The strongest opening window in recent memory for what projects to be one of the deeper recruiting classes in the sport's history.

And the most important decisions are still to come.

Day 12 of the September 9 in-person contact window finds the Class of 2028 at a pivotal inflection point. The early rushers have locked in their schools. The mid-tier has followed. But Michaela Landers, Kylie Holden, Sophia Schlader, and Joley Bennett — four of the most elite prospects in the class — remain uncommitted, and the programs chasing their signatures are fighting some of the hardest recruiting battles of this cycle.

The Committed List Takes Shape

The locked-in names represent the full geography and competitive spectrum of college softball's elite programs. Finlee Williams, the No. 1 overall prospect, committed to Oklahoma — continuing a legacy of top-ranked prospects choosing Norman under Patty Gasso. Peyton Ushery, ranked No. 2, chose Georgia. Paityn Donalson — a two-way star from Lakeland, FL with a .693 batting average, 11 home runs, and 50 RBI — is bound for Florida State.

The SEC has collected aggressively in the early window. Braylyn Saenz committed to Oklahoma. Sophia Galindo and Nevaeh Wilson are headed to Tennessee. Kate Losh and Ryann Harris will play together at Florida. Siena Myers chose South Carolina. The Big 12 added Star Gutierrez to Texas Tech. And Arizona landed the in-state pair of Ridling and Stell, solidifying a recruiting class that strengthens the Wildcats' southwestern pipeline.

Thirty-six commitments in 12 days is a pace that reflects the urgency the September 9 window creates. Coaches who spent the summer identifying targets made their in-person visits immediately when the calendar allowed — and the results followed quickly.

The Uncommitted Elite

What makes the next phase of the Class of 2028 window so compelling is the caliber of players still available. Michaela Landers, Kylie Holden, Sophia Schlader, and Joley Bennett are not players who slipped through the cracks of the early window — they are players who have been visited by every major program in the country and are taking their time to make the right decision.

Bennett, a five-star prospect, has drawn heavy interest from LSU and the SEC's top programs. Schlader is among the most recruited pitching prospects in the class. Landers and Holden represent the kind of impact players that reshape a program's ceiling when they arrive on campus.

The programs that land these four signatures will have made enormous upgrades to their 2028 class. The programs that don't will spend the back half of the window recalibrating.

What's Next

The in-person contact window opened September 9 and runs through the fall season. A first rankings update for the Class of 2028 is expected in mid-October — and by that point, several of the currently uncommitted elite will likely have made their decisions.

With 1,226 Extra Innings nominations — a record for this class — the depth extends well beyond the top 10 or top 20. Programs at every level of Division I are evaluating prospects who could develop into impact players by 2028. The commitment wave that has defined the first 12 days of the window is not slowing down.

The Class of 2028 is already elite. The next wave of commitments will determine whether it's historically elite.
