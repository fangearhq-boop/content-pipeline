Nine Days: Torres and Kavan Are Building What Three-Peat Bids Are Made Of

By Sarah Mitchell | I Love Softball | September 20, 2026

Texas is nine days from the Sept. 29 fall opener, and at the center of the back-to-back champions' preparation is a partnership that could define the most ambitious season in program history.

Nine days from now, McCombs Field in Austin hosts a fall opener that carries more weight than most regular-season games in this sport. Texas — the back-to-back Women's College World Series champions, 53-12 in 2026, winners of consecutive national titles — opens their fall schedule on September 29 against Murray State. Admission is free. The stakes are anything but.

At the center of what Mike White's staff is building this fall is the emerging partnership between Isa Torres and Hannah Kavan. Two players from two different programs, brought together in Austin by a shared mission: win a third consecutive national championship and cement Texas softball's place in the sport's historical record.

Torres: The Player of the Year Comes Home

Isa Torres arrived in Austin as the 2026 D1Softball Player of the Year — a Georgetown, TX native who spent her collegiate career at Florida State before transferring to the program closest to home with the biggest ambition.

Her 2026 numbers made her one of the most decorated hitters in college softball: .530 batting average, 16 home runs, 56 RBI. Those are numbers that belong in a conversation about the best offensive seasons in recent memory. And now they belong to a Texas team that already won the 2026 WCWS without her.

Torres' arrival adds a dimension to the Longhorns' offense that opposing pitchers will need new answers for. The question in fall camp is how quickly she builds the chemistry and comfort in Austin that will make that production translate to the biggest stages.

Kavan: The WCWS MOP Whose Next Challenge Is Unprecedented

No one in Women's College World Series history has done what Hannah Kavan has done. The senior pitcher is the only player ever to win the Most Outstanding Player award at the WCWS twice — and she accomplished it in back-to-back appearances.

The three-peat attempt is the most ambitious goal Kavan has faced in her collegiate career. A third national championship, added to a résumé that already includes two, would make her one of the most accomplished players in the sport's history. Mike White's staff has won the NFCA Coaching Staff of Year for the second consecutive season — evidence that the culture and planning that produced back-to-back titles is intact heading into Year 3 of this run.

Kavan's fall preparation is as much about sharpness as it is about conditioning. The September opener against Murray State is a first look — an opportunity to establish form, feel the competitive environment, and begin building toward May and June.

What's Next

Texas opens fall ball on September 29 at McCombs Field in Austin against Murray State — free admission, 6 PM CT. The fall schedule closes November 6 at Texas A&M's Davis Diamond. All home games at McCombs are free for the public.

The three-peat would place Texas alongside only two programs in the sport's history: UCLA, which won consecutive national titles from 1988 to 1990, and Oklahoma, which claimed three straight from 2021 to 2023. Both accomplishments stood for years before they were matched. Texas is nine days from the start of a fall schedule that will either confirm or complicate that ambition.

Nine days. Torres. Kavan. McCombs. The road to history starts there.
