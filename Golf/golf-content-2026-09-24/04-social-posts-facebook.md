### STORY 2: LIV Golf BC Partners $300M Restructuring

**FB Long-Form (10:30 AM ET)**
LIV Golf filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on September 8 — and now the details of its potential rescue are coming into focus.

London-based private equity firm BC Partners has proposed a $300 million investment to bring LIV Golf through restructuring and re-emerge as a new entity in early 2027. The striking part of the deal: under the BC Partners plan, players would become majority owners of the reorganized tour.

But the deal isn't done. It's conditional. A "requisite number of players" — specifically, holders of two-thirds of the total player claims by dollar amount AND one-half by number — must formally commit to the restructured tour within 35 days of the September 8 bankruptcy filing. That deadline falls in mid-October, approximately 19 days from now.

LIV's liabilities were estimated at between $500 million and $1 billion at the time of filing. The PGA Tour has explicitly said it is not currently considering a returning member programme. The players who turned down the January 2026 window — Jon Rahm, Bryson DeChambeau, Cameron Smith — now face a starkly different question: do they commit to a player-owned LIV that might survive, or risk being between tours if the restructuring falls apart?

### STORY 3: International Team at Medinah — The Houdini Moment

**FB Long-Form (1:00 PM ET)**
The International Presidents Cup team has been trying to break a 28-year losing streak. Today, they play at a course that knows something about impossible comebacks.

Medinah Country Club hosted the 2012 Ryder Cup — the event where Europe came back from a 10-6 deficit heading into Sunday singles to stun the United States and win 14.5-13.5. It is one of the most dramatic sporting reversals in golf history, and it happened on this same course.

The International team, captained by Geoff Ogilvy and featuring Hideki Matsuyama, Tom Kim, Min Woo Lee, Adam Scott, and Ryo Hisatsune, has never won a Presidents Cup in the modern era. Their 1998 win at Royal Melbourne remains the only time the non-US team has prevailed, and since then they've lost 12 consecutive editions against an increasingly dominant American squad.

History isn't on their side. The venue, though, carries its own mythology. Europe's miracle on this same grass in 2012 showed what golf can produce when a team refuses to accept the narrative written for them. If there's ever a moment for the International team to author their own version of that story, it might be here.

### STORY 5: LIV Players' Choice — Commit or Face Uncertainty

**FB Long-Form (8:30 PM ET)**
Jon Rahm, Bryson DeChambeau, and Cameron Smith turned down the PGA Tour's January 2026 offer. Now LIV Golf itself is in bankruptcy — and they have roughly 19 days to decide whether to commit to its restructured version.

The BC Partners private equity deal would make LIV players majority owners of a reorganized tour set to emerge in early 2027. It's a compelling offer in some ways: ownership stake, reduced Saudi control, and a fresh institutional backer. But it requires player buy-in by mid-October to survive.

The PGA Tour has closed the door. CEO Brian Rolapp confirmed a returning member programme is "not currently contemplated." Brooks Koepka remains the only player who accepted the January window — and he paid $5 million to charity and forfeited five years of equity to do so.

So where does that leave Rahm, DeChambeau, and Smith? They're eligible for the PGA Tour's major-winners' programme in theory, but the door is no longer open. They're signed LIV players in a tour that is restructuring. And they have about three weeks to formally commit to whatever LIV becomes next.
