Rahm, DeChambeau, Smith: Commit to Restructured LIV or Face the Unknown — 19 Days to Decide

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | September 24, 2026

In January 2026, the PGA Tour offered Jon Rahm, Bryson DeChambeau, and Cameron Smith the chance to come back. Each of them said no. Brooks Koepka said yes, paid $5 million to charity, and forfeited five years of equity. The others calculated that LIV Golf's future was worth the choice to stay.

Two months later, LIV Golf filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy with between $500 million and $1 billion in liabilities. Now the calculation looks very different — and Rahm, DeChambeau, and Smith have approximately 19 days to make another decision.

What the BC Partners Deal Means for Players

London-based private equity firm BC Partners has proposed a $300 million investment to restructure LIV Golf into a player-majority-owned entity. Under the plan, the players who commit become the owners. They would have institutional backing, reduced Saudi Public Investment Fund control, and a tour positioned to re-emerge in early 2027.

The deal requires formal commitment from players holding at least two-thirds of total player claims by dollar amount and at least half by number of players — all within 35 days of the September 8 bankruptcy filing. That clock expires in mid-October.

The PGA Tour Door Is Closed

This is the part that changes the math from January. PGA Tour CEO Brian Rolapp confirmed in mid-September that a returning member programme is "not currently contemplated." Koepka's path — major winners accepting accountability and paying back into the ecosystem — was the window. It's closed now.

Rahm, eligible via his 2023 Masters and 2023 U.S. Open victories, and DeChambeau, eligible via his 2024 U.S. Open win, could theoretically still pursue reinstatement discussions. But the Tour's posture suggests that path is uncertain and unlikely in the near term.

What's Next

Players must weigh three paths: commit to the BC Partners restructuring and bet on LIV's revival, decline and hope another path opens, or negotiate something outside either framework. The mid-October deadline is real. If the requisite commitment threshold isn't met, the $300 million deal fails and LIV Golf's future becomes far more uncertain. Watch for public statements from top LIV players in the next two weeks as the deadline approaches.
