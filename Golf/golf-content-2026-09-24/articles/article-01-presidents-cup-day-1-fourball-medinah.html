Presidents Cup Day 1: Five Fourball Matches Kick Off the Week at Medinah

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | September 24, 2026

The 2026 Presidents Cup opened Thursday at Medinah No. 3 with five fourball matches, launching a four-day competition that the International team hasn't won since 1998 — and that the United States has dominated through 12 consecutive victories.

Five points are available on Day 1. In the history of the Presidents Cup, early momentum has rarely determined the final result in isolation, but setting the tone matters, particularly for an International team that desperately needs to build confidence against an American side that has made this competition look routine.

The Matchups

Captain Brandt Snedeker chose to lead off with the world's No. 1 player, Scottie Scheffler, partnering Sam Burns against Sungjae Im and Min Woo Lee. It's a signal of intent: send your best early and set the tone.

Cameron Young and Xander Schauffele face Ryo Hisatsune and Hideki Matsuyama — a matchup that pits two of the hottest American players against the International side's Japanese core. Matsuyama, a 2021 Masters champion, is one of the International team's most reliable performers.

Justin Thomas pairs with captain's pick Jackson Koivun against Corey Conners and Nico Echavarria. Wyndham Clark and Collin Morikawa — a pairing of two technical players who thrive in match play conditions — take on Ryan Fox and Adam Scott. The session closes with Patrick Cantlay and Chris Gotterup against Tom Kim and Si Woo Kim.

The International Team's Challenge

The United States leads the all-time Presidents Cup series 13-2. Since the International team's victory at Royal Melbourne in 1998, they have lost 12 consecutive editions to American squads that have outmatched them in roster depth nearly every time.

The 2026 International team, captained by Geoff Ogilvy, carries real talent: Matsuyama, Tom Kim, Min Woo Lee, Adam Scott, and Ryo Hisatsune are all capable of beating any American player on a given day. The question has never been individual quality. It's been finding a way to beat a roster that goes ten deep with major-caliber players.

What's Next

Day 2 features foursomes on Friday. The overall match runs through Sunday. The International team has won this competition before — 1998 showed it's possible. Whether Medinah in 2026 becomes their venue, or whether it becomes another chapter in the United States' long run of dominance, will be determined hole by hole across four days.
