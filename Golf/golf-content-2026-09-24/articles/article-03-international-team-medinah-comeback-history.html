The International Team Is Searching for Their Medinah Miracle — This Course Has Done It Before

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | September 24, 2026

In 2012, the United States team walked into Sunday at the Ryder Cup at Medinah Country Club with a 10-6 lead. They needed just 4.5 points from 12 singles matches to win. Europe needed 8.5. The number was astronomical. The comeback was real.

Europe won 8.5-3.5 in singles. They won 14.5-13.5. Medinah had produced one of the greatest moments in international golf history — and the venue's DNA remembers it.

The International Presidents Cup team arrived at the same course on Thursday carrying 28 years of losing and 12 consecutive defeats. Whether the venue's comeback mythology transfers across team competitions and across a decade is something only four days of golf can answer.

This International Team Is Different

Captain Geoff Ogilvy, the 2006 U.S. Open champion, assembled a roster that is genuinely talented rather than simply competitive. Hideki Matsuyama, the 2021 Masters champion, is one of the best players in the world. Tom Kim has been one of the most electric young players on tour. Min Woo Lee, Ryo Hisatsune, Sungjae Im, Adam Scott, Corey Conners, Nico Echavarria, Ryan Fox, and Si Woo Kim round out a group that has legitimate weapons at every level of the draw.

The International team has never been incapable of beating American players. In individual matches, they win often enough. The problem has always been doing it consistently enough, over four days, to accumulate the points that win the Cup.

The History They're Chasing

The International team won the only Presidents Cup they have ever won in 1998 at Royal Melbourne. The score was 20.5-11.5 — not a narrow victory. It was comprehensive. Since then, 12 editions have come and gone, and the American machine has improved with each iteration as the U.S. player development pipeline has accelerated.

What changed in 2012 at the Ryder Cup was a team that believed despite the numbers. Europe's captain, José María Olazábal, kept faith when the math said otherwise. The International team needs the same belief.

What's Next

Day 1 points set the psychology for the rest of the week. Every International win on Thursday matters not just for the scoreboard but for the confidence that tomorrow is possible. The venue knows what an improbable comeback looks like. It happened right here fourteen years ago. The International team is hoping the ground remembers.
