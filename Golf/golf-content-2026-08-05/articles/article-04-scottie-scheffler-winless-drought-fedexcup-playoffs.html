Scottie Scheffler's Winless Drought: World No. 1 Hunting a Breakthrough Before the Playoffs

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | August 5, 2026

The numbers tell a story that almost defies comprehension. Scottie Scheffler is ranked No. 1 in the world. He leads the FedExCup standings heading into the playoffs. He has 20 PGA Tour victories on his résumé — a number that, for most players, would represent an entire career's worth of success.

He has not won since January.

Seven months. Five runner-up finishes. A consistency record that would make most professionals envious. And yet, by the sport's most fundamental measure — victories — Scheffler has been on an extended drought that nobody expected would last this long.

The Last Win: American Express, January 2026

Scheffler's 20th career PGA Tour victory came at the American Express on the Stadium Course at PGA West in January. He won by four shots, finishing at 27-under par — a dominant performance that looked at the time like the beginning of another high-volume win season for the world's best player.

It has not worked out that way. Since that victory in the California desert, Scheffler has contended regularly, closed well, and finished second more often than anyone would expect from a player of his caliber. Five runner-up finishes, including one at the 3M Open behind Michael Kim's historic 59 — the round that became one of the year's most-discussed individual performances.

The drought has not been for lack of trying. Scheffler has been in contention. He simply has not been able to complete the final push from second to first.

Still No. 1 — In Everything But Wins

Here is what makes Scheffler's situation genuinely unusual: the winless stretch has not dented his rankings position or his FedExCup standing. He sits atop the FedExCup points list heading into the playoffs, which is a testament to how consistently good he has been even without reaching the winner's circle.

The runner-up finishes accumulate points. The consistent top-10 performances accumulate points. Scheffler has been so reliably excellent at every step below victory that he has remained the FedExCup leader despite the drought. But professional golfers are measured by wins, and the sport's narrative machinery notices when the world's best player goes months without one.

He skipped The Open Championship at Royal Birkdale — a decision that drew comment — and Ryan Fox claimed the Claret Jug in his absence. Rory McIlroy beat Scheffler by one at the 2026 Masters in what was a major finish for the ages. These near-misses and absences have layered onto the drought narrative and given it texture.

Playoffs Provide the Stage

Scheffler has committed to the FedEx St. Jude Championship beginning August 13 at TPC Southwind in Memphis — the first leg of the FedExCup Playoffs. If the drought ends during the playoffs, the narrative writes itself.

The FedExCup Playoffs format adds a dimension that regular-season events cannot replicate. At East Lake for the TOUR Championship, the staggered starting strokes format means that Scheffler — as the FedExCup leader — would enter at -10, a meaningful advantage over the field. If he plays to his ability over three weeks, the combination of FedExCup seeding and his form could produce a playoff win that doubles as a season statement.

Scheffler droughts do not historically last. He has won four times in a single PGA Tour season before. This one, at seven months, already feels unusual by the standards of his own career. The question the playoff stretch will answer is whether the drought ends with a bang — a title and a FedExCup championship — or whether 2026 ends as the year Scheffler was the best but someone else won.

What's Next

Scheffler did not enter the Wyndham Championship, so the regular season ends without a final chance at win No. 21 before the playoffs. His first opportunity comes August 13 at TPC Southwind in Memphis.

He will enter as the FedExCup leader, with all the strokes-ahead seeding advantage that comes with it. The world's best player in a three-week playoff format is a formidable combination. If it goes as the standings suggest it should, the drought ends in Atlanta at East Lake.

But seven months of runner-up finishes is a reminder that golf does not always go as the standings suggest.
