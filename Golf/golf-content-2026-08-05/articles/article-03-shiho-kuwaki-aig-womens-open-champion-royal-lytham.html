Shiho Kuwaki Wins AIG Women's Open: Japan's New Major Champion at Royal Lytham

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | August 5, 2026

On August 2, at Royal Lytham & St Annes on England's Lancashire coast, Shiho Kuwaki became a major champion.

The Japanese golfer defeated Germany's Esther Henseleit in a two-hole playoff to win the AIG Women's Open — golf's final women's major of 2026, and the 50th staging of the championship. It was Kuwaki's first LPGA Tour title. Her first major. A career-defining moment claimed at one of the most historic venues in women's golf.

How the Playoff Unfolded

Both Kuwaki and Henseleit finished regulation tied at 5-under-par 279. Kuwaki had fired a final-round 70 to stay in contention after entering Sunday in the mix. Henseleit, the German star looking for her second major championship, matched her stride for stride through the final holes of regulation and pushed the championship to extra holes.

Over the subsequent two playoff holes, Kuwaki proved the steadier competitor. She closed out Henseleit to claim the trophy, the title, and the distinction of being Japan's newest major champion. The moment added to an already compelling 2026 narrative across women's golf — a year that has produced first-time major champions and storylines across every major on the calendar.

The Overnight Leader Falls Back

Yealimi Noh had entered Sunday as the overnight leader, carrying the momentum of her week's best golf into the final round. She shot a 74 and finished third — a painful reminder of how unforgiving major championship Sundays can be. Noh will carry the memory of a lead surrendered at a major, motivation that tends to sharpen elite players in future major fields.

Nelly Korda and Jeeno Thitikul, ranked No. 1 and No. 2 in the world respectively, tied for fourth. For both players, a major remains elusive in 2026 — a detail that underscores how wide open the women's game has become at the major level. The world's two best players arrived at Royal Lytham and still could not close the gap on a first-time champion.

Historic Prize Money

The AIG Women's Open paid out from a championship-record $10 million purse. Kuwaki collected $1.5 million as champion. Henseleit received $994,669 as runner-up. The prize money represents a continuation of the dramatic increase in women's golf purses over recent years and speaks to the commercial trajectory of the LPGA's most important events.

The $10 million purse at the AIG Women's Open places it among the highest-paying tournaments on the women's tour and signals a continued investment in the growth of the women's game at the major championship level.

The 50th Staging

The 2026 AIG Women's Open marked the 50th edition of the championship — a milestone that brought additional weight and ceremony to the week at Royal Lytham. The championship has grown considerably since its founding, from a national invitation event to a globally competitive major drawing the world's best players from the LPGA Tour, LET, and beyond.

Kuwaki's win adds Japan to the list of nations celebrating women's major champions in 2026, alongside the expanding roster of countries whose players are competing and winning at the highest levels of the game. Her breakthrough is both a personal achievement and a moment for Japanese golf broadly.

What's Next

Shiho Kuwaki departs Royal Lytham as the AIG Women's Open champion, with $1.5 million in earnings and the kind of confidence that comes from winning a major. How she carries that momentum through the remainder of the LPGA season will be one of the storylines worth tracking.

Esther Henseleit will reset and refocus after coming one hole short of a second major. Nelly Korda and Jeeno Thitikul, still searching for 2026 major victories, turn their attention to the rest of the schedule.

The AIG Women's Open is complete. A new major champion has been crowned. Shiho Kuwaki will not be a footnote — she is a champion.
