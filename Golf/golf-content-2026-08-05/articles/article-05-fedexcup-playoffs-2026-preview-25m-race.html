FedExCup Playoffs 2026 Preview: $25M Race Begins in One Week

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | August 5, 2026

One week from Thursday, the PGA Tour regular season is over and the FedExCup Playoffs begin. The three-event, three-week playoff sequence is the sport's most significant annual postseason structure and culminates in a $25 million bonus to the FedExCup champion. Everything that has happened since January — every round, every finish, every point accumulated — has been building toward this.

Here is everything you need to know.

The Schedule

The FedExCup Playoffs unfold across three consecutive weeks at three different venues:

FedEx St. Jude Championship — August 13-16
TPC Southwind, Memphis, Tennessee. The top 70 players in FedExCup points after the Wyndham Championship advance to Memphis. After the St. Jude Championship, the top 50 advance.

BMW Championship — August 20-23
Bellerive Country Club, St. Louis, Missouri. The top 50 from Memphis compete. After the BMW Championship, the top 30 advance to the season finale.

TOUR Championship — August 27-30
East Lake Golf Club, Atlanta, Georgia. The top 30 players in the FedExCup standings compete for the title and the $25 million bonus.

The Format at East Lake

The TOUR Championship at East Lake does not begin with every player at even par. The FedExCup standings determine a staggered starting position that rewards season-long consistency.

The player entering East Lake ranked No. 1 in FedExCup points begins the tournament at -10. Each subsequent position steps down proportionally, with the No. 30 seed starting furthest behind. A player who barely qualified in 30th position can still win the FedExCup title if they shoot low enough over 72 holes — but the math becomes significantly harder with every position further back.

This format means the standings entering Atlanta matter enormously. The No. 1 seed — currently Scottie Scheffler — holds a meaningful structural advantage simply from their regular-season performance. It also means that a player who finishes fourth in the TOUR Championship on the golf scorecard might be the FedExCup champion when the starting strokes are factored in.

The Current Standings Picture

Scottie Scheffler leads the FedExCup standings as the regular season approaches its end. He has not won a tournament since January but has been so consistently excellent in top-10 finishes and runner-up positions that he remains atop the points table. If the Wyndham Championship this week does not dramatically shake the standings, Scheffler will enter Memphis as the FedExCup points leader — the position that carries the most strategic value when East Lake arrives.

The Wyndham Championship matters most for the bubble — the players jostling around positions No. 65 through No. 75 — where one strong finish or one missed cut can be the difference between a season that continues and a season that ends this weekend.

Why the Playoffs Matter

The FedExCup Playoffs represent a different gear of golf. The fields are smaller, the stakes are higher, and the format — three weeks of progressive elimination — creates a postseason intensity that regular-season events simply cannot replicate.

Memphis at TPC Southwind is a traditional stroke-play event where the field thins. St. Louis at Bellerive is a second-round pressure test with 50 of the world's best competing. Atlanta at East Lake is the closest thing professional golf has to a championship final — 30 players, staggered starts, $25 million on the line.

For casual fans, the playoffs are also the most accessible entry point into caring about PGA Tour golf. The stakes are clear, the structure is simple, and the best players in the world are competing in consecutive weeks. It is three weeks of must-watch content for anyone who wants to understand what the top of men's professional golf looks like under pressure.

What's Next

The Wyndham Championship this week at Sedgefield Country Club provides the final data point — final adjustments to the standings, final bubble drama, final chances for players to improve their position before Memphis.

Then, on August 13, the FedExCup Playoffs begin. Three events. Three weeks. The best 70 players in men's professional golf reduced to 50, then to 30, then to one champion and a $25 million check.

The race starts in Memphis. It ends in Atlanta. Buckle up.
