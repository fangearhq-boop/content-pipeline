### STORY 3: Shiho Kuwaki Wins AIG Women's Open — Japan's First Major Champion of 2026

**Long-Form Post (1:00 PM ET):**
Japan's Shiho Kuwaki is a major champion.

On August 2, at Royal Lytham & St Annes, Kuwaki defeated Germany's Esther Henseleit in a two-hole playoff to win the AIG Women's Open — golf's final women's major of the year, at the tournament's 50th staging.

Both players finished regulation tied at 5-under 279. Kuwaki had fired a final-round 70. Henseleit, looking for her second major title, pushed the champion to extra holes before Kuwaki closed it out. First LPGA Tour title. First major. Career-defining.

The prize money is historic. Kuwaki collected $1.5 million from a championship-record $10 million purse. Runner-up Henseleit received $994,669. The world's top women's players also competed: Nelly Korda and Jeeno Thitikul, ranked No. 1 and No. 2 in the world, tied for fourth. The overnight leader Yealimi Noh, who had led entering the final round, shot a 74 and finished third.

Kuwaki's win adds Japan to the list of nations celebrating major champions in 2026 alongside New Zealand (Ryan Fox, The Open Championship) and multiple nations in the men's game.

### STORY 4: Scottie Scheffler's Winless Drought — Zero Wins Since January for the World's Best Player

**Long-Form Post (3:30 PM ET):**
Scottie Scheffler is the No. 1 player in the world. He is No. 1 in FedExCup standings. He has 20 PGA Tour victories.

He has not won a tournament since January.

Scheffler's last win was the American Express, a desert event on the Stadium Course at PGA West that he dominated by four shots at 27-under. That was his 20th career title. Since then, he has posted five runner-up finishes — including one at the 3M Open behind Michael Kim's historic 59 round — without breaking through for a 21st.

On paper, this is absurd. The world's best player, in arguably the best stretch of his career in terms of consistency, cannot quite get back to the winner's circle. He is still accumulating points. He is still FedExCup No. 1 heading into the playoffs. He will still be the favorite at East Lake when the TOUR Championship comes around at the end of August.

But professional golfers are measured by wins. And seven months without one — for a player who has won four times in a single season before — is a gap that does not go unnoticed.

The FedExCup Playoffs begin on August 13. Scheffler has committed to playing. If the drought ends in Memphis or St. Louis or Atlanta, the narrative writes itself.

**Long-Form Post (6:00 PM ET):**
One week from Thursday, the FedExCup Playoffs begin. Here is exactly what you need to know.

**The schedule:**
- FedEx St. Jude Championship — August 13-16, TPC Southwind, Memphis, TN (top 70 in FedExCup points advance)
- BMW Championship — August 20-23, Bellerive Country Club, St. Louis, MO (top 50 advance from Memphis)

This means a player who finishes No. 30 in Memphis can still win the FedExCup if they shoot low enough — but the math gets harder with every position you start behind.
