A Lim Kim's Hole-in-One Headlines 64, Builds 3-Shot Lead at CME Tour Championship

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | November 21, 2026

Golf has a way of delivering the perfect moment at the perfect time, and at the CME Group Tour Championship on Friday, A Lim Kim delivered exactly that. The South Korean star hit a 7-iron from 168 yards into the par-3 16th hole at Tiburón Golf Club in Naples, Florida, for a hole-in-one — the kind of single shot that can flip the energy of a tournament instantly. Kim finished her second round at 7-under 64, moving to 15-under 129 and taking a three-shot lead over defending champion Jeeno Thitikul at the LPGA season finale.

The Shot That Defined Round 2

A hole-in-one is rare enough in professional golf that it carries automatic significance. When it happens during the second round of the richest event in women's golf history — a $11 million purse with $4 million to the winner — it carries added weight. Kim's 7-iron on No. 16 was precision executed under pressure on a course that demands nothing less.

Beyond the drama of the ace itself, Kim's 64 was a comprehensive display of the controlled, accurate golf that makes her such a consistent presence at the top of LPGA leaderboards. Her 65-64 start to this week puts her in an ideal position with two rounds to play.

Thitikul Still in Pursuit

Atthaya Thitikul — known as Jeeno — enters the weekend three shots behind Kim, but the defending champion's presence on the leaderboard cannot be overlooked. Thitikul won this tournament last year with a final score of 26-under, one of the most dominant performances in CME Group Tour Championship history. She knows this course, knows how to close, and has the talent to make up three shots over 36 holes.

Thitikul shot 67 in her second round to stay within range. For a player of her caliber, maintaining a scorecard within reach of a motivated champion is exactly the right position to be in heading into Saturday.

The Stakes

The CME Group Tour Championship carries the richest prize in women's golf history. The $11 million purse represents the LPGA's commitment to player compensation, and the $4 million winner's check is life-changing even for established professionals. For Kim — seeking a major statement finish to cap a consistent season — the financial and competitive stakes this weekend are as high as they come on the LPGA Tour.

Beyond the money, winning the season's final event at this scale would cement Kim's status among the LPGA's true elite. She has the lead. She has the momentum. She has the form.

What's Next

Third and fourth rounds of the CME Group Tour Championship take place over the weekend at Tiburón Golf Club. Kim carries a three-shot lead into Saturday, but Thitikul's presence and the compressed leaderboard behind mean every stroke on the final two days matters. Nelly Korda, the world's No. 1 player, is at 9-under and looking to charge. Can Kim hold the advantage she's built? After a hole-in-one and 64 in Round 2, the South Korean star is playing golf that makes the question feel somewhat rhetorical — but in sport, nothing is settled until the final putt drops.
