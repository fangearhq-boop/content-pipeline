Sergio Garcia's Ryder Cup Career Appears Over as LIV Golf Exit Filing Underscores Golf's Divide

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | October 3, 2026

Sergio Garcia wants out of LIV Golf. The question is whether getting out will give him back what he lost by joining.

The 46-year-old Spaniard filed a motion in New Jersey bankruptcy court this week asking for the explicit termination of his LIV Golf contract — not a rejection, but a termination. The legal distinction matters: a termination clearly establishes Garcia's status as a free agent, which is critical for a professional athlete trying to schedule tournaments and arrange sponsorships for the coming year.

His stated destination is the DP World Tour, which he left to join LIV in 2022. But what he may not be able to recover is the Ryder Cup — the competition that defined a significant portion of his career legacy.

The Filing and What It Means

LIV Golf filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on September 8, 2026, with liabilities of $500 million to $1 billion after Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund ended its financial commitment to the league. Garcia's filing argues that while his LIV contract remains technically in place, the legal uncertainty around it prevents tournament organizers, sponsors, and other partners from making arrangements with him — a real and immediate problem for a professional athlete who needs to plan months in advance.

Garcia plays for Fireballs GC in LIV Golf. The filing also comes amid a related court filing from a LIV Golf Australia venue that says it has not been paid — another indication of how tangled LIV's financial situation has become.

The Ryder Cup Question

Garcia has played in 10 Ryder Cups for Europe, the most of any player in history, and he holds the record for most points ever accumulated in the event. He was a pillar of European teams from 1999 through 2023, the player European captains built around, the competitor who delivered on the biggest stages.

Since joining LIV in 2022, Garcia has been ineligible for European Ryder Cup consideration. That was a tradeoff he accepted — the LIV deal, reportedly worth nine figures, was substantial. But LIV's bankruptcy and the inability to return to DP World Tour through standard means has left Garcia in a situation where the Ryder Cup path may simply be closed.

SI.com reported this week that Garcia's Ryder Cup career "appears to be over." At 46, even if he returns to the DP World Tour in 2027, the timeline to rebuild ranking points and team standing before the 2027 Ryder Cup at Adare Manor in Ireland is extremely tight.

A Career at a Crossroads

Garcia won the 2017 Masters — his only major championship — and has been one of the most talented and recognizable golfers of his generation. His LIV chapter was financially lucrative but competitively and reputationally complicated.

He reportedly believes LIV Golf will relaunch in some form, and he has expressed confidence publicly that the circuit will return. But the decision to file in bankruptcy court suggests that confidence has its limits — and that securing his own future cannot wait for the courts to resolve LIV's broader situation.

What's Next

Garcia is waiting for a bankruptcy judge to rule on his motion. If granted, he will be free to negotiate with the DP World Tour and other organizations for 2027 competition. The 2027 Ryder Cup at Adare Manor could theoretically still be in reach — but the math is difficult, and the window is narrow.

Whatever happens with his Ryder Cup legacy, Garcia is trying to play professional golf again. Sometimes that has to be enough.
