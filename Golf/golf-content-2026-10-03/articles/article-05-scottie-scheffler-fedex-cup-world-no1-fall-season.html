Scottie Scheffler: World No. 1, FedEx Cup Champion, and the Standard for the Fall Season

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | October 3, 2026

The FedEx Cup Fall season is here, and the question at the top of the world rankings is not who is No. 1 — it is how far back everyone else is.

Scottie Scheffler enters October as the PGA Tour's undisputed standard-bearer. He won the 2026 FedEx Cup, leads the tour in earnings at $30,937,524, and has posted three wins and 13 top-10 finishes across a regular season that confirmed what his results over the past three years have suggested: he is the best golfer in the world, and it is not particularly close.

The Official World Golf Ranking agrees. Scheffler is No. 1, a position he has held for more than 175 weeks across his career.

The Rankings Picture

Behind Scheffler, the rankings tell a story of a tour in generational transition.

Rory McIlroy holds at No. 2. The Northern Irishman remains one of the most accomplished players of his era — a four-time major champion who earned $11.5 million in 2026 — and he has shown no signs of stepping back from the game. But Scheffler's gap at the top has been significant enough that the conversation around the world No. 1 ranking rarely changes anymore.

Cameron Young sits at No. 3. Young, who has developed steadily since turning professional, has quietly become one of the best players on tour without always getting the credit that comes with flashier results. His rise to the top three of the world ranking is one of the quieter stories of the 2026 season.

What Scheffler's Season Looked Like

Three wins. Thirteen top-10 finishes. More than $30 million in earnings.

Those numbers put 2026 among the strongest seasons any PGA Tour player has put together in the modern era. Scheffler has won at least twice in each of the last four seasons and has now won the FedEx Cup to go with his major championship victories.

What makes his dominance notable is its consistency. He is not a player who wins one week and disappears. He contends constantly, finishes in the top 10 at a rate that is historically exceptional, and converts when he has the lead. That combination of volume and execution is what separates great players from generational ones.

The Fall Season Ahead

The FedEx Cup Fall events offer players a chance to build world ranking points, adjust standing on various lists, and set themselves up for 2027. For Scheffler, the fall is an opportunity to add to a season that is already exceptional. For McIlroy, Young, and the rest of the field, it is a chance to close the gap — at least on paper — heading into the winter.

The Bank of Utah Championship is already underway at Black Desert Resort in Utah, and subsequent fall events will unfold through the remainder of October and November. Scheffler is expected to pick his spots, as he typically does in the fall, targeting events where his game translates well to the course and conditions.

What's Next

The 2027 major schedule will be the real proving ground for Scheffler's continued dominance. The Masters, US Open, The Open Championship, and PGA Championship will reset the conversation, and McIlroy — with four majors to Scheffler's current total — remains the player most capable of making a legitimate chase.

For now, though, October belongs to the world No. 1. And in 2026, that means it belongs to Scottie Scheffler.
