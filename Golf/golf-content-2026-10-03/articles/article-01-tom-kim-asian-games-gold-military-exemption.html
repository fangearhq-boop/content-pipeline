Tom Kim Wins Asian Games Gold, Secures Military Exemption With Final-Round 64

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | October 3, 2026

Tom Kim played one of the defining rounds of his career Saturday — and the stakes went well beyond a trophy.

The 24-year-old South Korean fired a final-round 64, making five birdies and an eagle at Kasugai Country Club East Course in Japan, to win the individual gold medal at the 2026 Asian Games. His 18-under total gave him a three-stroke victory over a group of runners-up that included Kazuki Higa of Japan, Ding Wenyi of China, and Sadom Kaewkanjana of Thailand.

The win meant Kim would not have to serve in the South Korean military — at least not now.

The Military Service Rule

Under South Korean law, all able-bodied males are required to serve between 18 and 21 months in the military. Professional athletes who earn gold medals at the Olympics or Asian Games are typically granted exemptions, making certain international competitions high-stakes in a way that goes beyond rankings points or prize money.

For Kim, who travels from Presidents Cup events to Asian Games venues and back, the timing of this tournament was everything. A gold medal meant staying on tour through the peak years of his career. A lesser result could have meant a lengthy absence at exactly the wrong moment in his development.

He got the gold.

A Tiger Woods Message and a 64

Before teeing off in the final round, Kim received an inspirational message from Tiger Woods. Kim did not elaborate publicly on the specific contents, but whatever Woods told him, Kim took it to heart.

He played the round without a bogey, converting a long eagle putt at a par-5 and making five birdies to go six-under on the day and build an insurmountable lead.

"It's a crazy feeling," Kim said after the round.

It is hard to argue with that assessment. Kim navigated one of the highest-pressure rounds in the modern game — not for a major championship, not for a FedEx Cup, but for years of his professional life — and he made it look routine.

A Career in Context

Tom Kim turned professional at 19 and became one of the fastest-rising players on the PGA Tour, recording multiple wins and top-25 finishes in the world ranking. At 24, he is widely regarded as one of the game's brightest young talents — and one of the players most likely to challenge for major championships in the coming decade.

That future was briefly in jeopardy. Military service at 24 or 25 would have meant returning to professional golf in his late 20s, older and potentially removed from the competitive rhythm that has made him so effective.

The gold medal removed that possibility. Kim will stay on tour and continue his career uninterrupted.

Fellow South Korean S.H. Kim also competed in the men's event, though Kim took the individual gold for himself.

What's Next

With the Asian Games wrapped, Tom Kim returns to the PGA Tour schedule for the fall season. The FedEx Cup Fall events are now underway, and Kim — now playing with the freedom that comes from having resolved the biggest uncertainty hanging over his career — is expected to be a consistent presence in the world top 25 through the end of the year.

For South Korean golf fans, October 3 will be a date to remember. For Tom Kim, it is the day he kept his career on track.
