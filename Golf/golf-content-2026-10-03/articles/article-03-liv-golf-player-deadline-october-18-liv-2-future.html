LIV Golf's October 18 Deadline: Players Have 15 Days to Decide on LIV 2.0

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | October 3, 2026

Fifteen days. That is how long LIV Golf players have to decide whether their future is in professional golf's most uncertain league — or somewhere else entirely.

October 18 is the deadline for players to confirm whether they intend to participate in LIV 2.0, a proposed relaunched version of the circuit backed by London-based private equity firm BC Partners. The pitch: 10 events in 2027, split between international stops and US venues, timed around the four major championships.

Whether those 10 events actually happen depends, in large part, on who says yes.

How LIV Got Here

LIV Golf filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on September 8, 2026, in a New Jersey court, reporting liabilities of $500 million to $1 billion against assets of $100 million to $500 million. The filing came after Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, which had poured roughly $6 billion into the circuit since its 2022 launch, declared LIV Golf "no longer consistent" with its investment strategy.

The season ended without its planned finale in Michigan, which was cancelled after the bankruptcy filing. The 2026 LIV individual championship went to Jon Rahm, who leads the final standings at 1,057.54 points, ahead of Bryson DeChambeau at 788.70 and Joaquin Niemann at 565.19.

What LIV 2.0 Looks Like

BC Partners, a London-based credit firm, has been in negotiations with LIV since the PIF announced its exit. The proposed structure for 2027 would include five international events and five US stops — a dramatically scaled-back schedule compared to the 14-event season that PIF was funding.

Players would own a stake in the new venture — reportedly around 52.5 percent — in what LIV has positioned as a players-first model. Whether that ownership structure survives the bankruptcy proceedings and any investor requirements remains unclear.

The Rahm and DeChambeau Factor

The two names that matter most when it comes to investor confidence are Jon Rahm and Bryson DeChambeau.

Rahm, a two-time major champion (Masters, US Open) and the 2026 LIV individual champion, and DeChambeau, a two-time US Open winner, are LIV's highest-profile attractions. Reports indicate both are linked to potential departures from the league — and losing either one would significantly weaken any pitch to BC Partners or future investors.

LIV stars have also been warned publicly that a return to the PGA Tour in 2027 is "remote," which could factor into some players' calculations about LIV 2.0 as the only viable professional alternative.

What's Next

October 18 will provide the first concrete indication of how many players are willing to commit to the new structure. If Rahm and DeChambeau stay, LIV 2.0 has a credible foundation. If they leave, the calculus for every other player changes significantly.

For fans of the LIV players, this two-week window is the most important stretch of the off-season. The choices made before October 18 will shape where these golfers compete — or whether they compete at all — in 2027.
