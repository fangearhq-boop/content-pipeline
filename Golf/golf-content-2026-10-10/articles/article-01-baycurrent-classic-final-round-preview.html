Baycurrent Classic Final Round Preview — Brennan, Bauchou, Bridgeman Share the Lead at -13

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | October 10, 2026

Sunday at the Baycurrent Classic is set up to be one of the most compelling final rounds of the PGA Tour's fall schedule. Three players share the lead at -13 after 54 holes, with a five-man group at -12 — including former world No. 1 Justin Thomas and veteran Adam Scott — positioned to pounce on any stumble from the front.

Brennan, Bauchou, and Jacob Bridgeman enter Sunday in a three-way tie, separated by nothing except what they do on the course today. It is a leaderboard that rewards boldness and punishes mistakes: in this kind of traffic, the winner will need to play well enough to pull away, not just wait for others to fall back.

The Leaders

Of the three players at the top, Bridgeman is the name that most captures the imagination. The young PGA Tour player has shown flashes of brilliance across his early career, and a co-lead heading into a final round on a week with an $8 million purse is the clearest opportunity he has had to make his mark. Whether he has the experience to close it out under pressure is the question he answers today.

Brennan and Bauchou are both capable of holding the lead. A three-way tie gives each player less margin than a sole lead, but the same opportunity: shoot a low number, and the tournament is yours.

The Five-Man Threat at -12

The quintet one shot back reads like a Sunday leaderboard assembled for maximum pressure. Justin Thomas has won majors and knows what closing round golf feels like. Adam Scott has a Masters to his name and the kind of ball-striking that holds up when it matters. Keith Mitchell, Sebastian Jaeger, and J. Smith complete a group that collectively understands how to construct a round.

One shot is nothing in Sunday conditions. A birdie from any of the five at the first hole they play, while the leaders hold steady or bogey, changes the dynamic entirely. This final round will require the leaders to stay aggressive rather than protective.
