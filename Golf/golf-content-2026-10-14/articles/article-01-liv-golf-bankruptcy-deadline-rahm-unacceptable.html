LIV Golf's Oct. 25 Deadline: Rahm Calls Terms "Unacceptable," Garcia Is Free, and the League Faces Its Reckoning

By Ryan Calloway | October 14, 2026 | Golf Fanrecap

LIV Golf has formally filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy as part of a restructuring agreement with BC Partners. Players now have until October 25 to decide whether they are committing to "LIV 2.0" — or leaving. Eleven days. A simple yes or no. And the man whose signing was supposed to define the league's legitimacy has already given his answer.

Jon Rahm, LIV Golf's three-time consecutive individual champion, has publicly called the proposed LIV 2.0 restructuring terms "unacceptable." He will not participate. The golfer whose move from the PGA Tour to LIV was the most discussed transaction in the sport's recent history is now, as of this week, on his way out.

The Bankruptcy and What It Means

BC Partners, the private equity firm attempting to restructure the league, has committed an initial $4 million of a possible $300 million total investment. Bankruptcy hearings are scheduled for October 22 and November 5. A host venue for a planned LIV Golf Adelaide event has already sued the league, demanding confirmation of its 2027 tournament obligations by November 6.

The league says it is confident of reaching a "critical mass" of player commitments to LIV 2.0 before the October 25 deadline. Bankruptcy experts quoted in coverage of the proceedings have expressed skepticism.

Sergio Garcia has been officially released from his LIV Golf contract through the bankruptcy court. David Puig has also departed. Two players gone before the deadline has even arrived.

The Rival Tours Draw Their Lines

The DP World Tour's response has been direct. CEO Guy Kinnings announced that any player who commits to LIV 2.0 will not be eligible for DP World Tour membership. The European circuit, which spent years navigating its relationship with LIV Golf amid the Saudi-backed league's growth, has now made its position clear: choose.

The PGA Tour's position is equally unambiguous. Commissioner Brian Rolapp confirmed that the Tour has no current plan to offer LIV golfers a route back to PGA Tour membership. Players who left — and who commit to LIV 2.0 — are not being invited to return.

Reports suggest the DP World Tour is quietly negotiating 2027 playing opportunities for players who depart LIV, conditional on those players rejecting LIV 2.0. That creates a path for players on the fence: walk away from LIV 2.0 by the deadline and potentially land on the European circuit next season.

What Rahm's Exit Means

Rahm's rejection of LIV 2.0 is the single most newsworthy element of the deadline week for one specific reason: he was the face of LIV's most expensive and highest-profile acquisition. If even Rahm finds the terms unacceptable, the league's ability to present a compelling pitch to the players it most needs to retain becomes significantly harder.

Rahm won the 2026 LIV Golf individual title — his third consecutive — at the season that ended at Indianapolis in August. His individual championship was confirmed before the restructuring terms were unveiled. Legion XIII, the team he captained, won the 2026 team championship for the second consecutive year. None of that changes the math of an October 25 deadline that Rahm has already decided doesn't work for him.

What's Next

The October 25 player commitment deadline is the next hard marker. Bankruptcy hearings follow on October 22 and November 5. The Adelaide venue lawsuit has its own November 6 trigger date. Within the next three weeks, the question of whether LIV Golf survives in any form — and in what shape — will be substantially closer to an answer.
