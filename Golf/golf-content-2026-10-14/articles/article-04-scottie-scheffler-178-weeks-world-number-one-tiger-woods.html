178 Weeks and Counting: Scottie Scheffler and the Quiet Chase for Tiger's Record

By Ryan Calloway | October 14, 2026 | Golf Fanrecap

Scottie Scheffler entered the week of October 12, 2026, in his 178th consecutive week as the world's No. 1 ranked golfer. That is more than three years of uninterrupted dominance at the top of a ranking that measures every result, at every significant tournament, across every major tour worldwide. It is the longest consecutive streak at No. 1 in the sport right now — and it is 103 weeks short of Tiger Woods' record of 281.

One hundred and three weeks is roughly two years. For Scheffler to break the all-time consecutive record, he would need to remain world No. 1 continuously through at least the middle of 2028. Nothing in his recent form, his competitive schedule, or the ranking points gap between him and the rest of the field suggests that is impossible.

What the Numbers Say

World rankings are calculated on a rolling points system that rewards recent results at the most significant events. To stay No. 1 for 178 consecutive weeks, Scheffler has had to produce results consistent enough to keep his points total above everyone else's — through illness, course changes, rough stretches, and the inevitable tournaments where nothing goes right.

Sky Sports' rankings coverage entering this week noted that Scheffler's gap over Rory McIlroy at No. 2 is larger than McIlroy's gap over virtually any other player in the field. That cushion matters: a player can lose a handful of ranking points in a bad month and still protect the No. 1 position if the gap beneath them is wide enough.

The current world rankings for the week of October 11–12: No. 1 Scheffler, No. 2 McIlroy, No. 3 Matt Fitzpatrick, No. 4 Cameron Young, No. 5 Wyndham Clark.

Context for Tiger's Record

Tiger Woods' record of 281 consecutive weeks at No. 1 was set across the peak of the most dominant individual run in professional golf's history. Woods also holds the career record for total weeks at No. 1 by a significant margin. Scheffler's career total is approximately 212 weeks as of early October 2026, reflecting his rapid rise through the rankings after his breakout 2022 season.

The consecutive weeks record is a different kind of milestone from total weeks — it requires continuity rather than accumulation. Woods built his 281-week stretch across a period in which no one could credibly challenge him at the top of the rankings. Scheffler has produced a version of that same dominance, across a field that has included major winners and consistently elite performers at every level of the tour schedule.

What's Next

The PGA Tour's fall schedule continues with the Butterfield Bermuda Championship at Port Royal Golf Course in Bermuda, October 22–25. Scheffler's next significant ranking test will come at the major championship events in 2027, where the points at stake are highest and the competition most concentrated.
