Down Three With 12 Matches Left: How Jacob Bridgeman Won the Presidents Cup for America

By Marcus Cole | October 14, 2026 | Golf Fanrecap

The numbers entering Sunday singles at Medinah Country Club were not in the United States' favor. The score was USA 7.5, International 10.5. The Americans needed 9 points from 12 remaining matches just to win the Presidents Cup — a 3-point deficit with one session left to play. The International team, captained by Geoff Ogilvy, had done what the home side often cannot: it had seized control at a historically American venue.

What happened next was, according to Golf Channel's Brandel Chamblee, "the greatest" Sunday performance in Presidents Cup history. The United States won 9.5 of the 12 available singles points. The final score: USA 17, International 13. America's 11th consecutive Presidents Cup title.

How the Deficit Was Built

The narrative of the comeback cannot be told without the narrative of how the hole was dug. On Friday, in the Foursomes session, the International team swept the United States 5–0. A complete shutout in a team competition format, at a venue that historically favors the home side. Captain Brandt Snedeker's team went from Thursday's 3–2 four-ball lead to an overall 5–3 deficit after one of the most dominant single-session performances in the event's modern history.

Saturday's four-ball session went 2.5–1.5 to the United States. Not enough. Entering Sunday, the Americans trailed by three with one session remaining.

The Clinching Moment

Jacob Bridgeman was playing in his first Presidents Cup. The American rookie — who had most recently been part of a co-lead going into the final round of the Baycurrent Classic in Yokohama — carried a match against Min Woo Lee that stayed tight throughout. At the end, Bridgeman won 1-up. It was his first Presidents Cup point. It was also, as the results settled across the course, the clinching point. USA 17, International 13.

Russell Henley and Nick Taylor halved the final match. But by then, the trophy had already been decided.

The Record That Keeps Building

The United States' 11th consecutive Presidents Cup victory extended the most lopsided streak in the history of a team competition format. The overall record now stands at 14–1–1. The one loss was at Royal Melbourne in 1998. The tie was also on Australian soil, in 2003. Every Presidents Cup played in the United States, and every Presidents Cup played in the modern era of the sport, has ended with an American victory.

Snedeker's team's comeback from three points down entering the final session added another chapter to that history — and gave Bridgeman a moment he will not forget.

What's Next

The next Presidents Cup is scheduled for 2028. The Ryder Cup — the higher-profile team competition between the United States and Europe — takes place at Adare Manor in Limerick, Ireland, in September 2027.
