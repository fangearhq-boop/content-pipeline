Win on Sunday, Report to Medinah on Thursday: Jacob Bridgeman's Perfect Presidents Cup Arrival

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | September 21, 2026

Brandt Snedeker announced Jacob Bridgeman as one of his six Presidents Cup captain's picks on September 1. At the time, Bridgeman was a solid pick — a player whose form warranted the selection, but not one arriving with an undeniable announcement moment to silence any second-guessing.

He has that now.

Bridgeman closed out the 2026 Biltmore Championship on Sunday at 26-under par, winning by two shots over Ben James and banking $900,000. It is his second PGA Tour title of 2026. He is flying to Chicago this week. He reports to Medinah Country Club on Thursday.

The timing is flawless.

Why Momentum Matters in Team Golf

The Presidents Cup is not a 72-hole individual stroke play event where a player can grind through a rough stretch over four days and find their game by the weekend. Match play is immediate. One bad hole can lose a point. One cold stretch can cost the team a full session.

Players who enter team events in confident form carry something beyond technique — they carry a swagger in decision-making that makes the hard shots easier to commit to, the pressure putts less loaded. That intangible confidence is what separates a player who is physically ready from one who is ready.

Bridgeman is ready. He just proved it for four days in Asheville against a full PGA Tour field. That proof does not expire in the five days between the Biltmore's final round and Medinah's opening fourballs.

The Partnership Question

Snedeker now has a decision to make that most captains would love to have: which pairing best maximizes Bridgeman's hot hand on Day 1? Fourballs rewards aggressive scoring — and a player coming off a 26-under winning week has zero hesitation about going for pins.

A pairing with Scheffler — the world's best player — would give Snedeker two players capable of carrying a session on their own. A pairing with a streaky ball-striker who benefits from Bridgeman's putting pressure could create a different kind of dynamic. The options are good in a way they are not for every captain making decisions about six picks.

What's Next

Bridgeman packs for Chicago. The Presidents Cup begins Thursday. His arrival story — winning a PGA Tour event the week before and walking into Medinah on a career high — is one of the best individual narratives at this year's competition. Watch him in the Day 1 fourballs to see how Snedeker deploys him while the momentum is at its peak.
