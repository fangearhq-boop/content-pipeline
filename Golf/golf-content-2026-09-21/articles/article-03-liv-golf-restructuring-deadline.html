LIV Golf's October 13 Deadline: Players Hold the Tour's Fate in Their Hands

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | September 21, 2026

Twenty-two days. That is how long LIV Golf's players have to decide whether the tour they left the PGA Tour to join will have a future worth committing to.

On September 8, 2026, LIV Golf filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of New Jersey. The tour's estimated assets of between $100 million and $500 million were overwhelmed by liabilities in the $500 million to $1 billion range. After years of lavish signing fees and operational spending funded by Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, the math finally caught up.

But BC Partners — a London-based private equity firm — arrived with a lifeline. The question now is whether LIV's players will grab it.

The BC Partners Deal

Under the Restructuring Support Agreement, BC Partners would inject $300 million into the reorganized tour. The terms are significant: the new entity would be majority owned by players, creating an ownership structure that aligns the tour's long-term interests with the people actually competing on it.

BC Partners receives a 2% share of all revenue generated for seven years after the restructured tour reaches profitability, and the right to purchase one expansion team for $1 after two additional teams are added. Those terms are not particularly punishing for a $300 million commitment — they suggest BC Partners sees long-term value in what LIV can become if the restructuring succeeds.

The Saudi PIF, which has invested an estimated $6 billion in LIV since its founding, is providing $49.6 million in DIP (debtor-in-possession) financing to keep operations funded through the court process.

The Player Threshold

The deal only activates if a "requisite number" of players commit within 35 days of the September 8 petition date — a window that closes around October 13. The requirement: two-thirds of eligible player claims by dollar amount, and half of players by number, must sign on.

Jon Rahm is the tour's largest creditor, owed more than $100 million under his original signing contract. His decision — which has not been publicly confirmed — carries disproportionate weight in the "dollar amount" threshold. If Rahm's claims count toward the two-thirds requirement, his commitment (or refusal) could effectively decide the outcome regardless of what other players do.

The Alternative

If enough players do not commit, the restructuring fails and LIV Golf's future becomes deeply uncertain. The court process would then determine what happens to assets, contracts, and the four announced 2027 events in Saudi Arabia, Australia, South Africa, and Hong Kong.

For the players who built their careers around LIV's higher purses and team-based format, walking away from the restructuring is not consequence-free. Returning to the PGA Tour requires navigating reinstatement processes and waived grounds — and the relationship between LIV's players and the traditional tour remains complicated.

What's Next

October 13 is the date to watch. If the player commitment threshold is reached, BC Partners' $300 million moves forward and LIV Golf begins its path to a restructured 2027 schedule. If it is not reached, the sport's most disruptive experiment since the World Golf Championships faces an uncertain end. The players who took the money now decide what it was all worth.
