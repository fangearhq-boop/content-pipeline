Presidents Cup 2026: Everything You Need to Know Before Medinah Kicks Off Thursday

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | September 21, 2026

In three days, the best players in the world gather at Medinah Country Club in Illinois for the 16th Presidents Cup. If you haven't been paying attention to the rosters, the venue history, or the storylines that make this edition worth watching — here is everything you need.

The Venue

Medinah Country Club is one of American golf's most prestigious addresses. The club's Course No. 3 — the championship layout — has hosted three Ryder Cups: 1991, 1999, and 2012. That last one delivered one of the sport's most dramatic moments when Europe mounted a historic final-day comeback to stun the United States team in what became known as the Miracle at Medinah.

The course is tree-lined, long, and demands precision. Its greens are receptive to high approach shots but punishing of missed cuts. For a team competition where pressure compounds every decision, Medinah is exactly the kind of course that separates the steady from the spectacular.

The Rosters

The United States team, captained by Brandt Snedeker, lines up as follows:

Automatic qualifiers: Scottie Scheffler, Cameron Young, Wyndham Clark, Sam Burns, Russell Henley, Collin Morikawa.

Captain's picks: Xander Schauffele, Justin Thomas, Patrick Cantlay, Chris Gotterup, Jackson Koivun, Jacob Bridgeman (fresh off a Biltmore Championship win yesterday).

The International team, captained by former U.S. Open champion Geoff Ogilvy, features Si Woo Kim, Hideki Matsuyama, Ryan Fox, Tom Kim, Min Woo Lee, and Adam Scott as automatic qualifiers, rounded out by six captain's picks.

The Storyline: Jackson Koivun

Of all the players making their Presidents Cup debuts in 2026, none carries more intrigue than Jackson Koivun. The 21-year-old turned professional from Auburn University just months ago and earned a captain's pick after Snedeker identified him as one of the game's most compelling young talents.

Koivun has never competed in a major team event at this level. The Presidents Cup is his introduction to the particular pressure of match play at the top of the sport — where the crowd, the scoreboards, and the weight of national representation combine in ways that no individual event can replicate.

How Koivun handles that environment will be one of the most interesting things to watch across all four days at Medinah.

The History and Stakes

The United States has won 13 of 15 Presidents Cup editions since the event began in 1994. The International team's only wins came in 1998 (at Royal Melbourne) and in a tied match in 2003. America's dominance in this event is one of the most lopsided records in team sports.

Whether that history should bring comfort or complacency to the U.S. team is a question Snedeker has surely addressed in the team room. The International team arrives motivated to snap a streak. The Americans arrive with depth, experience, and a fresh winner in Bridgeman who just flew in from Asheville.

What's Next

The competition begins Thursday, September 24, with Day 1 Fourballs starting at 12:30 PM local time. NBC and Golf Channel will broadcast all four days. The event runs through Sunday, September 27.
