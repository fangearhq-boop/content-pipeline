Nelly Korda Returns at Walmart NW Arkansas — Can the World No. 1 Shake the Solheim Cup Loss?

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | September 21, 2026

Nelly Korda returns to LPGA Tour competition this week, and the Walmart NW Arkansas Championship could not arrive at a more interesting moment in her season.

The tournament runs September 25-27 at Pinnacle Country Club in Rogers, Arkansas — the venue's 20th anniversary hosting this event. The format is 54-hole stroke play with no cut, $3 million purse, 144-player field. For Korda, arriving as the world's No. 1 player and a two-major champion in 2026, it is the first individual tournament of consequence since the Solheim Cup.

And the Solheim Cup did not go well.

The Season So Far

By any individual measure, Korda's 2026 LPGA season has been exceptional. She won the Chevron Championship — the tour's season-opening major — and followed with a U.S. Women's Open title. Two majors in one year is a standard of performance that defines a player's career. Korda is not having a good season; she is having a historically significant one.

Then came the Solheim Cup at Bernardus Golf, and the United States fell 15-13 to Europe. The Americans entered Sunday singles tied at 8-8 and needed 6.5 points from 12 matches. They did not get there. Korda led a roster that included some of the best talent in the women's game and still came up short on the final afternoon.

Team losses carry a different weight than individual defeats. In individual events, a player controls the outcome entirely. In a team format, the final score reflects collective performance, and processing a loss where you played your part but the team still fell short requires a particular kind of mental reset.

The Arkansas Stage

The Walmart NW Arkansas Championship has a 20-year relationship with Pinnacle Country Club that gives the event a settled, familiar character. It is not one of the LPGA's majors or season-ending events, but $3 million in prize money and a no-cut format create genuine competitive stakes for the full 144-player field.

For Korda, this is precisely the kind of tournament where you channel disappointment into discipline. No big stage pressure, no championship trophy waiting at the finish line — just 54 holes of golf in front of an engaged Arkansas gallery. A clean week of ball-striking and putting can re-center a player coming off an emotionally complicated team event.

What to Watch

The best players in golf share a consistent trait: they do not let a bad result — individual or team — define the next event. Korda's response to the Solheim Cup loss will say something about where her head is as the 2026 LPGA season moves toward its final stretch. A dominant performance in Rogers would confirm that the individual excellence of the first half of her season remains fully intact.

What's Next

The Walmart NW Arkansas Championship begins September 25. Three rounds, no cut, $3 million on the line. Korda, at No. 1 in the world, is the player to beat — and the player whose week will be most closely watched for what it reveals about the Solheim Cup's impact.
