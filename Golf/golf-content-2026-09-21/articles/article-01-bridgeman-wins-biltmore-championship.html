Jacob Bridgeman Wins the Biltmore Championship — His Second Title in 2026 and Perfect Timing Before Medinah

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | September 21, 2026

Jacob Bridgeman could not have scripted a better week to win a golf tournament.

Bridgeman closed out the 2026 Biltmore Championship at The Cliffs at Walnut Cove in Asheville, North Carolina on Sunday with a final total of 26-under par — two strokes clear of Ben James — to claim the $900,000 winner's check and his second PGA Tour title of the 2026 season.

On Thursday, he reports to Medinah Country Club as a United States Presidents Cup captain's pick.

The Win

Bridgeman's performance at Walnut Cove was the work of a player firing on every cylinder. A 26-under total in a 72-hole event represents sustained excellence — not a hot weekend scramble, but four consistent rounds of quality golf against a competitive field. His two-shot margin over James gave the final leaderboard a definitive shape: Bridgeman was the best player at this tournament, and it wasn't particularly close at the top.

The win is his second of the 2026 PGA Tour season, making him one of a small group of multiple winners this year. Multiple wins in a single season is the benchmark that separates solid tour players from players having great years. Bridgeman is now clearly in the latter category.

The Captain's Vindication

When U.S. Presidents Cup captain Brandt Snedeker announced his six captain's picks on September 1, Bridgeman was on the list alongside Xander Schauffele, Justin Thomas, Patrick Cantlay, Chris Gotterup, and Jackson Koivun. It was a pick that raised some eyebrows — not because Bridgeman is a bad player, but because captain's picks always invite scrutiny about who was left off the list.

Three weeks later, Snedeker's read of Bridgeman's form and character looks exactly right. The player he chose is walking into Medinah as one of the hottest golfers on the planet, coming off a wire-to-wire (or near it) performance that ended with a $900,000 check and a trophy.

Bridgeman responded to the captain's faith by playing his best golf. That is the best possible outcome from a selection.

What the Timing Means

There are few things more valuable in team golf than arriving with momentum. The Presidents Cup asks players to compete in formats — fourballs and foursomes — that require both individual excellence and partnership chemistry. A player who is confident in their own game brings that energy into every partnership they form.

Bridgeman will walk into Medinah with the kind of confidence that cannot be manufactured in a team room or a practice round. It comes from doing it under tournament pressure, in front of a leaderboard, with something at stake. He did exactly that at Walnut Cove.

What's Next

The Presidents Cup begins Thursday, September 24, at Medinah Country Club. Bridgeman joins Scottie Scheffler, Collin Morikawa, Xander Schauffele, Cameron Young, and the rest of Snedeker's squad. He arrives as a proven winner this week — and one of the most compelling individual storylines in the U.S. team's preparation. Watch for Snedeker to leverage Bridgeman's hot hand in the opening day fourball pairings.
