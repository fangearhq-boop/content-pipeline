LIV Golf's 2026 Season Is Over. The Off-Season Drama Is Just Beginning.

By Marcus Cole

September 15, 2026

The 2026 LIV Golf season has concluded. The final event played out in Indianapolis, Legion XIII claimed the team championship, and a 22-year-old named Michael La Sasso made history by becoming the youngest individual winner in the circuit's short existence.

On paper, those are the stories. But LIV Golf's 2026 season will be defined by what happened off the course, in bankruptcy court and boardrooms, far from any fairway.

What Happened on the Course

Legion XIII — the team captained by Jon Rahm — won the 2026 team championship, adding to what has become the most successful team partnership in LIV's history. Rahm, who signed one of the most lucrative deals in the circuit's brief existence, remains the face of LIV Golf on the competitive side.

La Sasso's milestone as the youngest LIV winner at 22 was one of the feel-good moments of the season. The 2026 campaign also featured a significant format change: events expanded to 72 holes of stroke play, abandoning the original 54-hole format that had drawn criticism from traditionalists since the league's launch.

Before the season even began, two of LIV's most prominent names departed. Brooks Koepka and Patrick Reed both returned to the PGA Tour, leaving a roster that was already leaner at the very top of the world rankings.

The Financial Reality

On September 8, LIV Golf filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection. The filing was not a surprise to those who had been following the circuit's finances closely, but it was still a significant moment for a league that launched with Saudi Public Investment Fund backing and hundreds of millions in player contracts.

The Saudi PIF responded by providing $49.6 million in DIP (debtor-in-possession) financing to keep the circuit operational through the restructuring process. The financing confirms that the PIF has not walked away from the project, but it also makes clear that the original financial model is no longer viable.

BC Partners is leading restructuring discussions around what is being called "LIV 2.0" — a restructured league in which players would hold majority equity ownership. The concept is a significant departure from the original top-down model, where the PIF controlled all equity and players were paid fixed contracts.

The Rahm Question

Jon Rahm is owed more than $100 million from his original signing deal, making him the most prominent figure in the creditor proceedings. How his situation is resolved — and whether he remains with a restructured LIV — will be one of the defining questions of the off-season.

The PGA Tour has made its position clear: PGA Tour CEO Brian Rolapp has stated there is no current reinstatement pathway for LIV players. The departure of Koepka and Reed before the 2026 season required individual negotiations, not a systemic pathway, and the current state of LIV's bankruptcy makes any broad reunification discussions even more complicated.

What's Next

Despite the bankruptcy filing, four 2027 events are already announced: Saudi Arabia, Australia, South Africa, and Hong Kong. The existence of those events suggests that LIV 2.0, in some form, is genuinely the plan rather than a managed wind-down.

What that restructured league looks like — and who plays in it — will be determined over the coming months in bankruptcy proceedings that are only beginning. For LIV Golf, 2026 was a season of on-course completion and off-course collapse. The question is whether what comes next resembles anything that was promised when this experiment began.
