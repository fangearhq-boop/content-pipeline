Scottie Scheffler's 2026 Season Was Historic. The Numbers Prove It.

By Jake Torres

September 15, 2026

The 2026 PGA Tour season is over. The final chapter was written on August 30 at East Lake Golf Club, where Scottie Scheffler shot a final-round 4-under 66 to win the TOUR Championship by three strokes over Viktor Hovland. When the scorecard was signed and the trophy was lifted, it marked the conclusion of one of the most statistically dominant seasons any golfer has ever produced.

The only question worth asking about Scheffler's 2026 is not whether it was great — it clearly was — but whether we will see anything like it again.

The Records

Start with the money. Scheffler earned $30,937,525 in official PGA Tour prize money in 2026, setting a new single-season earnings record. Add the $23 million FedEx Cup bonus, and his season total exceeds $53 million in PGA Tour earnings alone — a figure that would have seemed impossible a decade ago.

The career earnings milestone is the one that places Scheffler permanently in the conversation about the greatest players of all time. He now stands at more than $130 million in career earnings on the PGA Tour, having passed Tiger Woods' all-time record of $120,999,166. Woods spent two decades accumulating that number. Scheffler reached it at an age when most players are still trying to establish themselves as consistent Tour winners.

The 2026 scoring average of 67.96 is perhaps the most staggering single-season statistical achievement. That mark is the lowest on the PGA Tour since 1983 — a reminder of just how precisely Scheffler is controlling a golf ball at his peak.

The Wins and the Drought

Scheffler won three times in 2026: the American Express in January, the FedEx St. Jude Championship, and the TOUR Championship. Three wins in a single season, including a playoff-ending major event, would be the defining achievement of most players' careers.

What makes Scheffler's season more complex — and arguably more impressive — is the 210-day stretch between the American Express and the FedEx St. Jude in which he did not win. During that period, he posted five runner-up finishes. He was never off the leaderboard, never far from the top of every tournament he entered, but the wins stopped coming.

When the drought ended at the FedEx St. Jude, it was as if the season reset. He went on to add the TOUR Championship and close out the FedEx Cup with a performance that left no ambiguity about who the best player in the world is.

The Company He Keeps

Only Tiger Woods (2007, 2009) and Rory McIlroy (2016, 2019, 2022) have won multiple FedEx Cups. Scheffler joined that group with his second title. The list has three names on it. All three are players whose careers will be debated for generations.

The GOAT conversation that has simmered around Scheffler for the past two seasons is no longer a fringe discussion. It is the central debate in professional golf, and his 2026 season has given those who argue on his behalf another chapter of evidence.

What's Next

Scheffler will represent Team USA at the Presidents Cup, which opens September 24 at Medinah Country Club. He is one of six automatic qualifiers on Brandt Snedeker's squad. After Medinah, the attention will shift to the 2027 major schedule and whether Scheffler can add to a major haul that already includes some of the game's most prestigious trophies.

The 2026 PGA Tour season is finished. But the conversation about what Scheffler has built over the past several years is only getting louder.
