Next Stop Bermuda: PGA Tour FedExCup Fall Resumes October 22–25 at Port Royal

By Ryan Calloway

The PGA Tour takes a week off following last weekend's Baycurrent Classic in Yokohama, Japan. When the FedExCup Fall season resumes, it does so at one of the most distinctive venues on the calendar — Port Royal Golf Course in Bermuda, home of the Butterfield Bermuda Championship from October 22–25.

Port Royal and What Makes It Different

Port Royal is a public golf course built into Bermuda's coastline, and it plays nothing like the manicured parkland courses that dominate the American tour schedule. The layout winds through the island's terrain — past stretches of Atlantic coastline, over elevation changes that a flat scorecard can never fully communicate, into and out of wind corridors that make club selection a conversation rather than a calculation.

Bermuda's wind is the defining element. Players who fight it instead of working with it pay a price. The holes that run along the ocean — particularly the par-three 16th, with its cliff-side green above the water — are among the most photographed on the tour schedule. The setting rewards players who manage their emotions alongside their game.

The Fall Bubble

Four events remain in the FedExCup Fall season after this week's break: Bermuda, the VidantaWorld Mexico Open (October 29–November 1), and two further rounds. Players finishing in the top 60 of the FedExCup Fall standings earn status for the following season's full schedule.

For the players inside the bubble — those within striking distance of that top-60 cutoff — there are very few chances left to move. The Bermuda Championship is not just another 72-hole event. For those players, it is a do-or-die opportunity in a setting that makes golf difficult even when the pressure is low.

Coming Off Yokohama

Michael Brennan won the Baycurrent Classic last weekend, his third PGA Tour title and second of 2026. He enters Bermuda with momentum, points, and the attention that a recent winner carries into the next event. The question the Fall season consistently asks is whether a player's form translates across different course types and conditions.

Port Royal's demands are specific. Speed on greens differs from Japan. Wind factors don't exist at Yokohama Country Club the way they do on a coastal Bermuda layout. Bermuda is its own test, and the Fall season's diversity is part of what makes the bubble race compelling.

The tour is ten days away from Bermuda. The standings are alive.
