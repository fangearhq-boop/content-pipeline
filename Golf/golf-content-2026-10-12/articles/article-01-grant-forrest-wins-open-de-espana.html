Grant Forrest Wins Open de España — Double Major Invitation and a Birdie for the Ages

By Ryan Calloway

Grant Forrest birdied the 18th hole at Real Club de Campo Villa de Madrid on Sunday to win the 2026 Open de España, claiming his third DP World Tour title and earning invitations to both the 2027 Masters at Augusta National and The Open Championship at St Andrews.

The Scottish golfer finished at 14-under, one stroke ahead of Eugenio Chacarra and Victor Perez, who shared second place at 13-under. Chacarra — the Spanish home favourite — had held the clubhouse lead before Forrest's decisive finish.

The Birdie That Won Everything

Chacarra made his run. Perez was in contention. But when Forrest stood on the 18th tee at Real Club de Campo Villa de Madrid with the tournament in the balance, he delivered the shot that mattered. The birdie putt fell. The crowd's reaction, the scoreboard shift, the sudden mathematics of the leaderboard — all of it turned in Forrest's favour in one stroke.

It is the kind of finish that defines a career. Forrest had won twice on the DP World Tour before Sunday, both titles earned in Scotland. This was his first victory on foreign soil, in a country that had its own player in serious contention for the title. That context makes the win harder. He rose to it anyway.

The Prize That Comes With It

Open de España winners receive invitations to the following year's Masters Tournament and The Open Championship. For Forrest, that means Augusta National in April 2027 and St Andrews in July 2027 — two of the four majors, on two of the most celebrated courses in the sport's history.

His prize money from the win amounts to approximately €567,564 — roughly $637,500. The Race to Dubai points haul strengthens his position in the DP World Tour's season-long standings.

Chacarra's Near Miss

Eugenio Chacarra arrived at the Open de España as the Spanish favourite, and for much of Sunday's final round he gave the Madrid crowd what they wanted. His 13-under total held the clubhouse lead before Forrest came home. A one-shot loss at your home Open is a different kind of defeat — not a collapse, but a near miss that will stay with him.

Forrest's victory does not diminish what Chacarra and Perez produced. Both players finished at 13-under on a course that demands precision and punishes lapses. The margin at the top was one shot, and on a different day it might have been the other way.

A New Chapter

Three DP World Tour titles at 26 is a career with genuine momentum. Forrest has won in Scotland twice and now in Spain. Augusta and St Andrews await in 2027. The game's grandest stages are no longer theoretical destinations — they are confirmed dates on his calendar.
