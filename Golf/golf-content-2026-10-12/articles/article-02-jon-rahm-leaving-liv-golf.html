Jon Rahm Is Leaving LIV Golf — And He's Owed Up to $150 Million

By Jake Torres

Jon Rahm is walking away from LIV Golf. His attorney, John Beck, told a New Jersey bankruptcy court that Rahm considers the proposed terms of LIV's 2027 relaunch — known internally as LIV 2.0 — unacceptable, and that both sides are working toward a separation agreement. The deadline for that deal is October 15.

If no agreement is reached by then, the matter could return to court on November 5.

Rahm is reported to be owed between $100 million and $150 million by the bankrupt league — money that, at this stage, the league cannot pay.

How It Started

Rahm signed with LIV Golf in December 2023 for a reported contract that made him one of the highest-paid players in the history of the sport. He was a two-time major champion, a former world No. 1, and the kind of name that changed the conversation about LIV's legitimacy as a professional tour.

The move was controversial. It was also transformative for LIV's public profile. Where the league had signed recognizable names before, Rahm was a player at the peak of his career — not winding down, but at the stage where titles were still being won and rankings still being climbed.

What LIV Became

LIV Golf filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in a New Jersey court in September 2026, after Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund withdrew its financial backing. The league listed liabilities between its players and creditors, with Rahm among the highest-profile names owed significant sums.

LIV's CEO has said the league intends to return in 2027 under a player-first ownership model. Rahm — through his attorney — has made clear he will not be part of that plan. The proposed terms were, in Beck's words, unacceptable.

The PGA Tour Question

Returning to the PGA Tour is not straightforward for Rahm. When the Tour introduced a Returning Member Program following LIV's difficulties, Rahm declined to participate. No formal reinstatement path is currently available to him.

PGA Tour CEO Brian Rolapp stated clearly in August that there is no merger between the organizations and no conversations toward one. Rahm's situation — a world-class golfer with no tour to play on and significant unpaid obligations from his previous employer — is one of the most complex in the sport's recent history.

The Broader Picture

Rahm is not the only LIV player owed money by the bankrupt league. He is simply the most prominent one, and his departure, when finalized, will be the clearest symbol of what LIV's bankruptcy means for the players who bet their careers on it.

Golf's landscape has been transformed since 2021, not always for the better. Where the next chapter leads — for Rahm, for the LIV players, and for the PGA Tour — is still being written.
