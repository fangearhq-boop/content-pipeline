### STORY 2: LIV Golf Bankruptcy — The Money Owed

**Long-Form Post (10:30 AM ET)**

Here are the exact amounts that LIV Golf owes its top players, according to the Chapter 11 bankruptcy filing:

• Jon Rahm: $7.5 million
• Bryson DeChambeau: $5.7 million
• Dustin Johnson: $5.5 million
• Cameron Smith: $4.8 million
• Tyrrell Hatton: $3.4 million
• Brooks Koepka: $1.7 million

LIV Golf filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on September 8 in New Jersey. The tour lists liabilities between $500 million and $1 billion, and assets between $100 million and $500 million. Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund — LIV's primary backer — announced in April that it would end funding after the 2026 season.

Now, London-based private equity firm BC Partners is proposing a restructured LIV 2.0 in which players would hold majority ownership. An independent board of Gene Davis and Jon Zinman would oversee the transition. But the deal requires a "requisite number of players" — two-thirds by claim amount, half by number — to commit by mid-October.

Rahm, DeChambeau, and Smith have the most to lose if the deal falls apart. They also have the most leverage.

The mid-October deadline is days away.

### STORY 4: Patrick Reed PGA Tour Return

**Long-Form Post (3:30 PM ET)**

Patrick Reed, the 2018 Masters champion, is coming back to the PGA Tour.

After leaving LIV Golf, Reed spent 2026 rebuilding his career on the DP World Tour in Europe, competing and accumulating Race to Dubai ranking points to secure a European Tour card. His one-year eligibility suspension from his last LIV appearance expired around August 25, officially reopening the PGA Tour door.

Reed is expected to play PGA Tour events in the fall season and make a full return for 2027.

His path back is different from Brooks Koepka's — the only other prominent LIV player to return. Koepka paid $5 million to charity and forfeited five years of tour equity. Reed is rebuilding through competitive play on the European circuit rather than via a buy-back arrangement.

For fans of the 2018 Masters, one of golf's most passionate and polarizing competitors is working his way home.

### STORY 5: PGA Tour 2028 Two-Tier Structure

**Long-Form Post (6:00 PM ET)**

The PGA Tour has announced what professional golf's top circuit will look like starting in 2028: a two-tier structure.

The Championship Series will include the top 90 players on the PGA Tour by ranking — the elite group competing at the sport's highest level. Below that, the Challenger Series will house the rest of the membership.

Every year, 20 players will earn promotion from the Challenger Series into the Championship Series, creating a clear pathway from emerging talent to the top tier.

It's a structure familiar to sports fans who follow English soccer, where clubs earn promotion from the Championship into the Premier League based on performance. Golf is borrowing that model.

For players currently outside the top 90, the Challenger Series creates a defined pathway to the top — and a reason to grind. For elite players, the Championship Series provides a curated competitive environment.

The announcement comes as the broader professional golf landscape — including LIV Golf's restructuring — continues to evolve.
