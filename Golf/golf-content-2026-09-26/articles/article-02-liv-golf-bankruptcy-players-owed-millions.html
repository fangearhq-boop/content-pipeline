LIV Golf Bankruptcy: What Players Are Owed and What BC Partners Is Offering

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | September 26, 2026

When LIV Golf filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection on September 8 in New Jersey, the filing made public something that had been the subject of speculation for months: exactly how much money the tour owes its top players. The numbers are significant, and the mid-October deadline to determine the tour's future is closing in.

The Creditor List

According to the Chapter 11 filing, LIV Golf owes the following amounts to its prominent player-creditors:

Jon Rahm: $7.5 million. Bryson DeChambeau: $5.7 million. Dustin Johnson: $5.5 million. Cameron Smith: $4.8 million. Tyrrell Hatton: $3.4 million. Brooks Koepka: $1.7 million.

The filing lists LIV Golf's total liabilities at between $500 million and $1 billion, against assets of between $100 million and $500 million. Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund — the tour's primary backer — announced in April 2026 that it would cease funding after the 2026 season. PIF has since provided $49.6 million in bankruptcy financing to keep operations running through the restructuring process.

The BC Partners Proposal

London-based private equity firm BC Partners is proposing a restructured version of the tour, which has been described as LIV 2.0. Under the BC Partners plan, players would hold majority ownership of the reorganized tour. An independent board comprised of Gene Davis and Jon Zinman would provide governance oversight.

The restructured tour is expected to re-emerge in early 2027 if the deal closes. But the deal requires a critical mass of player support: two-thirds of player creditors by claim amount, and one-half by number of creditors, must commit to the restructured entity before mid-October — approximately 35 days after the September 8 filing date.

The Players' Decision

For Rahm, DeChambeau, and Smith — collectively owed more than $18 million — the decision carries enormous financial stakes. Committing to LIV 2.0 means accepting equity in a player-owned restructured tour in exchange for outstanding compensation. Walking away means joining an unsecured creditor queue in a bankruptcy proceeding with uncertain recovery prospects.

The PGA Tour's position has not changed. Commissioner Brian Rolapp confirmed earlier this year that the door is closed on returning LIV players under the current framework, with Koepka's return — which cost the player $5 million in charitable contribution plus five years of equity forfeiture — described as a one-off exception tied to specific circumstances.

What LIV's Season Looked Like

LIV Golf's 2026 season ended earlier than scheduled, with the final event held in Indianapolis on August 20-23. The abbreviated finish reflected the financial reality the tour was navigating as the bankruptcy filing approached. For players who joined LIV in the expectation of guaranteed contracts and a flourishing alternative circuit, the 2026 season became a very different story than the one they signed up for.

What's Next

The mid-October commitment deadline is the next significant date in LIV Golf's bankruptcy process. Watch for announcements from individual players as the deadline approaches — particularly Rahm, DeChambeau, and Smith, whose decisions will effectively determine whether LIV 2.0 becomes a reality. Golf Fanrecap will continue tracking the story as it develops.
