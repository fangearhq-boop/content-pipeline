### STORY 1: BMW Australian PGA R1

**Post 1 — Long-Form (8:00 AM ET)**
Window: 8:00 AM ET

Round 1 is in the books at The Lakes in Sydney, and the story of the day at the BMW Australian PGA Championship has two characters: Australian Haydn Barron, who shot a lights-out 64 to lead at -8, and Min Woo Lee, who somehow carded a 68 without his 8-iron.

Lee's response? He went out, hit it solid all day, and shot -4 — four strokes behind Barron, but well inside the top of the leaderboard. "I played solid, which is all I asked for," he said afterward.

Cameron Smith opened with a 69 (-3) in a strong early showing for the Queenslander. Adam Scott is also in the field, making The Lakes this week a preview of the Australian Open showdown at Kingston Heath next week.

### STORY 2: Marco Penge RSM Classic Win

**Post 2 — Long-Form (10:30 AM ET)**
Window: 10:30 AM ET

If you wanted a story to remember from the last PGA Tour event of the 2026 season, Marco Penge delivered one for the ages.

Penge entered the RSM Classic at Sea Island ranked 120th in the FedExCup Fall standings — 21 spots outside the automatic card cutoff. By Sunday evening, he was a PGA Tour champion with a two-year exemption through 2028.

The final act came in a four-way playoff between Penge, Haotong Li, Max Greyserman, and Michael Thorbjornsen — four players who had all watched the leaderboard all week knowing their careers depended on the result. Penge ended it on the first extra hole, draining a 4-foot birdie putt when the others couldn't match.

It was his first PGA Tour victory, and it came in the highest-stakes moment possible. David Lipsky was the player whose spot in the top 100 Penge took with his win.

That's the RSM Classic in its purest form: the season finale, everything on the line, the drama of a sport where livelihood and performance converge on 18 holes.

### STORY 3: Thitikul CME Three-Peat

**Post 3 — Long-Form (1:00 PM ET)**
Window: 1:00 PM ET

Three consecutive CME Group Tour Championship titles. No one had ever done it before.

Atthaya "Jeeno" Thitikul claimed her third straight title at Tiburón Golf Club in Naples, closing with a final-round 65 to win by two strokes over Aki Iwai. The $4 million check pushed her career earnings to approximately $24.29 million — past Annika Sörenstam and into the top spot on the LPGA Tour's all-time money list.

That combination — a historic three-peat and an all-time earnings record — in the same event is the kind of storyline that rarely lines up so cleanly. At 23 years old, Thitikul has now earned $12 million across just three events at the CME season finale. Her 2026 season, with five wins and a potential single-season earnings record, cements her status as the defining player of this era of women's golf.

The LPGA season is over. And it ended with one of its greatest champions making history one more time.

### STORY 5: Dan Hicks Tribute

**Post 5 — Long-Form (6:00 PM ET)**
Window: 6:00 PM ET

This Thanksgiving week, golf pauses to remember one of its most beloved voices.

Dan Hicks, NBC Sports' lead golf play-by-play announcer, died on November 12 at age 64. He joined NBC in 1992 and became the network's voice of golf in 2000, forming a memorable partnership with analyst Johnny Miller that defined major championship coverage for a generation of fans.

Over more than three decades, Hicks called 15 or more Olympic Games, narrated Tiger Woods' greatest moments, and sat beside the microphone for some of the sport's most dramatic hours. His most iconic call came at the 2008 U.S. Open at Torrey Pines, when Woods made a par putt on the 18th hole to force a playoff with Rocco Mediate — a call that captured the drama of the moment as few could.

The tributes have been immediate and heartfelt. NBC Sports called him an extraordinary colleague. The PGA Tour said his narration filled our living rooms for more than 25 years. Amanda Balionis, whose own broadcasting career grew alongside Hicks in the NBC Golf world, called him "a truly great man."

He is survived by his wife, Hannah Storm, and their three daughters. For everyone who watched golf on NBC, Dan Hicks was the voice that told you something important was happening. The sport is quieter without him.
