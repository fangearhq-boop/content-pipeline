Dan Hicks, NBC's Voice of Golf for Three Decades, Dies at 64

By Jake Torres | November 26, 2026

Dan Hicks, the play-by-play announcer who served as the voice of NBC Golf for more than three decades, died on November 12 at the age of 64. His passing was announced by his family and by NBC Sports, where he had worked since 1992 and become the face of the network's golf coverage since taking over as lead announcer in 2000.

He is survived by his wife, Hannah Storm, and their three daughters.

The Voice That Defined Major Moments

Hicks did not approach golf as a sport to be explained. He approached it as a sport to be experienced, and his instinct for when to speak and when to let silence do the work gave his calls a quality that resonates even now. His most famous moment came at the 2008 U.S. Open at Torrey Pines, when Tiger Woods stood over an 18th-hole putt that would either win the tournament or force a playoff with Rocco Mediate. Hicks was on the call. The putt fell. The crowd erupted. And Hicks found exactly the words the moment demanded.

That clip has been replayed thousands of times since, as a document of one of golf's greatest competitive performances and as a document of what a great broadcaster does with it.

A Career That Stretched Across the Sport's Biggest Stages

Beyond golf, Hicks covered more than 15 Olympic Games for NBC — a range that speaks to his value as a broadcaster across multiple sports and contexts. He called Notre Dame football for NBC alongside golf, and his ability to transition between formats while maintaining the same fundamental warmth and authority marked him as one of the rarest kinds of sports voices.

His partnership with analyst Johnny Miller was central to NBC's golf identity for most of his tenure. Miller's directness and willingness to critique paired naturally with Hicks' even-handed delivery — a combination that produced some of the most honest and entertaining golf commentary of the modern era.

The Tributes

NBC Sports called Hicks "an extraordinary colleague." The PGA Tour said his narration "filled our living rooms for more than 25 years." For broadcast journalist Amanda Balionis, who came up in the world of golf broadcasting that Hicks had shaped, he was "a truly great man." The tributes shared a common thread: they were not about his abilities, though those were considerable. They were about who he was in the room, in the booth, to the people around him.

That is the reputation worth having. Dan Hicks had it.

What's Next

NBC Sports will continue its golf coverage heading into the 2027 PGA Tour season, which opens in January. The network will carry many of the same events Hicks called throughout his career. A generation of golf fans will watch those broadcasts with his voice still in their memory — the voice that told them something important was happening and made them feel, wherever they were watching, that they were there for it. Dan Hicks was 64 years old.
