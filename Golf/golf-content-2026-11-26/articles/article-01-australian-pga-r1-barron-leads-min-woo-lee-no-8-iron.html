Australian PGA R1: Haydn Barron Leads at -8, Min Woo Lee Shoots 68 Without His 8-Iron

By Ryan Calloway | November 26, 2026

Round 1 of the 2026 BMW Australian PGA Championship at The Lakes in Sydney has produced its first genuine story of the season, and it arrived before the first ball was even struck: Min Woo Lee left his 8-iron in Las Vegas.

What makes it stranger is why. Lee gave the club to Formula 1 drivers Carlos Sainz and Lance Stroll during a golf lesson ahead of his departure from the United States. The club has since been located, but it is not expected to arrive in Sydney before at least the third round. Lee's response to this equipment deficit was a four-under 68, four strokes off the lead.

"I played solid, which is all I asked for," he said after his round.

Barron Sets the Pace

While Lee's story dominated the post-round conversation, Haydn Barron's round is the number that matters on the leaderboard. The Australian shot a 64 to open the tournament at -8, leading by two strokes entering the second round. It was an authoritative start by a player who has consistently contended in DP World Tour events across the southern hemisphere swing.

Barron's round had the kind of back-nine momentum that sets up a lead — multiple birdies in a stretch on a course that rewards precision approach play and penalizes the kind of driving errors that The Lakes can expose. His cushion heading into Friday's second round gives him some margin while still needing four more rounds to defend.

Smith and Scott Set the Australian Storyline

Cameron Smith opened with a 69 (-3) in a solid start that keeps him within range of Barron after one round. Smith, the 2017, 2018 and 2022 Australian PGA champion, is playing under a past-champion exemption and carrying the ambition of an Australian swing double: the BMW Australian PGA this week, followed by the Australian Open at Kingston Heath next week.

Adam Scott, whose career history at this event includes a 2013 Australian PGA title, is also competing at The Lakes. Scott is 46 years old and still competing at the highest level of international golf, and his presence adds another dimension to a field that has accumulated genuine narrative weight heading into the first full round of competition.

What's Next

Round 2 continues Thursday at The Lakes, with Min Woo Lee and the rest of the field back on course. Barron's -8 lead gives him room to work with, but the second round at an Australian summer venue typically produces scoring as conditions warm. Lee's 8-iron situation will be resolved by the third round; for now, he navigates without it. The BMW Australian PGA Championship runs through Sunday, with the Australian Open at Kingston Heath following immediately the week after.
