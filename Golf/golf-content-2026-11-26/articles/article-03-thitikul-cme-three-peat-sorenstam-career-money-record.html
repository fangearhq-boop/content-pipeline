Thitikul Makes CME History with Three-Peat, Passes Sörenstam on LPGA Career Money List

By Marcus Cole | November 26, 2026

Atthaya "Jeeno" Thitikul closed the 2026 LPGA Tour season the same way she closed the previous two: with a final-round charge at Tiburón Golf Club in Naples that left the opposition unable to respond. Her 65 on Sunday was good enough to win the CME Group Tour Championship by two strokes over Aki Iwai, earning the $4 million top prize for the third year in a row.

No one had ever won the CME Group Tour Championship three consecutive times before. Thitikul has now done it, and in doing so, she added a career milestone that places her performance in a broader historical context: the win pushed her career earnings past Annika Sörenstam's on the LPGA Tour's all-time money list, a total now approaching $24.3 million.

Three-Peat, First of Its Kind

The CME Group Tour Championship has been played since 2014, and in that span no player had been able to win it back-to-back-to-back. Thitikul, who was 20 years old when she won her first in 2024, completed the feat at 23. The event's format — a 72-hole individual stroke play conclusion to the LPGA season, with the richest prize in women's golf — has historically exposed players to the pressure that derails repeat champions.

Thitikul absorbed that pressure and converted it. She came into the final round having done whatever it took to reach the top of the board across the week, and her closing 65 — with birdies throughout — was the kind of round that settled any remaining questions about her status as the tour's dominant force.

Passing Sörenstam

The career earnings milestone adds another layer to what Thitikul has built in a short time. Annika Sörenstam is widely considered the greatest female golfer in the history of the sport, and her career earnings represented a benchmark that seemed distant just a few years ago. Thitikul's $12 million in CME Group Tour Championship prize money alone — $4 million per win across three consecutive years — accelerated her trajectory to a point where she arrived at this season's finale already within reach of the record.

She is 23. The numbers will keep climbing.

What's Next

The 2026 LPGA Tour season is complete. Thitikul ends it with five wins, the season's richest prize three times over, and her name atop the tour's all-time earnings list. The 2027 season begins in January, and the question for the rest of the tour is the same one it has been for the last three years: how do you beat a player who seems to arrive at the most important moment of the year with something in reserve that no one else can find? The answer, so far, has not been found.
