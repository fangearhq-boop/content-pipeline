Scottie Scheffler: 1 Win, 5 Runner-Ups, and a Player of the Year Race He Could Lose

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | August 11, 2026

The numbers don't make immediate sense. Scottie Scheffler is World No. 1 for the 167th consecutive week. He leads the FedExCup standings entering the postseason. He has nine top-5 finishes in 2026. He is the +500 favorite to win this week's FedEx St. Jude Championship. By almost any conventional metric, he is the best golfer in the world.

And yet, as the playoffs begin Thursday in Memphis, there is a legitimate question about whether Scheffler will win PGA Tour Player of the Year.

The answer turns on a single number: one. That is how many times Scheffler has won in 2026. The American Express, back in January — his 20th career PGA Tour victory — is the only checkmark in the win column after 17 or more events. He has five runner-up finishes, zero major titles, and one missed cut (the Scottish Open). He has been more consistent than any player on tour. But consistency and wins are not the same thing.

The Gotterup Factor

Chris Gotterup has three wins in 2026. That's the lead the 26-year-old holds in the POTY race by the metric that typically matters most to voters — victories. Scheffler's consistency argument is powerful, but the award historically rewards players who convert their chances, and Scheffler's five runner-ups represent five very expensive moments where he could not close.

Golf Channel analyst Johnson Wagner has publicly predicted that Scheffler will finish the season with three wins total, meaning he needs two more victories in the three playoff events. Win two of the three remaining events, and the narrative flips — from "the world's best player who couldn't close" to "the champion who delivered when it counted most."

The Statistical Paradox

Only Scheffler and Cameron Young (in 2022) have had five or more runner-up finishes in a single PGA Tour season in the last four decades. That's a remarkable and uniquely painful kind of distinction. Scheffler himself has acknowledged the frustration: "I've been really close to winning some tournaments, and that can be frustrating."

His dominance shows up in every aggregate measure. His nine top-5 finishes are among the most on tour. His single missed cut came at the Scottish Open, ending a 35-event top-25 streak. He has not been poor. He has been extraordinary, with a flaw: he hasn't converted.

In a normal year, that profile would be enough for POTY. But Gotterup's three wins have reframed the conversation.

The Playoffs as a Reckoning

The FedEx Cup Playoffs are Scheffler's best opportunity to settle the debate on his own terms. Winning the FedExCup title would carry enormous weight — it is, in many voters' minds, the most important non-major prize in the sport. If he wins the Tour Championship and adds a second win along the way, the POTY argument becomes very difficult to make against him.

But if he finishes the season without converting — if three more runner-up finishes pile up instead — the question will follow him into the offseason.

What's Next

The FedEx St. Jude Championship begins Thursday, August 13, at TPC Southwind in Memphis. Scheffler is the +500 favorite. He'll be followed closely by McIlroy (+1,300) and a field hungry to knock the world's best player off the leaderboard. The BMW Championship follows August 20-23 at Bellerive CC in St. Louis, and the Tour Championship closes the season August 27-30 at East Lake in Atlanta.
