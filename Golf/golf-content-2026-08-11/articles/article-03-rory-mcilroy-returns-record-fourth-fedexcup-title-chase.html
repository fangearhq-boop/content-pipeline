Rory McIlroy Returns: Chasing a Record 4th FedExCup Title in Memphis

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | August 11, 2026

Rory McIlroy spent much of the 2026 PGA Tour regular season prioritizing what matters to him most. He skipped the RBC Heritage. He sat out the Cadillac Championship and the Travelers Championship. He played just five of eight Signature Events. He has been publicly candid about where his energy goes: family first, majors second, the rest of the schedule when and where it makes sense.

The results were mixed in a strictly conventional sense. He finished 32nd at the US Open and 40th at The Open Championship. He never contended in the events he entered during the summer stretch.

None of that matters this week. The FedEx Cup Playoffs begin Thursday, and Rory McIlroy is in Memphis.

A Career Already Redefined

To understand what McIlroy is chasing in the postseason, it helps to understand what he has already accomplished in 2026. In April, he won his second consecutive Masters title at Augusta National — back-to-back green jackets, a feat achieved previously only by Jack Nicklaus, Nick Faldo, and Tiger Woods. He also won The Players Championship, generally considered the fifth major of the season.

His career major total now stands at six, tying him with Phil Mickelson, Nick Faldo, and Lee Trevino on the all-time list.

Against that backdrop, the FedExCup might seem like a lesser prize. It isn't — not to McIlroy, and not historically. He has won it three times (2016, 2019, 2022), and a fourth title would break the all-time record and establish him as the format's defining champion.

The Case For and Against

The case for McIlroy runs through his history. Three-time FedExCup champions have a habit of showing up when playoff pressure peaks. His game elevates in high-stakes environments. The format — three events, a shrinking field, enormous points available — rewards exactly the kind of player McIlroy is at his best.

The case against is straightforward: he hasn't been playing his best golf lately. The Open Championship and US Open results were disappointing. He hasn't had a regular-season win since The Players in the spring. There will be rust to shake in Memphis.

But McIlroy is listed at +1,300 at TPC Southwind — a significant number that reflects respect for his ability rather than recent form. The market knows what he can do.

What's Next

The FedEx St. Jude Championship runs August 13-16 at TPC Southwind in Memphis. McIlroy (+1,300) will face Scheffler (+500) as the field of 70 opens play Thursday. The top 50 advance to the BMW Championship (August 20-23, Bellerive CC, St. Louis). The top 30 from that event earn a spot at the Tour Championship (August 27-30, East Lake, Atlanta), where the $25M FedExCup bonus is awarded.
