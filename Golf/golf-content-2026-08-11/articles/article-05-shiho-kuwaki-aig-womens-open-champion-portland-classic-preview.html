Shiho Kuwaki Wins AIG Women's Open; Portland Classic Starts Thursday

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | August 11, 2026

The 50th AIG Women's Open ended at Royal Lytham & St Annes on August 2, and it ended with one of the most dramatic finishes in the championship's history.

Shiho Kuwaki of Japan stood on the 18th green of the second sudden-death playoff hole knowing her opponent, Germany's Esther Henseleit, had just made bogey. Kuwaki two-putted for par. She had won her first LPGA major title, in her seventh major championship start, at one of the sport's most storied venues.

How It Unfolded

The 50th edition of the championship seemed ready to produce a different ending. Yealimi Noh of the United States entered Sunday's final round with a three-shot lead and the momentum of a player who had been the best in the field all week. She shot 74. When she finished at -4, Noh dropped to third place — two shots behind the players who had passed her.

Henseleit created the playoff on the 72nd hole by holing a stunning 40-foot birdie putt that brought her level with Kuwaki at -5 (279). Both players had played beautifully all week, and in sudden death, the drama continued. On the second playoff hole, Henseleit's tee shot struck a marshal and finished in a bush. She made bogey. Kuwaki made par. The championship was decided.

The final leaderboard: Kuwaki -5 (playoff winner), Henseleit -5, Noh -4, and Nelly Korda and Jeeno Thitikul tied for fourth at -2.

Kuwaki's Place in History

The victory was Kuwaki's first LPGA major title and the second consecutive AIG Women's Open won by a Japanese player. Her $1.5 million prize came from a record $10 million purse — the largest in women's major championship history.

For Kuwaki, the win completed a journey from promising prospect to major champion in seven tries at this level. For Noh, Sunday was a brutal reminder of how thin the margin is between history and heartbreak at golf's highest stage.

The LPGA Moves On to Portland

The AIG Women's Open was the final women's major of 2026. Attention now shifts to The Standard Portland Classic, which begins Thursday, August 13, at Columbia Edgewater Country Club in Portland, Oregon. The event carries a $2 million purse and opens the post-major stretch of the LPGA schedule.

Korda, Thitikul, and the rest of the world's best will reset in the Pacific Northwest, where a full slate of events — and the push toward the LPGA's own season finale — begins in earnest.

What's Next

The Standard Portland Classic runs August 13-16 at Columbia Edgewater Country Club in Portland, Oregon ($2 million purse). The LPGA schedule continues through the fall with the tour's major season complete, attention now turning to the Race to the CME Globe standings and the year-end finale.
