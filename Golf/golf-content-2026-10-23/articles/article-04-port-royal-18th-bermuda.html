Port Royal's Uphill 18th — The Hole That Decides the Bermuda Championship

By Ryan Calloway | October 23, 2026

Port Royal Golf Course in Southampton, Bermuda, is a public links-style layout carved along clifftops and coastal terrain. Most of its holes are demanding in their own ways. But none of them does what the 18th does — ask a player who has just completed 17 holes of pressure golf to climb an uphill par-4 to a small, exposed green at the top of the rise, while the Atlantic wind does everything in its power to redirect the ball away from where it needs to go.

The Butterfield Bermuda Championship has produced memorable finishes because of what the 18th demands. Understanding the hole is understanding the tournament.

The Design Challenge

The 18th at Port Royal is an uphill par-4. The tee shot plays to a generous fairway, but the approach is where the hole reveals itself. Players face an uphill shot to a green that is small relative to the distances involved, situated at the top of the rise with wind exposure on multiple sides.

When the Bermuda breeze is light — as it was for much of Thursday's first round — the hole is manageable. The scoring average across the field in Round 1 reflected those conditions. But Bermuda's weather is changeable, and when the wind stiffens, the 18th transitions from a demanding hole to a genuinely penal one. A miss short or left leaves an extremely difficult up-and-down. A miss long puts players in trouble behind the green on a slope where any chip requires a delicate touch.

How It Has Shaped Outcomes

In past Bermuda Championships, the 18th has been where leads have evaporated and where unlikely contenders have come from nowhere. A player who holds their nerve under pressure on that final approach, with the green elevated above them and the gallery gathered around the natural amphitheater the rise creates, tends to be the kind of player who wins at Port Royal.

For leaders coming down the stretch, the mental challenge is as significant as the physical one. A birdie on 18 feels like a statement. A bogey can cost a tournament.
