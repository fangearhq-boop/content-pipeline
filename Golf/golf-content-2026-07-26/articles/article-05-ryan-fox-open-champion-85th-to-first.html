Ryan Fox: From Tied 85th After Round 1 to Open Champion — The Most Remarkable Journey of 2026

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | July 26, 2026

The further you get from the final result, the more extraordinary Ryan Fox's Open Championship victory becomes. Most champions build their case across four days. Fox spent the first two days building a case for why he might miss the cut. What he produced in the final 36 holes defied not just expectation, but the standard framework by which major championship victories are measured.

Fox finished at 270 (-10) to win the Claret Jug, one shot better than Cameron Young. Sam Burns finished third. The scoreboard shows a champion. The full story starts two days earlier, at tied 85th place.

The Numbers No One Expected

After Round 1: Fox was tied for 85th place. At that position in a major, the primary concern is reaching the cut. The conversation about where you might finish on Sunday has not yet begun — because Sunday, for 85th place after Round 1, is not yet guaranteed.

After Round 2: Fox had climbed to tied 52nd. Better, but still far from the conversation. The cut had been made. Sunday was now on the schedule. Winning it was not something the leaderboard suggested.

Round 3: 62. That score, at an Open Championship venue, is the kind of round that relocates a player onto the leaderboard and into the conversation in a single afternoon. Fox made the climb that the first two rounds had made look improbable.

Round 4: 68. Good enough to hold against a final-round charge from Young and the rest of the field. One shot better than Young's best effort to catch him.

The final 36-hole total of 130 matches the Open Championship record for the lowest final two rounds in the tournament's history. Fox did not just win — he won with a closing stretch that belongs in the record books alongside the tournament's most memorable performances.

Cameron Young's Pursuit

Young's runner-up finish deserves acknowledgment. He played the kind of final round that, in most Opens, is sufficient to win. Fox was simply better over the last 36 holes — and in a major championship, being one shot worse than the champion is the difference between holding the Claret Jug and watching someone else hold it.

Young has established himself as one of the best players in the world who has not yet won a major. His runner-up at this Open Championship is another entry in that ledger. The major will come.

What Fox Said About It

When asked what the Claret Jug meant to him, Ryan Fox's answer came from a different place than most victory speeches. "I used to get up in the middle of the night to watch this tournament back home." Back home is New Zealand. The Open Championship, played at the great links courses of Britain, broadcasts at hours that require an alarm for viewers in the Southern Hemisphere. Fox set that alarm as a kid. He watched, and he competed on a vision of what winning might feel like.

That quote is the story. The statistics are extraordinary. The record-tying 130 is verifiable. But the image of a New Zealand boy setting an alarm to watch the tournament he would one day win — that is the kind of detail that makes a major champion's victory resonate beyond the sport.

New Zealand on the Claret Jug

The Open Championship is the sport's oldest major, and the Claret Jug's roll of champions spans more than 150 years. Ryan Fox's name now joins that list as New Zealand's representative. The global reach of The Open — the reason a kid on the other side of the planet sets an alarm to watch it — is precisely why a victory like Fox's lands with the weight it does. He watched it as a fan. He won it as a champion.

What's Next

Ryan Fox carries the Claret Jug and the status of a major champion into the remainder of the 2026 season. His world ranking will climb significantly with the win, opening doors to events and fields that were previously out of reach. For the PGA Tour's upcoming FedEx Cup Playoffs, for Ryder Cup conversations, and for every major that follows, Fox is now a different kind of player — one the field will study and respect in a way it did not before the final round at Royal Portrush. The climb from 85th to champion is complete. What comes next begins from the top.
