Lottie Woad Defends Women's Scottish Open Title at -21, Erases Jenny Shin's Five-Shot Lead

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | July 26, 2026

At the halfway mark of the Women's Scottish Open at Dundonald Links, Jenny Shin led by five shots. It was the kind of lead that, in most professional golf tournaments, translates to a trophy. This was not most tournaments. England's Lottie Woad had other plans.

Woad won the Women's Scottish Open at -21, three shots ahead of Hyo Joo Kim in second. Shin's five-shot advantage at the midpoint did not survive the final 36 holes. Woad's back-to-back titles at Dundonald Links are now a fact of this tournament's recent history — and a clear signal about her standing among the world's best women's players.

How a Five-Shot Lead Disappeared

Leads evaporate in golf for any number of reasons — a change in conditions, a nervous front nine, a charging competitor who finds everything. At Dundonald Links, Woad gave a masterclass in what a competitor can accomplish when they commit fully to the task in front of them rather than watching the scoreboard.

The five-shot gap that Shin carried into the weekend was real. Her play through 36 holes had been exceptional. But professional golf rewards consistency across four rounds, not two, and Woad proved the more consistent player across the full week. The margin at the end — three shots clear of Kim, with Shin below that — tells the story of how completely Woad's late rounds dismantled the halfway order.

Hyo Joo Kim and the Rest of the Field

Kim's -18 finish was a strong performance on its own terms. Three shots back of a player who defended her title at -21 is not a disappointing week — it is a near-miss against someone who played better. Kim was the only player who gave Woad a genuine test in the final rounds, and her runner-up position reflects sustained quality throughout the tournament.

Shin's fall from the top of the leaderboard will be the secondary narrative of the Women's Scottish Open, but it should not overshadow Woad's achievement. Shin played good golf through the weekend. Woad played great golf. That is the more important story.

What Back-to-Back Titles Mean

Winning the same event in consecutive years is a meaningful benchmark in any sport. It requires not just the talent to win once, but the sustained excellence and course management to win again on the same course against a field that now knows exactly what you are capable of. Woad accomplished it at Dundonald Links with authority.

The fact that she won while erasing a five-shot deficit — not by holding a lead, but by manufacturing one — makes the second title more impressive than the first in some respects. Champions who can win from behind demonstrate a different kind of competitive character.

What's Next

Lottie Woad heads from Dundonald Links directly into the most important week on the women's golf calendar: the AIG Women's British Open. She arrives with back-to-back Scottish Open titles, current form that is difficult to dispute, and the momentum of an athlete who has spent the last week proving that five-shot leads are only as large as the player behind you will allow. The Women's British Open field will have taken notice.
