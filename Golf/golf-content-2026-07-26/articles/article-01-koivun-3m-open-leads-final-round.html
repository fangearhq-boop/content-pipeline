Jackson Koivun's Historic 61 Puts Him Three Clear Entering 3M Open Final Round

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | July 26, 2026

There are rounds that simply have to be experienced to understand. Jackson Koivun's Round 3 at the 3M Open was one of them. The 21-year-old played TPC Twin Cities on Saturday in a manner that will be discussed in Minnesota golf circles for years — a 61 that included a back-nine score of 28, a new tournament record, punctuated by eagles on both the 12th and 18th holes.

When the dust settled, Koivun stood at -20 for the tournament, three shots clear of Emiliano Grillo and Eric Kohles heading into Sunday's final round. He had climbed from deep in the field to the top of the leaderboard with a performance that was not a fluke or a run of made putts — it was systematic, aggressive golf that turned a tournament into a chase.

The Round That Changed Everything

Koivun's back nine on Saturday was the story of the day, possibly the story of the week at TPC Twin Cities. A score of 28 on any back nine on the PGA Tour is rare. At TPC Twin Cities, it's a tournament record. Making eagles on two different holes in the same back nine — the 12th and the 18th — means he was not simply riding hot putting. He was creating scoring opportunities and converting them.

The 61 in total tied some of the more remarkable rounds in 3M Open history. In the context of a tournament weekend that already featured Michael Kim's historic 59 in Round 1, the leaderboard at TPC Twin Cities has become one of the most compelling in PGA Tour 2026.

Koivun is 21 years old. He entered the week without significant name recognition among casual golf fans. By the time the sun set on Saturday, that had changed.

The Final Round Picture

A three-shot lead is meaningful in professional golf — but it is not insurmountable, particularly when one of the chasers is Scottie Scheffler. The world's top-ranked player sat at -14 entering Sunday, six shots behind Koivun. A six-shot deficit is a mountain, but Scheffler has demonstrated this season and throughout his career that he is capable of shooting 64 on a course where others shoot 69.

Grillo and Kohles at -17 represent more immediate threats. Either player closing with a round in the 60s could pressure Koivun significantly if the young leader loses any early momentum. Min Woo Lee and Beau Hossler at -16 add further depth to a Sunday chase that has the makings of a memorable final round.

Michael Kim, whose 59 in Round 1 captured national attention, fell to tied sixth at -15 after a quieter Round 3. His story has been extraordinary regardless of what Sunday brings — you do not shoot 59 in a PGA Tour event and not leave a mark on the tournament's history.

What This Win Would Mean

PGA Tour wins at age 21 are not rare — but they are not common, either. The players who win early tend to share certain characteristics: elite ballstriking, composure under pressure, and the ability to produce their best golf when the stakes are highest. Koivun's Round 3 was a statement on all three fronts.

A first Tour win in a week that has featured some of the most exciting scoring in recent 3M Open memory would be a defining early chapter in what looks like a significant career. Whether he closes it out or not, the 61 stands on its own.

What's Next

The 3M Open final round tees off Sunday with Koivun holding the lead. Following the 3M Open, the PGA Tour heads toward the FedEx Cup Playoffs as the 2026 season approaches its conclusion. For Jackson Koivun, Sunday is an opportunity to put an exclamation point on a week that has already redefined his place in the PGA Tour conversation.
