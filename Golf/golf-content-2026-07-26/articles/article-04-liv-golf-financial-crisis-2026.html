LIV Golf Financial Crisis Deepens: Every Remaining Tournament Reportedly "On the Fence"

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | July 26, 2026

The situation at LIV Golf has moved from uncertain to alarming. New reporting from inside the organization has produced the most troubling assessment yet of the league's financial condition: high-ranking LIV insiders are now saying that every remaining tournament on the 2026 schedule is "on the fence."

That is not a phrase typical of a sports organization in a difficult transitional period. It is a phrase that describes a league in crisis — one where the structural foundation that supported four years of competition is actively shifting beneath the organization's feet.

The PIF Withdrawal and What Followed

Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund announced its withdrawal of $5 billion in investment, effective after the conclusion of the 2026 LIV season. That announcement set off the sequence of events now unfolding. PIF was not simply a financial backer — it was the source of LIV Golf's ability to offer guaranteed contracts, prize money, and the operational infrastructure that made the league functional from its 2022 launch.

Without PIF's continued commitment, LIV Golf entered what its own board is now managing as a full restructuring. The league is not folding. But the conditions under which it will continue — and whether it will continue in its current form — are actively in question.

Gene Davis, Jon Zinman, and the Search for Bridge Capital

The appointment of Gene Davis and Jon Zinman to LIV Golf's board is the most telling indicator of where the organization stands. Davis and Zinman are not golf executives. They are not sports business veterans. They are corporate restructuring specialists — the category of professional brought in when an organization needs to rapidly assess its liabilities, identify viable paths forward, and secure bridge financing before the gap between what it has and what it needs becomes unbridgeable.

Davis and Zinman are currently seeking capital from private equity firms or other potential investors. The search for bridge capital is, by definition, urgent. Bridge capital funds operations while a longer-term solution is arranged — it is not a sign of stability, but of an organization moving quickly to avoid insolvency.

What This Means for Players and Remaining Events

LIV Golf's players hold guaranteed contracts — one of the league's foundational promises from its launch. The viability of those guarantees under full restructuring is a question that players and their representatives are, presumably, now asking directly. The answer will depend significantly on whether Davis and Zinman can secure the bridge financing they are seeking.

The events "on the fence" are those remaining on the 2026 schedule after the current LIV Golf UK event concludes. Whether those events proceed as planned, are modified, or are postponed is not yet known. The language from insiders — "on the fence" — suggests the decisions have not been made and will depend on the outcomes of ongoing capital conversations.

What's Next

LIV Golf's leadership has not made a public statement on the reported crisis at the level described by insiders. The coming weeks will determine whether bridge capital can be secured and what the league's 2026 schedule looks like as a result. For players, fans, and the professional golf world watching LIV's situation, the next announcements from Davis, Zinman, or LIV Golf's communications team will carry significant weight. The league that challenged the PGA Tour's structure now faces questions about whether it can complete its own season.
