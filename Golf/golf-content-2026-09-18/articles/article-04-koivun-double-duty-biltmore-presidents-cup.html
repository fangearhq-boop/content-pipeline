Jackson Koivun's 10-Day Stretch: Biltmore to Medinah

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | September 18, 2026

Feature

Jackson Koivun is 21 years old and playing in the middle of the best 10 days of his professional career. This week he is competing at the Biltmore Championship at The Cliffs at Walnut Cove in Asheville. Six days from now, he reports to Medinah Country Club for his Presidents Cup debut. The two events do not overlap — but the transition from one to the other requires a mental and competitive gear shift that most players do not face until much later in their careers.

Koivun was a Brandt Snedeker captain's pick for the 2026 USA Presidents Cup team. He is the youngest player on the roster. The selection reflects what Koivun has shown in 2026 — a PGA Tour season that earned him enough recognition that Snedeker chose him over a large field of candidates.

Individual vs. Team

Stroke play and match play are different games. In stroke play — the format for every regular tour event including Biltmore — every shot counts toward a running total. A bogey on one hole is a setback that needs to be recovered. The mathematics are unforgiving and cumulative.

Match play, the format used for the Presidents Cup's four-ball and foursomes sessions, runs hole by hole. Losing a hole costs a point, but the hole is over. The crowd dynamic is different, the team element changes individual decision-making, and experience in the format — or the lack of it — shows. Koivun has the talent to handle both contexts. The question his 10-day window answers is whether he can handle them back to back without letting one affect the other.

Why the Timing Matters

Most 21-year-old professional golfers are figuring out PGA Tour travel schedules and working on their games on off weeks. Koivun is wrapping up an individual tournament in North Carolina's mountains and then boarding a flight to Illinois to represent his country in an international competition. The scale of what he is managing is not normal for his age.

Snedeker's decision to pick him was a statement of confidence. How Koivun performs at Biltmore — and then how he performs in his first Presidents Cup matches — will say something about whether that confidence was well placed. The opportunity is real. So is the compressed timeline it comes on.
