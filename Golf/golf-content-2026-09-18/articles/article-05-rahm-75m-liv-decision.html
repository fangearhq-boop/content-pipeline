Jon Rahm Is Owed $7.5 Million. His Oct. 13 Decision Is Golf's Most Watched Call.

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | September 18, 2026

LIV Golf

Jon Rahm is listed in LIV Golf's bankruptcy proceedings as the league's largest individual creditor, owed approximately $7.5 million. Twenty-five days from today, he will need to decide whether to commit to LIV 2.0 under BC Partners' restructuring plan — or walk away from whatever portion of that money the deal would return and find another path forward.

The decision matters beyond the personal finances. Rahm is the most visible player in the LIV Golf ecosystem, the most respected by peers across the sport, and the one whose public signal will influence how others lean. Whatever Rahm does, a significant number of LIV players with smaller claims will use as their guide.

A Narrower Set of Options Than It Looks

Rahm's options may appear to run in several directions, but the terrain narrows quickly. The PGA Tour has maintained that there is no path back for players who joined LIV under current policy. Commissioner Rolapp stated that position directly, and it has not been retracted or softened. Without PGA Tour access, the only elite professional golf available to Rahm outside of LIV 2.0 is the DP World Tour.

Rahm holds a DP World Tour card and has returned to compete on that circuit in 2026. The DP World Tour is not a consolation prize — it runs global events, includes major championship access, and provides Ryder Cup eligibility for Spain, which matters to Rahm. But it is not the platform he spent the core years of his career building on, and it is not where most American golf fans' attention sits.

What Committing Means

If Rahm commits to LIV 2.0 and the player threshold is met, BC Partners' $300 million investment proceeds. LIV 2.0 targets a 2027 restart. Rahm would be one of the founding players of a restructured league — presumably with recovered financial claims and a competitive home outside the established tour structure.

The risk in committing: BC Partners' 2027 restart is a plan, not a guarantee. If commitments fall short or the restructured league encounters further financial problems, Rahm could be back in the same position with fewer options.

What Walking Away Means

If Rahm declines to commit, the player threshold becomes harder to meet. If it fails entirely, LIV Golf fully liquidates. Rahm's $7.5 million claim moves through the bankruptcy process with no guarantee of recovery — and whatever fraction of it is eventually paid out comes without the competitive structure that justified the original contract.

Walking away means DP World Tour as the primary competitive home, with whatever open doors might eventually appear elsewhere. It is a stable career. It is not the trajectory Rahm built before 2023.

What's Next

The October 13 deadline is 25 days away. Rahm has not publicly stated his intentions. His decision — and the decisions of Bryson DeChambeau and Dustin Johnson, whose combined claims with Rahm total $18.7 million — will determine whether the dollar-amount threshold for the BC Partners deal is reachable.
