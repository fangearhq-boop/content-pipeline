LIV Golf's 25-Day Countdown: What Happens on October 13

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | September 18, 2026

LIV Golf

The most consequential date in professional golf's off-course drama is now 25 days away. October 13, 2026 is the deadline by which a requisite number of contracted LIV Golf players must formally commit to LIV 2.0 for BC Partners' $300 million rescue investment to proceed. Miss the thresholds, and the restructuring deal likely collapses — taking any prospect of a 2027 restart with it.

LIV Golf filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in New Jersey with more than $500 million in debt. BC Partners, a London-based private equity firm, has proposed a $300 million investment as the structured path out of bankruptcy. That investment comes with conditions, and the player commitment threshold is the most important one.

The Thresholds

For the BC Partners deal to proceed, at least two-thirds of eligible player claims by dollar amount must commit to LIV 2.0 — and at least half of eligible players by headcount must also sign on. Both thresholds need to be met. Either one falling short is enough to jeopardize the deal.

That math makes the three largest creditors disproportionately important. Jon Rahm is owed approximately $7.5 million, making him LIV's largest individual creditor. Bryson DeChambeau and Dustin Johnson hold additional claims. Together, Rahm, DeChambeau, and Johnson account for roughly $18.7 million in player claims — a concentration that gives each of their decisions outsized weight in calculating whether the dollar-amount threshold is reached.

The Competing Pressures

The case for committing to LIV 2.0 is straightforward: a BC Partners deal means players receive some portion of what they are owed, and LIV 2.0 in 2027 is the only structured competitive path that doesn't run through the PGA Tour or DP World Tour frameworks. For players who gave up their Tour cards to join LIV, a viable restart matters.

The case against is more complicated. PGA Tour commissioner Rolapp has stated directly that there is no path back for LIV players under current policy. That statement has not been walked back. Without PGA Tour access, the only alternative tour available to players like Rahm is the DP World Tour — which Rahm has retained his card for and returned to compete on in 2026.

The $300 million BC Partners deal also includes $300 million in long-term sponsorship contracts through 2029. Whether those contracts represent genuine value or projections depends on whether LIV 2.0 can actually launch and sustain a 2027 season.

What Liquidation Looks Like

If the player threshold is not met and the BC Partners deal falls through, the most likely outcome is full liquidation of LIV Golf. That means the player claims — including Rahm's $7.5 million — move through the bankruptcy process with no guarantee of recovery. Players who were counting on future LIV earnings would have no competitive home outside the tours they left.

Twenty-five days is a tight window. Decisions from the biggest creditors are not yet public. The outcome of October 13 is genuinely uncertain.

What's Next

The 35-day commitment window runs from LIV's September petition date, making October 13 the hard deadline. Players have not yet confirmed publicly which direction they are leaning. The next significant development will likely come from a major creditor — and Rahm's name leads every conversation about who moves first. #LIVGolf
