### STORY 3: LIV Golf Oct. 13 Deadline — Long-Form Post

**Post Window:** 1:00 PM ET

The most consequential date in LIV Golf's future isn't a bankruptcy filing or a court hearing. It is October 13, 2026 — 25 days from today.

That is the deadline by which a "requisite number" of contracted LIV players must formally commit to LIV 2.0 for BC Partners' $300 million rescue investment to proceed. The specific threshold requires at least two-thirds of eligible player claims by dollar amount, and at least half of eligible players by headcount. Miss those marks, and the BC Partners deal likely collapses, taking the prospect of a 2027 restart with it.

Three names dominate the conversation: Jon Rahm, Bryson DeChambeau, and Dustin Johnson. Together, their player claims total approximately $18.7 million — with Rahm the largest individual creditor at roughly $7.5 million. Their decisions carry disproportionate weight in calculating whether the dollar-amount threshold is reached.

The case for committing: a BC Partners deal means some portion of what they are owed gets paid, and LIV 2.0 in 2027 is the only structured path back to competitive professional golf outside the PGA Tour and DP World Tour frameworks. The case against: the PGA Tour still has no reinstatement path open, but DP World Tour is accessible for players like Rahm who retained that card. Waiting — and watching LIV fully liquidate — might be a calculated bet.

Twenty-five days. Golf's most dramatic off-course saga isn't over yet.

### STORY 5: Rahm's $7.5M Decision — Long-Form Post

**Post Window:** 6:00 PM ET

Jon Rahm's name appears on LIV Golf's bankruptcy creditor list with approximately $7.5 million owed. That makes him the league's largest individual creditor. And 25 days from now, he will have to decide whether to become one of LIV 2.0's founding players — or walk.

The decision matters far beyond Rahm's own financial interest. His decision-making will signal to dozens of players with smaller claims whether the smart money is committing to BC Partners' restructuring plan or treating LIV as a concluded chapter and finding another path. Rahm is the most visible LIV player and the most respected by his peers. What he chooses, others will follow.

His options are narrower than they appear. The PGA Tour has maintained its position that there is "no path back" for LIV players under current policy. That statement came from commissioner Rolapp directly, and it has not been walked back. Without PGA Tour access, Rahm's competitive options without LIV 2.0 center on the DP World Tour, where he has retained his membership — and where he returned to compete this year.

A DP World Tour career is not nothing. It is elite professional golf, with major championship access, Ryder Cup eligibility for Spain, and global events. But it is not the PGA Tour, and it is not the platform Rahm built his original career on.

The Oct. 13 deadline is 25 days away. His answer comes within that window.
