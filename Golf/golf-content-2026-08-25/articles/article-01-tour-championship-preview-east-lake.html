Tour Championship Preview: Scheffler Leads, Clark Is Hot — Who Claims the FedExCup at East Lake?

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | August 25, 2026

The 2026 PGA Tour season ends Thursday at East Lake Golf Club in Atlanta. The Tour Championship — 30 players, no cut, all starting at even par — is the straightest test in professional golf's postseason: the best score over 72 holes wins the FedExCup and the biggest prize check of the year. No trickery, no starting-strokes format, no advantages baked in from the regular season. You have to earn it over four rounds, and only one player leaves East Lake as champion.

Scottie Scheffler enters as No. 1 seed and the betting favorite at +300. He has led the FedEx Cup points standings heading into the Tour Championship for five consecutive years. He won the FedExCup in 2024. He is, by any fair measure, the best player in the world for a sustained stretch — and East Lake, which rewards precision iron play, relentless birdie-making, and scrambling when needed, fits a player of his all-around caliber perfectly.

The counternarrative walks through the door in the form of Wyndham Clark. Clark won the BMW Championship at Bellerive just days ago, closing with three consecutive birdies to seal a three-shot victory. He arrives at East Lake as the No. 3 seed, fresh off a win, with the rhythm and confidence that only comes from posting a number that wins a golf tournament in front of people.

The Field Behind Them

Matt Fitzpatrick holds the No. 2 seed and represents the kind of relentless consistency — top-20 results week after week — that defines FedEx Cup points success. Cameron Young is No. 4 and has shown flashes of brilliance throughout the season. Si Woo Kim, Chris Gotterup, Collin Morikawa, Sam Burns, and Tommy Fleetwood complete a top 10 that would look credible at a major championship.

Rory McIlroy, who finished third at Bellerive, is in the field and represents a player who seems to peak in August and September — his Open Championship victory at Royal Birkdale is recent evidence that he remains a threat in premium events. East Lake is a different kind of test than links golf, but McIlroy's ballstriking and competitiveness make him relevant in any field.

The format rewards patience. East Lake is not a birdie-fest; it's a course where making a bogey at the wrong time costs you position and momentum. The player who avoids the big number while hunting birdies methodically tends to separate from the field by Sunday afternoon. That description fits Scheffler better than almost anyone alive.

What's Next

Thursday's first round will quickly establish whether Clark can sustain his BMW form or whether Scheffler asserts the consistency that has defined his season. Watch the leaderboard through 36 holes — players who are within two or three shots of the lead at the halfway point have historically been the ones who contend on Sunday. This is a 72-hole test, but East Lake's pressure tends to clarify the picture early.

By Sunday evening, one name will have the FedExCup. Whether that name belongs to the favorite, the form player, or someone who simply puts together the best four rounds of golf — that's the answer East Lake will deliver.
