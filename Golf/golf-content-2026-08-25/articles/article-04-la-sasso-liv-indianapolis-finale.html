La Sasso Shocks Rahm to Win LIV Golf Indianapolis — Legion XIII Defends Team Title in 2026 Season Finale

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | August 25, 2026

The 2026 LIV Golf season ended with a genuine upset. Michael La Sasso finished at 18-under to beat Jon Rahm by a single shot and claim the individual title at LIV Golf Indianapolis — the final event of the LIV schedule for the year. La Sasso, playing steady and composed golf across Sunday's final round, birdied three of his last six holes to hold off Rahm's charge and earn the title that almost no one predicted he would take. Sergio Garcia and Bryson DeChambeau tied for third at 16-under.

The result was a reminder that LIV Golf's individual format creates the conditions for surprise. In a 54-hole, no-cut event with a 48-player field and no world ranking implications, the competitive pressure takes a different shape than the traditional tour grind — and sometimes that shape produces a result that defies the established hierarchy. La Sasso delivered exactly that.

On the team side, there was no surprise at all. Jon Rahm's Legion XIII successfully defended their team championship with a four-player aggregate of 44-under over four rounds — nine shots clear of HyFlyers in second and eight ahead of Ripper in third. Legion XIII has now won back-to-back team titles, cementing their status as LIV Golf's most complete squad.

The Season's End and What Comes Next

Indianapolis was the last event on LIV Golf's 2026 calendar. The league has faced questions throughout the year about its financial framework — specifically about the structure of PIF's funding, which has been characterized in reporting as a lending arrangement rather than pure investment. Multiple outlets have noted concerns about LIV's ability to guarantee its full event slate and payouts, and some players have reportedly experienced delays in receiving tournament winnings from earlier stops.

LIV Golf CEO Greg Norman has declined to guarantee all remaining event commitments beyond 2026, and Ian Poulter has suggested publicly that the league may be running out of time to secure its future. What the 2027 schedule looks like — and who remains committed to playing it — is the primary question facing the league as its season concludes.

What's Next

For La Sasso, Indianapolis is the signature result of his career — a win against a field that includes three-time major champion Rahm, former U.S. Open champion DeChambeau, and a former Ryder Cup stalwart in Garcia. He will carry that result into whatever the 2027 LIV calendar looks like. For Rahm and Legion XIII, the team title is a piece of genuine pride in a season where individual glory went to someone else. The league's individual and team story closed out at Indianapolis. What happens next is a different question entirely.
