Wyndham Clark Wins BMW Championship at Bellerive, Birdies Last Three Holes to Close in Style

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | August 25, 2026

Wyndham Clark did not win the BMW Championship gently. Playing the final three holes of Bellerive Country Club on Sunday, he made birdie on 16, birdie on 17, and birdie on 18 — closing out a three-shot victory over Patrick Cantlay at 17-under 263 with a finish that announced exactly what kind of form he carries into next week's Tour Championship at East Lake. Rory McIlroy was solo third. Clark earned $3.6 million from the $20 million purse and moved to No. 3 in the FedEx Cup standings.

The closing birdie-birdie-birdie was the tournament's defining stretch — not because Clark needed all three to win, but because making birdies on a challenging finishing sequence while leading a playoff event is the kind of clutch execution that separates players in pressure situations. He did not blink. He did not play conservatively. He went after the pins and made the putts.

This is Clark's second victory of the 2026 calendar year, and it continues a pattern of performance that has established him firmly in the game's top tier. His ballstriking has been elite for months, and his putting — historically the weaker part of his game — has found the consistency that turns a good player into a champion. The combination is pointing upward at exactly the right moment.

Cantlay and McIlroy

Patrick Cantlay finished in solo second, continuing a season that has included multiple runner-up finishes without the breakthrough win that his talent suggests is imminent. Cantlay's game from tee to green is among the best on the PGA Tour; converting that ball-striking into more victories remains his unfinished business. He will be among the names to watch at East Lake, where grinding precision over 72 holes suits his profile.

McIlroy's third-place result was a quietly strong performance after a summer that included his third-place at the BMW. He enters the Tour Championship in solid form, well inside the top 30, and with the pedigree and competitive makeup to threaten at a venue where he has performed well in the past.

What's Next

Clark heads to East Lake carrying the momentum that every golfer entering a season finale wants: a recent win, clear form, and the confidence that comes from having closed out a major playoff event with consecutive birdies. Scottie Scheffler is still the No. 1 seed and the favorite by any objective measure — but Clark's argument for why this week could be different has never been more compelling. The Tour Championship starts Thursday. Clark's birdie-making does not need to stop here.
