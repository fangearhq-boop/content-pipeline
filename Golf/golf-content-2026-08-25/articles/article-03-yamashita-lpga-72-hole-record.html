Miyu Yamashita Ties LPGA 72-Hole Scoring Record With 9-Shot Wire-to-Wire Win in Edmonton

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | August 25, 2026

Miyu Yamashita played four rounds of the CPKC Women's Open at Royal Mayfair Golf Club in Edmonton without ever giving the field a realistic look at her. She led from the first day to the last, and finished with a 23-under 257 total — a nine-stroke victory over Yuri Yoshida that also tied the all-time LPGA Tour 72-hole scoring record. Wire-to-wire. Record-tying. By nine shots. Yamashita did not come to Edmonton to compete. She came to dominate.

The 72-hole scoring record is one of the most difficult benchmarks in women's professional golf to reach, requiring not just skill but sustained excellence across four rounds at a score that simply has not been touched in the history of the tour. Yamashita's rounds were consistent throughout — her scoring pace never wavered, the field never closed the gap, and when Sunday came the question was only by how much. The answer was nine, and a place in the record books alongside the game's all-time scoring greats.

Yoshida, her Japanese compatriot, finished second at 14-under. Sei Young Kim was third at 13-under. Gina Kim, Ruoning Yin, and A. Huang finished in a tie for fourth at 12-under. The result made it a distinctly Japanese 1-2, reflecting the strength of the Japanese women's game on the international circuit in 2026.

What the Win Means

Yamashita won $412,500 from the $2.75 million purse — a significant check in a season that has seen her build a reputation as one of the most dangerous players in women's professional golf. But the number that carries more weight is 257. That total is not something a player stumbles into; it requires four rounds of near-perfect golf, aggressive pin-seeking, and the mental fortitude to hold the pace when a field at a professional-level event is hunting you from every angle.

Royal Mayfair played soft over the week, which contributed to the low scoring environment. But a record-tying performance still requires execution, and Yamashita executed with extraordinary precision. The LPGA Tour will be paying close attention to her for the remainder of the 2026 season and into the next campaign.

What's Next

The LPGA Tour's postseason schedule comes into focus in the weeks ahead, with significant events remaining before the year-end standings are determined. Yamashita's Edmonton performance puts her firmly in contention for season-long honors and cements her status as a legitimate threat in any field she enters. A 72-hole performance that ties a record does not happen twice in a career — it is the kind of result that defines a golfer's identity and raises the expectation that carries them forward.
