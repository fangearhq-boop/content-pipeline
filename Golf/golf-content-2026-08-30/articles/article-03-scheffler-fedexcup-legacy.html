Five in a Row: Scottie Scheffler's FedExCup Reign Is Without Precedent in PGA Tour History

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | August 30, 2026

FedExCup · Scottie Scheffler · PGA Tour · Tour Championship

Before the final round of the 2026 Tour Championship tees off at East Lake Golf Club, one number stands above everything else in professional golf this season: five. Five consecutive years as the FedExCup No. 1 seed. Five straight seasons in which Scottie Scheffler has been the undisputed top earner of FedExCup points from the opening tournament to the finale. No player in the history of the FedExCup era has done it. Not four times, not three times in a row. Not anyone.

What the Record Actually Means

The FedExCup No. 1 seed is not simply a statistical footnote. It is the most meaningful single-season ranking in professional golf — a measure of consistency, performance in majors, performance in elevated events, and the ability to maintain elite output across a full 10-plus month schedule.

To do it once is a great season. To do it twice is excellence. To do it five consecutive times, across a span of multiple calendar years with different course setups, different competition levels, and different physical and mental challenges, is something that requires a word beyond excellence. It is dominance of a kind that professional golf has rarely seen at this sustained level.

The practical consequence of Scheffler's five consecutive No. 1 seed finishes: he has started every Tour Championship since 2022 with the maximum staggered-start advantage — meaning he has entered each FedExCup finale as the player closest to the trophy before a single ball is struck. That structural advantage, built entirely by his regular-season performance, is the system working exactly as designed.

The $23 Million Bonus

Scheffler's $23 million FedExCup No. 1 bonus is already locked regardless of what happens in today's final round. That bonus — on top of his tournament prize money across the 2025-26 season — makes this one of the most financially significant years in the history of professional sports. It also reflects the PGA Tour's investment in rewarding the season's most consistent performer.

He enters the Tour Championship final round at -12, tied for third, three shots off Viktor Hovland's lead. The bonus is secured. The trophy is not — and that distinction matters to a player who does not measure seasons by financial metrics alone.

Scheffler's Season in Context

The 2025-26 season that produced this fifth consecutive No. 1 seed has included the usual Scheffler hallmarks: multiple wins in elevated events, consistent contention in majors, and a ball-striking floor that few players in the history of the game have matched at sustained levels. At 21 PGA Tour wins entering today's final round, Scheffler has already built a Hall of Fame résumé before he has reached his peak years.

The Tour Championship victory — if he closes the gap on Hovland today — would add one more chapter to a season record. But the legacy is not contingent on what happens in the next few hours. It is already written.

What's Next

The Tour Championship final round tees off today at East Lake Golf Club. Whether Scheffler wins the tournament or finishes behind Hovland and Gerard, his place in FedExCup history is secure. Five consecutive No. 1 seeds is the record. For now, it belongs to one player.
