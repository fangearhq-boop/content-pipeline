LIV Golf at a Crossroads: PIF Is Out, a New Deal Is Close, and the 2027 Season Is Not Yet Guaranteed

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | August 30, 2026

LIV Golf · PIF · Golf Business · 2027 Season

As the 2026 PGA Tour season reaches its finale at East Lake, the rival tour that launched four years ago with Saudi backing is navigating the most uncertain stretch in its short history. Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund — the sovereign wealth fund that created LIV Golf and bankrolled it through its first four seasons — is withdrawing as the primary funder after 2026. The era that began with nine-figure player contracts and a $6 billion investment is over. What comes next is still being determined.

The PIF Exit

The Public Investment Fund's withdrawal from LIV Golf is not a sudden decision. Over the course of four seasons — from the 2022 London debut through the 2026 Indianapolis finale, which Jon Rahm won for his third consecutive individual title — PIF spent an estimated $6 billion or more on operations, player salaries, prize money, and tournament infrastructure.

That investment bought LIV Golf legitimacy, global events, and the biggest names in professional golf. It did not buy the one thing LIV most needed: a pathway to the mainstream of professional golf or a sustainable independent business model. Now PIF is stepping back, and LIV Golf's leadership is responsible for securing the league's own future.

The New Deal

According to Forbes, LIV Golf is "well on its way" to closing a new investment deal worth between $250 million and $350 million. The deal would fund LIV Golf through at least the 2027 season and support the creation of what the league is internally calling "LIV 2.0" — a restructured version of the tour with a new independent board of directors and a multi-partner investment model rather than a single state-backed funder.

LIV CEO Scott O'Neil said the new investor deal is "signed" and described the close as near-complete, with a September target. An independent board has already been formed. The organizational structure of a post-PIF LIV Golf is at least partially in place.

The 2027 Season: Announced but Not Certain

LIV Golf has announced four 2027 tournament dates: Saudi Arabia, Australia, South Africa, and Hong Kong. Those four events represent the beginning of a planned 2027 calendar. But LIV leadership has been explicit that the announced schedule is contingent on the new funding deal closing.

Not everyone is confident it will. Greg Norman, who founded LIV Golf and served as its first CEO, said in a recent interview that he would not guarantee the 2027 season happens. Ian Poulter, one of LIV's marquee names, has spoken publicly about the possibility that "time might run out." Two people who know LIV Golf from the inside, expressing uncertainty about its next season, is a notable signal.

The Players' Situation

More than 50 players are currently contracted to LIV Golf. Among them is Jon Rahm, who just completed his third consecutive individual title at the 2026 Indianapolis finale — arguably the most decorated record of the LIV era. Those players, many of whom forfeited PGA Tour cards and Ryder Cup eligibility to sign LIV contracts, are waiting on resolution that has not yet arrived.

If the new funding deal closes in September as O'Neil has suggested, the 2027 season proceeds and player contracts carry forward. If the deal does not close, LIV Golf players face a golf landscape in which the PGA Tour has continued growing without them and readmission pathways are not straightforwardly available.

What's Next

The September target for closing the new investment deal is the pivotal date. If it closes, LIV Golf 2.0 is real and the 2027 season moves forward. If it does not close on that timeline, the uncertainty that has surrounded LIV Golf this summer will deepen. Fifty-plus professional golfers, four announced tournament host cities, and a $250 million deal are all waiting on the same outcome.
