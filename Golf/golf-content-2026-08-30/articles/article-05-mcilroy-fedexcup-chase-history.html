McIlroy's FedExCup Chase: Three Titles Down, One Away from Standing Alone in History

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | August 30, 2026

FedExCup · Rory McIlroy · PGA Tour · Tour Championship · East Lake

The numbers are exact and the implication is clear. Rory McIlroy has won the FedExCup three times — in 2016, 2019, and 2022. Tiger Woods has also won the FedExCup three times. Right now, the two most celebrated players of the modern era share the all-time record for FedExCup titles, tied at three apiece. A fourth FedExCup for McIlroy changes that forever.

The Record and What It Means

The FedExCup is the PGA Tour's season-long championship, encompassing points accumulated across dozens of events from October through August. Winning it three times, across a span of six seasons as McIlroy has done, requires the kind of sustained excellence that separates great players from the all-time greats.

Tiger Woods won the FedExCup in its inaugural 2007 season, then again in 2009, and a third time in 2013. Those three titles, spread across seven years, established the record that McIlroy tied with his 2022 victory. A fourth title for McIlroy would give him sole possession of the greatest FedExCup record in history — the measure of consistent excellence across a full PGA Tour season, repeated more times than anyone.

McIlroy knows this. He has spoken openly about his FedExCup legacy and his desire to stand alone at the top of the all-time standings. The record is not incidental to his motivation today.

The Path from Here

McIlroy enters the Tour Championship final round approximately six shots off the lead — a deficit that requires a near-perfect round of golf to close while also needing the leaders to stumble. A 62 or 63 from McIlroy, combined with stumbling from Viktor Hovland, Ryan Gerard, and Scottie Scheffler above him, would put him in the FedExCup conversation.

The FedExCup title is not awarded purely to the Tour Championship winner — it incorporates full-season points accumulated across the entire PGA Tour season, adjusted by the staggered-start format. McIlroy's position on the overall FedExCup points table, along with today's round, will determine whether the record stays tied or falls to him alone.

It is a tall order. McIlroy has made a career of tall orders. The reigning Masters champion, who won at Augusta earlier this year, is not a player who approaches deficits with doubt. He approaches them as problems to solve.

McIlroy's Season

The Masters victory at Augusta earlier this year was the most significant win of McIlroy's season and one of the defining moments of his career — completing a career Grand Slam that had eluded him for over a decade. A Masters champion entering the Tour Championship with a chance to also claim the FedExCup record is the kind of season that becomes the subject of retrospectives.

Even if the record stays tied after today, the 2025-26 season is among the best of McIlroy's career. But if he can find a round today at East Lake that matches the best of his career — and if the leaders cooperate — sole ownership of the greatest FedExCup record in history is still on the table.

What's Next

The Tour Championship final round tees off at East Lake Golf Club today. By late afternoon, the FedExCup champion will be crowned. Whether it is McIlroy — chasing history — or one of the leaders ahead of him, today's round at East Lake will decide one of the most storied individual records in PGA Tour history.
