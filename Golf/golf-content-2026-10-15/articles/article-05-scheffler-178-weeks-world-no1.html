Scheffler's ~178 Weeks at No. 1: Tiger's Record Is Two Years Away

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap

October 15, 2026

This is a storyline that doesn't get nearly enough attention.

Scottie Scheffler is approaching approximately 178 consecutive weeks as the world's No. 1 ranked golfer — the longest uninterrupted stretch of dominance in the post-Tiger era of professional golf. Week by week, quietly, a record that once seemed untouchable is coming into view.

The Number That Matters: 281

Tiger Woods held the world No. 1 ranking for 281 consecutive weeks, a streak that ran from June 12, 2005 to October 30, 2010. It's one of the most comprehensive demonstrations of sustained dominance in professional sports — five-plus years of being not just the best, but demonstrably, measurably better than everyone else in the game.

As of mid-July 2026, Scheffler was beginning his 166th consecutive week at No. 1. Extrapolating forward to mid-October puts the current estimated count at approximately 178 consecutive weeks. The math from there: to break Tiger's record, Scheffler must hold No. 1 until approximately October 2028 — roughly two full years from now.

That's not a guarantee. It's not even a likelihood anyone should casually assume. But the fact that it's calculable — that it's on the same timeline — is remarkable on its own.

The Gap Is the Story

What makes Scheffler's streak sustainable is not just his results. It's the OWGR gap between him and the rest of the field.

As of mid-2026, Scheffler held approximately 743 OWGR points against Rory McIlroy's approximately 377 points at No. 2. That's nearly a 400-point lead. In the OWGR system, which weights recent results across rolling periods, maintaining a margin that large requires consistent, elite-level play — but it also means short-term variance (missing a cut, skipping an event, or finishing mid-field for a stretch) doesn't immediately threaten the streak.

To dethrone Scheffler, someone would need to win multiple major events and numerous regular tour events over an extended period while Scheffler simultaneously entered a relative slump. That combination, while possible, is genuinely unlikely given the current landscape.

Historical Context

Scheffler's streak already places him in the rarest company in the history of the world golf rankings, which have been maintained since 1986. Only Tiger Woods and Greg Norman have held No. 1 for longer total periods — consecutive or otherwise. Approaching 200 total weeks at No. 1 puts Scheffler in a tier that fewer than a handful of players have ever reached.

What makes this stretch different from historical comparisons is the competitive depth of the modern game. The global player pool is deeper, the international tour schedules more demanding, and the OWGR algorithm more rigorous than what Norman navigated in the late 1980s. Scheffler's dominance exists within a more competitive environment than perhaps any previous No. 1 holder faced.

What Could Stop the Streak

The honest answer is injury. Scheffler's margins are wide enough that a slump alone is unlikely to cost him No. 1 in the short term. A significant injury — one that keeps him off the course for several months — would erode his OWGR points while a resurgent McIlroy or another player strings together a run of wins. That's the realistic scenario that ends the streak before October 2028.

Absent injury, the record chase continues week by week. It may be the most underappreciated streak in professional golf right now.

What's Next

Scheffler's next confirmed PGA Tour start is the Butterfield Bermuda Championship, October 22-25 at Port Royal Golf Course. Each week he competes — or doesn't — adds to or maintains his OWGR lead. The next major update to the record count comes with the next official OWGR release. At the current trajectory, the 200-consecutive-week milestone is on the near-term horizon, and the 281-week record remains the distant summit.
