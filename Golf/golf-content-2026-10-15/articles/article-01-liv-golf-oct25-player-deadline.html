LIV Golf's Oct. 25 Player Deadline: 10 Days to Decide the Tour's Future

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap

October 15, 2026

Ten days. That's all that remains before LIV Golf reaches one of the most pivotal moments in its brief, turbulent history.

The October 25 player decision deadline — extended from an earlier October 13 cutoff — will determine whether BC Partners' $300 million restructuring plan clears the player support threshold required to move forward. If it does, LIV 2.0 becomes a real organization. If it doesn't, the league that spent billions reshaping professional golf faces a much bleaker path out of Chapter 11.

The Restructuring Math

LIV Golf filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in New Jersey on September 8, 2026. BC Partners, a London-based credit firm, has committed initial funding toward the restructuring plan. But the deal has a condition: at least 50% of LIV players with outstanding claims — representing two-thirds of the total player claim value — must sign with LIV 2.0 for the restructuring to proceed.

Reports from Front Office Sports suggest LIV has secured "more than enough verbal commitments" to clear the threshold. Verbal commitments, however, are not signed agreements. The October 25 deadline is when those commitments must become official.

One source of caution: a court-appointed attorney in the bankruptcy proceedings has warned there is "a significant risk" the tour doesn't emerge from Chapter 11. That's not spin — it's a frank assessment of how uncertain the outcome remains.

Who Is Out

Judge Kaplan has approved LIV's motion to reject 19 player contracts, including those of Bryson DeChambeau, Dustin Johnson, Bubba Watson, Ian Poulter, and Lee Westwood. That doesn't mean those players are barred from LIV 2.0 — contract rejection in bankruptcy is a legal mechanism, not necessarily a final severance. But it does change the terms under which they could return.

Jon Rahm has been the most explicit about his intentions. In court proceedings on October 7, Rahm declared he will not participate in LIV 2.0. His attorneys called the restructuring terms "unacceptable." Rahm has committed to play the Abu Dhabi Championship on the DP World Tour this fall, and his legal team's statements leave little ambiguity about his 2027 trajectory.

Bryson DeChambeau's situation has an added layer: his agency is disputing a $1.2 million claim in the bankruptcy proceedings, contending it is not owed any money. That dispute is separate from the question of whether DeChambeau himself signs with LIV 2.0.

The Creditors' Committee

Yesterday, October 15, was the scheduled meeting of the unsecured creditors committee — a group that includes only one current LIV player, Michael La Sasso. The committee's composition suggests most of the major player decisions are playing out separately from the formal creditor process, in direct negotiations with LIV's restructuring team.

What's Next

The October 25 deadline is the first real test of whether LIV 2.0 has a viable player base. In the next 10 days, expect additional public statements from players who are walking — or staying. If enough sign, the restructuring moves forward and BC Partners' $300 million commitment becomes operative. If not, the bankruptcy proceeding enters a far more uncertain phase.

Either way, the outcome will immediately reshape the 2027 player market for the PGA Tour and DP World Tour. Free agents with cash-out clauses or rejected contracts become eligible to negotiate elsewhere — and they're already doing exactly that.
