Butterfield Bermuda Championship Preview: Port Royal, Wind, and the Race for FedExCup Points

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap

October 15, 2026

One week from today, professional golf travels to one of its most unique venues — and one that rewards players with something different than sheer length or course-knowledge accumulated over decades.

The Butterfield Bermuda Championship runs October 22-25 at Port Royal Golf Course on Bermuda's South Shore. It's a $6 million event with 500 FedExCup Fall points on the line, and it's the kind of tournament where the winner is often a player who reads the wind better than anyone in the field that week.

The Course: Port Royal

Port Royal Golf Course was designed by Robert Trent Jones and opened in 1970. It plays as a par-71 at 6,828 yards — modest by modern tour standards, but that number doesn't tell the real story. The course sits on Bermuda's South Shore, exposed to Atlantic trade winds that can swing from 10 to 30 miles per hour in the same round. A hole that plays into a gentle breeze in the morning becomes a completely different challenge by afternoon when the wind shifts.

Elevation changes along the coast add visual difficulty on top of the club-selection puzzle. The course rewards patience and precision over aggression — particularly on approach shots where misjudging wind strength by a single club can produce a bogey or worse.

Notably, Port Royal is a public course. It's not a private members club or a tournament-only facility — it's accessible to recreational golfers who want to play where the pros play. That accessibility gives Bermuda's Championship week a different energy than most tour stops.

The Signature Hole: 16th

No preview of Port Royal is complete without the par-3 16th. At approximately 235 yards over water, it's a hole that has ended more than a few tournament runs over the course of the Bermuda Championship's history. The elevated tee and the ocean view make it visually spectacular. The combination of carry distance, wind uncertainty, and a green that's tough to hold from the wrong side makes it genuinely treacherous.

Players who are leading on Sunday afternoon face a birdie opportunity that can feel more like a trap than a chance. A par at 16 on a breezy day is a professional victory.

Defending Champion: Adam Schenk

Adam Schenk won the 2025 Butterfield Bermuda Championship at -12 (272 total), edging Chandler Phillips by one stroke. It was a grind-it-out performance on a course that punishes mistakes, and Schenk navigated it better than anyone that week.

Defending on Port Royal isn't easy. The field composition in Bermuda includes players who schedule specifically to peak in wind-dependent conditions — they've studied the course, they know the hole locations that create danger, and they arrive with a specific game plan. Schenk will need to replicate his 2025 form against a field that has had a full year to study what he did.

FedExCup Fall Context

With 500 FedExCup Fall points available, the Bermuda Championship matters for players looking to build positioning heading into the final stretch of the fall schedule. Several players on the PGA Tour's projected bubble — needing wins or top finishes to secure cards or improve their standing — will target Bermuda as a winnable event in a field that isn't loaded with major champions.

That dynamic often produces first-time winners or surprise contenders at Bermuda. The combination of wind skill, course management, and a field without the sport's absolute elite creates genuine opportunity for mid-range PGA Tour talent.
