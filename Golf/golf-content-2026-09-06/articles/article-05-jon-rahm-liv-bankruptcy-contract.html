Jon Rahm's $100 Million Question — What LIV Bankruptcy Means for Golf's Biggest Contract

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | September 6, 2026

When Jon Rahm left the PGA Tour for LIV Golf, he did not leave quietly. He left with what was reported to be the most lucrative player contract in professional golf history — a deal worth more than $100 million in guaranteed money.

Now, with LIV Golf expected to file for Chapter 11 bankruptcy as soon as this week, the contract that defined his move is at the center of the sport's most complicated financial reckoning.

Rahm is not alone. Every LIV player with a guaranteed contract faces uncertainty. But Rahm's reported sum makes his situation the most discussed, the most consequential, and the one the rest of the field watches most closely.

Three Years, Three Titles, One Question

Rahm's time at LIV has not been without success. He recently won his third consecutive LIV Golf individual title at Indianapolis — a dominant run that underscores his continued elite-level play. Whatever else has gone wrong with the business, Rahm's performance on the course has not been in doubt.

But performance and payment are separate conversations now. The league that guaranteed his money is approaching insolvency, and the Saudi sovereign wealth fund that bankrolled the operation is pulling its financial support after this season. PIF, which spent more than $5 billion to launch and sustain LIV, has made its decision. The era of unlimited funding is over.

The Three Paths

Players in Rahm's position are being offered settlements described as "pennies on the dollar" by sources familiar with the negotiations. The figure — a fraction of what was promised — presents each player with a version of the same decision.

One path: accept the reduced settlement and remain with a restructured "LIV 2.0" — a smaller circuit with roughly 10 events, player equity, and reduced purses. The money is less. The competitive calendar is smaller. But the tour continues.

A second path: accept the settlement and walk away, potentially pursuing reinstatement to the PGA Tour or DP World Tour. The timeline for that process is not guaranteed, and the terms of reinstatement would be subject to negotiation with the tours.

A third path: reject the settlement and pursue full contractual claims through the bankruptcy proceedings. This is the slowest route, with no guaranteed outcome, and extends the uncertainty indefinitely.

For Rahm, the financial stakes of that choice are larger than for almost anyone else in the field.

What's Next

The bankruptcy filing, expected in a New Jersey federal court in the week of September 7, will set the legal framework for everything that follows. Player decisions will cascade over the weeks and months after the filing. For Rahm — who gave up a world No. 1 ranking, multiple major championships, and an established place in the PGA Tour order to chase LIV's promise — the resolution of that decision will define the back half of his career. Golf is waiting to see which path he chooses.
