LIV Golf Bankruptcy Filing Expected This Week — PIF Ends the Experiment

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | September 6, 2026

The week that changes professional golf may have arrived.

LIV Golf is expected to file for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection as soon as the week of September 7, according to reporting from the Financial Times and confirmed by multiple outlets. Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, which provided the billions that made LIV Golf possible, is ending its financial commitment to the league following the 2026 season. After more than $5 billion spent since the league's 2022 launch, PIF has determined it will not continue to fund the operation as currently structured.

The filing, expected in a New Jersey federal court, would be one of the most significant events in professional golf's modern history.

What Chapter 11 Means

A Chapter 11 filing is a restructuring, not a shutdown. The distinction matters. Under Chapter 11, LIV Golf would continue to operate while working through its financial obligations under court supervision. PIF is expected to provide less than $100 million in debtor-in-possession financing — emergency funding that keeps the lights on during the proceedings.

Simultaneously, LIV has been in negotiations with BC Partners, a private capital firm that could invest up to $300 million in LIV's assets as part of a restructured entity. The proposed "LIV 2.0" would be smaller than the current league: a 10-tournament global schedule, reduced purses, and a player equity structure intended to align incentives differently than the guaranteed-money model that defined LIV's original pitch to stars.

The Player Problem

The most immediate human impact falls on the players. LIV's model was built on guaranteed contracts — multi-year deals that promised players money regardless of performance or tour health. Those deals are now in jeopardy.

Settlement offers have reportedly been sent to contracted players. The terms, described by sources as "pennies on the dollar," represent a fraction of what the players were promised. Jon Rahm — whose contract is reportedly worth more than $100 million — is among those facing the most consequential decision of his professional career.

Every LIV player now faces a version of the same choice: accept the reduced settlement and stay with a restructured LIV, accept the settlement and leave (potentially pursuing reinstatement to the PGA Tour), or reject the settlement and pursue full contractual claims through the bankruptcy process — a slower and less certain path.

What's Next

The week of September 7 will likely mark the beginning of a legal and structural process that takes months to resolve. The filing date, the shape of the BC Partners deal, and the player decisions that follow will collectively determine what professional golf looks like on the other side. For fans who watched LIV reshape the sport — for better or worse — the bankruptcy proceedings represent the final chapter of an era that began with a simple and disruptive premise: that guaranteed money could lure golf's stars away from the established order. It worked, for a while. Now the bill has come due.
