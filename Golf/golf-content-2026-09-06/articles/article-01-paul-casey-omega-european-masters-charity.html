Paul Casey Wins Omega European Masters — and Pledges Every Penny to Crans-Montana Fire Victims

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | September 6, 2026

Paul Casey won the Omega European Masters on Sunday afternoon at Crans-sur-Sierre Golf Club. He finished at 16-under par, held on from a three-shot lead he built the day before, and lifted the trophy in the Swiss Alps after one of the most emotionally charged weeks professional golf has seen in years.

The score mattered. But the story of this week was never really about the score.

On Saturday evening, after posting a stunning 7-under 63 in the third round — including a 29 on the front nine, six birdies in seven holes — Casey stood before the cameras and told the world what he intended to do with his prize money. His voice broke. The words came out anyway.

All of it. Every euro. Going to the victims of the Le Constellation bar fire.

The Fire That Changed Crans-Montana

On New Year's Day 2026, a fire broke out at the Le Constellation bar in Crans-Montana — the resort village that sits just minutes from the Crans-sur-Sierre Golf Club. The blaze killed 41 people and injured 115 more. It was one of the deadliest disasters in recent Swiss history, and it scarred a community that Casey has visited and competed in throughout his career.

"There's a lot to play for," Casey said Saturday, barely holding back tears.

He was talking about winning. He was also talking about something larger than golf.

The Round That Won It

Casey's third round was the turning point. He birdied six of seven holes on the front nine at Crans-sur-Sierre, posting a 29 to make the turn in brilliant form. He added more gains on the back and signed for a 63 — a round that moved him from the chasing pack into a three-shot lead with one round to play.

At 49, Casey is among the oldest players competing regularly on the DP World Tour. His age is not incidental to the story — it is part of it. To post a round like that, on a moving day, with the context of the week weighing on his mind, required something beyond technique. It required resolve.

Sunday's final round confirmed it. Casey closed it out and walked off the 18th green as champion.

What's Next

Paul Casey will not be keeping his prize money from the 2026 Omega European Masters. It will go to the families and survivors of the Le Constellation fire — a decision that transforms what might have been a fine late-career victory into something that will be remembered far beyond golf. For the residents of Crans-Montana who watched a competitor they know well put on the best performance of his recent career in their town, in their memory, this week will mean everything.
