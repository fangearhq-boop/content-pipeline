### STORY 1: Paul Casey Wins Omega European Masters

Paul Casey won the Omega European Masters on Sunday at Crans-sur-Sierre — and he did it in the most unforgettable way imaginable.

The 49-year-old Englishman closed out a 16-under week in the Swiss Alps after posting one of the rounds of the year on Saturday: a 7-under 63, including a staggering 29 on the front nine. He entered the final round with a three-shot cushion and held on to claim the title.

But the score was almost secondary to what Casey had announced the night before. A visibly emotional Casey pledged to donate his entire prize money from this week's tournament to victims of the Le Constellation bar fire — a tragedy that struck Crans-Montana on New Year's Day, killing 41 people and injuring 115 more. The resort community is just minutes from the golf course where Casey has played so many times throughout his career.

"There's a lot to play for," Casey said, barely holding back tears.

At 49, Casey is among the oldest winners on the DP World Tour in recent years. His win is the kind of story that transcends sport — a veteran player at the end of his career finding meaning, community, and victory in the same moment.

### STORY 2: LIV Golf Bankruptcy Filing Expected This Week

The most consequential week in LIV Golf's short history may be upon us.

According to multiple reports, LIV Golf is expected to file for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection as soon as the week of September 7 — a move that would formalize what golf fans have watched unfold for months. Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, which poured more than $5 billion into LIV since the league's 2022 launch, is ending its funding commitment after this season. PIF is expected to provide less than $100 million in debtor-in-possession financing to keep LIV operating during proceedings.

The league has been in talks with BC Partners, a private capital firm reportedly willing to invest up to $300 million in LIV's assets as part of a restructuring. Under the proposed "LIV 2.0" framework, the tour would shrink to 10 events with player equity and reduced purses.

Players have already received settlement offers described by sources as "pennies on the dollar." Jon Rahm — reportedly owed more than $100 million in guaranteed money — faces one of the most consequential decisions in his career.

Whatever happens this week, golf will not look the same on the other side.

### STORY 3: Solheim Cup Eve — Practice Begins Tomorrow at Bernardus Golf

After months of anticipation, the 2026 Solheim Cup is here.

Practice rounds begin Monday, September 7, at Bernardus Golf in Cromvoirt, Netherlands — the first Solheim Cup ever played on Dutch soil. The PING Junior Solheim Cup also opens Monday. Match play competition begins Friday, September 11, with foursomes and fourballs on Friday and Saturday, and singles on Sunday.

For USA captain Angela Stanford, Monday marks the beginning of the week her team has prepared for all year. World No. 1 Nelly Korda leads a 12-player squad that also includes Jennifer Kupcho, Lilia Vu, Rose Zhang, and Megan Khang. Europe captain Anna Nordqvist has said pairings decisions were a "puzzle" — but the week to solve it has arrived.

The United States are the defending champions, having won the 2024 Solheim Cup 15.5-12.5. Europe won three straight Solheim Cups before that. The momentum could go either direction.

Whatever happens at Bernardus Golf this week, it starts tomorrow.

### STORY 4: Jackson Koivun — The 21-Year-Old Heading to Medinah

Three months ago, Jackson Koivun was a college golfer at Auburn.

This September, at age 21, he will represent the United States at the Presidents Cup at Medinah Country Club — the second-youngest American ever selected for the event, behind only Jordan Spieth, who was 20 when he made his debut in 2013.

Koivun turned professional in June and made his mark almost immediately. In just his third professional start, he won the 3M Open in July — shooting a tournament-record 25-under 259 and defeating world No. 1 Scottie Scheffler by three strokes. USA captain Brandt Snedeker selected Koivun as one of his six captain's picks on September 1.

"I just have a gut feeling," Snedeker said.

The Presidents Cup runs September 24-27 at Medinah Country Club No. 3 in Medinah, Illinois. International captain Geoff Ogilvy will look to pull off the upset against one of the deepest American teams in years. But it may be the youngest player on the board — the kid from Auburn who was in college just three months ago — who gives Team USA its most electric moments.

### STORY 5: Jon Rahm's $100M Question — What LIV Bankruptcy Means for Golf's Most Valuable Contract

When Jon Rahm signed with LIV Golf, it was reportedly the most expensive player deal in professional golf history.

Now, as LIV prepares for a Chapter 11 bankruptcy filing as soon as this week, Rahm finds himself at the center of the sport's most consequential financial story.

Rahm — who recently won his third consecutive LIV Golf individual title at Indianapolis — is reportedly owed more than $100 million in guaranteed money from his LIV contract. With the league now sending settlement offers to players described as "pennies on the dollar," Rahm faces the same three-way decision confronting every contracted LIV player:

Accept a reduced settlement and remain with a restructured LIV 2.0 — a smaller 10-event circuit with player equity but far less money. Accept a settlement and walk away, potentially pursuing reinstatement to the PGA Tour or other tours. Or reject the settlement and pursue his full contractual claims through the bankruptcy process — a slower, uncertain road with no guaranteed outcome.

The sport he helped reshape is now reshaping itself around him. Whatever Rahm decides, it will set a precedent for every other player in LIV's roster.

Golf's next chapter is being written in a New Jersey federal courthouse.
