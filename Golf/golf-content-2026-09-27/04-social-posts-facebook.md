### STORY 3: LIV Golf Bankruptcy

**Long-Form Post (1:00 PM ET)**
LIV Golf's future is now in the hands of its players — and there are 16 days to decide.

Here's the full picture of where things stand:

📌 September 8: LIV filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in New Jersey federal court. Total liabilities: $500 million to $1 billion. The trigger: Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund pulled its backing.

💰 The rescue deal: London-based private equity firm BC Partners is offering $300 million to fund a restructured "LIV 2.0" where players would become majority owners. The Saudi PIF agreed to provide $49.6 million in temporary financing to keep the lights on during the process.

⏰ The deadline: October 13. Players representing at least two-thirds of total claim value AND at least half of total claim count must commit to the restructuring — or the deal collapses.

💵 What players are owed: Jon Rahm is the largest creditor at approximately $7.5 million. Bryson DeChambeau is owed approximately $5.8 million. Dustin Johnson and Cameron Smith are also listed as unsecured creditors. Total owed to players: approximately $45 million.

🚪 The PGA Tour: Closed the return window. No pathway currently for LIV players.

The question is whether Rahm, DeChambeau, Smith, and the rest will bet on themselves by committing to LIV 2.0 — or walk away from the money they're owed and try to find another path forward.

October 13 is 16 days away. Comment below — do you think LIV 2.0 will actually happen? 👇

### STORY 5: Lauren Coughlin's Nine-Birdie LPGA Record

**Long-Form Post (3:30 PM ET)**
Nine consecutive birdies to open a round. That's what Lauren Coughlin did at the Walmart NW Arkansas Championship on Saturday.

Nine. In a row.

It is an LPGA Tour record — the most consecutive birdies from the start of a round in the tournament's history.

The remarkable part? Coughlin's historic start didn't even put her in the lead. Yuna Nishimura was so dominant this week that Coughlin's nine consecutive birdies still left her chasing.

That's how exceptional the scoring has been at Pinnacle Country Club in Rogers, Arkansas this week. Low numbers everywhere — but one player, Nishimura, managed to be 5 shots better than everyone else.

History was made in both Round 2 performances — and only one of them is being talked about as the lead story. Sometimes golf is just like that.
