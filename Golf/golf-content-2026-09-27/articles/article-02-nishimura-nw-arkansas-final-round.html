Yuna Nishimura Leads NW Arkansas Championship by Five — One Round From First LPGA Tour Win

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | September 27, 2026

Yuna Nishimura has done everything but win on the LPGA Tour. On Sunday at Pinnacle Country Club in Rogers, Arkansas, she has 18 holes to finally change that.

The 26-year-old from Japan enters the final round of the Walmart NW Arkansas Championship with a five-shot lead at 16-under par — a margin built on back-to-back rounds of 8-under 63 that matched the tournament's all-time 36-hole scoring record. The leaderboard is stacked behind her, but no one is close enough to make this feel inevitable.

How She Got Here

Nishimura's first round was impressive. Her second round was something more. She opened Thursday at 8-under 63, sharing the lead with a crowded field in a week when low scoring was the norm across the entire leaderboard.

Then she went out Friday and did it again. Another 63. Another flawless display of iron play and putting at a Pinnacle Country Club course that was yielding birdies freely to the entire field — and still couldn't hold Nishimura's pace.

The 36-hole total of 16-under matched the tournament record for scoring through two rounds. Nishimura hadn't just taken the lead; she had taken it emphatically, turning a crowded tournament into a one-player show.

The Chase Pack

Five shots back heading into Sunday, three players share second place at 11-under: Auston Kim, Mary Liu, and LPGA rookie Michelle Zhang. All three will need Nishimura to stumble, and significantly, to have any chance of forcing a playoff.

Zhang, in her debut LPGA season, has been one of the stories of the week — but the arithmetic is daunting. A five-shot swing over 18 holes is not impossible in professional golf, but it requires the leader to play well above even par while the chasing players put together something extraordinary.

Nishimura has not shown any cracks over the first 36 holes. There is no reason yet to believe Sunday will be different.

The Longer Wait

What makes Sunday meaningful goes beyond a single tournament result. Nishimura has six victories on the Japan LPGA Tour, where she established herself as a consistent contender and a fan favorite. But she has spent four seasons on the LPGA Tour circuit without breaking through — four years of near-misses, top-ten finishes, and weekends that ended just short of the winner's circle.

Sunday in Arkansas is the closest she has been. A five-shot lead with 18 holes to play is exactly the kind of position a player dreams about, and exactly the kind of position that can feel fragile when a career win has remained just out of reach.

The LPGA Tour has seen first-time winners finally break through after long waits. Nishimura's story fits that arc. She has the talent, the scoring record to prove it, and now the lead to back it up.

What's Next

The final round tees off Sunday morning at Pinnacle Country Club. Nishimura plays in the final pairing alongside whoever is closest entering the day. The scoring has been low all week — any of the chasers putting together a 61 or 62 will need help from the front.

If Nishimura closes it out, she becomes the latest Japanese player to win on the LPGA Tour — a circuit that has seen significant success from Japanese women over the past decade. More importantly for her, it ends four seasons of chasing a win she has clearly had the game to achieve.

Eighteen holes. Five shots. Yuna Nishimura is one final round from the win that changes everything.
