Lauren Coughlin Opens Round With Nine Consecutive Birdies — Sets LPGA Tour Record

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | September 27, 2026

Nine consecutive birdies. From the very first hole. An LPGA Tour record — and somehow not even the biggest story of the day.

Lauren Coughlin made history Saturday at the Walmart NW Arkansas Championship, opening her second round at Pinnacle Country Club in Rogers, Arkansas with nine straight birdies. The feat set the LPGA Tour record for consecutive birdies from the start of a round — and then the tournament moved on without her as its lead story, because Yuna Nishimura went out and shot 63 to lead the field by five shots.

That context doesn't diminish what Coughlin did. It just tells you how extraordinary the scoring was this week in Arkansas.

Nine in a Row

The mechanics of nine consecutive birdies to open a round are worth sitting with for a moment. Not one bogey. Not one par to break the rhythm. Hole after hole, Coughlin stepped up and made birdie — nine times in a row, from the first swing of her round to the ninth hole.

In tour golf, making three or four consecutive birdies feels special. A player making five or six in a row draws attention across the course. Nine consecutive birdies is the kind of number that sounds like a typo when you first hear it — and then you check, and it's right.

Pinnacle Country Club was yielding low scores all week. The greens were receptive, the course set-up allowed aggressive play, and the field was consistently scoring in the low 60s. But Coughlin's nine-birdie run exceeded even the exceptional baseline of a low-scoring week. She was on a pace that would have produced one of the lowest individual rounds in LPGA Tour history if she had maintained it.

The Irony of the Record

Here is the most remarkable part of Coughlin's historic round: she never led the tournament.

Nishimura's back-to-back 63s put her at 16-under for the tournament by the end of Friday. Coughlin's nine-birdie start on Saturday was spectacular — but it was chasing a player who had already built an extraordinary lead through two rounds of her own historic scoring.

In any other week, in any other tournament, nine consecutive birdies to open a round would be the defining headline. At NW Arkansas in September 2026, it was the second-best scoring story of the day. That is a testament to how Nishimura played, and how exceptional the week as a whole turned out to be at Pinnacle Country Club.

Coughlin's Place in the Field

Despite her historic start, Coughlin finished Saturday without the tournament lead. The five-shot deficit she was chasing proved too large even for nine consecutive birdies to overcome in a single round. She entered Sunday's final round as part of the chase pack trying to run down Nishimura's commanding 16-under total.

Her round will be remembered long after the specifics of her standing on Saturday's leaderboard fade. Records like this one outlast any single event. Coughlin wrote her name into LPGA Tour history at Pinnacle Country Club — in a week when that history happened to compete for attention with another player's equally extraordinary performance.

What's Next

The final round of the Walmart NW Arkansas Championship tees off Sunday with Nishimura holding a five-shot lead and Coughlin among the players trying to close the gap. Whatever happens in the final round, Saturday's record will stand on its own as one of the tournament's lasting moments.

Nine consecutive birdies to open a round. An LPGA Tour record. In a week of extraordinary scoring — Lauren Coughlin made extraordinary history, even if the story of the week belongs to someone else.
