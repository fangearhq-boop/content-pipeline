LIV Golf Files Chapter 11, BC Partners Offers $300M Rescue — October 13 Deadline Looms

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | September 27, 2026

LIV Golf's future now hinges on a deadline 16 days away, a $300 million rescue offer from a London private equity firm, and the willingness of its biggest stars to bet on themselves one more time.

The tour filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in New Jersey federal court on September 8, ending the Saudi-backed era of the circuit and triggering a restructuring process that will determine whether professional golf's most disruptive league survives into 2027 — or disappears entirely.

What Triggered the Filing

The filing came after Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund withdrew its financial backing from LIV Golf. The PIF had been the sole funding source for the tour since its launch, bankrolling player contracts, tournament purses, and operational costs across events on multiple continents.

When that backing disappeared, the numbers left behind were stark. Court documents listed total liabilities between $500 million and $1 billion. The organization had no viable path to continue operations without a replacement funding source — and no replacement had been arranged before the filing.

The PIF did agree to provide $49.6 million in debtor-in-possession financing to keep the tour's operations running during the bankruptcy process. That funding is a bridge, not a solution. It expires when the restructuring either succeeds or collapses.

The BC Partners Rescue Plan

Into the gap stepped BC Partners, a London-based private equity firm. Their offer: $300 million to fund a restructured entity called "LIV 2.0," with a crucial structural difference from the original tour. Under the BC Partners plan, players would become majority owners of the league — transitioning from contracted employees to equity stakeholders.

The target timeline for a relaunch under this structure is 2027, pending successful completion of the bankruptcy process and player sign-on.

The mechanics of the deal require a specific threshold of player participation to trigger. Players representing at least two-thirds of total claim value, and at least half of total claim count, must formally commit to the restructuring by October 13. If that threshold isn't met, the BC Partners deal falls apart.

What Players Are Owed

The court filings revealed the scale of what LIV players are collectively owed. Total unpaid obligations to players amount to approximately $45 million. The individual creditor amounts reveal who has the most at stake in the October 13 decision.

Jon Rahm, who left the PGA Tour for LIV in one of the tour's highest-profile signings, is listed as the largest creditor at approximately $7.5 million. Bryson DeChambeau is owed approximately $5.8 million. Dustin Johnson and Cameron Smith are also among the listed unsecured creditors.

The structure of the October 13 threshold means that Rahm, DeChambeau, and a handful of other high-value creditors hold significant leverage. If the players owed the largest sums do not commit, reaching two-thirds of total claim value becomes mathematically difficult regardless of how many smaller creditors sign on.

The PGA Tour Position

The PGA Tour has closed the return window for LIV players. There is no stated pathway for players who left the Tour to return, even in the event of LIV's complete collapse. That position could change — the Tour has reversed course before — but as of now, players who walk away from the BC Partners deal have no obvious home.

The Korn Ferry Tour, Asian Tour, and DP World Tour represent potential alternatives for players who don't commit to LIV 2.0 and can't return to the PGA Tour. For players like Rahm, who was ranked among the world's top players before his departure, the calculus is different — but no less difficult.

What's Next

October 13 is the hinge date. In 16 days, the golf world will learn whether a sufficient coalition of LIV players has committed to the BC Partners restructuring. If they have, the bankruptcy process moves toward confirmation and a 2027 relaunch. If they haven't, the deal collapses and the tour's assets enter a liquidation process.

The question facing every LIV player is whether the $300 million from BC Partners and a majority ownership stake in a relaunched league is worth more than walking away from the money they're already owed and finding a different path.

For Rahm, DeChambeau, and the rest, there is no neutral answer. The clock is running.
