International Team Leads 10.5-7.5 Heading Into Presidents Cup Sunday Singles — History Within Reach

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | September 27, 2026

The International Team is on the doorstep of the most historic victory in Presidents Cup history.

Entering Sunday singles at Medinah Country Club, Geoff Ogilvy's International squad holds a 10.5–7.5 lead over the United States — the first time in the event's 32-year history that the Internationals have held a lead on American soil heading into the final day. They need just five points from 12 singles matches to win.

They have never done this before. Not on American soil. Not anywhere outside Australia since 1998.

How They Got Here

The week unfolded in a way that would have seemed improbable before it started. The Internationals played controlled, composed golf across four sessions of team competition, building their lead methodically against a U.S. team that has dominated this event for three decades.

The turning point came on Day 2, when the International Team swept the foursomes session 5-0 — a near-unprecedented result that turned a close contest into a commanding advantage. The U.S. team fought back on Day 3, winning the morning four-ball session 2.5–1.5 behind a strong showing from Chris Gotterup and Cameron Young, who went 2-0 on Saturday. But the afternoon foursomes session split 2-2, leaving the Internationals in control at 10.5–7.5.

The Singles Lineup

Twelve matches. Five points needed. The stage is set for a day that could rewrite the history of the Presidents Cup.

Scottie Scheffler, the world No. 1, takes on Nico Echavarria in what could be the marquee match of the day. Scheffler went 2-1-0 through the team sessions — the best record of any American player — and will be the U.S. team's most potent weapon in the comeback attempt.

Ogilvy's International squad includes Hideki Matsuyama, Tom Kim, Min Woo Lee, Adam Scott, Corey Conners, Sungjae Im, and Ryan Fox — a team with genuine depth and the collective motivation of 28 years of failure to carry into Sunday.

The Historical Weight

The International Team's only Presidents Cup victory came in 1998 at Royal Melbourne in Australia, a 20.5–11.5 win. Since then: 12 consecutive losses. The event has become so one-sided that the U.S. team's all-time record stands at 13–2.

Medinah has its own place in team golf history. This is the same venue where Europe staged their miraculous 2012 Ryder Cup comeback, coming from 10-6 down to win the cup on the final day. Sunday at Medinah carries the weight of that memory — and the International Team will be drawing on every ounce of it.

What's Next

By the end of Sunday, one of two stories will be written. Either the International Team completes the most historic Presidents Cup victory in history, winning on U.S. soil for the first time ever. Or the United States pulls off an extraordinary comeback, needing to win eight of twelve singles matches to retain the trophy.

Either outcome will be remembered for a generation. The International Team has never been this close. This is their moment.
