Bank of Utah Championship Preview — PGA Tour Fall Resumes at Black Desert Resort

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | September 27, 2026

The Presidents Cup wraps up Sunday at Medinah. By Monday morning, the PGA Tour pivots to Utah, where the Bank of Utah Championship begins a week-long test at Black Desert Resort — and the race for 2027 Tour cards resumes with urgency.

The FedEx Cup Fall series is the Tour's survival competition. Eight events. Limited overlap with the major schedule. And for the players just outside full membership status, every stroke matters more than it does any other time of year.

The Venue: Black Desert Resort

Black Desert Resort sits in southwestern Utah's distinctive volcanic landscape — a setting unlike anything else on the PGA Tour schedule. The course winds through lava fields and red rock terrain that have made it a visual landmark since it opened, and a logistical and strategic puzzle for the players who tackle it.

The resort course does not yield to the typical power-and-wedge formula that dominates most Tour events. Its unusual terrain creates angles and lies that reward course management and creativity off the tee. Players who have been here before arrive with a meaningful advantage over those experiencing the layout for the first time.

The Stakes: 2027 Tour Cards

The Bank of Utah Championship runs September 28 through October 4. It is the second of eight events in the 2026 FedEx Cup Fall series, following the Biltmore Championship in Asheville that opened the fall swing.

The fall series exists for one primary purpose: to determine who holds a PGA Tour card for 2027. Players who do not hold full status coming out of the regular season use these eight events to accumulate points toward securing their card. The pressure is real and the margin for error is thin.

After Utah, the fall schedule moves to Japan for the Baycurrent Classic on October 5-11, then the Butterfield Bermuda Championship. Two of the eight fall events are entirely new for 2026, including the Biltmore Championship that opened the series.

The Bigger Picture

For full Tour members who played the Presidents Cup or are otherwise exempt from the fall series grind, the Bank of Utah Championship offers a chance to build FedEx Cup points or simply stay sharp heading into the fall and winter stretch.

For everyone else, it is one of eight lifelines. Players outside the card bubble need strong weeks repeatedly, and they need them starting now. A missed cut in Utah is not just a bad week — it is a week that cannot be recovered with one good round later.

The fall schedule compresses what the regular season spreads across nine months into eight events. Every leaderboard position, every made cut, every birdie on Sunday afternoon means something to someone's livelihood.

What's Next

First round tee times go off Monday, September 28 at Black Desert Resort in Utah. The field will be announced in the days ahead, but expect a mix of Tour veterans protecting their status and younger players looking to establish themselves with a breakout fall.

Golf doesn't stop for history or heartbreak. The Presidents Cup story gets written Sunday at Medinah. By Monday, the next chapter is already starting in the Utah desert.
