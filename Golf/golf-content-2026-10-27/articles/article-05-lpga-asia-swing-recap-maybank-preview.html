LPGA Asia Swing Wraps With Three Different Champions — Maybank Championship in Kuala Lumpur Next

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | October 27, 2026

Jeeno Thitikul, Ruoning Yin, and Yuri Yoshida each won in succession across the LPGA Tour's October Asia swing, setting up an unpredictable finish to the stretch in Malaysia.

Three tournaments. Three different champions. The 2026 LPGA Tour Asia swing has been a showcase for the breadth of the women's game, with no single player able to establish dominance across the consecutive events that began in Hawaii in early October and concluded in South Korea this past weekend.

The closing leg of the swing, the Maybank Championship in Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia, begins October 29. Based on the three events that preceded it, predicting a winner is genuinely difficult.

Three Events, Three Winners

The swing opened at Ko Olina Golf Club in Hawaii, where Jeeno Thitikul won the LOTTE Championship at 21-under par (October 1–4). Thitikul is one of the LPGA Tour's most consistent players, and her Hawaii win was a wire-to-wire statement. From Hawaii, the tour flew to Asia.

The Buick LPGA Shanghai (October 15–18) produced a different winner in Ruoning Yin, who won at 11-under par on a course that suited her controlled, course-management approach. Yin's win in Shanghai, in front of a home crowd, was one of the most emotionally charged victories of the LPGA calendar to this point.

The BMW Ladies Championship at Pine Beach Golf Links in South Korea (October 22–25) delivered the swing's third different champion in Yuri Yoshida of Japan, who claimed her first career LPGA title in a tight final round against South Korean opposition.

The Maybank Championship

The Maybank Championship in Kuala Lumpur runs October 29 through November 1 and closes the Asia swing. Malaysia offers a distinct challenge compared to the three events that preceded it — a different climate, different grass conditions, and a course that typically rewards versatility.

With Thitikul, Yin, and Yoshida all having won in the stretch, and none of them dominant across multiple events, the Maybank field figures to be wide open. Players who arrived in Asia seeking a first swing win still have a chance. Players who already won will look to confirm they can back it up.
