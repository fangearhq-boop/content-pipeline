Spieth Opens Bogey-Free 65 to Share St. Jude Lead — and Put BMW Hopes Back Alive

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | August 15, 2026

Jordan Spieth arrived at the FedEx St. Jude Championship ranked 54th in the FedExCup standings, outside the top 50 that advance to the BMW Championship next week, and without a single top-10 finish in the entire 2026 PGA Tour regular season.

Then he went out Thursday and shot a bogey-free 5-under 65 to share the first-round lead.

The five-way tie includes Michael Thorbjornsen, Kurt Kitayama, Michael Kim, and Jake Knapp. Spieth is the story. His Round 1 has him projecting to move inside the top 50 in the FedExCup standings — which means the BMW Championship, which felt out of reach when the week began, is back on the table.

A Season of Survival

Spieth's path to the 2026 FedEx Playoffs was extraordinary in its own way. He qualified for the playoffs — the 70-player field competing at the St. Jude this week — despite posting zero top-10 finishes in the regular season. He got in because enough players didn't, and because enough starts and made cuts accumulated into a standings position that just barely held.

This is his 14th consecutive year reaching the FedEx Cup Playoffs. That streak speaks to his consistency at finding a way, even in seasons that don't produce his best golf. He described himself heading into this week as being "in a very different place" than recent struggles had suggested.

The 65 on Thursday backed it up. Bogey-free. In contention. The Spieth who emerged at Augusta National so many years ago — the player who makes everything feel consequential — returned for a round at TPC Southwind.

The BMW Picture

Of the 70 players competing at the St. Jude, the top 50 in the FedExCup standings after Sunday advance to the BMW Championship. Max Homa entered at 52nd — also on the bubble — and is also in play this week. The survival race within the race is one of the most compelling threads of the playoff opener.

Spieth's strong Round 1 moved him in the right direction. He sits at 6-under after two rounds, five back of leader Scottie Scheffler, but still very much in position to finish high enough to move through to the BMW. Scheffler's course-record 61 in Round 2 reshuffled the leaderboard, but Spieth's position — 5 back of a leading Scheffler — is not a position from which meaningful FedExCup points movement is impossible.

Sources for this article include ESPN, PGA Tour, AthlonSports, golf.com, and Yahoo Sports, all reporting August 13-14, 2026.

What's Next

Round 3 is Saturday at TPC Southwind. Spieth will need to keep making birdies and avoiding big numbers to hold his FedExCup position. Rounds 3 and 4 this weekend will determine his fate in the 2026 playoffs. The BMW Championship follows next week for the top 50 who survive Memphis.
