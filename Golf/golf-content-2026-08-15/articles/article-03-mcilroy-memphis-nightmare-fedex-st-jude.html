McIlroy Returns to Memphis — Opens 4-Over and Sits 68th of 69 in Playoff Opener

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | August 15, 2026

Rory McIlroy skipped the FedEx St. Jude Championship in 2025. He returned to Memphis in 2026 — and his first round was a disaster.

McIlroy opened with a 4-over par Round 1 at TPC Southwind, landing 68th out of 69 players in the field. Sky Sports captured it directly: "Rory McIlroy makes woeful start at FedEx St. Jude Championship with four-over par opening round." Central Oregon Daily headlined it as: "FedEx Cup playoffs winners, losers: Rory McIlroy near bottom at St. Jude Championship."

For a player who won the 2026 Masters — successfully defending his title and joining a short list of players to win back-to-back Augusta crowns — and who entered the playoff as 12th in the FedExCup standings chasing a record fourth title, the Memphis start was not what anyone expected.

What Went Wrong

The specifics of McIlroy's Round 1 at TPC Southwind were not immediately detailed across all sources. What is clear from multiple reports is that the 4-over total placed him at the bottom of the field — far from the form that won him Augusta in April and positioned him as one of the marquee names for the FedEx playoff run.

McIlroy had spoken before the event about being "keen to see where his game is" after a break since The Open Championship. He returned refreshed but the opening round did not reward the wait. He was paired with Scottie Scheffler for Round 2, presenting one of the starker contrasts the week could manufacture: Scheffler, coming off a course-record 61 and leading at 11-under; McIlroy, fighting from 4-over and near the bottom of the standings.

The Stakes for McIlroy

The FedEx St. Jude Championship sends the top 70 in FedExCup standings on to the BMW Championship. Despite his 12th-place FedExCup ranking, a poor finish at the St. Jude can complicate McIlroy's points position and change his seeding heading into the Tour Championship at East Lake — where the $23M FedExCup prize is ultimately decided.

There is also a separate concern: McIlroy has been flagged as potentially falling one event short of the 15-event minimum required to maintain dual status on both the PGA Tour and the DP World Tour. He expressed confidence Wednesday that he would "reach a compromise" with the Tour on the matter — but the situation is an additional variable in an already complicated week.

Sources for this article include Sky Sports, PGA Tour, Central Oregon Daily, Golf Channel, and ESPN, all reporting August 13-15, 2026.

What's Next

McIlroy needs a significant turnaround across Rounds 2 through 4 to salvage his St. Jude week. Round 3 is Saturday, the final round is Sunday. Given his FedExCup ranking, he is unlikely to fall out of the BMW Championship field — but the FedEx playoff seeding and Tour Championship positioning depend on how he performs over the weekend.
