### STORY 1: Scheffler's Course-Record 61 Gives Him 3-Shot Lead at FedEx St. Jude Heading Into R3

#### Long-Form Post — 8:00 AM ET

Scottie Scheffler is in complete control of the FedEx St. Jude Championship.

On Friday, Scheffler fired a 9-under 61 in Round 2 at TPC Southwind — tying the course record. He birdied his first five holes. He made six birdies in his first seven holes. By the time his round was over, he had drained 54 feet of birdie putts and produced what CBS Sports called "the best round of the week by three shots."

The result: Scheffler stands at 11-under par, leading Sungjae Im and Viktor Hovland by three shots heading into Round 3 on Saturday.

It's a dominant display from the player who already locked up the FedExCup regular-season No. 1 ranking for the fourth time in five years, earning a $10M bonus in the process. He didn't need a course-record round to confirm his stature — but he delivered one anyway.

Round 3 begins Saturday. Scheffler tees off at 1:45 PM ET alongside Im. You can watch on Golf Channel from 1 to 3 PM ET, then on CBS from 3 to 6 PM ET. ESPN+ provides early streaming coverage starting at 7:45 AM ET.

With three shots of breathing room and the best ball-striking of any player at TPC Southwind this week, Scheffler is the overwhelming favorite to win the playoff opener. But 36 holes remain, and Im and Hovland have proven they can compete.

Watch for Saturday's round to set the tone for the FedEx playoff narrative heading into the BMW Championship.

### STORY 2: Spieth's Bogey-Free 65 Gives BMW Championship Hope — 54th to Projected Top 50

#### Long-Form Post — 10:30 AM ET

Jordan Spieth shouldn't be here — and that's exactly what makes this story worth watching.

Spieth arrived at the FedEx St. Jude Championship ranked 54th in the FedExCup standings. The top 50 advance to the BMW Championship next week. Spieth was outside that cutoff. He had zero top-10 finishes in the entire 2026 PGA Tour regular season. This was supposed to be a survival mission at best.

Then he went out Thursday and shot a bogey-free 5-under 65 to share the first-round lead.

It's a five-way tie with Michael Thorbjornsen, Kurt Kitayama, Michael Kim, and Jake Knapp — but Spieth is the story. After his Round 1, he is projected to move inside the top 50 in the FedExCup standings, meaning the BMW Championship is back within reach. He described himself as being "in a very different place" heading into this week.

This is Spieth's 14th consecutive year qualifying for the FedEx Cup Playoffs. That streak is remarkable in itself — but the fact that he reached the playoffs this year without a single top-10 finish may be even more remarkable. He qualified because enough players didn't.

Max Homa, also on the bubble at 52nd entering the week, is also in play this week. The FedEx survival race within the race is one of the most compelling threads running through the St. Jude weekend.

Spieth isn't done yet.

#### Long-Form Post — 1:00 PM ET

Rory McIlroy's return to Memphis has not gone according to plan.

The world No. 2 skipped the 2025 FedEx St. Jude Championship. He returned in 2026 — and opened with a 4-over round at TPC Southwind that left him 68th out of 69 players in the field.

Sky Sports captured it directly: "Rory McIlroy makes woeful start at FedEx St. Jude Championship with four-over par opening round." Central Oregon Daily went with: "FedEx Cup playoffs winners, losers: Rory McIlroy near bottom at St. Jude Championship."

McIlroy was paired with Scottie Scheffler for Round 2 — as different a contrast in opening-round form as you could construct. Scheffler birdied six of his first seven holes. McIlroy was fighting to save his playoff week from the bottom of the standings.

The stakes for McIlroy are real. The top 50 in FedExCup standings after St. Jude advance to the BMW Championship next week. A player of McIlroy's ranking was expected to cruise through this first stage. Instead, he needs a serious turnaround across the weekend just to keep the playoffs going.

This is the FedEx format doing exactly what it's designed to do: create urgency from the very first event. For McIlroy, urgency arrived on Thursday afternoon in Memphis.

### STORY 4: LPGA Portland Classic R2 — Yubol at 12-Under, R3 Saturday at Columbia Edgewater

#### Long-Form Post — 5:00 PM ET

The Standard Portland Classic is at the halfway point, and Arpichaya Yubol has opened up a lead.

After two rounds at Columbia Edgewater Country Club in Portland, Oregon, Yubol sits at 12-under par and leads the field by one shot. Patty Anannarukarn and Somi Lee Schubert are tied for second at 11-under. Hye-Jin Choi is also at 11-under. Jeeno Thitikul, Eri Hara, Ruoning Zhang, and Haeran Ryu are lurking at 9-under — three shots back, well within range.

Round 3 is Saturday, Round 4 is Sunday — the tournament concludes August 16, with the $2M purse on the line.

One side story worth following: Megha Ganne is making her professional LPGA debut this week. She received a field exemption through the Inkster Award, which goes to the highest-ranked college golfer in their final year of eligibility. Ganne was a four-time All-American at Stanford, was part of two NCAA title-winning teams, and won the U.S. Women's Amateur last summer at Bandon Dunes. This week is her first event as a professional.

Next up after Portland: the CPKC Women's Open, August 20-23 at Royal Mayfair Golf Club in Edmonton, Alberta, with a $2.75M purse. It's one of the marquee events of the LPGA season, and the Portland Classic winner will enter with serious momentum.

That's the reality of the FedEx Cup Playoffs format — and with two rounds remaining at TPC Southwind, the math is unforgiving.

The headline bubble stories heading into the week were Jordan Spieth (54th in the FedExCup) and Max Homa (52nd). Both were outside the top 50 cutoff. Spieth responded immediately: a bogey-free 65 in Round 1 put him in a share of the lead and projected him to move inside the top 50. His story has turned, at least for now.

On the other end: Rory McIlroy opened 4-over and sat 68th of 69. Even with his high FedExCup ranking entering the week, a missed cut or a poor finish could complicate his playoff position.

Scottie Scheffler, sitting on a 3-shot lead at 11-under, is in no danger. His course-record 61 in Round 2 has virtually locked up his passage to the BMW and then the Tour Championship — where he enters as the top seed (FedExCup No. 1) regardless of this week's result.

But for 20 players staring at the cutline, every birdie on Saturday and Sunday could be the difference between a BMW Championship tee time and a flight home.

The FedEx playoff pressure is real. This is where it starts.
