Michael La Sasso, 22, Becomes the Youngest Winner in LIV Golf History at Indianapolis

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | October 9, 2026

Nobody saw it coming. Michael La Sasso, a 22-year-old rookie on HyFlyers GC with zero top-10 finishes to his name all season, walked off the 18th green at LIV Golf Indianapolis as the youngest individual winner in the league's five-year history. One clutch birdie, one final-round 65, one stroke ahead of Jon Rahm — and La Sasso had written one of the most unexpected stories of the 2026 season.

The win came at LIV's season finale in Indianapolis, the last individual tournament before the league's uncertain offseason. It was the kind of conclusion that golf occasionally produces: a player nobody expected, on a stage nobody thought he was ready for, delivering at the only moment that mattered.

The Final Round

Jon Rahm, the three-time LIV individual season champion, gave the field everything it needed on Sunday. Rahm shot a 7-under 63 in his final round, briefly taking a one-shot lead before dropping a stroke early. La Sasso, playing in the same window of pressure, kept his composure.

The moment came on the par-5 18th hole. La Sasso needed to get up and down to seal the win, and he did — converting the birdie and closing at 18 under to finish one ahead of Rahm's 17-under total. It was the kind of finish that veteran players spend years learning how to execute. La Sasso did it in his first full season.

Who Is Michael La Sasso?

La Sasso came to LIV Golf as a rookie this season after a college career at the University of Mississippi that ended with an NCAA championship. He captured the title with an 11-under total, becoming just the second NCAA champion in Ole Miss history. That pedigree earned him a roster spot on HyFlyers GC, Phil Mickelson's team.

His LIV season before Indianapolis was quiet. He had just one top-30 finish and no top-10s across the full slate of events. His best result was a tie for 12th. There was no statistical reason to look at La Sasso as a contender entering the Indianapolis finale. He was a young player learning the professional game, not a threat to the Rahms and established names at the top of the rankings.

And then he won.

HyFlyers GC's Best Day

The team result added another layer to the story. HyFlyers GC, one of the 13 franchises in LIV Golf, finished as team runner-up at Indianapolis — the best team result in the franchise's history. Legion XIII, Rahm's team, won the Team Championship by six shots. But for HyFlyers, runner-up was a significant milestone, and La Sasso's individual win was the kind of performance that defines a team's identity going forward.

Mickelson, who helped build the HyFlyers GC roster, expressed pride in La Sasso's achievement. The rookie's future, though, is unclear — he said he wasn't sure where or when he would be playing next, given LIV Golf's ongoing bankruptcy proceedings.

LIV's Uncertain Backdrop

The Indianapolis win happened against a backdrop of fundamental uncertainty for LIV Golf itself. The league filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in September 2026 after the Saudi Public Investment Fund announced it would stop funding operations after this season. A deal with a new lead investor has been reported but is not yet finalized. The league's 2027 future remains an open question.

For La Sasso, the uncertainty about LIV's structure is a real constraint on his next steps. His title win is genuine and historic within the league's record books. What comes after it — on which tour, at which events — is still being worked out.

What's Next

La Sasso is 22 years old with an NCAA title and now a professional tournament win at the highest level available to him. Wherever he competes next — on LIV, on the PGA Tour, on a developmental circuit — he arrives with a record that no one had on him before Indianapolis. The youngest winner in LIV Golf history is now also one of the most interesting stories of the next chapter of professional golf's ongoing reorganization. His next start will be worth watching.
