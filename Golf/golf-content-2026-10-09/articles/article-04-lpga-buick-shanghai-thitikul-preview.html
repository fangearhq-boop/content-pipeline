Buick LPGA Shanghai Is Next — Thitikul Leads the Season Heading Into the Asian Swing

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | October 9, 2026

The LPGA Tour's Asian Swing picks up significant momentum next week with the Buick LPGA Shanghai, set for October 15-18 at Qizhong Garden Golf Club in Shanghai, China. It is one of the circuit's marquee international events and arrives at a moment in the season when standings are beginning to solidify — but remain competitive enough that a strong performance can change the picture entirely.

Leading the season heading into Shanghai is Jeeno Thitikul, the world's No. 1-ranked women's golfer, who has held the top of the LPGA money list through the year. Her presence at Shanghai makes the event a natural focal point for the week's competitive coverage.

The Shanghai Event

Qizhong Garden Golf Club has hosted the LPGA Shanghai for years and has a well-established profile as a demanding test that rewards precision and patience. The $3.2 million purse makes it one of the more substantial paydays on the fall schedule and draws a quality international field.

The Asian Swing is a distinctive stretch on the LPGA calendar — a run of events in Asia that takes the tour through some of the largest golf markets in the world and provides international players, particularly those from Asia-Pacific nations, a chance to compete in front of home-country audiences. Thitikul, from Thailand, is a natural draw for those galleries.

Thitikul's Season in Context

Jeeno Thitikul has been the dominant force on the LPGA Tour through 2026. Her position atop the season money list reflects consistent elite performance — the kind of season that requires showing up week after week and converting opportunities into results. The No. 1 world ranking is a reflection of sustained excellence, not a single hot stretch.

Coming into Shanghai, the question is whether Thitikul can add to her win total and further separate herself in the season standings, or whether one of the international fields that populate these Asian Swing events will produce a challenge. The quality of the Asian Swing fields tends to be strong, with Asian tour members mixing with the LPGA's full-time regulars in a setting where the competition is genuine.

What the Fall Stretch Means

The LPGA Tour's fall calendar is where the season's final narrative takes shape. Season-end rankings, tour card standings, and end-of-year award positioning can all shift in the final weeks. Shanghai is a meaningful event on its own, but it is also the first in a sequence that leads to the end of the season.

Players who perform well on the Asian Swing tend to enter the U.S. stretch of the schedule with momentum, confidence, and — depending on their results — improved standing in whatever races they're chasing. For Thitikul, leading the season into this stretch is the ideal position. The work now is converting that position into a finish she can carry into 2027.

What's Next

The Buick LPGA Shanghai tees off October 15 at Qizhong Garden Golf Club. Thitikul and the rest of the LPGA field make the journey to China this week, with first-round coverage beginning Wednesday local time. The full field has not yet been announced, but a $3.2 million purse and a prestigious venue will draw the tour's best. Watch for Thitikul's tee time and first-round pairing to determine the early storylines when the event gets underway.
