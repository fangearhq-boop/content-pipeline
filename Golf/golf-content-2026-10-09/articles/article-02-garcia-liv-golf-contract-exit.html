Sergio Garcia Clears Legal Path to Leave LIV Golf as Bankruptcy Court Lifts Contract Stay

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | October 9, 2026

Sergio Garcia is one step closer to formally ending his time with LIV Golf. A U.S. bankruptcy court has lifted the automatic stay on Garcia's contract with the league, clearing the legal path for his lawyers to deliver a formal termination notice. The ruling is the most significant legal development in Garcia's exit process since LIV Golf filed for Chapter 11 protection on September 8, 2026.

For Garcia, the ruling is a relief. For the golf world watching how LIV's collapse plays out, it is a signal of how one player at least has navigated the bankruptcy's legal maze.

How Garcia Got Here

Garcia signed a multi-year extension with LIV Golf in December 2025 — a commitment that appeared, at the time, to extend his involvement with the Saudi-backed league well into the future. He did not renew his DP World Tour membership for the 2026 season, which left him contractually tied to LIV with no fallback on the European tour circuit.

When LIV filed for bankruptcy protection nine months later, an automatic stay went into effect — a standard legal mechanism that freezes most actions related to a debtor's assets, including contracts. For Garcia, this created an uncomfortable limbo: his LIV contract technically remained in force, but the league it bound him to was operating under court supervision with no clear future.

His lawyers argued that the unresolved contract was becoming a practical obstacle. Tournament organizers and sponsors, they said, were hesitant to engage with Garcia while his contractual status remained uncertain. The stay was hurting his career independent of any league outcome.

What the Court Order Actually Says

The court's order is narrower than a full contract termination. It lifts the automatic stay "solely" to allow Garcia's counsel to deliver a termination notice to LIV Golf's bankruptcy estate. Garcia still has to take that next step himself — delivering the notice is not automatic.

The distinction matters because Garcia's release is not yet complete. The order creates the legal door; Garcia's lawyers walking through it finalizes the departure. That process is ongoing, and Golf Monthly reports that Garcia's team expects the contract to ultimately be terminated. But as of now, the full exit is not signed and sealed.

What Comes Next for Garcia

Garcia's stated plan is to return to the DP World Tour for the 2027 season. He has been one of that circuit's most decorated players over his career and has deep roots in European golf. A return there would allow him to compete in Rolex Series events and on a stage where he has historically performed at a high level.

Whether he can recapture form at 37 remains to be seen. But the legal path is now clear, and Garcia's focus appears to be on moving forward rather than backward toward LIV.

Rahm's Situation Is Different

Jon Rahm, the three-time LIV individual season champion and one of the league's most prominent players, remains in a different legal position. His contract with LIV has not been subject to the same court order, and his situation is described as unresolved. Rahm and Garcia took different paths within LIV, and their exits — if they come — will not follow the same timeline or legal framework.

Garcia's case may become a template for other LIV players watching how the bankruptcy process unfolds. Or it may remain singular, given the specific terms of his contract and the consent of LIV's legal team to the lifting of the stay.

What's Next

Garcia's legal team is expected to deliver the termination notice in the coming days. Once that is filed, the process of formally unwinding his LIV contract begins. Assuming the notice is accepted and the termination proceeds, Garcia would be free to resume membership on the DP World Tour in time for the 2027 season. For a 2017 Masters champion who has spent the last several years in the LIV ecosystem, a return to the tour circuit where he built his legacy would be a significant chapter in what has already been one of professional golf's most eventful careers.
