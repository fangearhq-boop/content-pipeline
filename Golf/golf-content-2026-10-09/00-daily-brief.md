### STORY 2: Sergio Garcia Freed from LIV Contract — Court Lifts Stay
**Tier:** T1 FOLLOW UP
**Assigned Writer:** Jake Torres

A U.S. bankruptcy court has lifted the automatic stay on Sergio Garcia's LIV Golf contract, clearing the legal path for Garcia to submit a formal termination notice to the league. LIV Golf filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on September 8, 2026. Garcia, who had signed a multi-year extension with LIV in December 2025, argued through his lawyers that the unresolved contract was creating hesitation among tournament organizers and sponsors. The court's order permits the stay to be lifted "solely" so Garcia's counsel can deliver a termination notice. Garcia has indicated he plans to return to the DP World Tour in 2027.

**Key facts:**
- LIV Golf filed Chapter 11 on September 8, 2026
- Court lifted automatic stay on Garcia's contract (October 2026)
- Order permits termination notice to be delivered to LIV

### STORY 3: La Sasso Youngest LIV Winner Ever — HyFlyers GC Runner-Up in Indianapolis
**Tier:** T2 NEW
**Assigned Writer:** Marcus Cole

Michael La Sasso, 22, became the youngest individual winner in LIV Golf history at the league's season finale in Indianapolis. A rookie for HyFlyers GC and a former University of Mississippi NCAA champion, La Sasso closed with a 65 to finish at 18 under, one stroke ahead of Jon Rahm, who shot a final-round 63 but came up short. La Sasso clinched with a clutch birdie up-and-down at the par-5 18th. Before Indianapolis, his best result was a tie for 12th. Legion XIII, Rahm's team, won the Team Championship. HyFlyers GC finished as team runner-up — the best team result in franchise history.

**Key facts:**
- La Sasso, 22, youngest winner in LIV Golf history (first 5 seasons)
- Closed 65, 18-under total; beat Rahm by one stroke (Rahm shot 63)
- Birdie up-and-down at par-5 18th sealed the win
- Former NCAA champion, University of Mississippi
