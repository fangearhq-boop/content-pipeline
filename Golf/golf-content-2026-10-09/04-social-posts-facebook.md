## Story 2 — Sergio Garcia LIV Exit (Posting Window: 10:30 AM ET)

**Post:**
Sergio Garcia has cleared a major legal hurdle on his way out of LIV Golf.

A U.S. bankruptcy court has lifted the automatic stay on Garcia's contract with the league, which filed for Chapter 11 on September 8. The court's order allows Garcia's legal team to formally deliver a termination notice — clearing the path for the 2017 Masters champion to pursue other options.

Garcia had signed a multi-year extension with LIV in December 2025 but argued that the unresolved contract was making tournament organizers and sponsors hesitant to deal with him. His plan is to return to the DP World Tour for the 2027 season.

It's worth noting: the order permits the termination notice to be delivered, but the full release isn't finalized yet. Jon Rahm's contract with LIV remains a separate, unresolved situation.

For Garcia, after years in the LIV ecosystem, a return to European tour golf looks closer than ever.

## Story 3 — La Sasso Youngest LIV Winner (Posting Window: 1:00 PM ET)

**Post:**
This one came out of nowhere — and it was incredible.

Michael La Sasso, 22, won the LIV Golf Indianapolis season finale to become the youngest individual winner in the league's five-year history. A rookie on HyFlyers GC and a former University of Mississippi NCAA champion, La Sasso had zero top-10 finishes all season entering the event.

Then he shot a 65 on Sunday to finish at 18 under, edging Jon Rahm — who fired a brilliant 63 — by one stroke. The clincher: a birdie up-and-down at the par-5 18th. Rahm had briefly taken the lead, then dropped a shot early, and La Sasso never looked back.

HyFlyers GC, Phil Mickelson's team, finished as team runner-up — the best result in team history. Legion XIII, Rahm's squad, won the Team Championship.

La Sasso now faces uncertainty about where he'll play next, with LIV's future under Chapter 11. But for one week in Indianapolis, he was the best golfer in the world.
