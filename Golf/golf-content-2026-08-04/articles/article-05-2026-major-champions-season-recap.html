The 2026 Major Champions — Four Different Winners, One Unforgettable Season

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | August 4, 2026

The 2026 major season is complete. Four tournaments, four champions, and a collection of stories that will shape how this year is remembered when the final FedEx Cup rankings are posted. Here's the full accounting.

Rory McIlroy — The Masters

Rory McIlroy won back-to-back Masters titles at Augusta National in April, claiming his second consecutive green jacket. Back-to-back Masters wins are rare in the sport's history — the demands of Augusta National, the physical and mental weight of defending a title at the world's most scrutinized tournament, and the depth of the field make repeating genuinely difficult.

McIlroy's second consecutive Masters title places him in conversation with a short list of players who have dominated Augusta across multiple years. It confirms a stretch of major championship form at Augusta specifically that had not been apparent in his career before 2025.

Aaron Rai — PGA Championship

Aaron Rai won the PGA Championship, becoming the first English winner of that major since 1919 — a 107-year gap closed in one result. The PGA Championship winner's list is long and diverse, and the absence of an English winner for more than a century reflects how long that gap had been sitting in the record books.

Rai's win was a performance that answered questions about whether he could produce at major championship level — a player who had competed consistently on the European tour and occasionally in major settings without the title to show for it. The PGA Championship gives him a result that defines a career, regardless of what follows.

Wyndham Clark — US Open

Wyndham Clark won the US Open at Shinnecock Hills, going wire-to-wire at one of the most demanding venues in American golf. Shinnecock Hills is a course that produces difficult conditions by design — firm fairways, fast greens, winds off the Long Island coast that shift the challenge from hole to hole throughout any given day.

Leading a US Open from start to finish at Shinnecock Hills requires a player to maintain their game under sustained pressure at a course that punishes small errors more than almost any other major venue. Clark did it across four rounds, which earns the result a significance beyond the trophy itself.

Ryan Fox — The Open Championship

Ryan Fox won The Open Championship at Royal Birkdale, finishing at 10-under and one shot ahead of Cameron Young. The margin was as thin as a major championship can produce — one stroke, on the final hole of the final round at one of links golf's most storied venues.

Fox's win was the first major championship title of his career. Royal Birkdale is a test that rewards players who understand links conditions — the bump-and-run, the wind management, the decision-making that links golf demands differently from any other format. Fox's 10-under total, arrived at one shot ahead of a player who had his own chance to win, was earned across four days of conditions that separated the field as much as the course itself did.

Young's runner-up finish at Birkdale adds to a pattern of major near-misses for an American player who has been consistently in contention without the result. Fox takes the Claret Jug. Young continues to wait.

The Season in Four Results

McIlroy at Augusta. Rai at the PGA. Clark at Shinnecock Hills. Fox at Royal Birkdale.

Four different players, four different countries, four different styles of win. The 2026 major season did not produce a dominant figure who claimed multiple titles — it produced four distinct champions, each with a story that belongs to them and to the tournament they won.

As the FedEx Cup Playoffs begin on August 13, these are the players who have defined the major season. Whatever the next three events produce, the 2026 year in golf majors is already written.
