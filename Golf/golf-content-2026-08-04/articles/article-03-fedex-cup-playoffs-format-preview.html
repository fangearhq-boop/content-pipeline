FedEx Cup Playoffs: How the Format Works — From 70 Players to One Champion

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | August 4, 2026

The FedEx Cup Playoffs begin in nine days. Starting August 13 at TPC Southwind in Memphis, the PGA Tour's season-ending postseason runs across three consecutive events, cutting the field at each stop until 30 players compete in the finale at East Lake Golf Club in Atlanta.

Here is how it works.

Event 1: St. Jude Championship

The first playoff event runs August 13-16 at TPC Southwind in Memphis, Tennessee. The top 70 players in the FedEx Cup standings enter the week eligible to compete. After 72 holes, the field cuts to the top 50 players in the updated standings, who advance to the BMW Championship.

TPC Southwind is a course with genuine difficulty — tight fairways, Bermuda grass that demands precision around the greens, and conditions that historically produce a varied leaderboard. Players who arrive with FedEx Cup cushion can play conservatively for position. Players on the bubble at the bottom of the top 70 have to play to win.

Event 2: BMW Championship

The second playoff event runs August 20-23 at Bellerive Country Club in St. Louis, Missouri. The top 50 players from the updated standings after Memphis compete. After 72 holes, the bottom 20 are eliminated — the top 30 advance to the TOUR Championship.

Bellerive is a course with major championship history, having hosted the PGA Championship twice. The setting and conditions suit players who can navigate length and complexity across four days. The BMW Championship is the last opportunity to move into the top 30 and secure a place at East Lake.

Event 3: TOUR Championship

The TOUR Championship runs August 27-30 at East Lake Golf Club in Atlanta — the FedEx Cup's home course. The top 30 players in the standings compete in a 72-hole stroke play event, and all 30 start the week at even par.

The format is straightforward: 72 holes of stroke play, beginning from even par, and the player with the lowest score at the end of Sunday's round wins the FedEx Cup title. There are no staggered starting scores based on FedEx Cup position — just golf, 72 holes, and the player who shoots the best total wins.

East Lake is a course where the pressure of a final round with a major title on the line produces the kind of Sunday golf that defines the event. The venue has hosted the TOUR Championship for many years, and its configuration — tight, demanding, requiring precision — suits the moment.

Who's Playing

The FedEx Cup standings entering the playoffs reflect a season in which consistent performance has separated the field. Players like Scottie Scheffler — who has spent 201 weeks at No. 1 in the Official World Golf Ranking, 167 of them consecutively — enter as the headliner of a field that includes every significant name from the 2026 PGA Tour season.

The players who arrive at East Lake on August 27 will be the 30 who have sustained their form across a full regular season plus two playoff events. Three weeks from now, one of them will be the FedEx Cup champion.
