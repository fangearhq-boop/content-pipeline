Koivun Made the Cut at -7 — Now Comes the Sprint From Asheville to Medinah

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | September 19, 2026

Jackson Koivun / Biltmore Championship / Presidents Cup

Jackson Koivun made the cut at the 2026 Biltmore Championship. Through 36 holes at The Cliffs at Walnut Cove in Asheville, the 21-year-old sits at -7 — eight shots behind 36-hole leader Neal Shipley, with two rounds still to play. He is not in championship contention, but that is not the only thing happening in his week.

On Tuesday, September 22, Koivun is expected at Medinah Country Club for Presidents Cup practice rounds. Five days from now, he plays in the opening session of a biennial team event where he will be the youngest member of the USA squad.

The Biltmore Rounds

Koivun opened with a -3 (68) on Thursday, a solid but not spectacular start in a field where the top of the leaderboard was already moving to -7 by the end of day one. Friday's second round went better — he shot 67 (-4), making a birdie on the par-5 No. 8 by reaching the green in two and converting. That put him at -7 through two rounds and safely inside the cut line, but 8 back of a Shipley who posted a career-low 62 to take the overnight lead.

The gap is too large to realistically overcome across two rounds even with preferred lies in play. Koivun needs a 64 or better today and another low round Sunday to have any realistic chance at winning — and Shipley would have to come back to him. The honest read is that Koivun is playing for a respectable finish and the additional FedExCup Fall points, while keeping his body and mind competitive before the format shift to match play.

What Medinah Brings

Brandt Snedeker named Koivun as one of his six captain's picks for the USA Presidents Cup team. At 21, Koivun is the youngest player on either side of the draw in this year's event. His selection reflects both his form in 2026 and his match-play temperament — qualities that Snedeker evidently assessed as valuable in a team competition where momentum and energy within a squad matter as much as raw scoring statistics.

The transition from individual stroke play to team match play is significant. In stroke play, the focus is entirely on your own scorecard — managing bogeys, protecting leads, grinding through the field. In match play, each hole is its own reset. A bogey only matters if your opponent made par. The mental calibration is different, and players who compete in both formats in the same week sometimes need a round or two of practice to fully shift gears.

Jacob Bridgeman, also a USA Presidents Cup pick, struggled in Round 2 at Biltmore after a hot start to the week. He and Koivun will both arrive at Medinah having played competitive golf through the weekend, which is a different kind of preparation than a full week of practice-round reps — but it is also live competitive golf, and some players carry that momentum into team events effectively.

What's Next

Koivun plays Round 3 at the Biltmore Championship on Saturday September 19, then Round 4 on Sunday September 20. Practice rounds at Medinah Country Club start Tuesday September 22. The 2026 Presidents Cup competition runs September 24–27. For Koivun, the next eight days represent a compressed version of what a full professional golf season looks like: individual grind one week, team event the next.
