LIV Golf's 24-Day Countdown: BC Partners Deal, 52.5% Player Ownership, and the Math That Decides It

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | September 19, 2026

LIV Golf / Bankruptcy / BC Partners / Jon Rahm

Twenty-four days remain until October 13 — the deadline by which LIV Golf's restructuring deal with BC Partners must be validated or effectively abandoned. The clock has been running since September 8, when LIV filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in New Jersey with more than $500 million in debt. The restructuring support agreement LIV entered with BC Partners on that same day set out the terms: players must commit, in sufficient numbers and sufficient dollar amounts, within 35 days of the filing.

That window closes on October 13. As of September 15, the player whose commitment matters most — Jon Rahm, LIV's largest individual creditor at $7.47 million — had not given it.

What the BC Partners Deal Offers Players

The restructuring plan as filed represents a significant shift from the tour's original ownership structure. Public Investment Fund of Saudi Arabia, which backed LIV Golf from its founding and injected billions into the project, will fully exit as a backer. PIF has agreed to provide a $49.6 million debtor-in-possession loan to keep operations running through the Chapter 11 process, but once the deal closes, PIF is out.

In their place: BC Partners, the London-based private equity firm, would invest $300 million in a recapitalized LIV Golf. The ownership split under the deal is 52.5% to participating players, 45% to BC Partners and any additional new investors, with a small percentage reserved for other stakeholders. LIV's stated goal is to emerge from bankruptcy and begin operating LIV 2.0 as early as the 2027 season.

The Commitment Threshold

The deal requires that players holding at least two-thirds of total eligible player claim dollar amounts, and at least one-half of eligible player headcount, must commit to the restructured tour within the 35-day window. Both thresholds must be met simultaneously — meeting one without the other does not satisfy the condition.

This is where the concentration of large claims matters. The top creditors hold disproportionate influence over whether the dollar-amount threshold is satisfied. Jon Rahm at $7.47 million is the largest single claim. He and the next two largest creditors — Bryson DeChambeau and Dustin Johnson — collectively represent $18.7 million in claims. A bloc of the tour's highest earners that declines to commit could pull the dollar-amount total below the required threshold even if a numerical majority of players say yes.

Where Rahm Stands

Rahm's September 15 public comment was notable for what it contained and what it did not. He described seeing "opportunity" on the DP World Tour — the European-based tour where he reached a separate agreement in May 2026, paying outstanding fines and committing to a required quota of events. That agreement restored his Ryder Cup eligibility for the 2027 event in Ireland.

LIV Golf has petitioned the bankruptcy court for permission to reject all existing player contracts. If granted, that would formally end Rahm's contractual obligations to LIV — allowing him to walk away without breach, while still having his $7.47 million claim in the bankruptcy process. The petition's outcome is pending, but the filing itself signals that LIV's restructuring plan is built on players re-opting in voluntarily under new terms, not on enforcing the old contracts.

What's Next

The October 13 deadline is 24 days away. If the commitment thresholds are met, the BC Partners deal proceeds, LIV Golf emerges from bankruptcy, and a restructured tour targets a 2027 launch. If the thresholds are not met, the deal's framework suggests the proposed transaction would not proceed as structured — and the tour built on billions in Saudi investment would face a very different outcome. Player decisions — starting with the largest creditors — will determine which path materializes.
