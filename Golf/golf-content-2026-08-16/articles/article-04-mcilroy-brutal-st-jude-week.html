Rory McIlroy's Brutal Week at the FedEx St. Jude Championship Is Over — BMW Still Waits

By Marcus Cole | August 16, 2026

McIlroy opened with a 74 (+4), never threatened the leaderboard, and finished near the bottom of the field. His FedExCup standing almost certainly gets him to Bellerive. His form is a different question entirely.

Rory McIlroy's week at the FedEx St. Jude Championship ended the way it began: with question marks. He opened on Thursday with a 74 (+4) — a number that Golf Channel headlined immediately — and he was never a factor in a tournament won by Sam Burns. He finished near the bottom of the 70-player field.

The mathematics of his FedExCup situation offer some comfort. McIlroy entered the St. Jude ranked No. 12 in the season-long standings. A poor performance at the Playoff opener hurts, but it does not eliminate players with that kind of cushion. He almost certainly advances to the BMW Championship at Bellerive Country Club next week.

Getting there and arriving healthy are two different things.

The Sequence of a Difficult Week

The 74 in Round 1 set a tone McIlroy could never fully escape. He posted a 70 in Round 2, which kept him technically alive but well off the pace of a tournament won at -20 or better. Round 3 brought more difficulty. He never found the rhythm that has defined his best weeks on Tour.

Pre-Round 4, Heavy.com ran a report headlined "Concerning Rory McIlroy News Emerges Before Final Round." The specifics were not fully detailed in available reports, but the fact of the headline — and the week it capped — is telling enough. Something beyond the scorecard was not right in Memphis.

His post-round comments, filtered through coverage from Sky Sports and Golf Channel, painted a picture of a player who knows things are not where they need to be and is searching for answers.

The Form Problem Is Real

McIlroy has been one of the best players in the world for most of his career. His record at major events and at high-pressure moments is exceptional. But form in professional golf can leave without warning, and it can be slow to return.

The concern heading into the BMW is not about his eligibility — he will be at Bellerive. The concern is about what version of McIlroy shows up. A player of his caliber who loses form can still make cuts and accumulate points. A player of his caliber who finds form can win tournaments. At Bellerive, with the Tour Championship implications on the line, the gap between those two outcomes is meaningful.

His ranking is safe. His game needs work.

What's Next

McIlroy heads to Bellerive Country Club in St. Louis for the BMW Championship (August 20–23). The course is returning to the PGA Tour schedule after hosting the 2018 PGA Championship. Its characteristics — long, demanding, punishing of errant drives — have historically suited McIlroy's ball-striking. If there is a circuit breaker anywhere in his current form, a course that rewards power and precision is where it might switch on. He will need it to. The Tour Championship field is the top 30 from BMW; a player ranked No. 12 who endures another difficult week will survive, but the margin is narrowing.
