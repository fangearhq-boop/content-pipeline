Akie Iwai Wins the LPGA Portland Classic — Twin Sister Chisato Sprays Her with Champagne

By Ryan Calloway | August 16, 2026

The 23-year-old Japanese rookie closed with a bogey-free 66 for a 4-shot win at -24. Her twin sister, Chisato, finished T-3 at the same tournament and was first on the 18th green with the champagne bottle.

Akie Iwai won the LPGA Portland Classic on Sunday. That sentence is accurate. But it misses almost everything that matters about what happened on the 18th green at Columbia Edgewater Country Club when the final putt dropped.

Akie's twin sister, Chisato Iwai, had just finished her own round — T-3 at -19, a week that would have been the story of anyone else's life. She walked to the 18th green, watched her sister close out the win, and reached for the nearest champagne bottle.

That image is the story of the 2026 LPGA season so far.

How Akie Won

Akie Iwai shot rounds of 67-67-64-66 to finish at -24, four shots clear of second-place Gurleen Kaur (-20, who shot 65 on Sunday). She was meticulous in the final round — no bogeys, two birdies on her closing holes, and the kind of composed control that looks effortless from the outside and is anything but.

She is 23 years old. She is a rookie on the LPGA Tour. She is now a winner.

Chisato Iwai finished at -19, sharing T-3 with Linn Grant. Grace Kim was fifth at -18. The leaderboard was stacked with talent. Akie beat all of them.

Two Twins, Two Wins

The deeper story requires a trip back to May. Chisato Iwai won the LPGA event at Mayakoba. At the time, it was a remarkable story — a Japanese rookie in her first season, winning on one of the Tour's most visually stunning courses. It seemed like the kind of thing that would define Chisato's 2026 season.

Then, on August 16 in Portland, her twin sister Akie won too.

Both Iwai twins have now won in their debut LPGA season. Both are 23. Both came from Japan. Both arrived in the United States largely unknown to American golf audiences. In the span of four months, they have become two of the most recognizable stories in women's golf.

This is not a coincidence of talent. This is what elite athletic families produce — two people raised in the same environment, coached with the same philosophy, competing with the same drive, and delivering the results that follow from that.

The Champagne Moment

After Akie's final putt fell, Chisato was already at the 18th green. The champagne that followed was not staged for cameras or arranged by sponsors. It was a twin sister celebrating her twin sister's win in the most natural, joyful way available to her.

Sports produces moments of genuine human warmth at the intersection of competition and family. What happened on the 18th green in Portland belongs in that collection. Akie soaked in champagne, laughing, with her sister who had just come off a T-3 finish of her own. Neither of them needed anything more than this moment.

What's Next

The LPGA field heads north to Edmonton, Alberta, for the CPKC Women's Open (August 20–23), which carries a $2.75 million purse. Both Iwai sisters are expected to be in the field. Watch for Chisato to respond to her sister's win the same way Akie responded to Chisato's Mayakoba title — by finding her own moment to celebrate. This rivalry between sisters is one of the best storylines in golf right now.
