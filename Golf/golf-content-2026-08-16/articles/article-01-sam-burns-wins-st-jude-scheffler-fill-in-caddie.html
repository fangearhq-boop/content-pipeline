Sam Burns Wins the FedEx St. Jude Championship as Scheffler's Caddie Drama Unfolds

By Marcus Cole | August 16, 2026

Burns entered Round 4 as the chaser and finished as the champion. Scottie Scheffler entered with a 2-shot lead and the world's No. 1 ranking — and caddied his final round alongside the PGA Tour's chaplain after Ted Scott left for a family emergency Saturday night.

Sam Burns of Shreveport, Louisiana, is the FedEx St. Jude champion. He won the $20 million Playoff opener on Sunday, converting a third-round 62 into a final-round title run that will define his career. He enters the BMW Championship as one of the most dangerous players in the FedEx Cup field.

But the story that will be remembered long after the trophy has been polished and put away is what happened to Scottie Scheffler on Saturday night.

The Night Before

Scheffler entered the final round with a 2-shot lead. He had spent the week playing like the No. 1 player in the world — which he is — and had every reason to believe Sunday would be a coronation. Then his caddie, Ted Scott, received word of a family emergency and left the tournament Saturday night.

Scott has been on Scheffler's bag for years. Their partnership has produced major championships, world No. 1 rankings, and countless clutch moments under pressure. He was not there on Sunday.

In Scott's place, the PGA Tour arranged for Brad Payne — the Tour's full-time chaplain — to carry the bag. Payne flew in at approximately 2am Sunday morning. He is a man deeply respected by everyone on Tour for his pastoral work and his care for players and their families. He is not a professional caddie. He had never looped for Scheffler in competition.

Scheffler's Final Round

The outcome: Scheffler finished T-3. NBC Sports confirmed the result. His own post-round explanation was quiet and telling: "just ball wasn't going in."

That is not an excuse. Players at Scheffler's level make putts regardless of who is reading the greens. But the caddie-player partnership in professional golf is one of the most underappreciated collaborations in sports. It involves line reads, yardage calculations, wind assessment, course management strategy, and perhaps most importantly — the calming voice in the most pressurized moments. Payne provided everything he could. The result was T-3 instead of a win.

JJ Spaun also charged in the final round, closing the gap on the leaders before falling short. The leaderboard was alive heading into the back nine. In the end, it was Burns who separated himself when the pressure was highest.

Burns Delivers

Burns had shot a 62 in Round 3, hitting 15 of 18 greens in regulation and making everything he looked at. He entered Sunday at T-2, one shot behind the favorite. He played Sunday's round with the controlled aggression of a player who knew he had already proven he belonged at the top of the board.

The win is Burns's biggest. He is 29 years old, a Louisiana native who grew up watching players come through this event and dreaming about what it would feel like to win it. On Sunday in Memphis, he found out. The winner's share of the $20 million purse is his. More importantly, his FedEx Cup position is now exactly where he wants it heading into Bellerive.

What's Next

Burns heads to the BMW Championship at Bellerive Country Club in St. Louis (August 20–23) as one of the players to watch. A win at the St. Jude translates into the kind of form and confidence that can carry through consecutive Playoff events. Scheffler will be there too, almost certainly still among the favorites — Ted Scott's situation aside, Scheffler's overall body of work this season puts him comfortably into Bellerive's top-50 field. The question for him is psychological: how quickly can he reset from a week that unraveled in the most human way possible? The BMW will tell us.
