Jordan Spieth Secures His 15th Consecutive FedEx Playoffs Berth at the BMW Championship

By Jake Torres | August 16, 2026

Spieth opened the FedEx St. Jude with a bogey-free 65 and advanced from No. 54 in FedExCup standings into the top 50. Zero top-10s in the 2026 regular season. Still here.

Jordan Spieth will play in the BMW Championship at Bellerive Country Club next week. He has played in the FedEx Cup Playoffs every year since 2012. That is 15 consecutive years. That number is what the 2026 St. Jude result represents — not a breakthrough week, not a return to dominance, but the kind of stubborn competitive survival that defines Spieth's entire career.

How He Got Here

Spieth arrived at the FedEx St. Jude Championship as the No. 54 seed. The top 70 players in FedExCup points qualify for the St. Jude; the top 50 after the St. Jude advance to BMW. He was sitting just outside the safety zone, with no margin for error.

He opened with a bogey-free 65 in Round 1, sharing the early lead. He played alongside Tommy Fleetwood and finished the week around -6. When the final standings were calculated, Spieth had moved from No. 54 to inside the top 50.

It was not a marquee performance. It was exactly enough. For someone who had not recorded a top-10 finish in the entire 2026 regular season, exactly enough is what he needed.

The 15-Year Streak

Context matters here. Fifteen consecutive years in the FedEx Playoffs is an extraordinary number. It means that in every single season of Spieth's professional career on the PGA Tour, he has earned enough points during the regular season to qualify for the Playoffs. That includes the years when his game was struggling, when his putting deserted him, when injuries complicated his schedule, and when rivals were dominating the conversation.

The 2026 regular season has been Spieth's quietest in memory. No top-10 finishes. Stretches of inconsistency that produced real questions about where his game is and where it's headed. He came into Memphis needing a result to extend the streak, and he delivered a bogey-free first round when his season depended on it.

That ability to access something under pressure — to post a number when the alternative is going home — has been a defining characteristic of Spieth's career at every stage. It showed up again this week.

What's Next

Spieth heads to Bellerive Country Club in St. Louis for the BMW Championship (August 20–23). The BMW field shrinks from 50 to the top 30 who advance to the Tour Championship. Spieth will need another strong performance to keep his season alive beyond Bellerive. The course returned to the PGA Tour schedule after its last major appearance in 2018, and its demands — firm fairways, demanding greens, significant rough — reward precision ball-striking above all else. That has historically been one of Spieth's strengths. If there is a week on the back half of this schedule where he can go low, Bellerive is a reasonable candidate. He will need to find it.
