Scheffler Wins Tour Championship, Passes Tiger Woods in All-Time PGA Tour Career Earnings

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | September 1, 2026

Scottie Scheffler came to East Lake Golf Club on Sunday with a three-stroke deficit and left with everything. The Tour Championship. The FedEx Cup. And the all-time record for PGA Tour career earnings.

Scheffler shot a final-round 4-under 66 — tied for the best round of the day — to erase Viktor Hovland's three-stroke overnight lead and win the 2026 Tour Championship at -16. Hovland, who led by three entering Sunday, shot 2-over 72 and finished at -13. The three-stroke swing in one afternoon told the full story of the gap between where Hovland was and where Scheffler is.

History Made on Two Fronts

The $10 million winner's check for Scheffler did more than pay the bills. It pushed his all-time PGA Tour career earnings past $130 million — officially surpassing Tiger Woods's record of $120.9 million in official prize money. Scheffler is 30 years old. His prime years, by nearly any measure, are still ahead of him.

The FedEx Cup itself carries equal historical weight. Only three players in the history of the competition have won multiple titles: Tiger Woods, Rory McIlroy, and now Scottie Scheffler. His first came in 2024. His second arrived Sunday, following an unparalleled run of five consecutive No. 1 FedEx Cup seeds — a record that has no historical parallel.

It is Scheffler's 22nd PGA Tour victory, his third of the 2026 season following wins at the American Express and the FedEx St. Jude Championship.

The Sunday Surge

Hovland's collapse was not the story; Scheffler's charge was. His back nine on Sunday at East Lake featured the kind of aggressive, consistent ball-striking that has defined his game throughout the season. He birdied when the tournament demanded birdies and avoided the mistakes that cost Hovland on a course that offers no forgiveness to loose swings.

When the final numbers were posted — Scheffler at -16, Hovland at -13 — the FedEx Cup bracket was settled. Scheffler's fifth consecutive No. 1 seed produced its expected result: a FedEx Cup champion who simply refused to let the Tour's best players beat him when it mattered most.

What's Next

Scheffler is expected to rest following the Tour Championship before returning for the fall schedule. He will anchor Team USA at the Presidents Cup (September 24-27, Medinah Country Club, Illinois), where the Americans are looking to recover from their 2025 Ryder Cup disappointment. Beyond team golf, the rest of Scheffler's 2026 schedule remains to be determined — but his place at the top of the world rankings, and at the top of golf's all-time earnings list, is no longer a question. It is a fact.
