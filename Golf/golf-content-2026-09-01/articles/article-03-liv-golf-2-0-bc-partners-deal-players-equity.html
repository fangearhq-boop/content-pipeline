LIV Golf 2.0 Is Taking Shape — BC Partners Deal Closing This Month, Players to Become Equity Holders

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | September 1, 2026

For the better part of 2026, the question hanging over LIV Golf has been simple: what happens after Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund exits? The answer, according to LIV CEO Scott O'Neil, is becoming clear — and the league that emerges from this transition will look significantly different from the one that launched in 2022.

O'Neil confirmed that a deal with a new lead investor has been signed and approved by the LIV Golf board, with closing expected in September. BC Partners' credit division has been identified as leading the financing package, with a deal reportedly in the range of $250 million. If the transaction closes as expected, LIV Golf will have a new financial foundation from which to operate beyond 2026 — when PIF, which has invested an estimated $5 billion or more in the league's first four years, officially exits its role as primary backer.

What LIV 2.0 Looks Like

The restructured league is being built around a fundamentally different model:

  • Fewer events: 10 annual events (down from 14 in the 2026 season), split evenly between 5 in the United States and 5 internationally.
  • Reduced purses: Tournament prize pools will drop significantly from the $30 million-per-event scale that characterized LIV's early years. Players will be compensated primarily through equity rather than cash.
  • Player ownership: O'Neil described making players "majority equity holders" — calling it "a first for a major global sports league." Under this model, LIV's contracted players would own a meaningful share of the league they compete in.

The equity model represents a fundamental shift in how professional golf leagues compensate players — and it requires buy-in from the sport's biggest names to be viable. Investors need assurance that enough of LIV's A-listers will remain committed to 2027 before they finalize the deal.

The Stars' Response

That buy-in is not guaranteed. Greg Norman, LIV's founding commissioner, said publicly that he cannot guarantee the 2027 season. Ian Poulter has expressed genuine uncertainty, saying "I think time might run out" when asked about LIV's prospects. More than 50 players remain under contracted arrangements, but their willingness to convert from guaranteed-cash deals to equity positions is the open question.

Jon Rahm, who claimed his third consecutive LIV individual title in the 2026 season — while La Sasso won the Indianapolis season finale — represents the caliber of player the league needs to retain. Whether Rahm and his peers accept the new compensation model will be the defining factor in whether LIV 2.0 actually launches.

What's Next

September is the month O'Neil has identified for closing the deal. If it closes, LIV Golf will enter a transition period through the end of 2026 before relaunching with a smaller, leaner structure in 2027. If negotiations stall or key players decline to commit, the league faces a very different kind of future. The golf world is watching — and September may tell us everything.
