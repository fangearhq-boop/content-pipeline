### STORY 1: Scheffler Wins Tour Championship — Passes Tiger in Career Earnings

**Long-Form Post (8:00 AM ET)**
On Sunday at East Lake, Scottie Scheffler did what Scottie Scheffler does.

He came from behind. He shot 4-under 66 on the final day — tied for the best round of the round — to erase a three-stroke deficit and claim the 2026 Tour Championship. His final score of -16. Viktor Hovland, who led by three entering Sunday, shot 2-over 72 and could only watch as Scheffler walked away with the trophy, the $10 million winner's check, and the FedEx Cup title.

He has officially surpassed Tiger Woods's all-time record of $120.9 million in official prize money — at 30 years old, with what figures to be the prime of his career still ahead.

Five consecutive No. 1 FedEx Cup seeds. Two FedEx Cup titles. 22 wins. A Masters title. And now, the greatest career earnings number in PGA Tour history.

The era belongs to Scottie Scheffler.

### STORY 2: Presidents Cup Captain's Picks — Team USA Revealed Today

**Long-Form Post (9:00 AM ET)**
The 2026 Presidents Cup is 23 days away — and today, the final rosters take shape.

The PGA Tour is revealing the captain's picks for both Team USA and the International Team today, completing the 12-man rosters that will compete September 24-27 at Medinah Country Club in Medinah, Illinois.

Captain Brandt Snedeker leads Team USA, which already includes some of the biggest names in the game: Scottie Scheffler (who just won the Tour Championship), Cameron Young, Wyndham Clark, Sam Burns, Russell Henley, and Collin Morikawa. The captain's picks — announced today — fill out the remaining six spots.

Geoff Ogilvy captains the International Team, which draws from players outside the United States and Europe (the Ryder Cup covers Europe). The International side always features strong representation from Australia, South Africa, South Korea, and beyond.

For Team USA, this Presidents Cup carries extra motivation. The 2025 Ryder Cup did not go the way the Americans hoped, and Medinah — one of the most storied venues in American golf — is where they intend to make a statement. Medinah Country Club's Course No. 3 is a par-72 layout stretching 7,657 yards, redesigned in 2024, and it hosts 30 total matches over the four-day competition.

This is team golf's biggest event outside the Ryder Cup. And Team USA is ready to prove it.

### STORY 3: LIV Golf 2.0 — BC Partners Deal Getting Closer

**Long-Form Post (3:30 PM ET)**
LIV Golf's survival past 2026 is coming into focus — and the picture looks significantly different from the circuit that launched in 2022.

LIV CEO Scott O'Neil has confirmed that a deal with a new lead investor is signed and approved by the board, with closing expected in September. BC Partners' credit division has been identified as leading financing package discussions, with a deal in the range of $250 million. The arrangement would allow LIV to continue beyond 2026 when the Saudi Public Investment Fund — which has invested $5 billion or more in the league's first four years — officially exits its role as primary backer.

But the LIV Golf that emerges will be a smaller, leaner operation. Here is what LIV 2.0 looks like:

→ 10 annual events (down from 14 in 2026)
→ 5 events in the United States, 5 internationally
→ Significantly reduced tournament purses
→ Players become majority equity holders — a model O'Neil called "a first for a major global sports league"

The critical condition: enough of LIV's marquee names must commit to 2027 for investors to feel confident. Commissioner Greg Norman has said he cannot guarantee the 2027 season. Poulter has expressed uncertainty. Dozens of players remain under contract — but their willingness to accept equity over cash is the open question.

LIV's 2026 season concluded with Jon Rahm claiming his third consecutive individual title and La Sasso winning the Indianapolis finale. The league's competitive product has been compelling. Whether the financial model can be reborn under new ownership is the story of the next 30 days.

### STORY 5: Solheim Cup 2026 — USA vs. Europe, 10 Days Away in the Netherlands

**Long-Form Post (8:00 AM ET)**
In 10 days, the best women golfers in the world will face off at one of the sport's most prestigious team competitions — and it is happening in the Netherlands.

The 2026 Solheim Cup takes place September 11-13 at 's-Hertogenbosch (Den Bosch), in the Netherlands. Team USA faces Team Europe in the biennial match-play competition that mirrors the Ryder Cup in its intensity and its stakes. Twenty-eight players — 14 per side, drawn from the LPGA Tour and the Ladies European Tour — compete for the Solheim Cup trophy and the pride of their respective federations.

The 2026 LPGA Tour season has been impressive, with players like Ruoning Yin — who just won the FM Championship at TPC Boston on August 30 — showcasing the depth of talent on the American side. Meanwhile, European players on the LET have been competitive all season, making this a genuinely unpredictable match-play showdown.

After the emotional intensity of any Ryder Cup year, the Solheim Cup carries its own charge. Women's golf's biggest team event deserves the spotlight it gets every two years. The Netherlands in September is a spectacular backdrop.

Ten days. Count them down.
