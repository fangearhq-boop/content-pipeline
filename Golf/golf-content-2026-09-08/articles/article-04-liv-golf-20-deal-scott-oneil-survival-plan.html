LIV 2.0: Scott O'Neil's $250-350M Deal to Save Golf's Rebel League

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | September 8, 2026

Even as LIV Golf prepares for bankruptcy, CEO Scott O'Neil is working on a deal worth $250-350 million to create a scaled-down successor circuit funded by private equity, potentially with players converting guaranteed money into equity stakes.

LIV Golf is facing bankruptcy. But not everyone has accepted that it's over.

Scott O'Neil, LIV Golf's chief executive, is reportedly close to completing a deal worth between $250 million and $350 million that would create "LIV 2.0" — a restructured, scaled-down version of the circuit designed to survive without Saudi Arabian sovereign wealth funding. According to multiple reports citing sources familiar with the deal, O'Neil has been pursuing private equity investment as a replacement for the Public Investment Fund, which is ending its financial commitment after 2026.

BC Partners, a London-based private equity firm, has been identified as a potential investor in the restructured entity. The deal structure would differ fundamentally from the original LIV model.

The New Structure

The LIV 2.0 framework, as described in reports, involves several significant changes from the original model:

Players who were owed guaranteed contract money — one of LIV's primary competitive advantages in recruiting — would receive a portion of that money converted into equity stakes in the new entity rather than cash payments. The bet: that a restructured, viable circuit would eventually generate returns that exceed what a pennies-on-the-dollar bankruptcy settlement would provide.

The playing schedule would be reduced from the current format. LIV moved to 72-hole events in 2026 in an attempt to attract more PGA Tour players and improve its standing in major championship qualifying points discussions. A scaled-down version might revert to a more compressed schedule to reduce operational costs.

The overall organization would be significantly smaller than the enterprise PIF built — which employed more than 300 staff before the current round of layoffs — with a focus on running a sustainable core competition rather than building the global media and sponsorship infrastructure LIV originally envisioned.

The Central Questions

Two questions determine whether LIV 2.0 succeeds or follows its predecessor into the record books.

First: will the players who were owed the largest sums — the DeChambeau-Rahm-Smith tier of the roster — accept equity stakes in a new entity rather than the cash they are owed? For some, the calculation might be straightforward: a guaranteed amount in bankruptcy proceedings might yield five cents per dollar, while an equity stake in a functioning circuit could theoretically return more over time. For others, the appeal of immediate PGA Tour reinstatement — if it becomes available — might outweigh any long-term upside in a restructured LIV.

Second: can a private-equity-funded version of LIV secure the broadcast distribution and sponsorship deals that the original Saudi-backed version never could? The fundamental commercial problem for LIV was always that it lacked a major U.S. television deal that would have built mainstream audience and attracted advertising revenue. Without the PIF's ability to subsidize indefinitely, LIV 2.0 would need to achieve commercial sustainability far more quickly.

Golf's Uncertain Landscape

The LIV story — including its potential 2.0 chapter — is playing out alongside the PGA Tour's own evolution. The Tour added private investment and increased purses for signature events. The DP World Tour strengthened its multi-year partnership with the Asian Tour. The major championships remain independent and universally respected.

Whether a scaled-down LIV fits into this landscape, or whether its best players end up applying for PGA Tour reinstatement, will be one of golf's defining storylines through 2027 and beyond.

What's Next

O'Neil's deal timeline is reportedly in its final stages. A formal announcement — or the absence of one — will clarify whether LIV 2.0 becomes reality or whether the circuit's story ends with the 2026 bankruptcy filing.
