Solheim Cup 2026: Three Days Out — What to Watch When Competition Begins Friday

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | September 8, 2026

Team USA and Team Europe are in the final preparation phase at Bernardus Golf in the Netherlands, with competition set to begin Friday. Here's your full preview of the 2026 Solheim Cup.

The Solheim Cup begins in 72 hours. The course is ready, the teams are assembled, and the Netherlands is hosting women's golf's most prestigious team competition for the very first time.

Today at Bernardus Golf in Cromvoirt is the final scheduled practice round before the 2026 Solheim Cup shifts from preparation to competition. Friday, September 11 brings the opening foursomes — four alternate-shot matches that will set the tone for a weekend that could go in either direction.

Here is what to watch.

Team USA's Strengths

Angela Stanford leads a Team USA that arrives as defending champion, having won the 2024 Solheim Cup 15.5-12.5. The defending champion holds the Cup in the event of a 14-14 tie — a significant structural advantage.

The anchor is Nelly Korda, the world's No. 1 player, who carries a 10-5-1 individual record in Solheim competition. Korda has been among the most dominant players in women's golf over the past several years, having won multiple majors and LPGA events in her prime. At a team event that rewards consistent excellence over four pressure-packed sessions, a player of Korda's profile changes every opponent's calculus.

Stanford's three captain's picks add dimension and intrigue: Lindy Duncan, Megan Khang, and Rose Zhang. Zhang in particular has been one of the most discussed selections — a player whose ball-striking and composure under pressure mirror some of the qualities that make elite team competitors.

The automatic qualifiers — Angel Yin, Jennifer Kupcho, Yealimi Noh, Auston Kim, Lauren Coughlin, Allisen Corpuz, Andrea Lee, and Alison Lee — give USA depth and balance across the full 12-player lineup.

Team Europe's Counters

Anna Nordqvist's Europe side arrives as the challenger, seeking to reclaim the Cup that slipped away in 2024. The European backbone runs through two players: Charley Hull and Lottie Woad.

Hull, playing in her eighth consecutive Solheim Cup, is the veteran presence that any great team competition squad needs — a player who has been in every possible match scenario, who raises her game in the biggest moments, and who represents the emotional core of the European challenge. Carlota Ciganda, another veteran, adds a second anchor of experience.

Woad, ranked No. 5 in the world, is the breakout name on this European roster — the young player whose performance could define whether Europe has enough top-end talent to match USA across all four sessions.

Nordqvist's four captain's picks — Leona Maguire, Julia Lopez Ramirez, Nastasia Nadaud, and Mimi Rhodes — round out a team built to be competitive from the top of the card to the bottom.

Format Breakdown

The 20th Solheim Cup plays 28 total matches across three days. Friday brings four alternate-shot foursomes in the morning and four better-ball fourballs in the afternoon. Saturday repeats the same format. Sunday features 12 singles matches, one per player per team. The first team to accumulate 14.5 points wins the Cup outright. In the event of a 14-14 tie, the defending champions retain.

Foursomes are generally considered the highest-pressure format — the alternate-shot requirement amplifies every error and demands the kind of partnership chemistry that teams spend months developing. Europe has historically been more comfortable in foursomes formats at these team competitions. USA tends to excel in fourballs and singles.

What's Next

The opening foursomes session begins Friday, September 11 at Bernardus Golf in Cromvoirt, Netherlands. Qualifying Saturday, more sessions Saturday, then singles Sunday September 13.
