Scottie Scheffler Leads USA at Medinah: World No. 1 and FedExCup Champion Headlines Snedeker's Presidents Cup Team

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | September 8, 2026

Scottie Scheffler enters the 2026 Presidents Cup as the world's No. 1 player and FedExCup champion, anchoring a Team USA that will be heavily favored at Medinah Country Club on September 24-27.

When the 2026 Presidents Cup tees off at Medinah Country Club on September 24, one player will define every opposing team's preparation: Scottie Scheffler.

The world's No. 1 player and 2026 FedExCup champion headlines a Team USA that captain Brandt Snedeker has built around one of the most complete rosters in Presidents Cup history. Scheffler led all automatic qualifiers, determined by cumulative FedExCup points through the end of the 2026 season, setting a tone that ripples down the entire American lineup.

Scheffler's Profile

In team competition formats, Scheffler's profile translates differently than his individual game. His ball-striking consistency — the quality that has made him the dominant player in world golf — is precisely the attribute that performs best in alternate-shot foursomes, where one errant shot can unravel an entire hole. Snedeker will almost certainly deploy Scheffler as a top-of-the-card anchor in foursomes, pairing him with a player whose short game and creativity can complement Scheffler's fairways and greens precision.

His FedExCup championship adds another dimension to his profile at Medinah. The Tour's season-long points race rewards sustained excellence across the sport's biggest events — a quality that distinguishes great team competition players from streaky ones. Scheffler has it.

The Full USA Lineup

The six automatic qualifiers alongside Scheffler are Cameron Young, Wyndham Clark, Russell Henley, Sam Burns, and Collin Morikawa. Each has won on Tour in recent seasons, and the group's collective recent form is among the strongest any American Presidents Cup team has assembled through the automatic qualifier process.

Snedeker's six captain's picks span experienced team competition veterans and promising debutants. Patrick Cantlay, Xander Schauffele, and Justin Thomas bring Presidents Cup and Ryder Cup experience — players who have been in pressure matches, who know how to compete across four sessions in a condensed competition format. Their presence gives Snedeker lineup flexibility: he can slot experience anywhere in the draw.

The three first-time Presidents Cup players — Jacob Bridgeman, Chris Gotterup, and 21-year-old Jackson Koivun — add a different kind of dimension. Koivun, who left Auburn University earlier this year to turn professional, represents Snedeker's highest-profile expression of confidence in a young player. Bringing a player months into his professional career to a Presidents Cup requires genuine conviction in his ability to handle the occasion.

The Matchup

Geoff Ogilvy's International team is not without weapons. Hideki Matsuyama remains the player USA will plan around — his foursomes record in Presidents Cup competition is the best on the International side historically, and any draw Snedeker constructs will account for where Ogilvy deploys him.

But the structural reality of this Presidents Cup is the same as most of its predecessors: the United States has won 11 of 15 editions. Medinah — the site of two PGA Championships and a Ryder Cup — is not neutral territory for the International side. Home crowd, home venue, and a roster anchored by the world's No. 1 player represent a significant collective advantage.

The International team's best modern-era challenges have come on foreign soil, where the American crowd advantage is absent and the format's compressed intensity can produce upsets. At Medinah in September 2026, with Scheffler at the top of USA's lineup, the path to an International upset is narrow.
