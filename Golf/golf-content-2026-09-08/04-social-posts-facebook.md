### STORY 2: LIV Golf Bankruptcy

**FB Long-Form Post**
Window: 10:30 AM ET

After four years and as much as $8 billion in Saudi Arabian investment, LIV Golf is preparing to file for bankruptcy.

Reports from Semafor, Newsmax, and multiple other outlets confirm that Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund has informed LIV Golf that its funding will end after the 2026 season. The league is now laying the groundwork for a US Chapter 11 bankruptcy filing that could come as early as next week.

The human toll is significant. LIV Golf employed more than 300 staff members — the majority of whom have already been laid off. Players who were promised hundreds of millions in guaranteed contracts are receiving settlement offers worth pennies on the dollar. Bryson DeChambeau, Jon Rahm, and Cameron Smith — three of LIV's highest-profile names — are among those with the largest financial exposure.

How did we get here? Despite PIF's enormous investment, LIV never secured a major U.S. broadcast deal, struggled to attract younger fans who gravitated toward the PGA Tour's narrative-driven events, and ultimately couldn't bring enough of the sport's top players away from the established tour. The much-discussed merger with the PGA Tour never materialized.

Four years after launching with the ambition to revolutionize professional golf, LIV Golf is preparing for its final chapter.

### STORY 4: LIV 2.0 Deal

**FB Long-Form Post**
Window: 3:30 PM ET

Even as LIV Golf prepares for bankruptcy, its CEO is working to keep some version of the league alive.

Scott O'Neil, who has been trying to transform LIV into a sustainable commercial enterprise, is reportedly close to finalizing a deal worth between $250 million and $350 million to create "LIV 2.0" — a scaled-down, restructured version of the circuit. The plan would replace Saudi PIF as the primary funder with private equity investment, with BC Partners cited as a potential partner.

Under the restructured model, players who were owed large guaranteed contracts might receive equity stakes in the new entity rather than cash. The playing schedule would be reduced from the current format, and the organizational structure would be slimmed down significantly from the $5-8 billion enterprise that PIF built.

The key question: would the sport's top LIV players — those who left the PGA Tour for guaranteed money — accept equity deals in place of the cash they were promised? And with the PGA Tour maintaining its position that there is no reinstatement path for LIV golfers, what are their alternatives?

The next few weeks will determine whether LIV continues in any form or whether 2026 marks its final season entirely.
