Australian Open Preview: McIlroy Headlines Kingston Heath for December Showpiece

By Marcus Cole

November 27, 2026 | Melbourne

The BMW Australian PGA Championship at The Lakes in Sydney is not yet complete, but the golf world's attention is already beginning to drift south toward Melbourne. The Australian Open at Kingston Heath Golf Club (December 3-6) brings together one of the strongest fields assembled for a December event anywhere in the world, with Rory McIlroy leading a cast that spans continents and generations.

The Field

McIlroy arrives chasing his second Stonehaven Cup. His presence alone elevates the event's international profile, and his enthusiasm for Australian golf has been genuine — he has spoken publicly about the quality of the southern hemisphere's December calendar and what it means to compete on the sandbelt.

Adam Scott represents the host nation's most decorated active player, a former world No. 1 who carries enormous weight with Australian galleries. His participation at Kingston Heath, a course that embodies everything the Melbourne sandbelt stands for, will draw particular attention from local fans.

Cameron Smith's presence is conditional on whatever happens at The Lakes this weekend, but his inclusion in the Australian Open field means Australian golf fans could follow one of the sport's great comeback stories for two consecutive weeks. Min Woo Lee, the Perth native and emerging DP World Tour talent, rounds out the Australian contingent as a crowd favorite with the shotmaking to challenge on a demanding course.

Sahith Theegala, the PGA Tour player making the trip to the southern hemisphere, adds American interest to the event and continues a pattern of international players recognizing the value of competing in Australia's December window.

Kingston Heath: A Sandbelt Classic

Kingston Heath Golf Club is one of Australia's genuinely great courses — a links-influenced design where the premium is on shot-making and course management rather than length alone. The sandbelt style of golf, rooted in the Melbourne suburb of Cheltenham, produces courses that test every part of a player's game and reward those who can maneuver the ball in multiple directions.

The course has not hosted the Australian Open since 1990, making this year's return a significant occasion for the club and for Australian golf history. Kingston Heath in top condition for a major national championship represents one of the sport's most desirable setups.

The Bigger Picture

Both the Australian PGA Championship and the Australian Open have operated as DP World Tour co-sanctioned events, connecting the southern hemisphere calendar to the European circuit. Starting in 2027, a formal PGA Tour-DP World Tour-Golf Australia partnership commences, deepening that connection and raising the international stakes of these events further.

This year's Australian Open, held before that partnership formally takes effect, functions as both a compelling event in its own right and a preview of the elevated role these two weeks will play in the global golf calendar going forward.

What's Next

The Australian Open runs December 3-6 at Kingston Heath Golf Club, Melbourne. The full field will be confirmed as the Australian PGA concludes this weekend. McIlroy has indicated plans to remain in Australia following the Australian Open for additional commitments, underscoring the commitment international players are making to this window of southern hemisphere golf.
