Cameron Smith's Road Back: Post-LIV Comeback and the Path to the PGA Tour

By Jake Torres

November 27, 2026 | The Lakes, Sydney

Cameron Smith stood on the 14th fairway at The Lakes on Friday afternoon and hit a golf shot. The result — an eagle on the par-5, one of the highlights of his second-round 64 — was the kind of moment that reminds you what Smith is capable of when everything clicks. It was also a reminder of what his absence has cost the sport.

Smith is playing in his first DP World Tour event since LIV Golf's collapse earlier in 2026. Saudi backers pulled funding from the breakaway circuit, leaving dozens of players — including some of the sport's most recognizable names — suddenly without a home. Smith is now working his way back to the stage his talent deserves.

The Stakes This Week

The path is specific. Smith needs to win the BMW Australian PGA Championship this week, win next week's Australian Open at Kingston Heath, or finish in the top 10 of the DP World Tour order of merit for the season. The quickest and most satisfying route runs through The Lakes, a course where Smith has won four times as a professional.

He enters the weekend four shots off the lead held by Germany's Nicolai Von Dellingshausen at 13-under. McKibbin of Northern Ireland is at 10-under. Smith sits at 9-under following his second-round 64 — a round that included the eagle at 14 and signaled to the field that he is very much in this tournament.

"That's where I want to be now, for sure," Smith told the Sydney Morning Herald when asked about returning to the PGA Tour. The quote is direct and unambiguous. He is not entertaining consolation paths. He wants the tour where the best players compete, and he is spending this week at home in Australia trying to earn his way back to it.

What LIV's Collapse Changed

When LIV Golf launched in 2022, it fractured professional golf in ways that proved difficult to manage. Smith was among the highest-profile departures, leaving behind a PGA Tour career that included his 2022 Open Championship title at St Andrews. He chose to decline a two-year DP World Tour card he had earned after winning the Australian PGA that same year.

When Saudi backers pulled out in 2026, that chapter ended. Players like Brooks Koepka found their way back via the PGA Tour's Returning Member Program earlier in the year. That program, per PGA Tour CEO Brian Rolapp, is not available to Smith. His route back must come through performance.

So here he is, in Sydney, four shots back entering the weekend of the same tournament he has won four times before.

The Australian Open Provides a Safety Net

Even if this week does not go perfectly, Smith has another opportunity. The Australian Open at Kingston Heath in Melbourne runs December 3-6. McIlroy headlines the field, and Smith's participation — coming off whatever result he posts at The Lakes — will be one of the defining storylines of that event as well.

Australian golf fans have watched one of their own navigate an unconventional path through the sport's most turbulent period. The 64 on Friday, the eagle on 14, the four shots separating him from the lead — these are not abstract statistics. They are the specific details of a comeback that Australian golf wants to see completed.

What's Next

Smith plays rounds 3 and 4 on Saturday and Sunday at The Lakes. A win this week would open the PGA Tour door. If he falls short, Kingston Heath awaits the following week. Either way, Cameron Smith is back in professional golf's most meaningful conversations after years spent on the outside looking in.
