December Golf in Australia Is the World's Most Underrated Sports Window

By Jake Torres

November 27, 2026 | Australia

The PGA Tour shuts down. The DP World Tour wraps its European season. The world's attention turns to end-of-year reviews and next-year projections. And quietly, in Sydney and Melbourne, two of the best weeks of professional golf take place under blue Australian summer skies.

This is December golf Down Under. It is worth your full attention.

The Two-Week Window

This week: the BMW Australian PGA Championship at The Lakes in Sydney (November 26-29). The tournament carries a record purse of AU$2.5 million on the DP World Tour. Nicolai Von Dellingshausen leads at 13-under after two rounds, with Tom McKibbin at 10-under and Cameron Smith surging to 9-under after a second-round 64 that included an eagle on the par-5 14th. Amateur Haydn Barron led outright after the opening round. The week has delivered exactly what elite golf in Australia tends to deliver: compelling stories, genuine competition, and performances worth watching.

Next week: the Australian Open at Kingston Heath Golf Club in Melbourne (December 3-6). Rory McIlroy, Adam Scott, Cameron Smith, Min Woo Lee, and Sahith Theegala are in a field that would not be out of place at any event on the global calendar. Kingston Heath — a sandbelt classic that last hosted the Australian Open in 1990 — is a venue that makes the golf look as good as it plays.

What Makes This Calendar Underrated

Part of what gets overlooked about Australian summer golf is the contrast it provides. While the sport's Northern Hemisphere audience is in the depths of winter, Australia operates at peak golfing conditions — firm fairways, clear skies, warm temperatures that keep the turf fast and the scoring interesting.

The courses help. The Lakes is a testing layout that demands precision. Kingston Heath is among the finest courses in the world, full stop. Players who make the trip south are rewarded with conditions that showcase the best of what golf has to offer as a viewing and playing experience.

Then there are the storylines. Cameron Smith using this fortnight to chart a path back to the PGA Tour. McIlroy chasing his second Stonehaven Cup. Adam Scott, perennial and beloved, competing at home in December. Min Woo Lee building his reputation against elite company. These are not provincial stories dressed up as important ones. They are genuinely consequential for the sport.

What 2027 Changes

Starting in 2027, a formal partnership between the PGA Tour, the DP World Tour, and Golf Australia takes effect. That partnership will deepen the international significance of the Australian summer events, formally connecting them to the global tours in ways that make them even more attractive to elite players and even more relevant to the broader golf audience.

This year's events are, in a sense, the last version of the Australian summer calendar before that upgrade. They are already excellent. What comes next should be even more so.

What's Next

The BMW Australian PGA Championship concludes Saturday and Sunday at The Lakes in Sydney. The Australian Open follows immediately at Kingston Heath, Melbourne (December 3-6). Two weeks, two elite events, two iconic venues. If you have not been following Australian golf this month, now is the moment to start.
