Von Dellingshausen Takes Control at Australian PGA After Round 2

By Ryan Calloway

November 27, 2026 | The Lakes, Sydney

Germany's Nicolai Von Dellingshausen has seized command of the BMW Australian PGA Championship after a dominant second round at The Lakes in Sydney, moving to 13-under par and opening a three-shot lead as the tournament heads into the weekend.

Von Dellingshausen's performance through the first two rounds has been the defining story of the week at The Lakes. His 13-under total puts him comfortably ahead of the chasing pack with two rounds remaining in the DP World Tour event, which carries a record purse of AU$2.5 million.

Leaderboard After Round 2

Tom McKibbin of Northern Ireland sits in second place at 10-under par, three shots back. McKibbin has been a consistent presence on leaderboards across the DP World Tour this season and enters the weekend very much in contention.

Cameron Smith has emerged as the most compelling storyline of the second round. The four-time Australian PGA champion shot a brilliant 64 on Friday, including an eagle on the par-5 14th hole, to surge to 9-under par. Smith enters the weekend four shots off the lead — a deficit that is fully manageable across two final rounds at a course he knows exceptionally well.

Amateur Haydn Barron, who captured imaginations by leading the tournament outright after the opening round at 8-under par, has seen the professional field move past him as the elite players asserted themselves over 36 holes. His performance remains one of the week's most memorable moments regardless of where he finishes.

Von Dellingshausen's Path to the Lead

The German's accumulated performance over two rounds reflects precision and consistency that has not wavered under the conditions at The Lakes. At 13-under through 36 holes, he has built a lead that demands those chasing him play near-perfect golf to overtake him.

The BMW Australian PGA Championship is part of the DP World Tour's expanded international calendar, and Von Dellingshausen's performance here would represent a significant result on the European-based circuit. The record AU$2.5 million purse underscores how seriously Golf Australia and the DP World Tour have invested in building the event's prestige.

Smith's Charge Creates a Story Within the Story

While Von Dellingshausen holds the lead, all eyes on Saturday will follow Cameron Smith. His 64 in round 2 — featuring that eagle at the par-5 14th — was the kind of round that signals a player has found something. Smith has won this tournament four times. He knows The Lakes. And this week carries stakes that extend far beyond the leaderboard.

Smith needs a win at the Australian PGA or the Australian Open next week at Kingston Heath to secure a path back to the PGA Tour. Four shots back entering the final two rounds is not a comfortable position, but it is not an impossible one either — not for a player of Smith's caliber at a venue where he has lifted the trophy before.

What's Next

Rounds 3 and 4 are scheduled for Saturday and Sunday, November 28-29. Von Dellingshausen will play the final groups alongside McKibbin and, potentially, Smith. The tournament winner claims the lion's share of the AU$2.5 million purse and significant DP World Tour points.

Following the Australian PGA, attention shifts to Melbourne for the Australian Open at Kingston Heath (December 3-6), where Rory McIlroy, Adam Scott, Min Woo Lee, and Sahith Theegala join Smith in a field that has generated significant international interest.
