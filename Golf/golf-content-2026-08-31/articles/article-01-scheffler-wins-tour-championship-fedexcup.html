Scheffler Wins Tour Championship — Second FedExCup Title in Three Years, Passes Tiger on Career Money List

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | August 31, 2026

Tour Championship · FedExCup · Scottie Scheffler · PGA Tour

Scottie Scheffler is the 2026 FedExCup champion, and he earned it the way champions do — by making the decisive move when it mattered most. Trailing by three shots entering Sunday's final round at East Lake Golf Club in Atlanta, Scheffler fired a 4-under-par 66 — the lowest round of the day among the lead groups — to pass overnight leader Viktor Hovland and finish at 16-under for the championship. The $10 million winner's share pushes his career earnings past Tiger Woods on the PGA Tour's all-time money list. And his second FedExCup title in three years places him alongside Woods and Rory McIlroy as the only players in history to win multiple FedExCup championships.

The Comeback

Viktor Hovland had the championship in his grip entering Sunday. The Norwegian, who had made over 100 feet of putts in each of his first three rounds, led by three at the 54-hole mark at 15-under. His putter had been the story of the week. On Sunday, it went silent.

Hovland made only one birdie on the final day and closed with a 2-over 72, finishing alone in second at 13-under. He earned $5 million from the FedExCup pool — a significant reward, but not the one he had positioned himself for entering the final round.

Scheffler, meanwhile, was methodical. He made the birdies he needed, avoided the mistakes Hovland could not, and steadily climbed the leaderboard as Sunday afternoon played out in his favor. By the 18th green, the deficit of three shots had become a winning margin of three. A 4-under 66 is remarkable on any Sunday at East Lake — it was the lone under-par final round among the leading pairings.

The Historic Achievement

Scheffler joins Tiger Woods and Rory McIlroy as the only players to win the FedExCup more than once. Woods won it in 2007 and 2009. McIlroy has claimed the title three times — 2016, 2019, and 2022. Scheffler won his first in 2024 and his second this Sunday. He is 30 years old.

The FedExCup No. 1 seed for the fifth consecutive year — unprecedented in the modern Tour — Scheffler has made the Tour Championship feel like his personal autumn tradition. Five trips to East Lake as the top seed. Two championships in three appearances in the field. The consistency of excellence is genuinely without modern parallel.

Passing Tiger on the Money List

The $10 million winner's share was more than a prize — it was a historical marker. With his FedExCup earnings now counted, Scheffler's PGA Tour career earnings have surpassed $130 million, passing Tiger Woods on the Tour's all-time career money list. Woods accumulated $120.9 million in official PGA Tour earnings over a career spanning three decades. Scheffler reached and exceeded that total before his 31st birthday.

The record reflects both the dramatic increase in prize money over the past decade and Scheffler's extraordinary win rate. Twenty-two PGA Tour victories including two Masters titles form the backbone of his earnings — wins that compound in prize value as Tour purses have grown year over year.

What's Next

With the FedExCup playoffs complete, Scheffler's 2026 PGA Tour season wraps at its apex. The fall schedule resumes with the Biltmore Championship (September 17-20) and the Presidents Cup at Medinah (September 24-27), where Scheffler will anchor a loaded United States team. A player of his caliber rarely stays idle for long. But for now — the 2026 FedExCup trophy belongs to Scottie Scheffler, and he earned it the only way that matters.
