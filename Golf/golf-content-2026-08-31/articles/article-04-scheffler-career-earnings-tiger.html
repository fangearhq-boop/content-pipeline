Scheffler Passes Tiger Woods on PGA Tour's All-Time Career Money List — $130 Million and Counting

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | August 31, 2026

PGA Tour · Scottie Scheffler · Records

Scottie Scheffler crossed a milestone on Sunday that Tiger Woods spent three decades building — and Scheffler did it before his 31st birthday. The $10 million winner's share from the 2026 Tour Championship pushed Scheffler's career PGA Tour earnings past $130 million, officially surpassing Tiger Woods on the Tour's all-time career money list. Woods had accumulated $120.9 million in official PGA Tour earnings across a career that stretched from the 1990s into the 2020s. Scheffler has now exceeded that total in less than five full seasons as a Tour member.

The Context of the Record

Two forces have combined to make this record possible at a pace Tiger Woods never could have achieved: dramatically higher purses and an extraordinary win rate. PGA Tour prize funds have grown substantially over the past decade, meaning each victory Scheffler records is worth far more in dollar terms than the equivalent win in Tiger's peak years of the early 2000s. Tiger's 82 wins — the PGA Tour's all-time record — were accumulated when tournaments routinely offered $5-7 million in total prize money. Scheffler's wins have been recorded at purses that frequently exceed $20 million.

But the win rate itself is also genuinely remarkable. Twenty-two PGA Tour victories. Two Masters titles. Two FedExCup championships. Five consecutive years as the FedExCup No. 1 seed — a streak unprecedented in the modern Tour. Scheffler has made winning feel ordinary, which is perhaps the most extraordinary thing about his career to date.

What the Number Reflects

Career money is an imperfect measure of greatness — the era inflation makes direct comparison to Tiger's career nearly impossible. A dollar earned on Tour in 2026 is not the same as one earned in 2002. But the record is real, and it carries symbolic weight that numbers with appropriate caveats still cannot fully deflate. Scheffler has surpassed the career financial total of the most dominant golfer in history — a player who defined an era and set standards the sport spent two decades chasing.

The money list milestone is one marker among several now associated with Scheffler's career trajectory. At 30 years old, he has 22 wins, which gives him a realistic — if still long — path to Tiger's all-time record of 82. The pace required (60 more wins) is historic. So is everything else Scheffler has done.

What's Next

Scheffler's 2026 PGA Tour season officially concludes with his FedExCup title. The fall schedule resumes in mid-September, with the Presidents Cup at Medinah Country Club on September 24-27 providing the next marquee opportunity for the world's No. 1 player to compete. At 30, with a second FedExCup, two Masters, and now the all-time career money record, Scheffler has made the case — statistically and competitively — that he is the best player of his generation. Whether that translates into a pursuit of Tiger's win record is a story that is far from its final chapter.
