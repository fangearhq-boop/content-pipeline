LIV Golf Secures New Lead Investor as PIF Exits — BC Partners Signs $250M+ Term Sheet for LIV 2.0

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | August 31, 2026

LIV Golf · Business · 2027 Season

LIV Golf has found its financial lifeline for 2027 — but the league that replaces the original version will look considerably different from what more than 50 players signed up for. Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, which poured more than $5 billion into LIV Golf since its 2022 launch, has officially confirmed it will end funding at the close of the 2026 season. In its place: Ted Goldthorpe of London-based private equity firm BC Partners has signed a term sheet worth at least $250 million for the 2027 season. LIV CEO Scott O'Neil has stated he hopes to finalize and close the deal in September.

LIV 2.0 — A Leaner Operation

The proposed structure for 2027 represents a significant reduction in scale. The annual schedule would be cut from 14 events to 10, and per-event prize money would drop from $30 million to approximately $15 million per event. A group of minority investors — more than a dozen parties reportedly interested — would contribute additional capital alongside BC Partners to fund what the league describes as a "player-driven" ownership model.

If the deal closes on O'Neil's September timeline, LIV's total prize pool for 2027 would approximate $150 million — less than half the cumulative value players have been competing for under the PIF-backed structure. The format shift, while significant, is being framed by league leadership as a more sustainable foundation for the league's long-term existence.

Uncertainty Remains High

Commissioner Greg Norman has declined to guarantee 2027 season commitments, and the players waiting for clarity are doing exactly that — waiting. Ian Poulter, one of the highest-profile names in the league since its launch, said recently that "time might run out" for him and that he "genuinely hasn't decided yet" whether to return to LIV in 2027 under the restructured model.

Jon Rahm, who claimed his third consecutive individual LIV title in 2026 — most recently at the Indianapolis finale, with La Sasso winning the team title — has multiple years remaining on his contract. But the framework surrounding those contracts is in flux as the ownership structure changes, adding legal and financial complexity to what is already an uncertain situation.

The critical question for the 50-plus contracted players is not whether a deal exists in term-sheet form, but whether it closes — and whether the deal that closes actually delivers the security, structure, and prize money that made LIV attractive in 2022. A signed term sheet and a funded season are different things.

PIF's Exit and What It Means

The PIF's departure marks the end of the original LIV experiment as it was conceived. The Saudi sovereign wealth fund's investment of more than $5 billion over four years bought the league enormous prize pools, high-profile player contracts, and a sustained challenge to the PGA Tour's dominance of professional golf. It did not buy a sustainable business model on its own terms.

The league's ongoing framework negotiations with the PGA Tour — which have proceeded in fits and starts since the 2023 framework agreement announcement — have added additional uncertainty to where elite professional golf's business model ultimately lands. A finalized BC Partners deal would give LIV a degree of independence from those negotiations, but 10 events at $15 million each is a different proposition than the league players originally chose.

What's Next

O'Neil has set September as the target for deal closure, which would give LIV enough runway to plan a 2027 schedule and communicate commitments to players before the window for decisions closes. Whether the deal closes on that timeline — and whether players like Poulter, Rahm, and others ultimately find the restructured terms acceptable — will determine whether LIV Golf enters 2027 as a functioning competitive league or something smaller still. The clock is running.
