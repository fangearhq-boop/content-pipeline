### STORY 1: Scheffler Wins Tour Championship

**Long-Form Post (8:00 AM ET)**
Scottie Scheffler did it again — and he did it the hard way.

Down three shots entering Sunday's final round at East Lake Golf Club, Scheffler fired a 4-under 66 — the lowest round of the day among the lead groups — to pass overnight leader Viktor Hovland and claim the 2026 Tour Championship at 16-under par. Hovland, who had led by three shots entering Sunday, made only one birdie and closed with a 2-over 72 to finish solo second at -13.

Scheffler wins $10 million and his second FedExCup title in three years, joining Tiger Woods and Rory McIlroy as the only golfers in history to win multiple FedExCup titles. The 30-year-old Texan makes it look routine — a fifth consecutive year as the FedExCup No. 1 seed, an unprecedented streak — but the ability to come from behind on Sunday at the season's final event speaks to something rarer than statistics can capture.

The comeback was methodical. Scheffler made birdies when he needed them, avoided bogeys when others around him were struggling, and never stopped applying pressure. By the time he walked off the 18th green, the scoreboard told the full story: the best player in golf, winning when it mattered most, one more time.

### STORY 2: Ruoning Yin FM Championship

**Long-Form Post (10:30 AM ET)**
Twenty-three years old. Six career LPGA Tour victories. And a win delivered on one of the most dramatic final holes of the 2026 season.

Ruoning Yin of China won the 2026 FM Championship at TPC Boston, closing with a 2-under 70 for a 14-under total of 274 — good for a two-shot win over Hyo Joo Kim. The final round drama came in the back nine: Yin ran in a 25-foot birdie putt on the par-5 12th to share the lead with her playing partner Kim. Kim then three-putted the par-3 16th — missing a 9-foot par putt — to hand the advantage back to Yin. Yin closed it out with a birdie on the 72nd hole, the par-5 18th, to seal the two-shot victory.

It was Yin's sixth LPGA Tour win and her first in approximately two years — a return to the winner's circle on a stage that featured the second-largest non-major purse on the LPGA schedule. Allisen Corpuz finished alone in third at -11; Rose Zhang and Sei Young Kim shared fourth at -10.

The tournament had navigated weather drama — rain forced Round 2 and Round 3 to be played back-to-back on Saturday — but it delivered a Sunday that justified every minute of patience.

### STORY 3: LIV Golf Secures Lead Investor — LIV 2.0 Takes Shape

**Long-Form Post (1:00 PM ET)**
LIV Golf's future is coming into focus — and it looks very different from what players signed up for in 2022.

Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, which invested more than $5 billion in LIV since its launch, has officially confirmed it will end funding at the close of the 2026 season. But the league has found a replacement: Ted Goldthorpe of London-based BC Partners has signed a term sheet worth at least $250 million for the 2027 season. CEO Scott O'Neil says he hopes to finalize and close the deal in September.

If it closes, LIV 2.0 will be a leaner operation. The proposed structure cuts the annual schedule from 14 events to 10, and reduces per-event prize money from $30 million to approximately $15 million. A group of minority investors — more than a dozen parties reportedly interested — would contribute additional capital to create what the league describes as a "player-driven" model.

But significant uncertainty remains. Commissioner Greg Norman has declined to guarantee 2027 season commitments. Ian Poulter said recently that "time might run out" and that he "genuinely hasn't decided yet" whether to return. Jon Rahm, who claimed his third consecutive individual title in 2026 with La Sasso winning the Indianapolis finale, has multiple years left on his contract — but the framework around those contracts is changing.

Fifty-plus players are waiting. The clock is running.

### STORY 4: Scheffler Passes Tiger on Career Money List

**Long-Form Post (3:30 PM ET)**
The numbers are staggering — and Scottie Scheffler just made them bigger.

With his $10 million winner's share from the 2026 Tour Championship, Scheffler surpassed Tiger Woods on the PGA Tour's all-time career money list, pushing his career earnings past $130 million. Tiger's record — $120.9 million in official PGA Tour earnings accumulated over a career spanning from the 1990s through the 2020s — has stood as the standard of accumulative excellence for more than two decades.

Scheffler is 30 years old and has barely been on Tour for five full seasons.

The pace is unprecedented. Twenty-two PGA Tour victories. Two Masters titles. Two FedExCup championships. Five consecutive years as the FedExCup No. 1 seed. The rate at which Scheffler has accumulated wins and purse checks has no historical parallel in the modern game — not since Tiger's peak seasons in the early 2000s, and even then the prize funds were a fraction of today's values.

The career money list tells one version of the story. The wins list tells another. At 30, Scheffler has the clearest path of any active golfer to Tiger's all-time record of 82 PGA Tour victories. He already has 22.
