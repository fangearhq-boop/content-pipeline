Scheffler's Impossible 2026: Five Runner-Ups, Winless in Four Majors, and Still World No. 1

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | July 28, 2026

The PGA Tour officially confirmed Scheffler's fifth runner-up of the year on July 26. The paradox of his season gets stranger the more carefully you look at it.

The PGA Tour published a headline on July 26 that encapsulates Scottie Scheffler's 2026 season with complete precision: "Scottie Scheffler settles for fifth runner-up in 2026, tips cap to Jackson Koivun at 3M Open."

Five runner-up finishes. One win — the American Express in January, by four strokes at -27 under, his 20th PGA Tour victory. Since then, Scheffler has been the second-best player at five different tournaments, including the 3M Open, where he shot an 8-under 63 in the final round and lost by three to a 21-year-old in his third professional start.

He is still World No. 1. He still leads the FedExCup standings. He still holds the PGA Tour's lowest scoring average. Scheffler's 2026 is, statistically and practically, one of the most unusual dominant seasons in the Tour's modern history.

The Major Championship Absence

Scheffler went winless in all four major championships of 2026. At The Open Championship at Royal Birkdale, he led through 54 holes and faltered on Sunday, finishing third. His proximity to major victories — leading heading into final rounds, playing at a level that would win most weeks — produced nothing in the most important events of the year.

In 2024, Scheffler won seven tournaments. In 2025, he won six, including multiple majors. The expectation in 2026 was continuation of that trajectory. What happened instead is the version of Scheffler who plays brilliantly, scores just below winning, and watches the trophy go to someone else.

The Historical Rarity

The PGA Tour, in its coverage of the 3M Open, noted that Scheffler and Cameron Young in 2022 are the only players in the last 40 years to record five or more runner-up finishes in a single season. That is not a crowded list. It reflects a specific kind of excellence — performance that reaches the highest levels consistently without converting it into victories.

Young's 2022 season, with six runner-ups and zero wins, drew comparisons to some of the Tour's legendary near-miss years. Scheffler's 2026 is now in the same conversation, with the complicating factor that Scheffler is the defending two-time Tour dominant force, not a player trying to break through.

What Scheffler Said

"I think I've been really close to winning some tournaments," Scheffler said after the 3M Open, "and that can be frustrating."

The understatement is characteristic. Close to winning some tournaments. Five times second. Winless at four majors. A final-round 63 that lost to a player who turned pro 24 days ago. Scheffler's 2026 is frustrating in exactly the way he described — and more so for anyone watching from the outside who knows what he is capable of.

The FedEx Cup Playoffs begin next. There will be more opportunities. The question is whether the season that has made the world No. 1 into the Tour's most decorated also-ran can turn before it is over.
