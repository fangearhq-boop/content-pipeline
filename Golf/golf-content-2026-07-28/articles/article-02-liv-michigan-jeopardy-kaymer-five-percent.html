LIV Golf's Michigan Season Finale Has Zero Infrastructure Built — A Team Captain Gives It 5% Odds

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | July 28, 2026

The event is a month away. The site looks like a golf course, not a tournament venue. A captain publicly said "highly unlikely." And the money isn't there.

The LIV Golf season-ending team championship is scheduled for August 27-30 at The Cardinal at Saint John's Resort in Plymouth Township, Michigan. That is four weeks from now. According to reporting from the Detroit News and Front Office Sports dated July 23, the site currently has no infrastructure installed — no hospitality suites, no concert stage, no player walking bridge over the signature pond, no signage. Nothing.

Martin Kaymer, the captain of Cleeks GC and a former major champion, was asked about the event's prospects. He gave it a 5% chance of happening. "Highly unlikely," he said, in public, about his own league's flagship end-of-season event.

That is where LIV Golf's 2026 season currently stands.

The Financial Background

The root of the Michigan situation is funding. Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, which has invested approximately $5.3 billion in LIV Golf since the league launched in 2022, has cut off financial support. LIV is seeking at least $300 million in replacement capital to complete the 2026 season.

The contractors engaged to build out the Michigan event venue have had their travel and accommodation canceled. Some are waiting for payment. The physical silence on the Plymouth Township site — four weeks before a major professional golf championship — reflects what is happening at the organizational level.

LIV already canceled its New Orleans event earlier in 2026. The New Orleans cancellation was the clearest pre-Michigan signal that the financial situation had moved from difficult to critical.

What the Michigan Event Was Supposed to Be

The LIV team championship was designed as the capstone of the league's season — a team-format finale that would close out the individual and team standings for 2026. The venue at Saint John's Resort in Plymouth Township was selected in part for the spectacle possibilities of its setting, including the signature pond that was to be crossed by a player walking bridge that has not been built.

The August 27-30 dates coincide with a stretch of the PGA Tour's regular season where LIV's year would historically conclude with fanfare. Instead, as of late July, the event's survival is being publicly doubted by one of its own team captains at a 5% probability.

What's Next

The event remains on LIV Golf's official schedule. That is the only positive signal available. Whether LIV secures the capital needed to deliver the Michigan event — or whether the season ends with another canceled entry — will likely be known within the next two weeks, given how little setup time remains before the August 27 start.

For the players, the team standings and the team championship payout are at stake. For the league, the question is whether it can complete its own season. Martin Kaymer put the odds at 5%. The four weeks remaining will determine which side of that number LIV ends up on.
