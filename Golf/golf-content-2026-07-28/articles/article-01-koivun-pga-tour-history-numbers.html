The 21-Year-Old Who Just Made PGA Tour History in His Third Start — Koivun by the Numbers

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | July 28, 2026

The win itself was remarkable. The full context of what Jackson Koivun did at TPC Twin Cities is something different.

Jackson Koivun won the 3M Open on Sunday in his third professional start. That sentence is already unusual. The full scope of what it means has been arriving in coverage Monday, and the context keeps adding layers to what happened at TPC Twin Cities in Blaine, Minnesota.

According to PGA Tour reporting, Koivun is the youngest American to win a PGA Tour event by multiple shots in 94 years. He is the second-fastest player in the history of the PGA Tour to earn a victory. He turned professional approximately 24 days before he won — making his debut at the John Deere Classic and reaching his first winner's circle before the month was out.

He is 21 years old.

The Numbers

At TPC Twin Cities, Koivun shot -25 for the week — a new 72-hole tournament record for the 3M Open. His final-round score was not published in available post-race coverage, but the winning margin was three strokes over Scottie Scheffler, the world No. 1, who closed with an 8-under 63 and still lost by three. This was not a close contest. It was not a playoff. Koivun won clean.

The payout: $1,584,000 from an $8.8 million purse. The FedExCup points: 500, enough to vault him into the FedEx Cup Playoffs. The career moment: first Tour win, delivered in an event where he entered the week with two professional starts and zero cuts made.

What He Did at Auburn

Koivun grew up in Chapel Hill, North Carolina, and built a college record at Auburn that the sport had not seen before. He won 11 times in his collegiate career, the Auburn record. His scoring average was 68.89. He became the first SEC golfer to win three consecutive SEC Player of the Year awards in 60 years. He won the Ben Hogan Award, the Jack Nicklaus Award, and the Fred Haskins Award — twice each. Auburn won the NCAA championship in June 2026, and Koivun was at the center of that team.

He then turned pro and needed 24 days to win on Tour. The transition from college golf to the PGA Tour is one of the most analyzed, most frequently failed passages in the sport. Koivun completed it before the first month was finished.

What Scheffler's 63 Means for Context

Scottie Scheffler shot 63 in the final round of the 3M Open. That is an exceptional score. The world No. 1 played one of the best rounds of the tournament on Sunday and lost by three shots to a player in his third professional start. That is the measure of what Koivun did in Minnesota.

Scheffler now has five runner-up finishes in 2026 and one win — the American Express in January. The contrast between those two players' weeks says everything about the moment Koivun just had.

The records will stand for some time. The youngest American to win by multiple shots in 94 years. The second-fastest ever to win on Tour. -25 at TPC Twin Cities. Three strokes over the best player in the world. Third professional start.

The numbers tell the story. They will keep doing so for a long time.
