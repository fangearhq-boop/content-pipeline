### STORY 2: LIV Michigan Canceled

**Long-form Post (10:30 AM ET)**

It's official. LIV Golf has canceled the Michigan event.

The $40 million team championship — the season finale, scheduled for August 27–30 at The Cardinal at Saint John's Resort in suburban Detroit — is off.

This is the second cancellation of LIV's 2026 season. New Orleans, originally scheduled for June 25–28, was the first. LIV's original 2026 schedule had 14 events. It's now down to 12 completed or surviving.

Two events remain:
• LIV New York — Trump National Bedminster, NJ — August 6–9 — $30M purse
• LIV Indianapolis — The Club at Chatham Hills — August 20–23 — $30M purse

Last week, Martin Kaymer — captain of Cleeks GC — said the Michigan event was "highly unlikely" to be played after a captains' meeting. He estimated 5% odds. CEO Scott O'Neil led that meeting and, when asked directly if he could guarantee all remaining events would happen, he couldn't.

There won't be a LIV team champion this year. Michigan was the season finale where that title would have been decided.

Two events left. A CEO who can't guarantee they happen. The 2026 LIV Golf season looks very different from what was announced.

### STORY 3: LIV Golf Financial Crisis

**Long-form Post (1:00 PM ET)**

Let's talk numbers.

LIV Golf's international entity — LIV Golf Ltd — lost £461.8 million in 2024 alone. One year. £461.8 million. That's roughly $600 million at current exchange rates.

The monthly burn rate is approximately $100 million. The revenue ratio is $8.50 spent for every $1 earned.

Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, which has spent more than $5 billion funding LIV since the first tournament in June 2022, announced in April that it will stop funding the league at the end of the 2026 season.

Bloomberg reported this week that LIV is laying the groundwork to file for Chapter 11 bankruptcy at the end of this season. The plan reportedly includes relocating LIV's headquarters to the US specifically to access US bankruptcy law.

In May, CNBC reported LIV was seeking $250 million to $350 million from new investors. CEO Scott O'Neil told CNBC to "take PIF at their word" when pressed about the funding cliff — not a statement brimming with confidence.

LIV Golf launched with the stated goal of transforming professional golf's economics. The economics of LIV itself tell a different story.

Five billion dollars. Three years. Two events left on this season's schedule. A bankruptcy plan in place.

### STORY 4: Rocket Classic / FedExCup Bubble

**Long-form Post (3:30 PM ET)**

The final Rocket Classic starts Thursday.

Rocket Companies announced it will not renew its title sponsorship after the 2026 season. That means this week's event at Detroit Golf Club — July 30 through August 2 — is the last time the tournament carries that name.

For players in the field, the ending of a sponsorship era matters far less than what's at stake: 500 FedExCup points. The same 500 points that Jackson Koivun just won at the 3M Open, which moved him from unranked to inside the top 70 and into the FedExCup Playoffs.

Two regular-season events remain before the Playoffs begin. This is the first.

Before the 3M Open, the bubble watch included names like Brooks Koepka (82nd), Tony Finau (83rd), Andrew Novak (84th), and Billy Horschel (101st). After the 3M Open results, those positions have shifted. The Rocket Classic is a chance to move dramatically, in either direction.

The Wyndham Championship (August 6–9) is the final regular-season event. After that, the field for the FedEx St. Jude Championship — the first Playoff event, open to the top 70 — is set.

Top 70 advance. Top 50 make the BMW. Top 30 make the Tour Championship.

Two weeks to define a season. Detroit is the first one.

Now let's talk about what that actually means for the rest of his 2026 season.

Eleven career collegiate wins. Two-time winner of the Ben Hogan Award, Nicklaus Award, and Haskins Award. The 2026 NCAA champion. He left college as the clear No. 1 amateur in the country.

He was the fourth rookie winner of the 2026 PGA Tour season. He's historically one of the fastest players ever to win after turning professional.

This week he could play the Rocket Classic. Then the Wyndham. Then the FedExCup Playoffs in back-to-back-to-back weeks.

A 21-year-old who turned pro six weeks ago playing in the FedExCup Playoffs. That's where this is.
