From Third Start to FedExCup Playoffs — Jackson Koivun's Historic Summer Just Got More Historic

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | July 29, 2026

Jackson Koivun won the 3M Open in his third professional start. The $1,584,000 prize and 500 FedExCup points that came with it did something neither he nor anyone around him probably planned for: they sent a 21-year-old who turned pro six weeks ago to the FedExCup Playoffs.

Jackson Koivun turned professional approximately 24 days before the 3M Open began. He played two events first. He made the cut in one of them. Then he went to TPC Twin Cities and won by three shots over Scottie Scheffler, who closed with a 63 in the final round and it wasn't enough.

The win was immediately historic. Youngest American to win on the PGA Tour in 94 years. Second-fastest player in Tour history to win after turning professional. A 25-under-par tournament record at 259. The fourth rookie winner of the 2026 season.

The consequences of that win extend beyond Sunday evening at Minneapolis.

The FedExCup Math

Winning the 3M Open came with 500 FedExCup points and $1,584,000 in prize money. Those 500 points moved Koivun from outside the rankings entirely to inside the top 70 — the qualification cutoff for the FedEx St. Jude Championship, the first Playoffs event, running August 13–16 at TPC Southwind in Memphis.

He is going to the FedExCup Playoffs. A player who turned professional six weeks ago. A 21-year-old who had played two PGA Tour events before winning his third.

Depending on his position after the regular season ends — the Rocket Classic runs this week and the Wyndham Championship (August 6–9) is the final regular-season event — Koivun could also advance to the BMW Championship (top 50) or the Tour Championship at East Lake (top 30).

What He Left Behind

The college career Koivun walked away from in June was extraordinary. Three consecutive SEC Player of the Year awards — the first player to accomplish that in 60 years. Eleven career wins at Auburn. The Ben Hogan Award twice, the Nicklaus Award twice, the Haskins Award twice. He won the 2026 NCAA Championship before turning pro. He was the clear No. 1 amateur in the country when he left.

He left to turn pro. Twenty-four days later, he was a PGA Tour champion.

What Comes Next

The Rocket Classic at Detroit Golf Club starts Thursday (July 30). Koivun is eligible and may play. The Wyndham Championship follows August 6–9 in Greensboro. Then the FedExCup Playoffs begin.

The benchmark most are watching: how does a 21-year-old with three career starts perform in the pressure environment of the Playoffs, against the full depth of the Tour's top 70? The expectation, reasonably, is that the experience curve catches up eventually. What Koivun has demonstrated so far is that his timeline is not predictable.

He won in his third start. He earned a Playoffs berth in his first month as a professional. Whatever comes next for Jackson Koivun in 2026, it's already one of the most unusual rookie arcs the Tour has seen in a generation.
