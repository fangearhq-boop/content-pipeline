LIV Golf Lost £461.8 Million in One Year — And Bloomberg Says Bankruptcy Is Being Planned

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | July 29, 2026

LIV Golf Ltd's international entity lost £461.8 million in 2024 alone. The league's monthly burn rate is approximately $100 million. It spends $8.50 for every $1 earned. Saudi Arabia's PIF is pulling funding after this season. Bloomberg reports Chapter 11 bankruptcy groundwork is underway.

The numbers behind LIV Golf's collapse are now public, and they are staggering.

LIV Golf Ltd — the league's international operating entity — reported a loss of £461.8 million in 2024. One calendar year. At current exchange rates, that is approximately $600 million. The monthly burn rate across the organization is approximately $100 million. For every $1 in revenue LIV generates, it spends roughly $8.50.

Bloomberg reported this week that LIV Golf is laying the groundwork to file for Chapter 11 bankruptcy at the end of the 2026 season. The plan reportedly includes relocating LIV's headquarters to the United States to access US bankruptcy law and protections.

PIF's Exit

Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund announced in April 2026 that it will stop funding LIV Golf at the end of this season. Since LIV's first tournament in June 2022, PIF has invested more than $5 billion in the league. That funding pipeline closes when 2026 ends.

In May 2026, CNBC reported LIV was seeking between $250 million and $350 million from new investors. The league was preparing to take its pitch to potential backers. In June, CEO Scott O'Neil addressed the funding cliff directly, telling CNBC to "take PIF at their word" — a statement that offered little reassurance to observers watching LIV's calendar contract around it.

The Operational Reality

The cancellation of the Michigan event — LIV's season finale — is the most visible consequence of these numbers, but it is not the only one. LIV New Orleans was canceled earlier this season. The original 14-event schedule is now down to 12, with two events remaining whose viability O'Neil himself declined to guarantee.

The contrast with the league's founding ambition is sharp. LIV Golf launched in June 2022 with the explicit goal of disrupting professional golf's economic structure. It offered guaranteed contracts to players regardless of performance, built a team-based format, and drew some of the sport's highest-profile names with purses that exceeded what the PGA Tour offered. Three years later, the financial architecture that made that possible is no longer there.

What's Next

The end-of-2026 window is when Bloomberg's reported bankruptcy timeline would activate. Two events remain in this season's schedule. If LIV does file Chapter 11, the question becomes whether any version of the league emerges from restructuring — and what that version looks like without PIF's backing and without the financial guarantees that were the central recruitment tool.

For the players still on LIV rosters, the calendar and the bankruptcy timeline are the same question asked in different terms: how much of this season is left, and what comes after it.
