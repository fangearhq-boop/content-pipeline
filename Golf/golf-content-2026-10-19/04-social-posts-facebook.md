### STORY 1: Bermuda Championship Preview

**Long-Form Post**
The PGA Tour heads to Bermuda this week, and Port Royal Golf Course is a different kind of test.

The Butterfield Bermuda Championship runs October 22-25 at Port Royal in Southampton. At 6,828 yards and par 71, it is the shortest layout on the PGA Tour calendar. What makes it hard isn't the distance — it's the wind. Port Royal sits on the water and funnels Atlantic wind through fairways and across greens in ways that can completely change what a hole plays like from one round to the next.

The $6 million purse carries 500 FedExCup points. Adam Schenk won here last year at 12-under par. He's back this week as the defending champion.

The field of 120 also includes Austin Lemieux — son of hockey legend Mario Lemieux — who earned his spot at a Monday qualifier by shooting 66. He'll play as an amateur. The Bermuda Championship has been running since 2019, and it has never had a back-to-back champion. Every edition has produced a different winner.

Round 1 tees off Thursday. Golf Channel has coverage.

### STORY 2: Jon Rahm LIV Exit

**Long-Form Post**
Jon Rahm's exit from LIV Golf is now a filed legal document. The next stop is a New Jersey courtroom.

Rahm's separation agreement with LIV was filed in bankruptcy court around October 15. A judge is scheduled to hear the case on November 5. Under the agreement's terms, Rahm is released from all remaining performance obligations — he owes LIV nothing. He can sign with any tour, league, or sponsor without notifying or seeking permission from LIV. His name, image, and likeness rights are returned to him.

Rahm told the court through his attorney that he reviewed the proposed terms of LIV's restructured league and found them unacceptable. He walked away from what some reports estimated as more than $100 million in remaining contract guarantees.

The PGA Tour has said it will not hold conversations with any LIV player still legally under contract. That clock runs through November 5. Rahm is reportedly targeting the Abu Dhabi Championship, which runs November 5-8, as his first event back.

The process is not finished yet. But the path is clear.

### STORY 4: Schenk Defending

**Long-Form Post**
Adam Schenk won the 2025 Butterfield Bermuda Championship at 12-under par. He navigated Port Royal's wind-battered layout, scrambled when he had to, and made the putts that mattered.

Now he comes back as the defending champion.

Defending a title means facing the same course that elevated you, competing against a field that has studied how you won. At Port Royal, the challenge is consistent: the course is the same par-71 layout, the same wind can roll in off the Atlantic, and the same premium on scrambling and putting remains.

The Bermuda Championship has been running since 2019. In seven editions, no player has won it twice. Todd won in 2019. Gay won in 2020. Herbert in 2021, Power in 2022, Villegas in 2023, Campos in 2024, Schenk in 2025. Seven events, seven champions.

Schenk has a chance to become the first back-to-back winner in the tournament's history. Round 1 is Thursday.
