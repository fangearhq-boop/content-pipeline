Chacarra Wins in India, Takes Race to Dubai Lead After Back-to-Back Contention Weeks

He was one stroke back in Spain last Sunday. He won in India this Sunday. Eugenio Chacarra is now the No. 1 player in the Race to Dubai.

By Marcus Cole | October 19, 2026 | Golf Fanrecap

The Win

Eugenio Chacarra won the DP World India Championship at Delhi Golf Club on Sunday, October 18. The $4 million event was the second DP World Tour event of the fall stretch, and Chacarra held his form through the final round to claim the title and the Race to Dubai lead that came with it.

Tommy Fleetwood was the defending champion heading into the week. Patrick Reed held the Race to Dubai lead entering the event. Neither finished ahead of Chacarra. When the final round was over, the standings had shifted — Chacarra moved into the top spot in the Race to Dubai.

The Back-to-Back Run

What makes this win particularly notable is the week before it. At the Open de España, Chacarra finished runner-up — one stroke behind Grant Forrest. Runner-up finishes are easy to overanalyze. The natural question after a near-miss is whether a player is ready to close the deal or whether that final-round experience will be difficult to shake.

Chacarra answered it seven days later. He traveled from Spain to India, competed in a different time zone at a different course, and produced a winning performance. The two weeks together — runner-up, then champion — tell a story of a player who is competing near the top of his game consistently, not in isolated flashes.

Race to Dubai Implications

The Race to Dubai is the DP World Tour's season-long points competition, with the title awarded at the season's final event in Dubai. Heading into the India event, Patrick Reed led the standings with a margin of 166 points over Chacarra. That margin has been erased. Chacarra now sits at No. 1.

Reed had established his lead through consistent results earlier in the season. The India Championship reshuffled the top of the standings at a critical stage of the European calendar, with several events remaining before the Dubai season finale.

What This Means

For Chacarra, winning in India is both a points achievement and a momentum statement. He has won on tour before, but back-to-back weeks in contention — including a victory to close the stretch — is the kind of form that changes how a field views a player. He is no longer a name to watch. He is the name to beat right now.

The Race to Dubai has a long way left to run. Patrick Reed is still near the top of the standings. Other contenders will make their cases in the weeks ahead. But after this past Sunday in Delhi, the Race has a new leader, and his name is Eugenio Chacarra.
