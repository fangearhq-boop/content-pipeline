Austin Lemieux Makes His PGA Tour Debut at Bermuda — Mario's Son Earned His Spot

He left hockey. He picked up golf. He shot 66 at a Monday qualifier and took one of four available spots. Austin Lemieux is playing the PGA Tour this Thursday.

By Jake Torres | October 19, 2026 | Golf Fanrecap

Who He Is

Austin Lemieux is the son of Mario Lemieux — two-time Stanley Cup champion, 1992 Conn Smythe Trophy winner, Hockey Hall of Famer, Pittsburgh Penguins legend. Growing up with that name meant growing up with a specific kind of expectation attached to it. Austin played hockey. He spent time in the USHL and then two seasons at Arizona State University. He never signed an NHL contract. He retired from the sport in 2020.

Then he started playing golf seriously.

How He Got to Bermuda

Earning a spot in a PGA Tour field as a non-exempt player means competing at Monday qualifiers — one-round events held before each tournament where players compete for a limited number of spots in the main field. This past Monday, Austin Lemieux played a qualifier in Winter Haven, Florida, with 67 other players competing for four available spots.

He shot 66. That score finished tied for second behind PGA Tour winner Adam Long and was comfortably inside the qualifier's cutoff. Lemieux took one of the four spots. He will tee it up at Port Royal Golf Course in Southampton, Bermuda, this Thursday as an amateur — he is not turning professional for the event. His first PGA Tour start is official.

The Golf Resume

Lemieux has built a credible amateur record. Earlier this year, he won the 2026 WPGA Mid-Amateur Championship — the Western Pennsylvania Golf Association's title for amateur players 25 and older. On October 6, he won a Minor League Golf Tour event, edging Jake Springer in a playoff.

These are not marquee victories. The Minor League Golf Tour is a development circuit, and regional amateur titles are a long way from PGA Tour competition. But they represent a player who is competing at the highest amateur level available to him and winning. His 66 at the qualifier was not a fluke from someone with no business in the field. It came from a golfer who has been shooting those kinds of numbers.

What to Watch For

Port Royal Golf Course is the shortest layout on the PGA Tour, which theoretically reduces the distance gap between Lemieux and professionals who have a decade of tour experience. Wind remains the great equalizer. On a calm day, the scoring gaps between professional and amateur players become more apparent. On a windy one, everyone scrambles.

Lemieux will not be mistaken for a contender this week. He is a 23-year-old amateur making his first PGA Tour start on a course he has likely never played. The expectation is that he competes hard, learns what this level feels like, and hopefully makes the cut to play the weekend.

But he earned his way into this field on a Monday in Florida, one 66 at a time. He is there because he belongs there. And that's the whole story, really.
