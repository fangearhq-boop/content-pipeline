2026 Bermuda Championship: Port Royal Preview and What to Watch Thursday

Port Royal is the shortest course on the PGA Tour. Wind makes it one of the most demanding. The 2026 Butterfield Bermuda Championship starts Thursday.

By Ryan Calloway | October 19, 2026 | Golf Fanrecap

The Event

The Butterfield Bermuda Championship begins October 22 at Port Royal Golf Course in Southampton, Bermuda. It runs through Sunday the 25th. The purse is $6 million, with 500 FedExCup points going to the winner. The field holds 120 players. This is the seventh edition of the event, which launched in 2019.

Port Royal is a par-71 layout measuring 6,828 yards — the shortest course on the PGA Tour calendar. That number alone doesn't tell the full story. Port Royal sits exposed to Atlantic wind, and the conditions can shift so dramatically between rounds that what was a straightforward birdie hole on Thursday becomes a par-saving scramble on Saturday. Wind is not a secondary factor here. It is the primary one.

What the Course Rewards

Because Port Royal is short, players with strong iron games and precise approaches can attack it under calm conditions. But the wind equalizes much of that advantage. What consistently produces results here is the scrambling game — getting up and down from spots you weren't supposed to be, keeping the round alive when a gust pushes a second shot into trouble.

Putting matters at Port Royal too, particularly on the greens that slope toward the water. The statistical categories that tend to predict success here are approach play, around-the-green performance, and birdie rate on the shorter par 4s in the 350-400 yard range. A player who can take advantage of those holes while managing the longer ones in the wind has the profile to win.

The Defending Champion

Adam Schenk won the 2025 Butterfield Bermuda Championship at 12-under par. His victory was the product of consistent ball-striking and a reliable short game through the week's conditions at Port Royal. He returns this week as the defending champion.

No player has won this event in back-to-back years. The tournament's seven editions have produced seven different winners: Brendon Todd in 2019, Brian Gay in 2020, Lucas Herbert in 2021, Seamus Power in 2022, Camilo Villegas in 2023, Rafael Campos in 2024, and Schenk in 2025. Whether Schenk can become the first repeat winner here is one of the central questions of this week.

The Field Story

The Bermuda Championship tends to attract players who see a fall-event win as a realistic path to keeping their card or improving their standing for the following season. The 120-player field includes a range of PGA Tour veterans, conditional members, and Monday qualifiers.

This year's most notable qualifier is Austin Lemieux — son of hockey legend Mario Lemieux — who shot 66 at a Monday qualifier in Winter Haven, Florida to earn one of four available spots. He will play Port Royal as an amateur. It is his first PGA Tour start.

How to Watch

Round 1 begins Thursday, October 22. Golf Channel carries the tournament's coverage. The format is 72-hole stroke play, standard cut after 36 holes, with the final round on Sunday October 25.

Port Royal's exposure to wind means early morning and late afternoon conditions can differ significantly. Keep an eye on the leaderboard after each round — the scoring average here can swing several shots between calm and windy days. That unpredictability is what makes the Bermuda Championship one of the more entertaining fall events on the calendar.
