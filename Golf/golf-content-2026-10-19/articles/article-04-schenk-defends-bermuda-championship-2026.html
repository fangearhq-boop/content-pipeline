Schenk Returns to Port Royal: Can the Defending Champion Win Back-to-Back in Bermuda?

Adam Schenk won the Bermuda Championship last year at 12-under. He comes back this week as the first defending champion with a real chance to make history.

By Ryan Calloway | October 19, 2026 | Golf Fanrecap

The Defense

Adam Schenk won the 2025 Butterfield Bermuda Championship at Port Royal Golf Course with a 12-under par total. He navigated the wind-exposed layout through four rounds, leaned on his scrambling game when conditions pushed shots offline, and made the putts that defined his margin of victory.

Now he returns to the same course, the same Bermuda wind, and the same 120-player field with a target on his back. Defending champions at PGA Tour events carry both the psychological weight of expectation and the practical advantage of course knowledge. At Port Royal, that knowledge matters.

The History

The Butterfield Bermuda Championship has been played seven times, and it has produced seven different champions. Brendon Todd won in 2019 when the event was established. Brian Gay followed in 2020. Lucas Herbert won in 2021, Seamus Power in 2022, Camilo Villegas in 2023, Rafael Campos in 2024, and Schenk in 2025.

No player has won this tournament more than once. The reasons vary. Port Royal's conditions shift year to year depending on wind. The fall-event field includes motivated players looking to secure cards and status. The short course and relatively modest yardage mean that an amateur or a conditional tour member can compete with a proven winner when the wind is blowing hard enough.

Schenk has a chance to break the pattern. He is the only player who knows exactly what it takes to win here, because he has done it.

What Made Him Win in 2025

Schenk's 2025 victory at Port Royal was built on consistency rather than dominance. He did not separate himself from the field through power — the course doesn't reward that. He hit enough greens, got up and down when he missed them, and made a sufficient number of putts on greens that are complicated by slope and wind.

Port Royal rewards players who stay patient, avoid big numbers, and take the birdies the course offers without forcing ones that aren't there. Schenk's game fits those requirements. The question is whether that fit holds across four rounds in a defending role rather than as a contender approaching the course fresh.

This Week

Schenk tees it up Thursday in Round 1. He is the obvious story of the week at Port Royal — the returning champion on the same course, chasing history that no one in this event has ever reached. Whether he plays his way into contention through the weekend, or whether a different name emerges on the Bermuda leaderboard, will become clear soon enough.

Seven editions. Seven champions. One man has a chance to change that record starting Thursday.
