### STORY 1: LIV Golf Is Done

**Long-Form Post — 8:00 AM ET**
⛳ Five years. Billions of dollars. The world's best players. And it's over.

LIV Golf has officially abandoned its restructuring plan. At a November 5 bankruptcy hearing, the league's attorney told the court they were "winding down operations" rather than pressing forward with the planned LIV 2.0 relaunch. A formal liquidation plan was filed November 10, with a January hearing that would finalize the league's dissolution.

What happened? Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund pulled its support in April. The original Chapter 11 filing in September tried to save the league — but the restructuring plan needed a critical mass of players to sign on, and it never got there.

LIV Golf launched in 2022 with enormous ambitions: guaranteed contracts, massive prize money, and a team format designed to bring global golf to new audiences. It drew the sport's biggest names and forced the PGA Tour into one of its most uncomfortable periods. Whatever you thought of LIV Golf, it changed the sport. The PGA Tour had to respond. Players got paid more. The game was disrupted.

And now it's done. Where do Jon Rahm, Bryson DeChambeau, Dustin Johnson, and Cameron Smith go from here? That's the question golf is waking up to this week.

### STORY 2: PGA Tour on LIV Players

**Long-Form Post — 1:00 PM ET**
🏌️ The PGA Tour is not rolling out a welcome mat.

With LIV Golf heading for liquidation, the sport's most prominent defectors — Jon Rahm, Bryson DeChambeau, Dustin Johnson, Cameron Smith, and others — are suddenly without a home. And according to the PGA Tour's CEO, there is currently no program in place to welcome them back.

The Tour is waiting for LIV's bankruptcy proceedings to fully conclude before making any decisions. That is a reasonable legal posture — and also a way of sending a message.

The best available route back is through the DP World Tour. The top 10 players in the DP World Tour's season-long points standings who are not otherwise exempt earn PGA Tour cards for 2027. Patrick Reed, who left LIV earlier this year, is reportedly No. 2 on that list. That is the door that is open — but not every former LIV player will want to, or be able to, take it.

Professional golf's landscape is about to look very different in 2027. The realignment is just beginning.

### STORY 4: Novak Mexico Open Win

**Long-Form Post — 10:30 AM ET**
🏆 Andrew Novak is a PGA Tour champion — by five shots, at 25-under par.

Novak's win at the VidantaWorld Mexico Open in Vallarta on November 1 was dominant from start to finish. He closed at 25-under, edging out Steven Fisk and Kristoffer Ventura by five strokes to claim the $1,080,000 winner's share from the $6,000,000 purse.

This was his first individual PGA Tour title. He had previously won the 2025 Zurich Classic as part of a team format with Ben Griffin — but this was a solo statement. Novak now has his PGA Tour card secured through 2028 and enters a new phase of his career with the confidence that comes from knowing he can get the job done alone when the pressure is highest.

The fall season is turning into a compelling race, and Novak has made himself a central figure.

### STORY 5: LPGA Pace-of-Play Rules

**Long-Form Post — 6:00 PM ET**
⏱️ The LPGA Tour is getting serious about slow play — and it starts in 2027.

Under a new policy announced by the Tour, warning allowances are being eliminated from the pace-of-play framework. Starting next season, players who fall behind pace will be fined immediately — no first warning, no grace period.

The move is designed to speed up rounds and address a problem that has drawn increasing attention from fans and broadcasters. Long rounds have been a friction point in professional golf for years, and several tours have tried various approaches to address it. The LPGA's new policy is one of the most direct: skip the warnings and go straight to financial consequences.

Whether it changes behavior on course, we will find out in 2027. But the message is clear: slow play has a cost, and the LPGA is making sure everyone knows it.
