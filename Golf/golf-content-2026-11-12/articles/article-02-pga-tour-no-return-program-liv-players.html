PGA Tour CEO: No Plans to Welcome Back LIV Players as Golf's Future Realigns

By Jake Torres | November 12, 2026

With LIV Golf entering its wind-down phase, the PGA Tour's CEO has confirmed there is no current program to welcome back the players who defected, leaving Jon Rahm, Bryson DeChambeau, Dustin Johnson, and others facing an uncertain future in professional golf.

The PGA Tour Is Not Opening the Door

As LIV Golf heads toward liquidation, the sport's most prominent defectors are searching for answers. The PGA Tour's CEO has provided a clear one — for now, the door is not open.

In comments reported by Bloomberg Law, the Tour's leadership confirmed there are no current plans to establish a returning-member program for LIV players. The Tour has said it will wait for the LIV bankruptcy proceedings to fully conclude before making any formal decisions about pathways back — a position that is both legally cautious and strategically firm.

This is not an unexpected stance. When LIV Golf signed players to lucrative guaranteed contracts, it asked them to give up their PGA Tour memberships — in some cases their entire competitive base. The Tour was locked in legal conflict with LIV for much of the past three years, and the idea that it would immediately roll out a welcome mat the moment LIV folded was always unlikely.

The DP World Tour Pathway

The most viable route back to the PGA Tour for former LIV players runs through the DP World Tour. The European-based circuit, which has a co-sanctioned relationship with the PGA Tour, awards cards to the top 10 players in its season-long Race to Dubai standings who are not otherwise exempt. That pathway is open — but it is not without complications.

Patrick Reed, who made the move from LIV to the DP World Tour earlier this year, is reportedly No. 2 in the Race to Dubai. If he maintains that position, Reed would earn a PGA Tour card for 2027. His path illustrates what is possible — but also how narrow the window is for players who left later in the year with limited DP World Tour events to play.

For Rahm, DeChambeau, Johnson, and others who are higher-profile but further from the DP World Tour's events, the calculus is more complicated. They may not have enough rounds on that tour to build meaningful standings positions. Alternative pathways — sponsor exemptions, Monday qualifiers, international tour events that feed PGA Tour status — exist but represent significant reductions in competitive standing from what these players experienced at the peak of their careers.

Brooks Koepka's Return as a Template

The most prominent LIV-to-PGA-Tour return to date is Brooks Koepka, who returned under a January program that required him to accept a five-year forfeiture of potential equity in the PGA Tour's Player Equity Program. That was a significant financial concession — and a signal about how the Tour plans to handle these situations. Returning players will not receive the same treatment as those who stayed, and the financial cost of the return is meant to be meaningful.

Whether the Tour softens that position in light of LIV's total collapse, or holds firm as a matter of principle, is one of the defining questions facing professional golf heading into 2027.

What's Next

The January 7 LIV Golf liquidation hearing will mark the formal end of the league. What follows will be a complex negotiation — legal, commercial, and human — involving some of the most recognizable names in world golf seeking to rebuild competitive careers in a landscape they helped reshape, and which now awaits their return on its own terms.

The PGA Tour holds most of the cards. For now, they are not showing their hand.
