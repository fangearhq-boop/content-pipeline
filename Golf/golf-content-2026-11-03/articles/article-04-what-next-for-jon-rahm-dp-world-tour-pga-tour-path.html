What's Next for Jon Rahm? A Path From LIV to the DP World Tour — and Eventually the PGA Tour

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | November 3, 2026

Jon Rahm gave up $100 million. Now the question is: what does he do next?

The two-time major champion confirmed his exit from LIV Golf in early October 2026, with his attorney telling a bankruptcy court that Rahm had reviewed LIV's restructured 2027 terms and found them "unacceptable." It was a stunning moment — a player walking away from nine figures rather than continuing with a league he no longer believed in. Now, Rahm faces the task of rebuilding his competitive career outside the LIV ecosystem.

The Most Likely Landing Spot: DP World Tour

Multiple reports indicate that Jon Rahm is likely to compete on the DP World Tour in 2027. The European-based circuit has always been at the core of Rahm's golfing identity. Born in Barrika in the Basque Country of Spain, Rahm turned professional through the DP World Tour pathway and built his early career on the circuit. He is one of Europe's most beloved players and a cornerstone of the Ryder Cup team — a competition that has defined some of the most memorable moments of his career.

The DP World Tour, for its part, would welcome Rahm back with open arms. LIV Golf's collapse has already begun returning players to European golf, and Rahm's name would bring an enormous boost of credibility, coverage, and competitive quality to the tour's 2027 season.

The PGA Tour Timeline

A return to the PGA Tour — where Rahm won the 2021 U.S. Open at Torrey Pines and the 2023 Masters at Augusta National — is more complicated. Players who participated in LIV Golf typically face a one-year waiting period from their final LIV event before regaining PGA Tour eligibility. Given that Rahm's participation in LIV Golf continued through the 2026 season before the bankruptcy voided his contract, his earliest potential PGA Tour return would be sometime in 2028.

The waiting period is not automatic — it depends on how the PGA Tour interprets the rules in the context of LIV's bankruptcy and the involuntary nature of Rahm's exit. There is a reasonable argument that players whose contracts were voided by bankruptcy rather than voluntarily terminated should be treated differently. Whether the PGA Tour pursues that distinction remains to be seen.

The Major Question

For Rahm, the most pressing competitive concern is not tour affiliation — it is majors. The four major championships each have their own qualification criteria, and a player of Rahm's world ranking and stature will find ways to qualify regardless of which tour he calls home. Augusta National, in particular, invites past champions to the Masters with no restrictions — meaning Rahm, as a 2023 Masters champion, has a permanent invitation to the event that represents his greatest triumph.

Rahm is 31. His best years are arguably still ahead of him. The competitive fire that made him one of the world's best players before LIV has not dimmed — and the desire to add to his major haul, represent Europe at the Ryder Cup, and reclaim his standing atop world golf will drive every decision he makes in 2027 and beyond.

A Second Chapter

In a sense, Rahm's story mirrors a larger narrative playing out across professional golf. Players who left for LIV are now navigating the consequences of a league that did not survive. Some will face long roads back; others, like Rahm, have the talent and the profile to rebuild quickly.

Giving up $100 million is an extraordinary act. But it also represents a clear-eyed assessment: the LIV that Rahm signed up for no longer exists, and the restructured version isn't worth staying for. The next chapter — wherever it takes him — starts now.

What's Next

Jon Rahm is expected to formally announce his 2027 plans in the coming weeks. A DP World Tour membership announcement before the new season begins would allow him to participate in early 2027 events and rebuild his competitive ranking ahead of the major season. His situation regarding PGA Tour reinstatement will likely be clarified by the tour before the end of the year.
