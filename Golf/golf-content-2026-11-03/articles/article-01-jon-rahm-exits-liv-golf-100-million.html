Jon Rahm Exits LIV Golf, Walks Away From $100 Million Over 'Unacceptable' Terms

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | November 3, 2026

Jon Rahm is done with LIV Golf — and he is walking away from approximately $100 million to prove it.

The two-time major champion's attorney confirmed to a bankruptcy court that Rahm had reviewed the terms of LIV Golf's planned 2027 relaunch and found them "unacceptable." The announcement, which came in early October 2026, ends Rahm's association with the Saudi-backed breakaway tour and delivers what may be a fatal blow to LIV's hopes of reemerging as a credible competitive force.

The Terms He Rejected

LIV Golf filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection on September 8, 2026, with the league listing more than $500 million in debt. The bankruptcy filing voided player contracts, leaving members free to negotiate their futures. LIV subsequently reached a restructuring deal with investment fund BC Partners and unveiled plans to relaunch in 2027 as a smaller 75-player tour with a player-majority ownership structure — a significant scaling back from its original format.

Rahm's attorney, John Beck, told the court his client found those revised terms unacceptable. The restructured league, with its smaller fields and shift away from team golf, apparently did not meet the standard Rahm required to continue his involvement.

By walking away, Rahm is forfeiting approximately $100 million still owed to him by the tour under the terms of his original contract — a stunning sum that underscores just how committed he was to the original LIV vision, and how thoroughly that vision has collapsed.

A Polarizing Chapter

When Rahm left the PGA Tour in late 2023, his signing was one of the most seismic moments in LIV Golf's brief and turbulent history. A two-time major champion — winner of the 2021 U.S. Open at Torrey Pines and the 2023 Masters at Augusta National — Rahm represented the kind of elite, active talent that LIV had struggled to attract. His departure from the PGA Tour sent a message that LIV was no longer just a haven for aging stars; it was a serious competitive destination.

The reaction was fierce. Many in the golf world were critical of Rahm's decision, viewing it as a betrayal of the sport's established structures. Others saw it as a rational response to an extraordinary financial offer. What few could have anticipated was that less than three years later, the league would be insolvent and its biggest star would be searching for a new home.

What Comes Next

Multiple reports indicate that Rahm's most likely destination in 2027 is the DP World Tour, the European-based circuit that served as his professional home before LIV Golf. The DP World Tour has always been central to Rahm's identity as a golfer — born in Barrika, Spain, he won multiple times on the tour and famously secured a Ryder Cup spot representing Europe.

A return to the PGA Tour — where Rahm won both of his major championships — is a more complex proposition. Players who competed on LIV Golf typically face a one-year waiting period from their final LIV event before regaining PGA Tour eligibility. Given that Rahm's LIV participation continued through the 2026 season, his earliest potential PGA Tour return would be sometime in 2028.

For Rahm, now 31, the path back to the biggest stages in golf is long but navigable. The Masters, the U.S. Open, The Open Championship, and the PGA Championship all have their own qualification criteria — and a world-ranked player of Rahm's calibre is unlikely to miss many of them regardless of his tour affiliation.

LIV's Biggest Problem

For LIV Golf's organizers, Rahm's departure is devastating. He was the single biggest name associated with the restructured tour — a credibility anchor for a league trying to convince players, sponsors, and broadcasters that LIV 2.0 was worth backing. Without him, the 75-player field becomes a far harder sell.

Bryson DeChambeau, who alongside Rahm was listed as one of the highest unsecured creditors in the bankruptcy filing, has not yet publicly confirmed his own plans. His decision — and those of other notable LIV players — will go a long way toward determining whether the 2027 relaunch ever happens.

The era of unlimited Saudi spending on professional golf is over. What comes next for Rahm, for LIV, and for the sport itself is the defining story of golf heading into 2027.

What's Next

Jon Rahm is expected to confirm his 2027 schedule in the coming weeks. The DP World Tour's new season begins in early 2027, and Rahm would likely seek to participate from the start to rebuild ranking points and competitive momentum. His path back to the PGA Tour, potentially in 2028, will depend on how quickly the dust settles on LIV's bankruptcy proceedings and how the tour interprets the waiting period rules for LIV participants.
