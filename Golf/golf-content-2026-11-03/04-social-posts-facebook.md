### STORY 2: LIV Golf's Collapse

**FB Long-Form Post 2** | Posting Window: 10:30 AM ET
The Saudi-backed experiment that reshaped professional golf is in bankruptcy court.

LIV Golf filed for Chapter 11 protection on September 8, 2026, revealing more than $500 million in debt. Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, which had bankrolled the league to the tune of more than $5 billion over five years, decided not to continue its financial support beyond the 2026 season.

What happens next? LIV has secured a restructuring deal with investment fund BC Partners and says it plans to re-emerge in 2027 as a smaller, 75-player tour with a player-majority ownership structure. But with Jon Rahm — its biggest star — already announcing his departure, and player contracts having been voided by the bankruptcy filing, the road to 2027 is long and uncertain.

### STORY 4: What's Next for Jon Rahm?

**FB Long-Form Post 4** | Posting Window: 3:30 PM ET
Jon Rahm gave up $100 million. Now the question is: what comes next?

Reports from multiple sources suggest Rahm will compete on the DP World Tour in 2027 — a natural landing spot given his European roots and the fact that the DP World Tour has always been his professional home before LIV Golf.

The PGA Tour, where Rahm won the 2021 U.S. Open and the 2023 Masters, is a different proposition. Players who left for LIV Golf typically face a one-year waiting period from their last LIV event before returning to the PGA Tour. That timeline would put Rahm's earliest possible PGA Tour return at 2028.

For a player of Rahm's calibre, the competitive desire to be back on the biggest stages — the majors, the Ryder Cup — will drive every decision. The path is long, but it's there.
