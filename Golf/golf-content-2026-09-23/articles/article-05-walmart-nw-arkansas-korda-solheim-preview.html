Walmart NW Arkansas Championship Preview: Korda Returns as the World's Best Player

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | September 23, 2026

Nelly Korda arrives in Rogers, Arkansas this week as the world's No. 1 player, the holder of two 2026 major titles, and the only member of the US Solheim Cup team who went through last weekend without losing a match. She returns to LPGA Tour competition at the Walmart NW Arkansas Championship — $3 million purse, 54-hole stroke play at Pinnacle Country Club, beginning Thursday September 25 — as the most accomplished women's golfer in the world. She is also walking in from a team loss.

The United States lost the 2026 Solheim Cup 13-15 to Europe. Korda contributed four points individually. She went 4-0 overall, including a 4&2 win in Sunday singles against Europe's Mimi Rhodes. The team around her was not as sharp, and Europe played a stronger collective match. Korda did her job. The result was still a defeat.

Korda's 2026 Season

By any measurement, Nelly Korda is having a historically significant year. She won the 2026 Chevron Championship, the LPGA Tour's first Major of the season. She followed it with victory at the 2026 US Women's Open, making her a two-Major winner in a single year at 27 years old. The Rolex Rankings have her at No. 1 in the world, a position she has defended across a season that has not allowed her much time to breathe.

The Solheim Cup interrupted the LPGA Tour's regular schedule last weekend. Korda competed — and she was exceptional at the individual level. But team results carry weight that individual stats don't, and the American loss will sit with every member of that Solheim team until the next edition of the event.

In Rogers, Korda gets to play for herself again. Stroke play, her own scorecard, her own game against the field. The format suits her.

The Post-Solheim Context

Seventeen players from the US Solheim Cup team are in the field at Pinnacle Country Club this week. For all of them, this is the first LPGA Tour event since the Solheim Cup ended, and the task is redirection — channelling the competitive energy from a team format back into individual stroke play.

Europe's players are largely competing on the LET (Ladies European Tour) schedule this week, making the NW Arkansas field more American in composition than a typical LPGA event. But the international players in the field — including several who were at the Solheim — provide genuine competition. A 54-hole tournament at this level does not offer much margin for error.

Pinnacle Country Club has hosted the Walmart NW Arkansas Championship for years. The course is known for its receptive greens and the scoring opportunities it presents to players with strong iron play — a profile that suits Korda, whose ball-striking remains among the tour's most consistent.

What's Next

The first round at Pinnacle Country Club begins Thursday, September 25. With 54 holes and no cut, every player in the field competes through Sunday. Korda enters as the favourite by ranking and recent form. Whether she closes out NW Arkansas with a third title of the season — or whether one of the other Solheim returnees redirects their energy into a breakthrough — is the story the scoreboard will write starting Thursday. The final round is Sunday, September 27.
