Presidents Cup Eve: The International Team's 28-Year Wait Comes Down to Tomorrow at Medinah

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | September 23, 2026

Tomorrow morning at Medinah Country Club, the 2026 Presidents Cup begins. The Opening Ceremony is scheduled for 11:05 AM CDT, and the first of five fourball matches tees off at 11:35 AM. By the time the sun goes down Thursday, the International team will either have broken one of the most lopsided streaks in professional golf — or extended it to yet another year.

The United States leads the all-time Presidents Cup series 13-2. The International team has not won this competition since 1998, when they defeated the Americans 20.5-11.5 at Royal Melbourne. That was 28 years ago. Twelve consecutive defeats have followed. The drought is longer than some of the players competing tomorrow have been alive.

The Teams

US Captain Brandt Snedeker will reveal his Day 1 pairings tonight. His squad represents the depth of the current PGA Tour — a roster of players who have spent the past year competing at the highest level of the game. The Americans arrive as heavy favorites, as they have in almost every Presidents Cup of the modern era.

International Captain Geoff Ogilvy — the 2006 US Open champion — has assembled what many observers consider one of the most talented International rosters in the event's history. Hideki Matsuyama, the 2021 Masters champion, anchors the lineup. Tom Kim brings the kind of electric ball-striking that can win holes on any given shot. Min Woo Lee, Adam Scott, Corey Conners, Sungjae Im, and Ryan Fox round out a squad with genuine depth from first pair to last.

Ogilvy has not oversold what this team can do. He has, instead, made clear that this group believes it can win — and that belief, at a venue like Medinah, is not nothing.

Why Medinah Matters

In 2012, Medinah Country Club hosted the Ryder Cup. Europe arrived for Sunday's singles session trailing 10-6 — needing a near-sweep to retain the trophy they had won in Wales two years earlier. What followed became one of the defining days in the sport's history. Europe won 8.5 of the available 12 points in singles to complete a comeback that seemed impossible before it happened.

The analogy is not perfect. The Presidents Cup and the Ryder Cup are different events with different formats and different competitive histories. But the venue carries meaning. Ogilvy knows the history. His players know the history. And the knowledge that Medinah has already hosted one impossible comeback gives the International team something to hold onto as they prepare to try for another.

The Format Thursday

The first session at every Presidents Cup is fourball — best-ball match play, where each player plays their own ball and the team scores the better of the two. Five matches over the course of Thursday's morning and afternoon wave will give each side an initial read on where the points are coming from. The pairings Snedeker and Ogilvy choose for Thursday will signal their strategies for the week.

The US typically looks to establish an early lead and let the weight of history do the rest. The International team's path to winning almost always runs through taking the first session — a fast start that puts pressure on the Americans rather than the other way around.

What's Next

Pairings from both captains land tonight. The Opening Ceremony at 11:05 AM CDT Thursday sets the stage. The first fourball matches begin at 11:35 AM. By Thursday evening, there will be a score on the board — and the International team will either be chasing history or making it. After 28 years and 12 consecutive defeats, the wait ends one way or another starting tomorrow at Medinah.
