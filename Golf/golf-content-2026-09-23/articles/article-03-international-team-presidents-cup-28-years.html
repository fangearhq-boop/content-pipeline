The International Team's 28-Year Wait: Who They Are and Why Tomorrow Is Different

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | September 23, 2026

The last time the International team won the Presidents Cup was 1998 at Royal Melbourne. The score was 20.5-11.5. The winning margin was nine full points. It was, at the time, a convincing statement that the rest of the world could compete with the United States at the highest level of team golf.

Since then: twelve consecutive defeats. The series record stands at 13-2 in the Americans' favour. Some of the players on tomorrow's International roster were children when Melbourne happened. The drought is not just a statistic. It is the defining condition of this event for an entire generation of non-American professional golfers.

The 2026 Roster

Captain Geoff Ogilvy — the 2006 US Open champion at Winged Foot — has built what many observers consider the most competitive International squad the Presidents Cup has seen in years. The depth is genuine from first pair through last.

Hideki Matsuyama is the anchor. The Japanese star won the 2021 Masters at Augusta National, becoming the first Japanese player to win a Major championship. He is 30 years old, near the peak of his powers, and has been one of the best ball-strikers on the planet for a decade. His presence alone changes how the US team has to think about every session.

Tom Kim is one of the most exciting young players in the world — a South Korean with the kind of raw talent and competitive composure that wins matches in team formats. Min Woo Lee, the Western Australian whose short game is among the tour's best, adds flair and precision. Adam Scott, the 2013 Masters champion, provides veteran leadership and a proven big-game temperament.

Corey Conners, Sungjae Im, and Ryan Fox round out a roster without a genuine weak pairing in the lineup. On paper, the 2026 International team compares favourably to any squad the event has produced.

Why Paper Has Not Been Enough

The International team has had talented rosters before. They had Adam Scott in his prime. They had K.J. Choi. They had Vijay Singh. The format of team match play, where individual rankings matter less than chemistry, pairing strategy, and momentum, has consistently favoured the United States — a team that plays the PGA Tour together every week and arrives at the Presidents Cup with a shared competitive context that the International team, drawn from multiple tours across multiple continents, cannot replicate as easily.

Ogilvy has acknowledged this. His preparation has focused not just on player selection but on building the chemistry that makes a team function under pressure. The week of practice before competition begins is, in his words, as important as the names on the starting list.

Medinah as a Variable

Venue matters in match play. Medinah Country Club is not neutral ground — it sits outside Chicago, a US city, in front of a partisan American crowd. But it is a course with its own history as a site of remarkable reversals.

The 2012 Ryder Cup at Medinah produced one of golf's most improbable results. Europe trailed 10-6 entering Sunday singles and won 8.5 of the available 12 points to retain the trophy. The crowd was silenced. The outcome that seemed certain reversed in the span of an afternoon. Ogilvy and his players know that Medinah has already been the site of a team golf miracle. They intend to write the next one.

What's Next

Five fourball matches begin at 11:35 AM CDT Thursday. The International team's history in this event says they will lose. Their roster says they have the tools to win. How those two things resolve over the next four days of competition is the story of the 2026 Presidents Cup. The 28-year wait ends one way or another starting at Medinah on Thursday morning.
