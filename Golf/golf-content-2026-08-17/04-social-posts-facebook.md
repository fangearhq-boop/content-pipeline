### STORY 1: BMW Championship Preview

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 8:00 AM ET

The FedExCup Playoffs are entering crunch time. Three days from now, 50 of the world's best golfers tee it up at Bellerive Country Club in St. Louis for the BMW Championship — and only 30 of them will get to play the following week at the Tour Championship.

This is the moment that separates the season from the championship.

Scottie Scheffler arrives as the top seed, carrying his massive regular season points lead into a venue that should suit his game well. Bellerive is a par 70 layout at 7,448 yards — a long, demanding test that rewards precisely the qualities Scheffler has displayed all season: elite ball-striking, accurate driving, and mental composure under pressure.

But the storyline of the week may belong to Sam Burns. The Louisiana native won the FedEx St. Jude Championship last week at TPC Southwind, rallying from a difficult situation (Scheffler's regular caddie left for a family emergency, opening the door) to claim one of the most emotional playoff victories in recent memory. Burns arrives at Bellerive with momentum, confidence, and the validation of a playoff win.

Rory McIlroy, Xander Schauffele, Cameron Young, Collin Morikawa, and Hideki Matsuyama are among the marquee names rounding out the field. The $20 million purse makes this one of the richest events on the PGA Tour calendar, and the stakes of every shot are amplified by the knowledge that 20 players will leave St. Louis with their season effectively over.

Bellerive last hosted a major when Brooks Koepka won the 2018 PGA Championship with a 264 total. This week's BMW field might not match the depth of a major, but the pressure and intensity of the FedExCup format creates its own unmistakable drama.

August 20-23. The countdown is on.

### STORY 2: CPKC Women's Open — Korda and Henderson in Edmonton

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 10:30 AM ET

The biggest women's golf event in Canada is almost here — and it's arriving with a field worthy of its $2.75 million purse and its stunning venue.

The 52nd CPKC Women's Open opens this week (August 19-23) at Royal Mayfair Golf Club in Edmonton, Alberta. Royal Mayfair is a Stanley Thompson design from 1922, routed through the North Saskatchewan River Valley with mature trees lining its fairways and demanding precision on every approach. It's the third time Royal Mayfair has welcomed the championship, and the setting — nestled in Edmonton's river valley — makes for some of the most visually striking golf on the LPGA Tour calendar.

The headliner is world No. 1 Nelly Korda, who has been dominant in 2026. Korda has three major championship victories this season, including the Chevron Championship in April. She arrives in Edmonton as the clear favorite — but women's golf has a way of reminding favorites why the scoreboard matters more than rankings.

Defending champion Brooke Henderson is the other name everyone in Canadian golf will be watching. Henderson won at Mississaugua last year to claim her 14th LPGA title, and she'll play in front of a passionate Canadian crowd — one of 15 Canadian players in the field — with the opportunity to do something few golfers manage: defend a national title at home.

Past champions at the CPKC Women's Open include LPGA legend Lorena Ochoa and a young Lydia Ko, who won in 2013 at just 15 years old at Royal Mayfair. The history of this event is rich, and this year's edition adds another layer with Korda's dominant season as the backdrop.

Who will lift the trophy in Edmonton?

### STORY 3: Rory McIlroy's BMW Redemption

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 1:00 PM ET

Rory McIlroy has had better weeks. A lot better.

The three-time FedExCup champion finished 66th at the FedEx St. Jude Championship last week — his worst result in recent memory at a playoff event. He finished 8-over par across four rounds without shooting a single sub-par score. For a player who has won this competition three times and who carried genuine championship ambitions into Memphis, it was a stunning result.

But McIlroy isn't panicking. Golf Channel reported he "finds encouragement" in the week despite the disappointing result, and that his focus has immediately turned to Bellerive. He's right to look ahead.

Bellerive Country Club sets up as a much better venue for McIlroy's game than TPC Southwind. The long, demanding par-70 layout at 7,448 yards rewards exactly the qualities that have made McIlroy one of the most feared players in the world: power off the tee, elite mid-to-long iron play, and the ability to attack par 4s and 5s with aggressive, controlled ball flights. This is a course where better ball-striking wins — and McIlroy is one of the best ball-strikers on the planet when he's dialed in.

The last time the PGA Tour visited Bellerive was the 2018 PGA Championship, where Brooks Koepka shot a 72-hole total of 264 — one of the lowest scores in major championship history. That record-setting performance came on the same course McIlroy will walk this week.

Notably, Koepka — the defending champion from that 2018 event — failed to qualify for the 2026 FedEx Cup Playoffs entirely. That context is a reminder of how quickly fortunes can change in professional golf, and why McIlroy, despite last week's stumble, remains one of the most dangerous players in the BMW field.

Redemption starts Thursday.

### STORY 4: LIV Golf Indianapolis — Team Championship at Pete Dye's Final Course

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 3:30 PM ET

LIV Golf returns to The Club at Chatham Hills in Westfield, Indiana this week — and it's a venue with a genuinely compelling piece of golf history attached to it.

The course at Chatham Hills was designed by Pete Dye, the legendary architect behind some of the most iconic and demanding layouts in the game (Sawgrass, Whistling Straits, Harbour Town). It was also Pete Dye's final course design — a fitting last chapter from one of the sport's greatest architectural minds.

Jon Rahm has already clinched his third consecutive LIV Golf Individual Championship. He enters Indianapolis with 944.54 points and a 218-point margin over the field — a dominant performance that has established him as the undisputed individual star of LIV's 2026 season. With the individual title already secured, the team championship storyline takes center stage at Chatham Hills.

Beyond the competitive action, Chatham Hills promises a festival atmosphere. Thomas Rhett performs on Friday night. Disco Lines brings a high-energy set on Saturday. LIV Golf's signature combination of competitive golf and concert entertainment makes Indianapolis one of the circuit's most-attended events.

The Club at Chatham Hills has been voted Best Golf Course in Indiana — and Pete Dye's fingerprints are everywhere, from the signature railroad ties to the strategic risk-reward design that makes every hole a decision.

August 21-23. Western Indiana. Golf's alternative circuit puts on its season finale.

### STORY 5: Jordan Spieth at BMW Championship — 15 Straight and Still Fighting

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 6:00 PM ET

Jordan Spieth almost didn't make it to Bellerive. Almost.

The three-time major champion entered the FedEx St. Jude Championship ranked 54th in the FedExCup standings — outside the top 50 that qualify for the BMW Championship. He needed a strong performance in Memphis just to keep the dream alive, and he delivered: a bogey-free 65 in the opening round helped push him to inside the top 50 and secure his 15th consecutive BMW Championship berth.

Fifteen straight. Think about what that streak represents. Spieth has qualified for the second round of the FedExCup Playoffs every year since 2012. Through injury, through stretches of inconsistency, through the kind of putting struggles that would have ended lesser players' careers, he has always found a way to be standing at the BMW tee in August.

This year has been different. He's winless in 2026 — his least productive season in years. He came into the playoffs without a top-10 finish all season. And yet here he is, at Bellerive Country Club, with one of the most intriguing opportunity structures in golf: 50 players walk in, 30 advance to East Lake, and the points are reset so dramatically that anyone in the top 30 has a mathematical shot at the FedExCup title.

For Spieth, the Tour Championship at East Lake is more than a destination. It's validation — proof that despite the difficult year, he still belongs in the conversation with the game's best. His first FedExCup title came in 2015, and he has been chasing a second ever since.

BMW Championship. Three days. One question: can Jordan Spieth remind the world why he's still one of golf's most compelling figures?
