CPKC Women's Open: Nelly Korda Meets Brooke Henderson at Royal Mayfair

By Jake Torres | August 17, 2026

The biggest women's golf event in Canada opens Wednesday at one of the country's most historic courses — and the matchup between the world's best player and the defending champion makes it must-watch television.

The 52nd CPKC Women's Open begins August 19 at Royal Mayfair Golf Club in Edmonton, Alberta — and the field assembled for this week's $2.75 million event is as compelling as any the LPGA Tour has to offer. World No. 1 Nelly Korda leads the way. Defending champion Brooke Henderson returns to defend a title she won in front of a passionate Canadian crowd. Fifteen Canadian players make up a contingent that gives the week its distinctive national character.

This is the biggest week in Canadian women's golf, and it has the talent to match the occasion.

Korda's 2026 Season Has Been Extraordinary

Nelly Korda arrived in Edmonton as the clear favorite and the story of the LPGA Tour's 2026 season. She has won three major championships this year, including the Chevron Championship in April. She is the world's No. 1 ranked player by a significant margin. She has played with a consistency and composure this season that has made the rest of the field look like they're playing a different sport.

And yet women's golf has a way of humbling even the most dominant players. The LPGA Tour's depth is real. On any given week, 20 players can win. Korda knows that better than anyone — she has been on both sides of upsets before. She arrives in Edmonton as the favorite, but she arrives knowing the scoreboard on Sunday is the only thing that matters.

Royal Mayfair is a par 70 layout at 6,452 yards, designed by the legendary Stanley Thompson in 1922 and routed through the North Saskatchewan River Valley. Its mature tree lines and precision approach demands will test every player in the field, including Korda. Patience and iron control will be rewarded here. Wild swings from birdie to bogey will be punished.

Henderson Has a Nation Behind Her

Brooke Henderson won the CPKC Women's Open last year at Mississaugua to claim her 14th LPGA title. Returning to defend that championship, on Canadian soil, in front of a crowd that considers her a national treasure — that is a narrative the sport of golf rarely produces this cleanly.

Henderson anchors the 15-player Canadian contingent competing this week, and the energy surrounding her tee times at Royal Mayfair will be unlike anything most of the field has experienced. Canadian fans understand the history here. They understand what Henderson's victories mean. They show up in force, and they make noise.

The pressure that creates is real. But so is the advantage. Henderson has played in front of Canadian galleries her entire career. She knows how to use the energy rather than be consumed by it.

A Course Rich in History

Royal Mayfair is hosting the CPKC Women's Open for the third time in the championship's history. The Stanley Thompson design has aged gracefully — its river valley setting gives it a visual distinction that stands out on the LPGA Tour calendar, and its routing through mature trees creates the kind of strategic golf that rewards thinking players over power hitters.

Lydia Ko won at Royal Mayfair in 2013 at just 15 years old, becoming one of the youngest major winners in the sport's history. Lorena Ochoa, the dominant force in women's golf during her era, is also among the past champions at this event. The history of the CPKC Women's Open — and of Royal Mayfair specifically — provides a rich backdrop for this week's competition.

What's Next

The CPKC Women's Open runs August 19–23 at Royal Mayfair Golf Club in Edmonton, Alberta. Pro-am and practice rounds continue through Tuesday. Competitive rounds begin Wednesday. With Korda gunning for a fourth LPGA win of 2026 and Henderson fighting to do something few players in any sport manage — defend a national title at home — the week promises to deliver exactly the kind of storylines the LPGA Tour does best.
