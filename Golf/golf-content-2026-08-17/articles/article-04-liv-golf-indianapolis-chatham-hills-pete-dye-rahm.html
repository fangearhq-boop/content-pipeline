LIV Golf Indianapolis: Jon Rahm, Pete Dye's Final Course, and the Team Title Still Up for Grabs

By Ryan Calloway | August 17, 2026

The LIV Golf circuit heads to Westfield, Indiana for its season-closing team championship event — on a course that carries one of the most significant labels in golf architecture history.

The Club at Chatham Hills in Westfield, Indiana sits 25 miles north of Indianapolis, surrounded by rolling farmland and country roads that seem far removed from professional golf's traditional center of gravity. But this week, it is exactly where golf's alternative circuit wants to be — and the venue it has chosen carries a piece of history that no other course on the LIV Golf schedule can claim.

The course at Chatham Hills was designed by Pete Dye. It was Pete Dye's final course design before his death in January 2020.

Pete Dye's Last Chapter

Pete Dye spent more than five decades reshaping what golf courses could be. He designed Sawgrass — the TPC course that hosts THE PLAYERS Championship and its iconic island green 17th hole. He designed Whistling Straits, host of multiple major championships. He designed Harbour Town Golf Links. He designed Crooked Stick. He designed Kiawah Island's Ocean Course, host of the 1991 Ryder Cup and two PGA Championships.

His signature was everywhere at Chatham Hills: railroad ties framing bunkers and water hazards, risk-reward decision points on nearly every hole, greens that punish indecision and reward precision. The Club at Chatham Hills has been voted Best Golf Course in Indiana by golfers who know it well. Dye built it to last, and he built it to challenge.

Walking Chatham Hills this week is, in a real sense, walking the last thing Pete Dye built for golf. That context is not lost on the professionals who will compete there.

Rahm Has Already Won the Individual Title

Jon Rahm clinched his third consecutive LIV Golf Individual Championship earlier in the 2026 season. He enters Indianapolis with 944.54 points and a 218-point margin over the field — numbers that make the individual competition a formality. He will compete at Chatham Hills as a champion who already knows the outcome of his personal race.

That shifts the competitive framing entirely. The team championship is the live storyline at Indianapolis. With the individual title settled, every meaningful result this week belongs to team standings and postseason pride. LIV Golf's team format — which assigns players to squads and tracks aggregate performance across the season — reaches its conclusion in Indiana.

Festival Golf in Western Indiana

LIV Golf Indianapolis follows the circuit's established template of combining competitive golf with concert entertainment. Thomas Rhett performs Friday night. Disco Lines brings a high-energy set on Saturday. The atmosphere that results — golf fans mixing with concert-goers on a Pete Dye course that most of them have never seen — is precisely the format LIV has used to build its distinct brand identity.

The event is scheduled for August 21–23 with a shotgun start format that keeps the competitive window tight and the entertainment footprint broad. For golf fans in the midwest, it represents one of the rare opportunities to see world-class players on a marquee Pete Dye design without traveling to Florida, Wisconsin, or the Carolinas.

What's Next

LIV Golf Indianapolis runs August 21–23 at The Club at Chatham Hills in Westfield, Indiana. The individual championship narrative belongs to Rahm. The team championship adds competitive urgency to a week that might otherwise feel like a victory lap. And Pete Dye's fingerprints on every fairway and bunker give the venue a resonance that makes the event worth paying attention to regardless of where you stand on professional golf's current landscape.
