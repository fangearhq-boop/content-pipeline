BMW Championship Preview: Scheffler, Burns, and the Battle for 30 Spots at Bellerive

By Ryan Calloway | August 17, 2026

The FedExCup Playoffs reach their decisive moment this week as 50 players converge on Bellerive Country Club in St. Louis — and only 30 of them will have a season left when it's over.

Three days from now, the BMW Championship tees off at Bellerive Country Club in St. Louis, and the FedExCup Playoffs move into their second-to-last chapter. Fifty players qualify. Thirty advance to the Tour Championship at East Lake. Twenty go home. The format is as clean and brutal as anything in professional golf, and the $20 million purse attached to it makes Bellerive one of the richest events on the PGA Tour calendar.

This is the week where careers pivot. This is the week where regular-season accomplishments mean almost nothing anymore.

Scheffler Arrives as the Clear Favorite

Scottie Scheffler enters the BMW Championship as the No. 1 seed in the FedExCup standings, carrying a points lead built over one of the most dominant regular seasons in recent PGA Tour history. His game is built precisely for the challenges Bellerive presents.

Bellerive Country Club is a par 70 layout measuring 7,448 yards — long, demanding, and unforgiving of directional mistakes. The course rewards accurate driving, elite iron play from mid-to-long range, and the kind of mental composure that separates top players from everyone else under pressure. Those are exactly Scheffler's three best attributes. He doesn't win ugly. He wins by executing the plan better than everyone else on the course.

Bellerive last hosted a major in 2018, when Brooks Koepka shot a 72-hole total of 264 to win the PGA Championship — one of the lowest totals in major championship history. The course has been stretched and refined since, but its fundamental character remains: long hitters who control their irons win here. Scheffler fits that profile as well as anyone in the field.

Sam Burns Arrives with Momentum

If Scheffler is the chalk, Sam Burns is the story. The Louisiana native won the FedEx St. Jude Championship last week at TPC Southwind, converting a third-round 62 into a Sunday title run that will be remembered as one of the more dramatic Playoff victories in recent years. He heads to Bellerive with the validation of a Playoff win and the confidence of a player who just went through the fire and came out the other side holding a trophy.

Burns's ability to sustain that momentum into a second consecutive Playoff event is one of the most compelling questions of the week. Players who win at St. Jude and then win or contend at the BMW are rare. But it happens — and Burns's game, particularly his ball-striking and ability to build momentum over four rounds, gives him a legitimate path at Bellerive.

The Rest of the Field

Beyond Scheffler and Burns, the BMW Championship field is rich with marquee names. Rory McIlroy, a three-time FedExCup champion, arrives looking for redemption after a 66th-place finish at St. Jude that left him 8-over par without a single sub-par round. Xander Schauffele, Cameron Young, Collin Morikawa, and Hideki Matsuyama round out a group of former major champions and consistent contenders who all know how to navigate pressure-packed formats.

The field of 50 means the fringe of the top-30 cut line is where the real drama unfolds. Players ranked 25th through 45th in the FedExCup standings enter knowing their entire season could come down to four rounds in St. Louis. That pressure produces some of the most compelling golf of the Playoff calendar.

What's Next

The BMW Championship runs August 20–23 at Bellerive Country Club in St. Louis. The top 30 in the FedExCup standings after the event advance to the Tour Championship at East Lake Golf Club in Atlanta (August 27–30). The points are reset dramatically at the Tour Championship, meaning anyone inside the top 30 carries a realistic mathematical chance at the FedExCup title. For 20 players, however, the 2026 season ends on Sunday in St. Louis.
