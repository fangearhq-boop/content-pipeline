Jordan Spieth's 15-Year Streak: Winless in 2026 but Still Very Much Alive

By Jake Torres | August 17, 2026

He entered Memphis ranked 54th, nearly out of the Playoffs before they started. He leaves with his 15th consecutive BMW Championship berth — and a genuine shot at the Tour Championship if he can find his form at Bellerive.

Jordan Spieth almost didn't make it to Bellerive. Let that settle for a moment, because the context matters: Jordan Spieth, three-time major champion, 2015 FedExCup winner, one of the most decorated players of his generation, entered the FedEx St. Jude Championship ranked 54th in the FedExCup standings — four spots outside the cut line that qualifies for the BMW Championship.

He needed Memphis. He delivered in Memphis. A bogey-free 65 in the first round pushed him inside the top 50, and he did enough over the remaining rounds to hold his position. His 15th consecutive BMW Championship berth is secure. The streak lives.

What the Streak Actually Means

Fifteen consecutive appearances at the BMW Championship stretches back to 2012. In that time, Spieth has navigated the kind of career arc that would have ended lesser players' runs years ago. He broke onto the scene as a teenager with impossible composure. He dominated from 2015 through 2017, winning three majors in three years, nearly completing the Grand Slam at The Masters in 2016 before one of golf's most famous collapses on the back nine Sunday. He struggled with his putting mechanics for three seasons in a way that made analysts question whether he would ever recapture what he had.

And yet here he is, in August of 2026, standing on the tee at Bellerive for the 15th straight year. The streak is not a statistic. It is a testimony to professional golf's basic truth: showing up when it matters is harder than it looks, and Spieth has shown up, every single year, regardless of form.

The Challenge Ahead at Bellerive

Spieth has not won on the PGA Tour in 2026. He has no top-10 finishes from the regular season. He arrives at the BMW Championship carrying the weight of a year that has not produced the results he expected — and with only four rounds at Bellerive standing between him and a Tour Championship appearance.

The top 30 in the FedExCup standings after the BMW advance to East Lake. The points reset at the Tour Championship is dramatic enough that any player inside the top 30 carries a mathematical shot at the title. For Spieth, reaching East Lake would represent more than just a tournament appearance. It would be validation — evidence that despite a difficult year, he still belongs in the game's elite conversation.

Bellerive's par 70 layout at 7,448 yards sets up as a place where Spieth can compete. His iron play, when it's working, is elite. His short game around the greens remains one of the best on Tour. The question, as it always is with Spieth, is his putter. If it cooperates at Bellerive, he is capable of any result on this leaderboard.

What's Next

The BMW Championship runs August 20–23 at Bellerive Country Club in St. Louis. Spieth will tee it up having entered the week inside the top 50 — comfortably enough to keep the Tour Championship dream alive if he can put together four competitive rounds. His first FedExCup title came in 2015 at East Lake. A decade later, with a streak intact and a career that has survived everything thrown at it, the question is whether he can get back there. Bellerive is the answer — or the end of the road for 2026.
