Rory McIlroy at Bellerive: A Course Built for Redemption

By Marcus Cole | August 17, 2026

After a stunning 66th-place finish at the FedEx St. Jude Championship, McIlroy heads to a venue that rewards exactly the qualities that have made him one of the most dangerous players in the world.

Rory McIlroy finished 66th at the FedEx St. Jude Championship last week. Sixty-sixth. He shot 8-over par across four rounds in Memphis without once breaking par. For a player who has won the FedExCup three times, who has four major championships and a résumé that places him among the greatest players of his generation, it was a jarring result.

And yet McIlroy isn't done. He's far from it.

What Went Wrong at St. Jude

Golf Channel reported after the final round that McIlroy "finds encouragement" despite the 66th-place finish, and that his focus had immediately shifted to the BMW Championship at Bellerive. That framing — finding encouragement in a week of 8-over golf — is worth examining.

TPC Southwind, the host of the FedEx St. Jude Championship, demands precise iron play to short, tight targets. It is not a venue that rewards the kind of aggressive, sweeping ball flight that McIlroy generates at his best. His game translates better to longer, more open layouts — courses where he can use his distance off the tee to attack par 4s and 5s with mid-irons rather than wedges. Southwind punishes the profile. Bellerive does not.

Why Bellerive Changes Everything

Bellerive Country Club is a par 70 at 7,448 yards. It is one of the longest courses in FedExCup Playoff rotation. It rewards big-hitting, long-iron play, and the ability to attack holes from distance with controlled, penetrating ball flights. Those are the exact qualities that have made McIlroy one of the most feared players in the world for over a decade.

The last time the PGA Tour played Bellerive was the 2018 PGA Championship, when Brooks Koepka shot 264 to win — one of the lowest 72-hole scores in major championship history. Koepka won that week by being the most accurate driver and the best long-iron player in the field under pressure. McIlroy can do those same things when he's dialed in. Bellerive gives him the right canvas.

Notably, Koepka did not qualify for the 2026 FedExCup Playoffs at all — a reminder of how quickly fortunes shift in professional golf, and why McIlroy's one rough week should not be treated as a trend. Three FedExCup titles says the trend runs the other direction.

The Stakes for McIlroy This Week

McIlroy enters Bellerive ranked approximately No. 12 in the FedExCup standings — a position that comfortably places him inside the top 30 required to advance to the Tour Championship at East Lake. He does not need to win this week to extend his season. But for a player of McIlroy's ambitions, cruising into the Tour Championship ranked 12th is not the objective. He has won this competition three times. He wants to win it again.

A strong performance at Bellerive would accomplish several things: it would reset the narrative from the St. Jude disaster, position him inside the top-10 seed range heading into East Lake's staggered starting format, and restore the confidence of a player whose ball-striking ability is never really in question — only his putting consistency and ability to convert when his iron play generates the right looks.

What's Next

McIlroy tees it up at the BMW Championship beginning August 20 at Bellerive Country Club in St. Louis. The top 30 from this week's field advance to the Tour Championship at East Lake Golf Club (August 27–30), where the points reset gives every qualifier a mathematical path to the FedExCup title. For McIlroy, the path to a fourth FedExCup title runs through Bellerive first. And if ever there was a course to remind the world what Rory McIlroy is capable of, it is this one.
