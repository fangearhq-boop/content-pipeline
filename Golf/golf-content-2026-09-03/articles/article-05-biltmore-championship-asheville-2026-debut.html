The Biltmore Championship Is Two Weeks Away — Here's Why It's the Most Intriguing New Event on Tour

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | September 3, 2026

The last time a PGA Tour event was played in the Blue Ridge Mountains of North Carolina, Ben Hogan was competing professionally and the world was months away from entering a global war. More than 80 years later, the Tour returns — and the inaugural Biltmore Championship could be one of the most memorable debut events the circuit has seen in decades.

The tournament is scheduled for September 17-20 at The Cliffs at Walnut Cove in Arden, North Carolina, just south of Asheville. It is the first event on the 2026-27 FedExCup Fall calendar, opening a stretch of autumn tournaments that precede the new season's official start.

The Course

The Cliffs at Walnut Cove is a Jack Nicklaus-designed course that sits in a mountain valley at the foot of the Blue Ridge Parkway, bordering the Pisgah National Forest. The layout measures 7,167 yards — significant length at elevation — with the forested mountain terrain creating a visual backdrop unlike anything else on the PGA Tour schedule. It is a private course, and the Biltmore Championship will represent most Tour players' first experience of the property.

Nicklaus-designed courses at Tour level tend to reward ball-striking precision over raw length — the architect's philosophy favors defined landing zones, demanding approach angles, and short game tests that punish inaccuracy. At elevation and in a mountain valley, wind patterns can be unpredictable in September, adding a strategic layer to an already complex test.

The Sponsors

The event's co-title sponsorship structure is distinctive. The Biltmore Estate — the Vanderbilt family's 8,000-acre private estate in Asheville, the largest privately owned home in the United States — and Explore Asheville, the region's tourism authority, signed a four-year agreement with the PGA Tour to bring the championship to western North Carolina.

The Biltmore Estate is one of America's most recognizable landmarks, drawing more than a million visitors per year to its grounds, gardens, and vineyard. Its co-title status gives the Biltmore Championship an identity that stands apart from the typical corporate tournament sponsorship — it is a place as much as a brand, and that distinctiveness translates into a richer event experience for both players and spectators.

Asheville's Recovery

The significance of the Biltmore Championship is not limited to golf. Asheville was severely impacted by Hurricane Helene, and the region's recovery — economic, physical, and psychological — has been a multi-year effort. The arrival of a PGA Tour event carries real meaning for the community: it signals that Asheville is open, vibrant, and capable of hosting world-class sporting events.

The Athlon Sports coverage of the tournament framed it explicitly as a symbol of that recovery — a sporting beacon tied to regional identity and resilience. For players in the field who are aware of Asheville's story, that context adds dimension to the week beyond the competitive scorecard.

What's Next

The official field list for the Biltmore Championship has not been released as of September 3. Given its position as the Fall's opening event, the field will draw from the full Tour card roster — players looking to build early momentum for the 2026-27 FedExCup standings. Expect the entry list to arrive early next week.

The Biltmore Championship broadcasts on Golf Channel with supplementary coverage on PGA Tour Live via ESPN+, Sirius XM radio, and the World Feed for international distribution. First tee shots September 17.
