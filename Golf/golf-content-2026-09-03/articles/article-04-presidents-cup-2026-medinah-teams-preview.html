Presidents Cup 2026: Full Teams Set, Three Weeks to Medinah

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | September 3, 2026

Three weeks from today, the 2026 Presidents Cup begins at Medinah Country Club in Medinah, Illinois. Both 12-player squads are finalized. The storylines are building. And one of the most lopsided streaks in professional team golf is about to face its latest test.

Team USA has won ten consecutive Presidents Cup editions. The International team has not won since 1998 — a 28-year drought that defines the psychological context of every edition of this competition. Captain Geoff Ogilvy's International squad arrives knowing that history does not favor them, and that breaking it would be among the most significant results in team golf since the sport went global.

Team USA

Captain Brandt Snedeker leads a squad anchored by world No. 1 Scottie Scheffler. The six automatic qualifiers — determined by the FedExCup points standings — are Scheffler, Cameron Young, Wyndham Clark, Russell Henley, Sam Burns, and Collin Morikawa. All six are proven performers at Tour level and most have Presidents Cup or Ryder Cup experience.

Snedeker's six captain's picks tell a story about both experience and future investment. Chris Gotterup, Xander Schauffele, Justin Thomas, Harrison Bridgeman, and Patrick Cantlay fill the veteran slots. The sixth pick — Jackson Koivun — is 21 years old and the youngest captain's pick in Presidents Cup history. His selection reflects Snedeker's belief that Koivun's talent is ready for the international team stage, and a desire to introduce one of American golf's most promising young talents to the Presidents Cup environment early.

International Team

Geoff Ogilvy, the 2006 U.S. Open champion, captains a squad built around a core of experienced international stars. Si Woo Kim topped the International points standings to qualify for his fourth Presidents Cup. Hideki Matsuyama, Tom Kim, Min Woo Lee, and Adam Scott round out the veterans. Ryan Fox, making his debut, adds a new dimension from the DP World Tour.

Ogilvy's six picks extend the team's depth: Corey Conners, Nico Echavarría, Sungjae Im, Rikuya Hisatsune, Nick Taylor, and Christiaan Bezuidenhout. The International team's challenge is familiar: significantly less depth than the American squad, requiring their top players to carry heavier loads across the three competition days.

Why Medinah Matters

Medinah No. 3 is not just a golf course — it's a venue with a specific gravitational pull in the history of team golf. The 2012 Ryder Cup, "The Miracle at Medinah," saw Europe come back from 10-6 down in the final day's singles to win 14.5 to 13.5. The gallery size, stadium atmosphere, and intensity of crowds at Medinah are legendary in the sport.

USA will command the partisan advantage — a Presidents Cup home crowd is reliably enthusiastic. But the International team knows what Medinah can produce in terms of drama. If they can stay within touching distance after the first two days, Sunday's singles could belong to anyone.
