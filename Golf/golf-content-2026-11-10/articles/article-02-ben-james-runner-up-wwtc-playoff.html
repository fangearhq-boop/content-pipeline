Ben James's Missed Birdie from 8 Feet Sends World Wide Technology Championship to Blades Brown

By Jake Torres | November 10, 2026

Eight feet. That was the distance between Ben James and his first PGA Tour title on Sunday evening in Los Cabos.

He missed it.

James had matched Blades Brown over 72 holes at the World Wide Technology Championship, both players finishing at 23-under par 265 to force a sudden-death playoff. When they reached the second playoff hole, James faced a makeable birdie putt inside eight feet — the kind of look that any Tour player would take in a playoff situation. He pushed the putt, Brown made his own birdie, and the championship went to the 19-year-old rookie.

A Long Week, Nearly Enough

James had played exceptional golf across the four regulation rounds at El Cardonal at Diamante. His 23-under total was a performance that would have won the championship in most weeks. The problem was that Brown matched it, forcing the extra holes and setting up the climactic finish.

The playoff had reached its second hole before the miss, meaning James had survived once already. That adds to the sting — one more hole, one more chance gone.

Michael Kim finished in third place at 22-under 264, one stroke out of the playoff. Kim's performance added quality to the leaderboard and confirmed that the low scoring at El Cardonal was earned against a competitive field.

The Nature of Playoff Golf

Playoff losses in golf are among the sport's most discussed and analyzed moments precisely because the margins are so thin. James shot the same score as a teenager who went on to become the youngest Tour winner in over 80 years. That is not a commentary on failure — it is a reflection of how good both players were over those 72 holes.

An eight-foot putt in a playoff is not a given even for the best players in the world. The circumstances demand a level of composure that is impossible to simulate in practice, and the miss does not define James's week. His performance from tee to green over four days absolutely does.

What's Next

James will return to competition with a runner-up result at an event with a $6,000,000 purse on his record. That is a career marker and a demonstration of his ability to compete at the highest level under pressure. The near-miss in Los Cabos will stay with him, but so should the evidence that he belongs in the conversation among Tour contenders heading into the stretch of the season.

The PGA Tour schedule continues through November 22. James's next opportunity to convert a close finish into a victory is not far away.
