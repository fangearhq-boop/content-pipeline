LIV Golf Enters Court-Supervised Restructuring as Rahm Claims 2026 Individual Points Title

By Ryan Calloway | November 10, 2026

LIV Golf's 2026 season has concluded, and with it comes a significant announcement that has cast uncertainty over the league's future.

The organization has announced a Court-Supervised Restructuring Process, a legal and financial mechanism that will determine what comes next for the league that disrupted professional golf when it launched. The 2026 season ran 12 official events from February 5 through August 30, and those events are now concluded. What happens to the organization beyond this restructuring remains to be seen.

Rahm Wins Individual Points Title

Jon Rahm claimed the 2026 LIV Golf individual points title, finishing the season with 259.90 points — well clear of Bryson DeChambeau in second place with 200.97, and Lucas Herbert in third with 127.03. Rahm's title was the season's clearest marker of individual excellence in the league.

For Rahm, the season capped a period in which he has established himself as one of professional golf's most versatile and dominant players — capable of winning on any circuit, in any format. His points total at LIV in 2026 reflects consistent high-level performance across a full season of events.

What Restructuring Means

Court-supervised restructuring is a formal legal process that typically involves creditors, investors, and organizational stakeholders working toward a resolution of financial and operational challenges. It does not automatically mean that an organization will cease to exist, but it does signal that significant changes are ahead.

For the players who competed in LIV Golf in 2026, the season's conclusion and the restructuring announcement raise questions about the future of their competitive home and the trajectory of a league that attracted some of the sport's biggest names when it launched.

The Broader Context

LIV Golf's arrival in the professional golf landscape fundamentally reshaped discussions about player compensation, tour loyalty, and the structure of the sport globally. Whatever the outcome of the restructuring process, the league's impact on conversations about how professional golf is organized and how players are paid has been permanent.

Major organizations in the sport — the PGA Tour, the DP World Tour, and others — will be watching the restructuring process closely, as its outcome could affect player movement, tour fields, and the broader competitive landscape of professional golf for years to come.

What's Next

The Court-Supervised Restructuring Process will unfold in the months ahead, with details of the outcome to be determined. For fans of Jon Rahm, Bryson DeChambeau, and the other competitors who gave the 2026 LIV Golf season its competitive quality, the near-term question is where they will compete in 2027 and beyond.
