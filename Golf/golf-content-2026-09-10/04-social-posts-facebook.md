After a week of practice rounds, opening ceremonies, and anticipation, the 2026 Solheim Cup competition begins Friday morning at Bernardus Golf in Cromvoirt, Netherlands. Foursomes begin Friday. Fourballs on Saturday. Twelve singles on Sunday, September 13, decide the winner.

### STORY 2: LIV Golf Bankruptcy Day 2

**Post 2 — Long-Form**
Window: 8:30 AM ET

LIV Golf filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection on Monday, September 8. Today is Day 2 of the court process — and the shape of what comes next is beginning to emerge.

The Saudi Public Investment Fund, which funded the league's $5 billion-plus operation over four years, is providing $49.6 million in debtor-in-possession financing to keep LIV's operational activities running during the restructuring period. That funding buys time — for the court process to advance, for BC Partners to finalize the LIV 2.0 deal, and for the 48 contracted players to decide their individual paths.

The creditor list, now part of the public bankruptcy filing, makes visible what reporting has suggested for months. Jon Rahm is listed as approximately $7.4 million unsecured — a fraction of the estimated $100 million-plus he was owed under his original LIV contract, the rest of which may be treated differently in the restructuring. Bryson DeChambeau is listed at approximately $5.7 million contingent. Cameron Smith and Dustin Johnson are also among the prominent names in the creditor filings.

The term "unsecured creditor" carries weight in bankruptcy proceedings. It means that Rahm, DeChambeau, and others are in line behind secured creditors — the PIF financing, any outstanding vendor contracts, and legal fees — to receive recovery from the estate's assets. In a Chapter 11 restructuring, unsecured creditors typically receive cents on the dollar, if anything from the existing entity.

LIV 2.0, funded by BC Partners, represents the path where player equity stakes replace cash owed. Players who accept LIV 2.0 convert their outstanding guaranteed money into ownership stakes in the new league, competing in 10 events next year with announced venues in Saudi Arabia, Australia, South Africa, and Hong Kong.

The decisions that come in the next weeks will define where the sport's most prominent LIV-era players land.

### STORY 3: Rahm and DeChambeau's Options

**Post 3 — Long-Form**
Window: 1:00 PM ET

Three paths. Two high-profile players. And a deadline that is not yet publicly announced but will not stay open indefinitely.

Jon Rahm and Bryson DeChambeau are the two most commercially significant players in LIV Golf's creditor list. Rahm, the three-time LIV individual champion and the player whose $150-300 million guaranteed signing in 2024 defined the financial scale of the Saudi-backed league's ambitions, is now listed as an approximately $7.4 million unsecured creditor in the bankruptcy filing. DeChambeau — the two-time US Open champion who followed Rahm from the PGA Tour — is listed at approximately $5.7 million contingent.

The money they are owed as unsecured creditors will not arrive in full under almost any realistic bankruptcy outcome. Which makes the choice facing both of them not primarily a legal question but a professional one: where do they play next?

**Option 1: LIV 2.0 with BC Partners**
LIV 2.0 is the pre-packaged restructuring vehicle. BC Partners, a London-based private equity firm, is expected to fund a scaled-down 10-event alternative circuit beginning in 2027. Players who join LIV 2.0 convert outstanding guaranteed money owed to them into equity stakes in the new entity. The upside is a stake in what LIV 2.0's architects believe could become a sustainable alternative to the PGA Tour. The downside is accepting less cash than they were promised, betting on an unproven restructured organization with no track record.

**Option 2: Court Claims**
Players who decline LIV 2.0 can pursue their creditor claims through the bankruptcy court process. Given that Rahm is listed as unsecured and DeChambeau as contingent, the recovery under this path would likely be partial and delayed — potentially years of litigation. Most legal experts in similar restructuring scenarios advise creditors that the certainty of a negotiated settlement generally exceeds the expected recovery from full litigation against an insolvent entity.

**Option 3: PGA Tour or European Tour Return**
PGA Tour CEO Brian Rolapp stated publicly in early September that there is "no current path back" for LIV players — naming Rahm, DeChambeau, and Cameron Smith specifically. That statement does not foreclose individual applications; it signals that the Tour is not opening a general amnesty window. For Rahm specifically, as a European player, the DP World Tour — which has maintained its own schedule and partnership with the PGA Tour — represents a third option that circumvents the American reinstatement question entirely.

The decisions will come. The question is when and in which direction.

### STORY 4: Presidents Cup 2 Weeks Out

**Post 4 — Long-Form**
Window: 3:30 PM ET

Fourteen days.

That is how long Brandt Snedeker's USA team has to prepare for the 2026 Presidents Cup at Medinah Country Club — a competition the American side has won in 13 of the 15 editions ever played, but one that always carries the unspoken reminder that the International team is not without its own weapons.

Snedeker's roster is anchored by Scottie Scheffler — the world No. 1 and 2026 FedExCup champion — alongside Collin Morikawa, Sam Burns, Wyndham Clark, and a dozen players who collectively represent the deepest pool of American talent assembled for a Presidents Cup in recent memory. The captain's picks include Patrick Cantlay, Xander Schauffele, Justin Thomas, and 21-year-old Jackson Koivun — the Auburn product who won the 3M Open in July in just his third professional start, setting a tournament record in the process. Koivun is the second-youngest American Presidents Cup selection in history, behind only Jordan Spieth's selection at 20 in 2013.

International captain Geoff Ogilvy has built a team centered on Hideki Matsuyama, the defining individual star of the International side's recent history. Si Woo Kim, Tom Kim, Tony Finau, and Min Woo Lee give the International squad firepower at multiple positions. Adam Scott — among the longest-serving International representatives — brings match play experience and leadership.

The International team has never won the Presidents Cup since the 1998 edition in Melbourne. Ogilvy, a former US Open champion, is aware of that history. He has spoken publicly about the need for the International team to approach match play differently than in previous editions — building early momentum in foursomes rather than relying on singles to close gaps.

Medinah Country Club hosts what should be the most watched Presidents Cup in years. Scheffler as the American anchor and Matsuyama as the International answer frames the competition's most compelling narrative.

### STORY 5: Biltmore Championship Preview

**Post 5 — Long-Form**
Window: 8:30 PM ET

Next week, the PGA Tour visits western North Carolina for the first time.

The Biltmore Championship — September 17-20 at The Cliffs at Walnut Cove in Arden, North Carolina — is an inaugural event, a new entry in the Tour's fall swing that introduces a Jack Nicklaus signature design to professional tournament golf and brings the Tour to the Asheville region for the first time in the modern era.

The fall swing sits outside the FedExCup points season, which concluded with Scottie Scheffler's dominance at the Tour Championship last month. That means the Biltmore Championship will draw a field motivated differently than a regular-season event — players chasing ranking points, players working on specific aspects of their games ahead of the Presidents Cup the following week, and players who genuinely want to be part of a new event's inaugural chapter.

The Cliffs at Walnut Cove is a private club in the Blue Ridge Mountain foothills. Nicklaus's design is built around the natural elevation changes of the site, which provides dramatic visual staging that the Tour's broadcast team will likely make full use of. The Asheville region has been rebuilding its broader regional profile following the flooding damage from Tropical Storm Helene in 2024, and the PGA Tour's arrival represents a meaningful moment for that recovery story.

The field will be announced in the coming days. Watch Golf Fanrecap for coverage through the week.
