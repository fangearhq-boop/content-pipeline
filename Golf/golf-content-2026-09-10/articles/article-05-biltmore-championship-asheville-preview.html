Biltmore Championship: The PGA Tour Comes to Asheville for the First Time

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | September 10, 2026

The inaugural Biltmore Championship runs September 17-20 at The Cliffs at Walnut Cove in Arden, North Carolina — the first PGA Tour event ever held in western North Carolina. Jack Nicklaus's signature design and a field chasing ranking points set the stage for a new event's debut.

The PGA Tour's fall swing has always served a different purpose than the regular season. The FedExCup points race concluded last month with Scottie Scheffler's dominance at the Tour Championship. What remains on the calendar before the new season begins is a series of events that attract players with different motivations: world ranking points, specific course preparation, a chance to compete in a first-time event before it establishes its own history.

The Biltmore Championship, starting September 17, represents exactly that kind of occasion. The Tour has never played western North Carolina before. Beginning next week, it does.

The Course and Setting

The Cliffs at Walnut Cove is a private club in Arden, North Carolina, situated in the Blue Ridge Mountain foothills south of Asheville. Jack Nicklaus designed the course around the natural elevation changes of the site — a terrain feature that produces dramatic visual staging and strategic variety that flat-ground courses rarely offer.

Nicklaus signature designs tend to reward a complete game. The elevation changes at Walnut Cove affect club selection and shot shape in ways that favor players who can work the ball in both directions and whose iron games hold up under the specific demands of mountain terrain. Length off the tee matters — it always does on Tour — but precision into elevated or downhill greens matters more than it would on a links-style layout.

The Tour's broadcast team will have significant staging to work with. Blue Ridge Mountain foothills in mid-September provide visual material that has made events at similarly situated venues appointment viewing — Augusta National's setting is the most famous example, but mountain courses generally photograph well, and Walnut Cove's terrain will give the production team compelling natural elements to work with.

The Asheville Region Context

The Asheville area has been rebuilding its broader regional profile since the flooding damage caused by Tropical Storm Helene in 2024. The storm caused significant damage across western North Carolina, affecting communities throughout the region. The recovery process has been ongoing, and the PGA Tour's arrival in the area represents more than an addition to the tournament schedule — it signals that the region is returning to its prior standing as a tourism and hospitality destination.

Major sporting events bring economic activity and national attention. An inaugural PGA Tour event in a region that has spent two years in recovery carries meaning beyond the competition itself. The Tour's decision to establish this event was made with that context in view.

The Field and What Players Are Playing For

The fall swing field will be announced in the days before the event. Players without full-season exemptions use fall events to secure their Tour cards for the following season, making every fall swing event a high-stakes opportunity for players on the bubble. Alongside those players will be established Tour members looking for a final tune-up before the Presidents Cup at Medinah the following week — a one-week gap that makes the Biltmore Championship a relevant preparation event for players in Snedeker's consideration.

World ranking points remain in play through the fall swing. Players who want to move up the rankings heading into the new year have opportunities in these events that the regular-season schedule's depth and competition level makes harder to come by. The Biltmore Championship, as an inaugural event with a developing field identity, represents an opportunity for players motivated by ranking movement.
