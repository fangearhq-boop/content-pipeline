LIV Golf Bankruptcy Day 2: What the Chapter 11 Process Means — and What Happens Next

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | September 10, 2026

LIV Golf filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection on September 8. On Day 2 of the process, the contours of the restructuring are becoming clearer: PIF provides $49.6M in DIP financing, all player contracts are terminated, and 48 players are now weighing their options.

Chapter 11 bankruptcy filings are not endings. They are, in the legal definition, a structured pause — a mechanism for an insolvent organization to continue operating while it reorganizes its debts, assets, and obligations under court supervision. LIV Golf's filing on September 8 initiated exactly that process.

Day 2 of the LIV Golf bankruptcy proceeds with the case formally in the hands of the New Jersey federal court. The players, the creditors, the Saudi Public Investment Fund, and BC Partners are now operating within a framework defined by the US Bankruptcy Code rather than private contracts. Understanding what that means requires separating the legal process from the broader narrative.

The PIF's $49.6M DIP Financing

Debtor-in-possession financing is one of the most common features of Chapter 11 cases involving operating businesses. It allows the debtor — in this case, LIV Golf — to continue operational activities while the bankruptcy case proceeds, funded by a lender who receives priority status over existing creditors in exchange for providing liquidity during the restructuring period.

The Saudi Public Investment Fund, which funded LIV Golf's operations over four years at an estimated total of $5 billion or more, is providing $49.6 million in DIP financing. That amount is relatively modest compared to the fund's prior investment — it suggests PIF is providing enough to keep the organization functionally alive during the restructuring without committing to the scale of investment that characterized the original LIV Golf project.

The DIP financing buys time. Specifically, it buys time for BC Partners, the London-based private equity firm that has been in discussions with LIV Golf's leadership, to finalize the LIV 2.0 investment and restructuring package. Reports from before the filing indicated BC Partners is targeting an investment of $250-350 million to fund a scaled-down 10-event alternative circuit beginning in 2027.

What Happened to Player Contracts

Under Chapter 11 rules, the filing of a bankruptcy petition does not automatically terminate every contract — but executory contracts (those with ongoing obligations on both sides) can be rejected through the court process. LIV Golf's player contracts, which involved guaranteed payments owed by LIV to players in exchange for competitive commitments, fall into a category where the debtor (LIV Golf) effectively cannot perform its obligations going forward.

The practical consequence is that all player contracts are effectively voided by the filing. Players who signed guaranteed deals — including Jon Rahm, reported at approximately $7.4 million unsecured in the creditor filings, and Bryson DeChambeau, at approximately $5.7 million contingent — become creditors of the estate for the amounts they are owed. They are no longer under competitive obligation to LIV Golf because LIV Golf cannot fulfill its side of the contract.

This frees players to negotiate with other tours, subject to those tours' own membership rules. It also means players are unsecured creditors competing with other claims against the estate's available assets.

The Creditor Hierarchy

In bankruptcy proceedings, creditors are paid in order of priority. Secured creditors — those whose loans are backed by specific assets — have the highest claim. The PIF's DIP financing would be at the top of that hierarchy. Next come administrative claims, including legal fees and court costs. General unsecured creditors, which is where most player contracts fall, receive whatever remains after priority claims are satisfied.

In practice, this means that even players with substantial guaranteed contracts may recover a small fraction of what they were owed — or nothing at all — if the estate's assets are insufficient to reach unsecured claims after priority claims are paid. The offers of LIV 2.0 equity in lieu of cash settlement are designed to provide an alternative recovery path that avoids this uncertainty.

What's Next

The immediate next steps in the LIV Golf bankruptcy are: the court approving the DIP financing and operational continuity plan, BC Partners formalizing the LIV 2.0 investment agreement, and players being presented with formal settlement offers that quantify the equity-for-debt conversion. Each of those steps involves court approval and legal process that will likely take weeks. Follow Golf Fanrecap for updates on the bankruptcy proceedings and player decisions as they emerge.
