Presidents Cup 2026: Two Weeks Out at Medinah, USA Brings Its Deepest Team in Years

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | September 10, 2026

The 2026 Presidents Cup at Medinah Country Club is 14 days away. USA captain Brandt Snedeker leads the deepest American team assembled in recent memory. International captain Geoff Ogilvy has Matsuyama as the anchor and a team built to win early.

Medinah Country Club has hosted major championships and Ryder Cups. In two weeks, it hosts the 2026 Presidents Cup — the biennial team competition that pits an American side against the rest of the world, with the notable exception of Europe. The USA has won 13 of the 15 editions ever played. The International team's one victory came in 1998 in Melbourne. Every Presidents Cup since has carried the same structural question: does the gap in depth between the two sides ever close enough for that record to change?

Geoff Ogilvy, the International captain and former US Open champion, has spent months building the argument that it might. September 22-27 will provide the answer.

Brandt Snedeker's American Team

USA captain Brandt Snedeker has assembled a roster built around one foundational truth: Scottie Scheffler is the best player in the world, and an American team with Scheffler as its anchor is structurally difficult to beat over five team sessions.

Scheffler arrives at Medinah as the world No. 1 and 2026 FedExCup champion — the defining player of the current PGA Tour season and a competitor whose combination of precision and consistency makes him the ideal Presidents Cup anchor. Collin Morikawa, Sam Burns, and Wyndham Clark bring established major-championship pedigree and match play capability to the lineup.

The captain's selections complete the picture. Patrick Cantlay and Xander Schauffele bring the most experienced Presidents Cup partnership on the team. Justin Thomas, a former major champion, adds a player who has competed in multiple team events and knows how to perform under the specific pressure that Presidents Cup matches create.

The most talked-about pick is 21-year-old Jackson Koivun — the Auburn product who won the 3M Open in July in his third professional start, setting a tournament record. Koivun is the second-youngest American Presidents Cup selection in history, behind only Jordan Spieth's selection at 20 in 2013. How Snedeker deploys a player of Koivun's raw talent against experienced International competition in the opening foursomes sessions will be one of the defining strategic questions of the week.

Geoff Ogilvy's International Team

The International side's challenge is structural: in every Presidents Cup edition, the American depth advantage has eroded the International team's ability to win at the back of the lineup even when the top of the roster matches up well. Ogilvy's answer is to build a team capable of generating early momentum in foursomes before the lineup depth becomes decisive.

Hideki Matsuyama is the International team's defining figure — a former Masters champion, a player whose match play record shows he can compete against any American in the lineup on any given day, and the anchor that gives Ogilvy credibility in every session. Si Woo Kim and Tom Kim give the International side a Korean presence with firepower at multiple positions. Tony Finau, whose power and length play particularly well in foursomes, provides a different dimension.

Adam Scott is among the longest-serving International representatives, a player who has been part of every recent Presidents Cup and whose match play experience and leadership in the team environment carry weight beyond his individual record. Min Woo Lee adds youth and aggression to the International lineup.

Ogilvy has spoken publicly about the need to approach match play differently — building early momentum in foursomes sessions rather than relying on singles to close large point deficits. The International team has attempted that approach before. Whether it materializes over five sessions at Medinah is the central question of the competition.

The Format and What It Means

The Presidents Cup uses the same three-day match play structure as the Ryder Cup: foursomes (alternating shot), fourballs (best ball), and singles. Five team sessions produce 30 total points, with 15.5 required to win. The USA's structural advantage in depth has historically made the singles sessions decisive — American depth wins points in matches 7-12 that the International team cannot match.

Medinah's course setup — a long, demanding layout with water features that reward precision over length — theoretically suits players who control their ball flight under pressure. That description fits Scheffler, Morikawa, and Matsuyama in equal measure. The question is whether the International team can keep the point total close enough through the team sessions that singles becomes a winnable session rather than a damage-limiting one.
