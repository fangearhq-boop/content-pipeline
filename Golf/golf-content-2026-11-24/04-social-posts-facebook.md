### STORY 1: Marco Penge Wins RSM Classic

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 10:30 AM ET
Marco Penge walked onto the 18th green at Sea Island on Monday needing a birdie just to get into a playoff. He made it. Then he had to come back and do it again on the first playoff hole.

The RSM Classic ended with Penge defeating Max Greyserman, Michael Thorbjornsen, and Haotong Li in a dramatic four-way playoff — all four had finished regulation at 16-under 266. The winning moment was a 4-foot birdie putt, nerves compressed into a single stroke that delivered Penge his first PGA Tour title and $1,332,000.

The story behind the win is as good as the win itself. Penge is an Englishman who grew up in Crawley, West Sussex. He earned his PGA Tour card for 2026 through the Race to Dubai after winning twice on the DP World Tour — first at the 2025 Hainan Classic, then the Danish Golf Championship. He also spent two months suspended from the tour for a betting violation, an episode he navigated with the kind of resilience that shows up again in what he accomplished Monday.

He entered the final round 120th in the FedExCup Fall standings. His win moved him to 64th and secured his card. Sometimes a season gets saved on the last day of the last event.

This one was saved with a birdie putt on the 18th hole. Twice. #Golf #PGATour

### STORY 2: LIV Golf Wind-Down Continues

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 1:00 PM ET
In the end, it came down to a New Jersey courtroom.

LIV Golf filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection on September 8, 2026. Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, which had spent what reports estimated at $5 billion across the LIV experiment, ended its support. BC Partners came in with restructuring, then pulled out. On November 5, attorneys for the league told the court it was over — no LIV 2.0, no relaunched version of the league. Wind-down.

The attorney's words were remarkably direct: the debtors were "ultimately unable to bring the key parties to a fully consensual arrangement." January 2027 is the liquidation target. The PGA Tour, meanwhile, continues to fight subpoenas filed as part of the proceedings.

Questions remain. Can the $15.6 million in pre-filing payments to seven executives be reversed? What happens to the financial commitments LIV made to players? Those details will get worked through in court over the coming months.

What is certain is that the era is ending. Professional golf ran a four-year experiment with a rival league funded at a scale never seen before in the sport. It is now over, and the tours, players, and governing bodies are in the process of figuring out what comes next.

### STORY 3: BMW Australian PGA Championship Preview

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 3:30 PM ET
The DP World Tour's new season begins this week, and it begins in Sydney.

The BMW Australian PGA Championship runs from Thursday, November 26, through Sunday, November 29, at The Lakes Golf Club — the club's first time hosting the event since 1987. The tournament carries AU$2.5 million in prize money, the largest purse in its 121-year history, and serves as the season-opening event for the DP World Tour.

Min Woo Lee is among the pre-tournament favorites, which will be welcome news to Australian golf fans who have watched him develop into one of the tour's more exciting players. The Lakes is a classic course that will reward precision and course management alongside the power that tends to dominate modern professional golf.

For the DP World Tour, this event carries added significance given the impending arrival of roughly 19 former LIV players into the tour's ranks. The 2026 season may start with familiar faces, but the landscape of the tour is about to shift considerably when those players begin competing.

For now, the attention turns to Sydney, to a historic course, and to a week of golf that opens a new chapter.

### STORY 4: Former LIV Players Setting Course for DP World Tour

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 6:00 PM ET
The aftermath of LIV Golf's collapse is reordering professional golf's player landscape in ways that will take the full 2027 season to fully understand.

Around 19 former LIV players — including Bryson DeChambeau, Jon Rahm, and Louis Oosthuizen — have secured spots on the DP World Tour as the bankruptcy wind-down proceeds. These are names that have been absent from European Tour events for three or more years, and their return will reshape fields, raise purse interest, and change the conversation around the tour considerably.

For DeChambeau and Rahm specifically, this is a significant next step. Both were among LIV's highest-profile signings when the league launched. DeChambeau was a polarizing but commercially valuable presence; Rahm won LIV's Individual Championship. Their returns to the DP World Tour represent a reintegration of talent into a circuit that never quite matched LIV's financial firepower but always offered competitive golf at the highest level.

The PGA Tour has stated that reinstatement decisions for former LIV players will not come until early 2027. That timeline means the DP World Tour gets these players first — which is not nothing.

### STORY 5: PGA Tour 2026 Season Closes

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 8:30 PM ET
The 2026 PGA Tour season is done. The RSM Classic at Sea Island wrapped it up on Monday with one of the more dramatic finishes the event has produced — a four-man playoff won by Marco Penge, an English first-timer who had been on the verge of losing his card.

Before the season-ending drama, there was Scottie Scheffler winning the Tour Championship at East Lake and securing the FedExCup. There was Blades Brown becoming one of the youngest PGA Tour winners in 95 years. There was an unusual autumn defined as much by what was happening off the course — LIV Golf filing for bankruptcy, the sport's landscape shifting under everyone's feet — as by what was happening on it.

Professional golf enters 2027 with more questions than it has had in years, and many of them are interesting ones. Where do 19 former LIV players land? What does the PGA Tour look like when it integrates them? Who emerges? Who doesn't?

The 2026 season asked some of those questions. The 2027 season will begin answering them.
