DeChambeau, Rahm, Oosthuizen Among 19 Former LIV Players Moving to DP World Tour as Redistribution Accelerates

By Marcus Cole | November 24, 2026 | Golf Fanrecap

With LIV Golf's bankruptcy wind-down proceeding through the courts and a January 2027 liquidation target now in place, professional golf's redistribution of talent is becoming concrete. Approximately 19 former LIV players — including Bryson DeChambeau, Jon Rahm, and Louis Oosthuizen — have secured places on the DP World Tour as the most significant reshuffling the sport has undergone since the LIV era began in 2022.

The PGA Tour's formal reinstatement process for former LIV players is not expected to begin until early 2027. That timeline means the DP World Tour is the first stop for many of these players — a situation that transforms the European circuit's competitive landscape in ways that will take time to fully measure.

The Names and What They Bring

DeChambeau was one of LIV Golf's most commercially visible signings when the league launched. His power game, his analytical approach to the game, and his willingness to engage publicly made him one of the faces of the LIV experiment for three-plus years. His return to established tour golf brings that profile back to events that will attract significantly more viewership interest than they otherwise would.

Rahm won LIV Golf's Individual Championship, confirming that competitive quality was present even as the league's financial structure collapsed around it. As one of the sport's most complete players, his presence on the DP World Tour will raise the competitive standard at whatever events he enters and will attract the kind of sponsor and broadcast attention the tour covets.

Oosthuizen's situation is different — a veteran player entering the later stages of a distinguished career, moving to a tour where he can compete effectively without the pressures of the LIV format that he had been part of for several years.

What This Does for the DP World Tour

The arrival of roughly 19 players of LIV pedigree fundamentally changes the DP World Tour's competitive identity. Fields that were already strong become genuinely elite. Prize money discussions will likely intensify as the tour makes the case to sponsors that its events now feature the kind of names that drive television ratings and ticket sales.

The challenge for existing DP World Tour members is finding space in fields that will now be more crowded with higher-ranked players. Some displacement of established tour members from marquee events is an inevitable consequence of an influx at this scale.

What's Next

The DP World Tour season opens this week at the BMW Australian PGA Championship in Sydney, before the full field expansion begins to take shape in 2027. PGA Tour reinstatement decisions — which will determine which former LIV players can also return to that circuit — are due in early 2027. The reintegration is happening on multiple timelines simultaneously, and the picture will not be fully clear until both tours have worked through their respective processes. What is clear is that professional golf is restructuring, and it is restructuring quickly.
