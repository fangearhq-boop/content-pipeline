Marco Penge Wins RSM Classic in Four-Man Playoff for First PGA Tour Title

By Marcus Cole | November 24, 2026 | Golf Fanrecap

Marco Penge birdied the 18th hole at Sea Island to get into a playoff. Then he birdied the 18th again to win it. The English golfer's first PGA Tour title came in the most dramatic circumstances the RSM Classic has produced — a four-man playoff involving Penge, Max Greyserman, Michael Thorbjornsen, and Haotong Li, all having finished regulation at 16-under 266.

Penge won on the first extra hole with a 4-foot birdie putt, claiming $1,332,000 from the $7,400,000 total purse and completing one of the more remarkable final-round turnarounds of the 2026 PGA Tour season. He entered the last day 120th in the FedExCup Fall standings — the threshold between keeping and losing his PGA Tour card — and left with his name in the winner's circle and a move to 64th on the list.

The Playoff Moment

Making birdie on the 72nd hole to extend a tournament is high-pressure in the most direct sense. Making birdie on the same hole again to win the entire thing — against three players who have done the same — requires something beyond technique alone. Penge demonstrated that he has that something Monday at Sea Island.

The four-way tie after regulation was the seventh playoff in RSM Classic history. With any of Greyserman, Thorbjornsen, or Li capable of winning, the playoff required Penge to execute when the moment mattered most. His four-foot winning putt converted as cleanly as a putt can, and with it came a first victory on the PGA Tour that few at Sea Island would have predicted at the start of the week given his standings position.

Penge's Path to This Win

Marco Penge was born in Crawley, West Sussex, England, and came up through golf's developmental system before turning professional in 2017. His path to the PGA Tour ran through the DP World Tour, where he won twice in 2025 — first at the Hainan Classic, then the Danish Golf Championship — and finished well enough in the Race to Dubai to earn dual membership on the PGA Tour for 2026.

His career has included challenges. A two-month suspension for a betting violation — placing bets on tournaments he was not competing in — was an episode he handled openly and returned from strongly. He has also been public about managing ADHD, crediting his understanding of it as a meaningful part of what has helped his career develop.

Monday's win brings all of that into a sharper context. It is a first PGA Tour title for a player who earned his way here through the European pathway, navigated some difficult moments along the way, and delivered when his season was on the line.

What's Next

With the RSM Classic serving as the final event of the 2026 PGA Tour season, Penge heads into the offseason as a PGA Tour winner for the first time. His exemption through the win and his improved standings position give him a foundation for 2027 that looked very different at the start of the final round. The other three playoff participants — Greyserman, Thorbjornsen, and Li — each earned $559,933. For Penge, the week ended with considerably more than money.
