PGA Tour 2026 Season Closes After RSM Classic — Scheffler, Penge, and Transformation Define the Year

By Ryan Calloway | November 24, 2026 | Golf Fanrecap

The 2026 PGA Tour season is over. The RSM Classic at Sea Island delivered its final-day drama — Marco Penge winning a four-man playoff to claim his first title — and with it the last competitive golf the tour will produce until the new season begins. What the full year produced deserves more than a brief note: it was a season defined by its competitive action and by forces from outside the ropes that made the year unlike any other in modern professional golf's history.

Scottie Scheffler won the Tour Championship at East Lake and secured the FedExCup for another year. That story is the kind of season-defining performance that anchors a tour's identity — a No. 1 player performing like a No. 1 player on the biggest stages. Scheffler has built his place at the top of the game through a combination of skill and consistency that few players in the sport's history have matched at his age.

The Surprising Stories

Blades Brown's victory earlier in the fall stood out as one of the year's most surprising individual results — a final-round 63, a 7-shot rally, and a winner who became one of the youngest PGA Tour champions in 95 years. Moments like that are what make a season register beyond its statistics. They create memories that fans who were watching carry forward.

Penge's RSM Classic win operates in a similar category. Getting in on the final hole, winning on the first playoff hole, moving from 120th to 64th to keep his card — these are the kinds of specifics that define a week at Sea Island in a year most players would describe as complicated.

The Backdrop That Changed Everything

The 2026 PGA Tour season competed for column inches with the most significant structural story professional golf has seen in decades. LIV Golf filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy. Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund ended its support. Lawyers confirmed a wind-down. January 2027 is the liquidation target.

That backdrop gave every tournament some part of its meaning — whether players on the LIV circuit were competing elsewhere, whether negotiations between the tours had progressed or stalled, whether the future looked more or less settled. By the time the RSM Classic concluded on Monday, the future was at least more defined than it had been a year ago. The LIV era is ending. What replaces it is still being arranged.

What's Next

The DP World Tour's season opens this week in Sydney. The PGA Tour's new season will begin in January 2027 with a field composition that will start to reflect the sport's new reality. Former LIV players will begin integrating. Reinstatement decisions will be made. The professional golf calendar will need to accommodate names that have been absent from its events for three or more years.

It is, by any measure, an offseason worth watching. The 2026 season gave a lot to follow on the course. The 2027 season promises to begin with a great deal to follow off it.
