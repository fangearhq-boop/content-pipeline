LIV Golf's Wind-Down Advances Through Bankruptcy Process as January Liquidation Target Nears

By Ryan Calloway | November 24, 2026 | Golf Fanrecap

LIV Golf's end is unfolding in a New Jersey courtroom in stages, and each stage carries the league further from the sport it disrupted for three years. The league filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection on September 8, 2026, following Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund withdrawal of support. On November 5, attorneys confirmed in New Jersey Bankruptcy Court that there would be no relaunched version of the league — no LIV 2.0. January 2027 is the target for liquidation to complete.

Gibson Dunn attorney Matthew Williams delivered the confirmation that many had expected but few had seen formalized: the debtors were "ultimately unable to bring the key parties to a fully consensual arrangement." The language was precise, the implication absolute. LIV Golf is not restructuring into something new. It is winding down into nothing.

What Got Us Here

LIV Golf launched in 2022 with a mandate backed by Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund and a willingness to spend at a scale professional golf had never seen. Signing fees estimated in the hundreds of millions, guaranteed contracts, and a format designed to appeal to television and casual fans — LIV built the structure of a rival league quickly and recruited some of the sport's most prominent names to populate it.

The result was three years of legal battles with the PGA Tour, internal debates about the future of professional golf's structure, and an attempt at a merger that collapsed before it was formalized. PIF ultimately ended its funding commitment after the 2026 season concluded, citing an inability to find a viable path to a financially sustainable league. BC Partners came in as a potential partner, then withdrew as well.

The collapse did not come suddenly. It came through a sequence of steps that left the league without the financial support it needed to continue operations.

Open Questions

The bankruptcy process still has work ahead of it. Among the open questions is whether $15.6 million in payments made to seven executives before the Chapter 11 filing can be reversed as part of the wind-down. The PGA Tour continues to contest subpoenas filed in connection with the case, a legal thread that runs alongside the broader bankruptcy proceedings.

For the players who signed with LIV — many of whom accepted large guaranteed contracts — the financial terms of those contracts and what happens to unpaid commitments through the wind-down process will be a matter the courts work through over the coming months.

What's Next

The January 2027 liquidation target gives all parties a horizon to plan around. Former LIV players who have already secured spots on the DP World Tour — approximately 19 have done so, including Bryson DeChambeau, Jon Rahm, and Louis Oosthuizen — are ahead of that timeline. PGA Tour reinstatement decisions for former LIV players are expected no earlier than early 2027. Professional golf's redistribution is underway, and the RSM Classic ending Monday with a new PGA Tour champion was a reminder that the sport's main circuits have continued operating throughout the LIV saga. They continue now.
