LIV Golf's Future Is Uncertain: Rahm Wins Third Straight Title as Saudi Funding Dries Up

By Ryan Calloway | August 29, 2026

The 2026 LIV Golf season is over. Jon Rahm won his third consecutive individual championship title, cementing his status as the dominant player in the league's short history. Legion XIII took the team title. And now the entire enterprise — the players, the events, the $500 million-plus that Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund poured into professional golf — faces an uncertain future heading into 2027.

Rahm's Third Straight Title

Rahm's run of three consecutive LIV Golf individual titles is the defining individual achievement in the league's existence. La Sasso won the Indianapolis finale at 18-under, edging Rahm by one shot in the closing event, but Rahm's overall season total was enough to claim the third consecutive crown.

Legion XIII, Rahm's team, also took the team championship — a complete sweep of the individual and team honors. For Rahm personally, the achievements are significant. Whether they carry weight in the broader golf world remains tied to what happens to LIV Golf itself.

PIF Withdrawing — What That Means

Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, which bankrolled LIV Golf from its 2022 launch through the 2026 season, is reportedly withdrawing after 2026. PIF's backing was the financial engine behind the league — guaranteed contracts for top players, prize money that rivaled or exceeded the PGA Tour's best events, and the infrastructure to run a global 14-event tour.

Without PIF, the arithmetic of LIV Golf changes dramatically. CEO Scott O'Neil has confirmed that a deal with a new investor has been signed and he expects to close it in September. But a signed deal and a closed deal are different things, and the golf world is watching closely to see whether that process completes on schedule.

Norman and Poulter Signal Concern

Greg Norman, speaking to Skratch, did not offer reassurance. The LIV Golf commissioner declined to guarantee a 2027 season and said he would rather see the league "end" than "wither away." That is a notable statement from the person most associated with building LIV Golf in the first place.

Ian Poulter offered a similarly cautious take: "I think time might run out." He added that he genuinely has not decided what he would do if LIV did not continue — a sentiment that speaks for many of the 50-plus players who currently compete on the circuit.

Players in Limbo

More than 50 elite professional golfers face real uncertainty. Many of them left the PGA Tour for LIV in 2022 and 2023, some giving up major eligibility and tour membership in the process. The landscape has shifted considerably since then — a framework agreement between PGA Tour and PIF was discussed, though its exact status is unclear.

For the players, the calculus is complicated. Some have paths back to the PGA Tour. Others do not. If LIV Golf does not continue after 2026, the options for those without clear alternatives are limited.

What's Next

CEO Scott O'Neil has indicated he expects the new investor deal to close in September. That timeline — just weeks away — will be the first major signal of whether LIV Golf 2027 is genuinely viable or whether the league's run is ending.

Player decisions on future contracts, the PGA Tour's response, and the final structure of any new investor deal will all become clearer in the coming months. The 2026 season is over. The 2027 chapter has not been written.
