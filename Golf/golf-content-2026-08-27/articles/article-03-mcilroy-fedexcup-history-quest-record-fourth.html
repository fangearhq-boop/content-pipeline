Rory McIlroy Is Chasing a Record No One Has Ever Set: A Fourth FedExCup Title

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | August 27, 2026

Rory McIlroy won his first FedExCup in 2016. His second in 2019. His third in 2022. No player in the history of the format has ever won four.

Tiger Woods, the greatest player of his generation, won two FedExCup titles. McIlroy already has three. On Thursday at East Lake Golf Club in Atlanta, he teed off in Round 1 of the Tour Championship with the record within reach — one week, one tournament, one championship away from standing completely alone in the history books.

The Road That Brought Him Here

The journey to East Lake in 2026 was not McIlroy at his smoothest. The FedEx St. Jude Championship in Memphis produced a 66th-place finish that had pundits questioning whether the Northern Irishman had the form to compete at the season's final stage.

He answered at the BMW Championship in Denver, posting a T3 finish that was good enough to keep him in the 30-player Tour Championship field. That result kept the record alive. McIlroy arrived at East Lake with his game returning to a level that, at its peak, has made him the most accomplished player of his era.

He is also the defending Masters Tournament champion, a title that has underlined the completeness of his game in major championship conditions. The pressure of East Lake — the cameras, the history, the weight of the record — is nothing new for a player who has competed at the top of professional golf for the better part of two decades.

What a Fourth Title Would Mean

In a pre-tournament press conference at East Lake, McIlroy spoke carefully about the historical implications of what he is chasing.

"I've had a complicated year," he said. "But that's what makes this week special. You have to earn your way here."

A fourth FedExCup title would not merely be another trophy. It would represent a category of achievement that separates McIlroy from every other player in the format's history — including Woods, who mastered almost every other dimension of professional golf but never reached three FedExCup titles.

It would also answer a persistent question about McIlroy's legacy: not whether he is great — that question was answered long ago — but whether he belongs in the conversation about the game's all-time greats. A fourth FedExCup, added to four major championships and a career of sustained excellence, shifts that conversation decisively.

The Challenge Ahead

East Lake is not surrendering the record easily. Scottie Scheffler, who arrived as the No. 1 seed for the fifth consecutive season with 21 career wins and a dominant Memphis victory behind him, is the most formidable obstacle in the field. Matthew Fitzpatrick, Wyndham Clark, and Tommy Fleetwood — the defending champion — are all capable of winning this week.

The format offers McIlroy every chance. There are no staggered starts. Every player begins at even par. If McIlroy's best golf returns over 72 holes, the record is achievable.

That is the question East Lake will answer by Sunday evening.
