LPGA FM Championship Round 1: Miranda Wang Returns to TPC Boston to Defend Her Title

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | August 27, 2026

Miranda Wang is back at TPC Boston. The defending FM Championship champion returned to Norton, Massachusetts on Thursday for the opening round of the 2026 LPGA FM Championship — one of the richest and most competitive events on the women's golf calendar.

Wang won the 2025 edition of this event at 20-under par, holding off Jeeno Thitikul by a single shot in a wire-to-wire performance that announced her as a legitimate force at the highest level of the LPGA Tour. It was the kind of win that players spend entire careers trying to replicate.

Now she has to do it again. And the same players who pushed her last year are back.

The Course and the Challenge

TPC Boston is a par-71 layout that demands accuracy from the tee and sharp iron play into greens that can be unforgiving under tournament pin positions. The bent-grass putting surfaces reward patience; the tree-lined fairways punish ambition. Scores can cluster quickly at LPGA events on this style of course, which means the margin between champion and first loser is measured in moments rather than miles.

Wang's -20 performance in 2025 was exceptional by any measure. Matching or exceeding that total as the defending champion — with every player in the field studying how she did it — is a different kind of challenge entirely.

The Competition

Jeeno (Atthaya) Thitikul enters Round 1 with the kind of motivation that produces career-best performances. The Thai star was one shot away from winning this event in 2025 and has spent the intervening twelve months demonstrating that her game belongs at the very top of women's professional golf.

Nelly Korda, one of the game's most decorated players and a former world No. 1, brings a record of championship golf that spans multiple continents and formats. At a course that suits her precise ball-striking, Korda is a genuine contender from the first tee.

Charley Hull, the English star who has grown into one of the LPGA Tour's most popular and competitive figures, rounds out a top tier of competitors that will push Wang from the opening holes.

The $4.4 million purse — among the largest on the LPGA Tour calendar — ensures that the field has arrived at TPC Boston fully motivated.

What's Next

Round 1 is underway Thursday. The FM Championship runs through Sunday, August 30, with the champion crowned at TPC Boston following 72 holes of LPGA competition.

Wang has said she is comfortable returning to a course where she has tasted major success. Whether that comfort translates to a second consecutive title depends on 72 holes of execution against the best players in women's professional golf.
