Five Straight No. 1 Seeds, 21 Wins: Scottie Scheffler's Season Is Historically Unprecedented

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | August 27, 2026

There are benchmarks in professional golf that define eras. Jack Nicklaus and his 18 major championships. Tiger Woods and his 15. Sam Snead and his 82 PGA Tour victories. These are the numbers that frame how good the best players in the history of the game have been.

Scottie Scheffler is building his own set of numbers — and this year at East Lake, he adds another chapter to what is becoming the most dominant sustained run in modern professional golf.

Five consecutive seasons as the No. 1 FedExCup seed. Twenty-one career PGA Tour victories. A $23 million bonus locked in before the Tour Championship begins. A FedExCup title in 2024. And a regular season that ended with an eight-shot victory at the FedEx St. Jude Championship in Memphis — a margin so large it borders on surreal at the elite level of professional golf.

The Numbers in Context

Five straight seasons as the No. 1 seed means five straight seasons as the best player on the PGA Tour by the tour's own point-based measure. It means that every competitor in the field at East Lake has spent the last five years watching Scheffler set the standard they are trying to meet.

His 21st career win at the FedEx St. Jude was achieved by eight shots — not two, not four, but eight. In a field of PGA Tour professionals competing at one of the season's final playoff events, Scheffler won by a number that would be remarkable at a regular tour event and borders on absurd at a $20 million playoff.

The $23 million bonus figure represents the tour's acknowledgment that over the course of an entire season, across dozens of events and millions of competitive shots, Scheffler was the best. Not occasionally. Consistently.

Winning the FedExCup Again

Scheffler won the FedExCup title in 2024, claiming it at East Lake in what was another dominant performance. He arrives in 2026 seeking to become a two-time FedExCup champion — a mark that would pair with his sustained No. 1 seedings to paint a portrait of era-defining excellence.

The Tour Championship format offers every player an equal starting point: no staggered starts, all 30 players beginning at even par. That means Scheffler cannot coast on a structural advantage. He has to play his way to the title over 72 holes of championship golf at a course that demands precision and patience.

He has shown, repeatedly, that he can do exactly that.

The Question No One Can Answer

When people talk about the greatest players in golf history, the conversation has traditionally centered on major championships. Scheffler's major count is growing. But the FedExCup — with its season-long points race, its playoff series, and its finale at East Lake — offers a different kind of measurement: who is the best player over the course of an entire season, not just a single week?

By that measure, Scheffler has answered the question five consecutive times.

The only remaining question at East Lake is whether he answers it a sixth time — and adds a second FedExCup trophy to go alongside his 21 wins, his $23 million bonus, and his place in the conversation about the greatest players this generation of golf has produced.
