Tour Championship Round 1 is Live: Scheffler Hunts His Second FedExCup at East Lake

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | August 27, 2026

The season-ending finale of professional golf is underway. The Tour Championship opened Round 1 at East Lake Golf Club in Atlanta on Thursday, with 30 of the world's best players competing for a $40 million prize fund — the richest in PGA Tour history.

At the center of everything, as he has been for five consecutive seasons, is Scottie Scheffler.

The No. 1 seed arrives at East Lake on the back of one of the most dominant regular seasons professional golf has ever witnessed — 21 career PGA Tour wins, a $23 million FedExCup bonus already locked in, and an eight-shot victory at the FedEx St. Jude Championship in Memphis just weeks ago. He won the FedExCup in 2024. He is here to win it again.

The Format: No Staggered Starts

A critical distinction of this year's Tour Championship is one that separates it from earlier iterations of the playoff format: there are no staggered starts. All 30 players begin Round 1 at even par, competing on identical terms from the opening tee shot.

That means every stroke matters from the first hole. There is no built-in advantage for the top seeds and no deficit baked in for those who arrived via the BMW Championship at the edge of qualifying. The leaderboard will reflect actual performance over 72 holes of championship golf.

East Lake Golf Club, one of American golf's most storied venues and the longtime home of Bobby Jones, suits this format precisely. The Donald Ross design demands precision, patience, and the ability to manage a course that rewards smart play over athletic brute force.

The Field Around Scheffler

Scheffler will not walk into Sunday unchallenged. The 30-player field represents the entirety of elite professional golf in 2026.

Matthew Fitzpatrick, the No. 2 seed, is among the most consistent ball-strikers in the game and a past U.S. Open champion. Wyndham Clark, the BMW Championship winner and No. 3 seed, arrives with the momentum of a recent victory. Tommy Fleetwood is the defending Tour Championship champion and knows East Lake better than almost anyone in this week's field.

And then there is Rory McIlroy.

The three-time FedExCup champion — 2016, 2019, 2022 — is chasing a record that has stood untouched since the format's inception. No player has ever won four FedExCup titles. McIlroy's path here was uneven: a 66th-place finish at the FedEx St. Jude in Memphis gave way to a T3 at the BMW Championship, enough to earn his East Lake berth but not the form trajectory he would have wanted.

Whether McIlroy's best golf returns under the pressure of a historic chase is one of this week's defining storylines.

Television and Coverage

Round 1 coverage is live on ESPN and Golf Channel. The event runs through Sunday, August 30, with the FedExCup champion crowned following the final round.

Scheffler and Fitzpatrick are paired together in what figures to be the week's marquee grouping, teeing off at approximately 2 PM ET on Thursday.

What to Watch

The question at the Tour Championship is always the same and never simple: can anyone match Scheffler when he is locked in? Over the past five seasons, the answer has almost always been no. He has been the best player in professional golf by a margin that most statistical models struggle to quantify.

But East Lake has a history of producing unexpected outcomes. The no-staggered-starts format removes the safety net. And a 30-player field includes enough firepower to put genuine pressure on anyone — even the most dominant player of his era.

Round 1 is live. The FedExCup finale has begun.
