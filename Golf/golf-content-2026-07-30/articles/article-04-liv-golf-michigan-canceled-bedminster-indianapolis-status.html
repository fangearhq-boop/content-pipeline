LIV Golf Is Down to Two Events — And the CEO Can't Promise Either Will Happen

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | July 30, 2026

Michigan is officially canceled. The 2026 LIV season, which started with 14 events planned, is down to Bedminster (Aug 6-9) and Indianapolis (Aug 20-23). When asked to guarantee those survive, CEO Scott O'Neil declined to do so.

LIV Golf's 2026 schedule has contracted to its minimum. The Michigan event — a $40 million team championship that was the league's season finale — has been officially canceled, the second cancellation this year after New Orleans. The original 14-event schedule is now effectively 12, with only two events remaining before the season ends.

Those two events are Bedminster (August 6-9, $30 million) and Indianapolis (August 20-23, $30 million). Scott O'Neil, LIV Golf's CEO, was asked publicly whether he could guarantee both would take place. He said he could not.

That statement deserves some weight. This is not a spokesman hedging about weather or logistics. O'Neil is the chief executive of the organization, and when he declines to confirm that two events already on his schedule will actually happen, the financial picture behind that uncertainty is relevant context.

What the Numbers Say

LIV Golf Ltd, the league's international entity, reported a loss of £461.8 million in 2024 alone. The organization has been burning approximately $100 million per month, spending an estimated $8.50 for every $1 it earns in revenue. The Saudi Public Investment Fund — which has invested more than $5 billion in LIV since the league launched in 2022 — announced in April that it would stop providing funding at the end of the 2026 season.

Bloomberg reported in recent months that LIV has been laying the groundwork for Chapter 11 bankruptcy proceedings, with a potential relocation of headquarters to the United States as part of that process. LIV had also been seeking $250 to $350 million in outside investment. As of this week, no such investment has been publicly confirmed.

What Michigan's Cancellation Means for 2026

Michigan was not just another event on the schedule. It was the team championship — the format that distinguishes LIV from conventional individual stroke play tours. The 12 LIV Golf teams compete across the season, and Michigan was where the team champion was supposed to be crowned. With no Michigan, there will be no LIV team champion in 2026. The primary competitive format that LIV uses to differentiate itself from the PGA Tour will produce no champion this year.

Martin Kaymer, captain of Cleeks GC, had said publicly weeks ago that he gave the Michigan event a 5 percent chance of happening. He was right. The infrastructure wasn't being built, contractors weren't being paid, and the site showed no signs of preparation as the event's supposed date approached.

What Comes Next

Bedminster begins August 6. Indianapolis begins August 20. Both are on the calendar. Neither is guaranteed. If LIV completes the season with both events intact, it will have run 12 of its 14 planned tournaments in 2026 — a meaningful collapse from what was announced at the start of the year, but not a total dissolution.

The more consequential question is what happens after Indianapolis. With PIF withdrawing funding, LIV either finds new investors, files for bankruptcy, or ceases to exist in its current form. O'Neil told CNBC to "take PIF at their word" when asked about the funding cut. Whether that reflects confidence or resignation is, at this point, genuinely unclear.

What's Next

Bedminster on August 6-9 is the next LIV Golf event. Between now and then, any announcement about the league's financial structure or the status of Indianapolis will change the picture significantly. At this point, LIV Golf's end-of-season timeline is the most consequential story in professional golf outside the PGA Tour's playoff run.
