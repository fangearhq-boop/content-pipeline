Bradley Is 76th. Koepka Is 84th. Finau Is 87th. Two Events Left.

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | July 30, 2026

The FedExCup regular season ends after this week's Rocket Classic and the Wyndham Championship next week. Only the top 70 advance to the playoffs. Three significant names — Keegan Bradley, Brooks Koepka, and Tony Finau — are all outside that line with 1,000 points left to play for.

The FedExCup Playoffs begin August 13 at TPC Southwind. Before that, the top 70 players in the points standings need to be identified. As of this week, three players who would normally expect to be in a different conversation are fighting to make that number.

Keegan Bradley, the US Ryder Cup captain, is 76th. Brooks Koepka, a four-time major champion, is 84th. Tony Finau is 87th. None of them are in positions that reflect their career standing in the game. All of them have two events — the Rocket Classic (this week) and the Wyndham Championship (Aug 6-9 in Greensboro) — to climb into the top 70.

Combined available points across both remaining events: 1,000.

The Bubble Line

Jackson Koivun is sitting at exactly 70th after his 3M Open victory last week gave him 500 FedExCup points. Koivun's win moved him off the outside of the bubble and onto its edge — which is precarious in its own way. If Koivun has a poor week at the Rocket Classic and one of the players behind him performs well, the line shifts. The bubble is not a wall; it moves with every round played.

Jordan Spieth is approximately 55th, which places him safely inside the top-70 cut for FedEx St. Jude but on the edge of a different bubble — the top-50 threshold that determines who advances to the BMW Championship at Bellerive. The top 30 from there advance to the Tour Championship at East Lake, where the FedExCup title is decided. Every tier narrows and the stakes at each one compound.

What Bradley, Koepka, and Finau Need

Five hundred points are available at the Rocket Classic. Five hundred more at Wyndham. A tournament win at either event would provide enough points that any of the three could vault from outside the 70 into a position safe enough to survive the second event without needing to perform heroically. But none of them have made the kind of sustained consistent runs this season that would suggest a win is likely.

The more realistic path: enough top-10 or top-15 finishes at both events to accumulate points gradually. The problem is that the players directly ahead of them on the points list — players in the 65-70 range — will also be competing this week. A player in 67th who finishes in the top 20 at the Rocket Classic makes the chase harder for everyone below them.

The Stakes Beyond the Points

For Koepka and Bradley in particular, there is a reputational dimension to the situation. Koepka is a four-time major champion who finished runner-up at Augusta this year. A missed playoff would be the kind of season-ending footnote that follows a player into the offseason conversations about priorities and preparation. Bradley, similarly, has enough profile to make 76th feel like a story — and he has chosen to play in Detroit this week, which suggests he takes that story seriously.

The Rocket Classic and Wyndham are not glamour events. They close the regular season after the major cycle is over and the biggest names are either resting or preparing for playoffs. But for the players fighting to stay in the tournament series, they are as important as any event all year.

What's Next

The Rocket Classic concludes Sunday, August 2. The FedExCup standings will update after each round. The Wyndham Championship begins August 6 — the final opportunity for players outside the top 70 to change their playoff status. The top-70 field for FedEx St. Jude at TPC Southwind locks in after Wyndham ends August 9.
