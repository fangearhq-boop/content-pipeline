Scottie Scheffler Isn't in Detroit This Week. He's Been Winless Since January. He's Still No. 1.

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | July 30, 2026

Scheffler won the American Express in January — his 20th career title. Since then: four major near-misses, five runner-up finishes, a missed cut at the Scottish Open, and still the World No. 1 and FedExCup No. 1. This week he's skipping the Rocket Classic to rest before the playoffs.

The last time Scottie Scheffler won a professional tournament was January 2026 — The American Express, his 20th career PGA Tour victory. He hasn't won since. He went to all four major championships and came close enough in each to generate legitimate tension at the finish, but didn't win any of them. He has five runner-up finishes on the season. He missed the cut at the Scottish Open the week before The Open Championship — his first missed cut in four years.

He is still the World No. 1. He is still the FedExCup No. 1. And this week, he is not at Detroit Golf Club for the Rocket Classic.

Scheffler has elected to rest before the FedExCup Playoffs begin on August 13 at TPC Southwind in Memphis. Rory McIlroy — who won the Masters in April, Scheffler's most painful near-miss — is making the same decision. Two players at the top of the world rankings have looked at the last two regular-season events and concluded that their time is better spent preparing for what comes after.

A Year That Defies Easy Description

Scheffler's 2026 is genuinely difficult to categorize. He won only once in a year when he has been the best player in nearly every tournament field he entered. He finished one shot behind McIlroy at Augusta after a difficult Friday. He came up short at Shinnecock Hills. He tied Tommy Fleetwood for fourth at Royal Birkdale with a 7-under total that would have won in most Opens. He faded over the weekend at Aronimink in the PGA Championship.

In golf, there is a word for the experience of playing well enough to win and not winning: unlucky. But applied consistently over four majors and an entire season, unlucky eventually starts to look like something else — a small gap between what a player can do and what winning requires. Whether Scheffler has closed that gap or is still looking for it is the question his season hasn't yet answered.

What the Points Say

The FedExCup points system rewards consistency, and Scheffler has been almost absurdly consistent. His five runner-up finishes and handful of top-5 results across the regular season have accumulated enough points that no one has overtaken him in the standings despite him winning only once. He is the FedExCup No. 1 heading into the playoffs, which means he enters the Tour Championship at East Lake with the best possible starting position when the staggered-scoring playoff format kicks in.

That structure rewards the regular season's best performer — which is, by any honest accounting, Scottie Scheffler. The playoffs will be the test of whether being best in points translates into best in execution when one major event determines the title.

Why the Rest Makes Sense

The Rocket Classic and the Wyndham Championship are not events that Scheffler needs for FedExCup positioning. He is No. 1 in the standings with enough points that nothing short of a catastrophic series of missed cuts would move him out of the top tier. The more sensible use of his next two weeks is preparation and recovery — a chance to sharpen his game for a three-event sprint that runs from August 13 through August 30.

A missed cut at the Scottish Open was unusual for Scheffler. He came to Royal Birkdale a week later and nearly contended. The pattern suggests that fatigue is a variable in his performance in a way it hasn't been historically. The decision to skip Detroit is consistent with a player who has looked at the remainder of his year and decided that the events that matter most to him are the ones that haven't happened yet.

What's Next

Scheffler's next expected start is the FedEx St. Jude Championship at TPC Southwind on August 13. From there, the BMW Championship and the Tour Championship follow in consecutive weeks. If he wins the FedExCup, his 2026 will be remembered as a dominant year under an unusual set of circumstances. If he comes close again without winning, the conversation about whether he can close in the biggest moments will continue into 2027.
