The Final Rocket Classic Is Playing on a Course That Didn't Exist Last Year

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | July 30, 2026

Detroit Golf Club spent $16.1 million on a restoration that changed almost everything: the par, the length, the bunkering, the green shapes. Round 1 of the last-ever Rocket Classic begins on a fundamentally different test — with Cameron Young as the favorite and the field's two biggest names watching from home.

The final Rocket Classic began Thursday at a course the players have never actually played. That sounds like an exaggeration until you list the changes: Detroit Golf Club converted its par from 72 to 70. It added 300 yards to the scorecard. Holes 7 and 17 were converted from par 5s into long par 4s — one playing 505 yards, the other 537. The total number of bunkers increased from 84 to 91. Every green was rebuilt to match the original 1914 design by Donald Ross. The large pond near the 14th hole was removed.

The $16.1 million restoration wasn't a renovation in the traditional sense — it was a return. The club commissioned Tyler Rae to take Detroit Golf Club back to Ross's original intent, which had been modified, simplified, and worn away over more than a century of use. The result is a course that is simultaneously older and newer than anything the PGA Tour has played here since the Rocket Classic began in 2019.

What Changes on the Scorecard

The move to par 70 is the most visible change. In the previous format, holes 7 and 17 were legitimate par 5 birdie opportunities — places where a long hitter could reach in two and make the round. Now both holes demand a full second shot approach into Ross-style greens surrounded by the restored original bunkering. PGA Tour power rankings analysts noted before the tournament that distance remains the primary factor, but the added length and narrower margins around the greens have reduced the scoring floor relative to prior Rocket Classic editions.

The restored bunkers have sharper faces and are distributed in the positions Ross originally intended — not where decades of course management had gradually relocated them. Players who studied the old Detroit Golf Club through video or statistical packages built on prior Rocket Classic data are working with incomplete information this week.

The Field Without Scheffler or McIlroy

Scottie Scheffler and Rory McIlroy are both absent from Detroit this week, having elected to rest before the FedExCup Playoffs begin on August 13. Their absence opens the field in a meaningful way. Cameron Young — who has already won The Players Championship and the Cadillac Championship in 2026 — enters as the clear favorite. A third win of the season for Young would represent a genuine statement entering the playoff stretch.

Aldrich Potgieter defends the title he won at age 20 in 2025 — a victory that established him as one of the more compelling young players on the Tour. Jackson Koivun is also in the field, returning just one week after winning the 3M Open in his third career professional start. Keegan Bradley, Brooks Koepka, and Tony Finau are all here as well, on the wrong side of the FedExCup playoff bubble and needing significant points to save their seasons.

The Last Time at This Address

Rocket Companies is exiting its sponsorship after 2026, which makes this the final Rocket Classic regardless of format or course changes. The tournament has been played here since 2019, making it one of the newer stops on the PGA Tour calendar. Its departure will leave a gap in the Michigan summer sporting calendar that has been filled since 2019 with a genuine major-adjacent event drawing national-level field quality.

Whether the venue finds a new sponsor or whether Detroit Golf Club's renovation ultimately supports another Tour event is unknown. What is known: the players teeing it up Thursday are playing the final chapter of this tournament's short history — on a course that has never hosted a professional event in its restored form.

What's Next

Round 1 results will indicate how the restored course plays under competition conditions. The cut comes after 36 holes on Friday. Final round Sunday, August 2. For the players fighting for FedExCup playoff position, every shot this week carries weight that extends well beyond the $10 million purse and into what comes after.
