Korda, Ryu, and Ko Tee Off Together — History Is One Win Away at Royal Lytham

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | July 30, 2026

Round 1 of the AIG Women's Open is underway at Royal Lytham & St Annes. The marquee group — Nelly Korda, Haeran Ryu, and Lydia Ko — teed off at 8:42 AM. One more major win for either Korda or Ryu ties Inbee Park's 2013 record.

The 2026 women's major season has been defined by a remarkable coincidence: two players have split the four biggest titles in the sport with exact symmetry. Nelly Korda won the Chevron Championship and the US Women's Open. Haeran Ryu won the KPMG Women's PGA Championship and the Evian Championship. Four majors, two champions, no overlap.

On Thursday morning at Royal Lytham & St Annes, those two players walked to the same tee box for Round 1 of the AIG Women's Open. Alongside them: Lydia Ko, the 2024 AIG Women's Open champion. It is the kind of grouping that tournament organizers imagine and players try not to overthink — and the stakes attached to it are genuine historical weight.

One more major win for either Korda or Ryu would give them three in a single calendar year. The record is held by Inbee Park, who won the ANA Inspiration, the US Women's Open, and the Women's British Open in 2013. No player has matched it since. Two players have a legitimate chance to do so in the span of a single tournament this week.

Korda's Position

Korda enters Royal Lytham as the world No. 1 and the betting favorite. Her 2026 major season has been exceptional: she won the Chevron in April and the US Women's Open in June, establishing herself as the dominant force in women's golf entering the summer. She has four career majors. A win at Lytham would give her five — and make her just the second player since Park in 2013 to win three women's majors in a year.

She is also chasing the career Grand Slam, a victory at every major championship. The AIG Women's Open is the one she has not yet won. The motivation is compounded: historical milestone, personal milestone, and outright domination of a season that has already been exceptional.

Ryu's Position

Haeran Ryu arrived at Lytham on a trajectory that might be even more surprising. At the start of 2026, few analysts ranked her among the favorites to win multiple majors. She won the KPMG in May and then the Evian in July, inserting herself into the Park conversation at the same moment Korda was tracking it. The Evian win, in particular, gave her the necessary credentials: she is not riding momentum into this tournament, she is entering it as a proven major champion for the second time in a summer.

The parallels between the two players' 2026 seasons are almost symmetrical enough to seem scripted. Which makes the coincidence of their Round 1 grouping feel less like randomness and more like the season demanding an audience for whatever is about to happen.

The Course

Royal Lytham & St Annes is one of the most demanding links courses on the women's major rotation. It plays at par 71 with 174 bunkers — a number that rewards position over power and punishes any hint of carelessness off the tee. The coastal setting means wind is a constant variable. A player with a lead on a calm afternoon can watch it evaporate in the same conditions that looked gentle two hours earlier.

The $10 million purse is a record for the women's game. The field of 144 includes every significant name in women's golf. But for the next four days, the conversation will begin and end with two players: the ones who've shared everything and still have one more thing to decide.

What's Next

Round 1 results will set the early narrative at Lytham. With Korda and Ryu in the same group through the first two rounds, there will be no ambiguity about who is outplaying whom when the first cut comes. Round 2 follows Friday, with the cut after 36 holes. Final round Sunday, August 2.

The women's major season has been building toward this all year. Whether the record falls this week — or survives intact for another year — the AIG Women's Open at Royal Lytham is exactly the right stage for the question to be answered.
