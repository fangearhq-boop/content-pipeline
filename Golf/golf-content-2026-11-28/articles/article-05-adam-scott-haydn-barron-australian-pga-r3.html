Adam Scott at 9-Under, Haydn Barron Still in the Top Five: The Australian PGA's Supporting Storylines Heading Into Sunday

By Ryan Calloway

November 28, 2026

The BMW Australian PGA Championship enters its final round with Nicolai Von Dellingshausen controlling the leaderboard at 16-under, but the event's most compelling human stories belong to two players not positioned to win the title. Adam Scott is at 9-under par through 54 holes at The Lakes in Sydney, well positioned for a top-ten finish that carries meaning regardless of where it lands in the final standings. Haydn Barron, an amateur, is at 11-under and has remained inside the top five of the professional DP World Tour field across all three rounds.

Adam Scott at The Lakes

Scott's presence at the BMW Australian PGA Championship is itself a commitment to the Australian summer circuit that he has maintained consistently throughout his career. At 9-under through three rounds, he is seven shots behind Von Dellingshausen — not in a position to win, but in a position to finish high in the standings of one of the most prestigious events on the DP World Tour calendar.

What makes Scott's performance notable in context is what it represents: a former world No. 1 who won the Masters in 2013, still competing at a high level in front of his home crowd, still producing 9-under totals across three rounds of a professional event. His participation in the Australian PGA and the Australian Open next week at Kingston Heath are fixtures in the domestic calendar that Australian golf fans anticipate. He brings credibility and history to events that benefit from both.

Haydn Barron's Amateur Week

Barron's story at the BMW Australian PGA Championship is unusual by any measure. The amateur arrived at The Lakes as a lesser-known name in a professional field and immediately announced himself by shooting 8-under par in the opening round — leading the event outright ahead of the entire professional contingent after day one. The DP World Tour field includes ranked professionals with careers built on managing exactly this kind of pressure, and Barron handled the overnight lead as well as anyone could have expected.

Through round 3, he is at 11-under. That is two shots better than Adam Scott and one shot better than Cameron Smith. For an amateur to maintain a top-five position across 54 holes of a professional co-sanctioned event is a genuine competitive result, not a first-round anomaly. Barron's week at The Lakes will be one of the data points cited in the coming years when discussing the depth and standard of Australian amateur golf.

What's Next

Both Scott and Barron play the final round on Sunday, November 29. Scott's finish here will inform his preparation heading into the Australian Open at Kingston Heath (December 3-6), where he is part of a field also featuring Rory McIlroy, Cameron Smith, and Min Woo Lee. Barron, as an amateur, will carry the results of this week into whatever competitive plans he has ahead — a performance of this caliber in a professional field is the kind of result that opens doors.
