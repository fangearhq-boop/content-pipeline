Australian Open Preview: McIlroy Headlines Kingston Heath Field for Dec. 3-6 Stonehaven Cup

By Marcus Cole

November 28, 2026

The 2026 Australian Open is set for December 3-6 at Kingston Heath Golf Club in Melbourne, returning one of golf's great national championships to the sandbelt for the first time since 1990. Rory McIlroy headlines a field that includes Cameron Smith, Adam Scott, Min Woo Lee, Sahith Theegala, and defending champion Rasmus Neergaard-Petersen — a lineup that makes next week one of the strongest standalone events in world golf during the Australian summer.

The Course

Kingston Heath is one of Australia's most celebrated sandbelt courses, sitting alongside Royal Melbourne and Metropolitan in the cluster of Melbourne parkland layouts that form one of the great concentrations of classic golf architecture anywhere in the world. The course last hosted the Australian Open in 1990 and has since remained on the rotation of venues that the Golf Australia selection committee returns to for major events. Sandbelt courses test shotmaking and course management in ways that reward players who can shape the ball and control trajectory — characteristics that tend to suit elite ball-strikers who can also manage the short game demands of firm, fast greens.

The Field

McIlroy is the most prominent international player in the field and arrives chasing his second Stonehaven Cup. His game travels well to Australian conditions and sandbelt layouts, which reward the kind of controlled ball-striking that defines his best form. Smith and Scott anchor the Australian contingent — both have competed in enough Australian Opens to know what Kingston Heath requires, and both carry the additional motivation of competing in front of a home crowd at a venue that holds historical significance.

Min Woo Lee, the Western Australian who has become one of the most popular players in Australian golf, adds another home-country angle. Theegala represents the American contingent and has shown the ability to perform in international events away from the PGA Tour schedule. Neergaard-Petersen, the defending champion, brings the experience of winning this event and will start with that advantage in a field that respects historical form.

What's Next

The Australian Open follows immediately after the BMW Australian PGA Championship, which concludes Sunday at The Lakes in Sydney. Players in both fields — including Smith, Scott, and others — transition directly from one event to the next with minimal gap. Kingston Heath will host the first round on Wednesday, December 3, with the final round scheduled for Saturday, December 6. The Stonehaven Cup is one of the oldest national open trophies in world golf, and December 2026 brings a field capable of producing a memorable winner.
