PGA Tour Hires Brian Rolapp as Its First CEO: What the New Role Means as Jay Monahan Exits at Year-End

By Jake Torres

November 28, 2026

The PGA Tour is adding a CEO for the first time in its history. Brian Rolapp, who previously served as a senior executive at the NFL, has been hired as the Tour's first chief executive officer — a newly created position designed to separate commercial and business leadership from the governance functions traditionally held by the Commissioner. Jay Monahan, who has served as PGA Tour Commissioner since 2017, is stepping down at the end of 2026 when his contract expires.

What the CEO Role Does

The PGA Tour has historically concentrated authority in the Commissioner role, which handles both Tour governance and business operations. The creation of a CEO position signals an intent to separate those functions — placing commercial leadership, media rights, sponsorship and business strategy under a dedicated executive while keeping governance, player relations and rules oversight under a separate Commissioner role.

Rolapp brings a background in professional sports business from his time at the NFL, where he was involved in NFL Network and NFL Media operations. His experience in sports media rights and commercial partnerships is relevant to the PGA Tour's immediate priorities: its broadcast agreements, digital distribution strategy, and international expansion plans all require sustained executive attention that the Commissioner role, with its governance obligations, cannot fully provide.

Monahan's Tenure

Jay Monahan's nine years as Commissioner were defined by periods of transformation and turbulence. The LIV Golf disruption — funded by Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund — split the professional golf world and drew Monahan into a years-long public dispute with players who defected and the organization that funded them. The framework agreement discussions between the PGA Tour and PIF, which were announced in 2023, produced extended negotiations that were ultimately not resolved during Monahan's tenure.

LIV Golf wound down in 2026 following PIF's withdrawal of backing, and the professional golf landscape has stabilized — but the period left the PGA Tour with questions about its structure, governance, and commercial strategy that the new CEO hire is intended to address.

What Comes Next

The PGA Tour has not yet named a successor to Monahan's Commissioner role, nor clarified whether the Commissioner function will continue in its current form or be restructured. Rolapp is expected to begin in his CEO role in 2027. The new structure — a CEO handling business operations alongside a separate governance Commissioner — mirrors how some other major sports organizations operate, though professional golf has distinct characteristics that make direct comparisons imperfect.

For players, sponsors, and broadcasters, the most immediate question is continuity: how the Tour operates during the transition period and what priorities the new executive structure sets for 2027 and beyond.
