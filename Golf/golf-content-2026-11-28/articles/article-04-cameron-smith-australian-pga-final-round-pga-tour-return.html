Cameron Smith at 10-Under Entering Australian PGA Final Round: What Sunday Means for His PGA Tour Return

By Jake Torres

November 28, 2026

Cameron Smith is at 10-under par through three rounds of the BMW Australian PGA Championship, sitting six shots behind leader Nicolai Von Dellingshausen as the field prepares for Sunday's final round at The Lakes in Sydney. The four-time Australian PGA champion surged into contention with a second-round 64 that included an eagle, then held his position through round 3 without being able to close the gap on the German leader. Sunday represents his clearest remaining opportunity to impact a result that carries significant implications for his path back to the PGA Tour.

The Route Back to the PGA Tour

Smith left the PGA Tour when he joined LIV Golf, the Saudi-backed breakaway circuit that disrupted professional golf for several years before winding down in 2026 following PIF's withdrawal of funding. His return to international competition through the DP World Tour — the BMW Australian PGA Championship is a co-sanctioned DP World Tour event — is a deliberate first step in a reintegration process that requires results, not just appearances.

To return to the PGA Tour, Smith needs either a finish in the top of the DP World Tour order of merit or a win in an event that carries a PGA Tour card as part of the prize package. The Australian PGA and the Australian Open both have pathways in that direction. A win at The Lakes on Sunday would not automatically restore his PGA Tour card, but it would establish him as a competitive presence on the DP World Tour and create momentum heading into the Australian Open at Kingston Heath next week.

Sunday's Task

Six shots in a final round is a significant deficit. Von Dellingshausen has been consistent throughout this event and enters Sunday having led for two consecutive days. For Smith to win, he would need a low round — something in the range of 64 or 65 — combined with Von Dellingshausen failing to match his usual scoring pace. That combination is possible but not probable.

What is more achievable is a top-five finish that demonstrates to the DP World Tour and the broader golfing world that Smith's competitive level remains high. His second-round 64 was the kind of performance that shows he can still produce elite scoring when the game is on. A similar round on Sunday, even without winning, would reinforce the narrative of his comeback and set a strong foundation for the Australian Open.

What's Next

Smith plays in the final round of the BMW Australian PGA Championship on Sunday, November 29, at The Lakes. The Australian Open at Kingston Heath follows on December 3. Both events are part of the Australian summer stretch that Smith and the domestic contingent have circled as the core of their competitive schedules heading into 2027.
