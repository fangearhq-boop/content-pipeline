LIV Golf 2.0: BC Partners Leading $250-350M Deal, Players to Become Majority Owners

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | August 9, 2026

The future of LIV Golf is no longer theoretical. Bloomberg and the Financial Times reported on August 5 that BC Partners' credit arm is leading a financing deal worth $250 million to $350 million that would fund the league's continuation through a structural reinvention — LIV 2.0 — beyond Saudi Arabia's involvement.

LIV's CEO confirmed the broad outlines in statements to Golf Digest: a lead investor deal had been "signed by the investor and approved by the board," with more than 12 additional minority investor parties in discussions. The September 2026 close date would place the deal within months of the Saudi Public Investment Fund's planned exit at year-end.

Why the Structure Is Changing

PIF announced in April that it would stop funding LIV Golf at the end of 2026. The sovereign wealth fund has invested approximately $5 billion in the league since its 2022 launch — a figure that represents an extraordinary level of financial commitment to a sports project that has yet to generate revenue at a scale that justifies the outlay.

The exit was not a surprise to those inside the league. Saudi Arabia's Vision 2030 investment priorities have shifted, and the strategic value of continuing to bankroll a golf league that remains locked out of a full merger with the PGA Tour has diminished. PIF got its disruption. What comes next is not Saudi Arabia's problem to solve.

BC Partners' involvement reframes LIV from a government-backed project into something resembling a more conventional private equity play — one where the return on investment depends on league viability, broadcast rights, and eventual monetization of a sport that still commands massive global audiences.

Players as Majority Owners

The most structurally significant element of LIV 2.0 is the equity arrangement. Under the new deal, players would become majority equity holders in the league. Tournament purses will decrease — meaning players will earn less week-to-week than they did under PIF's generous funding — but their compensation shifts toward equity in the enterprise itself.

This model is not unique in professional sports; athlete equity in leagues has precedents, particularly in newer leagues looking to attract talent without matching established competitors' cash. But in golf, where independent contractors have spent decades fighting for tournament purse increases, becoming majority owners of the enterprise that employs them represents a genuine structural departure.

The Financial Times reported that BC Partners' investment is contingent on LIV retaining enough star players. That clause creates a feedback loop: players decide whether to stay partly based on the deal's viability, and the deal's viability depends partly on whether players stay.

The 2027 Season

Ten events are planned for LIV's 2027 season. That is a reduction from recent schedules, which reflects both the lower purse model and the realities of building a sustainable calendar without unlimited funding. Whether the events will remain at the high-profile venues that defined LIV's early identity — or shift to more commercially viable alternatives — will depend on the final shape of the deal.

The September close is now the most important date in golf business for the remainder of 2026. If BC Partners and LIV complete the financing on schedule, the league enters 2027 as a restructured, player-owned enterprise. If the deal falls apart — if BC Partners walks, or if too many star players choose to return to the PGA Tour — the league faces an existential question that $5 billion in Saudi funding was never going to answer forever.

What's Next

The September 2026 close date is the next milestone. Before that, LIV Golf New York's final round plays out Sunday at Trump National Golf Club in Bedminster, New Jersey — a tournament that proceeds entirely independent of the business negotiations but that will be watched carefully as a barometer of the league's on-course product. Joaquin Niemann leads at -14. The golf continues regardless of what the boardrooms decide.
