FedExCup Playoffs Begin Thursday: McIlroy Chases Historic Fourth Title in Memphis

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | August 9, 2026

The PGA Tour regular season ends Sunday at the Wyndham Championship. When the final putt drops in Greensboro, the FedExCup Playoffs officially begin — and the most compelling stretch of the 2026 golf calendar gets underway.

First stop: TPC Southwind in Memphis, Tennessee, for the FedEx St. Jude Championship. The event runs August 12-16, 2026, with a $20 million purse and 750 FedExCup points on the line. Seventy players — the top 70 in FedExCup points after the Wyndham Championship concludes tonight — will tee it up Thursday in what amounts to the first true elimination event of the year.

McIlroy and the Record That Keeps Growing

Rory McIlroy headlines the St. Jude field, and the narrative attached to his appearance in Memphis is unlike anything the FedExCup has produced before.

McIlroy has won the FedExCup three times. Three is already the all-time record — he holds it alone. A fourth title would not merely add to the record; it would push the standard to a place that may be unreachable for anyone else competing today.

The FedExCup was introduced in 2007. In the 19 seasons since, no one came close to three titles until McIlroy did it. The idea that someone could win four in a modern era of unprecedented global competition speaks to the kind of sustained excellence that defines the very short list of all-time greats.

McIlroy's 2026 season sets him up for the run. He arrives in Memphis healthy and motivated, competing in a three-event sprint that rewards the players who peak at exactly the right moment.

The Field That Will Compete

Scottie Scheffler enters as the No. 1 seed, bringing 4,123 FedExCup points accumulated across a 2026 season that has been simultaneously dominant and frustrating. Scheffler is the world's best player by every metric — ranking, points total, major near-misses — and yet he hasn't won since The American Express in January. The playoffs represent his clearest opportunity to correct that.

Tommy Fleetwood, the defending FedExCup champion, is in the field. Fleetwood won the 2025 FedExCup in a statement run through the playoffs that established him as one of the most dangerous players in the world when the pressure builds.

Justin Rose, the defending St. Jude champion, returns to TPC Southwind with a title to defend. Rose won Memphis in 2025 in a performance that reminded the golf world that experience and precision can still beat power on the right course setup.

The Playoff Structure

The FedExCup Playoffs operate on a straightforward elimination structure that rewards consistency across all three events while placing maximum premium on performing when the field is thinnest.

After Memphis, the top 50 advance to the BMW Championship at Bellerive Country Club in St. Louis, running August 20-23. Twenty players are cut from the playoff field at that threshold.

The top 30 after Bellerive advance to the Tour Championship at East Lake Golf Club in Atlanta, running August 27-30. The Tour Championship uses a staggered starting-score format, meaning the FedExCup standings coming into Atlanta directly influence a player's position at the start of the final event.

The FedExCup champion is crowned at East Lake. For McIlroy, Scheffler, Fleetwood, or any of the 70 players heading to Memphis on Thursday, that trophy is three weeks away.

What's Next

The Wyndham Championship final round wraps Sunday in Greensboro, locking the 70-player field for Memphis. First-round tee times for the FedEx St. Jude Championship begin Thursday, August 13. TPC Southwind has hosted the event since 2019 and plays as one of the more demanding courses on the playoff rotation — a track that tends to separate the contenders from the pretenders before the tournament reaches the weekend.
