Scheffler's Season in Full: Five Runner-Ups, Zero Wins, and a Streak That Just Snapped

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | August 9, 2026

Scottie Scheffler is the best golfer in the world. He has been for the better part of three years. His World No. 1 ranking — now in its 201st cumulative week, 167th week overall in his current reign — is not a product of momentum or narrative. It is a product of relentless, grinding excellence that manifests itself in almost every tournament he enters.

And yet the 2026 PGA Tour season has produced one of golf's most unusual storylines: Scheffler is so good that his extraordinary year feels like a near-failure because it doesn't include a win after January.

The Mathematics of a Difficult Year

Scheffler won The American Express in January — his 20th career PGA Tour victory. What followed in the months since has been a collection of finishes that would define the season of virtually any other player in the world as a breakout success.

Masters: runner-up. RBC Heritage: runner-up. Cadillac Championship: runner-up. Travelers Championship: runner-up. 3M Open: runner-up.

Five times in 2026 — after January — Scheffler has stood at the top of a leaderboard or within a shot of it on Sunday and come home second. No player has been more consistently excellent while simultaneously coming up short of the win.

Scheffler acknowledged the frustration after one of those runner-ups. "I've been really close to winning some tournaments, and that can be frustrating," he said. The candor was notable — Scheffler is not typically a player who shows the weight of unmet expectations publicly. That he said it out loud spoke to how much the near-misses have accumulated.

The Streak That Defined and Then Ended His Season

Alongside the five runner-ups was something even more remarkable: a streak of 35 consecutive top-25 finishes. Across more than a full calendar year, Scheffler did not finish outside the top 25 in a single event he played. That is not a hot stretch. That is sustained, compounding excellence that borders on the unprecedented in modern professional golf.

The streak ended at the Genesis Scottish Open. Scheffler missed the cut — his first missed cut in four years. Golf Channel described it as ending a "crazy streak." The superlative was not wrong. Thirty-five consecutive top-25 finishes is not something the record books see often, and its end — regardless of context, regardless of travel schedule or course fit — added a new layer to the season's narrative heading into the playoffs.

Scheffler still leads the FedExCup with 4,123 points. His season, objectively measured, is a success. But success in professional golf is measured ultimately by wins, and the most dominant player in the world hasn't won since January.

What the Playoffs Mean

The FedExCup Playoffs, beginning Thursday at TPC Southwind in Memphis, represent Scheffler's best opportunity to rewrite the story before 2026 ends. He enters as the No. 1 seed — which means he carries a structural advantage into the Tour Championship if he makes it that far. The format rewards season-long dominance in a way that could translate directly into a title even if someone else shoots a lower score at East Lake in the final week.

But winning one of the three playoff events — Memphis, Bellerive, or East Lake — would do more than earn FedExCup points. It would answer the question that has hovered over his 2026 season since January: can Scheffler, the best player in the world, win when everyone is watching and the stakes are highest?

What's Next

Scheffler tees it up Thursday at the FedEx St. Jude Championship at TPC Southwind in Memphis as the No. 1 seed and the heavy favorite. Three events remain in the 2026 season. If this year teaches us anything about Scheffler, it is that close is not the same as done — and the playoffs have a way of clarifying things that the regular season leaves unresolved.
