#### Long-Form Post

The PGA Tour regular season wraps up tonight at Wyndham — and when the final putt drops in Greensboro, the FedExCup Playoffs officially begin.

First stop: TPC Southwind in Memphis, Tennessee, for the FedEx St. Jude Championship. The tournament runs August 12-16, 2026, with a $20 million purse and 750 FedExCup points on the line.

Rory McIlroy headlines the 70-player field. He arrives in Memphis chasing something unprecedented: a fourth FedExCup title, which would extend the all-time record he already holds. McIlroy has won the FedExCup three times, and three is already a record shared by no one else. A fourth would be a statement about longevity and consistency that the game has never seen.

Scottie Scheffler enters as the No. 1 seed with 4,123 points — a commanding lead that reflects his dominant 2026 season even without a victory since January. Tommy Fleetwood, the defending FedExCup champion, is in the field. So is Justin Rose, the defending St. Jude champion.

After Memphis, the top 50 advance to the BMW Championship at Bellerive Country Club in St. Louis (August 20-23). The top 30 from there head to the Tour Championship at East Lake in Atlanta (August 27-30).

The stakes escalate with every cut. But it starts Thursday in Memphis.

### STORY 3: Scottie Scheffler Winless Streak

#### Long-Form Post

There is no more fascinating storyline in golf right now than Scottie Scheffler's 2026 season.

Scheffler is World No. 1. He is the FedExCup points leader with 4,123 points — a comfortable margin entering the playoffs. He has been, by virtually every measure, the best player on the planet for the better part of three years.

And yet. He hasn't won since The American Express in January. That was his 20th career PGA Tour victory. Since then: five runner-up finishes. Masters. RBC Heritage. Cadillac Championship. Travelers Championship. 3M Open. Five times he's been the last man standing — second.

The Scottish Open added a fresh wrinkle. Scheffler missed the cut, ending a remarkable 35-consecutive top-25 finishes streak. Thirty-five starts without once finishing outside the top 25. That's not a slump — that's historic consistency. But it snapped, and it added a new layer to the narrative heading into the most important stretch of the season.

Scheffler himself has acknowledged the frustration. "I've been really close to winning some tournaments, and that can be frustrating," he said. Close isn't the same as winning.

The FedExCup Playoffs, beginning Thursday in Memphis, give Scheffler a chance to rewrite the story. He'll enter every event as the favorite. The question the golf world is asking: when does "close" become a win?

### STORY 4: LIV Golf 2.0 — BC Partners Deal

#### Long-Form Post

The future of LIV Golf is coming into focus — and it looks significantly different from what launched in 2022.

Bloomberg and the Financial Times reported on August 5 that BC Partners' credit arm is leading a $250 million to $350 million financing deal that would fund LIV's continuation as a restructured league: "LIV 2.0." The deal is expected to close in September 2026.

The structural changes are significant. Players will become majority equity holders in LIV 2.0 — a shift from the current model where Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund has bankrolled everything. Tournament purses will decrease, with player compensation shifting toward equity stakes in the league itself. LIV's CEO stated the lead investor deal had been "signed by the investor and approved by the board," and that more than 12 additional minority investor parties are in discussions.

The Saudi exit was expected. PIF announced in April that it would stop funding LIV at the end of 2026, having invested approximately $5 billion since 2022. BC Partners' involvement — if the deal closes — means the league survives but evolves into something more resembling a player-owned venture.

Ten events are planned for the 2027 season. Whether enough star players remain committed to their LIV contracts when the financial structure shifts will likely determine how compelling the league looks next year.

The September close date makes this one of the most significant business decisions in golf heading into the fall.

Two concurrent final rounds, two different worlds, one Sunday: the PGA Tour fighting for FedExCup playoff lives at Sedgefield, and LIV Golf chasing individual and team glory in New Jersey. For golf fans, it's one of the busier Sundays of the summer.
