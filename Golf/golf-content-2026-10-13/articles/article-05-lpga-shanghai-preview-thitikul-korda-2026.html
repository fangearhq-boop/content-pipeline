Thitikul Takes Four Wins Into Shanghai — The LPGA's Asian Swing Begins Wednesday

By Jake Torres | October 13, 2026 | Golf Fanrecap

Atthaya Thitikul won the Lotte Championship last week at Hoakalei Country Club in Hawaii. It was her fourth LPGA Tour title of 2026. Nelly Korda, entering that event with four wins herself, now shares that total. They are even.

The Buick LPGA Shanghai opens on Wednesday, October 15, as the first event of the LPGA's Asian swing — and the first direct opportunity this season for either player to separate herself from the other.

The Race at the Top

No player has dominated women's golf in 2026 the way Thitikul and Korda have approached the season in parallel. Thitikul's run began in Hawaii, where she won the Lotte Championship with a dramatic close — birdie-birdie on 17 and 18 after driving into the water on 16. Korda's four victories were spread across the calendar with consistent, high-pressure performances on varied courses.

What separates them at this point is style, not results. Thitikul's game leans on aggressive shot-making and an ability to recover when the course pushes back. Korda's strength is consistency — she rarely misses fairways, rarely makes the kind of mistakes that cost players tournaments, and accumulates birdies over the course of 72 holes through precision rather than flair.

At the Buick LPGA Shanghai, which typically rewards ball-striking and course management in equal measure, both players enter with legitimate claims to the title.

What a Fifth Win Would Mean

For Thitikul, a fifth LPGA win in 2026 would surpass Ariya Jutanugarn's all-time record for the most career LPGA victories by a Thai golfer and put her 2026 season in the conversation with the best single-season performances in recent tour history. Five wins in a season is a benchmark that places a player among the elite of any given era.

For Korda, a fifth win would represent a statement of equivalence — matching or surpassing whatever Thitikul produces — in a season that, from the outside, has looked like a genuine head-to-head competition for the title of best women's golfer in the world.

The Asian Swing and CME Globe

After Shanghai, the LPGA continues its Asian swing with the BMW Ladies Championship (October 22-25) and the Maybank Championship (October 29 to November 1). All three events carry CME Globe points that factor into seeding and performance bonuses for the season-ending CME Group Tour Championship. With the year's final stretch now underway, the pressure of the points race compounds the significance of each event.

What's Next

The Buick LPGA Shanghai begins Wednesday, October 15. Tee times and the full field have not been officially announced as of this writing, but Thitikul, Korda, and the full strength of the LPGA Tour are expected to compete. After Shanghai, the season's final run of events will determine both the winner of the 2026 tour season and the shape of the CME Globe standings heading into the season finale. The race has no clear leader. That is what makes these next few weeks worth watching.
