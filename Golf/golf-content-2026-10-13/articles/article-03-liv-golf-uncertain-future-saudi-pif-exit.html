After Indianapolis, LIV Golf's 54 Players Wait to See If There Is a 2027

By Marcus Cole | October 13, 2026 | Golf Fanrecap

The 2026 LIV Golf season is over. Michael La Sasso won the season finale at Indianapolis. Jon Rahm finished runner-up with a 63. The team competition is settled, the points table is closed, and the league's fourth season has officially concluded.

Now, the 54 full-time players on the LIV roster wait.

Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund — the sovereign wealth fund that launched and bankrolled LIV Golf starting in 2022 — has announced it will end its financial involvement in the league following the conclusion of the 2026 season. The announcement did not come with a transition plan, a named buyer, or a confirmed 2027 schedule. It came with a question mark where a future should be.

What LIV Built

LIV Golf's four seasons reshaped professional golf in ways that will outlast whatever happens to the league itself. The bidding wars it triggered drove up compensation for Tour players, even those who never played a LIV event. The team format it introduced created a competing model for how elite golf could be organized and broadcast. The players it attracted — including Rahm, Dustin Johnson, Brooks Koepka, Phil Mickelson and others — raised the quality of the competition and the profile of the enterprise.

For the players who joined, the financial rewards were significant. Guaranteed money replaced the week-to-week uncertainty of the PGA Tour. The schedule was lighter. The prize funds were large. Whatever the uncertainty around 2027 looks like, the four seasons of competition LIV delivered were real, contested, and in many cases financially transformative for the players who competed.

What's Unresolved

The mechanics of the transition are unclear. The PIF's exit does not automatically dissolve the league — it means the current funding model ends. LIV could theoretically survive under new ownership, a media partnership, or a restructured agreement with the PIF under different terms. None of those paths have been publicly confirmed.

For players still under contract, the situation is complicated. Jon Rahm had reportedly been offered a substantial separation agreement that would allow him to exit the league ahead of the PIF's withdrawal. Other players have had varying contract structures, and their options in 2027 depend heavily on whether LIV continues and in what form, whether the PGA Tour reopens its doors to returning players, and whether any new structure emerges from the negotiations that have quietly been ongoing for months.

The PGA Tour has not made a formal announcement about reinstatement policy for LIV players. That conversation, which began in earnest during the 2023-2024 framework discussions, remains unfinished.

What's Next

The most immediate question is whether any announcement about LIV Golf's 2027 plans comes before the year ends. Industry observers expect some clarity before the new year, though the specifics of any announcement — a sale, a partnership, a formal wind-down — are not yet public. For players like Rahm, whose options appear wider than most given his stature and marketability, the next weeks will shape decisions that affect the rest of their careers. For the broader game, the LIV chapter is not yet closed. But with Indianapolis behind it, the 2026 season of the most disruptive force in modern golf is officially over.
