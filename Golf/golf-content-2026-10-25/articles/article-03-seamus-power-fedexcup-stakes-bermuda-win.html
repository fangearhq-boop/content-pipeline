Power's FedEx Cup Stakes: Why Sunday in Bermuda Could Transform His 2026 Season

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | October 25, 2026

There are two types of players who co-lead a final round in professional golf. Those who are playing to win a trophy. And those who are playing to change the course of their season. Seamus Power is playing for both.

The Irish veteran enters Sunday at Port Royal co-leading the Butterfield Bermuda Championship at 16-under, alongside Austin Smotherman. But the stakes extend far beyond the $1.08 million winner's check. Power sits No. 103 in the FedEx Cup Fall standings — and a tournament victory would catapult him to approximately No. 53, a dramatic improvement that opens the door to continued involvement in the fall playoffs and the security that comes with it.

The Numbers That Matter

The FedEx Cup Fall operates as a points race, with players needing to finish inside the top 70 to advance through the stages. At No. 103, Power is outside that cut line heading into the final stretch of the fall schedule. A win at Bermuda's $7.2 million event — which carries a large FedEx Cup point allocation — rewrites his situation entirely. It is the kind of result that turns a difficult fall into a successful one in a matter of four-and-a-half hours.

Power Has Been Here Before

What makes Power's position genuinely compelling is that he has not merely survived at Bermuda — he has thrived here. The 2022 Bermuda Championship winner, Power knows Port Royal's quirks, its winds, and the creative demands it places on a player's imagination. His third-round 67 on Saturday was played with calm precision, including a memorable recovery at the par-3 16th hole where an unpredictable gust sent his ball into a back bunker rather than the front pin he was targeting.

That kind of adaptability — accepting the wind, recalibrating the shot, getting the job done — is exactly the mindset that wins in Bermuda. Power has shown all week that he has it.

What's Next

Power goes out in the final pairing alongside Smotherman and Taylor Moore at 8:46 AM ET. For Power, Sunday is about process — committing to his game plan and trusting the preparation. The FedEx Cup math will take care of itself if he can find the bottom of the cup one more time than everyone else by the end of 18 holes. A Bermuda win in 2026 would be more than just a title. It would be a lifeline for his season.
