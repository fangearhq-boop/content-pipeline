Five Runner-Ups, One Win: Scheffler's 2026 Season Defies Explanation

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | July 27, 2026

Scottie Scheffler shot a 63 on Sunday at TPC Twin Cities. On most days, in most years, a 63 from the world No. 1 on the final day of a PGA Tour event closes it out. On Sunday, July 27, 2026, a 63 finished second by three shots — behind a 21-year-old playing his third professional start.

That encapsulates Scheffler's 2026 season as well as any single data point. He is still world No. 1. He is still playing extraordinary golf. And he has five runner-up finishes this year against just one win — the American Express in January.

The Numbers That Don't Add Up

Over the last two years of PGA Tour competition, Scheffler was the most dominant golfer on the planet. Seven wins in 2024. Six wins in 2025. Back-to-back Player of the Year awards. A period of sustained excellence that drew comparisons to the peak seasons of Tiger Woods.

The 2026 version looks different. Not because his game has declined — a 63 in final-round pressure is not a declining player — but because the wins have stopped converting at anything close to the rate the golf world came to expect. Five runner-ups is not a cold streak. Five runner-ups is a pattern.

The losses have come in varied ways. Sometimes the field has played better. Sometimes Scheffler has led and been overtaken. At the 3M Open, a 21-year-old on his third professional start simply went wire-to-wire and didn't give him a real opening.

What Scheffler Himself Acknowledges

Scheffler has been candid about the frustration. After his final-round 63 at the 3M Open, he addressed the accumulation of near-misses directly.

"I think I've been really close to winning some tournaments, and that can be frustrating," he said.

That is not the language of a player who has lost his form. It is the language of a player experiencing what happens when the margins that used to go his way stop going his way — at least for a stretch. The golf is still there. The results have not followed.

The Larger Context

World No. 1 rankings are built on consistency over time, and Scheffler's body of work over the last several years is comprehensive enough that five runner-ups and one win in a half-season does not threaten his standing at the top of the sport's hierarchy. He is still the standard against which every other player is measured.

But the 2026 win drought — if it extends much longer — will become one of the more unusual chapters in the career of one of the most dominant golfers of the modern era. Players in Scheffler's position don't usually spend extended periods this close to winning without getting over the line.

What's Next

The PGA Tour season continues through the FedEx Cup Playoffs, and Scheffler remains among the favorites at every event he enters. His ranking, his consistency, and his track record all suggest that a run of wins is more likely than not in the back half of 2026. Sunday's result at TPC Twin Cities was a reminder that the expected outcome in professional golf is rarely guaranteed — even when the world's best player shoots 63.
