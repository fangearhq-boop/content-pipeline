LIV Golf Needs $400M to Finish Its 2026 Season — And It Doesn't Have It

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | July 27, 2026

LIV Golf needs $400 million in funding to complete its remaining 2026 season. Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund — which has spent more than $5.3 billion building the circuit since 2022 — is withdrawing its ongoing financial support. Restructuring specialists have been installed on the board. Multiple events are described as "on the fence." Bankruptcy is being considered.

The tour that was supposed to permanently reshape the professional golf landscape is now fighting for survival.

The Funding Gap

LIV Golf's 2026 season was planned as a continuation and expansion of the format the tour has built since its launch. With PIF's backing, it had operated as if cost were not a real constraint — signing players to enormous guaranteed contracts, building a broadcast infrastructure, and running events at premium venues around the world.

The withdrawal of PIF's ongoing support changes every one of those calculations. The $400 million gap between what LIV needs to complete its 2026 calendar and what it currently has is not a rounding error. It is an existential sum.

Restructuring Specialists Take the Board

Gene Davis, of PIRINATE Consulting Group, and Jon Zinman have been installed on LIV Golf's board as restructuring specialists. Their mandate is to seek bridge capital — funding that would allow the tour to complete its 2026 schedule while a longer-term financial solution is identified.

The appointment of restructuring specialists is a significant signal. These are professionals whose expertise is in managing organizations under severe financial stress. Their presence on the board tells a clear story about where LIV Golf stands.

Events at Risk

With the funding gap unresolved, multiple remaining 2026 LIV events are described internally as "on the fence." That language means their staging is conditional — dependent on bridge capital being secured before their scheduled dates. If the funding is not found, events may be canceled or postponed.

For players who signed with LIV Golf on the basis of guaranteed contracts and a full event schedule, the possibility of truncated seasons creates both financial and professional uncertainty. The players who left the PGA Tour for LIV face a circuit that may not complete its own calendar.

The PGA Tour Watches Closely

The PGA Tour is monitoring LIV Golf's financial situation, according to sources familiar with the matter. The tour and LIV have had an adversarial relationship since LIV's 2022 launch, which was accompanied by suspensions and legal action. A proposed merger framework was discussed in 2023 but never completed.

LIV Golf's financial distress reopens questions about what the professional golf landscape looks like if the circuit cannot survive in its current form. Whether the PGA Tour would absorb players, events, or infrastructure from a collapsed LIV is a question whose answer is not yet clear.

What's Next

The immediate timeline depends on whether Davis and Zinman can secure bridge capital in the coming weeks. If they can, the 2026 season continues. If they cannot, the conversation shifts to bankruptcy proceedings and what happens to the players, the contracts, and the events that LIV Golf had promised.

The sport of professional golf has not seen a situation like this before. LIV Golf arrived with unlimited resources and reshaped the competitive landscape. The question now is whether it can survive long enough to see 2027.
