Jackson Koivun Wins 3M Open: 21-Year-Old Beats World No. 1 Scheffler in Third Pro Start

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | July 27, 2026

Jackson Koivun had missed the cut in his first two professional starts. He was 21 years old, a college champion who had not yet figured out the gap between the NCAA Tour and the PGA Tour. And then, on Sunday at TPC Twin Cities, he shot a bogey-free 66 to win the 3M Open at a tournament-record 25-under par — beating world No. 1 Scottie Scheffler by three shots.

It is one of the great debut wins in recent PGA Tour history. Scheffler, the two-time defending Player of the Year who had won seven events in 2024 and six in 2025, shot a final-round 63. It was not enough. Koivun's wire-to-wire performance — he held at least a share of the lead after every round — was complete, calm, and historically dominant.

The College Champion Goes Pro

Koivun arrived on the PGA Tour this summer with a significant pedigree. He led Auburn University to the 2026 NCAA Championship team title, one of the most decorated college careers of his generation. But professional golf is a different world, and his first two starts were humbling ones — missed cuts that offered no indication of what was coming.

At TPC Twin Cities, Koivun played four rounds of controlled, precise golf on a par-71 layout that punishes mistakes. He made none that mattered. His final-round 66 was bogey-free — and it came on the biggest day of his career, with Scheffler charging hard behind him.

"I just kept telling myself to play my game," Koivun said after accepting the trophy. "I didn't want to look at the leaderboard."

His total of 259 strokes — 25-under par — broke the tournament record at TPC Twin Cities.

Scheffler's 63 Falls Short

Scheffler's final round was remarkable in its own right. A 63 on any PGA Tour course on a Sunday is elite golf. It moved him up the leaderboard and applied enormous pressure on the young leader. Koivun absorbed it.

For Scheffler, the result extends one of the more puzzling stretches of his career. He entered 2026 with back-to-back dominant seasons and has instead accumulated five runner-up finishes against just one victory — the American Express in January. His world No. 1 ranking remains intact, but the wins have not followed the way they once did.

"I think I've been really close to winning some tournaments, and that can be frustrating," Scheffler said after his round.

A Historic First Win

The $1,584,000 winner's check is a life-changing amount for any first-time winner, but the real currency of Koivun's 3M Open victory is the credibility it immediately establishes. He is not a prospect anymore. He is a PGA Tour winner — and one who beat the world's best player to earn that title.

The PGA Tour has seen stunning debut wins before, but Koivun's combination of youth, college pedigree, quick professional timeline, and the identity of the player he beat make this one stand out. He went from missed cut to record-holder in three starts.

What's Next

Koivun's 3M Open win earns him a two-year PGA Tour exemption and berths in several upcoming marquee events, including The Northern Trust and potentially the TOUR Championship depending on how his FedEx Cup points stack up. He is no longer a name to watch — he is a name to know.

For the PGA Tour, the story of Sunday at TPC Twin Cities is exactly what the tour needs: a new face, an electric performance, and one of the great upsets of the 2026 season.
