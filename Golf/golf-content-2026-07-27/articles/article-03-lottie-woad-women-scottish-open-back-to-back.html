Lottie Woad Wins Women's Scottish Open Back-to-Back at -21

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | July 27, 2026

Lottie Woad won the AIG Women's Scottish Open in her professional debut in 2025. She returned to Dundonald Links in 2026 as the defending champion — and she left with the trophy again.

Woad finished at 21-under par, three shots clear of Hyo Joo Kim, to claim back-to-back Women's Scottish Open titles. She entered the final round with a three-shot lead and played with the assurance of a champion to protect and extend it. The win is one of the most complete weeks of golf on the women's circuit this season.

From Debut to Defender

Woad's 2025 Women's Scottish Open win was immediately one of the most compelling stories in professional golf — a debut victory at a major-adjacent event sent shock waves through the LPGA and Ladies European Tour. Expectations around her 2026 follow-up were, accordingly, significant.

She met every one of them. Defending a title, on the same course, against a field that was ready for her this time, is a different task than winning it by surprise. Woad played Dundonald Links as if she owned it — which, for the second year running, she effectively does.

Her -21 total on a links layout that demands precision, patience, and creativity off the tee represents one of the best aggregate scores the event has seen.

Kim Falls Short Despite Strong Week

Hyo Joo Kim mounted the strongest challenge in the field, ultimately finishing at 18-under par. Her 2026 season has been one of consistent excellence on both the LPGA and LET, and her runner-up at Dundonald Links reinforces her standing as one of the world's best players.

But this week belonged to Woad. The English player held her lead through a final round that had the feel of a champion playing within herself — never forcing, never panicking, simply executing.

Eyes on the British Open

The win could not come at a better time for Woad. The AIG Women's British Open — one of the five women's majors — is next week at Royal Porthcawl in Wales. Woad arrives there as a two-time Scottish Open champion and one of the most in-form players in women's golf.

The Scottish Open has long served as a warm-up event for the British Open on the men's side, and the same dynamic plays out on the women's circuit. A player who wins Dundonald Links and then performs at the British Open makes a particular kind of statement about their game.

Woad is 23 years old. She has now won the Women's Scottish Open in each of her two professional seasons. A major feels like a matter of time.

What's Next

Woad heads directly from Dundonald Links to Royal Porthcawl for the AIG Women's British Open. The field will be the strongest she has faced in her career. After this week, she is one of its most compelling entries.
