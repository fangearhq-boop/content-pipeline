LIV Golf's Moment of Truth: Nine Days to the October 25 Deadline as Exits Mount and DeChambeau Waits

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | October 16, 2026

Nine days from today, LIV Golf's player commitment deadline arrives. The fate of the league's restructured second era depends on what happens before October 25 — and right now, the exits are louder than the commitments.

Jon Rahm, Abraham Ancer, Adrian Meronk, and David Puig have confirmed they will not participate in LIV 2.0. Rahm's lawyer has stated publicly that the terms presented are unacceptable to his client. These are not rumors or unnamed sources — they are confirmed departures from four players who collectively represented some of LIV's most recognizable talent.

Who's In, Who's Out

On the commitment side, Richard Bland, Victor Perez, and Cameron Tringale have publicly pledged to continue with the new entity. That list is shorter than league backers had hoped for at this stage of the process.

Tyrrell Hatton is reported to be on his way out, though no official statement has been made. The English player's departure, if confirmed, would represent another significant name leaving the roster.

Then there is Bryson DeChambeau. According to multiple reports, BC Partners Credit — the funding group that has pledged $300 million for LIV's restructuring — has identified DeChambeau as the player they most want to retain. He is the most prominent undecided figure in the current countdown, and his decision carries weight beyond his individual contract: it signals whether the league can retain a name that casual fans recognize.

The Structural Picture

BC Partners' $300 million commitment is contingent on securing, in the language of the bankruptcy proceedings, "a critical mass of players sufficient to operate a bona fide professional golf league." The October 25 deadline exists to determine whether that threshold can be met.

During a recent bankruptcy hearing, a LIV attorney told the court that the organization's fate should be clear "by the end of the month." That tracks precisely with the deadline. Attorneys have also indicated the deadline could be extended further if needed — some outlets describe October 25 as a target rather than a hard stop. Whether it moves again depends on whether the commitments materialize.

The DP World Tour has added a meaningful constraint to the decisions of undecided players by confirming that competitors in LIV 2.0 will not be permitted to also compete on the European circuit in 2027. That forecloses a hedge that might have made LIV 2.0 more attractive to players sitting on the fence.

What Happens Next

The bankruptcy court handling LIV Golf's restructuring will receive an update before the end of October. If BC Partners has the commitment level it needs, the path to LIV 2.0 continues. If it does not, the league as a viable competitive entity is at risk of dissolving entirely.

Players who have already confirmed their departures are free to pursue opportunities elsewhere — the PGA Tour, the DP World Tour, or other circuits. Jon Rahm's return to a major tour will be one of the most-watched stories in golf regardless of where he goes.
