Choi Takes 36-Hole Lead at Buick LPGA Shanghai — Thitikul Four Back as Defending Champion Stumbles

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | October 16, 2026

After two rounds at Sheshan Golf Club in Shanghai, it is South Korea's Hye-Jin Choi who stands alone at the top of the Buick LPGA Shanghai leaderboard. At 6-under 138, Choi holds a two-shot lead over Amy Yang and Grace Kim heading into the weekend.

Atiara Thitikul, the defending champion and the most decorated player on the LPGA Tour in 2026, is four back at 142, tied for seventh. The weekend is where this tournament will be decided.

The Leaderboard After 36 Holes

Choi opened with a bogey-free 64 on Thursday — one of the cleanest rounds of the first day. She backed it up with a 74 on Friday to reach 138 total, two clear of Yang and Kim who share second place at 140. Erika Hara, Rio Takeda, and Carlota Ciganda sit in a tie for fourth at 141.

Thitikul's round 2 was a challenge. After opening at 69 on Thursday, she shot 73 on Friday — a respectable score in difficult conditions, but not one that kept pace with Choi. The gap between the defending champion and the leader stands at four shots entering round 3.

Thitikul's Context

Thitikul comes into Shanghai with four LPGA victories in 2026 — a dominance that made her the clear narrative center of this tournament when it began Thursday. She won this title in 2025. She was the player everyone was watching.

A four-shot deficit after 36 holes is not insurmountable. The LPGA's best players have come from further back on weekends. Thitikul has the game to close — the question is whether Choi gives her anything to chase or extends the lead.

The defending champion's task is clear: post a low number in round 3 on Saturday and get back into contention before Sunday. A 66 or better would bring her to within one or two of the lead. Anything higher and the path gets narrow.

Choi's Position

Hye-Jin Choi is a proven LPGA performer with multiple tour victories on her résumé. The bogey-free 64 in round 1 established her immediately as a contender, and the ability to follow that with a solid 74 under more difficult conditions shows composure. Leading at the halfway point of a major LPGA event in China, with Thitikul four back and in pursuit, is a pressure situation. She has handled it through 36 holes.

Two more rounds remain. The course plays differently on the weekend when the wind picks up over Sheshan's layout. Choi's ability to manage that over the final 36 holes will determine whether she wins her first title in Shanghai or whether Thitikul writes another chapter of her dominant 2026 season.

What's Next

Round 3 begins Saturday at Sheshan Golf Club. Round 4 follows Sunday. Thitikul will need to close significant ground. Choi will need to hold it. The tournament is wide open with two rounds remaining — but two shots is a meaningful buffer when you have already been tested by difficult conditions.
