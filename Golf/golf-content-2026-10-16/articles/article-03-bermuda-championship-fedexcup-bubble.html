Bermuda Championship One Week Out: Port Royal and the FedExCup Bubble Race Heat Up

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | October 16, 2026

Six days from today, the PGA Tour's FedExCup Fall resumes in one of its most unusual settings: a small Atlantic island where the wind writes the scorecard as much as the player does.

The Butterfield Bermuda Championship tees off October 22-25 at Port Royal Golf Course in Southampton Parish, Bermuda. The event carries a $6 million purse and 500 FedExCup points — a meaningful total for players fighting to crack the top 60 on the points list before the Fall season ends.

The Course and the Wind

Port Royal was designed by Robert Trent Jones in 1970 and has the feel of a classic public course — wide, accessible, and forgiving when conditions allow. In calm weather it can become a birdie parade, and leaderboards at past Bermuda Championships have reflected that: Adam Schenk won at 12-under par in 2025.

The word "calm" does not describe Bermuda's typical weather in late October. The island sits in the Atlantic Ocean, and wind — whether it comes off the ocean or churns through the course's elevated sections — changes the game entirely. A track that yields 65s in still air becomes a par-preservation exercise in a 20 mph headwind. Players who manage their games conservatively tend to hold up better than those who chase birdies in adverse conditions.

Past champions include Rafael Campos (2024), Camilo Villegas, and Seamus Power — a list that reinforces the idea that Bermuda rewards steady, complete golfers rather than a specific type of power game.

The FedExCup Stakes

The Bermuda Championship is one of five FedExCup Fall events remaining on the schedule. With 500 points available to the winner and qualifying points distributed through the top-65 finishers, every position on the Bermuda leaderboard carries FedExCup significance.

For players sitting just outside the top 60 — the cutoff for the signature events that drive the following season's earning potential — a strong result at Port Royal can be the difference between a full 2027 schedule and a grind to earn exempt status back. The bubble race going into Bermuda is one of the most compelling sub-plots in professional golf this time of year.

What to Watch

The Bermuda field typically skews toward the middle tier of the PGA Tour — the event does not attract the same headliners as the signature events earlier in the season. What it does attract is players who need it. The combination of FedExCup urgency and wind-affected conditions on a unique Atlantic island course makes for consistently unpredictable golf.

Watch for first-round conditions to set the tone. If the wind cooperates in the opening two rounds, expect low scoring early and a tight leaderboard. If it blows from the start, the player who stays patient longest often ends up on top.

What's Next

Tee times for round 1 are released in the days preceding October 22. The tournament runs through Sunday, October 25 — the same day as the LIV Golf player deadline, the F1 United States Grand Prix, and what is shaping up to be one of the most crowded sports Sundays of the fall.
