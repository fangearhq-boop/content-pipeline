179 Consecutive Weeks at No. 1: Scottie Scheffler Is 100 Weeks Away From Breaking Tiger's Record

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | October 16, 2026

Scottie Scheffler enters this week in his 179th consecutive week as world No. 1, extending a streak that has been unbroken since approximately May 2023. The number has grown large enough to generate a natural question: how close is he to breaking the record?

The answer is that Tiger Woods is still 100 weeks ahead of him — and the chase runs through mid-October 2028.

The Record

Tiger Woods's record for consecutive weeks at world No. 1 stands at 281, set between June 12, 2005 and October 30, 2010. During that stretch, no player on earth came close to challenging his position. The dominance was statistical, but it was also perceptual — there was rarely a week during that five-year run when the question of whether Tiger might slip felt serious.

Scheffler faces different conditions. The modern PGA Tour is more competitive, prize purses are larger, and the player pool is deeper than it was in the mid-2000s. Rory McIlroy has consistently been the No. 2 player in the world over the past two years. Jon Rahm, now separating from LIV Golf, will return to a major tour with major ambitions. The field around Scheffler is real.

The Math

At 179 consecutive weeks entering this week, Scheffler needs approximately 102 more weeks to reach 281 and tie the Tiger record. To break it, he needs 103. Holding the No. 1 ranking from now through roughly mid-October 2028 would accomplish that.

His 2026 season supports the case for durability. He won the Tour Championship at East Lake to claim his second FedExCup title, added his third PGA Tour win of the season, and pushed his career earnings on the official PGA Tour money list past Tiger's total — a milestone that reflects both his dominance and the era's increased prize purses.

The Longer Record Is Different

Tiger's total weeks at No. 1 — not consecutive, but cumulative — stands at 683. That record would require Scheffler to remain at the top of the rankings for roughly another decade. It is not a record that any single player in the current era will challenge in any realistic time frame.

The consecutive-weeks record is different. It is achievable. It requires sustained excellence over two years from now — not perfection, but consistent dominance. Scheffler has demonstrated the ability to recover quickly from setbacks and return to form. The question is injury and circumstance more than talent.

What's Next

Scheffler does not have a confirmed start at the Bermuda Championship next week. His schedule through the end of 2026 includes appearances at selected international events before the new season begins. Every week he holds the top ranking moves the consecutive-weeks total one step closer to history.
