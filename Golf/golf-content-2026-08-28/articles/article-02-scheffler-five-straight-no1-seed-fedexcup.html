Five Straight No. 1 Seeds: Scheffler's FedExCup Dominance Is Historic

By Jake Torres | August 28, 2026

When Scottie Scheffler walked to the first tee at East Lake Golf Club this week, he did so as the No. 1 seed in the FedExCup Playoffs for the fifth consecutive year — a distinction no player in the history of the format has ever held. Five straight seasons of finishing atop the FedExCup standings. Five straight invitations to Scheffler's corner of history that no one else has even approached.

After Round 1, Scheffler sits at -5, three shots behind leader Min Woo Lee. He is not leading — but he is right where the most decorated player in the modern game should be: lurking, ready, and perfectly capable of going lower.

What Five Straight No. 1 Seeds Means

The FedExCup operates on a season-long points accumulation system that rewards both consistency and peak performance at the right moments. Earning the top seed requires not just winning — though Scheffler has done plenty of that, with 21 PGA Tour victories — but sustaining an elite level across a 47-event schedule that runs from October through August.

Over five full seasons, Scheffler has been the best player in the world for every single one of them. He won the FedExCup in 2024, adding the points-series crown to his collection of major championships and World Golf Rankings dominance. He is at East Lake in 2026 seeking his second.

The $23M Bonus Already Secured

One aspect of the No. 1 seed structure that occasionally gets overlooked: Scheffler has already secured a $23 million bonus by finishing atop the FedExCup standings before East Lake even began. The Tour Championship itself is, in one sense, a bonus for Scheffler — a chance to add the trophy to the financial package already won.

But for a player of Scheffler's competitive nature, the trophy is the point. He did not come to East Lake to coast on his season-long earnings. He came to win the tournament and cement another chapter in what is becoming a career unlike any other in the current era of professional golf.

The Birdie-Birdie Finish

Scheffler's Round 1 performance was solid, not spectacular — until the end. He closed with consecutive birdies at East Lake to post his -5 round. That kind of late-round acceleration is a Scheffler calling card. When the pressure is manageable, he manages it efficiently. When the leaderboard demands a charge, he usually delivers.

He was three shots back at the conclusion of Round 1 in a 30-player field. Three shots, three rounds to play, on a course he knows well, with a game that has been the standard of the sport for five consecutive years. The math still works in Scheffler's favour.

What's Next

Round 2 of the Tour Championship is underway at East Lake on August 28. Scheffler will need to make his move in the middle rounds to give himself a platform for a final-day run at Min Woo Lee's lead.

Five No. 1 seeds. Twenty-one wins. A $23M bonus already banked. The FedExCup trophy is the one thing this week can add. Scheffler is three shots back and counting.
