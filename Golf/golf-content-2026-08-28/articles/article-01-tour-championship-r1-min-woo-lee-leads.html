Tour Championship Round 1: Min Woo Lee Fires -8 to Lead East Lake, Scheffler Three Back

By Ryan Calloway | August 28, 2026

Min Woo Lee seized the Round 1 lead at the 2026 Tour Championship, posting an 8-under par opening round at East Lake Golf Club in Atlanta to build a three-shot advantage over a crowded group of contenders that includes several of the game's biggest names. Scottie Scheffler, the five-time consecutive No. 1 seed, sits five-under and in position to climb. Rory McIlroy, chasing an unprecedented fourth FedExCup title, opened at two-under but now faces a significant gap heading into Round 2.

Lee Sets the Pace

Min Woo Lee played solo for portions of his round after playing partner J.J. Spaun withdrew with a shoulder injury, but the Australian showed no signs of distraction. His 8-under round was the low round of the day by two shots and gave him a commanding position at a course where the par-70 East Lake layout demands precision from the jump.

Lee finished the round three clear of Viktor Hovland, Collin Morikawa, and Chris Gotterup, who share second place at 6-under. The trio represents a significant collection of talent — any of the three is capable of closing the gap quickly over the remaining three days.

Scheffler's Late Rush

Scheffler posted a five-under opening round and closed with consecutive birdies to establish himself firmly in contention at T5. The manner of his finish — accelerating late when others might coast — was characteristic of the player who has spent five consecutive years as the Tour's top-ranked points earner heading into East Lake.

Three shots back from a leader like Lee, in a 30-player field at a par-70 setup, is an entirely manageable deficit. Scheffler has the tools, the experience, and the East Lake history to make a move in Round 2.

McIlroy's Rollercoaster

Rory McIlroy's opening round told a more complicated story. The three-time FedExCup champion was two-over par at some point in his round — a worrying start at a venue where leaderboard separation comes quickly in the 30-player field. But McIlroy rallied, finished at two-under par, and now sits at T20.

Six shots back with three rounds to play is doable, but it will require McIlroy to go low in Round 2 and likely multiple times after that. He has done it at East Lake before — the question is whether his game is sharp enough to produce the kind of sustained run needed to catch Lee and pass Scheffler at the same time.

What's Next

Round 2 of the Tour Championship is underway at East Lake on August 28. Round 3 is scheduled for August 29, and the final round concludes the season on August 30. The winner claims $10 million from the $40 million total purse, along with the FedExCup trophy.

The leaderboard is open and the field is deep. Lee's lead is significant but not insurmountable. Saturday and Sunday at East Lake will determine who takes home golf's postseason crown.
