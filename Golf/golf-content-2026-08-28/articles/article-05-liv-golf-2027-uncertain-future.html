LIV Golf's 2027 Future Hangs in the Balance — Norman Bleak, Poulter Warns Time Is Running Out

By Jake Torres | August 28, 2026

As LIV Golf's 2026 season draws to a close — capped by La Sasso's win at the Indianapolis finale and Rahm's third consecutive individual title — the league's prospects for 2027 remain deeply uncertain. Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund is withdrawing its financial backing at the end of the current season, and the deal meant to replace it has not yet been signed. For the first time since LIV Golf launched in 2022, the circuit's continued existence is a genuine open question.

Greg Norman's Bleak Assessment

The most sobering words came from LIV Golf's founding CEO, Greg Norman, speaking in a recent interview with Skratch. "I hate to see it wither away," Norman said. "I would rather just see it end." The candour from the man who built LIV from the ground up was striking — an implicit acknowledgment that a slow death may be worse than a clean conclusion.

Norman's tenure with LIV ended in 2023, but his investment in what the circuit represents — disruption, opportunity, and alternative pathways in professional golf — has never wavered. That he would publicly consider ending it rather than watching it diminish is a signal of how seriously the funding crisis is being taken by those closest to its history.

Poulter's Warning

Ian Poulter, one of LIV's most prominent and loyal players, offered his own cautious read on the situation. "I think time might run out," Poulter said, adding that he genuinely hadn't decided what his own next step would be if the circuit doesn't continue in 2027. Poulter's comments reflect a broader anxiety among LIV's player roster — many of whom surrendered PGA Tour cards and faced professional uncertainty when they made the original switch.

For players who went all-in on LIV, the prospect of the circuit shutting down is not abstract. The question of where they play next — whether the PGA Tour would consider any reinstatement pathway, whether other circuits would absorb them — is real and pressing.

The New Investor Deal

LIV CEO Scott O'Neil has said that a deal with a new investor has been signed, with hopes to close in September. The specifics of the agreement have not been disclosed publicly. Whether it carries enough financial weight to sustain the circuit at its current scale — or any scale — remains unknown until the deal is officially concluded and disclosed.

There has been reporting around the concept of a LIV 2.0: a leaner structure with fewer events and reduced prize money. A compressed calendar and lower overhead could make the circuit viable without the full PIF backing that enabled its original ambitions. Whether that version of LIV Golf — smaller, quieter, less disruptive — would retain its player base and any remaining commercial momentum is a separate question.

Where the 2026 Season Left Things

The Legion XIII team claimed the 2026 LIV team title. Rahm's third straight individual crown cemented his status as the circuit's defining individual competitor. La Sasso's win at Indianapolis closed the season on a note that, however well-played, could not distract from the larger institutional cloud hanging over the circuit.

LIV Golf reshaped the professional game's economics, forced the PGA Tour into structural negotiations it would not have otherwise considered, and provided competitive golf of genuine quality. What it never achieved was the financial self-sufficiency that would have made it durable beyond its Saudi backing. That unresolved question is now the only one that matters.

What's Next

September is the target month for closing the new investor deal. If it closes, LIV Golf in some form continues. If it doesn't, the circuit faces a genuine shutdown scenario — and every player, sponsor, and broadcast partner who has attached themselves to it will need to find a new plan. The golf world is watching, and the answer may come before the year is out.
