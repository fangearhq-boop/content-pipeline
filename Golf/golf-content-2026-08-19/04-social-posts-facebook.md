The Michigan Team Championship — which was supposed to be LIV's season-closing finale — has been cancelled entirely.

In total, LIV players and teams have lost approximately $60 million in promised prize money across 2026, including the cancelled June Louisiana event.

Players are also reportedly waiting for owed payments.

LIV launched with Saudi billions and the promise of a rival league that would pay players at levels the PGA Tour couldn't match. As the funding dries up, the gap between that promise and reality has become one of the defining stories of professional golf's turbulent 2026.

The resume is extraordinary. In April 2025, McIlroy completed his career Grand Slam at Augusta National, ending an 11-year wait since his 2014 Open Championship and beating Bryson DeChambeau in a playoff. Then he went back to Augusta in April 2026 and won again — defending his Masters title, holding off Scottie Scheffler by one stroke despite surrendering a six-shot lead in the third round. But after the Masters high, 2026 has been uneven. At the FedEx St. Jude Championship, McIlroy finished 66th at 8-over par — zero sub-par rounds across four days of competitive golf. He enters Bellerive at No. 13 in the FedExCup standings. East Lake is well within reach — but the margin for error is thin, and the BMW field is deep. He sits at +1600 in the odds, second only to Scheffler, reflecting both his talent and the recognition that talent and current form are not the same thing right now.

Three FedExCup titles. A career Grand Slam. Back-to-back Masters. And he's working on his swing this week at one of America's most storied golf venues.
