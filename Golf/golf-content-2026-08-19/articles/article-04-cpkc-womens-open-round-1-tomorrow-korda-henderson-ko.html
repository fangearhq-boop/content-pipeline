CPKC Women's Open Round 1 Begins Tomorrow: Korda, Henderson, and Ko Set the Stage in Edmonton

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | August 19, 2026

Wednesday is pro-am day at Royal Mayfair Golf Club in Edmonton, Alberta. Thursday, the 52nd CPKC Women's Open begins in earnest — and the field assembled for one of the LPGA's premier events carries the kind of storyline density that makes this tournament worth circling on the calendar every summer.

The event runs August 20 through 23, with 156 players competing for a $2.75 million purse on the Stanley Thompson-designed par 70 (6,452 yards) that has hosted some of Canadian golf's defining moments. Today's pro-am gives the field a final relaxed look at a course that has not been kind to conventional assumptions about who will contend.

Nelly Korda: World No. 1 and Heavy Favorite

Korda has been the dominant force in women's golf through 2026. She won at least four events this season, including three major championships — the Chevron, the Riviera May Open, and the U.S. Women's Open. She arrives in Edmonton ranked No. 1 in the world and as the prohibitive betting favorite. No injury or withdrawal news has emerged. Korda simply wants to win.

The question for the field is not whether Korda is capable of claiming a fourth title this season — she plainly is — but whether Royal Mayfair's specific demands produce a leaderboard that can resist her for 72 holes. The par 70 layout, which winds through the North Saskatchewan River Valley, requires precision off the tee and touch around the greens. Korda has demonstrated she can win on any course type, which makes the assignment for the rest of the field genuinely daunting.

Brooke Henderson: Defending Champion and Hometown Hero

Brooke Henderson will tee it up Thursday as the defending champion, and the Albert Gallery — the faithful contingent of Canadian fans who follow Henderson's every shot at home events — will be in full voice from first light. Henderson won the 2025 edition of this tournament by closing with a 67 and finishing at -15, beating Minjee Lee by a single stroke for her 14th LPGA title. The defining Canadian golfer of her generation is playing in front of her people, defending a title she won in one of the most dramatic finishes the event has produced.

She anchors a 15-player Canadian contingent at the 2026 event — the largest such group in the tournament's history. For golf in Alberta and across Canada, this week is as significant as any event on the annual calendar.

Lydia Ko: Chasing History

Lydia Ko's relationship with Royal Mayfair is one of the more remarkable individual-to-venue bonds in modern women's golf. She won her first LPGA title here in 2013 at the age of 15, as an amateur — becoming the youngest winner in LPGA history at the time. She returned and won again. She won a third time. A fourth title at this specific venue would be something without equivalent in the tournament's 52-year history.

Ko won three Olympic gold medals over her career. She has been one of the defining figures of the LPGA for more than a decade. Thursday at Royal Mayfair, she will again be hunting a record that only she has any chance of setting.

The Rest of the Field

Beyond the three headliners, the CPKC Women's Open field includes Hyo Joo Kim, Miyu Yamashita, Ruoning Yin, Minjee Lee (who lost to Henderson by one shot last year), and Hannah Green. It is a depth that rewards Royal Mayfair's demands for precision over brute force — making the week's leaderboard genuinely difficult to predict beyond Korda's obvious favoritism.

What's Next

Round 1 tees off Thursday morning at Royal Mayfair. Live coverage is available through Golf Channel and the LPGA's streaming platforms. Friday brings Round 2, with the cut made after 36 holes. Weekend play follows Saturday and Sunday, with the champion crowned on the final afternoon. The leaderboard after Thursday will define the week's narrative — whether Korda has bolted clear, or whether the tournament shapes up as the competitive field it has every reason to be.
