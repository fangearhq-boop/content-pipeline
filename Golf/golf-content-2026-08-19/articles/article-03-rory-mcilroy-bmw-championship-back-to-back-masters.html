Back-to-Back Masters Champion Rory McIlroy Arrives at BMW Looking for His Game

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | August 19, 2026

No player walking into Bellerive Country Club this week carries a more striking combination of recent achievement and current uncertainty than Rory McIlroy. He is the reigning back-to-back Masters champion — one of the most decorated resumes in the history of golf — arriving at a $20 million FedExCup Playoff event at No. 13 in the standings after finishing 66th in the preceding event.

The achievements define the narrative. In April 2025, McIlroy completed his career Grand Slam at Augusta National, ending an 11-year wait since his 2014 Open Championship. He beat Bryson DeChambeau in a playoff on a Sunday afternoon at Augusta that will be discussed as long as the game is played — closing out a drought of 3,899 days since his last major and becoming just the sixth player in men's golf history to win all four Grand Slam titles.

Then he went back and did it again. In April 2026, McIlroy defended his Masters title, holding off Scottie Scheffler by one stroke despite surrendering a six-shot lead in the third round. He became the first player to win back-to-back Masters since Tiger Woods claimed the championship in both 2001 and 2002. Only four men in Masters history have achieved it: Jack Nicklaus in 1965 and 1966, Nick Faldo in 1989 and 1990, Tiger in 2001 and 2002, and now McIlroy.

The Summer Slump

What has followed the Augusta high has been more complicated. At the FedEx St. Jude Championship — the first of the three playoff events — McIlroy finished 66th at 8-over par. He did not post a single sub-par round across four days at TPC Southwind. His own assessment after the final round: "far away from where I need to be."

He flew home to South Florida to spend time working with longtime coach Michael Bannon on his swing before heading to Bellerive. Golf Channel framed his TPC Southwind week as one where he "finds encouragement" — a characterization that reads as a deliberate counterpoint to McIlroy's own bleak self-assessment. That tension between how McIlroy sees his game and how others frame it is a recurring feature of his public career.

He enters the BMW Championship at No. 13 in the FedExCup standings. East Lake is reachable — the top 30 advance from Bellerive — but a poor week could change that. The sportsbooks have him at +1600, second only to Scottie Scheffler (+290-300) on the odds board, reflecting both the ceiling of his talent and the reality of his current form.

What Bellerive Demands

Bellerive Country Club is a Robert Trent Jones Sr. design that plays at par 70 over 7,448 yards — among the longer courses on the PGA Tour calendar. The club has hosted the U.S. Open (1965, Gary Player) and the PGA Championship (1992, Nick Price), and it last hosted the BMW Championship in 2008, when Camilo Villegas claimed the title. The course has received significant bunker work — all of them dug up and resodded with bentgrass — since that last PGA Tour visit.

McIlroy's length off the tee remains one of his most reliable attributes even when other parts of his game are in flux. If the short game and iron play are dialed in following his post-St. Jude work with Bannon, Bellerive is a venue where he is capable of producing the kind of week the sportsbooks' second-favorite odds suggest he might.

What's Next

The BMW Championship begins Thursday at Bellerive. McIlroy needs a result that either confirms his East Lake position or, ideally, vaults him up the FedExCup standings. A strong week could set up a third FedExCup title run at East Lake — adding to a career already overflowing with accomplishment. The tournament will tell us which version of McIlroy has arrived in St. Louis: the Masters champion who can win anywhere, or the golfer who is still finding his way back to that level.
