LIV Golf's Financial Crisis: Saudi Funding Ends, Prize Money Halved, Lawsuit Filed

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | August 19, 2026

The financial architecture that made LIV Golf possible is collapsing in the open, and the Indianapolis event this week — now the sole season finale after Michigan was cancelled — is playing out against a backdrop of lawsuits, slashed prize money, and reports of players waiting on payments they were promised.

Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund announced in April 2026 that it would end its financial support of LIV Golf after the conclusion of this season. The announcement triggered a cascade of cuts that have fundamentally altered the product players signed contracts to join.

What Got Cut — and How Much

The numbers are stark. The Michigan Team Championship — which was scheduled as LIV's season-closing event — was cancelled entirely. The Indianapolis individual purse, which had been $20 million at prior LIV events, has been cut to $10.1 million. The winner in Indianapolis will earn $2.02 million. At every prior LIV event, the winner's check was $4 million.

The team championship prize pool, now held at Indianapolis rather than Michigan, totals $30 million across the 13 franchises. The winning team bonus has been cut from $11.2 million to $8.4 million. In total, LIV players and teams have lost approximately $60 million in promised prize money in 2026 — a figure that includes the cancelled June Louisiana event and its $30 million purse.

The Lawsuit

The financial distress is not limited to players. Fresh Tape Media — a production company that managed logistics and on-site production for LIV Golf Week, LIV's January preseason event held in West Palm Beach, Florida from January 12 to 14 — filed suit in New York Supreme Court on July 21 seeking more than $1.23 million in unpaid fees and interest. The lawsuit alleges LIV failed to pay for services already rendered.

Multiple outlets, including Golf Digest and Front Office Sports, have also reported that players themselves are awaiting payments they are owed. Specific names and amounts have not been publicly confirmed, but the reports add to a picture of an organization straining to meet its financial obligations.

What LIV Was and What It Became

LIV Golf launched in 2022 with the explicit promise of outspending the PGA Tour for talent, offering guaranteed money and reduced scheduling commitments to lure established players away from the traditional tour. The Saudi investment made that promise credible — and it worked, attracting Dustin Johnson, Phil Mickelson, Bryson DeChambeau, Jon Rahm, Cameron Smith, and others at significant cost.

Four years later, the PIF's withdrawal fundamentally changes the math. Without an unlimited bankroll, LIV cannot compete with the PGA Tour on prize money, and the argument that players should walk away from established careers and tour benefits for guaranteed LIV checks becomes considerably harder to make when those checks are being disputed in court.

Indianapolis this week proceeds as scheduled — with Jon Rahm's individual title already clinched and the team championship still live between 4Aces GC and Ripper GC, separated by 2.25 points. But the competitive action unfolds against the largest question in LIV's brief history: what comes next?

What's Next

The LIV Indianapolis season finale runs August 21-23 at The Club at Chatham Hills in Westfield, Indiana. After this week, LIV's 2026 season concludes. The organization has not publicly detailed its plans for 2027 or how it intends to operate with reduced or eliminated PIF funding. The lawsuits and payment disputes will continue to work through the courts. For the players who built their professional identities around LIV — and its guaranteed money — the offseason ahead carries uncertainty that the tour's early days never did.
