LIV Indianapolis Season Finale Preview: Perez Out, Rottluff In — Team Championship on a Razor's Edge

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | August 19, 2026

The LIV Golf season arrives at its finale with a roster change, a cancelled final event reshaping the schedule, and a team championship separated by 2.25 points. Victor Perez has withdrawn from LIV Indianapolis to be present for the birth of his second child — a decision that requires no second-guessing. Germany's Max Rottluff replaces him in the field.

Perez's departure is personally significant but competitively absorbed. He sits well above LIV's relegation zone in the individual standings and his Cleeks GC teammates will manage his absence. The more consequential context is what Indianapolis has become in the wake of LIV's financial restructuring: the sole season finale, after the Michigan Team Championship was cancelled following Saudi Arabia's PIF announcement that it would end funding after 2026.

The Team Championship Picture

Entering Indianapolis, the team standings are as tight as they could be without being tied. 4Aces GC — the franchise anchored by Dustin Johnson, Thomas Pieters, Thomas Detry, and Anthony Kim — leads Ripper GC (Cameron Smith, Marc Leishman, Lucas Herbert, and Elvis Smylie) by 2.25 points. Legion XIII sits third and is also within striking distance.

The team championship represents one of LIV's most genuinely distinctive competitive innovations — a format that creates narrative arcs independent of individual scoring. Players who might be having a quiet personal week can still have an enormous collective impact on the standings. The Indianapolis 72-hole, shotgun-start format gives every pairing maximum time on the leaderboard and maximum opportunity for the team dynamic to express itself.

With 2.25 points separating first and second, a single strong team round on any of the four days can produce a lead change. This is the kind of tight team championship that makes LIV's format argument most compelling — exactly the wrong time for the league to be dealing with financial turbulence as a backdrop.

Individual Standings — Rahm Clinched

Jon Rahm has clinched his third consecutive LIV individual championship — 944.54 points, 218 points clear of second-place Bryson DeChambeau (726.20 points). Niemann sits third at 553.66, with Lucas Herbert fourth at 541.41. The individual race at Indianapolis is therefore a competition for final positioning rather than the title itself, with DeChambeau holding onto second and others fighting to improve their standing in the final rankings.

Rahm's three consecutive individual titles represent the kind of dominance that defines eras. Whether that dominance continues into 2027 — under whatever organizational structure LIV adopts following the PIF's funding withdrawal — remains one of the most uncertain questions in professional golf.

The Venue and Atmosphere

The Club at Chatham Hills in Westfield, Indiana, holds a historically significant designation: it is the final course designed by Pete Dye before his passing in 2020. Dye's career produced some of the most beloved and feared courses in American golf — Harbour Town, TPC Sawgrass, Whistling Straits — and Chatham Hills represents his last creative statement on the design of a golf course. Thomas Rhett performs Friday evening. Disco Lines plays Saturday. The entertainment component is a signature of LIV's event production, and Indianapolis will deliver it in full regardless of the financial clouds above the organization.

What's Next

LIV Indianapolis begins Thursday, August 21 with the shotgun start format. The team championship will be settled across 72 holes, with every session carrying implications for the 2.25-point gap between 4Aces and Ripper GC. The final leaderboard Sunday will end the 2026 LIV season — and trigger the most uncertain offseason in the organization's brief history. For now: the golf begins Thursday. Two and a quarter points separate two of the best team rosters in the field, and that is a genuinely compelling reason to watch.
