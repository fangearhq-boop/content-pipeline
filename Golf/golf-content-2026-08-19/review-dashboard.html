NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Window: 8:00 AM ET The LIV Golf experiment is ending — and the financial collapse is happening out in the open. Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund announced in April that it would end its funding of LIV Golf after this 2026 season. What has followed is a cascade of consequences that have fundamentally changed the product players signed up for: The Michigan Team Championship — which was supposed to be LIV's season-closing finale — has been cancelled entirely. The Indianapolis individual purse has been cut in half: from $20 million to $10.1 million. The winner will earn $2.02 million — down from the $4 million prize that was standard at every prior LIV event. The team championship prize pool at Indianapolis: $30 million total, with the winning team bonus cut from $11.2 million to $8.4 million. In total, LIV players and teams have lost approximately $60 million in promised prize money across 2026, including the cancelled June Louisiana event. And then there's the lawsuit. Fresh Tape Media — a production company that managed logistics and on-site production for LIV's January preseason event in West Palm Beach — filed a $1.23 million suit in New York Supreme Court in July, alleging more than $1 million in missed payments and interest. Players are also reportedly waiting for owed payments. LIV launched with Saudi billions and the promise of a rival league that would pay players at levels the PGA Tour couldn't match. As the funding dries up, the gap between that promise and reality has become one of the defining stories of professional golf's turbulent 2026. ---

### Long-Form Post Window: 10:00 AM ET Rory McIlroy arrives at Bellerive Country Club this week as arguably the most accomplished active golfer in the world — and he knows his game is not where it needs to be. The resume is extraordinary. In April 2025, McIlroy completed his career Grand Slam at Augusta National, ending an 11-year wait since his 2014 Open Championship and beating Bryson DeChambeau in a playoff. Then he went back to Augusta in April 2026 and won again — defending his Masters title, holding off Scottie Scheffler by one stroke despite surrendering a six-shot lead in the third round. McIlroy became the first player to win back-to-back Masters titles since Tiger Woods won in 2001 and 2002. He is only the fourth man to accomplish it in Masters history. But after the Masters high, 2026 has been uneven. At the FedEx St. Jude Championship, McIlroy finished 66th at 8-over par — zero sub-par rounds across four days of competitive golf. He flew home to South Florida to work on his swing with longtime coach Michael Bannon. His own assessment of where his game stands: "far away from where I need to be." He enters Bellerive at No. 13 in the FedExCup standings. East Lake is well within reach — but the margin for error is thin, and the BMW field is deep. He sits at +1600 in the odds, second only to Scheffler, reflecting both his talent and the recognition that talent and current form are not the same thing right now. Three FedExCup titles. A career Grand Slam. Back-to-back Masters. And he's working on his swing this week at one of America's most storied golf venues. This is what elite athletic careers look like at the top: never settled. ---

In the individual standings, Jon Rahm's title is already clinched — his third consecutive LIV individual championship — with Bryson DeChambeau in second at 726.20 points.
