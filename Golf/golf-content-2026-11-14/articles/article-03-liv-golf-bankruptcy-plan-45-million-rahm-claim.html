LIV Golf Files Bankruptcy Plan: $45M+ Owed to Players, Rahm's Claim Exceeds $100M

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | November 14, 2026

LIV Golf filed its formal bankruptcy plan on November 10, 2026, targeting a January 2027 liquidation with a disclosure statement hearing set for November 24. At least $45 million is owed to players — with Jon Rahm's claim reportedly exceeding $100 million.

The numbers behind LIV Golf's collapse are becoming clearer, and they are staggering. On November 10, the Saudi-backed league filed its formal bankruptcy plan in a New Jersey court, targeting a January 2027 liquidation that would officially close the chapter on five years of professional golf's most expensive and controversial disruption.

At least $45 million is owed to players from their LIV contracts, according to filings. That number alone illustrates the scale of financial commitments the league had made to its roster — but no individual figure in the case is more striking than Jon Rahm's: the two-time major champion is reportedly owed more than $100 million from the nine-figure contract that brought him to LIV Golf in late 2023.

The Road to Liquidation

LIV Golf filed for bankruptcy protection in September 2026 after the Saudi Public Investment Fund — which had invested approximately $5 billion in the enterprise — announced it would end funding after the 2026 season. Without the PIF's backing, the league had no path to continued operation.

A subsequent attempt to restructure under a BC Partners-backed LIV 2.0 model collapsed when BC Partners terminated its Restructuring Support Agreement, citing a lack of player participation. Without player commitment and without fresh capital, the league's attorneys told the bankruptcy court that no viable restructuring transaction remained available — and the focus shifted from reorganization to orderly wind-down.

The November 10 bankruptcy plan filing moves that wind-down into formal legal territory. A disclosure statement hearing is set for November 24 — the next major procedural step — with the final wind-down trust ultimately responsible for payments to creditors, resolution of legal claims, and all remaining financial matters.

Rahm's Position

Jon Rahm's departure from LIV Golf was approved by the bankruptcy court, but the financial damage from that nine-figure contract is significant. Exactly how much of his reported $100-plus million claim will be recovered through the bankruptcy process — and over what timeline — depends on the complexity of the proceedings and the total pool of assets available for distribution.

Rahm's situation is unusual in its scale but representative of the broader challenge facing former LIV players: the financial structures that made the league attractive are now subject to legal proceedings that will play out over months, possibly years.

What's Next

The November 24 disclosure statement hearing will determine whether the bankruptcy plan provides creditors with adequate information to vote on it. The formal January 7, 2027 liquidation hearing follows as the next major milestone.

For professional golf broadly, the question now shifts to what the post-LIV landscape looks like for players, tours, and the sport's governing bodies. The PGA Tour has not indicated a reinstatement pathway for former LIV players, and that silence will continue to define the careers of some of the game's most recognizable names through the winter.
