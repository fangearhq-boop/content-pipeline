Crowe Carries One-Shot Lead as Darkness Suspends Austin Championship Round 2

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | November 14, 2026

Trace Crowe stands at 13-under par, one shot clear of Kevin Yu, as darkness forced a suspension of Round 2 at the Austin Championship. Brian Harman is two back at 11-under, with Crowe set to resume Saturday morning before Round 3 begins.

Trace Crowe opened the Austin Championship with a career-best 62 on Thursday and was building on that opening salvo when the lights went out — literally. Darkness forced play to be suspended during Round 2 at the Fazio Canyons Course at Omni Barton Creek Resort & Spa, leaving Crowe at 13-under through 16 holes and in solo possession of the lead heading into what will be an extended Saturday.

Crowe stands one shot clear of Kevin Yu, who fired a 66 in Round 2 to position himself as the most immediate threat. Brian Harman, the 2023 Open Champion, sits two behind Crowe at 11-under after rounds of 67 and 66 — a steadiness that has historically proven dangerous when tournament leaders feel pressure down the stretch.

Crowe's Historic Opening

Crowe's 62 in Round 1 set the tone for his week. That score represented a career-best opening round on the PGA Tour, and the momentum from that performance clearly carried over as he was building another low round before play was halted. Through 16 holes of Round 2, he had maintained his standard and extended his lead.

What makes Crowe's position compelling is the nature of his game — a ball-striker who is comfortable in Texas conditions and who has shown at this event that he can go low repeatedly. If he can close out Round 2 cleanly Saturday morning and carry that form into Round 3, he will be the story of the week in professional golf.

Harman the Dangerous Threat

At 11-under, Brian Harman is not simply a name on a leaderboard — he is a proven major champion who knows how to handle pressure and close tournaments. Harman's 67-66 through two rounds reflects the efficiency and experience that characterizes his game: he avoids the big numbers, makes birdies at the right times, and trusts his short game when scoring slows down.

In a standard 72-hole event with two rounds to play, a two-shot deficit is a comfortable erasing distance for a player of Harman's caliber. His presence in the top three means Crowe cannot afford a single loose round without watching a former major champion make a move.

What's Next

Crowe returns to the course Saturday morning to complete the final two holes of Round 2. After that, the entire field launches into Round 3 — an exhausting but familiar schedule in professional golf when weather forces a schedule compression.

The Austin Championship concludes Sunday, and with the $6 million purse and FedExCup Fall points on the line, every player inside the top 10 will be pushing hard. For Crowe, Saturday represents the opportunity to prove he belongs — and perhaps, by Sunday evening, to celebrate his first PGA Tour title.
