Korda Enters ANNIKA Round 3 Just Four Back — World No. 1 is Dangerous

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | November 14, 2026

Nelly Korda enters Saturday's Round 3 at the ANNIKA driven by Gainbridge at Pelican at 8-under par — four strokes behind the three-way leaders — and her record of making big moves in the final stretch of tournaments makes her the name to watch this weekend.

Four strokes behind. Twenty-four holes to play. Nelly Korda in the field.

That is the situation at the ANNIKA driven by Gainbridge at Pelican entering Round 3, and for the three co-leaders — Carlota Ciganda, Lindy Duncan, and Hye-Jin Choi — it represents an opponent who cannot be dismissed regardless of the gap.

Korda is the world No. 1 for a reason. Her ability to generate scoring runs and manufacture low rounds at Pelican Golf Club, a course she has played well in previous years, makes four strokes a recoverable deficit on a Saturday and Sunday where conditions can favor attack.

What Makes Korda Dangerous

The hallmarks of Korda's game — precision off the tee, elite iron play, and clutch putting — are precisely the ingredients for charging on a Pelican Golf Club layout that rewards accuracy and punishes mistakes. When she gets rolling in the low-30s for nine holes, she can close gaps that look comfortable on a Saturday leaderboard.

At 8-under entering the third round, Korda is well within the range that she has bridged before in LPGA competition. She is not scrambling — she is positioned, waiting for the round where everything falls together. On courses she knows and surfaces that suit her game, that round tends to come.

The Leaders' Challenge

Ciganda, Duncan, and Choi face the dual challenge of competing with each other while keeping one eye on the player chasing from behind. Any of the three leaders who stumbles on Saturday opens the door for Korda to close the gap before Sunday's final round — and once Korda is within striking distance, the leaderboard becomes significantly more complicated for everyone above her.

The mental aspect of holding a lead when the sport's best player is hunting you is not trivial. For Duncan and Choi, relative newcomers to the pressure of leading an LPGA event, Saturday's round will test not just ball-striking but the ability to manage thoughts and trust their games under a microscope.

What's Next

Korda will need to make her move in Round 3. If she reaches double-digits under par by Saturday evening, the ANNIKA at Pelican will have a final-round duel on its hands that the entire women's golf world will be watching.

Four shots is not insurmountable. For Nelly Korda, it is an invitation.
