### STORY 3: LIV Bankruptcy Details

**Long-Form Post — 1:00 PM ET**
The numbers behind LIV Golf's collapse are staggering — and Friday's details make the picture even clearer.

LIV Golf filed its formal bankruptcy plan on November 10, targeting a January 2027 liquidation. The disclosure statement — which outlines the wind-down plan for creditors — is set for a hearing on November 24. At least $45 million is owed to players who competed in the league.

The most eye-opening figure: Jon Rahm's claim reportedly exceeds $100 million from the nine-figure contract he signed when he joined LIV. A judge has approved Rahm's separation from the league, but the financial fallout from that contract is extraordinary.

The Saudi Public Investment Fund had invested approximately $5 billion into LIV before ending support after the 2026 season. BC Partners, which had pledged $300 million for a potential LIV 2.0, also walked away.

What comes next is a messy legal process. What comes after that is a professional golf landscape that will look different from anything we've seen in the past five years.

### STORY 5: Post-LIV Player Futures

**Long-Form Post — 6:00 PM ET**
When LIV Golf finally closes its doors, what happens to the players?

The PGA Tour has been clear: there is no reinstatement pathway until the bankruptcy proceedings are complete. That could take months. For players like Jon Rahm, Sergio Garcia, Tyrrell Hatton, and Louis Oosthuizen — who had already signaled they wouldn't continue with LIV 2.0 — the limbo period is real.

The DP World Tour offers one route back. Its top 10 season finishers earn PGA Tour cards, making it a viable path for marquee names who can compete at the highest level of European golf. But many former LIV players don't have guaranteed membership in the DP World Tour's bigger events, and Q-School is the only other option for some.

Brooks Koepka has already returned to the PGA Tour, with penalties. Bryson DeChambeau's LIV contract expires after the 2026 season, and his next move is unclear. The DP World Tour has also said it will not allow LIV players to compete in 2027, adding another wrinkle to the picture.

The post-LIV era will reshape professional golf. It's just not clear yet exactly how.
