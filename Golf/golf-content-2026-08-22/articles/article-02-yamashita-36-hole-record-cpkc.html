Yamashita Sets 36-Hole Scoring Record at CPKC Women's Open, Leads by Five Over Korda

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap

August 22, 2026

Miyu Yamashita is playing at a level that the CPKC Women's Open has not seen before.

The Japanese star shot a second-round 64 at Royal Mayfair Golf Club in Edmonton on Friday to reach -14 through 36 holes (62-64=126), establishing a new 36-hole scoring record at this tournament. Her lead over the field — specifically over World No. 1 Nelly Korda — stands at five shots heading into Saturday's third round.

Yamashita is not just leading. She is rewriting the tournament's history books while doing it.

A Historic Pace

Yamashita's opening 62 set the tone. Her 64 on Friday extended the statement.

A combined 126 over 36 holes on a par-70 layout is exceptional by any standard. But for it to also represent the scoring record at this specific tournament — played on a Stanley Thompson-designed course at Royal Mayfair that demands precision and course management — makes the number even more meaningful.

Yamashita's ball-striking through two rounds has been elite. Her iron play into Royal Mayfair's greens has given her birdie opportunities that she has consistently converted. The 64 on Friday was not a streak of hot putting masking an average round; it was a complete performance from tee to hole.

Korda in Second

Nelly Korda, the World No. 1, sits five shots back at -9.

The gap is significant but not insurmountable over two rounds. Korda has put together low-60s rounds before when she needs them, and in a $2.75M event on a course she is comfortable with, the motivation is not going to be a problem.

The question is whether Yamashita gives her anything to work with. If Yamashita continues to shoot in the 63-65 range, Korda would need to post something in the low 60s just to pull within two. That is a difficult gap to close against a player in this kind of form.

Other contenders trail further back. Defending champion Brooke Henderson is in the field; Henderson won the 2025 CPKC Women's Open by one shot over Minjee Lee and knows how to navigate Royal Mayfair under pressure. Lydia Ko, who famously won her first professional title at Royal Mayfair in 2013 as a 15-year-old, is also chasing a fourth CPKC title.

What's Next

Round 3 is Saturday at Royal Mayfair GC in Edmonton. Yamashita leads at -14, five shots ahead of Korda at -9. Golf Channel has coverage.

The $2.75M purse is on the line with two rounds remaining. Yamashita needs to maintain her focus against a field that includes the World No. 1 and a defending champion who knows what it takes to close at this tournament.

What Yamashita has done through two rounds is special. Saturday will tell us whether it leads to a title.
