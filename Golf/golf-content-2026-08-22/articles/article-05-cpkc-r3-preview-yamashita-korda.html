CPKC Women's Open R3 Preview: Yamashita's Five-Shot Lead vs. Korda's Chasing Power

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap

August 22, 2026

Saturday at Royal Mayfair Golf Club in Edmonton has one central question: can anyone chase down Miyu Yamashita?

Yamashita enters Round 3 of the CPKC Women's Open at -14, having set the 36-hole tournament scoring record with rounds of 62 and 64. Her lead over the field is five shots. The player in second place — World No. 1 Nelly Korda, at -9 — is the best candidate to make a run.

Two rounds remain at this $2.75M LPGA event. Saturday will tell us whether this is a coronation or a competition.

Yamashita's Position

A five-shot lead after 36 holes is a genuine advantage. History says most 54-hole leaders at LPGA events win. History also says that players who shoot in the low 60s for two consecutive days sometimes give shots back on the third.

The pressure of holding a lead at a major international event on a course where your closest rival is the World No. 1 is a specific kind of pressure. Yamashita will face it Saturday for the first time this week.

Her ball-striking through two rounds has been excellent. Her iron play into Royal Mayfair's par-70 layout — a Stanley Thompson design at 6,452 yards — has been precise and consistent. The question is whether that precision holds when the margin for error tightens emotionally.

The Korda Factor

Nelly Korda does not need to be reminded of what is at stake. She is the world's best women's golfer, and she is five shots back with 36 holes left in a $2.75M tournament.

Korda's ability to shoot low quickly is well-documented. She has closed gaps of this size before. A 63 on Saturday would pull her within two. A 62 would make it a one-shot game heading into Sunday.

The scenario that concerns Yamashita's camp is not Korda slowly chipping away hole by hole — it is Korda going on a birdie run on Royal Mayfair's scoring holes in the middle of the round, making a sudden move that forces Yamashita to respond rather than lead.

Other Contenders

Brooke Henderson, the defending CPKC Women's Open champion, is in the field. Henderson won the 2025 edition by one shot over Minjee Lee and has a deep connection to this tournament, this venue, and this Edmonton crowd. A Canadian player winning on home soil in front of 15 or more Canadian players in the field is one of this tournament's signature storylines every year.

Lydia Ko is also in the field chasing a fourth CPKC title. Ko won her first LPGA event at Royal Mayfair in 2013 at age 15 — the youngest LPGA winner in history at the time. This course and this tournament have a special place in her career.

Whether Henderson or Ko can make up ground on Yamashita's lead is another Saturday subplot worth watching.

What's Next

Round 3 tees off Saturday at Royal Mayfair GC in Edmonton. Golf Channel has coverage. Yamashita leads at -14, five shots over Korda at -9 with 36 holes remaining.

For Yamashita, Saturday is about staying in front. For Korda, it is about closing the gap enough that Sunday becomes a real contest. For everyone else in the field, it is about making enough noise to force both leaders to pay attention.

The CPKC Women's Open has produced historic scoring so far this week. Saturday has every reason to continue the trend.
