Scheffler Trails by Seven After BMW R2, But Back-Nine 30 Signals His Game Is Arriving

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap

August 22, 2026

Scottie Scheffler arrived at Bellerive Country Club as the World No. 1 and the FedExCup's top seed. He played the first round like neither of those things.

His opening +2 on Thursday left him in T44 and eight shots off the five-way lead. The BMW Championship was already looking like a week where Scheffler would need the weekend to find meaning beyond protecting his East Lake position.

Then came Friday's back nine.

Back Nine 30

Scheffler's Round 2 at Bellerive was not a complete transformation. He did not suddenly vault into contention. But his back nine — a 30, four under par in a stretch of holes — was a signal that the elite version of his game is available at this golf course.

He finished the round at -5 overall through 36 holes. From +2 to -5 is a seven-shot swing in one day, and it changes the conversation about what this week can be for Scheffler.

Seven shots back of Wyndham Clark is still a significant deficit. Clark has been playing extraordinary golf — 64-64 — and has given no indication of coming back to the field. Scheffler would need Clark to slip significantly and would need to shoot something in the 61-63 range on Saturday to have any realistic chance of winning this tournament.

Elite players have done it before. It is unlikely. But the back-nine 30 says Scheffler is playing for something this weekend, not just going through the motions.

The Bigger Picture

Scheffler's East Lake spot is not at risk regardless of what happens at Bellerive. He entered the BMW Championship as the FedExCup's top seed and has enough cushion that a poor week would not drop him out of the top 30.

But that context does not fully explain why a back-nine 30 matters. Scheffler competes to win, and the start of his BMW Championship — a first round that was not representative of his capabilities — has given him something to prove in his own terms.

The best version of Scheffler at Bellerive is a player who can threaten for the title. That version showed up on the back nine Friday afternoon.

What's Next

Round 3 is Saturday at Bellerive. Scheffler sits at -5, seven back of Clark. He needs two exceptional rounds to give himself a chance at the title. Clark needs to stumble. Both things are possible; neither is probable.

Watch Scheffler's front nine Saturday. If he continues the momentum from Friday's back nine, the leaderboard could look different by Saturday afternoon. If he opens flat again, the weekend becomes about preparing for East Lake.

The World No. 1 is not done at this tournament. How much he has left is Saturday's question.
