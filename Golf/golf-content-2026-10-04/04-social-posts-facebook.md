### STORY 3: LIV Golf Deadline — Nine Days to Decide

**Long-Form Post — 1:00 PM ET**
Professional golf's future gets a little clearer in nine days — and murkier between now and then.

LIV Golf filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in New Jersey court on September 8, 2026, with estimated liabilities of $500 million to $1 billion. The Saudi Public Investment Fund, which had poured roughly $6 billion into the league since 2022, ended its funding. BC Partners, the London-based private equity firm, stepped in with a $300 million restructuring proposal.

The catch: players must commit to LIV 2.0 by October 13. Under the term sheet, 50% of players by count must sign on, and the combined value of their claims must represent at least two-thirds of total player claims for the deal to go through.

The LIV 2.0 pitch — equity for players, 10 events in 2027, greater freedom to compete elsewhere — is compelling on paper. But Bryson DeChambeau is reportedly still undecided. Jon Rahm and Tyrrell Hatton have been linked to potential departures.

Nine days. The future of professional golf's alternative circuit hangs in the balance.

### STORY 4: Jon Rahm — $7.47M LIV Bankruptcy Claim

**Long-Form Post — 3:30 PM ET**
Jon Rahm signed a reported $300 million deal to join LIV Golf in late 2023. He was the league's highest-profile signing, the face of its ambition to rival the PGA Tour.

Now he is the league's largest unsecured creditor.

LIV Golf's Chapter 11 bankruptcy filing lists Rahm as owed $7,472,527.47 under a Player Participation Agreement — the biggest individual player claim in the filing. His Legion XIII team, which includes Tyrrell Hatton, Tom McKibbin, Caleb Surratt, and Max Rottluff, faces an uncertain operational future.

Rahm won the 2026 LIV individual standings. He is owed millions that the bankrupt league cannot currently pay. His future — whether that is LIV 2.0, a PGA Tour return, or something else — remains unresolved ahead of the October 13 commitment deadline.

The financial reality of a bankrupt league: the biggest signing is now the biggest creditor.
