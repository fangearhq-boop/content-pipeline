LIV Golf Players Have Nine Days to Decide — October 13 Deadline Looms as League's Future Hangs in Balance

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | October 4, 2026

The future of LIV Golf will come into clearer focus on October 13. That is the deadline — 35 days from the league's Chapter 11 bankruptcy filing on September 8 — by which players must commit to LIV 2.0, the proposed restructured league backed by London-based private equity firm BC Partners.

Nine days remain. Bryson DeChambeau, one of the league's highest-profile names, is reportedly still undecided. Jon Rahm and Tyrrell Hatton have been linked to potential departures. And the structural requirements of the deal mean that if not enough players sign on, the whole arrangement collapses.

Professional golf has been through a great deal since LIV launched in 2022. The next nine days may determine how much of it survives into 2027.

What the Deal Requires

BC Partners has proposed $300 million in total financing to rebuild LIV Golf around a new ownership model. Under the term sheet, the deal only proceeds if two conditions are met: at least 50% of players by count must commit, and the combined value of their unsecured claims must represent at least two-thirds of the total player claims in the bankruptcy filing.

Both thresholds need to be cleared simultaneously. A majority of players by headcount is not sufficient on its own — the two-thirds of total claim value requirement means that the highest-earning players, those who signed the biggest deals, carry disproportionate weight in whether the deal goes through.

Jon Rahm, whose Player Participation Agreement claim is the largest in the filing at more than $7 million, is therefore particularly important to the deal's viability. If Rahm opts out, the value threshold becomes significantly harder to reach.

What LIV 2.0 Offers

The pitch to players centres on several structural changes from the original LIV model. Under LIV 2.0, players would be majority owners of the league — a significant shift from the original structure, which positioned them as employees of Saudi-funded team franchises. Individual commercial rights — the ability to sign personal sponsors and endorsements freely — would be restored. And players would have considerably greater freedom to compete outside LIV Golf events, addressing one of the original model's most contentious restrictions.

The proposed 2027 schedule includes 10 events: five in the United States and five internationally. That is a reduced calendar from the league's original ambitions, reflecting the financial constraints of the restructuring.

Who's Undecided, Who's Out

DeChambeau, who signed a four-year, $125 million deal in 2022, has his contract expiring at the end of 2026 regardless of whether LIV 2.0 proceeds. Reports indicate he is still negotiating and has not confirmed participation in the new structure. His decision carries weight both as a player and as a claim holder in the bankruptcy proceedings.

Rahm and Hatton have been linked to potential exits, though neither has officially declared a departure. The PGA Tour's Returning Members Program has remained available as a pathway for LIV players.

What's Next

October 13 is the hard deadline under the term sheet. After that date, the picture becomes clearer: either LIV 2.0 has the player commitment it needs to proceed, or the restructuring deal collapses and the league faces a more uncertain path forward.

For professional golf broadly, the resolution of LIV's situation will significantly shape the competitive landscape heading into 2027 — determining where dozens of prominent players will compete and under what terms.
