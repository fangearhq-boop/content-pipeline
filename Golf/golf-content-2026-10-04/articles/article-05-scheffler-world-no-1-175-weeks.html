Scottie Scheffler Holds World No. 1 — 175-Plus Weeks at the Top and the Fall Season Just Beginning

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | October 4, 2026

Scottie Scheffler is not finished.

The World No. 1 golfer carries an Official World Golf Ranking of 14.21 points into the PGA Tour fall season — nearly five points clear of Rory McIlroy, who sits second at 9.84. Scheffler's run at the top of the rankings now spans 175 or more career weeks, a sustained period of dominance that has redefined what it means to be the best golfer in the world.

In 2026 alone, he won the FedEx Cup, earned over $30.9 million in prize money across three wins and 13 top-10 finishes, and extended a lead over McIlroy that was already substantial. The fall season — a stretch of events from October through the end of the year — offers more opportunities to add to those totals.

The Scale of the Lead

Numbers put Scheffler's position in context. The 14.21-to-9.84 gap over McIlroy in the OWGR represents roughly a 45% advantage in average points. In the history of the official rankings, very few players have maintained that kind of lead over an extended period.

Cameron Young, the current No. 3, sits further back. The world rankings beneath Scheffler are populated by excellent players — McIlroy, Young, and others — but none of them have been able to close the gap consistently over the course of 2026.

Part of the reason is that Scheffler wins at events where winning is hard. His three victories in 2026 have come at high-quality fields on courses that demand complete games. His 13 top-10 finishes indicate that even when he is not winning, he is collecting points and maintaining his position.

The FedEx Cup and What It Represents

The FedEx Cup is professional golf's season-long points competition, and winning it requires sustained excellence across the full schedule — not just peak performances at majors or designated events. Scheffler's 2026 FedEx Cup title, his second, reflects a player who shows up at a high level consistently throughout the year.

His $30.9 million in 2026 earnings — a figure that leads the PGA Tour by a significant margin — tells the same story. McIlroy's earnings were approximately $11.5 million for the season, a strong number in its own right, but a fraction of Scheffler's total.

The financial gap is a reflection of the performance gap. Scheffler has been, by any measurable metric, the best player in the world for an extended period.

What's Next

The fall season offers events that will determine the final FedExCup standings and provide a bridge to the 2027 schedule. Scheffler will be the name everyone watches and the benchmark everyone chases across those remaining events.

McIlroy, Young, and others will look for late-season momentum ahead of next year. But based on the 2026 season, anyone trying to unseat Scheffler from the world's top spot faces a very steep hill to climb.
