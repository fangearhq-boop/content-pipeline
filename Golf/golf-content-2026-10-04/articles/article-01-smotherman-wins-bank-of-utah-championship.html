Austin Smotherman Wins Bank of Utah Championship — First Career PGA Tour Victory at Black Desert Resort

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | October 4, 2026

Austin Smotherman is a PGA Tour champion.

The professional golfer who had made himself a consistent fixture on the PGA Tour — present and competitive, but always waiting for the moment everything came together — got that moment Sunday at Black Desert Resort Golf Course in Ivins, Utah. A final score of 17-under par at the Bank of Utah Championship, one shot ahead of Kevin Roy, delivered Smotherman his first career tour victory.

It is the kind of win that professional golf makes people wait for. Some players get theirs early. Others grind for years, building the game, learning the tour, absorbing the lessons of near-misses, before the right week at the right course finally arrives. For Smotherman, that week was October 1-4, 2026, at one of the sport's newer and most visually striking venues.

How the Week Unfolded

Smotherman set the foundation for this victory over the first two days. He opened with a 63, putting himself near the top of the leaderboard early. His second round — a 62, a career-low score — was the round that separated him from the field. By the start of Moving Day on Saturday, Smotherman held a one-shot advantage over Roy, the two players paired together in the final group.

That pairing continued into Sunday. Roy, to his credit, gave Smotherman a genuine test. At 16-under, Roy was close enough to make every decision on the back nine matter. The margin at the end — one stroke — captured how tight the final day was.

But Smotherman did not yield. He made the putts and the plays that a player needs to make when a first Tour win is on the line, and when the round was over, his name was at the top of the leaderboard.

Black Desert Resort as the Setting

Black Desert Resort Golf Course, set against the red rock landscape of Ivins, Utah near St. George, is one of the newer additions to the PGA Tour's fall rotation. The course's dramatic visual backdrop — black lava rock formations against the desert terrain — makes it one of the more distinctive venues on the calendar.

The Bank of Utah Championship offers a $6 million purse and 500 FedExCup points, making it a meaningful event within the fall schedule. Smotherman's win will earn him a substantial check and, more importantly, an exemption that extends his presence on the tour for the foreseeable future.

What's Next

The fall season continues across the PGA Tour, with multiple events remaining before the calendar turns to 2027. Smotherman will return to the field as a different player now — a winner, with the exemption status and the confidence that comes with it.

Kevin Roy, who pushed him all week, will look to turn a strong showing into a victory of his own in the weeks ahead. At 16-under, Roy played some of his best golf of the season. A first win for him may not be far away either.

For Smotherman, the Bank of Utah Championship is the line that separates what came before from what comes next.
