Jon Rahm Is LIV Golf's Largest Unsecured Creditor — $7.47 Million Claim in Chapter 11 Filing

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | October 4, 2026

When Jon Rahm left the PGA Tour to join LIV Golf in late 2023, it was the sport's most consequential defection. He signed a reported $300 million deal — the most money ever committed to bring a single player into the Saudi-backed league. He was LIV's statement signing: proof that the circuit could attract not just past-their-prime veterans but an active major champion in the prime of his career.

Now, with LIV Golf in Chapter 11 bankruptcy, Rahm is the league's largest unsecured creditor — listed in the filing as being owed $7,472,527.47 under a Player Participation Agreement.

The story of LIV Golf, told through a single data point, is this: the player they paid the most to come is now the player they owe the most.

What the Filing Shows

LIV Golf filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in New Jersey on September 8, 2026. The filing revealed estimated liabilities of between $500 million and $1 billion. The Saudi Public Investment Fund, which had committed roughly $6 billion to the league since its 2022 launch, ended its funding.

Among the creditors listed in the filing, player claims are a significant category. Rahm's claim — $7,472,527.47 under his Player Participation Agreement — is the largest individual player claim in the bankruptcy documents. Other players are also listed as creditors, and under the proposed BC Partners restructuring deal, their willingness to commit to LIV 2.0 will determine whether the league continues at all.

Rahm's LIV Career

Rahm led the 2026 LIV Golf individual standings with 1,057.54 points, having been the league's dominant individual performer. His Legion XIII team — which includes Tyrrell Hatton, Tom McKibbin, Caleb Surratt, and Max Rottluff — won the team competition.

The competitive results were strong. The financial arrangement, as the bankruptcy filing makes clear, was not. Payments under his Player Participation Agreement fell short, leaving a claim of more than $7 million outstanding.

Rahm's reported contract was structured over multiple years, and the $7.47 million claim represents the currently outstanding and unpaid portion — not the total value of his original deal.

The October 13 Decision

Rahm now faces a choice with significant financial and professional implications. Under the LIV 2.0 restructuring term sheet, his commitment would meaningfully help the deal reach its required two-thirds threshold of total player claim value. Without Rahm, that threshold is harder to meet.

Reports have linked both Rahm and teammate Tyrrell Hatton to potential departures from LIV, though neither has confirmed a decision. The PGA Tour's Returning Members Program remains an available option. The October 13 deadline for player commitments is nine days away.

For the player who bet his career on LIV Golf — and who LIV Golf bet its credibility on — the next nine days may determine where one of the best golfers in the world plays in 2027 and beyond.

What's Next

The October 13 commitment deadline will clarify Rahm's path forward. Whether he commits to LIV 2.0, pursues a PGA Tour return, or explores other options, his decision will carry implications that extend well beyond his own career. The fate of the BC Partners restructuring deal may depend partly on which direction he chooses.
