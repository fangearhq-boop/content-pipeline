Brooks Koepka Returns to the PGA Tour — The Most Significant LIV Departure of 2026

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | August 7, 2026

Four years after becoming one of LIV Golf's original marquee signings, Brooks Koepka is back on the PGA Tour.

The five-time major champion has returned to the Tour via the Returning Member Program, the mechanism established to allow players who had competed on LIV Golf to rejoin the PGA Tour after a defined transfer window. Koepka's return, confirmed through Golf Monthly's reporting on the 2026 transfer window, marks the most significant individual defection from LIV Golf to the PGA Tour this year.

His departure from LIV arrives at a moment of maximum uncertainty for the breakaway league. CEO Scott O'Neil announced on August 5 that LIV's board has approved a lead investor agreement and unveiled "LIV 2.0" — a restructured format with player majority ownership and 10 events beginning in 2027. The announcement came after weeks of reports suggesting LIV was awaiting critical funding with its final two 2026 events potentially in jeopardy.

Whether Koepka's return to the PGA Tour was influenced by LIV's instability or was a decision made independent of the league's financial situation has not been confirmed. What is clear is that he is back — and the timing is hard to ignore.

Five Majors. Four Years Away.

Koepka's record is one of the most distinctive in modern major championship history. He won the US Open in consecutive years (2017 and 2018) and the PGA Championship in consecutive years (2018 and 2019), making him the first player in the modern era to win back-to-back in two different majors. He added a fifth major at The Open Championship in 2024 while competing on LIV Golf.

He was among the first — and most high-profile — players to sign with LIV when the league launched in the summer of 2022. His departure at the time signaled that LIV could attract not just players in the later stages of their careers or those who had never broken through at the highest level, but genuine major champions in their prime competitive years.

The conversation around LIV's early signings inevitably centered on Dustin Johnson, Phil Mickelson, and a handful of others — but Koepka, with back-to-back wins in two separate major championships on his résumé, was a different order of defection. His presence gave LIV a certain credibility as a genuine competitive entity, not simply a retirement vehicle.

The Returning Member Program

The Returning Member Program is the formal mechanism through which LIV players can rejoin the PGA Tour following the transfer window process. The program exists as part of the evolving framework between the Tour and its departing and returning players — a recognition that the line between the two competing circuits has remained fluid in both directions since 2022.

Koepka's use of the program puts him in position to compete on the PGA Tour's schedule going forward, including in major championships and the FedExCup structure. At 36, with five majors and a competitive record that places him among the best players of his generation, his return to the Tour is not a sentimental homecoming — it is a competitive choice from a player who still intends to win at the highest level.

What It Means for LIV

Koepka's departure raises a question that LIV's restructuring announcement does not fully answer: how many more players will make the same choice?

LIV 2.0, with its player ownership model and new investor backing, is designed to be an attractive proposition. But the transition period — the period between the announcement of a restructuring and the actual commencement of LIV 2.0 operations in 2027 — is exactly the kind of uncertainty that pushes players toward the known stability of the PGA Tour's existing framework.

The Returning Member Program exists precisely because this kind of movement was anticipated. Whether Koepka's return triggers a wave of departures or represents an isolated individual decision will become clearer as LIV navigates its own reinvention over the coming months.

For now, the most decorated major champion still competing on LIV Golf's roster has left the building. And the PGA Tour has him back.
