FedExCup Playoffs Begin August 12 — The Full Preview From St. Jude to East Lake

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | August 7, 2026

The regular season ends Sunday at the Wyndham Championship. The postseason starts Tuesday at TPC Southwind. For the top players in professional golf, the FedExCup Playoffs are the only thing that matters now — and the chase for a $25 million prize begins in less than a week.

Here is everything you need to know about this year's playoff structure, the key storylines heading in, and what is at stake at each stop on the road to East Lake.

The Playoff Structure

The FedExCup Playoffs consist of three events, each with a progressively smaller field as players are eliminated from contention.

St. Jude Championship | August 12–16 | TPC Southwind, Memphis, Tennessee

The first playoff event takes the top 70 players from the FedExCup standings after the Wyndham Championship wraps on Sunday. TPC Southwind is one of the most demanding tracks on the PGA Tour calendar — a course where driving precision matters more than distance, and where the field will be separated quickly under playoff pressure. The top 50 players after St. Jude advance to the BMW Championship.

BMW Championship | August 20–23 | Bellerive Country Club, St. Louis, Missouri

Bellerive Country Club, which hosted the 2018 PGA Championship, returns as the site of the second playoff event. The field narrows to 50 players, and the competitive tension increases accordingly — every player at Bellerive knows that finishing outside the top 30 means their season ends there. The course's length and demanding rough historically reward complete players who can drive the ball and scramble when necessary. The top 30 after BMW advance to East Lake.

Tour Championship | August 27–30 | East Lake Golf Club, Atlanta, Georgia

The FedExCup season concludes where it always does — at East Lake, the historic course in Atlanta that serves as the permanent home of the Tour Championship. The 30-player field competes for the FedExCup title and the $25 million prize awarded to the overall champion. The staggered starting score format means that the FedExCup points leader enters with an advantage, which makes the standings at each preceding event critically important — not just as a survival mechanism, but as a competitive tool for the championship event itself.

The Points Leader Enters With a Target

Scottie Scheffler heads into the playoffs as the FedExCup points leader with 4,123 points across 17 events. The World No. 1 — who has not won since the American Express in January — will receive the maximum starting advantage at East Lake if he maintains his points lead through the first two playoff events.

Rory McIlroy, a four-time FedExCup champion, is among the other key names in the playoff field. McIlroy's record in the postseason format is unmatched — no player has navigated the playoff structure more successfully over the past decade — and he arrives with the institutional knowledge of what it takes to close at East Lake.

The Bubble Drama at Wyndham

Before the playoffs can begin, the final 70 spots must be confirmed. The Wyndham Championship serves as the last opportunity for players on the fringes of the top 70 to secure their postseason futures.

Jordan Spieth entered the week sitting at exactly No. 50 — firmly in the field but not safely insulated from a bad week pushing him out. He compounded his anxiety by hitting his opening tee shot out of bounds in Round 1, though his performance across the remaining rounds will determine whether he holds his spot.

For players outside the top 70 who need a win to force their way into the field — most notably Beau Hossler, who sits outside the playoff cutline — the Wyndham is not just another tournament. It is the only tournament that can save their season.

The Prize That Ends It All

At the end of three weeks, one player will stand at East Lake and collect the $25 million FedExCup prize. It is the largest prize in professional golf — a number that reflects both the commercial value of the Tour's postseason format and the expectation that the player who wins it has competed at the highest level for the better part of eight months.

The path from Memphis to Atlanta is not long in miles. In competitive difficulty, it is among the most demanding runs in professional sports. The field starts at 70 and ends at 30. The money increases at every stop. The pressure compounds with every missed cut and every player who falls short of the threshold.

It begins Tuesday at TPC Southwind. The 2026 FedExCup race is almost over, and nothing that happens now will happen quietly.
