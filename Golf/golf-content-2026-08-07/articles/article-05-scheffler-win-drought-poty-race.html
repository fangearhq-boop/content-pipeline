Scheffler's Win Drought: World No. 1 Is Seven Months Without a Victory — and the POTY Race Has Never Been More Open

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | August 7, 2026

Scottie Scheffler has spent 201 weeks at the top of the Official World Golf Ranking. He has won the Jack Nicklaus Award as PGA Tour Player of the Year four consecutive times — 2022 through 2025. He leads the FedExCup standings with 4,123 points. He holds the No. 1 ranking by a significant margin.

He also has not won a tournament since January 19.

The numbers exist in an uncomfortable tension — the most dominant player in the world by every aggregate measure, accumulating points and maintaining rankings without the one result that defines a season: a win. Since his 20th career PGA Tour victory at the American Express in January 2026 — a commanding 27-under, four-shot performance — Scheffler has finished runner-up five times without converting once at the top.

Heading into the FedExCup Playoffs, which begin August 12 at the St. Jude Championship, that tension is the defining story of the best player in the game.

Five Chances. Zero Wins.

Five runner-up finishes is not a streak of bad luck. It is a pattern that raises legitimate questions about the distance between where Scheffler's game currently sits and where it needs to be to close out tournaments against the best fields in the world.

The American Express in January was the picture of dominance. What followed was a sequence of near-misses that, individually, could each be explained by circumstance — a hot putter from a rival, a closing back nine that didn't quite cooperate, the inherent randomness of professional golf at its highest level. Collectively, five runner-ups across seven months is harder to attribute to chance.

What makes it stranger is that Scheffler's underlying numbers continue to reflect elite-level play. He does not appear to be struggling; he appears to be doing nearly everything right without getting the final piece to land correctly. Whether that represents a mental block, a marginal deficiency in close-out situations, or simply variance extending beyond its expected range is a question without a definitive answer.

The POTY Race Is the Most Open Since 2021

Four consecutive Player of the Year awards is an unprecedented streak by any modern standard. If 2026 ends without a win for Scheffler — or if a rival closes the gap with a dominant late-season run — the streak faces a real threat for the first time since it began.

CBS Sports Power 18 analysis has described the 2026 POTY race as the most open since 2021, pointing to the performances of several players who have kept themselves in the conversation while Scheffler has accumulated points without victories.

Cameron Young, currently ranked No. 3 in the world, has been rising through the season and remains a primary contender for the award. Tommy Fleetwood, consistent across the FedExCup events and the majors calendar, is in the conversation. Bryson DeChambeau, currently competing on LIV Golf but still factored into broader discussions about the sport's top performers, has also been cited in POTY analysis.

None of them have the résumé Scheffler does. All of them are closer to him on the 2026-specific ledger than they would normally be.

The Playoff Window

Three events remain in the 2026 FedExCup season: St. Jude (August 12–16), the BMW Championship (August 20–23), and the Tour Championship (August 27–30). All three carry maximum pressure. All three offer Scheffler the chance to add a win — or multiple wins — to a 2026 résumé that is otherwise excellent but incomplete.

As FedExCup points leader, Scheffler enters the playoff structure in the best possible position. He will receive the maximum starting advantage at the Tour Championship if his lead holds through St. Jude and BMW. On paper, he is the overwhelming favorite to win the FedExCup title and the $25 million prize.

The gap between being the favorite and winning is, of course, exactly what seven months of runner-ups has been examining.

The streak of five consecutive second-place finishes is not a catastrophe. Scheffler's 2026 season — even without a win since January — is one of the best in the sport. But the playoffs are when the sport's narrative simplifies down to its most essential version: who wins. Accumulation, consistency, and ranking points all feed into the structure. At East Lake, in late August, what matters is whether Scheffler can put the streak away.

He has three tournaments to find out. Given who he is — and given what he has done before — betting against him remains a mistake. But the POTY race is genuinely open in a way it has not been in years, and the window to change that belongs equally to everyone who has chased him across the 2026 season.

It starts next Tuesday in Memphis. For Scheffler, and for everyone trying to beat him, the time for accumulating is over.
