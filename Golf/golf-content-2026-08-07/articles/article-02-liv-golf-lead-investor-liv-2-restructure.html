LIV Golf Secures Lead Investor and Unveils "LIV 2.0" — Players to Hold Majority Equity in Restructured League

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | August 7, 2026

LIV Golf's future has been confirmed — and it looks fundamentally different from the league that launched in 2022.

CEO Scott O'Neil announced on August 5, 2026, that LIV Golf's board of directors has approved a lead investor agreement for the restructured entity. The investor's identity has not yet been disclosed, but multiple sources reported the investment at $250 million or more, with more than a dozen additional parties having expressed interest in taking minority stakes in the new entity.

The headline structural change: players will hold a majority equity stake in the restructured league. The format will shift to 10 events — five domestic US, five international — under the banner now being called "LIV 2.0," with operations beginning in 2027.

The announcement marks the most significant development in LIV Golf's short, turbulent history since the league launched as a Saudi-backed disruption to professional golf's established order.

The Saudi PIF Exit

To understand the weight of this announcement, it helps to understand what it replaces.

Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund bankrolled LIV Golf from its inception, investing a reported $5 billion in the league since 2022. PIF's backing made possible the blockbuster player contracts, the global event footprint, and the sustained financial pressure on the PGA Tour that reshaped the sport's commercial landscape across four seasons.

In April 2026, PIF announced it would end all funding at the close of the year. The sovereign wealth fund, whose sports investment portfolio spans everything from Premier League football to Formula 1, was stepping back from its most provocative bet.

That announcement left LIV Golf in a precarious position. Reports surfaced in the weeks that followed suggesting the league was awaiting approximately $400 million in replacement funding and might be forced to cancel its final two 2026 events if the money did not materialize. The specter of LIV Golf simply running out of road before it could pivot began to feel genuinely credible.

O'Neil's August 5 announcement put those fears to rest — at least for now.

What LIV 2.0 Looks Like

The restructured league will operate under a player ownership model, a concept that LIV's critics noted was absent from the original design. When LIV launched, the value proposition to players was straightforward: guaranteed money, no cut format, a different competitive environment. Ownership was never part of the pitch.

Under LIV 2.0, that changes. Players holding majority equity means the league's financial success — or failure — flows directly back to the athletes competing in it. It is a model closer to what exists in some NBA franchise structures and in various player-owned sports ventures. Whether it works for a golf league with LIV's particular history and roster dynamics remains to be determined.

The 10-event format — five in the United States, five internationally — is a reduction from LIV's previous footprint. The tighter schedule may reflect a more sustainable operating model or simply the reality of building around a new financial structure. What it signals is a league designed to survive rather than simply to disrupt.

Bryson DeChambeau and the Player Question

For LIV's current players, the restructuring raises immediate practical questions. Sports Illustrated reported that Bryson DeChambeau has been told to expect a new contract under the restructured entity, suggesting that player agreements from the original LIV format will need to be renegotiated or reconfirmed under the new ownership structure.

DeChambeau has been one of LIV's most visible faces — and one of the players whose return to the PGA Tour has been most frequently speculated about, particularly as LIV's financial situation grew uncertain. A new contract under LIV 2.0 would represent a commitment to the restructured league.

Not every LIV player is taking that path. Brooks Koepka, one of the original signings when LIV launched in 2022, has returned to the PGA Tour via the Returning Member Program. His exit, and the timing of it — coinciding almost exactly with LIV's announcement of its restructuring — has raised questions about whether others will follow.

A New Chapter for Golf's Most Contentious Experiment

LIV Golf arrives at this inflection point having fundamentally altered the economics of professional golf. Player contracts on the PGA Tour changed. The conversation about guaranteed money, rest weeks, and competitive structure shifted permanently. Whatever one thinks of LIV's origins — and opinions across the golf world remain sharply divided — the league reshaped the sport it entered.

LIV 2.0 is an attempt to write the next chapter without Saudi sovereign wealth as the primary author. Whether it succeeds will depend on how the unnamed investor and the new player ownership structure hold together under the pressures of running a professional sports league in a sport where the PGA Tour and DP World Tour still set the competitive agenda.

The announcement of a lead investor was the necessary first step. What follows it — the player rosters, the event calendar, the television deals — will determine whether LIV 2.0 is a genuine reinvention or a managed wind-down dressed up in new language.

For now, the league is alive. O'Neil and his board have bought themselves the runway to find out.
