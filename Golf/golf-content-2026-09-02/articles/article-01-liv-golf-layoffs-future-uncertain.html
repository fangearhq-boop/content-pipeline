LIV Golf Lays Off Majority of Staff After 2026 Season, Future of LIV 2.0 Still Uncertain

By Jake Torres

September 2, 2026

LIV Golf has informed the majority of its more than 300 employees that their jobs are ending, arriving three days after the circuit's 2026 season concluded at Indianapolis. The Saudi Public Investment Fund, which has bankrolled the league since its launch, is stepping away following a reported total investment of more than $5 billion. A prospective replacement investor exists — but the deal is not done, and the league's future beyond this year remains unresolved.

BC Partners and the Unfinished Deal

BC Partners, a London-based private equity firm with ties to GSE Worldwide — a talent agency that represents a significant portion of LIV's roster — has signed a term sheet in the range of $250 million to $350 million. LIV Golf CEO Scott O'Neil confirmed the agreement was "signed and approved" at the board level. But a final transaction has not been completed. The league had an informal deadline around September 1 that has now passed without a closing.

Sources close to the league say nearly every meaningful structural question remains open: whether the financing is equity or debt, whether additional investors need to join BC Partners, and whether enough high-profile players must formally recommit before the transaction can be called real. Employees in the United States and United Kingdom were warned of possible redundancies back in July, and that warning has now been acted upon.

What LIV 2.0 Would Look Like

The plan being pitched to potential investors and players is a substantially restructured league. LIV 2.0, as described, would run 10 events in 2027 — five international and five in the United States — down from the 14-event schedule of recent years. Event purses would be reduced. In exchange, players would become "majority equity holders" in the new structure, shifting the financial model away from Saudi-funded appearance fees toward a player-ownership stake.

Commissioner Greg Norman has declined to guarantee that a 2027 season will happen. Ian Poulter, one of the original LIV signatories, said publicly that "time might run out." Jon Rahm won his third consecutive LIV individual title in 2026. La Sasso won the Indianapolis finale. More than 50 contracted players are now in a holding pattern, waiting to see whether the league they joined — some surrendering Ryder Cup eligibility to do so — will still exist next year.

What's Next

The BC Partners transaction is the only credible path to a LIV 2027 season. If the deal closes in September, roster decisions and schedule announcements would need to follow quickly. If it does not close, the league's future becomes substantially more uncertain. The golf world is watching a $5 billion experiment reach a crossroads, and the answer is expected — but not guaranteed — before the end of the month.
