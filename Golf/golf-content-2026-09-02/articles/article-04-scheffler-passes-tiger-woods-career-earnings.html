Scottie Scheffler Passes Tiger Woods as PGA Tour's All-Time Career Earnings Leader

By Jake Torres

September 2, 2026

Scottie Scheffler is now the leading career earner in PGA Tour history. His $10 million victory at the Tour Championship pushed his official career earnings to $130,390,661, passing Tiger Woods' official PGA Tour total of $120,999,166. Scheffler reached the record at age 30 — two months after his birthday — in a season that was already historic before the final number landed.

The Numbers Behind the Record

In 2026 alone, Scheffler won three times on the official PGA Tour circuit: the American Express ($1,656,000), the FedEx St. Jude Championship ($3,600,000), and the Tour Championship ($10,000,000). His official 2026 season earnings of more than $15 million made him the first player in PGA Tour history to surpass $30 million in official earnings in a single season. Add the separate $23 million FedExCup bonus — not counted in official money — and his combined 2026 take was $53,937,525.

The record is official PGA Tour money only. Tiger's career earnings figure does not include his substantial international earnings; Scheffler's doesn't either. What the comparison does reflect is sustained performance on the PGA Tour across many seasons, with Scheffler's trajectory dramatically accelerated by a purse-inflation era that Tiger's prime predated.

What the Career Numbers Mean

The career résumé behind the earnings figure is equally striking. Scheffler has 22 PGA Tour victories. He has won the Masters twice. He has captured two FedExCup titles, in 2024 and again in 2026. He has been the world No. 1 ranked player for five consecutive seasons — an unprecedented run of sustained dominance at the top of the rankings. He has not had a season since 2022 without at least two wins.

Scheffler's caddie Ted Scott is reportedly collecting more in annual earnings than many tour professionals — a reflection of what it means to carry the bag for the sport's best player through an era of dramatically increased prize pools. The wealth generated around Scheffler has become a story in itself.

What's Next

Scheffler leads the United States team into the Presidents Cup at Medinah Country Club on September 24-27. After the team event, the PGA Tour Fall schedule continues through November. At 30 years old with no physical concerns and a game built on precision rather than power, the record he just set may not hold for long — assuming the person most likely to break it is himself.
