Scheffler Enters BMW Championship as Overwhelming FedExCup Favorite After 8-Stroke St. Jude Win

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap

Scottie Scheffler won the FedEx St. Jude Championship last week by 8 strokes — 17-under at TPC Southwind, with Si Woo Kim left as the runner-up from a distance that made the leaderboard look less like a competition and more like a demonstration. He enters the BMW Championship at Bellerive Country Club in St. Louis as the No. 1 FedExCup seed, more than 1,000 points clear of the No. 2 position, with $23 million in bonus money already locked in. The only question that remains — who can beat him? — does not have an obvious answer.

The St. Jude Performance in Context

An 8-stroke victory margin in a FedExCup Playoff event is not the result of a field having a bad week. It is the result of one player having an extraordinary one. Scheffler's 17-under total at TPC Southwind placed him in a category that playoff-event performances rarely inhabit — dominant enough to make the second, third, and fourth-place finishers feel irrelevant to the outcome from well before the final round began.

That kind of performance creates momentum that is difficult to quantify but impossible to ignore. Players who win by 8 strokes do not typically arrive at their next event fragile. They arrive with the evidence of what they can do still fresh in the field's memory — and in their own.

The FedExCup Position

Scheffler's FedExCup points total is not merely first — it is first by a margin that has effectively decoupled the standings discussion from the competition for the top position. Over 1,000 points separating him from No. 2 means that the FedExCup title, and the bonus structure that comes with it, is already determined at the top. He has locked in $23 million in additional earnings for holding the No. 1 position through the BMW Championship.

The financial context matters because the FedExCup bonus pool is one of professional golf's most significant non-tournament prize payouts. Scheffler has captured the top tier before the Tour Championship has even begun. Winning at Bellerive would add further to that total.

What the Field Faces

Rory McIlroy, Xander Schauffele, Collin Morikawa, Cameron Young, and Hideki Matsuyama are all at Bellerive, and all of them are capable of winning any golf tournament on any given week. The BMW Championship field is strong by construction — only the top 50 FedExCup earners qualified. But none of the players in the field arrives carrying what Scheffler carries: a freshly completed 8-stroke Playoff victory and a season that has produced multiple wins and now an all-but-certain FedExCup title.

The task for someone to win at Bellerive is not impossible. It is simply the hardest assignment in professional golf this week, because the player they need to beat is operating at a level that the rest of the field will need their best week to approach.

What's Next

The BMW Championship begins Thursday, August 20, at Bellerive Country Club in St. Louis, Missouri. The tournament runs through Sunday, August 23, with the top 30 finishers advancing to the Tour Championship at East Lake in Atlanta (August 27-30).
