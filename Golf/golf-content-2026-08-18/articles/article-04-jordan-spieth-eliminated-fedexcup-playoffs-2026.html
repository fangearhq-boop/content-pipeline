Jordan Spieth's 2026 PGA Tour Season Over: Eliminated From FedExCup Playoffs

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap

Jordan Spieth's 2026 PGA Tour season ended Sunday afternoon in Memphis, two spots short of the BMW Championship field, and the questions it raises will carry into the offseason and beyond. He tied for 19th at the FedEx St. Jude Championship — 4-under, rounds of 65-69-72-70 — and finished 52nd in the FedExCup standings. The top 50 advanced to Bellerive. Spieth was not among them.

How It Happened

Spieth entered the FedEx St. Jude Championship sitting at approximately No. 54 in the FedExCup standings — just outside the bubble. He needed to crack the top 50 after the event. A bogey-free opening 65 moved him briefly inside the cutline, and the hope was real. A player who opens with 65 in a Playoff event has earned the right to believe.

Then came rounds of 69, 72, and 70. The bogeys that had stayed off his scorecard Thursday reappeared in the weekend rounds, and the players he needed to pass held their positions or played past him. When the final scores were posted, Spieth had tied for 19th — a result that was creditable in isolation and insufficient for the task at hand. He finished two spots outside the cutline. The season was over.

The Numbers Tell the Story

The most striking data point from Spieth's 2026 is not the FedExCup finish — it is what preceded it. Spieth did not record a single top-10 finish during the entire 2026 PGA Tour regular season. He qualified for the FedExCup Playoffs nonetheless, making him the only player in the field to have done so without a top-10 result all year. The statistical distinction underscores just how consistent the rest of the qualification field was compared to how Spieth's year played out.

Three major championships decorate his career. A highlight reel that most golfers spend their careers chasing. And a 2026 season that produced no top-10 finishes — not in major weeks, not in elevated events, not in any of the regular-season tournaments where he competed.

A Streak Ends

Among the tangible markers of Spieth's prolonged excellence is a BMW Championship streak that stretched 15 consecutive years. Qualifying for the BMW Championship requires sustaining a level of competitive performance across an entire season — it is not an easy bar, and reaching it 15 straight times represents exactly the kind of sustained excellence that defines elite careers.

That streak is now over. The ending came quietly, on a Sunday in Memphis, without a dramatic last-putt elimination — just a leaderboard that settled into a shape that did not include Spieth's name in the top 50.

What's Next

Spieth has no more events in 2026. The BMW Championship field assembled without him. The Tour Championship at East Lake is a spectator event from his perspective. The question for the offseason is what the 2027 season brings for a three-time major champion who has not been able to find his footing across a full calendar year. Spieth has answered hard questions before — his 2017 U.S. Open drought, his post-2015 drought before re-emerging — and his career suggests he is capable of another answer when he finds it.
