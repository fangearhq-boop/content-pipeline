CPKC Women's Open Preview: Korda vs. Henderson Headlines Canada's Biggest Golf Week

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap

Canada's biggest week in women's golf arrives Wednesday, and the storylines are better than anything a promoter could dream up. The CPKC Women's Open — the 52nd edition of Canada's National Women's Open Championship — opens August 19 at Royal Mayfair Golf Club in Edmonton, Alberta, and doesn't close until August 23. The purse is $2.75 million. The field is 156 of the best women's golfers in the world. The central conflict is World No. 1 Nelly Korda trying to win a title that has eluded her, in a country that is very much hoping she fails.

The Course

Royal Mayfair Golf Club is a Stanley Thompson design that opened in 1922, making it one of Canada's most storied layouts. The par-70 course stretches 6,452 yards and has hosted the CPKC Women's Open multiple times — including the 2013 edition that produced one of women's golf's most extraordinary moments. Thompson's courses are known for their natural routing and dramatic elevation changes, and Royal Mayfair has maintained its reputation as a fair but demanding test across a century of play.

Korda's Case for History

Nelly Korda enters Edmonton on the back of one of the great LPGA seasons in recent memory. Four wins in 2026 — the Hilton Grand Vacations Tournament of Champions, the Chevron Championship, the Riviera May Open, and the U.S. Women's Open — including three major titles. She is the clear World No. 1, and the mathematical gap between her and the rest of the field reflects a player operating well above the competition.

What Korda does not have is a CPKC Women's Open title. That makes Edmonton a different kind of motivation. The world's best player chasing a meaningful gap in her résumé at a championship she has yet to claim is exactly the kind of narrative that drives audiences and competitors alike.

Henderson's Home Defense

Against Korda stands Brooke Henderson, and the setup could not be more compelling. Henderson is the defending CPKC Women's Open champion and a 14-time LPGA Tour winner who has built her career in front of Canadian fans who treat her like a national treasure. She leads a 15-player Canadian contingent — the home side's deepest representation in years — and will play every round to galleries that know exactly who she is and what it would mean for her to win.

The defending-champion-on-home-soil dynamic is one of sport's great storylines. Henderson has done this before, and the weight of it doesn't seem to diminish her game. It may well sharpen it.

Ko and the Third Dimension

Three-time CPKC Women's Open champion Lydia Ko adds a third iconic dimension to the week. Ko's first victory at Royal Mayfair came in 2013, when she was 15 years old and still an amateur — a result so improbable that it remains among the defining moments in women's golf history. She won this championship twice more after that. Ko is as much a part of this course's identity as Stanley Thompson's bunkers.

The Rest of the Field

Beyond the marquee names, the field is loaded with elite players who can win in any week. Hyo Joo Kim, Miyu Yamashita, Ruoning Yin, Minjee Lee, and Hannah Green are all in the field and all capable of contending at the highest level. A $2.75 million purse concentrated among 156 players will generate a legitimate leaderboard through all four rounds.

What's Next
