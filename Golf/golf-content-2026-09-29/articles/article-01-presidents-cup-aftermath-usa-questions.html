USA Won the Presidents Cup. The Analysis Is Brutal.

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | September 29, 2026

The United States won the Presidents Cup 17-13. They trailed by three points entering Sunday's singles matches at Medinah Country Club. They went 9.5 for 12 in the final session and extended their winning streak to 11 consecutive Presidents Cup victories. The trophy went back to America.

The coverage that followed was less celebratory. Golf Digest described it as "the Americans' historic rally that left more uncomfortable questions for everyone involved." Golf Channel asked, before the champagne was dry, whether the Americans will correct their mistakes before the 2027 Ryder Cup at Adare Manor.

Three Days of Being Outplayed

The broad analytical consensus on what happened at Medinah is difficult to argue with. Through the first three days of foursomes, fourballs, and team matches, the International team under captain Geoff Ogilvy consistently outplayed and outclassed the Americans. The U.S. went into the final day trailing on the scoreboard — a position they should not have been in against a team that does not yet have a Ryder Cup equivalent of depth, home crowd, or European venue pressure.

Sunday saved the result but not the narrative. Singles is where the U.S. has historically been strongest — the format favors depth over chemistry, and the American lineup has more depth than anyone. Taking 9.5 of 12 points in singles, while impressive in isolation, was what the schedule and the draw made possible. It does not make the first three days look better in retrospect.

What the Captains Said

U.S. captain Brandt Snedeker kept the focus on the Sunday performance when speaking to the media after the final putt dropped. "I think these guys never lost faith," he said. "Medinah has a history of funny things happening. Sure enough, they went out and played their asses off today. I'm so damn proud of those guys."

That is the statement of a captain who won and knows it. It is also the statement of a captain who knows what the first three days looked like.

Geoff Ogilvy's response was measured but real. "I'm incredibly proud of my team. They came to play. It's a very difficult thing to do to beat the U.S. in singles in the U.S., but we gave it a crack." What Ogilvy's team did was come within a Sunday reversal of winning the Presidents Cup on American soil for the first time in the event's history. They were four points short of doing exactly that.

The Ryder Cup Problem

The Presidents Cup is the warm-up. The Ryder Cup at Adare Manor in Ireland is the main event, and it is 12 months away. Europe will have home galleries, a purpose-built match-play course, a captain in Luke Donald who has run this program for four years, and the memory of nearly beating the Americans in their own backyard.

Jim Furyk, who will captain the U.S. at Adare Manor, has work to do. The questions about team chemistry, player selection format, and preparation that surfaced after this week are the questions that matter before October 2027. Winning the Presidents Cup does not answer them. It may have delayed the moment when they had to be asked.

What's Next

The PGA Tour resumes this week with the Bank of Utah Championship at Black Desert Resort in Ivins, Utah, beginning October 1. The Ryder Cup conversation has already started. It will not stop until Adare Manor.
