LPGA Asian Swing Opens in Shanghai: Thitikul Leads Season as Hwang's LOTTE Win Resets the Race

By Jake Torres | October 8, 2026 | Golf Fanrecap

The LPGA Tour's Asian swing is underway, with the Buick LPGA Shanghai at Sheshan Golf Club in China set for October 15-18 as the stretch run of the 2026 season begins in earnest. Atthaya "Jeeno" Thitikul arrives as the dominant player of the year, leading the season points standings, though the LOTTE Championship last week served notice that the picture is more complicated than the standings alone suggest.

At the LOTTE Championship in Hawaii, 22-year-old South Korean Youmin Hwang won on a sponsor exemption, closing with a birdie on each of the final four holes to finish at 17-under 271. Thitikul, who had led the tournament by three strokes entering Sunday, faded and finished back in the field. It was among the season's most dramatic final-round reversals, and it underscored that Thitikul's season-long dominance has not made every tournament a formality.

Thitikul's Season and What's at Stake

Despite the LOTTE setback, Thitikul remains the player to beat across the remainder of the LPGA calendar. Her consistency throughout 2026 has built a points cushion that gives her flexibility in the Asian swing events, but the stretch from Shanghai through the season finale offers multiple opportunities for challengers to close the gap.

The Buick LPGA Shanghai at Sheshan Golf Club carries a $3.2 million purse, making it one of the richer events in the Asian swing sequence. Sheshan is a demanding, tree-lined course where positioning off the tee is critical and approach play determines outcomes. International fields in Asian swing events are typically strong — the host nation's players compete with particular motivation, and the leaderboards often reflect a global range of contenders.

Hwang and Zibilski: Two First-Time Wins

Youmin Hwang's LOTTE win was one of two significant first victories in the recent stretch of LPGA events. Reagan Zibilski won the Epson Tour Championship with a final-round 62, earning her LPGA Tour card for 2027. The Epson Tour serves as the LPGA's developmental circuit, and a card earned through the Epson Tour Championship represents the culmination of a full season's work at a lower level of professional golf.

Zibilski's 62 in the final round at the Epson Tour Championship was the kind of closing number that announces a player's readiness for the next level. She arrives on the LPGA Tour in 2027 with momentum and with a week that will be difficult to forget.

Schedule and What's Next

The LPGA also released its 2027 schedule this week, with the LOTTE Championship relocating to Kapalua Resort in Hawaii beginning next year. The current LOTTE venue's final edition in its present form added a layer of significance to Hwang's win — she closed out an era at the course with one of the most dramatic finishes the tournament has seen.

After Shanghai, the Asian swing continues before the season closes at the CME Group Tour Championship in Naples, Florida on November 22. The season finale carries the tour's largest purse and typically determines the final season points title. Thitikul will enter Naples as the favorite if she performs in the Asian swing as expected — but Hwang's LOTTE win is a reminder that form can shift quickly, and that the final stretch of the LPGA calendar rarely resolves cleanly.
