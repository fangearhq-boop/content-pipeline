Smotherman Wins Bank of Utah in His 100th PGA Tour Start — A Number That Tells the Story

By Jake Torres | October 8, 2026 | Golf Fanrecap

Austin Smotherman shot 26-under par at Black Desert Golf Course in Ivins, Utah, and won the Bank of Utah Championship. It was his first PGA Tour victory. It was also his 100th career start on tour.

The number 100 carries a specific weight in professional golf that does not translate easily to other sports. Most players who turn professional and pursue PGA Tour membership never reach 100 starts. The card system, the Monday qualifiers, the conditional status, the gradations of priority — the tour is designed to continuously filter out everyone except those who keep producing results. To reach 100 starts requires years of consistency, competitive nerve, and the ability to survive in a field of the best players in the world without ever winning.

Smotherman defeated Doug Ghim by two strokes, closing at 26-under after responding each time a challenger closed the gap. He earned $1,080,000 and 500 FedExCup points — a meaningful haul in the FedExCup Fall series, which determines tour card security and positioning for the following season.

The Course and the Conditions

Black Desert Golf Course in Ivins, Utah, sits in red rock country in the southwestern corner of the state. The course opened to acclaim and has been a popular stop on the FedExCup Fall calendar since its addition. The scoring conditions during the Bank of Utah Championship allowed for deep scoring, with Smotherman's 26-under representing a winning total that reflects both the course's playability and the field's quality.

Ghim, the runner-up at 24-under, pushed Smotherman throughout the final rounds. The two-stroke margin was comfortable by the end, but it was not always comfortable to achieve. Smotherman's ability to hold his nerve — to produce results under pressure on a course where low scores were available to anyone in the field — was the distinguishing quality of his week.

What the Win Means

The 500 FedExCup points Smotherman earned are significant in the context of the fall schedule. The FedExCup Fall events serve as a bridge season, providing players on the bubble opportunities to secure their playing privileges and improve their priority ranking for the following year. A first win also comes with a two-year tour exemption, meaning Smotherman does not need to worry about his card for the foreseeable future.

Beyond the logistics, there is something worth acknowledging about what 100 starts represents. Most people who play competitive golf never stand on a PGA Tour tee box at all. Most who do stand there get far fewer than 100 chances. Smotherman used each of those 100 opportunities to stay competitive enough to be in contention when the right week arrived.

The right week arrived at Black Desert. He did not waste it.

What's Next

The PGA Tour's FedExCup Fall continues with the Baycurrent Classic in Yokohama, Japan (October 8-11), which is currently underway. After Japan, the Butterfield Bermuda Championship at Port Royal Golf Course (October 22-25) provides the next opportunity for FedExCup points accumulation. Smotherman arrives at these events with a win on his record, renewed status, and momentum that tends to follow a player's first career victory.
