### Long-Form Post 💰 The mystery investor behind LIV Golf 2.0 has a name: BC Partners. Bloomberg and the Financial Times identified the British private equity firm this week as the lead investor that LIV Golf CEO Scott O'Neil confirmed last month. The deal has not been officially confirmed by LIV or BC Partners, but the reporting from two major financial outlets is hard to dismiss. Here is what we know about the new structure: **The format.** LIV 2.0 will consist of 10 events — 5 in the United States and 5 international. The US events will be positioned in the weeks before major championships. The international events will be branded as "Team Majors." **The ownership shift.** Players will become majority equity holders of the new league. This is the structural detail that makes LIV 2.0 fundamentally different from the original model — rather than collecting appearance fees, top players would own a stake in what they're competing in. **The timeline.** The transaction is expected to finalize in September 2026. The new league launches in 2027. Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund — which has invested an estimated $5 billion into LIV since 2022 — exits entirely by the end of 2026. Golf's off-course landscape has shifted dramatically since LIV launched four years ago. Whether BC Partners can make the business work where PIF couldn't sustain it is the central question heading into 2027. 💬 Does LIV Golf 2.0 have a real future under private equity ownership — or is this still a long shot? ---
