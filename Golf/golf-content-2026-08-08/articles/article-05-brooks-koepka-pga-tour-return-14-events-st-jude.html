Brooks Koepka Is 14 Events Into His PGA Tour Return. His First Playoff Is Next Week.

By Jake Torres | August 8, 2026

"The weight has been lifted off my shoulders."

That was Brooks Koepka's description of his return to the PGA Tour — and the relief in those words is worth sitting with. This is a five-time major champion. Two-time US Open winner. Two-time PGA Championship winner. One of the most decorated golfers of his generation. And coming back to the Tour he left for LIV Golf felt, by his own account, like putting down something heavy he had been carrying.

Fourteen events into his return, with the FedExCup Playoffs beginning next week, the question is what that return actually amounts to on the scorecard.

What He Gave Up to Come Back

Koepka's return via the PGA Tour's Returning Member Program came with documented financial consequences. He accepted them all.

LIV Golf's new 2.0 structure will make top players majority equity holders of the league — an ownership stake with estimated earning potential of $50-85 million over five years. Koepka forfeited his claim to that equity by leaving.

He is also ineligible for the PGA Tour's $100 million FedExCup bonus program in 2026 — a financial incentive structure available to players who maintained their Tour status and didn't depart for LIV.

Additionally, as part of the terms of his return, Koepka made a $5 million charitable donation.

The combined financial cost — in potential equity, bonus ineligibility, and the charitable requirement — is substantial by any measure. He accepted it to be here.

The Return in Context

Koepka rejoined the PGA Tour in January 2026, making his return debut at the Farmers Insurance Open at Torrey Pines. He arrived under the Returning Member Program, which provides a structured pathway back for players who left for LIV Golf and chose to return.

The program requires a minimum of 15 co-sponsored events in the return season. Koepka is 14 events in. The FedEx St. Jude Championship next week — his committed playoff event — will be his 15th. He will have met the minimum requirement at the first playoff event of his Tour return.

That context gives the St. Jude an added dimension beyond the competitive one. It is both a milestone in his comeback and his introduction to the PGA Tour postseason after two years away.

The Larger Significance

Koepka's return is widely considered the most significant individual LIV defection of 2026. His decision to come back — and the financial sacrifice that accompanied it — draws a line in the sand about what the PGA Tour represents to players who value it above the alternative economics LIV offered.

Whether or not Koepka's game has returned to its major-winning peak, his presence in the FedExCup Playoffs is a statement. The players who stayed on Tour, ground through the season, and earned their playoff spots will see a former LIV player standing alongside them at TPC Southwind. The sport's internal dynamics are still sorting themselves out.

What's Next

Brooks Koepka will compete in the FedEx St. Jude Championship beginning August 13-16 at TPC Southwind in Memphis — his first FedExCup Playoffs event since returning to the PGA Tour. The $20 million event is the opening round of a three-event postseason. For Koepka, it is also the completion of his minimum 15-event Returning Member requirement, and the beginning of whatever this next chapter of his career becomes.
