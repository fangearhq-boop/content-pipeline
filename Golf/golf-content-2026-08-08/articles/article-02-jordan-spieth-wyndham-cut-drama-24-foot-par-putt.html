Jordan Spieth Made the Wyndham Cut the Only Way He Knows How — Barely, Dramatically, Unforgettably

By Jake Torres | August 8, 2026

Jordan Spieth hit his opening tee shot out of bounds in Round 1 at the Wyndham Championship. He finished the day at -2, shooting 68. The drama, it turned out, was just getting started.

On the final hole of Round 2 Friday at Sedgefield Country Club, Spieth needed par to make the cut. He stood over a 24-foot putt. He drained it. He is in for the weekend at -3, right on the cut number, and his season continues.

This is, at this point, simply how Jordan Spieth operates.

The Context That Makes It Matter

Spieth entered this week at No. 50 in the FedExCup standings — one of the most precarious positions in professional golf at this time of year. The FedExCup Playoffs accept the top 70 players from the season-long points list. At No. 50, he is inside the line, but barely. A missed cut here, combined with strong showings from players below him, could cost him his place in the postseason.

He has not won on the PGA Tour since April 2022. That drought spans more than four years, multiple near-misses, and a missed cut at the 2026 Open Championship last month. For a player of Spieth's profile — three major championships, former world No. 1, one of the most recognizable names in the game — the extended absence from the winner's circle is the dominant subplot of his career right now.

A missed cut at the Wyndham would have been the latest chapter in that story. A 24-foot par save prevented it.

The OB Tee Shot That Started It

Spieth's opening tee shot in Round 1 — which sailed out of bounds — set a tone of maximum difficulty for the week. There are few worse ways to start a tournament, particularly for a player whose standing requires a strong performance. That he recovered to shoot 68 in Round 1 was a credit to his short game and mental resilience. That he then needed a 24-footer to survive Round 2 was, for Spieth watchers, completely on brand.

No player in professional golf more consistently manufactures theatrical moments out of situations that should be straightforward. That quality has produced some of the greatest drama in major championship history. It also produces weeks like this one — where the cut line becomes an edge Spieth is always, somehow, standing right on.

The Weekend Ahead

Spieth is at -3, which puts him well off the lead held by Beau Hossler at -12. A charge up the leaderboard this weekend would require low scoring over two rounds — realistic at Sedgefield, where birdie opportunities exist throughout the course, but demanding nonetheless.

The FedExCup implications are straightforward. A strong finish — top 10, ideally a win — would build his points cushion heading into the postseason. A quiet weekend at -3 still gets him to the FedEx St. Jude Championship, but without the security that comes from accumulating points in the final regular-season event.

Spieth has won before in situations where his back was against the wall. He has also had weekends where the magic doesn't fully materialize. The question heading into Saturday and Sunday is which version shows up.

What's Next

Rounds 3 and 4 of the Wyndham Championship are scheduled for Saturday and Sunday at Sedgefield Country Club in Greensboro, North Carolina. The FedExCup regular season concludes with this event. The FedEx St. Jude Championship — the first playoff event — begins August 13 at TPC Southwind in Memphis. For Spieth, a strong weekend here could be the momentum shift his season has been waiting for.
