BC Partners Named as LIV Golf's Mystery Investor. Here Is What the New Structure Looks Like.

By Marcus Cole | August 8, 2026

The identity of LIV Golf's mystery lead investor is no longer a mystery. Bloomberg and the Financial Times reported this week that BC Partners — a British private equity firm — is the investor that LIV Golf CEO Scott O'Neil confirmed last month had received board approval for a deal to restructure and relaunch the league.

Neither LIV Golf nor BC Partners has officially confirmed the identification. But reporting from two of the world's largest financial news organizations is not easily dismissed, and it puts a name on an investment that had circulated as speculation for weeks.

The LIV 2.0 Structure

The deal isn't just a financial transaction. It represents a fundamental restructuring of what LIV Golf is as a business and a competition.

The new format will consist of 10 events: 5 in the United States and 5 internationally. The American events are being positioned in the weeks immediately before major championships — a strategic placement designed to maximize attention during periods when golf interest peaks globally. The international events will operate under the brand "Team Majors."

Player ownership is the most structurally significant change. Under LIV 2.0, players will become majority equity holders of the league — a departure from the original model where top players collected guaranteed appearance fees from Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund. If the league succeeds commercially, the players who own it benefit directly. If it struggles, they share in that too.

The Saudi Exit

The Public Investment Fund of Saudi Arabia has funded LIV Golf since its launch in 2022, with an estimated $5 billion invested over four years. That era ends with this transaction. PIF is exiting all funding by the close of 2026 — a withdrawal that leaves the new structure entirely dependent on BC Partners' capital and whatever revenue the league generates on its own.

That transition carries genuine uncertainty. PIF's backing provided essentially unlimited financial runway, regardless of whether LIV Golf was commercially sustainable. Private equity operates differently. BC Partners will expect a return on its investment. Player equity stakes create alignment, but they also create pressure to build something that actually makes money.

The Timeline

The transaction is expected to finalize in September 2026. The new LIV 2.0 league is scheduled to launch in 2027. Between now and then, the mechanics of the ownership structure, the specific event schedule, and the relationship with existing PGA Tour and DP World Tour contracts all need resolution.

Golf's professional landscape has been unsettled since LIV's arrival four years ago. The framework agreements between LIV, the PGA Tour, and the DP World Tour have produced negotiation without resolution. What LIV 2.0 means for the broader reconciliation process remains an open question — one that won't fully answer itself until the new league is operating.

What's Next

The LIV Golf 2.0 transaction is expected to close in September 2026, with the new league launching in 2027. Official confirmation from LIV Golf and BC Partners is still pending. On the playing side, LIV players who returned to the PGA Tour — including Brooks Koepka, who joined via the Returning Member Program — will compete in the FedExCup Playoffs beginning August 13 with the FedEx St. Jude Championship in Memphis.
