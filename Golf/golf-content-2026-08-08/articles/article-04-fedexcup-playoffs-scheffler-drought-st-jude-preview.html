Scottie Scheffler Leads the FedExCup and Hasn't Won Since January. The Playoffs Start in Five Days.

By Ryan Calloway | August 8, 2026

Five days from now, the FedExCup Playoffs begin with the FedEx St. Jude Championship at TPC Southwind in Memphis. Scottie Scheffler will tee it up as the FedExCup points leader. He has not won a golf tournament since the American Express in January.

"I've been really close to winning some tournaments," Scheffler said recently, "and that can be frustrating."

That quote — measured, honest, slightly resigned — captures the defining tension of Scheffler's 2026 season. He is the best player in the world by nearly any statistical measure. He has five runner-up finishes since his last win. He is heading into the postseason where, by design, the stakes are highest.

The Playoff Structure

The 2026 FedExCup Playoffs will be contested across three events:

FedEx St. Jude Championship — August 13-16 at TPC Southwind in Memphis. The top 70 players in FedExCup standings compete. The purse is $20 million, with $3.6 million going to the winner. Defending champion Justin Rose will be in the field. Rory McIlroy headlines the player list alongside Scheffler.

BMW Championship — August 20-23 at Bellerive Country Club in St. Louis. The field narrows to the top 50 in playoff standings after the St. Jude.

Tour Championship — August 27-30 at East Lake Golf Club in Atlanta. The top 30 qualify. The FedExCup prize is $25 million, with a staggered points structure that rewards the full season rather than just the final event.

Scheffler's Points Cushion

Scheffler enters the playoffs at 4,123 FedExCup points — a commanding lead that reflects a season full of elite golf even without a victory since January. Runner-up finishes still accumulate meaningful points, and Scheffler has been consistent enough that his cushion heading into the postseason is real.

That cushion matters structurally. The FedExCup format isn't purely a postseason event — it incorporates the full season's results. Scheffler's lead means he doesn't need to win the St. Jude to remain in the driver's seat. But winning it would end the drought, and ending the drought is its own motivation separate from the standings.

The Drought as a Story

Seven months without a win is unusual for a player of Scheffler's caliber. Five runner-up finishes in that span is evidence that the golf hasn't collapsed — he is consistently putting himself in position. The wins simply aren't arriving.

That creates a narrative the postseason will either resolve or amplify. If Scheffler wins at St. Jude, the drought becomes a footnote. If he contends and doesn't win again, the conversation becomes impossible to avoid heading into the Tour Championship.

Rory McIlroy will be the name most often mentioned alongside Scheffler at St. Jude. McIlroy has his own reasons to want to perform in the postseason. The combination of the two on the same leaderboard — and their respective trajectories — is the storyline that will define the first week of the FedExCup.

What's Next

The FedEx St. Jude Championship begins August 13 at TPC Southwind in Memphis. The Wyndham Championship — the final regular-season event — concludes this weekend, after which the final FedExCup standings are set and the playoff field of 70 is confirmed. Scheffler's place at the top of those standings is secure. What he does with the postseason is the question the next three weeks will answer.
