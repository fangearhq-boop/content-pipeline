Scheffler's Fifth Year at No. 1 — How He Became the Tour Championship's Permanent Favorite

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | August 26, 2026

For the fifth consecutive year, Scottie Scheffler walks into the Tour Championship as the No. 1 FedExCup seed. That sentence has become so familiar it risks losing its impact — which would be a mistake, because what Scheffler has done across five straight years at the top of professional golf's season-long competition is genuinely unprecedented.

Before the tournament begins Thursday, Scheffler has already cashed a $23 million bonus — the reward for leading the FedExCup standings all season under the points structure's tiered bonus system. Add the $10 million winner's share available at East Lake this week, and a Tour Championship victory would give him a season of earnings that few athletes in any sport could match. But Scheffler is not here for the money, and anyone who has watched him play knows that immediately. He is here because he wants the FedExCup again.

What He's Done in 2026

The résumé speaks simply. Scheffler won the FedEx St. Jude Championship in Memphis by eight strokes — the largest winning margin in the tournament's 68-year history at TPC Southwind — to claim his 21st career PGA Tour title. He won The American Express earlier in the season. He has maintained the No. 1 ranking in the Official World Golf Ranking. And he has done all of this in a 2026 season that featured depth, competition, and genuine challenges from Clark, McIlroy, and others.

A win at East Lake this week would give Scheffler the FedExCup for the fourth time. He won in 2024. The gap between win No. 3 (McIlroy in 2022) and Scheffler's next victory — if it comes Sunday — would be three years of Scheffler reaching this stage and competing at its highest level. No one has four. He could be the first.

What's Next

Scheffler tees off Thursday alongside Matt Fitzpatrick at 2:00 PM ET in the marquee first-round pairing. He is the betting favorite, the No. 1 seed, and the player the entire field knows they must beat. Four rounds of golf on a demanding course in Atlanta will determine whether the most dominant player of his era adds one more chapter to a legacy already being written in record books. He's five years in. He's not done yet.
