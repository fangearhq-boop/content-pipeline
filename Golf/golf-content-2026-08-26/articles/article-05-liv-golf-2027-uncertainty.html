LIV Golf 2026 Is Over — and 2027 Isn't Guaranteed

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | August 26, 2026

The 2026 LIV Golf season ended last week in Indianapolis, and the final scoreboard was dominated by a familiar name. Jon Rahm, despite posting 8-over in the final event to finish tied for 41st, accumulated enough season-long points to claim his third consecutive individual LIV title. He won in Hong Kong and Mexico City. The title came despite a week he'd rather forget — a dynamic Rahm himself acknowledged with characteristic candor: "I'll try to enjoy the title once I get over how bad I played this week."

Michael La Sasso won the Indianapolis finale outright at 18-under, one shot ahead of Rahm. Sergio Garcia and Bryson DeChambeau tied for third. Legion XIII successfully defended their team title, finishing at 44-under — nine shots clear of the HyFlyers. With La Sasso's victory, the 2026 LIV season closed with its full slate of events completed.

The Question Nobody Wants to Answer

The more consequential news out of Indianapolis was not who won. It was what LIV CEO Greg Norman said — or didn't say — when asked about the 2027 season. Norman declined to guarantee that the 2027 LIV Golf season would happen. That is not a reassuring response from the leader of a league whose players restructured their careers around its existence.

Former European Ryder Cup player Ian Poulter had already warned publicly that LIV may be running out of time to secure its financial future beyond this year. The league's funding model, which relies heavily on Saudi Public Investment Fund backing and has not yet produced a television deal in major markets comparable to the PGA Tour's broadcast agreements, has been a subject of persistent speculation about long-term viability.

For the 54 players who compete in LIV Golf — players who gave up Ryder Cup eligibility, PGA Tour membership, and in some cases significant public goodwill in exchange for guaranteed money and the LIV format — an uncertain 2027 creates a difficult planning horizon. Some may seek to negotiate PGA Tour reinstatement. Others may wait and see. But Norman's non-answer is the most alarming signal the league has sent since its inception.

What's Next

The path forward for LIV Golf runs through the same negotiating table where it has been sitting for months: a potential framework with the PGA Tour that has been discussed but not finalized. Without a clear financial commitment and broadcast deal, 2027 remains genuinely uncertain. Players, agents, and sponsors will be watching for official announcements in the coming weeks. The off-season between 2026 and 2027 may be the most consequential period in the league's short history.
