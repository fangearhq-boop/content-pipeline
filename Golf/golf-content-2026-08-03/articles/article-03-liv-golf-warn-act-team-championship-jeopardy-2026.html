LIV Golf Files WARN Act Notices as $400M Funding Gap Puts Team Championship in Jeopardy

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | August 3, 2026

LIV Golf filed WARN Act notices in both the United States and the United Kingdom this month. The WARN Act — the Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act — requires employers to give at least 60 days' advance notice before conducting mass layoffs or plant closings. Filing the notice is a legal signal. It does not mean layoffs are imminent. It means the organization has determined that it must prepare for that possibility.

LIV Golf's spokesperson stated that there are "no changes to LIV Golf's current workforce, operations, or schedule at this time." The filings say something different in the language organizations use when facing financial pressure that has reached a critical threshold.

The $400 Million Gap

Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund committed approximately $600 million to fund the 2026 LIV Golf season. Of that commitment, approximately $200 million has been delivered. The $400 million gap is real and has not been closed.

PIF announced earlier this year that it will not fund LIV Golf beyond the 2026 season, ending a financial relationship that has seen Saudi Arabia invest more than $5 billion into the league since 2022. LIV is operating on loans — PIF is lending, not investing in capital — as the organization seeks $250 to $350 million in outside investment to survive past this season.

That search has not publicly produced a result.

The Team Championship in Jeopardy

LIV Golf's Team Championship — the marquee season-ending event scheduled for late August — is described by people familiar with the situation as "in serious jeopardy of not being played." The league has already cancelled two 2026 events: New Orleans and Michigan. The Team Championship would be a third cancellation, eliminating the event that was designed to be the climax of LIV's competitive calendar.

Three events remain on the 2026 LIV schedule: LIV Golf New York at Trump National in Bedminster (August 6-9), LIV Golf Indianapolis (August 20-23), and the disputed Team Championship. The Bedminster event begins this Thursday. Indianapolis follows. The Team Championship's status is uncertain.

The Proposed Path Forward

LIV has outlined a business plan that includes a reduced annual schedule of 10 events, which the organization believes could reach profitability within three years under a new financial structure. The plan depends on securing outside investment or a new primary backer to replace PIF — neither of which has been announced.

The PGA Tour, DP World Tour, and Asian Tour formed an alliance in July at Royal Birkdale that runs through 2029. That alignment, and the Asian Tour's departure from LIV's financial ecosystem, further narrows the structural options available to LIV if it survives past 2026.

What This Week Represents

LIV Golf New York begins Thursday. Jon Rahm is on the verge of clinching the 2026 individual championship. The players who have built their professional lives around LIV's promises are competing for real prize money in real events that are still happening. The competitive product has not collapsed.

But the WARN Act notices are a formal, legal acknowledgment that the organization behind that product is facing a crisis it may not be able to resolve. A league that files WARN Act notices in two countries and simultaneously has its marquee event described as "in serious jeopardy" is a league in genuine peril. The players competing this week at Bedminster know it. The question is what comes next — and when.
