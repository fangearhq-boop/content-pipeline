Shiho Kuwaki Wins the 2026 AIG Women's Open in a Playoff at Royal Lytham

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | August 3, 2026

Shiho Kuwaki won the 2026 AIG Women's Open at Royal Lytham & St Annes on August 2, claiming her first LPGA major title in just her seventh major start. The victory came in one of the most dramatic finishes in recent women's major history — a playoff that turned on a 45-foot birdie putt, a tee shot that struck a marshal, and a two-putt par that meant everything.

Kuwaki and Germany's Esther Henseleit finished the 72nd hole tied at -5 (279). They did so because Henseleit had just holed a 45-foot birdie on the 18th to draw level, forcing extra holes in a women's major for the first time in recent memory.

What Happened in the Playoff

The two players returned to the 18th for the first playoff hole, and then a second. On that second extra hole, Henseleit's tee shot struck a marshal's ankle and finished in a bush. She made bogey. Kuwaki, unaffected by the chaos around her, two-putted for par to claim the title.

It was not the ending anyone had scripted. Henseleit had done something extraordinary — she had brought herself to the brink of a major championship with one of the most pressure-packed putts imaginable, then watched it slip away through misfortune on the next tee box.

Kuwaki claimed $1.5 million from the $10 million record purse, the largest in women's major championship history. She becomes Japan's second consecutive AIG Women's Open champion.

Noh's Final-Round Collapse

The day had begun with Yealimi Noh in command. She entered the final round at -7 — three shots clear of the field — having made three consecutive birdies from the par-3 9th in the third round. A lead of that size entering Sunday at a major is a position of genuine strength.

She could not hold it. The details of precisely how Noh's round unfolded — where the shots were dropped, at which holes the lead evaporated — are still being processed by a leaderboard that shifted dramatically over the course of Sunday. What the final result makes clear is that the -7 advantage entering the day did not translate into a championship. The tournament moved on without her.

Ryu's Grand Slam Bid Ends

Haeran Ryu had been the other major story of the week. She entered Royal Lytham having won the KPMG Women's PGA Championship and the Evian Championship earlier in 2026 — two majors, six weeks apart. A third consecutive major would have matched Inbee Park's 2013 record of three women's majors in one calendar year, one of the most coveted marks in the sport.

Ryu had led at the halfway mark at Royal Lytham, keeping the pursuit alive. She finished T6 at -1. The Grand Slam bid is over.

Two majors in a single season is still a remarkable achievement — a career-defining body of work by any measure. That Ryu came within striking distance of a third makes the near-miss significant without diminishing what she has already accomplished in 2026.

What the Win Means for Kuwaki

Kuwaki becomes Japan's second consecutive AIG Women's Open champion. The manner of the victory — navigating a playoff in which the decisive moment was not of her making but required absolute composure on the final green — speaks to a player who did not flinch when the tournament arrived at its most chaotic point.

Seven major starts. One major title. A two-putt par that could not have come under more pressure. Royal Lytham, August 2, 2026 will be the date she defines her career around for the rest of it.
