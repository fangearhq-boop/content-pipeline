Wyndham Championship 2026 Preview: The Last Regular-Season Chance for FedExCup Bubble Players

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | August 3, 2026

The PGA Tour's 2026 regular season ends this week. The Wyndham Championship runs August 6-9 at Sedgefield Country Club in Greensboro, North Carolina — the 34th and final event before the FedExCup Playoffs. The top 70 players in the FedExCup standings after Wyndham advance to the FedEx St. Jude Championship in Memphis the following week. For everyone else, the season is over in Greensboro.

The purse is $8.5 million. The winner collects 500 FedExCup points. For the players in the playoff picture, both numbers matter enormously. For the players near the bubble, they are the only numbers that matter at all.

Cameron Young Defends

Cameron Young returns to Sedgefield as the defending champion. He won the 2025 Wyndham Championship for his first PGA Tour title — a victory that announced him as a player capable of converting his considerable talent into a trophy. His return this week carries a different pressure: not the burden of a first win, but the expectation that comes with defending a title at a course that already holds a significant place in his career.

The top players in the FedExCup standings — Scottie Scheffler, Matt Fitzpatrick, Wyndham Clark, and Chris Gotterup — are not playing Wyndham. They are already comfortably inside the top 70 and resting before the postseason. The field belongs to the players who need to be in Greensboro, and Cameron Young is the name at the top of it.

Davis Riley's Rocket Classic Rise

One week ago, Davis Riley entered the Rocket Classic as the 146th player in the FedExCup standings, with 139 points and no realistic path to the playoffs. He closed in 63, birdied the 54th hole from 54 feet and the final hole from 10 feet, and won the tournament at -15. The 500 FedExCup points that came with the Rocket Classic title vaulted him from 146th to approximately 50-60th in the standings — from outside looking in to safely inside the top 70.

Riley's win at Detroit is the most direct illustration of what is at stake in Greensboro. One good week changes everything in the FedExCup regular season's final event.

The Bubble Players

Brooks Koepka withdrew from the Rocket Classic with an ulnar nerve injury affecting his left hand grip. He was 84th in FedExCup standings before that withdrawal. He is confirmed in the Wyndham field — playing through the injury in what may be his last chance to extend his 2026 season. Tony Finau and Keegan Bradley are also on the outside of the top 70 and confirmed at Sedgefield.

Jordan Spieth, Justin Thomas, and Hideki Matsuyama are also in the field — players with larger windows above the bubble but with enough at stake in the FedExCup standings that Wyndham represents meaningful points.

The tension of Greensboro lies in the compression of these different situations. Some players need one good round. Some need four. Some need to know that everyone above them on the leaderboard fails while they succeed. All of it plays out simultaneously over four days at Sedgefield.

What Comes After

The FedExCup Playoffs structure after Wyndham: the FedEx St. Jude Championship runs August 13-16 at TPC Southwind in Memphis (top 70); the BMW Championship follows at Bellerive in St. Louis (top 50); the TOUR Championship closes at East Lake in Atlanta (top 30). Each event cuts the field further. The regular season's final standings define which of those stages each player enters — and whether they enter any of them at all.

The Wyndham Championship has served as the regular season's bookend since 2008. Sedgefield Country Club is a course that rewards precision over power, a Donald Ross design that has produced dramatic finishes in the final regular-season week for nearly two decades. This week, it will do so again.
