Haeran Ryu's Grand Slam Bid Is Over. Her 2026 Season Is Still Historic.

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | August 3, 2026

The record Haeran Ryu was chasing belongs to Inbee Park. In 2013, Park won three women's major championships in a single calendar year — the ANA Inspiration, the KPMG Women's PGA, and the U.S. Women's Open. Three majors in one year remains one of the most rarified achievements in the sport. Nobody has matched it since.

Ryu came as close as anyone has in more than a decade. She won the KPMG Women's PGA Championship in June. She won the Evian Championship six weeks later. She arrived at Royal Lytham & St Annes for the AIG Women's Open with two consecutive major titles and a legitimate chance to make history.

She finished T6 at -1. Shiho Kuwaki won the title in a playoff. The pursuit is over.

What Ryu Had Already Done

Before the conversation turns to what Ryu did not accomplish, it is worth being specific about what she did.

The KPMG Women's PGA Championship in June was a major title on one of the LPGA's most demanding stages. The Evian Championship, won just six weeks later, is a major with international significance and a field that routinely includes the best players in women's golf. Ryu won both. She did it in less than two months. The KPMG and the Evian are not consolation prizes — they are two of the five women's major championships, and Ryu has two of them in 2026.

The question of how many players have won two women's majors in a single calendar year answers itself quickly: not many. The players who have done so are in a specific company. Ryu is in that company regardless of what happened at Royal Lytham.

Royal Lytham and the Near-Miss

Ryu led at the halfway point of the AIG Women's Open. The pursuit of a third consecutive major was alive through 36 holes at Royal Lytham. She could not sustain the performance through the weekend. She finished T6 at -1, eight shots behind Kuwaki's winning score of -5.

The gap between what Ryu accomplished and what she was reaching for is not a small one. Three consecutive majors would have been a historic first in the modern era. Two majors in a calendar year, which she has already achieved, is something different — remarkable, but not a first.

What this final result does is clarify what kind of year 2026 is for Ryu: extraordinary, not unprecedented. There is still a meaningful difference.

The Season That Remains

The LPGA calendar still has events remaining in 2026. Ryu has already secured a season that would define most careers. The question for the second half of her year is whether she can add to a total that already includes two major titles, or whether the rest of 2026 becomes a process of absorbing the Royal Lytham near-miss and returning to compete at the same level.

Players who reach for records and fall short often do so because they were good enough to be in the conversation in the first place. Ryu was good enough. She won two majors to prove it. The third one, on a different weekend, might still come.
