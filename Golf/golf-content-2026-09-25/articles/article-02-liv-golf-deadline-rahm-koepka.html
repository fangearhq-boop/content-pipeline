LIV Golf's 18-Day Countdown: Rahm Committed, Koepka Gone, and the Math That Matters

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | September 25, 2026

Eighteen days remain before October 13, the deadline for LIV Golf players to commit to the BC Partners restructuring deal. The picture is sharpening: the league's biggest names have chosen their paths, the math is coming into focus, and the question is no longer if the deal closes — it's whether the headcount threshold gets there.

Who's In, Who's Out

Jon Rahm — holder of LIV's largest-ever contract, reportedly around $150 million per bankruptcy filings — has committed to the restructured league. Bryson DeChambeau and Cameron Smith have also confirmed they will participate in the new structure. Dustin Johnson signed a new multi-year extension and continues as captain of 4Aces GC.

Brooks Koepka went the other direction. After reaching an "amicable and mutually agreed" early release, Koepka returned to the PGA Tour — paying $5 million and forfeiting equity to do so. That price tag, widely reported since September 24 filings, became the story: what does it say about a league when a player considers it worth $5 million to leave?

The Thresholds

Under the BC Partners deal, two conditions must be met for the $300 million rescue financing to close. First, at least two-thirds of eligible player claims by dollar value must agree to participate. With Rahm's $150 million contract anchoring the commitment side, that threshold is likely covered. Second, at least half of all eligible players by headcount must commit — and that's where uncertainty remains.

Mid-tier players who signed shorter deals or lack major performance clauses have more flexibility to wait. Some may prefer to hold out until the league's 2027 relaunch takes clearer shape before formally committing.

What's Next

October 13 is the date of truth. If both thresholds are met, LIV Golf's restructuring closes and the league begins building toward its promised 2027 restart under the new ownership structure. If the deal falls short — particularly on headcount — the financing could collapse, leaving LIV's future in genuine uncertainty. With 18 days remaining, the recruitment pressure now falls on mid-tier players who haven't yet declared.
