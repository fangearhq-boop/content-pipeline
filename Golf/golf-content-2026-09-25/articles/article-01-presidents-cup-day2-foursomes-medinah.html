Presidents Cup Day 2: USA Holds 3-2 Lead as Foursomes Begin at Medinah

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | September 25, 2026

The Presidents Cup moves into foursomes territory Friday at Medinah Country Club, with the United States carrying a 3-2 advantage earned during Thursday's opening four-ball session. Five matches teed off beginning at 2:05 PM ET, and the format shift fundamentally changes what each team needs to execute.

In four-ball, players compete with individual balls and post the best score per hole. In foursomes — alternate shot — teammates share a single ball, alternating swings from tee to hole. There is no safety net. Every other shot belongs to your partner, and course management becomes a shared negotiation rather than a personal calculation.

The Matchups

The USA sent out five pairs: Scottie Scheffler and Sam Burns, Russell Henley and Jacob Bridgeman, Wyndham Clark and Collin Morikawa, Justin Thomas and Cameron Young, and Xander Schauffele and Patrick Cantlay. Scheffler and Burns broke through in four-ball Thursday after going 0-4 together in their previous Presidents Cup appearances. Morikawa and Clark also won their Thursday pairing.

The International side counters with Hideki Matsuyama alongside Sungjae Im, Si Woo Kim paired with Tom Kim — the duo that handed the USA one of Thursday's two losses — and Adam Scott alongside Christiaan Bezuidenhout.

The Format Shift and What It Means

Foursomes historically favors teams with chemistry and complementary ball-striking tendencies. A long hitter paired with a precise iron player can be devastating — one sets up the hole, the other finishes it. Conversely, two aggressive players can compound each other's mistakes on a punishing layout like Medinah's No. 3 course.

The Internationals are hoping the format suits their pairings better than four-ball did. Their 2-3 deficit after Day 1 is not dire, but a pattern of losses in this format over 12 consecutive Presidents Cups has shown the USA to be more comfortable in the pressure environments these matches create.

What's Next

Golf Channel carries coverage from 2–7 PM ET. Day 2 results will determine which team enters Saturday's four-ball session with the momentum. The USA needs only to maintain its lead heading into the weekend; the Internationals need a session win to stay in contention for the first time since 1998.
