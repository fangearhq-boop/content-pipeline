The International Team's Medinah Math: Can History Repeat at the Course That Birthed a Miracle?

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | September 25, 2026

The International team trails 3-2 after Day 1 of the Presidents Cup, and the road ahead is steep. Not insurmountable — but steep in the specific way that 12 consecutive losses make any deficit feel steeper.

And yet. They are playing at Medinah Country Club. And this course knows what comebacks look like.

The 2012 Parallel

The most famous comeback in team golf history happened at this venue. Europe arrived at Sunday singles in the 2012 Ryder Cup trailing 10-6, needing 8 of 12 points just to tie. They won 8.5-3.5. Martin Kaymer held the decisive putt on the final hole. Rory McIlroy, Justin Rose, and Ian Poulter each contributed crucial points in a run that became known as the Miracle at Medinah.

The International team's current 3-2 deficit is far less severe than Europe's 10-6 hole. With 28 points still to be contested across three remaining sessions, the math is genuinely viable. The path requires winning Day 2 foursomes, taking Day 3 four-ball, and entering Sunday singles close enough that individual match results don't snowball against them.

The Assets Captain Ogilvy Has

Geoff Ogilvy, the 2006 U.S. Open champion serving as International captain, has real weapons. Hideki Matsuyama is one of the most decorated Presidents Cup performers in International history. Tom Kim and Si Woo Kim won their Thursday four-ball pairing together. Min Woo Lee brings ball-striking quality that translates across formats. Sungjae Im provides steady consistency.

The question isn't talent. The Internationals have not lacked talent in their 12-loss streak. The question is whether they can convert the talent into points in the moments that determine sessions — and whether the Medinah venue carries any psychological weight for a team that needs to believe in the possible.

What's Next

Day 2 foursomes results will determine whether the International team is building a genuine comeback or fading toward another one-sided conclusion.
