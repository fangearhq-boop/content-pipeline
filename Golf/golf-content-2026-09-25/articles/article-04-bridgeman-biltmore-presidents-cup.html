From Biltmore to Medinah: Jacob Bridgeman Brings a Career-Best 61 Into His Presidents Cup Debut

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | September 25, 2026

Six days ago, Jacob Bridgeman was in Asheville, North Carolina, shooting a 61 at The Cliffs at Walnut Cove to win the inaugural Biltmore Championship — rallying from five strokes back on Sunday to claim his second PGA Tour title of 2026 and $900,000. Today, he's at Medinah Country Club playing foursomes at the Presidents Cup.

The runway between those two moments — a career-best round and a team-event debut — is almost impossibly short. And yet it arrived at exactly the right time.

The Win That Set the Stage

Bridgeman's Biltmore Championship victory on September 20 was the kind of Sunday that creates momentum. His 61 (-10) final round included 10 birdies. He came from five behind to win by two. It was his second win of the 2026 PGA Tour season, cementing a year that has moved him from emerging player to confirmed Tour presence.

The win also locked up starts in the 2027 major championships — a significant career benefit attached to the seasonal victory. But the immediate reward is the intangible one: he walks into Medinah as the hottest player on either team.

The Match: Experience vs. Form

Bridgeman is paired with Russell Henley in the Day 2 foursomes, facing Hideki Matsuyama and Sungjae Im of the International team. The contrast is stark. Matsuyama has been a Presidents Cup stalwart across multiple editions, winning matches at some of the most critical moments. Im is a consistent performer under team-event pressure.

Against them: a first-timer riding a career high and his more experienced partner. Foursomes alternate-shot doesn't always favor the hotter individual — the format rewards partnership chemistry, strategic sequencing, and shared composure. Bridgeman and Henley will need to find their rhythm quickly.

What's Next

The result of the Henley/Bridgeman vs. Im/Matsuyama match carries weight beyond the individual point. A USA win would extend the lead and deliver a psychological blow to the International team's most reliable pairing. A loss would signal that Matsuyama and Im are ready to anchor an International comeback. Watch the early holes for which team establishes the tone.
