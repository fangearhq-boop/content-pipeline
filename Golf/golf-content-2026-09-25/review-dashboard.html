### Long-Form Post The Presidents Cup Day 2 foursomes are underway at Medinah Country Club, and the USA goes in holding a 3-2 lead after Thursday's four-ball session. Five matches began from 2:05 PM ET today. The five USA pairings: Scottie Scheffler and Sam Burns, Russell Henley and Jacob Bridgeman, Wyndham Clark and Collin Morikawa, Justin Thomas and Cameron Young, and Xander Schauffele and Patrick Cantlay. Against them: the International team featuring Hideki Matsuyama, Sungjae Im, Tom Kim, Si Woo Kim, and others. The foursomes format — alternate-shot, where each player hits every other shot from a shared ball — rewards precision and chemistry between partners in ways that four-ball doesn't. This is a different test from Thursday, and the Internationals are hoping it suits their partnership pairings better. A USA win today (3-2 or better from the session) would build a lead that's difficult to overcome heading into the weekend. An International win in foursomes sets up a dramatic finish. Rahm Committed, Koepka Gone — LIV's 18-Day Player Decision Countdown
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Jon Rahm is staying. Bryson DeChambeau is staying. Cameron Smith is staying. And Brooks Koepka is gone — paid his way out, reportedly $5 million plus forfeited equity, to return to the PGA Tour. That's the current ledger heading into the final 18 days before LIV Golf's player commitment deadline of October 13. Under the BC Partners restructuring deal, at least two-thirds of eligible player claims by dollar value — and half of players by headcount — must agree to participate in the new structure for the $300M rescue financing to close. Rahm reportedly holds LIV's largest-ever contract at around $150 million per bankruptcy filings. His commitment alone moves the needle significantly on the two-thirds-by-dollar threshold. Add DeChambeau, Smith, and Dustin Johnson (who signed a new extension as the 4Aces GC captain), and the headline names are largely secured. The headcount threshold is the more uncertain one. Mid-tier players who signed shorter deals, or players without major performance clauses, have more optionality. Some may prefer waiting to see how the league's 2027 relaunch takes shape before committing. October 13 is the moment of truth. Either the deal closes and LIV 2.0 starts building toward a 2027 restart, or the financing collapses and the league's future becomes genuinely uncertain. ---

Bridgeman at the Presidents Cup — Biltmore Winner on the Biggest Stage
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Jacob Bridgeman is having the best stretch of his career, and the Presidents Cup didn't wait for him to cool down. Last weekend, Bridgeman shot a career-best 61 on Sunday at the inaugural Biltmore Championship Asheville, rallying from five strokes back to win by two shots and earn $900,000. It was his second PGA Tour win of 2026 and cemented his place as one of the tour's emerging stars. Six days later, he's at Medinah Country Club in a Presidents Cup foursomes match alongside Russell Henley — facing off against Hideki Matsuyama and Sungjae Im. The foursomes format is a completely different test from stroke play. There's no individual safety net — every other shot is your partner's, and the mental calculus shifts from personal scoring to collective execution. But players on hot streaks bring something intangible: confidence, routine, a sense that the ball will find the fairway or the cup when it needs to. Bridgeman is 24 (estimated) and playing his first Presidents Cup. Matsuyama has played multiple. Experience favors the International side in that pairing. Form favors Bridgeman. We'll know more this afternoon. ---

The International Team's 1998 Formula — Can History Repeat at Medinah?
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Trailing 3-2 with 28 points still to play for, the International team is chasing history at Medinah Country Club — a course that doesn't mind being the venue for miracles. Twelve years before the International team last won the Presidents Cup, this same course hosted one of the most stunning reversals in team golf history. Europe trailed 10-6 entering Sunday singles at the 2012 Ryder Cup. By Sunday evening, they had won 8.5-3.5 of those matches and claimed the trophy 14.5-13.5. Martin Kaymer held his putt on the final hole to win. Justin Rose, Ian Poulter, Rory McIlroy — all contributed points in what became known as the Miracle at Medinah. The International team's situation isn't as dire as Europe's was in 2012, but the task is clear: they need to win Day 2 foursomes, win Day 3 four-ball, and build enough of a lead that Sunday singles — where the USA typically dominates — don't determine the Cup on their own. Captain Geoff Ogilvy, the 2006 US Open champion, has the horses: Matsuyama, Tom Kim, Min Woo Lee, Sungjae Im, Adam Scott. Whether they can use them in concert is the question. The Medinah grass has seen comebacks. The International team needs to start writing one today. ---
