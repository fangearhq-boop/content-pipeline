### STORY 1: Lindy Duncan Wins The ANNIKA

**Post 1A (Long-form) — 8:00 AM ET**
Lindy Duncan just won her first LPGA title — and the story behind it is worth taking a moment to appreciate.

She turned professional in 2013. She spent 13 years making starts, missing cuts, keeping her card, losing her card, earning it back, and showing up again. This weekend at Pelican Golf Club in Belleair, Florida, in her 222nd career LPGA start, she closed the deal.

Her winning score of 16-under 264 gave her a two-stroke victory over Hye-Jin Choi at The ANNIKA presented by Gainbridge. At 35 years old, she is the oldest first-time LPGA winner since 2008. She is the first maiden winner at The ANNIKA since the tournament began in 2020. And she earned $487,500 from a $3 million purse.

Golf rewards persistence more than almost any sport. Today, it rewarded Lindy Duncan.

### STORY 4: RSM Classic Preview

**Post 4A (Long-form) — 1:00 PM ET**
The final tournament of the 2026 PGA Tour season is almost here.

The RSM Classic at Sea Island Golf Club in Georgia runs November 19-22, closing out the FedExCup Fall schedule with $7.4 million in prize money and 500 FedExCup Fall points on the table.

Sea Island is a special venue. The Seaside and Plantation courses offer two entirely different challenges, and because a significant number of PGA Tour players call Sea Island home year-round, the field tends to be stronger than a typical fall event.

The RSM Classic is also the last chance for players on the PGA Tour card bubble to secure their playing status for 2027. For some, it is a true make-or-break week.

Sami Valimaki won the 2025 edition at -23. Maverick McNealy took 2024.

### STORY 5: LIV Golf Bankruptcy

**Post 5A (Long-form) — 6:00 PM ET**
LIV Golf's bankruptcy process is approaching its next key milestone, and the futures of dozens of players remain unresolved.

The league filed for Chapter 11 protection on November 10. Court filings show $45 million or more owed directly to players. Jon Rahm — the 2026 LIV individual champion — is reportedly owed in excess of $100 million. The next significant court date is November 24.

The PGA Tour has stated that it will not process reinstatement applications until the bankruptcy is fully resolved. That means the players who went to LIV are in a holding pattern, unable to return to the Tour's events regardless of their financial situation with the league.

The target for LIV's emergence from bankruptcy is January 2027. Whether that timeline holds, and whether a reorganized LIV continues in any form, will determine what options these players have heading into next season.

For now: they wait.
