From Losing His Card to Chasing History: Austin Smotherman Three Wins From the Record Books

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | November 16, 2026

Austin Smotherman enters Monday's Austin Championship final round three shots back with a chance at a third consecutive PGA Tour win — a stretch that would rank among the most remarkable individual runs in the tour's modern era.

Two years ago, Austin Smotherman finished 169th in the FedExCup standings and lost his PGA Tour card. It was a finish that most players do not recover from quickly, if at all. The tour's lower circuits are where many cards go to die, replaced by conditional status, Monday qualifying, and the slow fade from the main stage.

Instead, Smotherman went to the Korn Ferry Tour, won twice, and came back. And then he won twice more on the PGA Tour itself — back to back, October 4 and October 25 — and arrived at this week's Austin Championship three shots behind the leader with a chance to do something that has not been done in the modern era of tour golf.

The Two Wins That Built the Story

The Bank of Utah Huntsman Championship on October 4 was Smotherman's 100th career PGA Tour start. He beat Sahith Theegala by two strokes to claim his first title on tour. The timing was notable — a 100th-start win carries a recognizable symbolic weight — but what followed amplified it.

At the Bermuda Championship on October 25, Smotherman went to sudden death against Seamus Power and won. Back-to-back PGA Tour victories are rare in any context, and the fact that Smotherman produced them after regaining his card from the Korn Ferry Tour made the stretch an unlikely story at any point in the season.

What Three in a Row Would Mean

Three consecutive PGA Tour wins is a threshold that places a player in a specific category of greatness within a season. In the modern era, with fields of 150-plus players and improved average skill levels across the tour, sustaining that kind of winning streak requires a combination of form, confidence, and circumstances that rarely aligns for three consecutive weeks.

Smotherman is not winning three events in three consecutive weeks — these wins are spread across the fall schedule. But three consecutive starts that produced victories, within the same season, following the journey he has made from losing his card to this point, would represent the defining individual run of the 2026 PGA Tour calendar.

The Monday Morning Scenario

At -11, Smotherman needs to close three shots on a leader who has held this tournament from the beginning. That is a real gap, but not an insurmountable one over 18 holes on a course where scoring has been accessible all week. If Trace Crowe, who leads at -14, plays conservatively and the cluster of players at -13 also struggles, Smotherman's -11 starting position could become relevant in the back nine.

The conditions that would need to align: Smotherman playing one of his better ball-striking rounds, and the leaders finding enough friction in the final round to allow the leaderboard to compress. Neither condition is guaranteed. Both are plausible.

What's Next

Whatever happens Monday at Austin, Smotherman will arrive at the RSM Classic at Sea Island next week as one of the most compelling players on the fall schedule. Two wins already define his fall. A third would change the conversation about what kind of player he is for the rest of his career.

The journey from 169th place and a lost card to two consecutive wins and a chance at three is the kind of arc that professional golf occasionally produces, usually for players who refused to accept the alternative. Monday morning at Fazio Canyons Course will show us what the next chapter looks like.
