Austin Championship Final Round Begins Monday Morning — Crowe One Clear at Fazio Canyons

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | November 16, 2026

Rain washed out Sunday's scheduled final round at the inaugural Austin Championship, pushing the conclusion to Monday morning with Trace Crowe holding a one-shot lead at 14-under over a four-player cluster at 13-under.

The final round of the inaugural Austin Championship will be played Monday morning at Fazio Canyons Course at Omni Barton Creek Resort in Austin, Texas, after persistent rain made Sunday's round unplayable. Tee times begin at 7:30 AM CT, and what awaits the competitors is a leaderboard packed tight enough that the winner could come from any of the top five names on the board.

Trace Crowe stands one shot clear at 14-under, having led this tournament from the opening round. Behind him, three players sit at 13-under: Chad Ramey, Sami Valimaki, and Erik van Rooyen. Ben Kohles and Max Greyserman share sixth at 12-under. And Austin Smotherman, who comes to Austin having won his last two PGA Tour starts, sits at 11-under — three shots back but very much in the conversation.

Crowe's Wire-to-Wire Lead

Trace Crowe has been in control of this tournament from the beginning, and the fact that he still leads entering the final round reflects genuine consistency across three days of play on a course that has demanded precision and patience. His 14-under total through 54 holes represents rounds of 62, 69, and 71 — not identical in character but sufficient in total to leave him at the top of the leaderboard as the final round approaches.

A wire-to-wire win in a PGA Tour event is a recognized achievement, and Crowe is 18 holes away from adding that distinction to what would already be his first title on tour. The Monday morning format, with the week's accumulated fatigue and an unusual schedule, adds an element of unpredictability that Crowe will need to manage alongside the competitive pressure from the players directly behind him.

The Cluster at Minus-13

Three players at 13-under means three different stories and three separate sources of pressure on the leader. Ramey has been a consistent presence on the PGA Tour without a breakthrough win; Valimaki, the 2025 RSM Classic champion, brings a winner's composure to Monday's chase; van Rooyen, who co-led this event at some point during the week, knows what it feels like to be near the top of a leaderboard with something to prove.

Any of the three could make a run. A leaderboard this compressed in the final round means that a Crowe bogey or two is all that's required for the tournament to become a genuine multi-player race rather than a one-man wire-to-wire story.

Smotherman's Presence at Minus-11

Three shots back is a real deficit. But Austin Smotherman's last two PGA Tour outings ended in victories, and his ability to shoot low when the moment demands it makes his -11 position less like a spectator's seat than it might look on paper. If the leaders play conservatively and Smotherman plays aggressively, the gap between them is closable.

The historical significance of three consecutive wins adds a storyline to Monday's round that the leaderboard alone would not fully provide. Whether Smotherman can manufacture enough birdies to make it relevant — and whether the leaders in front of him stumble — are the two conditions that would need to align for one of the more unusual outcomes in recent PGA Tour history.

What's Next

The Austin Championship final round is live on Golf Channel on Monday morning, with coverage beginning at tee-off. The winner takes home the first Austin Championship trophy and collects points from a $6 million purse. After this week, the PGA Tour moves to the season finale: the RSM Classic at Sea Island, Georgia, running November 19-22.

Monday morning in Austin will determine how the tournament season's final chapter begins. Crowe is in position to write his name on this event's inaugural trophy. The question is whether anyone behind him gives him the opportunity to do it on his own terms.
