Lindy Duncan Wins The ANNIKA in Her 222nd Career Start, Oldest First-Time LPGA Winner Since 2008

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | November 16, 2026

Lindy Duncan ended a 13-year professional journey without a win on Sunday, closing at 16-under 264 at Pelican Golf Club to claim The ANNIKA presented by Gainbridge by two strokes over Hye-Jin Choi.

The numbers that defined Lindy Duncan's professional life before Sunday had accumulated over 13 years: 222 LPGA Tour starts, zero wins, an age of 35 that placed her beyond the age when most of the sport's attention focuses on emerging players. At Pelican Golf Club in Belleair, Florida, she rewrote every one of them in a final round that required a 67 to hold off a field that included some of the best players in women's golf.

Duncan's winning total of 16-under 264 gave her a two-stroke victory over South Korea's Hye-Jin Choi, who had made a late push on Sunday but could not close the gap. The win makes Duncan the oldest first-time winner on the LPGA Tour since 2008, and the first maiden winner at The ANNIKA since the tournament began in 2020.

The Weight of 222 Starts

What makes Duncan's win remarkable is the volume of professional experience she accumulated without breaking through. She turned professional in 2013 and has spent more than a decade as a full-time LPGA Tour player — earning her card, losing it, winning it back, and continuing to show up. Twenty-two dozen starts is not a failure across those years; it is a persistent professional presence in a sport where most players never reach triple digits.

What it also means is that the win came with full context. When Duncan birdied enough holes on Sunday to card a final-round 67, she did not do it as a young player who had not yet experienced sustained difficulty. She did it having experienced every version of the tour's demands and endured years without the result that tournament golf ultimately measures.

How the Week Unfolded

Duncan opened with a stunning 10-under 62 on Thursday that established her as the round-one leader. A second-round 66 kept her at the front of the leaderboard, and a third-round 69 on Saturday left her at -13 entering the final 18 holes with a one-stroke lead.

Sunday brought the kind of pressure that final-round leaders always face — Choi pushed back with a strong round that briefly closed the gap — but Duncan's 67 was steady enough to prevent any dramatic late swing. By the time the round finished, the two-stroke margin represented a controlled rather than a desperate closing performance from the champion.

The Prize and the Milestone

The ANNIKA is a $3 million purse event on the LPGA Tour schedule, and Duncan's winning share of $487,500 represents the largest single paycheck of her professional career by a substantial margin. The tournament is named for Annika Sorenstam, the Hall of Fame player who hosts the event at a course she considers one of her favorites on the LPGA calendar.

Beyond the financial reward, the win carries LPGA membership benefits and an exemption that will keep Duncan on tour through the 2027 season without the anxiety of card qualifying. It also carries the simpler satisfaction of a record book that now reads differently than it did 72 hours ago.

What's Next

The LPGA Tour's season finale, the CME Group Tour Championship, runs November 19-22 at Tiburon Golf Club in Naples, Florida. The CME is the richest event in women's golf, with a $11 million purse and a $4 million check for the winner. Whether Duncan has earned a spot in the field will depend on her CME Globe points position, but regardless, she arrives at the final stretch of 2026 as an LPGA Tour champion for the first time.

What she built over 222 starts brought her here. The 223rd changed everything.
