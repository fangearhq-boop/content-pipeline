LIV Golf Bankruptcy: Jon Rahm's $100M Question and What Comes Next for Players

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | November 16, 2026

With LIV Golf owing $45 million or more to its players and a November 24 bankruptcy hearing approaching, the futures of dozens of professional golfers who left the PGA Tour remain unresolved and complicated.

The November 24 date on the bankruptcy calendar is nine days away, and for the players caught inside LIV Golf's Chapter 11 proceedings, it represents the next moment when something — perhaps not everything, but something — might become clearer about what comes next. LIV filed for bankruptcy protection on November 10. What it left behind is a claims picture that includes $45 million or more owed directly to players and a set of contractual obligations that the bankruptcy court is now working through.

At the top of that list, by reported figures, is Jon Rahm. The 2026 LIV individual champion — he finished the season at 259.90 points, ahead of Bryson DeChambeau in second and Marc Leishman in third — is reportedly owed in excess of $100 million from his LIV contract. What he actually recovers, if anything, will depend on how the bankruptcy proceeds and what priority his claims hold in the court's distribution framework.

The PGA Tour's Position

The PGA Tour has been clear since the bankruptcy filing: there is no reinstatement path for LIV players until the bankruptcy process is complete. Players who left the tour for LIV cannot apply to return, cannot receive sponsor exemptions, and cannot otherwise reintegrate into the PGA Tour schedule while the legal proceedings are ongoing. The tour's position is a waiting posture — it will process reinstatement applications when the process concludes, not before.

What that timeline looks like in practice depends on how long the bankruptcy takes. The target for LIV's emergence from Chapter 11 is January 2027, based on the DIP (debtor-in-possession) financing structure that was approved in the initial proceedings. If that timeline holds, players could theoretically begin the reinstatement process in the first months of 2027. If it slips — bankruptcy proceedings are frequently extended — the timeline moves with it.

Contract Rejections and the Restructuring Question

Among the possibilities the bankruptcy opens is the rejection of existing player contracts. When a company in Chapter 11 seeks to reorganize or liquidate, it can petition the court to reject executory contracts — including player deals — as part of the process of reducing its obligations. Reports from earlier in the proceedings indicated that contracts with players including DeChambeau and Dustin Johnson could face rejection.

Contract rejection in bankruptcy does not mean the player owes the money back; it means the future performance of the contract is not required, and the player becomes a general unsecured creditor for damages. For players owed significant future payments, rejection could mean receiving a fraction of their contracted value, if anything, in the bankruptcy distribution.

Players at the Edges

Not everyone in LIV Golf is in Rahm's financial bracket. Phil Mickelson, Cameron Tringale, and Jason Kokrak were in the league's relegation zone entering the final stages of the 2026 season. For players at the lower end of LIV's financial structure, the bankruptcy represents a different kind of problem: limited future earning potential within a league that may not exist in its current form, combined with a PGA Tour reinstatement process that cannot even begin until the proceedings conclude.

These players face the most difficult version of the LIV calculus. The guaranteed money that attracted them to the league is now subject to court proceedings. The alternative — the PGA Tour — is inaccessible until the courts clear the way. The 2027 season is approaching, and their status for it remains genuinely unclear.

What's Next

The November 24 bankruptcy hearing will address several outstanding issues in the proceedings and may provide more clarity about the timeline and structure of whatever LIV Golf emerges as — or whether it emerges at all. The BC Partners-backed LIV 2.0 concept has been discussed as a possible reorganized entity targeting January 2027, but those plans are contingent on the court proceedings going a particular way.

For now, Jon Rahm, Bryson DeChambeau, and the rest of the LIV roster watch and wait. The 2026 LIV season is finished. The 2026 PGA Tour season is finishing this week. The question of where these players play their golf in 2027, and what they get paid for it, will not be answered until something happens in a New Jersey bankruptcy court.
