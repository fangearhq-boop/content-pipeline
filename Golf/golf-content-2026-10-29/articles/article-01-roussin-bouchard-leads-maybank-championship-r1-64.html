Roussin-Bouchard's Stunning 64 — Three Closing Birdies Put Her Atop Maybank Championship

By Ryan Calloway

October 29, 2026

LPGA Tour

Pauline Roussin-Bouchard finished her first round Thursday at the Kuala Lumpur Golf and Country Club the way golfers dream of finishing rounds: with three consecutive birdies. The sequence carried her to an 8-under 64 and a one-stroke lead at the LPGA Maybank Championship, establishing her as the early frontrunner at one of the tour's most prestigious international events.

Closing a round with three birdies in a row is not something that happens by accident. It requires nerve, execution, and the particular kind of confidence that separates players who win from players who contend. Roussin-Bouchard showed all three in the final holes of a round that left her leading the field in Malaysia.

A Statement First Round

An 8-under opening round at the Maybank Championship is the kind of score that sets the tone for the entire week. The KLGCC West Course, set in the heart of Kuala Lumpur, demands precision on approach shots and reward players who manage their way around its layout intelligently. Roussin-Bouchard did more than manage — she attacked, and particularly in the final stretch, she executed at the highest level.

The three closing birdies are worth examining not just for the scorecard they produced, but for what they say about her mental approach to the round. Many players in similar positions — at a good number, with the round near its end — shift into protective mode. They try not to drop shots rather than seeking to gain them. Roussin-Bouchard did the opposite, and it produced the round of the tournament through 18 holes.

She leads by one stroke, which means the field can catch her. But the player who shoots 64 with three closing birdies in Round 1 is not giving her rivals reason for confidence going into Friday.

Hull and the Competition Close Behind

Charley Hull opened with a 6-under round to sit in a share of fourth place, two strokes behind Roussin-Bouchard. Hull is one of the most exciting players on the LPGA Tour — capable of spectacular shot-making and, when things are clicking, the kind of player who can run through a leaderboard quickly over 72 holes.

Two strokes is a manageable gap at this stage. With three rounds remaining on a course where birdies are achievable, Hull will need only a slightly better day on Friday to move into contention at or near the top. Defending champion Miyu Yamashita is also in the field and will be looking to replicate last year's title-winning performance.

The Maybank Championship's International Stage

The Maybank Championship is one of the LPGA Tour's most significant international stops — a tournament with strong history, a high-quality field, and a setting in Southeast Asia that gives it a distinctive character. The event draws players from across the globe, and a first-round leaderboard featuring European, British, and Asian stars reflects the increasingly global nature of the women's game.

For Roussin-Bouchard, Thursday's performance puts her in the position every player wants: leading the tournament after the first round, with momentum and confidence carrying into the rest of the week.

What's Next

Round 2 of the LPGA Maybank Championship begins Friday at the KLGCC West Course in Kuala Lumpur. Roussin-Bouchard will tee off as the first-round leader, carrying her 8-under score and the knowledge that every player behind her is chasing. Charley Hull at -6, two back, will be looking to close the gap. Defending champion Miyu Yamashita and Lottie Woad are also in the field and positioned to make a move. The Maybank Championship runs through Sunday, November 1, with the winner emerging from one of the most competitive fields on the LPGA Tour's international calendar.
