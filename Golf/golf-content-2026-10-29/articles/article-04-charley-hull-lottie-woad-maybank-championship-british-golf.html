Charley Hull Sits Two Back After Round 1 at Maybank Championship as British Golf Shows Well in KL

By Jake Torres

October 29, 2026

LPGA Tour

Charley Hull made her intentions clear on Thursday at the Kuala Lumpur Golf and Country Club. A 6-under opening round put her in a share of fourth place at the LPGA Maybank Championship, just two strokes behind leader Pauline Roussin-Bouchard. Lottie Woad, one of the more exciting young talents in women's golf, is also inside the field at 1-under. Two British players positioned well after 18 holes of play in Malaysia is a development the golf community will be paying close attention to.

Hull's Positioning

Two strokes back after Round 1 is a position that translates cleanly to tournament contention. Hull is a player capable of low scoring on any given day — she has shown that capacity repeatedly on the LPGA Tour, and a 6-under first round demonstrates that her game is in good enough shape to go lower when the moment calls for it.

The question going into Friday is whether she can close the gap. A round matching Thursday's output from the leader, or better, puts Hull on or near the top of the leaderboard heading into the weekend. Roussin-Bouchard's 8-under round is reachable — it is two more birdies on a course where birdies are available.

Hull is one of the most popular players on the LPGA Tour internationally. Her personality, her social media presence, and the straightforward joy she takes from good golf have built a following that extends well beyond the core golf audience. A legitimate title run in Kuala Lumpur would bring significant attention to the Maybank Championship in its final rounds.

Lottie Woad in the Picture

Woad's 1-under opening round leaves her work to do if she wants to contend, but the KLGCC West Course offers enough scoring opportunities that a 5- or 6-under Friday could move her meaningfully up the leaderboard. She is one of the top-ranked players on the LPGA Tour at this stage of her young career, and her best golf can compete with anyone in the field.

The dynamic of two British players in a high-profile international event creates a natural teamwork narrative, even if they are competing against each other. Both players sharing a home flag in Malaysia adds a subplot that brings in fans who might not otherwise follow every shot of an LPGA event in Southeast Asia.

Defending Champion in Pursuit

Miyu Yamashita won the Maybank Championship in 2025 and is in the field this week looking to defend her title. The Japanese star's familiarity with the KLGCC West Course gives her a genuine advantage — she knows where the birdies are and where the trouble lies. A defending champion who is familiar with the conditions is never to be overlooked on a leaderboard, regardless of where she sits after the first round.

The field around the top of the Maybank Championship after 18 holes is competitive and talented. Hull is well-positioned. The tournament is far from decided. Friday's second round will clarify who is genuinely in the running as the week builds toward its conclusion.

What's Next

Round 2 of the Maybank Championship begins Friday at the KLGCC West Course. Charley Hull enters 6-under in a share of fourth, two strokes behind leader Roussin-Bouchard. Lottie Woad at 1-under has ground to make up but three full rounds to make it up. Defending champion Miyu Yamashita will also be in play. The Maybank Championship concludes Sunday, November 1 in Kuala Lumpur. With two British players in the conversation and a dramatic first-round leader on top, this tournament has plenty of story still to tell.
