Austin Smotherman Arrives in Mexico Chasing Three Consecutive PGA Tour Wins

By Jake Torres

October 29, 2026

PGA Tour

Austin Smotherman won the week before last. He won last week. He is in Puerto Vallarta this week trying to win again. If he does, he will have accomplished something that defines true excellence in professional golf — three consecutive wins on the PGA Tour, a run that separates a hot streak from a historic moment.

The 2026 VidantaWorld Mexico Open at Vidanta Vallarta teed off Thursday, and Smotherman enters the field carrying the momentum of back-to-back victories in October. Every other player in a 120-man field is aware of what he has done and keenly motivated to be the one who stops him.

The Rarity of Three Consecutive Wins

The modern PGA Tour is exceptional at spreading victories around. The depth of the field, the quality of course setups, and the pressure that comes with being a marked man in a third consecutive event all conspire against winning streaks of this length. When a player wins back-to-back events, it already represents a remarkable stretch. Three in a row puts them in rarefied historical company.

Smotherman's October run started with a playoff win at the Bermuda Championship and extended through the following week's event. The wins came in different conditions, against different fields, requiring different kinds of execution from tee to green. That versatility is what makes a streak meaningful — it demonstrates that the player is not merely catching a single favorable course match or getting lucky in a weak draw. Smotherman has proven himself over multiple weeks of sustained quality.

He has history at Vidanta Vallarta as well. The course is not unfamiliar to him, and his past rounds here suggest the venue suits his game. That combination — form and course affinity — is as favorable a setup as any player could hope for in a pursuit of history.

The Field That Stands in His Way

Wyndham Clark enters the Mexico Open as the betting favorite — a former major champion with the kind of ball-striking that tends to show up well at this course. Tony Finau's length and composure have made him a perennial contender at tournaments on soft, scoring-friendly layouts. Max Homa brings a complete game and the mental strength to handle pressure situations. Tom Kim is one of the most talented and engaging young players on tour.

Any of these players is capable of winning this week. But to beat Smotherman outright, they will likely need to beat his best golf — and right now, Smotherman's best golf has been very good for several weeks in a row.

What's Next

The VidantaWorld Mexico Open continues through Sunday, November 1 at Vidanta Vallarta in Puerto Vallarta, Mexico. Round 1 is underway Thursday, and the story of Austin Smotherman's three-win chase will intensify with every hole he plays. If he stays near the lead through the weekend, the golf world will be watching closely. If he wins, the October streak becomes one of the defining narratives of the 2026 PGA Tour season. Either way, he is the player to watch in Puerto Vallarta this week.
