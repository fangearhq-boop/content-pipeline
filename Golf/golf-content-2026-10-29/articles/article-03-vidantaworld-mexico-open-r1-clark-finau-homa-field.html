VidantaWorld Mexico Open Gets Underway in Puerto Vallarta — Clark, Finau, Homa in the Field

By Ryan Calloway

October 29, 2026

PGA Tour

The 2026 VidantaWorld Mexico Open teed off Thursday at Vidanta Vallarta in Puerto Vallarta, Mexico, opening one of the PGA Tour's most scenic and historically low-scoring events of the fall schedule. A 120-player field descended on the coastal course, including some of the game's most recognizable names — and one particularly in-form storyline that has captured the entire golf world's attention.

Austin Smotherman, coming off back-to-back PGA Tour victories, is in the field and chasing a historic third consecutive win. But he will face serious competition from a roster of established players who have proven they can win on any given week.

Key Players to Follow

Wyndham Clark arrives as the tournament's most heavily favored player in the betting markets. Clark's combination of power off the tee and touch around the greens has produced strong results in coastal conditions, and Vidanta Vallarta's layout rewards the kind of precise iron play that defines his best golf. He enters the week as the player with the most to lose — the favorite who needs to play like one.

Tony Finau brings his impressive length and a game built for scoring on receptive greens. His best weeks tend to come when the field spreads out early and his birdie rate allows him to build a lead rather than scramble from behind. Conditions in Puerto Vallarta — with soft fairways and amenable pin positions — could set up exactly the kind of scoring environment he thrives in.

Max Homa's relentless preparation and course management have been hallmarks of his recent success on tour. He approaches each event methodically, and a course he has played in previous years will give him a significant data advantage heading into the final two rounds of the week.

Tom Kim is one of the most exciting young players in professional golf — talented, aggressive, and capable of the kind of birdie run that shifts leaderboards in a hurry. Tournaments on lower-difficulty setups tend to suit players like Kim who can attack without the constant fear of disaster.

Course and Conditions

Vidanta Vallarta sits along Mexico's Pacific coast, with the warm ocean air and lush tropical setting that distinguishes it from inland desert courses or the tighter fairways of more traditional tournament venues. The course has produced low scoring in previous years, and this week appears set up similarly — rain forecast for Thursday morning was expected to soften the conditions further before most groups teed off.

Low scoring means a close leaderboard and late drama. Players who make their run on the weekend will be competing against a field that has likely already posted very low numbers, meaning Friday and Saturday rounds that stay close to the leaders matter enormously for positioning on Sunday.

What's Next

Round 1 continues throughout Thursday at Vidanta Vallarta, with the leaderboard taking shape as groups complete their opening 18 holes. Round 2 follows Friday, with the field cut reducing the group heading into the weekend. The VidantaWorld Mexico Open concludes Sunday, November 1. Leaderboard tracking and round-by-round updates will follow throughout the week. The story of Austin Smotherman's three-win chase makes this an event worth following from the first shot to the last putt on Sunday.
