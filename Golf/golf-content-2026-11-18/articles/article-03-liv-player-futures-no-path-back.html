PGA Tour Board Confirms No Reinstatement Path for LIV Players After November Meeting

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | November 18, 2026

The PGA Tour's board met Monday, November 16, and left without a road map for former LIV Golf players. Commissioner Brian Rolapp confirmed afterward that the Tour has no current plans to establish a reinstatement pathway — a decision that keeps more than a dozen of the world's most recognizable golfers in professional limbo.

LIV Golf announced November 5 that it would wind down operations after failing to secure investment for a proposed restructured league. The bankruptcy filing had been public since September, but the wind-down declaration made the question of player futures urgent. Golfers who had signed guaranteed LIV deals now face a sport where their old home won't take them back — at least not on any structured basis.

Who Is Affected

Jon Rahm, Bryson DeChambeau, and Dustin Johnson are among the highest-profile players without a clear home after LIV's closure. Each left the PGA Tour under different circumstances, and each faces different obstacles to returning.

Under existing Tour rules, players who departed for LIV are subject to a one-year ban based on their last LIV appearance before any reinstatement is considered. Even if the Tour established a formal pathway — which it hasn't — the timeline for most players to begin competing again would extend well into 2027.

The DP World Tour has emerged as an alternative for some, but its guaranteed money doesn't approach what LIV provided, and limited playing opportunities mean players ranked outside the top tier may struggle to get starts.

Koepka as the Exception

Brooks Koepka has already returned to PGA Tour competition, the only major LIV player to successfully negotiate a path back. His arrangement — which reportedly involved a $5 million charitable contribution and a five-year ban on equity stakes in competing ventures — set a high bar that the Tour has not shown interest in lowering.

Rolapp's post-meeting comments suggested that the Koepka precedent is an exception, not a template. The Tour appears content to let the LIV chapter close without rushing to integrate its former players, citing fairness concerns for members who never left.

What's Next

The PGA Tour's formal position may evolve as the bankruptcy proceedings conclude. A court date in late November was scheduled to address the wind-down plan, and the final legal resolution of LIV's assets and obligations could change the calculus for player reinstatement timelines.

For now, the players most affected are facing a critical offseason: weigh waiting for a PGA Tour update, pursue DP World Tour status, explore other global tours, or simply sit out until clarity arrives. The decisions made in the coming weeks will shape the competitive landscape of professional golf for years ahead.
