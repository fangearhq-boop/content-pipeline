LIV Golf Files for Bankruptcy, Plans "LIV 2.0" with BC Partners

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | September 30, 2026

LIV Golf filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on September 8, 2026, ending the original chapter of Saudi Arabia's ambitious breakaway golf project and opening the door to what CEO Scott O'Neil is calling "LIV 2.0."

The filing came after LIV Golf lost its Public Investment Fund (PIF) backing following the conclusion of the 2026 season, which ran from February 5 through August 30. The league has entered into a Restructuring Support Agreement with BC Partners Advisors L.P., which proposes a recapitalization of the business under new ownership.

What LIV 2.0 Would Look Like

According to reports, the restructured LIV Golf would look substantially different from its original form. The new entity would shift majority ownership to its players — a sharp departure from the Saudi sovereign wealth fund model that defined the league's first four years.

Operationally, the changes are significant. The schedule drops from 14 events to 10, with five events in the United States and five internationally. The field expands from 57 players to 75. A 54-hole cut is introduced for the first time — one of the most notable departures from the original format that drew criticism from traditionalists.

The team concept, one of LIV Golf's most distinctive features, would be rebuilt around nationalities rather than franchise-style rosters. The $30 million purses that initially lured players away from the PGA Tour are also expected to decrease under the new financial structure.

The 2026 Season's Final Chapter

The 2026 LIV Golf League season ended August 30 with 4Aces GC taking the team title and Jon Rahm finishing as the individual points champion. Rahm, who left the PGA Tour after winning the 2023 Masters and 2023 US Open, was the most prominent recruit LIV ever made — and finished the final season of the original era at the top of the standings.

The majority of LIV Golf's workforce was informed of employment terminations in the first week of September, just days before the bankruptcy announcement. The logistical wind-down of the original league was swift once the PIF funding ended.

What Comes Next

LIV Golf is hoping to re-emerge in the early portion of 2027 with the new structure in place. The league expects BC Partners and its other new financial backers to support the recapitalization, though the agreement remains subject to court approval through the bankruptcy process.

For players who signed long-term LIV contracts expecting security, the situation raises fresh questions. A player-owned model may be more sustainable in the long run, but the transition period carries uncertainty for everyone involved.

Golf's landscape has been irrevocably changed by LIV's four-year run. Whether LIV 2.0 survives to reshape it again, or becomes a footnote, will be determined in the months ahead.
