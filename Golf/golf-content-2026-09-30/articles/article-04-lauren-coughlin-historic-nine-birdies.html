Lauren Coughlin's Historic 9 Consecutive Birdies: A Round for the Record Books

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | September 30, 2026

On Saturday at the Walmart NW Arkansas Championship, Lauren Coughlin produced one of the most extraordinary rounds in the recorded history of professional golf. Starting her back nine on hole 10, she made nine consecutive birdies — a feat that has never been achieved to open any half of a round in the history of either the LPGA Tour or the PGA Tour.

The raw score on those nine holes: 26. The lowest ever recorded on any nine-hole stretch across both tours. The full round score: 10-under 61. She jumped 106 places up the leaderboard in a single afternoon at Pinnacle Country Club in Rogers, Arkansas.

Hole by Hole

Coughlin teed off on the 10th hole — playing the back nine first — and made a birdie. Then she made another on 11. And another on 12. Each subsequent hole added to the streak, and with each one, the weight of the history she was making grew heavier.

By the time she walked off the 18th green having made her ninth consecutive birdie, it was clear that what Coughlin had accomplished was not just a good round or a hot stretch. It was a record that, statistically, may never be matched.

The Context of Greatness

The LPGA Tour and PGA Tour have recorded rounds and statistics going back decades. The fact that no player on either circuit had ever made nine consecutive birdies to open a round half puts Coughlin's performance in perspective.

Her back-nine score of 26 on a par-36 nine represents a staggering 10-under performance for that half. The combined round of 10-under 61 reflects a player who was both historically hot on the back nine and still in solid form on the front nine.

She jumped 106 spots up the leaderboard — but did not win the tournament. Yuna Nishimura's wire-to-wire dominance was simply untouchable over three rounds, and Coughlin's Saturday explosion came too late to close the gap entirely.

A Round That Stands Alone

Golf is a sport that often measures greatness through victories. Coughlin did not win at NW Arkansas. But what she produced on Saturday exists outside that framing.

Nine consecutive birdies to open the back nine. A raw nine-hole score of 26. A full-round 61. Those numbers do not need a trophy to validate them. They speak entirely for themselves — and they will be referenced in golf record books for as long as the game is played.
