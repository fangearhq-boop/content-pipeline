USA Wins Presidents Cup 17-13 at Medinah, Claims 11th Straight

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | September 30, 2026

Team USA won the 2026 Presidents Cup 17-13 at Medinah Country Club, completing an 11th consecutive Presidents Cup victory in a Sunday singles session that ended the International team's hopes before the final match was decided.

Jacob Bridgeman clinched the Cup on the 18th hole, defeating Min Woo Lee 1-up to push the Americans past the points threshold they needed. USA won the Sunday singles session 9.5-2.5 — a dominant performance that reflected the week's overall story.

A Dominant Singles Session

Going into Sunday, the Americans had built a lead through the foursomes and fourball sessions across the first three days. The singles gave them the chance to put the result beyond doubt — and they did exactly that.

Two-time major champion Collin Morikawa was among the key contributors, as was 21-year-old rookie Jackson Koivun, who delivered one of the more impressive performances of the week at just 21 years old. Both players flipped their matches on the back nine to help seal the American victory.

Bridgeman's moment on the 18th was the final punctuation mark. His 1-up win over Lee capped a week in which the Americans never truly looked threatened.

The International Drought Continues

For the International team, captained by Geoff Ogilvy, the 2026 Presidents Cup follows a painful and familiar script. They have not won since 1998, when they defeated the United States at Royal Melbourne in Australia.

Since then, it has been 11 losses in a row. This year's team featured Hideki Matsuyama, Tom Kim, Min Woo Lee, and Adam Scott — a genuinely talented squad — but USA's depth proved decisive again.

The format question hangs over the event. At what point does the Presidents Cup's competitive imbalance become a structural problem rather than a run of American dominance? The answer has not yet arrived, but it grows harder to avoid with each passing edition.

What's Next

For Team USA, the Presidents Cup win marks another successful defense of the trophy they have held since the turn of the millennium. The next Presidents Cup is scheduled for 2028.

For the International team, the work of building toward that edition begins now. The talent is there. The structure may need to change.

At Medinah in 2026, the Americans were simply better. That is the straightforward truth of an 11th consecutive Presidents Cup victory.
