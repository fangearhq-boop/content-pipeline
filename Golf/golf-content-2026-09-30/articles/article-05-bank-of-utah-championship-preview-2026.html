Bank of Utah Championship Preview: FedExCup Fall Heads to Black Desert Resort

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | September 30, 2026

After Presidents Cup week at Medinah, the PGA Tour's FedExCup Fall schedule resumes Thursday with the Bank of Utah Championship at Black Desert Resort in Ivins, Utah — a par-71, 7,421-yard layout in the red rocks of southern Utah that has quickly become a fan favorite since joining the Tour calendar.

The tournament runs October 1-4 with a $6 million purse and a 120-player field. Rounds one and two begin Thursday and Friday, with weekend play Saturday and Sunday.

The Course

Black Desert Resort Golf Course plays at 7,421 yards with a par of 71. The layout in Ivins, Utah — in Washington County near St. George — features dramatic desert terrain and the kind of visual drama that has made it a distinctive stop on the FedExCup Fall schedule since the tournament began at this venue.

The course rewards birdies, but the combination of difficult early holes and penalty zones can punish players who press too aggressively. The 2025 edition saw Billy Horschel lead the field with 26 birdies but finish at just T11 after accumulating two doubles and a quad in his opening rounds.

Notable Players in the Field

The 120-player field includes a range of established Tour veterans and younger players chasing points and status in the fall stretch. Max Homa, Tony Finau, Matt Wallace, Marco Penge, and Billy Horschel are among the more recognizable names in the field this week.

Horschel is a compelling watch at Black Desert. His 2025 performance demonstrated that his game suits the course — nobody made more birdies — but converting that into a finish will require more consistency in the early going. He is one of six players in the field who have won five or more PGA Tour events.

What to Watch

The FedExCup Fall is a stretch of the season that rewards players who stay hot and stay consistent. Points accumulate across the fall events, and the Bank of Utah Championship offers a full points allocation for those who perform well.

With a course that has already proven its character, a field of 120 chasing $6 million, and the scenic backdrop of southern Utah, the Bank of Utah Championship provides a compelling start to October golf. Thursday's first-round scores will set the early leaderboard — and at Black Desert, it tends to move quickly.
