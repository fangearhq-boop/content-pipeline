### STORY 1: USA Wins Presidents Cup 11th Straight at Medinah

**FB Long Post**
Team USA has done it again. The United States won the 2026 Presidents Cup 17-13 at Medinah Country Club, claiming an 11th consecutive Presidents Cup victory and extending a streak that has now stretched to nearly three decades.

The decisive moment came Sunday on the 18th hole, where Jacob Bridgeman defeated Min Woo Lee 1-up to clinch the cup. USA dominated the Sunday singles session, winning 9.5 of the available 12 points (9.5-2.5).

Whether you think the Presidents Cup needs to find a more competitive format or believe the Americans simply deserve their dominance, one thing is certain: Team USA is the story every September.

### STORY 2: LIV Golf Files for Bankruptcy, Plans LIV 2.0

**FB Long Post**
The LIV Golf experiment, as originally conceived, is over.

On September 8, LIV Golf filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy after losing its Saudi Public Investment Fund backing following the 2026 season. The league has entered into a Restructuring Support Agreement with BC Partners Advisors, and CEO Scott O'Neil is already calling the future plan "LIV 2.0."

What does LIV 2.0 look like? A player-owned tour with 10 events in 2027 — five in the United States, five internationally. The field expands from 57 to 75 players. A 54-hole cut is introduced for the first time. The team concept gets rebuilt around nationalities rather than franchise-style rosters. And the $30 million purses that attracted players away from the PGA Tour? Those are coming down.

The 2026 LIV Golf season ended on August 30 with 4Aces GC winning the team title and Jon Rahm finishing as the individual points champion. It was one final chapter of the original LIV era before the lawyers took over.

What happens next is genuinely uncertain. But golf's landscape — already reshaped by LIV's four-year run — looks different again heading into 2027.

### STORY 3: Yuna Nishimura Wins Walmart NW Arkansas Championship

**FB Long Post**
Yuna Nishimura had been one of the best players in Japan for years. Six wins on the Japan LPGA. Consistent performances everywhere she teed it up. But LPGA Tour victories are a different thing entirely — and on Sunday in Rogers, Arkansas, she finally got hers.

Nishimura closed with a 5-under 66 at Pinnacle Country Club to finish at 21-under par, winning the Walmart NW Arkansas Championship by five shots in a wire-to-wire performance. Aki Iwai, Chanettee Wannasaen, and In Gee Chun tied for second at 16-under.

At 26, Nishimura becomes the LPGA Tour's third Rolex First-Time Winner of 2026, joining Yana Wilson and Gina Kim. She led from the first round and never wavered — a dominant display that left no doubt.

"Wire-to-wire" is often overused in golf. Nishimura earned the description.

### STORY 4: Lauren Coughlin's Historic Back Nine

**FB Long Post**
What happened on the back nine at Pinnacle Country Club on Saturday may never happen again.

Lauren Coughlin teed off on the 10th hole and made a birdie. Then she made another. And another. By the time she walked off the 18th green, she had made nine consecutive birdies to open her back nine — the most to open any half of a round in the recorded history of both the LPGA Tour and the PGA Tour.

Her raw score on the back nine: 26. That is the lowest raw score ever recorded on any nine-hole stretch across both tours.

Her full round: 10-under 61. She jumped 106 spots up the leaderboard in a single afternoon.

Coughlin didn't win the tournament — Yuna Nishimura was too consistent through all three rounds — but what she produced Saturday is the kind of thing that gets framed and hung on walls. A round that exists outside the context of the leaderboard. A round that just needs to be remembered.
