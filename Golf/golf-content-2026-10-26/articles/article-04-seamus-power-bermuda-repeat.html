Seamus Power Eyes History at Port Royal — Can He Win Bermuda Championship for Second Time?

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | October 26, 2026

Power won this tournament at this course in 2022. On Monday, standing tied at the top with Austin Smotherman, he has the chance to do something rare: win the same PGA Tour event in different seasons separated by four years.

Seamus Power has stood on this tee before. He has walked these fairways at Port Royal Golf Course in Bermuda, felt the Atlantic wind, judged the angles, made the putts that counted. In 2022, he did it better than anyone in the field and left the island as the Butterfield Bermuda Championship champion — one of the defining moments of his professional career.

On Monday morning, he has the chance to do it again. Power is tied with Austin Smotherman at 16-under par heading into a sudden-death playoff that begins at 8 a.m. ET on Golf Channel. Sunday's final round was abandoned due to dangerous high winds, shortening the event to 54 holes and creating the unusual scenario of two players sharing the 54-hole lead with the title still undecided.

Why Port Royal Suits Power

Port Royal is not a standard PGA Tour venue. Its location on the Bermuda coastline means wind is always a factor, and the course demands the kind of disciplined, patient approach that Power's game is built around. He is not a bombers-and-scoring machine player; he is a shot-maker who manages courses with intelligence. Those qualities have made him a consistent performer wherever he plays — and a particularly good fit for Bermuda.

When Power won in 2022, he did so in difficult, windy scoring conditions that played into his strengths. Monday is forecast to be similarly challenging, with scattered showers and gusts up to 30 mph. If anything, the conditions favor a player who has already navigated these circumstances at this course before.

Power's Playoff Pedigree

One of the more useful facts about Seamus Power heading into Monday's sudden death: he has been in a PGA Tour playoff before, and he won it. In 2021, Power bested J.T. Poston in a six-hole sudden-death marathon at the ISCO Championship, demonstrating exactly the kind of patience and composure that extended playoffs require. He is 1-0 in that situation on Tour, and that experience matters when a tournament title is on the line in one hole at a time.

His opponent, Austin Smotherman, is having an extraordinary month — but Smotherman has never been in a playoff before. Power knows what that specific pressure feels like. That knowledge is not nothing.

What's Next

If Power wins Monday, he will hold the Bermuda Championship title for the second time — a four-year span between his two victories at the same course, a distinction that would put his name alongside a small list of multiple-time winners at specific PGA Tour venues. He will also earn $1.08 million and add critical FedExCup points heading into the closing stretch of the fall season. A win Monday would move him up the rankings and strengthen his Tour card position heading into the winter.

For Seamus Power, Monday morning at Port Royal is not unfamiliar territory. It is, in the best possible way, exactly where he has been before.
