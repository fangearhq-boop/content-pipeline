Scottie Scheffler at 181 Consecutive Weeks at No. 1 — Tiger's Record Is 100 Away and Closing

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | October 26, 2026

The world's best golfer continues an unbroken run at the top of the rankings. With approximately two years left before he could theoretically match Tiger's all-time mark, Scheffler is no longer in the same galaxy as the record — he is in the same atmosphere.

There is no drama in the World Golf Rankings right now. The top spot belongs to Scottie Scheffler and has for years — and as of this week, Scheffler has spent approximately 181 consecutive weeks as the world's No. 1 golfer. He is, by a wide margin, the best player on the planet. The only remaining question of historical significance is how long that run lasts.

Tiger Woods holds the all-time record for consecutive weeks at World No. 1: 281. He set that mark during the apex of one of the most dominant careers in sports history. It once seemed like a number no one would approach. Scheffler has approximately 100 weeks to go.

The Math of History

One hundred weeks is two full years. For most golfers, maintaining No. 1 for two weeks is a career achievement. Scheffler has now done it for nearly three and a half years straight — and the question of whether anyone can unseat him in the next two years is, based on current evidence, genuinely hard to answer affirmatively.

The next-ranked players in the world are excellent golfers. But "excellent" is not enough to push Scheffler off the summit. He has combined major championship victories with consistent top-10 performances across every format and surface the Tour offers. His form in 2026 — which has included major hardware and continued dominance — gives no indication of a player on the downslope.

What It Takes to Stay at No. 1

The World Golf Rankings use a rolling two-year performance window, weighted toward recent results. Staying at No. 1 for consecutive years does not require winning every week — but it requires consistent, high-level play with enough major and high-points-value results to stay ahead of any rival who catches form. Scheffler has done this so consistently that the conversation has shifted from "will he lose No. 1?" to "can he approach Tiger's record?"

That question would have seemed absurd three years ago. It does not seem absurd now.

The Legacy in Progress

Tiger's 281-week record was built on a level of dominance that defined an entire era of golf. Matching it would not diminish Tiger's legacy — nothing could — but it would place Scheffler in a conversation reserved for the game's greatest figures. He may or may not get there. Injuries, form changes, and the sport's unpredictable nature all apply. But for now, week by week, Scheffler is making the case.

What's Next

Scheffler's 2026 season continues with several events remaining before the calendar turns. Each week at No. 1 is another entry in what is becoming one of the defining statistical runs in professional golf's modern era. The record is still 100 weeks away — but it was 281 weeks away not long ago. The gap is closing.
