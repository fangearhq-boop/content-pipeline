LIV Golf's October 25 Deadline Has Passed — What Happens Now?

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | October 26, 2026

The restructuring deadline for LIV Golf 2.0 came and went Sunday. With most stars having said no and Saudi Arabia's PIF withdrawing long-term funding, the breakaway tour's future has never been less certain.

The October 25 deadline is behind us. Whatever happens next to LIV Golf — and that question is now more urgent than ever — it will not be decided by a locker room consensus of committed superstars. The stars, by and large, are walking.

Jon Rahm, the player who became the face of LIV's competitive credibility and who won three straight individual titles in 2026 including the season-ending Indianapolis event, has confirmed he will not participate in a restructured "LIV 2.0." Adrian Meronk, David Puig, and other members of the talent roster that LIV spent enormous sums assembling have followed. The list of players who publicly committed to LIV 2.0 as of last week included just four names: Richard Bland, Cameron Tringale, Brendan Steele, and Ben Campbell.

The Financial Reality

The backdrop to all of this is straightforward. Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund — the financial engine behind LIV Golf since its launch — confirmed it is only funding the league through the conclusion of the 2026 season. PIF's exit from ongoing funding left the tour in search of a new business model and a new backer.

BC Partners Credit stepped forward with a proposed restructuring package: up to $300 million in financing, contingent on the league securing a "critical mass" of players willing to participate in LIV 2.0. That condition is now the crux of the matter. With most of the league's highest-profile names exiting, whether BC Partners considers the remaining roster sufficient to proceed with investment is the defining question hanging over the tour.

The Pressure From All Sides

LIV players face a complicated landscape regardless of the league's fate. The DP World Tour has made clear that players cannot hold dual membership in both it and LIV Golf in 2027. For players who want a path back to European Tour eligibility — and eventually the Ryder Cup — the decision to stay with a struggling LIV is not just a financial question but a career one.

Ian Poulter, one of the original LIV signings and a player whose high profile made early headlines, remained publicly uncertain about his future as of mid-October. His decision — and those of the many players who stayed quiet — will have been part of the October 25 calculus.

What This Means for Golf

LIV's arrival in 2022 upended professional golf's structure, diverted billions in Saudi money into the sport, produced genuine competitive action, and sparked the most consequential governance battle in the game's modern history. For two seasons, it operated as a genuine rival circuit with legitimate prize money and some of the sport's biggest names. Whether that chapter is now closing or whether BC Partners and a smaller player base can sustain something into 2027 and beyond is the question of the month.

What's Next

LIV's attorneys told a bankruptcy court that a decision on whether LIV 2.0 proceeds was expected by the end of October 2026. That window is closing. An announcement — whether it signals the tour's continuation, restructuring, or wind-down — could come any day. For the players who wagered their careers on the breakaway circuit, for the PGA Tour which has spent years navigating the disruption, and for golf fans who have watched the sport reshape itself in real time, the news when it comes will be significant.
