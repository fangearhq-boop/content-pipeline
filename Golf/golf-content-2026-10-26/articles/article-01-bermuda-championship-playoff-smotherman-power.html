Bermuda Championship Heads to Monday Playoff — Smotherman and Power Tied at 16-Under After Wind Chaos

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | October 26, 2026

Sunday's final round at Port Royal was abandoned due to dangerous high winds. With Austin Smotherman and Seamus Power deadlocked at the top, the 2026 Butterfield Bermuda Championship will be decided in sudden death on Monday morning.

Nature had the final say at the 2026 Butterfield Bermuda Championship. Sunday's scheduled final round at Port Royal Golf Course was abandoned when dangerous winds made play impossible, and the PGA Tour made the call to shorten the event to 54 holes. The full-field results are final — but the champion is not yet decided.

Austin Smotherman and Seamus Power finished Sunday tied at 16-under par, and they will contest a sudden-death playoff starting Monday morning at 8 a.m. ET. The format begins on the par-3 16th hole, with golf television tuning in on Golf Channel for what promises to be a dramatic early-morning finish at one of the PGA Tour's most atmospheric venues. The winner earns $1.08 million from the event's $6 million purse; the runner-up takes home $654,000.

The Weather Story

Port Royal in Bermuda is famous for its exposure to the Atlantic and the unpredictable conditions the island can generate. Sunday's forecast proved accurate in the worst way — gusts making competitive play unsafe, forcing the Tour's hand. The decision to reduce the event to 54 holes and schedule a Monday playoff was announced in advance, giving fans and broadcast partners time to adjust.

Monday's conditions are still expected to be challenging, with scattered showers and gusts up to 30 mph in the forecast. But the players will tee it up regardless — a playoff in difficult weather at Port Royal is, in many ways, exactly what the Bermuda Championship is supposed to be.

Smotherman: The Man on a Mission

Austin Smotherman enters Monday's playoff as perhaps the more surprising co-leader, but only if you hadn't been paying attention to his October. Earlier this month, Smotherman won his first PGA Tour event at the Bank of Utah Championship at Black Desert Resort in Ivins, Utah — on his 100th career start. He shot 26-under par and won by two shots over Doug Ghim in a performance that answered years of questions about when the breakthrough would come.

Three weeks later, he finds himself tied for the lead at Port Royal with a chance to become one of the rare players in PGA Tour history to win twice in the same calendar month. The moment is large, but Smotherman has shown this month that large moments do not bother him.

Power: The Course Knows Him

Seamus Power is no stranger to Bermuda's drama. The Irish player won this exact event — the Butterfield Bermuda Championship at Port Royal — in 2022, shooting a winning total of 16-under in similarly difficult conditions. He knows the course, understands the wind, and has proven he can close.

Power entered Monday's playoff with a 1-0 career record in PGA Tour sudden-death situations, having defeated J.T. Poston on the sixth extra hole to win the 2021 ISCO Championship. His composure and familiarity with both the playoff format and this specific golf course make him a formidable opponent for any rival on a Monday morning in Bermuda.

What's Next

One of these two players will leave Port Royal as the 2026 Bermuda Championship champion — and one will leave as the runner-up, still with a strong week's work and valuable FedExCup points in a fall season where every point matters for players chasing their tour card. For Power, a win would be his third career PGA Tour title and a second at Bermuda. For Smotherman, it would be a storybook October — two wins in a month, in only his second event since that emotional first victory at Black Desert Resort.

The playoff begins Monday at 8 a.m. ET on Golf Channel. Set the alarm.
