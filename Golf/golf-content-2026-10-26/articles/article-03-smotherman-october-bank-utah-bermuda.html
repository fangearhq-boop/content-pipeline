Austin Smotherman Is Having the Month of His Life — Bank of Utah Win, Now Bermuda Playoff

By Elena Voss | Golf Fanrecap | October 26, 2026

Three weeks ago, Austin Smotherman ended a 99-start drought with a wire-to-wire victory in Utah. On Monday, he plays a sudden-death playoff in Bermuda for a second PGA Tour title in the same calendar month.

If you told Austin Smotherman on September 30 that by October 26 he would have a PGA Tour win and be in a playoff for a second, he would not have believed you. Neither would most of the golf world. But October 2026 is writing its own script for Smotherman — and it has been the kind of chapter that golfers spend careers hoping to experience.

On October 4, Smotherman won his first PGA Tour title at the Bank of Utah Championship at Black Desert Resort in Ivins, Utah. It was his 100th career start on the circuit — the kind of round number that makes a breakthrough feel both fated and earned. He shot 26-under par, a tournament record, and won by two strokes over Doug Ghim. The win earned him $1.08 million, a two-year PGA Tour exemption, and enough of a momentum boost to carry him across the country to Bermuda.

From Utah to Bermuda

Port Royal Golf Course in Bermuda is as different from Black Desert Resort as golf gets — wind-swept, coastal, demanding patience and course management rather than the low-score opportunities a Utah resort course can offer. But Smotherman's game translated. He played three rounds at 16-under and shared the lead when Sunday's final round was wiped out by high winds, forcing the PGA Tour to shorten the Butterfield Bermuda Championship to 54 holes and set up Monday's sudden-death playoff.

Smotherman's opponent is Seamus Power — the 2022 Bermuda champion and a two-time Tour winner who knows this course and these conditions intimately. Power is formidable. But Smotherman has done nothing this month to suggest he is overmatched by anyone.

The Long Road to the First Win

Golf is a sport that rewards persistence on a timeline that humbles the impatient. Smotherman played 99 Tour events before his breakthrough — events where he built his game, learned from close calls and missed cuts, and maintained the belief that his level was good enough to win. The Bank of Utah win was not an accident or a product of a weak field holding up. He shot 26-under in tournament-record fashion. He navigated a double bogey on 13 in the final round and responded with a birdie on 16 to seal it.

That resilience — making the key shot after the big mistake — is something that does not develop in 10 starts or 50 starts. It comes from experience, and Smotherman now has it in spades.

What's Next

Monday's playoff at Port Royal begins at 8 a.m. ET on Golf Channel. If Smotherman wins, he joins a very short list of PGA Tour players to have won twice in the same calendar month — an extraordinary feat that would put an exclamation point on one of the more remarkable personal runs the Tour has seen in recent years.

Whatever happens Monday, Austin Smotherman's October 2026 is already a story worth telling. He broke through on start No. 100. He took that confidence across an ocean to Bermuda. Now he has one more shot.
