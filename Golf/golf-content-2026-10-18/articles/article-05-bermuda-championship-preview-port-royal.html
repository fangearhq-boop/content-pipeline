Butterfield Bermuda Championship Preview: Port Royal Awaits Oct. 22-25

Four days until tee time. Here is what to know about one of the PGA Tour's most distinctive venues.

By Jake Torres | October 18, 2026 | Golf Fanrecap

The Course

Port Royal Golf Course is a 7,175-yard par 71 set along the coast of Bermuda, fully exposed to the Atlantic Ocean. There is no shelter from the wind out there. The flags bend, the ball flight shifts, and the player who managed their game best — not the one who hit it the furthest — tends to finish highest.

Distance matters less at Port Royal than at almost any other stop on the PGA Tour. What wins here is iron accuracy and the ability to shape shots in both directions depending on what the wind is doing. A player who can hit a 6-iron to eight feet in a crosswind has an edge. A player who can only hit it straight has a problem.

The par 71 setup rewards patience. Birdies come on the reachable holes. The wind holes eat up scores. Managing the difficult holes at even par is sometimes enough to stay in contention.

The FedExCup Bubble

The Butterfield Bermuda Championship is part of the FedExCup Fall — a series of events that determine full PGA Tour membership for the following season. The top 60 players in the FedExCup Fall standings at the end of the series keep their cards and retain full event eligibility.

For players sitting between 55th and 70th in the standings right now, this week in Bermuda is not a casual start to an autumn week. It is a tournament they need. A top-10 finish can move a player from the cut line to safety. A missed cut can leave them staring at a conditional schedule.

That pressure changes how those players approach the week. They attack when they should sometimes lay back. They take on flags the course is telling them to miss. That desperation produces drama, and the Bermuda leaderboard tends to deliver it.

What to Expect from the Field

Bermuda is not a designated event — it does not carry the same field strength as the signature tournaments. What it does carry is players who want to be there, whether for FedExCup positioning or because Port Royal suits their game.

Watch for players with strong iron-play stats who have been consistent in fall events. Watch for anyone who has performed well at Port Royal before — the course rewards familiarity with its wind patterns and its specific demands on each hole.

Key Storylines

The FedExCup bubble race is the main narrative thread all week. That story plays out hole by hole and leaderboard by leaderboard, as players who need points try to get them and players with security try to take advantage of a manageable field.

The weather forecast for Bermuda in October is warm, with moderate humidity — but the wind is never predictable. A calm Thursday followed by a 25-mph Friday is entirely possible, and it changes who benefits and who suffers at Port Royal.

What's Next

The Butterfield Bermuda Championship begins October 22 and runs through October 25. Four rounds at Port Royal. Wind, precision, and FedExCup points on the line. The tour has had more glamorous stops on the fall schedule, but this one tends to matter more for more players than almost any other event of the season.
