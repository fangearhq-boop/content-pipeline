Ruoning Yin Wins Buick LPGA Shanghai for Seventh Career Title

A birdie on 16 broke the tie. A par-par finish sealed it. Back-to-back wins for the LPGA's most in-form player.

By Ryan Calloway | October 18, 2026 | Golf Fanrecap

The Shot That Decided It

Ruoning Yin and Hye-Jin Choi played alongside each other through most of the final round at Sheshan Golf Club, and through 15 holes they were tied. The Buick LPGA Shanghai was going to come down to one of them making a move.

Yin made it on the 16th. She made the birdie putt to go one ahead. Choi could not answer. They both parred 17 and 18, and that single stroke was the difference.

Yin closed with a 4-under 68 to finish at 11-under 277. Choi finished one back at 10-under. Amy Yang was third at 8-under.

The Numbers Say Everything

This is Yin's seventh LPGA Tour title, and it is her second win in consecutive starts. She won the week before this tournament and came back and won again.

Back-to-back wins in consecutive starts is not something that happens often on the LPGA. It requires the kind of game and mental consistency that most players never string together. Yin is doing it right now, in the middle of the tour's Asian swing, under conditions that test every part of a golfer's game.

She earned $480,000 from the $3.2 million purse. Seven titles. Two weeks. The numbers speak for themselves.

Choi's Near-Miss

Hye-Jin Choi entered the final round as co-leader. She played well enough to win on most Sundays. The problem was that Yin played better.

Choi held the lead for stretches of the week and forced the tournament into a head-to-head battle over the back nine. Going into the 16th tee they were tied. That she finished second is no failure — Yin simply outplayed her when the round was there to be won.

What This Means for the CME Globe

The CME Globe standings are the LPGA's season-long points race, with the finale coming at the end of the year. Two wins in two weeks pushes Yin toward the front of any serious CME Globe conversation.

The Asian swing is one of the final stretches of the LPGA calendar. Players are stacking points and separating themselves. Yin's form here — seven titles overall, two in a row — makes her the player everyone else on the leaderboard is chasing.

The $480,000 from Shanghai adds to what is already a strong season. The CME Globe finale carries the LPGA's largest purse. Yin, based on current form, has every reason to believe she can win it.

What's Next

The LPGA Tour continues its Asian swing before circling back toward the season-ending events and the CME Globe Championship. For Yin, the question is simple: can she keep this going?

Two weeks ago, the answer would have seemed ambitious. Right now, after back-to-back wins, it is the only question worth asking.
