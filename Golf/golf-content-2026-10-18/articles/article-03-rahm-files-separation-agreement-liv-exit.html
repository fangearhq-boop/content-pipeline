Rahm Files Separation Agreement to Exit LIV Golf — Hearing Set for Nov. 5

The terms are public. The hearing is scheduled. The path back to competitive golf is opening.

By Marcus Cole | October 18, 2026 | Golf Fanrecap

What Was Filed

Jon Rahm's legal team filed a separation agreement in New Jersey bankruptcy court, where LIV Golf's Chapter 11 proceedings are being handled. A hearing to confirm the agreement is scheduled for November 5.

The terms: Rahm releases his $7.5 million unsecured creditor claim against LIV Golf. In return, he regains full rights to his name, image, and likeness.

Rahm's lawyer told the bankruptcy judge that his client "has determined that those terms are unacceptable to him, and he will not be participating going forward in LIV 2.0." That is a definitive statement.

The Timeline

LIV Golf's deadline for players to commit to its reformed 2.0 structure is October 25 — one week away. Rahm's hearing is November 5. The two dates overlap in a way that tells a clear story: by the time LIV 2.0 finalizes its player commitments, Rahm is already in court to exit.

Rahm joined LIV in late 2023 in a deal reported to be worth $350 million. He never competed in LIV 2.0 after Saudi PIF exited the league and the restructuring began. He has been in a legal and organizational limbo for months while the bankruptcy proceedings played out.

The separation agreement, once approved by a judge on November 5, would formally end that limbo.

What Comes Next

A court-approved separation does not automatically mean Rahm walks onto a PGA Tour tee sheet in January. There are procedural questions around re-entry, world ranking restoration, and which events he would be eligible for and when.

But those are solvable problems. The bigger question — is Rahm free to return to competitive golf? — appears to be on the verge of a yes answer.

The PGA Tour has handled returning LIV players case by case. Sergio Garcia's contract was terminated by the bankruptcy court earlier this year. Rahm's situation differs in that he is negotiating a voluntary exit with agreed terms rather than a forced termination. The process should be cleaner.

What Rahm's Return Would Mean

Rahm, when last competing in full PGA Tour events, was a world No. 1 and a two-time major champion — the 2021 U.S. Open and the 2023 Masters. He was one of the three or four best players in the world before his move to LIV.

Time away changes rankings and sometimes form. But the talent that made Rahm a dominant player at Augusta and Torrey Pines does not disappear. His return would add to the major fields and the Ryder Cup conversation heading into 2027.

More than any individual storyline, Rahm back on the PGA Tour is another signal about where professional golf's center of gravity sits.

What's Next

November 5 is the date that matters. If the bankruptcy court approves the separation agreement, Rahm is legally free from LIV Golf and his return to competitive golf becomes a matter of logistics rather than law. The Oct. 25 LIV 2.0 commitment deadline will pass without his name on the list. The November 5 hearing is the next step.
