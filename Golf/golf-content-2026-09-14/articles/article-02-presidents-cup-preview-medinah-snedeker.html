10 Days to Medinah: Snedeker's USA Team Set for Presidents Cup — Koivun Headline Addition

By Jake Torres | September 14, 2026

With ten days until the first shot is struck at Medinah Country Club, Brandt Snedeker's United States team is complete. Scottie Scheffler leads a roster that includes one of the most surprising captain's picks in Presidents Cup history.

There are ten days left. The Presidents Cup begins September 24 at Medinah Country Club in Illinois, and the United States team is set. Captain Brandt Snedeker has made his picks, the International team under Geoff Ogilvy is assembled, and what was once an event circled on the calendar is now an event circled on the countdown clock.

For Snedeker, this is the moment all the preparation leads to. He has the roster he wants. He has six automatic qualifiers and six captain's picks, and the pick that has dominated the conversation since the list was announced is Jackson Koivun — 21 years old, turned professional from Auburn months ago, and now a member of the 2026 United States Presidents Cup team.

That takes some processing. Koivun graduated from amateur golf and made a Presidents Cup team in the same calendar year. The last time something similar happened at this level of team competition, it generated the kind of story that gets retold for decades.

The Full Roster

Six players earned their spots automatically: Scottie Scheffler, Cameron Young, Wyndham Clark, Russell Henley, Sam Burns, and Collin Morikawa. This is a group that requires no explanation. Scheffler remains the best player in men's golf. Morikawa brings a game built for match play. Young and Clark offer the kind of aggressive, momentum-shifting play that can swing a session.

Snedeker's six captain's picks: Chris Gotterup, Xander Schauffele, Justin Thomas, Patrick Cantlay, Jacob Bridgeman, and Koivun.

Schauffele and Cantlay each bring a 10-4-0 Presidents Cup record into this week. Those numbers represent years of playing in exactly this format, on exactly this stage, and producing results. Thomas is 10-3-2 — and has arguably been the most clutch Presidents Cup player in the event's modern era when singles come around. Bridgeman and Gotterup round out a deep team that has very few weaknesses on paper.

Then there is Koivun. Snedeker made a statement with that pick. He didn't have to take the 21-year-old. He could have gone with experience. Instead, he chose someone whose game he clearly trusts and whose competitive fire he has evidently seen up close. If Koivun performs, the pick looks visionary. That is the nature of a captain's selection.

Ogilvy's Internationals

Geoff Ogilvy, the 2006 U.S. Open champion, captains the International team — a collection of players from any nation outside the United States and Europe. That geographic restriction has historically made building a competitive International squad the harder task, and the results reflect it: the United States has won 13 of the 15 Presidents Cup editions contested before this one.

Ogilvy is one of the most respected figures in professional golf outside North America. He understands what the International team needs — and what it has historically lacked — better than almost anyone. Whether he can put together a squad capable of winning at Medinah against this U.S. roster is the central competitive question of the week.

The Presidents Cup has never been as consistently competitive as the Ryder Cup, and that gap is acknowledged openly within the sport. But Ogilvy's team will compete. They always do. The question is whether they can sustain competition across four days and 30 matches at a course that will draw a partisan American crowd.

The Venue

Medinah Country Club — Course No. 3, the one that matters — has hosted three Ryder Cups: 1991, 1999, and the 2012 edition that produced one of the most stunning Sunday comebacks in golf history. Europe trailed the United States 10-6 entering singles that year and won the Cup.

The Presidents Cup arrives at a course with a résumé unlike almost any other venue in American golf. The property is immaculate, the routing is demanding, and the atmosphere during a team event at Medinah can be exactly what the partisan crowds in the Chicago area are capable of generating.

For the International team, Medinah in September means playing away from home in the truest sense. USA wins Presidents Cups more often than not, and they win them at home by even wider margins. Ogilvy's team will need to be exceptional. They will need to play their best golf at a moment when their opponents will be playing in front of an enormous home crowd.

Ten days. September 24. The 2026 Presidents Cup at Medinah Country Club.

What's Next

The opening ceremony takes place the week of September 21, with competition beginning September 24. Foursomes and fourball sessions fill the first three days, with singles on Sunday September 27. Snedeker will spend the coming days finalizing his pairing strategies, determining which of his 12 players to sit in each session, and — critically — deciding where to put Jackson Koivun in his debut. The 2026 Presidents Cup begins in ten days.
