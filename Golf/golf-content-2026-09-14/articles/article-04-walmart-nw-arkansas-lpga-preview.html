Walmart NW Arkansas Championship Preview: Korda, Post-Solheim Returns, and LPGA's 20th Stop at Pinnacle

By Ryan Calloway | September 14, 2026

Eleven days after the 2026 Solheim Cup concluded in the Netherlands, the LPGA Tour returns to one of its most beloved regular season stops. The 20th Walmart NW Arkansas Championship brings a $3 million purse and a world No. 1 back from European team duty to Pinnacle Country Club in Rogers, Arkansas.

The Solheim Cup trophy is in European hands as of Sunday afternoon in the Netherlands. The next chapter of women's golf begins eleven days later in the American heartland — at Pinnacle Country Club in Rogers, Arkansas, where the Walmart NW Arkansas Championship marks its 20th anniversary at this venue.

Nelly Korda — world No. 1, two-time major winner in 2026 with the Chevron Championship and U.S. Women's Open — will be among the players making that transition. From team golf on Dutch soil to individual stroke play in the Ozarks, all within two weeks. That is the LPGA Tour schedule, and the players who navigate it best are usually the ones who win.

The Tournament

The Walmart NW Arkansas Championship, presented by P&G, runs September 25-27 at Pinnacle Country Club. The purse is $3 million. The field is 144 players. This is the 20th anniversary of the event at this venue — two decades of women's professional golf coming to a market that has consistently shown up and supported the tour.

Northwest Arkansas is not a major metropolitan market, but it consistently produces one of the best atmospheres on the LPGA Tour. The crowd that turns out at Pinnacle Country Club genuinely cares about women's golf, and the players have noticed. The event has earned its reputation as one of the tour's most player-friendly stops, and the 20th anniversary brings added weight to a tournament that has already established its identity.

Pinnacle Country Club's routing suits a variety of game types, and the 54-hole format compresses the competition in a way that rewards consistency over three days. There is no cut — the full field plays all three rounds — which means the leaderboard tends to stay busy throughout the week.

Korda's Return

For Nelly Korda, the Walmart NW Arkansas Championship represents the return to individual competition after the emotional intensity of Solheim Cup week. She played for the United States team at Bernardus Golf in the Netherlands, on a squad that entered Sunday singles tied 8-8 and ultimately fell 15-13.

Korda's 2026 already stands as one of the great individual seasons in women's golf. Two majors — the Chevron Championship and the U.S. Women's Open — along with the world No. 1 ranking she has held throughout the year. The Solheim Cup loss is a team result, not a reflection of where her individual game stands going into the final months of the season.

The transition from team golf back to stroke play is one of the more underexplored psychological challenges in professional sports. Team events run on a different energy — the crowd investment is different, the emotional stakes are different, and the mental preparation required is different from standing over a tournament putt alone. Some players emerge from Solheim Cups with their competitive intensity at an elevated level that carries directly into their next event. Others need a week or two to recalibrate.

Korda, based on how she has played through the compressed schedule of her 2026 season, looks like someone who maintains competitive output regardless of what came before. But the transition is real, and NW Arkansas will tell us something about where she is headed into the fall.

The Post-Solheim Factor

Korda is not the only player making this transition. Every LPGA Tour player who competed in the Netherlands faces the same adjustment. The European players who carried the Solheim Cup to victory — several of whom went 5-0 across all sessions — will also be in the NW Arkansas field if their schedule permits, and they arrive carrying both the emotional high of winning and the physical fatigue of one of the most demanding weeks in team golf.

There is no consensus on which way the Solheim Cup effect cuts. Players who went deep in match play competition sometimes emerge with sharpened competitive instincts. Others arrive at the first individual event of the post-Solheim calendar slightly off their rhythm. The players who manage that transition — whoever they are — may carry a built-in advantage at Pinnacle Country Club next week.

What's Next

The Walmart NW Arkansas Championship, presented by P&G, tees off September 25 at Pinnacle Country Club in Rogers, Arkansas. Tickets are on sale. The LPGA season continues — and with it, the race to the top of the final 2026 standings. After a Solheim Cup that produced European glory in the Netherlands, the individual game resumes in the American Midwest.
