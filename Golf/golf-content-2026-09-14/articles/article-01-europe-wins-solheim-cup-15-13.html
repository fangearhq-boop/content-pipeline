Europe Reclaims the Solheim Cup 15-13 — Lottie Woad Clinches in Netherlands, Carlota Ciganda Seals It

By Ryan Calloway | September 14, 2026

After entering Sunday singles tied at 8-8, Europe produced a decisive performance at Bernardus Golf to take the 2026 Solheim Cup 15-13. A 21-year-old Englishwoman delivered the winning point. An eight-time Solheim veteran finished the job.

It began as anyone could have predicted: too close to call, everything on the line.

Eight matches each. Eight points each. Twelve singles matches on a Sunday afternoon at Bernardus Golf in the Netherlands. The 2026 Solheim Cup had arrived at its defining moment in perfect balance. Europe needed 6.5 from 12 to win. So did the United States. Every point counted.

What followed was an afternoon that belonged to Europe — and two women in particular. Lottie Woad, 21 years old, world No. 5, stepped up in Match 8 against American Auston Kim and won 2&1 to give Europe their 14th point — and with it, the Solheim Cup trophy. The Bernardus Golf crowd, having turned out in extraordinary numbers to cheer on the team in front of them, responded with the kind of noise that carries across a golf course and into the history books.

Carlota Ciganda, playing in her eighth consecutive Solheim Cup, finished the job. Her 4&2 win over Jennifer Kupcho in Match 11 turned the party into an official coronation. Final score: Europe 15, USA 13.

Woad's Winning Moment

Ask any Solheim Cup veteran what Sunday singles require, and the answer is always the same: you need players who can handle the moment. Lottie Woad — appearing in only her second Solheim Cup — proved she is exactly that kind of player.

Playing Match 8 against Auston Kim, Woad controlled the early holes and never looked back. Her 2&1 victory gave Europe their crucial 14.5 points and secured the trophy before several matches behind her were even completed. At 21 years old, ranked world No. 5, she delivered when it mattered most.

The significance of the moment — a young English player clinching on Dutch soil in front of a European home crowd — was not lost on anyone watching. This is the kind of performance that defines a career.

Ciganda's Crowning Achievement

Carlota Ciganda has been the heartbeat of European Solheim Cup teams for more than a decade. Eight consecutive appearances. A record that speaks to consistency, longevity, and competitive excellence at the highest level of team golf. Her 4&2 defeat of Jennifer Kupcho completed the scoreboard — and completed a personal legacy that already stands as one of the greatest in Solheim Cup history.

For Ciganda, the 2026 Solheim Cup represents another chapter in a story that gets better every time it's told. She will play future editions, and she will continue to compete, but there is something particular about winning on a Sunday afternoon with a crowd at your back and a trophy at stake that exists outside of career statistics.

What's Next

Team USA returns home without the Solheim Cup for the first time since 2024. Captain Angela Stanford will face questions in the coming days about the singles lineup, the pairings strategy across the weekend, and whether the home-soil advantage at Bernardus Golf proved to be the decisive factor.

The next Solheim Cup is in 2028. The United States will have two years to rebuild and reload, with Nelly Korda — who will be in her prime through that period — at the center of every planning conversation. Europe, meanwhile, celebrates. They came home to the Netherlands and they left as Solheim Cup champions.
