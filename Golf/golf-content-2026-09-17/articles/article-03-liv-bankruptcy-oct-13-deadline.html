LIV Golf Bankruptcy: Oct. 13 Deadline, Rahm Owed $7.5M, and the LIV 2.0 Plan

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | September 17, 2026

The clock is running on LIV Golf's Chapter 11 bankruptcy. The next critical date is October 13 — 26 days away — when the restructuring plan requires further legal clarity in U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of New Jersey. What happens at that hearing will shape the future of professional golf's most disruptive experiment.

The Creditor List

The bankruptcy filing, made September 8, reveals what LIV Golf owes its players. Jon Rahm — the two-time major champion who signed one of the largest contracts in golf history when he left the PGA Tour in late 2023 — is the largest unsecured player creditor at $7.5 million. Bryson DeChambeau is owed approximately $5.8 million. Dustin Johnson, one of LIV's founding members, holds a $5.5 million unsecured claim.

These figures represent unsecured obligations only — the amounts players are owed that aren't backed by specific collateral. The total player obligations to LIV Golf run significantly higher. Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, which backed LIV from its inception and invested an estimated $6 billion total, filed a $49.6 million debtor-in-possession financing arrangement to keep the circuit operational through the restructuring. That financing is pending court approval.

The LIV 2.0 Plan

London-based private equity firm BC Partners is leading the restructuring effort. Their proposed LIV 2.0 structure envisions a return to competition in 2027 with four events: Saudi Arabia, Australia, South Africa, and Hong Kong. The most significant structural change would be a player-majority ownership model — a fundamental departure from the sovereign-fund-backed, top-down structure that defined LIV Golf's original run.

CEO Scott O'Neil is shepherding the process. Whether the player-ownership model can attract enough talent to make LIV 2.0 viable remains the central question. Without Rahm, DeChambeau, and Johnson — the circuit's three most recognizable current names — the rebuilt circuit's competitive credibility would be in doubt.

Rahm's Position

Rahm is the biggest wildcard. In recent days, he has described himself as "noncommittal" on LIV 2.0 and specifically mentioned the DP World Tour as an "opportunity." That language signals genuine openness to leaving the LIV structure entirely, though he has also indicated that full clarity won't come until "the current season is done."

His $7.5 million unsecured claim in the bankruptcy proceeding adds a legal dimension to his decision. Whether that obligation transfers to LIV 2.0 — and on what terms — is one of the questions October 13 may begin to resolve.

What's Next

The October 13 hearing is the next formal milestone in the bankruptcy process. Between now and then, BC Partners must continue negotiating with creditors, players, and PIF on the structure of LIV 2.0.
