84 Years Between Rounds — What Asheville's Return to PGA Tour Golf Means

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | September 17, 2026

The last time professional golf held a PGA Tour event in Asheville, North Carolina, it was 1942. The United States had entered World War II months earlier. The Tour would largely suspend its schedule by the following year. And Asheville — a mountain city in the western corner of North Carolina — quietly stepped back from the professional golf calendar for what would become eight decades.

Today, that changes.

The Biltmore Estate and Asheville's Identity

The Biltmore Championship takes its name from the Biltmore Estate — the 8,000-acre property and 178,000-square-foot chateau built by George Vanderbilt beginning in 1889 and completed in 1895. It remains the largest privately owned home in the United States. Its arrival in Asheville transformed a regional mountain town into a destination. Visitors came for the mansion, the gardens, and the surrounding landscape. They still do.

For more than a century, the Biltmore Estate has defined how the world thinks about Asheville. Naming a PGA Tour event after it is not a marketing decision — it's a recognition of what the estate means to the region's identity and to American cultural history.

The Host Venue

The Cliffs at Walnut Cove is the host course — a private club set against the Blue Ridge Mountains on the outskirts of Asheville. The course offers the kind of terrain that rewards precision over length, with elevation changes and tree-lined corridors that make club selection and shot shaping central to scoring well. It is the kind of course that suits a specific type of player, which is part of why early favorites like Jackson Koivun and Jacob Bridgeman — who possess both distance and control — are so highly regarded this week.

After the Flood

In September 2024, Hurricane Helene struck western North Carolina with devastating force. The flooding in and around Asheville was catastrophic — claiming lives, destroying infrastructure, and testing the city's community in ways it is still working through nearly two years later. The Biltmore Championship arriving in 2026 carries particular weight in that context. It is not just a return to professional golf. It is a statement about where Asheville is going.

Cities that host PGA Tour events benefit economically and reputationally — in tourism, in visibility, in the ongoing story they tell about themselves. For a city that has spent the last two years rebuilding, the Biltmore Championship represents forward momentum that goes beyond sport.

What's Next

Round 1 is live today. If this inaugural event succeeds — in attendance, in quality of field, in national television visibility — Asheville could become a recurring stop on the FedExCup Fall schedule.
