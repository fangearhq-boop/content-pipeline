Round 1 Is Live at the Inaugural Biltmore Championship — History Returns to Asheville

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | September 17, 2026

The wait is over. After 84 years, professional golf has returned to Asheville, North Carolina.

The inaugural Biltmore Championship is live at The Cliffs at Walnut Cove, opening the FedExCup Fall with a $5 million purse and a field that includes several of the most compelling players in the game. Round 1 runs Thursday through Sunday (September 17–20), with Golf Channel carrying television coverage from 3–6 p.m. ET today. PGA Tour Live on ESPN+ features groups starting at 7 a.m.

The Field and the Favorite

Jackson Koivun enters as the betting favorite at +1200. The 21-year-old out of Auburn turned pro in 2026, won the 3M Open in July, and was subsequently named to Brandt Snedeker's USA Presidents Cup team — one of the most remarkable rookie trajectories in recent Tour memory. This week in Asheville is his next chapter, and the pressure of the favorite's tag is something he'll have to manage alongside the looming Presidents Cup at Medinah next weekend.

Jacob Bridgeman sits at +1600. Also a Presidents Cup team member, Bridgeman brings consistent ball-striking and a style built for tight mountain courses like Walnut Cove. Ben James (+2200), Doug Ghim (+2500), and Davis Thompson (+2500) round out the early field of names worth tracking.

Justin Thomas withdrew Monday, opting to rest ahead of Medinah rather than play this week. His absence opens the leaderboard slightly, though it doesn't diminish what the field brings.

What's at Stake

The Biltmore Championship is the first event of the FedExCup Fall — the portion of the season that determines 2027 Tour card security. Players who didn't earn full status through the summer playoffs are chasing points here. For those who did, like Koivun and Bridgeman, it's an opportunity to build early momentum and arrive at Medinah with their game sharp.

The winner receives $900,000 from the $5 million purse and earns 500 FedExCup Fall points. For fringe-status players, the stakes are even higher — this is the kind of week where careers get extended or reconsidered.

The History Behind the Tournament

The last PGA Tour event held in Asheville was in 1942. Eight decades of American history passed between that event and today's first tee shot. The city grew into a regional destination, survived devastating flooding from Hurricane Helene in 2024, and has now emerged as the site of a landmark return for professional golf.

The Biltmore Championship takes its name from the Biltmore Estate, George Vanderbilt's 1895 mansion and the largest privately owned home in the United States. The name alone carries weight — it connects this tournament to a long arc of Asheville's history that few sporting events can claim.

What's Next

Round 2 runs Friday, with the cut expected after 36 holes. Television coverage continues on Golf Channel and ESPN+. For the Presidents Cup contingent in the field — Koivun, Bridgeman, and others — the tournament closes Sunday before the Medinah week begins in earnest.
