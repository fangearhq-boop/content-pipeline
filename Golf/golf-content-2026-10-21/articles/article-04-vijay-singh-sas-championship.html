Vijay Singh, 63, Wins SAS Championship on the Champions Tour

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | October 21, 2026

Vijay Singh won the SAS Championship last week at Prestonwood Country Club in Cary, North Carolina. The win itself was impressive. The context made it extraordinary: Singh was 63 years old and beat Ernie Els by two shots to claim the title.

Singh shot 9-under 207 across 54 holes. Els finished at 7-under. Alex Cejka and Tag Ridings tied for third at 6-under. The result moved Singh to No. 6 in the Schwab Cup standings — a significant rise in position ahead of the Champions Tour playoffs, which begin this Thursday in St. Louis.

Singh at 63

Vijay Singh's career trajectory is one of the more unusual in professional golf. He won three major championships — the 1998 and 2004 Masters and the 2004 PGA Championship — during a prime that included a stretch as the No. 1 ranked player in the world. He moved to the Champions Tour and continued competing at a level that regularly puts him in contention.

Winning at 63, on a tour that already tilts toward senior competition, still requires something most players can't sustain: consistent physical capability, competitive focus, and the ability to perform under final-round pressure when it matters. Singh had all three at Prestonwood.

The two-shot margin over Ernie Els — himself one of the most decorated players in Champions Tour history — suggests this wasn't a fluky result or a weather-shortened week. Singh played well over 54 competitive holes and won cleanly.

Schwab Cup Implications

The win came at a useful time. The Champions Tour playoffs begin Thursday at the Stifel Charity Classic in St. Louis, and Singh is now positioned at No. 6 on the Schwab Cup standings — inside the zone that historically produces meaningful results through the postseason.

Stewart Cink leads the standings, with Zach Johnson second and Steve Alker third. Singh's jump to No. 6 puts him close enough to the top that a strong three-tournament playoff run could change the final picture significantly.

The Big Fijian, Still Going

Vijay Singh earned a nickname — the Big Fijian — during a career that seemed to defy conventional expectations from the start. He came to prominence later than most, won major championships when others thought his best years might be passing, and has continued competing into an age when most professional athletes have been retired for decades.

The SAS Championship in 2026 adds another chapter. At 63, in a sport that demands precision and athletic consistency, Vijay Singh still wins.
