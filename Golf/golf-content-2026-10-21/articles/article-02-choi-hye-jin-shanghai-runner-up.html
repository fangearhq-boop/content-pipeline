Five Times Second: Choi Hye-jin's 124-Start Wait for an LPGA Title Continues

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | October 21, 2026

The number is five now. Five times Choi Hye-jin has been the runner-up on the LPGA Tour. Five times she has played well enough to win a tournament, and each time someone else has held the trophy. The most recent was Saturday in Shanghai, where Ruoning Yin beat her by a single stroke to defend the Buick LPGA title.

Choi finished at 10-under 278 in her 124th LPGA Tour start. Still without a win.

The Weight of the Pattern

In sport, one runner-up is a good week. Two runner-ups is a trend worth tracking. Five is a story that follows a player everywhere. Choi Hye-jin is exceptionally good at golf. Her consistency — in terms of making cuts, staying in contention, and finishing near the top — is a fact the stats bear out. She doesn't fade. She competes.

What she hasn't done is win, and in a sport where victories are the currency that defines careers, that distinction matters. Five runner-up finishes without a win in 124 starts is not a record of failure — it's a record of near-success that makes the absence of the final step more conspicuous, not less.

Shanghai was the latest example of how close that step has been. One shot. Eighteen holes of competition, and a single birdie more for Ruoning Yin was the entire difference.

What She Has Done

It's worth noting what a runner-up total of five across 124 LPGA starts actually represents. Choi has reached the top of competitive leaderboards repeatedly on one of the best professional tours in women's golf. She has beaten fields that include every major name on the Tour and forced a final-round performance from a defending champion to be denied.

The win will almost certainly come. Players with Choi's competitive level of consistency don't go their entire careers without breaking through. But in golf, the hardest thing to do is decide when, and no one gets to choose the timing.

The Long Game

Choi Hye-jin is still young enough to win multiple titles. The CME Globe season still has events remaining. The 2026 LPGA schedule continues, and so does she.

When the win finally comes, the number five will matter — not as a symbol of failure, but as context for what it took and how long the wait was. Sports have a way of giving those moments weight. Choi's will have plenty of it.
