Stifel Charity Classic: Champions Tour Playoffs Begin Thursday in St. Louis

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | October 21, 2026

The PGA Tour Champions postseason begins Thursday at Norwood Hills Country Club in St. Louis. The Stifel Charity Classic is the first of three playoff events that will ultimately determine the Schwab Cup champion, and with Stewart Cink holding a lead over Zach Johnson and Steve Alker heading in, the race carries genuine stakes across the 72-player field.

The event runs October 23-25. The winner takes home the top prize from a $2.3 million purse. The top 54 players in the field advance to the second playoff stage.

The Schwab Cup Race

Heading into St. Louis, three players sit at the top of the Schwab Cup standings: Stewart Cink is No. 1, Zach Johnson is second, and Steve Alker is third. The gap between them is meaningful but not insurmountable — which is exactly what makes a first playoff event worth watching.

Cink has been the most consistent player on Tour over the course of the 2026 season. His game suits the kind of measured, fairway-management approach that Champions Tour courses typically reward. He arrives in St. Louis with the advantage of position and the pressure of defending it.

Johnson and Alker both have the talent to narrow that gap over three events. Johnson, the former major champion, brings experience in high-stakes competition. Alker has been one of the more dangerous final-round closers on the Champions Tour in recent seasons. Either can make a move.

Norwood Hills and the Field

Norwood Hills Country Club is a classic parkland course — tree-lined fairways, fast greens, and the kind of layout where accuracy off the tee creates meaningful separation. The first playoff event typically sees a full and motivated field, with 72 players all aware that what happens this week either opens the door to the championship or begins to close it.

Vijay Singh, who won the SAS Championship last week and moved to No. 6 in the standings, arrives with momentum. The top end of the standings will be the focus, but any player ranked high enough to advance past the Stifel event is still alive for the Tour Championship.

Three Events, One Trophy

The Schwab Cup playoff structure creates cumulative pressure that builds across the three events. A strong Stifel result sets the stage; the middle event narrows the field; the Tour Championship finishes it. Cink has the best position, but a three-event playoff is long enough for the standings to change substantially.

The first tee shot goes Thursday morning. By Sunday in St. Louis, the Schwab Cup picture will look different. The question is how different — and who made it move.
