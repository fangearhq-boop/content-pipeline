Shiho Kuwaki Wins the AIG Women's Open at Royal Lytham — Japan's New Major Champion Claims Her First Title in a Playoff

By Marcus Cole | August 13, 2026 | Golf Fanrecap

Shiho Kuwaki is the 2026 AIG Women's Open champion. The Japanese golfer won the 50th edition of the tournament at Royal Lytham & St. Annes in a two-hole playoff over Germany's Esther Henseleit, claiming her first LPGA Tour victory and her first major championship in only her seventh major start. She earned $1.5 million from the tournament's $10 million total purse.

The victory is a landmark moment for Japanese women's golf — and the manner in which it arrived, through a dramatic playoff that only materialized because of one of the most clutch final-hole putts of the year, makes it a result that will be discussed in the sport for a long time.

The Shot That Made the Playoff

Kuwaki entered the 72nd hole with what looked like enough of a lead to close out the championship. What Esther Henseleit did on that hole changed everything. The German golfer, facing a deficit that would have ended her chances of a first major, holed what was described by witnesses as a "monster birdie putt" — a long, pressure-laden make that brought her level with Kuwaki and forced extra holes at one of golf's most storied links venues.

The roar from the Royal Lytham gallery was the sound of a major being extended against all expectation. Henseleit had given herself a chance she should not have had. Kuwaki had to gather herself and go again.

The Playoff

Over two playoff holes, Kuwaki won. The details of those holes — which parts of the course, which shots decided it — reflect what Royal Lytham requires even in a playoff: precision, nerve, and the ability to execute when there is nothing left to protect against except your own composure. Kuwaki had enough of all three. On the second playoff hole, the result was settled, and the trophy went to Japan.

For Henseleit, the near-miss is a difficult thing to contextualize in the immediate aftermath. She did everything right in the final holes of regulation, holed an extraordinary putt to force a playoff, and still came away without the championship. Those are the cruelest outcomes in major championship golf — and they are also the ones that define careers, one way or the other, depending on what comes next.

The Significance for Japanese Golf

Japan has produced major champions in women's golf before, but Kuwaki's victory adds to a growing sense that Japanese women's golf is in the middle of a generational wave. She won in only her seventh major start — a remarkably efficient path to a major championship title — and she won at the highest-profile venue on the LPGA's international schedule, under the most intense playoff pressure the sport produces.

For a sport that tracks global development as carefully as any, the AIG Women's Open champion being a Japanese player in 2026, on the 50th anniversary of the tournament, is both historically meaningful and commercially important for golf's growth in Asian markets. Shiho Kuwaki is the 2026 AIG Women's Open champion. Japanese women's golf has a new major champion.
