Scottie Scheffler Is No. 1 Again — And for the Fourth Time in Five Years, He Has to Prove It in October

By Ryan Calloway | August 13, 2026 | Golf Fanrecap

Scottie Scheffler has been here before. He entered the 2026 FedEx Cup Playoffs as the No. 1 seed for the fourth time in five years, carrying a 4,123-point lead over No. 2 Matt Fitzpatrick — the largest points advantage he has arrived at the postseason with. He is the best player in the world, by every metric the tour tracks, and Thursday at TPC Southwind he began the process of proving it for the eleventh time in eleven years of the game asking that question.

That the question keeps being asked — that the playoffs remain a genuine competition despite Scheffler's dominance — is one of the more interesting things about how the FedEx Cup format creates drama that pure season-long rankings cannot.

What Scheffler Did to Get Here

The 2026 regular season produced one of the most quietly astonishing Scheffler performance lines yet. He won once. He finished runner-up five times. He added five more top-10 results. Across the entire season, he missed exactly one cut. The consistency is almost mechanical — a relentless production of high finishes that accumulates into an insurmountable points position before the playoffs begin.

His 794-point lead over Fitzpatrick is not just a number. It represents the distance between what Scheffler produced this season and what a very good year from one of the tour's other elite performers produced. Fitzpatrick is a major champion, a Ryder Cup stalwart, and one of the most consistent European players of his generation. And Scheffler left him nearly 800 points behind after nine months of competition.

The Interesting Names Below Him

The full top 12 entering the playoffs reads as follows: Scheffler, Fitzpatrick, Cameron Young, Wyndham Clark, Chris Gotterup, Collin Morikawa, Si Woo Kim, Sam Burns, Tommy Fleetwood, Ludvig Aberg, Xander Schauffele, and Rory McIlroy.

McIlroy at No. 12 is the subplot that writes itself. He won the FedEx Cup in 2022, from East Lake, in a format where starting position is determined by points rank. He knows what it takes to catch a leader at the Tour Championship. But arriving at East Lake four strokes behind the No. 1 seed — because of how the staggered start works — means everything has to go right from the moment the first ball is struck in Memphis. Three weekends. A lot of golf left to play.

The Pattern That Cannot Be Ignored

Four times in five years as the No. 1 seed is not a coincidence or a statistical artifact. It is the story of one player recalibrating the standard of what excellence in professional golf looks like in this era. Scheffler has redefined consistency at the top of the world rankings in a way that draws comparisons to the game's most dominant periods.

But the FedEx Cup has its own logic. Every year that Scheffler arrives as No. 1, the tour asks whether the playoff format will produce the champion his season deserves — or whether the compressed, high-variance format of three individual events will hand the $25 million to someone who peaked at the right three weeks. That is the tension that makes the playoffs interesting even when the regular-season result feels foregone. Round 1 at TPC Southwind is where it begins.
