FedEx Cup Playoffs Begin: Round 1 of the St. Jude Championship Is Underway at TPC Southwind

By Ryan Calloway | August 13, 2026 | Golf Fanrecap

The FedEx Cup Playoffs are underway. Round 1 of the FedEx St. Jude Championship began Thursday at TPC Southwind in Memphis, Tennessee, launching the three-week postseason that will determine which player walks away with the $25 million FedEx Cup bonus and the title of the season's most complete performer.

Seventy players qualified for the postseason based on their season-long FedExCup points accumulation. The field arriving in Memphis represents the best the PGA Tour has produced over the last nine months. Every one of them earned their place — there are no sponsor exemptions in the playoffs.

How the Playoffs Work

The 2026 FedEx Cup Playoffs run across three events on three consecutive weekends. From the 70-player field in Memphis, the top 50 in points will advance to the BMW Championship on August 20-23 at Bellerive Country Club in St. Louis. From the BMW, the top 30 advance to the season finale: the Tour Championship, August 27-30 at East Lake Golf Club in Atlanta.

At East Lake, the scoring format changes. Players begin each round with a built-in score advantage that corresponds to their FedExCup standing entering the tournament — the No. 1 seed starts at 10-under par, the No. 2 seed at 8-under, down to the No. 30 seed starting at even par. The Tour Championship is won on cumulative score over four rounds, which means arriving at East Lake with as strong a seed as possible matters enormously. Every shot this week in Memphis is a shot that shapes what happens in Atlanta.

The St. Jude Championship carries a $20 million purse, with $3.6 million going to the winner.

The Field to Watch

Scottie Scheffler arrives in Memphis as the undisputed No. 1 seed, carrying a 4,123-point lead over No. 2 Matt Fitzpatrick. He has been No. 1 entering the playoffs for the fourth time in five years. His 2026 regular season — one win, five runner-up finishes, five other top-10s, and just one missed cut — is the definition of sustained excellence.

Rory McIlroy enters the postseason at No. 12, which means he begins the Tour Championship in Atlanta four strokes behind Scheffler before the first ball is struck. To overcome that deficit, McIlroy needs a strong performance in Memphis that advances his points position, and then either the same at the BMW or a better seed going into East Lake. He has won the FedEx Cup before — 2022 at East Lake, from behind — but the math requires things to go his way.

The rest of the top 12: Cameron Young (3), Wyndham Clark (4), Chris Gotterup (5), Collin Morikawa (6), Si Woo Kim (7), Sam Burns (8), Tommy Fleetwood (9), Ludvig Aberg (10), and Xander Schauffele (11) — a field that represents the full depth of international and American tour golf at its best.

Why Memphis Matters

TPC Southwind is a demanding test. The course rewards precision, punishes wayward tee shots, and historically produces low-scoring rounds when conditions allow but can bite at any moment when the Memphis heat and wind arrive. Players who come out of the first round with a low number can build momentum into the weekend. Players who struggle in Round 1 face a compressed window to recover before the cut comes into play.

Three weeks. Three events. One champion. It starts today.
