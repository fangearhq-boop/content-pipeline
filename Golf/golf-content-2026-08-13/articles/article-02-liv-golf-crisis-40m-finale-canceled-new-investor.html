LIV Golf Cancels $40M Season Finale, Loses Saudi Funding — But CEO Announces New Investor in "LIV 2.0" Pitch

By Jake Torres | August 13, 2026 | Golf Fanrecap

LIV Golf's 2026 season has entered its most turbulent stretch. The league has canceled its $40 million season-ending team championship — the second event scrapped from this year's schedule — while simultaneously losing its primary financial backer and announcing that a new investor has agreed to step in and reshape what LIV Golf becomes next.

The juxtaposition is almost impossible to write around: a league canceling flagship events and losing Saudi funding in the same weeks it is announcing a restructured future. That is the current state of professional golf's most disruptive experiment.

The Cancellation

The Michigan team championship — scheduled for August 27-30 at The Cardinal at Saint John's Resort in suburban Detroit — is off the calendar. The event was set to serve as LIV Golf's season finale, with a $40 million purse and the weight of the league's team competition format behind it. It is the second cancellation of the 2026 schedule; a $30 million New Orleans event was also scrapped in June.

The 2026 LIV season will now conclude at The Club at Chatham Hills in Westfield, Indiana — the event that was originally positioned before the finale. For a league that launched with promises of premier venues and unprecedented purses, ending a season in Indiana rather than at a purpose-built finale venue is a statement about the current resource constraints.

The Saudi Exit

Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund announced in April 2026 that it would end its financial backing of LIV Golf at the conclusion of this season. PIF was the engine that made the league possible from its 2022 launch — guaranteeing player contracts worth hundreds of millions of dollars, funding purses that made legacy tours look underpaying by comparison, absorbing operating losses as the league built its identity. Without PIF, the financial architecture that defined LIV Golf in its first four years disappears.

What that means for players on long-term LIV contracts — including Bryson DeChambeau, Jon Rahm, Brooks Koepka, and others who signed multi-year deals — depends entirely on how the ownership transition resolves. The contracts exist. Whether they are honored under new ownership, renegotiated, or contested legally is a question the September transaction date will begin to answer.

The New Investor and "LIV 2.0"

LIV Golf CEO Scott O'Neil made the restructuring announcement at Trump National Golf Club in Bedminster, New Jersey. He confirmed that a new lead investor has signed an agreement approved by the LIV Board, with more than a dozen additional parties expressing interest as minority investors. The identity of the lead investor was not disclosed.

O'Neil described the restructured league — which he has called "LIV 2.0" — as a scaled-down version of the original vision: five team majors and five U.S.-based team signature events. The most striking element of the proposal is that LIV Golf players would become majority equity holders in the league itself, which O'Neil has described as unprecedented for any major global sports league. The transaction is expected to finalize in September 2026.

The pitch is compelling on paper. A player-owned league, restructured around a focused event schedule, with new private capital instead of sovereign wealth, represents a meaningfully different entity than the one PIF built. Whether the unnamed investor brings the credibility and capital to make the vision real is the question that will define whether LIV Golf has a meaningful future beyond 2026 — or whether the league's best story ends with a canceled Michigan finale.
