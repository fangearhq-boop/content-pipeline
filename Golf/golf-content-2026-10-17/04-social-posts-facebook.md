### STORY 2: LIV Golf 2.0 — Eight Days Left

**Long-Form Post (1:00 PM ET)**
The clock is ticking on LIV Golf's make-or-break decision.

October 25 — eight days away — is the deadline for players to commit to LIV Golf's proposed 2027 restructured league, now known as LIV 2.0. The league needs a critical mass of players to secure a $300 million financing package from BC Partners Credit.

So far, the public scoreboard is not encouraging for LIV. Richard Bland has committed. Jon Rahm has said he won't participate. Adrian Meronk and David Puig are also reportedly out. Sergio Garcia's contract has ended, though he hasn't fully closed the door.

Then there's the DP World Tour's bombshell: any player who commits to LIV 2.0 will lose their DP World Tour card — and with it, their Ryder Cup eligibility. The membership application deadline is November 16. For European players who might have been on the fence, that's a significant new weight on the scale.

Eight days. The league's future hangs on what a few dozen professional golfers decide to do next.

### STORY 3: Jon Rahm's LIV Exit Path

**Long-Form Post (6:00 PM ET)**
Jon Rahm is leaving LIV Golf — but getting to wherever he goes next is going to take time.

Rahm's lawyer confirmed at a U.S. bankruptcy court hearing on October 7 that he will not participate in LIV 2.0. The two sides are now working toward a consensual separation agreement, with a court hearing scheduled for November 5 to approve it.

After that, the math gets complicated. The PGA Tour currently requires former LIV players to serve a 12-month suspension before returning to compete. If that clock starts after the November court hearing, Rahm's earliest realistic return would be in late 2027 — and a full 2027 PGA Tour season would be off the table entirely.

The DP World Tour adds another layer. With its November 16 membership deadline looming, Rahm's path back to European events — including Ryder Cup eligibility — is also uncertain.

For a former world No. 1 who won the Masters and US Open in 2023, the road back to the tour he left is proving longer than many expected.

### STORY 4: Butterfield Bermuda Championship Preview

**Long-Form Post (8:30 AM ET)**
Five days until Port Royal.

The Butterfield Bermuda Championship tees off October 22–25 at Port Royal Golf Course in Southampton Parish, Bermuda — the shortest course on the PGA Tour at 6,828 yards, par 71. The purse is $6 million and 500 FedExCup points are on offer.

For many players in the field, this isn't just another event. The FedExCup Fall is a points-accumulation period that determines which players start 2027 with full tour status. The bubble is real, the stakes are high, and Port Royal's wind can turn a low-scoring week into a survival test.

Adam Schenk won the 2025 edition at 12 under. Rafael Campos took the 2024 title. Past results have shown enormous variability depending on conditions — calm days produce birdie-fests, windy days become grind sessions.

Five days out. Time to start watching the forecast.

### STORY 5: Scheffler 180 Weeks at No. 1

**Long-Form Post (8:30 PM ET)**
Scottie Scheffler is quietly approaching one of golf's most remarkable records — and this week, he passes another milestone on the way there.

Scheffler reaches approximately 180 consecutive weeks at world No. 1 this week. Tiger Woods' record of 281 consecutive weeks at No. 1 is still roughly 101 weeks away. At Scheffler's current pace, that translates to about two more years of uninterrupted No. 1 status.

No player in the history of the world golf rankings — except Tiger Woods — has held the top spot for more than 135 consecutive weeks before Scheffler's current run. He has now more than doubled that figure, and the record is within range.

The quiet part: he is not chasing it. He is just playing golf. The record is chasing him.
