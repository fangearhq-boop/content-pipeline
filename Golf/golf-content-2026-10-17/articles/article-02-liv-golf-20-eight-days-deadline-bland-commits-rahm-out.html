Eight Days Left: LIV Golf's October 25 Deadline, Who's In, and Who's Walking Away

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | October 17, 2026

LIV Golf | LIV 2.0 | PGA Tour | DP World Tour | Golf Business

The October 25 deadline for LIV Golf's proposed 2027 restructured league is eight days away, and the picture forming around it is not encouraging for those trying to keep the circuit alive. Richard Bland has publicly committed. Jon Rahm has confirmed he is out. The DP World Tour has added a new and significant pressure to the equation.

What LIV 2.0 Needs

The restructured league — referred to as LIV 2.0 — is contingent on securing a critical mass of players sufficient to operate what courts have described as "a bona fide professional golf league." BC Partners Credit has committed an initial $4 million of a targeted $300 million financing package. But the money is not the bottleneck right now. The players are.

LIV extended its original October 13 commitment deadline to October 25, giving players more time to decide. The extension suggests the league was not getting the response it needed from its own roster.

Who Has Said What

The public ledger as of October 17 reads as follows: Richard Bland has publicly committed. Cameron Tringale has said he would commit if enough others follow. Victor Perez has expressed support for the relaunch. Jon Rahm, Adrian Meronk, and David Puig have indicated they will not participate. Sergio Garcia's contract with LIV has ended — he has not fully ruled out future involvement but is not confirmed in. Anirban Lahiri and Joaquin Niemann have not publicly declared their intentions.

For a league that needs dozens of players to launch a competitive schedule, the current confirmed commitment list is slim.

The DP World Tour Factor

The week's biggest new development on the LIV story came not from LIV itself but from the DP World Tour. The European circuit announced it will no longer grant conditional releases or accommodations to LIV players in 2027. Any player who commits to LIV 2.0 will need to apply for DP World Tour membership by November 16 — or lose their card.

Losing a DP World Tour card has a second-order consequence that matters enormously to European players: it strips Ryder Cup eligibility. Membership is required to play for Europe in the biennial team event. For players from Spain, Germany, Sweden, and elsewhere on the continent, the prospect of choosing LIV 2.0 and forfeiting their Ryder Cup future is a different kind of decision than it was a month ago.

What's Next

The October 25 deadline is eight days away. Some reports indicate the date could be extended further if needed, but for now it stands as the marker. The next major checkpoint after that is the DP World Tour's November 16 membership deadline. Between those two dates, the landscape of professional golf's next chapter will take its shape.
