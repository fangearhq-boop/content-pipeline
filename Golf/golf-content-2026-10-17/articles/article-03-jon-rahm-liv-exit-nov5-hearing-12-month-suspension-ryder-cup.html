Rahm's Road Back: A November Court Date, a 12-Month Suspension, and a Long Wait

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | October 17, 2026

Jon Rahm | LIV Golf | PGA Tour | DP World Tour | Ryder Cup

Jon Rahm will not play LIV 2.0. That part is settled. But what comes next for the 2023 Masters and US Open champion is a process that will take months — and the timeline for his return to mainstream tour golf is longer than many fans might expect.

What Happened at the October 7 Hearing

Rahm's lawyer, John Beck, told a U.S. bankruptcy court on October 7 that Rahm had independently reviewed the proposed terms for LIV Golf's 2027 restructuring and found them "unacceptable." Beck confirmed that Rahm would not participate in LIV 2.0, and that Rahm and LIV were in advanced discussions on a consensual separation agreement.

The court has scheduled a further hearing for November 5 to move toward approving that separation. Until it is approved, Rahm technically remains under his LIV contract — limiting what he can publicly announce or negotiate with other tours.

The 12-Month Clock

The PGA Tour's current rules require former LIV players to serve a 12-month suspension before being eligible to return to Tour competition. That clock does not start until the separation is formalized and approved.

If the November 5 hearing goes smoothly and the separation is approved, the earliest Rahm could be eligible for PGA Tour events would be approximately November 2027. That means missing the entire 2027 PGA Tour season — or at best competing in the final stretch of it, depending on the exact timeline and eligibility windows.

The Ryder Cup Question

Ryder Cup eligibility adds another layer of complexity. Playing for Europe requires DP World Tour membership, and the DP World Tour's deadline for 2027 membership applications is November 16. Rahm's team will need to navigate both the LIV separation and the DP World Tour membership question almost simultaneously.

The DP World Tour has announced it will no longer accommodate LIV players for 2027. Whether Rahm — who is actively separating from LIV — qualifies for different treatment than players who have committed to LIV 2.0 is not yet clear. The 2027 Ryder Cup at Adare Manor in Limerick, Ireland remains roughly a year away. Whether Rahm can be part of it is an open question.

What's Next

The November 5 bankruptcy court hearing is Rahm's next major checkpoint. A clean approval of the separation agreement starts the legal clock on his departure from LIV. From there, negotiating a return path — whether through the PGA Tour, the DP World Tour, or some combination — is the work of the months ahead. For a player who once held the world No. 1 ranking and won two majors in the same calendar year, the path back is navigable. It just takes time.
