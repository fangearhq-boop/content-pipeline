Hye-Jin Choi Holds Two-Shot Lead Into Round 3 at the Buick LPGA Shanghai

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | October 17, 2026

LPGA Tour | Buick LPGA Shanghai | Hye-Jin Choi | Women's Golf

Hye-Jin Choi has led the Buick LPGA Shanghai since the opening round, and as Saturday's third round gets underway at Qizhong Garden Golf Club, she carries a two-shot advantage into the penultimate day of a tournament she has controlled from the start.

How the Week Has Looked

Choi opened Thursday with a 64 — eight birdies, no bogeys — to post one of the cleanest scorecards of the week and build a three-shot lead over Spain's Carlota Ciganda after Round 1. The course in Shanghai demands precision, and Choi delivered it from the first hole.

Friday's conditions were different. Wind swept through Qizhong Garden Golf Club, and the whole field felt it. Choi didn't match her Thursday form, but she didn't need to. She played steadily, protected her position, and walked off the course still out front. The two-shot cushion she carries into Round 3 reflects a player who reads the tournament rather than just the leaderboard.

Who Is in the Hunt

Carlota Ciganda opened the week with a 67 and has been one of the closest pursuers. The Spaniard has experience winning on the LPGA and will be dangerous if Choi has any kind of stumble in the final 36 holes.

Atthaya Thitikul came into Shanghai as one of the most in-form players on any tour — four wins in 2026 — but the early rounds have not produced the separation she might have hoped for. She remains in position to contend, but catching Choi from two back in the final day and a half requires a different gear.

Choi's Case for the Title

The case for Hye-Jin Choi closing this out is simple: she has been the best player in the field through the first two rounds, she led on a day when the whole field was scoring well (Thursday), and she held on a day when the whole field was struggling (Friday). That combination — a floor and a ceiling working in the same week — is what winning players look like.

The case for a collapse is harder to make. Two shots is not a comfortable lead in professional golf, but it is a workable one. Choi has not given signs that she is playing tightly or protecting. She has been playing to win.

What's Next

Round 3 is taking place today in Shanghai. The final round is scheduled for Sunday, October 18 — which means Saturday night for U.S. viewers. A Choi victory would represent one of her biggest career results on the LPGA and would be a statement performance going into the tour's final stretch of the 2026 season.
