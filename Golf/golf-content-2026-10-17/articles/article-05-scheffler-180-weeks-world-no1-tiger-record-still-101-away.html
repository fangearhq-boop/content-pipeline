Scheffler at ~180 Consecutive Weeks at No. 1: Tiger's Record Is Still 101 Away

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | October 17, 2026

Scottie Scheffler | World Golf Rankings | PGA Tour | Tiger Woods

Scottie Scheffler reaches approximately 180 consecutive weeks at world No. 1 this week — another milestone in a streak that is gradually entering territory that most golf fans never expected to see again. Tiger Woods' record of 281 consecutive weeks is still roughly 101 weeks away. That is about two more years of uninterrupted dominance required to match it.

What 180 Weeks Means

The world rankings were introduced in 1986. In the nearly 40 years since, only one player has ever held the No. 1 position for more than 135 consecutive weeks before Scheffler began his current run: Tiger Woods. That context makes what Scheffler is doing worth pausing on, even in a week with bigger headlines competing for attention.

Scheffler first reached world No. 1 in March 2022 and has not relinquished the position since. He has now held it continuously for longer than any player other than Tiger — and he is still adding to the total.

The Math of the Chase

Tiger Woods held the world No. 1 ranking for 281 consecutive weeks during his era of dominance. Scheffler, at approximately 180 weeks, needs 101 more to match that figure. At the current rate — which assumes no disruption from injury, form dips, or unforeseen circumstances — he would reach 281 consecutive weeks in roughly late 2028.

The caveat is real. Tiger's record was not set easily. It required years of sustained excellence with no significant challenge from any other player. Scheffler has been that dominant in 2024 and 2026, but the tour is competitive, and a year of relative inconsistency could slow the count without ending it — or a long injury absence could end it entirely.

A Comparison Worth Making

The players who came closest to Tiger's record before Scheffler tell you something about how rare it is. Greg Norman, Nick Faldo, and Ernie Els all held the No. 1 ranking for extended periods but never approached the 281-week figure. The nearest any modern player got before Scheffler was the 100-plus weeks that Rory McIlroy accumulated at various points in his career — not consecutively, and not close.

What Scheffler is doing exists in a category with essentially one prior occupant. The question is whether he can stay in it long enough to write himself into the same sentence.

What's Next

Scheffler heads toward the end of the 2026 PGA Tour season with the No. 1 ranking intact and the streak continuing. The next major — the 2027 Masters at Augusta National — is roughly six months away. His performance between now and then will determine whether the streak hits 200 weeks before the Masters begins. At the current rate, it will.
