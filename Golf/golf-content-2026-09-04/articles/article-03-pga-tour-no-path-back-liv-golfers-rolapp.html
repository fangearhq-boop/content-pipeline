PGA Tour Commissioner Says No Path Back Planned for LIV Golfers DeChambeau, Rahm and Smith

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap

September 4, 2026

As LIV Golf collapses toward bankruptcy, the most obvious question in the sport is whether the players who left for the Saudi-backed circuit can return to the PGA Tour.

The answer, at least for now, is no.

PGA Tour CEO and commissioner Brian Rolapp told reporters the Tour has no current plan to reinstate LIV golfers — specifically naming Bryson DeChambeau, Jon Rahm and Cameron Smith as players who are not being welcomed back under any active pathway.

A Rebuilt Alliance Structure

Rolapp's position is buttressed by the PGA Tour's realignment of global golf governance. The Tour has announced a multi-year partnership with the DP World Tour (Europe) and the Asian Tour through 2029, creating a three-way alliance that covers the majority of elite professional golf outside of LIV and the LPGA.

The Asian Tour connection is particularly pointed. When LIV launched in 2022, the Asian Tour served as its feeder league — providing a second circuit for LIV players and giving the Saudi-backed organization a foothold in the global tour ecosystem. That relationship is now over: the Asian Tour is fully aligned with the PGA Tour, closing off the pathway that once gave LIV its international legitimacy.

What This Means for LIV Players

The commissioner's statement lands at a critical moment. Dozens of contracted LIV players are navigating bankruptcy settlement offers worth a fraction of their guaranteed money while simultaneously wondering whether they have a professional future anywhere meaningful.

For Rahm, DeChambeau and Smith — three of the biggest names who made the move — the PGA Tour's closed door makes a difficult situation harder. Each player sacrificed years of PGA Tour eligibility by joining LIV. Each built their career on PGA Tour platforms and relied on that membership for major championship access through the World Golf Rankings.

The PGA Tour's position is a calculated one: reinstating LIV players quickly or easily would undermine the leverage the Tour spent years building. Legal battles, diplomatic negotiations and the eventual breakdown of the Framework Agreement all contributed to a PGA Tour that emerged from the LIV era with its power structure intact. Rolapp is not going to give that away.

An Open Question on Formal Reinstatement

Rolapp's comments speak to current plans, not permanent policy. Golf analysts have noted that if LIV formally ceases operations — particularly through a bankruptcy dissolution rather than a restructuring — individual players could petition for reinstatement through existing PGA Tour processes. Each case would be reviewed on its merits.

But "no current plan" is not the same as "never" — and the Tour's refusal to pre-emptively open the door is a deliberate message about who holds the power in professional golf's new world order.

What's Next

LIV's bankruptcy proceedings — if they come this week as expected — will clarify which players are released from their contracts and under what terms. The PGA Tour will be watching, and how quickly individual players can demonstrate availability and intent to compete on recognized tours may determine how quickly that picture changes.

For now, the door is closed. The question is who gets to open it, and when.
