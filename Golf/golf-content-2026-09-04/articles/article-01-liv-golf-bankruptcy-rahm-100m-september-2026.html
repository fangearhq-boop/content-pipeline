LIV Golf Bankruptcy Filing Could Come This Week — Jon Rahm Owed More Than $100 Million

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap

September 4, 2026

The end may finally be here for LIV Golf as a going concern.

Reports indicate LIV Golf could file for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection as soon as the week of September 7 — days from now — as it scrambles to restructure operations and secure new investment before its debts overwhelm its remaining assets. The filing, if it comes, would represent the most dramatic denouement in professional golf's modern era.

Jon Rahm's situation captures the scale of the financial wreckage. The Spanish superstar — winner of the 2026 Indianapolis individual title — is owed more than $100 million from the $300 million signing agreement he received when he left the PGA Tour for LIV ahead of the 2024 season. He is not alone. Dozens of contracted players have guaranteed payments remaining beyond 2026, and the league has been sending settlement offers worth "cents on the dollar" to those players in an attempt to limit its obligations.

A Season That Ended in Silence

LIV's 2026 season concluded not with a celebration but with an awkward quiet. The Michigan team championship, which was to serve as the season finale with a $40 million purse, was cancelled. Instead, both the individual and team championships were decided in Indianapolis with a "drastically reduced" bonus structure and stripped-down entertainment offerings. Jon Rahm's Legion XIII won the team championship, earning $8.4 million — a fraction of what the original Michigan event had promised.

In the days that followed, LIV laid off the majority of its 300-plus staff members. Reports of employees receiving notices en masse circulated through the golf community.

The Saudi Public Investment Fund, which had bankrolled the operation to the tune of an estimated $6 billion since LIV's launch in 2022, formally ended its funding after the 2026 season. The PIF's withdrawal was not entirely a surprise — the fund had been signaling for months that it expected a new investor to take on the circuit's funding — but the combination of no deal yet signed and a bankruptcy clock ticking has created an acute crisis.

LIV 2.0: The Plan, If It Survives

LIV's leadership has been pitching a leaner model for 2027 under the banner "LIV 2.0": approximately 10 events — five international and five in the United States — timed around the four major championships to maximize star player availability. Crucially, the model would give players a greater equity stake in the competition itself, a structural change meant to align incentives between the league and its talent.

BC Partners Credit, a London-based investment firm, has been in discussions to provide new funding. But as of now, no deal has been finalized. Chapter 11 bankruptcy would allow LIV to continue operating in some form while restructuring its debts and pursuing that investment — but it would also create new uncertainty for players and sponsors alike.

What Happens to the Players?

For the 50-plus players contracted to LIV, the options are stark. They can accept the settlement offers and potentially return to other tours. They can reject the settlements and pursue their full claims through bankruptcy proceedings — a lengthy process with uncertain outcomes. Or they can hold out for LIV 2.0 and hope it materializes.

For players like Rahm, the financial stakes are enormous. Whether they can collect a meaningful portion of their guaranteed money through bankruptcy proceedings will depend on how LIV's assets are valued and how creditors are prioritized under Chapter 11 rules.

What's Next

A bankruptcy filing, if it happens this week, would trigger a cascade of legal and administrative events that would define LIV's fate over the following months. The golf world — and particularly the PGA Tour — will be watching closely. The next chapter of professional golf's power structure may be written in a courtroom before it's written on a leaderboard.
