### STORY 1: LIV Golf Bankruptcy

**Long-Form Post | 8:30 AM ET**

The end may finally be here for LIV Golf as we know it.

After four years and an estimated $6 billion in Saudi Public Investment Fund money, LIV Golf is staring down bankruptcy protection — potentially as early as this week. The timing is almost poetic: the league's 2026 season just concluded in Indianapolis with muted celebrations, a cancelled Michigan team championship and bonuses that fell far short of what players were promised.

Jon Rahm's situation is perhaps the most striking. When Rahm left the PGA Tour for LIV ahead of the 2024 season, he signed a deal reportedly worth $300 million. He is still owed more than $100 million of it. LIV has been sending settlement offers to players worth "cents on the dollar" — meaning Rahm and others could walk away with a fraction of what they were guaranteed.

The league has already laid off the majority of its 300+ staff members. BC Partners Credit, a London-based firm, has been in talks to provide new funding for a proposed "LIV 2.0" — a leaner 10-event format with player equity stakes, timed around the major championships. But that deal is not yet finalized, and Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection would allow LIV to restructure its debts while pursuing that new investment.

For 50+ contracted players, the next few days will be pivotal.

### STORY 2: Solheim Cup Preview

**Long-Form Post | 10:30 AM ET**

Three days.

That's how long until the 2026 Solheim Cup tees off at Bernardus Golf in Cromvoirt, Netherlands — the first time the competition has been held on Dutch soil in its 37-year history.

The rosters are set. The storylines are in place. And the desire to win burns on both sides of the Atlantic.

**Team USA** is led by captain Angela Stanford and headlined by world No. 1 Nelly Korda, who has been in extraordinary form this season with four wins and two major titles. Joining her is Rose Zhang (4-0 at 2024 Solheim), Megan Khang (7-3-3 career record), Lindy Duncan and seven others. USA won the 2024 Solheim 15.5-12.5 in Virginia and is chasing back-to-back victories for the first time since 2015/2017.

**Team Europe** is captained by Anna Nordqvist and features Charley Hull — world No. 7 and one of the most competitive match-play players in the history of the event, now making her eighth consecutive Solheim Cup appearance. World No. 5 Lottie Woad is among the rookies, and Europe has not forgotten the agony of that 2024 loss.

Competition begins Sunday. The full match schedule will be announced at the opening ceremony. Follow along — women's golf doesn't get bigger than this.

### STORY 3: PGA Tour "No Path Back"

**Long-Form Post | 1:00 PM ET**

As LIV Golf crashes toward bankruptcy, the question on everyone's lips is obvious: Can the LIV players come back to the PGA Tour?

The answer, at least for now, is no.

PGA Tour CEO Brian Rolapp told reporters this week that the Tour has no current plan to bring back players like Bryson DeChambeau, Jon Rahm or Cameron Smith. The PGA Tour has rebuilt its international alliances with a multi-year partnership through 2029 with both the DP World Tour and the Asian Tour — the same Asian Tour that used to serve as LIV's secondary feeder league, now fully aligned with the PGA Tour.

The stance is notable given the circumstances. LIV is financially imploding, its players are being offered pennies on contracts they signed in good faith, and many of them are watching their guaranteed money evaporate. And yet the PGA Tour's door is closed.

The Tour's position is a business decision: bringing back LIV players — especially on favorable terms — would undermine the leverage the PGA Tour won over years of legal battles and diplomatic maneuvering. There are still open questions about whether individual players could petition for reinstatement if LIV formally ceases operations. But Rolapp's statement makes clear the Tour is not rolling out the welcome mat.

For Rahm, DeChambeau, Smith and others, their options are narrowing fast.

### STORY 4: Presidents Cup Preview

**Long-Form Post | 3:30 PM ET**

The Solheim Cup kicks off Sunday. And just three weeks after that, the golf world gets its next great team competition: the Presidents Cup.

September 22-27, Medinah Country Club just outside Chicago will host the biennial showdown between Team USA and the International Team. Tiger Woods captains the Americans — a role he is playing for the first time — in a team that features Scottie Scheffler (the FedExCup champion) and Rory McIlroy (2026 Masters champion), among others. The International side is captained by Adam Scott.

The Presidents Cup is often overshadowed by the Ryder Cup in international prestige, but the individual match results have historically been compelling — and Medinah's history (Ryder Cup 2012) gives this venue a gravitas that few American courses can match.

Keep the calendar marked. After the Solheim Cup drama, golf fans barely get a week to breathe before the men take the stage.

### STORY 5: Walmart NW Arkansas Championship

**Long-Form Post | 6:00 PM ET**

The LPGA Tour marks a milestone this fall.

The Walmart NW Arkansas Championship presented by P&G celebrates its 20th anniversary on September 25-27 at Pinnacle Country Club in Rogers, Arkansas. For two decades, this event has been one of the LPGA Tour calendar's cornerstones — and it plays a unique role in 2026 by following so closely after the Solheim Cup (September 7-13).

The $3 million purse will draw 144 of the world's best female golfers, many of whom will be coming off the emotional highs and lows of Solheim Cup competition. Historically, Solheim Cup participants tend to either ride the confidence wave — or need a reset after the team competition intensity.

The event has been held at Pinnacle Country Club since 2007, giving the field familiarity with a track that rewards precise ball-striking and sharp course management. Past champions include major winners and LPGA hall of famers.

Twenty years. Still going strong. Rogers, Arkansas puts on one of the best weeks on the LPGA calendar.
