Ryan Fox Birdies the 72nd Hole to Win The Open Championship — New Zealand's First Modern Major Champion

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap

July 25, 2026

The 154th Open Championship at Royal Birkdale Golf Club ended the only way a great major championship should: with a birdie on the final hole that separated a champion from the rest of the field.

Ryan Fox, the New Zealand professional who has spent years knocking on the door of major championship contention, made that birdie on the 72nd hole on Sunday, July 19, to finish at 10-under par for the tournament — a 270 total over rounds of 72-68-62-68. He won the Claret Jug by one stroke over Cameron Young, who finished at 9-under, with Sam Burns third at 8-under. Scottie Scheffler and Tommy Fleetwood tied for fourth at 7-under.

The prize money: $3.2 million from a purse of $17.75 million. The points: 750 FedExCup points. But the real measure of what Fox accomplished is measured in something more durable than money or ranking.

The Round That Made the Champion

Fox's path to the title ran through a third-round 62 that moved him into serious contention — a scorched-earth performance across the Birkdale links that underlined his capacity for low scoring on the world's most demanding links layout. His final-round 68 was more controlled, more calculated, more tense. The birdie on 18 was not a comfortable tap-in. It was the shot that defined the week.

The winning margin of one shot over Cameron Young gave the championship the drama befitting a venue and a trophy of its stature. Young, who has become one of major golf's most persistent near-men, played a final round that would have won in most years. This year, it wasn't enough.

Sam Burns's third-place finish continues a 2026 season that has seen the Louisiana native compile results that suggest a major breakthrough of his own is not far away. Scheffler and Fleetwood, who came into the final round with legitimate title chances, ultimately fell short — Scheffler unable to replicate the dominant ball-striking that won him the title here as the defending champion, Fleetwood unable to convert the home-crowd energy into the birdie opportunities he needed.

What It Means for New Zealand

No New Zealand golfer had won a major championship in the modern professional era before Sunday at Royal Birkdale. The country has produced excellent professionals — players who have competed and contended at the highest level of the sport — but the major championship podium had remained elusive.

Fox's victory will reverberate through the golf development pipeline in New Zealand in ways that are difficult to quantify immediately but will be felt for a generation. The visibility a major championship winner brings to the sport in their home country is transformational. Young players across New Zealand now have a countrymen who has held the Claret Jug.

Australian and New Zealand media led their sports coverage with the story. Fox's return home with the trophy is the country's biggest golf moment in recent memory.

Royal Birkdale's Stage

Royal Birkdale staged the championship with the combination of difficulty and beauty that makes it one of the great Open venues. The Southport links, with its distinctive dunes, punishing rough, and holes that run in multiple directions relative to the prevailing winds, tested every facet of the players' games across four days.

The 2026 Open brought 750 FedExCup points to a tournament that draws the best of both the PGA Tour and the DP World Tour to compete on golf's most historic stage. Fox's winning total of 10-under par reflects a course that rewarded precision and penalized anything less.

What's Next

Ryan Fox claimed his first major championship at Royal Birkdale. The golf world now watches to see what comes next — whether this is the launching pad for a late-career major collection, or whether, as has been the case for many Open Champions, this is the singular greatest moment of a respected career.

The PGA Championship, the Masters, and the U.S. Open remain on Fox's major championship resume as victories he hasn't yet achieved. He now knows what it feels like to win the biggest events in the sport. That knowledge changes a player. What it does for the New Zealander remains to be discovered.
