Michael Kim Shoots 59 at the 3M Open — The PGA Tour's 16th Sub-60 Round in History

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap

July 25, 2026

Golf history was made Friday at TPC Twin Cities in Blaine, Minnesota. Michael Kim shot a 59 in the second round of the 3M Open — the PGA Tour's 16th sub-60 round in the history of the sport's most elite professional circuit.

Kim had opened the tournament with a 69 in Round 1. Then he came out and did what only 15 players have done before him in PGA Tour competition: he completed 18 holes in 59 strokes. The result: a 128 total, 14-under par, and a three-shot lead over the entire field heading into the weekend at a tournament that was already well underway when Friday turned into one of its most extraordinary days.

The Leaderboard After 36 Holes

Kim's position atop the leaderboard after Friday's round is formidable. At 14-under par, he holds a three-stroke advantage over the chasing group that includes Emiliano Grillo, Ben Kohles, and Chandler Phillips, all tied at 11-under. Jackson Koivun and Troy Merritt are one further back at 10-under, followed by a cluster of players within striking distance.

Scottie Scheffler, the World No. 1, shot a first-round 65 and remains in the field. Merritt, a journeyman PGA Tour professional known for grinding out results rather than historic performances, finds himself in an unexpected position on the leaderboard — playing his way into the conversation of a tournament where the story is very much someone else's.

The cut fell at 9-under, with 65 and ties advancing. The field is concentrated, competitive, and facing a three-shot deficit against a player who just made history.

What a 59 Means

The number 59 carries a weight in golf that doesn't exist in any other professional sport. Sixty strokes for 18 holes is already exceptional — sub-60 requires a near-perfect combination of ball-striking, putting, decision-making, and fortune. The small number of sub-60 rounds in PGA Tour history is a direct reflection of how rarely all those elements align simultaneously.

Al Geiberger was the first, shooting 59 in the 1977 Memphis Classic. Jim Furyk shot 58 in 2016 — still the PGA Tour's all-time low round. Every sub-60 round since Geiberger has been its own chapter in the history of the sport. Kim's 59 in Blaine is now part of that list permanently.

The context here is notable: Kim shot 59 in Round 2 of a standard strokeplay event, with two rounds still to play. The pressure of carrying the historical performance through a weekend of championship golf is a test of a different kind than the round itself. Sixty-seven 59-shooters in PGA Tour history have had to come back the following day and perform while the achievement was still fresh.

The 3M Open Context

The 3M Open at TPC Twin Cities is a regular PGA Tour event with an $8.8 million purse. The course, situated in Blaine, Minnesota, rewards target golf and birdie-making on a relatively forgiving layout — but forgiving doesn't mean easy. A 59 on any PGA Tour course, regardless of its difficulty rating, requires an exceptional round.

Rounds 3 and 4 take place this weekend. By Sunday evening, the 3M Open will have its champion. Whether that champion is Michael Kim or someone who charges from the pack on Sunday depends on how Kim handles the pressure of a three-shot lead and a once-in-a-career performance behind him.

What's Next

Round 3 begins Saturday at TPC Twin Cities. Kim tees off as the clear leader with the most watched scorecard in golf this weekend. His competitors — Grillo, Kohles, Phillips, and the rest — will be working to close the gap and force the story to become a Sunday competition rather than an inevitable procession.

The 59 is already locked in the record books. The victory is not. And that is exactly what makes the weekend at the 3M Open worth watching.
