LIV Golf Is Done: League Officially Winding Down After Losing Rescue Investment

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | November 20, 2026

LIV Golf is over.

At a bankruptcy hearing in New Jersey on November 5, lawyers for the embattled league declared that LIV Golf is "toggling to a value-maximizing wind-down." The restructuring plan — sometimes referred to internally as LIV 2.0 — has been abandoned. The BC Partners Credit rescue investment of approximately $300 million, which had been seen as a potential lifeline, was withdrawn.

There will be no 2027 LIV Golf season.

How It Ended

LIV Golf's collapse unfolded over much of 2026. Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, which had backed the league since its founding, pulled its funding in April 2026. Without the PIF's financial support, LIV filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in September.

What followed was a period of attempted restructuring. BC Partners Credit emerged as a potential rescuer, with reports placing the investment at approximately $300 million. That deal ultimately did not close, and without it, LIV had no viable path forward.

The league's last competitive event took place in Indianapolis in August 2026. At the time, it was not publicly framed as LIV's final tournament. In retrospect, Michael La Sasso's victory there stands as the last competitive result in LIV Golf's three-year history.

The Human Cost: Rahm's Situation

Among the players caught in the collapse, Jon Rahm's circumstances drew particular attention. The two-time major champion was one of LIV's most prominent signings, and his exit reportedly came at a steep price. Sky Sports reported that Rahm forfeited approximately $100 million in guaranteed contract claims in order to exit the league before its wind-down was formalized.

The figure, if accurate, represents one of the more stunning financial stories in golf's recent history — a sum forfeited in exchange for the ability to chart his own path forward.

What Comes Next for LIV Players

The wind-down of LIV Golf does not automatically return its players to the PGA Tour. The PGA Tour's existing policy imposes a one-year ban on players returning from LIV, and league officials have not announced any blanket exceptions or accelerated reinstatement pathways for the displaced roster.

That means players who competed in LIV Golf will need to navigate the reinstatement process individually — and the one-year clock starts from whenever they formally apply to return, not from LIV's closure date.

The DP World Tour's terms were also a factor. Rory McIlroy commented in recent weeks that it was "a shame" Jon Rahm had been the only LIV player unwilling to accept DP World Tour terms, suggesting that pathway had been available to others.

The End of an Era

LIV Golf launched in 2022 backed by Saudi sovereign wealth and a promise to transform professional golf. For a period it did disrupt the established order — drawing top players with guaranteed contracts, forcing the PGA Tour into defensive mode, and triggering a years-long negotiation process that never reached the merger both sides publicly discussed.

What it did not do was build a sustainable business. The league's departure leaves professional golf restructured but not unified. The PGA Tour remains the dominant circuit in the United States; the DP World Tour continues its season in Europe. The players LIV took with large promises now face the task of finding their way back.

At a New Jersey courthouse on November 5, that chapter closed.
