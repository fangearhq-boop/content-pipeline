Nelly Korda Fires 64 in Round 2 to Surge Into CME Group Tour Championship Contention

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | November 20, 2026

Nelly Korda had a quiet Thursday at Tiburón Golf Club. A four-under 68 left the world No. 1 tied for 20th, four strokes off the pace at the LPGA Tour's season finale. It looked, briefly, like the CME Group Tour Championship might slip away before it really began.

Then Friday happened. Korda went out and shot a 64 — six under par — to surge back into contention at the richest event in women's golf history.

The Round 2 Reset

Korda's 64 was the kind of round that reshapes a leaderboard. Coming into Friday, the story at the CME Group Tour Championship belonged to the three players who shared the Round 1 lead: Atthaya Thitikul, A Lim Kim, and Karis Davidson, all at minus-7 after opening 65s on Thursday.

Thitikul's round had been particularly eye-catching — she closed the first 18 holes with five consecutive birdies to reach minus-7. Davidson was making her tournament debut. Kim was steady from start to finish.

But Korda's Friday put her name squarely in the weekend conversation. The world's top-ranked player has not won in 2026 — and with $2 million waiting for the champion in Naples, that winless streak has fresh urgency attached to it.

What the CME Group Tour Championship Means

The CME Group Tour Championship carries the largest purse in women's golf history at $11 million. The champion takes home $2 million — a figure that underscores the LPGA Tour's growth and the significance of this event as the season's closing act.

Thitikul won this event in 2025, closing at minus-26 to claim the title. Korda was tied for third that year at minus-20. The two players know this course and each other's game well, which makes the weekend showdown between them — if the leaderboard holds — all the more compelling.

The Weekend Ahead in Naples

After two rounds at Tiburón Golf Club, the championship picture is coming into focus. Thitikul, Kim, and Davidson set the Round 1 pace; Korda's 64 means she arrives at the weekend in pursuit rather than chasing from deep.

England's Charley Hull was one shot off the R1 lead at minus-6, keeping herself in the mix. The field at Tiburón is not short of contenders.

For Korda, the goal is simple: end 2026 with a win. The CME Group Tour Championship is her last chance to do it. After Friday's 64, she's given herself every opportunity.
