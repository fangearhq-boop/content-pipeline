Anna Nordqvist's Pairings Puzzle — Inside Europe's Solheim Cup Strategy

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | September 5, 2026

When Anna Nordqvist says her Solheim Cup pairings feel like a "puzzle," she isn't complaining. She's describing a captain's ideal situation: more good options than she has session slots to fill.

The Europe captain has her 12-player roster set for the 2026 Solheim Cup at Bernardus Golf in the Netherlands. What she's working through now — and will be working through until the morning of each session — is who plays with whom, who sits, and when each player is best deployed over three days of competition starting September 11.

Why Pairings Matter in Match Play

The Solheim Cup's format demands strategic thinking that goes far beyond simply fielding your 12 best players. In foursomes — alternate shot — two players share a single ball and take turns hitting. Chemistry, communication, and complementary ball-striking are essential. A pairing that looks strong on paper can fall apart if the players' shot shapes don't fit together, or if their temperaments clash under match play pressure.

Fourballs — both players playing their own ball, best score counting — is different. There, you want aggression. Players who can go low, who don't blink when the match gets tight, who give their partner the freedom to attack because they know a solid score is already on the card.

Singles on September 13 are a different calculus again. The captain decides the order: do you front-load with your strongest players to take an early lead, or scatter them to apply pressure throughout the draw?

The Nordqvist Advantage

Anna Nordqvist has been in these matches. She is a three-time major champion — ANA Inspiration titles and the Women's British Open — and a Solheim Cup veteran who knows what it looks like when pairings click and when they don't. As a player, she experienced the dynamics she's now navigating as a captain.

That playing experience is an underrated asset in captaincy. Nordqvist understands which conversations to have with players, which pairings might surprise an opponent, and what kinds of challenges the Netherlands course conditions might create for particular game styles. Her preparation isn't just about the standings — it's about the match.

Veterans and Rookies

Europe's roster includes established Solheim Cup campaigners who have won and lost these matches before, alongside players who are new to this level of team competition. Managing that balance is part of the puzzle. Rookies need pairing partners who can steady them when the crowd gets loud, the pressure mounts, and experience suddenly feels like the only thing that matters.

At the same time, Nordqvist knows that rookies can win matches. Fresh energy, nothing to lose, a point earned that nobody expected — it's happened in every Solheim Cup. Her pairings strategy almost certainly factors in where a motivated rookie might be the right surprise choice.

What's Next

Nordqvist will announce her pairings session by session. The pairings for the first foursomes — Thursday, September 11 — will be released ahead of play. Fans, analysts, and the USA coaching staff will all study them immediately. The captain's puzzle becomes public information at that moment.

Until then, Nordqvist keeps her options close. The pieces are on the board. Match play starts September 11 at Bernardus Golf. The solution to the puzzle is on its way.
