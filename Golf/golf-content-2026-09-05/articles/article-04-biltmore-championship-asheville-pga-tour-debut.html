The Biltmore Championship Is Coming to Asheville — What to Know About the PGA Tour's Newest Event

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | September 5, 2026

The PGA Tour's 2026 schedule has a new entry. The Biltmore Championship Asheville debuts September 17-20 at The Cliffs at Walnut Cove in Arden, North Carolina. It is the first time this event has ever been played, and it places professional golf in one of the most scenically distinctive cities in the American Southeast.

Asheville, North Carolina, sits in the Blue Ridge Mountains in the western part of the state. It's known for its arts scene, independent food culture, proximity to the Appalachian Trail, and — as the event name signals — its association with one of America's most famous historic estates.

The Biltmore Name

The Biltmore Estate is the historic country house built by George Washington Vanderbilt II in the late 19th century. At 178,926 square feet, it remains the largest privately owned house in the United States. The estate sits on thousands of acres in Asheville and draws over a million visitors per year as a tourist destination, hotel, and event venue.

The tournament name connects professional golf to that legacy — and to a city whose identity is tied to the mountains, the estate, and a heritage of high-quality craftwork. The branding is intentional: this is a tournament that wants to feel like a destination event from day one.

The Cliffs at Walnut Cove

The venue is The Cliffs at Walnut Cove, a private residential golf community located in Arden, just south of Asheville. Western North Carolina's topography creates a natural stage for golf — elevation changes, mountain views, tree-lined fairways, and conditions that can shift quickly given the region's weather patterns.

Hosting a PGA Tour event at a private club requires significant logistical preparation: spectator infrastructure, media facilities, volunteer coordination, and course conditioning to tour standards. The Cliffs at Walnut Cove is hosting this event for the first time, making the debut a logistical milestone as much as a competitive one.

Why Now

The PGA Tour has actively worked to expand its geographic footprint to reach markets and fan bases that don't regularly see professional golf in person. Asheville represents that kind of market — an outdoors-oriented, culturally engaged city with a growing professional population and proximity to Charlotte, Knoxville, and the broader western Carolina region.

Adding a new event to the schedule also responds to the competitive landscape that has shifted in 2026. With LIV Golf's expected bankruptcy filing, the PGA Tour has an opportunity to consolidate its position as professional golf's primary competitive home. New events in new markets strengthen that position.

What's Next

The Biltmore Championship Asheville tees off September 17. Field and purse details will be confirmed in the days ahead. For western North Carolina, it is a moment the local golf community has anticipated — professional tour golf, in the mountains, with a venue that fits the landscape it's in.
