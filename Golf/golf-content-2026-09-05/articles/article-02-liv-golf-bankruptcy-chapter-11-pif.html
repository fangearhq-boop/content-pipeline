LIV Golf's Chapter 11 Bankruptcy Filing Is Coming — What It Means for Players and the Game

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | September 5, 2026

Four years after launching with Saudi billions and a promise to upend professional golf, LIV Golf is expected to file for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection as early as the week of September 7. The filing will go through federal bankruptcy court in New Jersey. The league that promised to change everything has run out of road.

For the 48 players on LIV's 12 teams — many of whom signed multi-year guaranteed contracts worth tens of millions of dollars — the filing creates immediate and serious uncertainty about what those contracts are now worth.

How It Got Here

LIV Golf launched in 2022 with backing from Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, the sovereign wealth fund that has invested across sports, technology, and real estate as part of the kingdom's Vision 2030 economic diversification strategy. The league recruited players aggressively, offering guaranteed money — a structure entirely foreign to traditional golf's prize-purse model — and promised a global tour with 14 events per season.

The strategy worked in attracting players. It never worked as a business. LIV struggled to secure major television distribution in its early years. A broadcast deal with The CW Network in the United States provided American reach, but ratings and advertising revenue never approached what was needed to offset player guarantees. The league relied on continuous PIF cash infusions to stay operational.

A hoped-for settlement or merger with the PGA Tour would have offered an exit. Those negotiations stalled and ultimately failed to produce a deal. Without the merger, LIV faced its guaranteed player obligations with no path to profitability in sight.

The PIF Loan and What Comes Next

The Saudi Public Investment Fund extended an emergency loan of less than $100 million to LIV Golf before the bankruptcy filing — a relatively small number given the scale of player contracts outstanding. That loan will make PIF a primary secured creditor in the bankruptcy proceedings.

Under Chapter 11, LIV can reorganize its debts or liquidate assets. Players with outstanding guaranteed contract payments are unsecured creditors — they stand behind secured lenders in priority. In a liquidation scenario, unsecured creditors historically receive a fraction of what they are owed. Players who received large upfront signing payments have already been paid those sums; those waiting on future installments face the most exposure.

The futures of LIV's 12 team entities — many owned by prominent investors and celebrities — are also uncertain. Some teams may have their own contractual claims. The bankruptcy process will take months to work through the competing interests.

What It Means for Professional Golf

LIV's bankruptcy, if it leads to dissolution, removes the primary alternative to PGA Tour and DP World Tour competition. Players who left for LIV and burned bridges with those tours face a complicated path back. The PGA Tour has not indicated blanket reinstatement policies for LIV players returning through a bankruptcy-driven dissolution.

The LIV experiment tested whether guaranteed money and a team format could attract a fanbase. The answer, ultimately, was not at scale — not without star power at the very top of the game, and not with a tour structure that left players with limited schedule flexibility and no world ranking points for years of its existence.

Professional golf continues. The Solheim Cup starts in two days. The Presidents Cup is coming. The PGA Tour just added a new event in Asheville, North Carolina. The game goes on — with or without the league that once claimed it would change everything.
