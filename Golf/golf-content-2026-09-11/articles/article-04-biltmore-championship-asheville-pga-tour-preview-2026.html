Six Days to Biltmore Championship — Asheville Gets Its First PGA Tour Event in More Than 80 Years

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | September 11, 2026

The inaugural Biltmore Championship tees off September 17 at The Cliffs at Walnut Cove in Arden, North Carolina — bringing a 100-plus player PGA Tour field to a Jack Nicklaus-designed course in the Asheville region for the first time in over eight decades, in a community still rebuilding from Tropical Storm Helene's 2024 devastation.

In six days, professional golf comes to western North Carolina for the first time in a very long time.

The Biltmore Championship, an inaugural event on the PGA Tour's FedExCup Fall schedule, begins September 17 at The Cliffs at Walnut Cove — a private club in Arden, North Carolina, set in the Appalachian mountains just south of Asheville. It is the first PGA Tour event in this region in more than 80 years, and the setting carries a particular resonance for a community that has spent the past two years recovering from the flooding and destruction left by Tropical Storm Helene in 2024.

The field: more than 100 professional golfers. The course: a Jack Nicklaus signature design at 7,167 yards. The schedule: September 17-20, with broadcast coverage on Golf Channel, PGA Tour Live on ESPN+, Sirius XM, and the World Feed.

The Course and Setting

The Cliffs at Walnut Cove is a Nicklaus-designed layout built through terrain that makes western North Carolina a distinctive golf destination — mountain topography, elevation changes, tree-lined fairways, and autumn views that, in mid-September, are beginning to show early color. It is a private club, which means the Biltmore Championship will bring PGA Tour players — and PGA Tour cameras — into a setting that most of the sport's audience has never seen.

The course plays at 7,167 yards and was designed to be both challenging and walkable, in the tradition of Nicklaus's signature design work. What that translates to in a competitive PGA Tour context is a course where length matters but where accuracy and course management off the tee can neutralize that advantage — particularly in the mountain conditions that can make wind difficult to read.

The Community Context

The Asheville region has been in a sustained recovery process since Tropical Storm Helene struck in September 2024 and caused catastrophic flooding across western North Carolina. The physical and economic damage to the area was severe, and the recovery — while ongoing — has brought the region significant attention from the broader public.

A PGA Tour event, with its television reach, its on-site economic activity, and its national press coverage, is the kind of spotlight that community leaders have cited as meaningful for the region's visibility and economic recovery narrative. The Biltmore Championship is partnered with the Biltmore estate, one of western North Carolina's signature tourist destinations, which provides both brand recognition and logistical infrastructure for an inaugural event.

FedExCup Fall Context

The Biltmore Championship is part of the PGA Tour's FedExCup Fall schedule, which sits outside the regular-season points race. That means players are competing for prize money and tour membership status rather than FedExCup ranking points, making the fall swing a series where field selections, player motivations, and competitive dynamics differ from the regular season.

For players grinding for tour cards, for younger players building résumés, or for established players looking for a win in a lower-pressure environment, fall swing events present a distinct opportunity. The Biltmore Championship's Asheville setting and Nicklaus course give it a physical profile that should attract a strong fall field — the combination of destination appeal and course quality tends to draw players who want to compete rather than those simply filling a schedule.
