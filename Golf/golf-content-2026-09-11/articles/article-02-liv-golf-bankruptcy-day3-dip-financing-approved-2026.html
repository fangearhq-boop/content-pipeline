LIV Golf Bankruptcy Day 3: Court Approves Initial Financing — BC Partners' LIV 2.0 Plan Now the Formal Path Forward

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | September 11, 2026

Judge Kaplan granted complex-case designation and approved LIV Golf's initial $49.6 million DIP financing from the Saudi PIF on Day 3 of the bankruptcy proceedings, clearing the first major procedural hurdle and confirming BC Partners' restructuring plan as the formal path to a 10-event LIV 2.0 circuit in 2027.

The LIV Golf bankruptcy case moved through its first significant procedural gate on Friday, three days after the league filed its Chapter 11 petition in New Jersey federal court.

Judge Kaplan granted LIV's request for complex-case designation and joint administration, and approved the league's initial financing and payment arrangements — including the $49.6 million debtor-in-possession facility from the Saudi Public Investment Fund. For a case of this complexity and media scrutiny, clearing those first approvals in three days is a signal that the court's calendar and the parties' preparation aligned effectively.

What it means practically: LIV Golf now has the structural framework and the immediate financing to continue operating through the reorganization. The PIF loan funds day-to-day administration of the estate. The complex-case designation allows more time for the process and signals the court recognizes this as a major reorganization, not a quick liquidation.

BC Partners: The LIV 2.0 Blueprint

The exit financing plan under BC Partners, the UK-based private equity firm, is now confirmed as the formal roadmap out of bankruptcy. BC Partners and potential minority investors will provide the exit financing needed for LIV to emerge from Chapter 11. The restructured entity — "LIV 2.0" in the shorthand that has taken hold in golf media — would be a smaller, 10-event circuit targeting a 2027 launch.

Venue plans under the BC Partners blueprint include events in Saudi Arabia, Australia, South Africa, and Hong Kong. That geographic spread represents a global footprint, though a reduced one compared to LIV's original aspirations. The financial scale of the new circuit would be smaller than what LIV offered players from 2022 through its final year — no guaranteed contracts, at minimum, and likely equity-for-debt conversion mechanisms for players who were owed the most under the original structure.

For the Saudi PIF, the DIP financing represents continued exposure to the LIV asset they have spent an estimated $5 billion backing since 2022. DIP lenders sit at the top of the repayment hierarchy — ahead of administrative claims and unsecured creditors — which means PIF's $49.6 million loan has priority status in the recovery waterfall.

What This Means for the Players

The 48 players whose contracts were automatically terminated when LIV filed for Chapter 11 protection on September 8 remain in the same position they were in on filing day: without contracts, without guaranteed compensation, and watching court proceedings to understand what, if anything, they will recover from what they were owed.

Jon Rahm holds the largest individual unsecured claim at approximately $7.4 million. Bryson DeChambeau's contingent claim is approximately $5.7 million. Cameron Smith is also among the leading creditors. Unsecured creditors in a case with this level of secured and administrative debt ahead of them — $500 million-plus in total obligations — typically recover a fraction of their claims, or nothing at all.

The DIP approval does not resolve the player situation. It does clarify the timeline: with initial financing in place, the case can move toward a reorganization plan, and the BC Partners structure can be formally presented to creditors. The terms under which players might participate in LIV 2.0 — potentially through equity stakes rather than the guaranteed contracts that made original LIV so attractive — will be a central question of the next phase.

What's Next

The next major hearings will involve the formal creditor committee, the reorganization plan timeline, and the terms under which BC Partners takes over as exit financer.
