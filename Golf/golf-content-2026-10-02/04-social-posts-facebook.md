### STORY 1: LIV Golf Bankruptcy

**FB Long-Form Post**
Window: 8:00 AM ET

The era of LIV Golf as we knew it is over.

The breakaway tour that launched in 2022 with Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund (PIF) pouring billions into guaranteed contracts filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on September 8, 2026. The numbers are staggering: liabilities between $500 million and $1 billion, against assets valued at just $100 million to $500 million.

The PIF — which funded the entire operation — has agreed to a $49.6 million debtor-in-possession loan and is fully exiting as a backer. London-based private equity firm BC Partners has been named as the new financial partner, with a plan to relaunch LIV Golf in the early portion of 2027.

LIV CEO Scott O'Neil called it a "court-supervised restructuring process" that provides "the time and framework to address previous financial obligations." For the players, the news is more stark: all player contracts have been terminated as part of the restructuring.

Jon Rahm — the 2026 LIV individual champion — is owed $7.47 million from this year alone, with reportedly more than $100 million in future guarantees potentially lost. He is far from alone.

What do you think — will LIV Golf come back in 2027? And if it does, will the players trust it?

### STORY 2: Sergio Garcia Contract Filing

**FB Long-Form Post**
Window: 10:30 AM ET

Sergio Garcia wants out — officially, legally, and completely.

The 2017 Masters champion filed legal papers on October 1 seeking explicit termination of his LIV Golf contract in the ongoing Chapter 11 bankruptcy proceedings in New Jersey. This is not the same as a contract rejection — and that distinction is at the heart of Garcia's case.

Under U.S. bankruptcy law, when a company "rejects" a contract, it is treated as a breach. For Garcia, that means being an unsecured creditor for whatever he was owed, but potentially not being a clean free agent for future tour membership. A clean termination, his lawyers argue, would give him "instant free agent" status and full flexibility to rebuild his career.

Garcia plans to play the DP World Tour in 2027 and has stated his belief that LIV Golf will have some form of a future. But he's clearly not waiting to find out.

For a player who was among Europe's greatest-ever Ryder Cup competitors, the bankruptcy saga carries a personal dimension beyond the money — it could affect his eligibility for future Ryder Cup consideration.

### STORY 5: Jon Rahm LIV Future

**FB Long-Form Post**
Window: 8:30 PM ET

Jon Rahm won the 2026 LIV Golf individual championship. He is also the player most financially exposed by the league's collapse.

Bankruptcy filings reveal Rahm is owed $7.47 million from the 2026 season alone. His total exposure is even greater: when Rahm left the PGA Tour ahead of the 2024 season, he signed a nine-figure contract with LIV Golf. The future guarantee portion of that deal — reportedly more than $100 million — is now deeply uncertain.

Rahm's competitive future is the most high-profile question mark coming out of the LIV Golf bankruptcy. He has been away from the PGA Tour and major tour infrastructure since 2024. A path back to established tours would require navigating the same legal uncertainty that Garcia, Mickelson, and others face from their LIV affiliations.

Whatever the financial outcome, Rahm remains one of the world's most talented players — a former No. 1, a Masters and U.S. Open champion. The question is where he plays next, and whether the sport's major events have a clear route for his return.
