LIV Golf Files for Bankruptcy: Players Owed Millions as Saudi-Backed Tour Collapses

By Ryan Calloway | October 2, 2026

The $5 billion breakaway tour that shook the golf world in 2022 has filed for Chapter 11 protection, leaving players holding contracts that are now terminated and future guarantees that may never pay out.

LIV Golf, the Saudi Arabia-backed breakaway tour that launched in 2022 with billion-dollar guaranteed contracts and a promise to revolutionize professional golf, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection on September 8, 2026. The filing in New Jersey courts revealed liabilities estimated between $500 million and $1 billion, against assets valued at between $100 million and $500 million.

The collapse comes after Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund (PIF), which had funded the tour's operations since its inception, announced in April 2026 that continued investment no longer aligned with its strategy. The PIF redirected its resources toward domestic projects and has agreed to provide a $49.6 million debtor-in-possession loan while fully exiting as a backer.

The Scale of What's Owed

Bankruptcy filings reveal the scope of what players are owed. Jon Rahm — who won the 2026 LIV Golf individual championship — tops the list at $7.47 million from this season alone, with reportedly more than $100 million in future contract guarantees at stake. Rahm signed a nine-figure deal with LIV ahead of the 2024 season; how much of that the bankruptcy proceedings will recover remains deeply uncertain.

The restructuring plan calls for all existing player contracts to be terminated. Players with future guarantees have become unsecured creditors in the proceedings, making recovery of those amounts a legal process that could take years and deliver cents on the dollar.

What Happens Next: LIV 2.0?

LIV CEO Scott O'Neil has described the process as "court-supervised restructuring" providing "the time and framework to address previous financial obligations and complete a transaction that will make the league's next phase a reality." London-based private equity firm BC Partners has been named as the incoming financial backer, with a plan to relaunch the tour in early 2027.

The 2026 LIV season — which ran from February 5 through August 30 — concluded with Rahm winning the individual championship and 4Aces GC claiming the team title. Whether those performances will matter for any 2027 re-entry into competition depends on how the restructuring unfolds.

The Broader Impact on Professional Golf

LIV Golf's collapse poses significant questions for the sport. Players who left the PGA Tour, DP World Tour, and other established circuits to join LIV now face uncertain paths back. The tour's opposition was characterized by long legal battles with the PGA Tour; the bankruptcy does not automatically restore players' status on those tours.

For the sport as a whole, the saga represents a cautionary study in breakaway leagues — the enormous capital required to sustain operations, the legal complexities of player contracts, and the fragility of tours that depend on a single financial backer rather than media rights, sponsorships, and ticket revenue built over decades.

What's Next

The bankruptcy proceedings in New Jersey will determine the timeline and terms of LIV's restructuring. Players like Sergio Garcia are already in court seeking contract clarifications. Jon Rahm, Dustin Johnson, Brooks Koepka, and others face decisions about their competitive futures. Whether LIV Golf emerges from bankruptcy as a functional 2027 tour or disappears entirely will be determined in the coming months.
