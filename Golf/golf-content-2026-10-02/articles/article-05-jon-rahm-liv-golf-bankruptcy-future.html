Jon Rahm Is Owed $100M+ From a Bankrupt Tour — Now What?

By Marcus Cole | October 2, 2026

The 2026 LIV Golf individual champion tops the list of players owed money in the bankruptcy filings, with a competitive future as uncertain as his financial recovery.

Jon Rahm won the 2026 LIV Golf individual championship. In the same year, he became the player most financially exposed by the league's collapse. Bankruptcy filings reveal Rahm is owed $7.47 million from the 2026 season alone — and that figure does not capture the full scope of what's at stake.

When Rahm left the PGA Tour ahead of the 2024 season, he signed a nine-figure guaranteed contract with LIV Golf — one of the largest individual athlete contracts in golf history. The future guarantee portion of that deal, reportedly exceeding $100 million, is now subject to a bankruptcy process that historically pays unsecured creditors significantly less than face value.

Champion of a Bankrupt League

Rahm's 2026 LIV title was hard-won — the Spaniard's talent has never been in question, and the individual championship on LIV's points-based format reflected genuine competitive excellence across the season. 4Aces GC claimed the team title alongside his individual honors.

The irony is stark: Rahm's peak performance on the LIV Golf stage coincides with the league's financial bottom. His 2026 earnings have been partially unpaid; the future guarantees that made his departure from the PGA Tour financially rational are now at risk.

The Path Back to Established Tours

Rahm's competitive future is the most consequential open question in golf's current landscape. He left the PGA Tour in 2024 and has not competed on its events since. His world ranking, which was No. 1 at the time of departure, has been affected by the limited major championships he was eligible for through LIV's schedule.

The legal landscape for LIV players returning to the PGA Tour, DP World Tour, and other established circuits involves ongoing negotiations and precedents being set in courts. A formal LIV Golf bankruptcy termination of contracts could accelerate those negotiations, but no automatic path exists.

At 31, Rahm has his best golf potentially ahead of him. A former Masters champion and U.S. Open winner, he remains capable of competing with the world's best at any major championship that permits LIV players — currently still a developing situation. His ability to navigate the legal landscape will determine whether his 2020s are remembered primarily as the years he won majors or the years he got tangled in a tour war.

What's Next

Rahm's lawyers are engaged in the bankruptcy process to maximize recovery of owed amounts. His competitive plans for 2027 will likely be clearer once the court proceedings define what clean contract terminations look like. The BC Partners-backed LIV 2.0 relaunch in 2027 is one option; returning to established tours is another; some combination of the two, if LIV re-emerges with recognized world ranking points, could be a third path. The world's most talented golfer has options — figuring out which ones are available is the work of the next several months.
