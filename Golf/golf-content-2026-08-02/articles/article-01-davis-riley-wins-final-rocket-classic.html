Davis Riley Wins the Final-Ever Rocket Classic With a Closing 63 at Detroit Golf Club

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | August 2, 2026

The Rocket Classic — a summer staple of the PGA Tour since 1986 — held its final edition this week at Detroit Golf Club. When Sunday was done, Davis Riley stood on the 18th green as its last champion, having produced one of the most surprising and emotionally resonant wins of the 2026 season.

Riley closed with a 7-under 63 in the final round, holed a 54-foot birdie putt on the 17th hole to seize the outright lead, then converted a 10-footer on 18 to clinch it by one shot. His final score of -15 placed him one clear of Michael Brennan, who finished runner-up at -14.

From 146th to Champion in a Week

The numbers that frame this win are remarkable. Riley entered the Rocket Classic ranked 146th in the FedExCup standings with just 139 points — a position that placed him well outside the top 70 needed to qualify for the FedExCup Playoffs. He had not won a PGA Tour event in more than two years.

Sunday changed all of that. The 500 FedExCup points that come with a tour win will carry Riley from 146th into approximately the 50-60 range — comfortably inside the cut line heading into the final regular-season event in Greensboro next week. Along with the trophy, he collected a winner's share of $1.8 million from the $10 million total purse.

The Final-Round Charge

Cameron Young and Patrick Cantlay had shared the 54-hole lead at this event. Young, who fired a brilliant 61 in Round 2 to vault 68 spots up the leaderboard, entered Sunday as one of the co-favourites. Cantlay, a former FedExCup champion, was another. Neither held on in the final round as Riley produced the kind of pressure-tested closing round that defined Sunday at Detroit Golf Club.

The defining sequence came late. Riley, knowing he needed birdies, delivered two in a row on the closing holes to take the outright lead and hold it. On a course that rewards patience and strategic positioning, Riley chose the decisive moments well.

Detroit Golf Club's Farewell

Detroit Golf Club dates to 1914 — a Donald Ross design that has hosted major championships across multiple eras of the sport. The recent renovation of the course had refreshed the routing while preserving its character. For fans and players who have come to associate this venue with the summer calendar, Sunday's final round carried the weight of a genuine farewell.

Rocket Companies, the Detroit-based mortgage giant that has served as the event's title sponsor, is exiting its PGA Tour partnership after 2026. The event does not have a replacement sponsor confirmed, and the tournament's future at this site is uncertain. What is certain is that Riley's Sunday charge will be the image the Rocket Classic is remembered by.

What Comes Next

For Riley, the calculus is now clear: he has earned his place in the FedExCup Playoffs with a week to spare. The Wyndham Championship in Greensboro next week offers additional points opportunity, but the pressure of the bubble — which drives so much of the late-season urgency — is off.

For the event, Sunday closed a chapter. Detroit Golf Club had hosted the PGA Tour's annual summer stop for four decades. It goes out with a champion who needed it most, a leaderboard that fought until the final green, and a 54-foot putt that will outlive the sponsorship that paid for the week.
