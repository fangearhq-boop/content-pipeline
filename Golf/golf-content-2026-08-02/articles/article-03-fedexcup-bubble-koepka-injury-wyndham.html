FedExCup Bubble Reshuffled: Koepka's Ulnar Nerve Injury Forces Wyndham Showdown

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | August 2, 2026

Brooks Koepka will not play the Rocket Classic this week. His withdrawal, confirmed on the weekend, came with a sobering diagnosis: an ulnar nerve injury affecting the grip sensation in his left hand. The injury, which had already caused him problems at the RBC Canadian Open earlier in the year, has now cost him 500 FedExCup points he could not afford to miss.

At 84th in the FedExCup standings, Koepka needed to play and needed to play well. He did neither. He will leave Detroit with no points and a narrowing window to qualify for the playoffs.

Riley's Win Reshapes the Picture

While Koepka watched from the sidelines, Davis Riley was rewriting his own FedExCup story. Riley entered the week 146th in the standings — deeply outside the top 70 cut line — and won the tournament outright with a final-round 63. Five hundred points awarded to the winner means Riley vaults into approximately the 50-60 range, going from bubble casualty to playoff participant in the span of a single Sunday.

The contrast tells the story of what one week at the right moment can do in this format. Riley will be at TPC Southwind in Memphis for the FedEx St. Jude Championship. Koepka's playoff fate hinges on Greensboro.

The Injury Context

Koepka's ulnar nerve issue is not new. The injury forced him out of the final round of the RBC Canadian Open earlier in 2026. He has since dealt with its effects through missed cuts at the US Open and the Genesis Scottish Open, and managed a T-28 at The Open Championship. The nerve affects grip sensation and feel — qualities that are non-negotiable for a player competing at the highest level of professional golf.

His withdrawal from the Rocket Classic was not unexpected to those tracking his situation. The question is whether he can compete at a level sufficient to gain enough ground in Greensboro, and whether the hand holds up for 72 holes after a week of rest.

The Full Bubble Picture After Detroit

Jackson Koivun — the 21-year-old who won the 3M Open in just his third professional start — entered the Rocket Classic sitting at approximately 70th in the FedExCup standings, right on the cut line. The exact impact of this week's results on his standing will depend on how he performed in Detroit and how the point distribution filters through.

Tony Finau sat at 87th heading into this week. Keegan Bradley at 76th. Both are outside the line. Jason Day also withdrew from the Rocket Classic, removing his opportunity to move up.

The Wyndham Championship — which begins its competitive rounds on August 6 at Sedgefield Country Club in Greensboro, North Carolina — is the final event of the FedExCup regular season. After its conclusion, the top 70 advance to the FedEx St. Jude Championship. Those outside do not.

A Loaded Greensboro Field

Koepka's confirmed entry into the Wyndham alongside Tony Finau, Jordan Spieth, Justin Thomas, Hideki Matsuyama, and Daniel Berger guarantees that Greensboro will be among the most consequential events of the 2026 regular season. Each of those players brings something to lose or gain.

For Koepka, the calculus is simple: make the cut, post a top-10 or better, and hope the numbers work. He has won five major championships. He knows how to compete under pressure. Whether his nerve injury allows him to do so at Sedgefield Country Club is the central question of the next seven days.
