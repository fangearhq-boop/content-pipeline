AIG Women's Open Final Round: Yealimi Noh Leads at -7 as Ryu Faces a Historic Comeback Challenge

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | August 2, 2026

The final round of the 2026 AIG Women's Open at Royal Lytham & St Annes is underway — and the leaderboard that will produce this year's women's major champion looks nothing like it did 24 hours ago.

Yealimi Noh, the American veteran who has been building towards a major breakthrough for years, stands alone at -7 with a three-shot lead. She made three consecutive birdies in Round 3, starting at the par-3 9th hole, to seize control of a tournament that Haeran Ryu had looked on course to dominate.

Ryu's Round 3 Collapse

Ryu entered Round 3 at Royal Lytham as not just the leader, but as a player chasing history. She had already won the KPMG Championship and the Evian Championship in 2026. A third major would match Inbee Park's 2013 record — the last time any player won three women's majors in a single calendar year.

For long stretches of Saturday's round, that record seemed within reach. Then came an 8-hole stretch in which Ryu made 5 bogeys — a sequence that unravelled her lead completely and left her at -4, three strokes behind Noh heading into Sunday.

Ryu is tied for 2nd with Esther Henseleit, Lucy Li, Jeeno Thitikul, and Shiho Kuwaki. The record is still mathematically possible — but it would require a final-round charge at a links course that has been unpredictable all week.

Noh's Opportunity

For Yealimi Noh, this is a career-defining Sunday. The 29-year-old American has been a consistent presence on the LPGA Tour without winning a major championship. Her three consecutive birdies in Round 3 were the signature sequence of the tournament so far — a run of golf that quietly decided who would lead heading into the final day.

Three shots is a meaningful cushion in a major championship final round, but Royal Lytham's links terrain and the quality of the players chasing her mean nothing is settled. The five-way tie at -4 includes players — Thitikul, Henseleit — who are entirely capable of shooting low on a Sunday.

Korda Out of Contention

The week's most disappointing story belongs to Nelly Korda. Having entered the championship with two major wins already in 2026 — the ANA Chevron Championship and the US Women's Open — Korda was one of the players who could have equalled Park's record. A pair of double bogeys in Round 3, including one on the 18th hole, left her approximately 8 shots off the lead and effectively ended her chances at a third 2026 major.

Korda remains one of the sport's most successful players of the current era. But Sunday's closing holes at Royal Lytham belong to someone else's story.

English Interest Remains

Charley Hull and Lottie Woad — two English players who represent the host nation's best hopes for a home major victory — remain in contention. Both have played consistently enough to be part of the conversation heading into Sunday, though they would need significant movement on a leaderboard that is already tightly contested at the top.

Lytham's history as a host of women's golf is long and storied. The possibility of an English champion on English soil would make for a particularly powerful conclusion to a dramatic final major of 2026.

What Sunday Brings

The AIG Women's Open final round at Royal Lytham & St Annes will decide several narratives simultaneously. For Noh, it is the chance to win a first career major. For Ryu, it is the chance to recover from a collapse and still claim a historic third major of the season. For the five players in the chasing pack, it is simply the chance to play the golf of their careers on the most consequential Sunday in women's golf's calendar.

The $10 million purse — a record for women's major championships — awaits the champion.
