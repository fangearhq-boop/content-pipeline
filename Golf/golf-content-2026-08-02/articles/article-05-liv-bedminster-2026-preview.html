LIV Bedminster 2026: Four Days Away, 13 Major Champions, and a Circuit's Uncertain Future

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | August 2, 2026

LIV Golf's New York event at Trump National Golf Club Bedminster, New Jersey is four days away — and by most measures, it should be one of the circuit's marquee events of 2026. The field of 57 players includes 13 major champions with a combined 23 titles. The venue is arguably the most recognizable on the LIV calendar. The New York market provides national attention and regional passion in volume.

And yet, heading into Bedminster on August 6-9, the questions surrounding LIV Golf's immediate and long-term future remain exactly what they were when the season began.

The Field

The major champion count at Bedminster is legitimate and extensive. Cameron Smith, Jon Rahm, Bryson DeChambeau, Joaquin Niemann, Tyrrell Hatton, Dustin Johnson, Patrick Reed, Phil Mickelson, Bubba Watson, Sergio Garcia — each of them has raised a major trophy, and several of them have done it multiple times. DeChambeau won a US Open. Rahm won a Masters and a US Open. Mickelson has six majors to his name.

The competitive product LIV is able to deliver at Bedminster is not in question. The players at this event represent genuine elite-level talent.

The Financial Question

What is in question is the circuit's structural and financial stability. LIV CEO Scott O'Neil, when asked publicly whether all remaining 2026 events are guaranteed to take place, has declined to commit to a direct answer. That reluctance is not accidental — it reflects the genuine uncertainty inside the organisation.

Two LIV events in 2026 have already been cancelled. The New Orleans event and the Michigan event both fell off the calendar this year. The Saudi Public Investment Fund, which has served as LIV Golf's primary backer since its founding and which has spent more than $5.3 billion in that capacity since 2022, has announced it will exit that funding role after 2026. Bloomberg and Front Office Sports have both reported on LIV's need for outside investment — a gap estimated at $250-350 million or more — that has yet to be fully resolved.

What Comes After Bedminster

The LIV calendar after Bedminster shows one more event: Indianapolis, scheduled for August 20-23. If both events take place as planned, LIV will complete a reduced 2026 schedule. What happens in 2027 and beyond, under a new funding structure or with a different organisational model, is a question the sport is watching closely.

For the players competing at Bedminster, the geopolitical and financial complexities of their circuit's ownership are secondary to the competition at hand. They are professional golfers. They compete for the purse, the team standing, and the individual title. Bedminster, whatever the broader context, remains one of the stronger fields in professional golf for any given week.

A Circuit at a Crossroads

The 2026 season has been, for LIV Golf, a season of contraction and uncertainty. The financial model that sustained the circuit through its launch years is changing. The two cancelled events are visible evidence of strain. O'Neil's guarded language about future events suggests the organisation is operating with less certainty than it once did.

Whether LIV Golf emerges from 2026 with a path forward or enters a period of restructuring and downsizing, the Bedminster event will be watched as a barometer of the circuit's current state. A clean, competitive, well-attended event goes some way toward demonstrating LIV's durability. Anything less adds to the narrative of a circuit under pressure.

Four days. Thirteen major champions. A golf course in New Jersey. And a lot of unresolved questions about what comes next.
