Jon Rahm Has 8 Days to Decide — and His Choice Could Make or Break LIV 2.0

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | October 5, 2026

Jon Rahm won the 2026 LIV Golf individual championship. He is also listed in the organization's bankruptcy filing as the largest unsecured creditor, with a claim of $7,472,527.47 from a Player Participation Agreement. He signed a reported nine-figure deal to join LIV ahead of the 2024 season. He has not yet publicly committed to LIV 2.0.

He has eight days.

The October 13 deadline for player commitments to the BC Partners restructuring deal is the single most important date in professional golf right now, and Rahm's position within that process is unlike any other player's. He is not simply one vote among many. He is the player whose claim value, if committed, contributes most meaningfully to clearing the two-thirds total claim value threshold required for the deal to proceed.

The Financial Complexity

Rahm's $7.47 million claim represents his 2026 Player Participation Agreement obligations — the payments he was owed from LIV for the completed season that the bankruptcy filing potentially interrupts. That number, while significant, is only one part of the picture.

Rahm's original LIV deal reportedly included future guaranteed payments that far exceed the 2026 claim. Those future obligations — often described as a nine-figure commitment — entered the bankruptcy in a different category, and their treatment in the reorganization is not yet fully determined. Whether committing to LIV 2.0 preserves any of those future payments is a question that Rahm's legal and financial advisers are almost certainly working through in the days before October 13.

Committing to the new structure on the wrong terms could mean surrendering leverage he currently holds as a creditor. Not committing risks his claim value not counting toward the threshold, potentially derailing the deal and leaving him as a creditor in a liquidating bankruptcy — a worse outcome than most restructuring scenarios.

The Golf Career Question

Beyond the financial calculus, Rahm faces the question every LIV player is answering differently: what is the right competitive home going forward?

A return to the PGA Tour is complicated. Rahm left in 2024 and has been competing outside the Tour's framework since. The Tour's rules on LIV players — and the ongoing legal and negotiating landscape between the two organizations — mean a clean return is not simply a matter of choosing to rejoin. The path exists, but it is not straightforward, and the disruption to world rankings and major championship eligibility adds additional variables.

LIV 2.0, if it proceeds, represents the most structured competitive home available to him outside a formal negotiated return to the PGA Tour. His record on LIV — individual champion in 2026 — suggests the competitive environment works for his game. The question is whether the new structure offers enough financial and competitive certainty to justify committing when the alternative remains unknown.

What's Next

Eight days. The countdown runs to October 13. Rahm's public statement — or the absence of one by the deadline — will tell the story. Watch for any indication of how the other large creditors (DeChambeau, Dustin Johnson) land in the days ahead: their decisions, combined with Rahm's, will determine whether LIV 2.0 clears the claim-value threshold and emerges from bankruptcy as a going concern. Golf's landscape beyond October 13 depends in large part on what Jon Rahm decides.
