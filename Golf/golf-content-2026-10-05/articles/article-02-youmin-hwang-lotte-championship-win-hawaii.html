Youmin Hwang Birdies the Final Four Holes to Win LOTTE Championship in Hawaii

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | October 5, 2026

Golf produces few moments as electrifying as a closing charge in Hawaii. On Sunday at Hoakalei Country Club in Ewa Beach, Youmin Hwang delivered exactly that, birdying five of her final six holes — including four consecutive birdies to close — to shoot a 5-under 67 and win the 2026 LOTTE Championship at 17-under par 271. The 22-year-old South Korean, competing on a sponsor exemption, won by one stroke over Hyo Joo Kim to claim her first LPGA Tour title.

It was a finish that required Hwang to make nearly everything she looked at when the tournament was on the line, and she did exactly that. The four straight birdies to close — each one tightening the screws on players who had spent the first 54 holes seemingly in control — told the story of a player ready for a moment that most players wait years to encounter.

What Made Sunday Remarkable

Jeeno Thitikul had entered the final round with a three-shot lead, the world watching, and every expectation that the LPGA Tour's most consistent performer through the year's middle stretch would convert one more advantage into a title. Thitikul had been excellent through 54 holes, sitting at 17-under and seemingly in command.

Hwang changed that conversation entirely. She had been quietly building throughout the round, and when the birdies started coming on the back nine they did not stop. Four consecutive birdies is not something even the best players in the world do routinely under Sunday pressure. Hwang did it in Hawaii, on a sponsor exemption, in her first LPGA Tour title moment. It was the kind of back-nine that stays in the memory of everyone who watched it.

Hyo Joo Kim finished at 16-under, one shot back. Minami Katsu of Japan took third at 15-under. World No. 2 Nelly Korda tied for fourth. The entire leaderboard presented — and Hwang outplayed all of them when it counted most.

A Career's Opening Chapter

Hwang came to Hawaii on a sponsor exemption — an invitation extended to a player outside the standard tour membership criteria. She will leave Hawaii as an LPGA Tour winner, with the full playing privileges that come with it. At 22, she has announced herself in the most emphatic way available: winning under Sunday pressure at a $3 million tournament in one of golf's most beautiful settings.

The LPGA Tour's field of young players has deepened considerably in recent seasons, with talent from South Korea, Japan, and across Asia producing elite-level results on a regular basis. Hwang joins a generation of players who have pushed the standards of women's professional golf to new heights. This first title is unlikely to be her last.

What's Next

Hwang's victory opens the door to full LPGA Tour membership for the 2027 season and beyond. The LPGA heads into the final stretch of the 2026 calendar with several events remaining, including opportunities for other players to strengthen their Tour Championship positions. For Hwang, the next tournament arrives with a new identity: LPGA Tour winner, and a name the field will now approach differently.
