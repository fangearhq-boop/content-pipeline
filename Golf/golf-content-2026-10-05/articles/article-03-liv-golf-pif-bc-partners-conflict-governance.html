LIV Golf's Path Forward Has a New Problem — Saudi Backers and BC Partners Are Clashing

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | October 5, 2026

LIV Golf appears to be making progress on player commitments ahead of the October 13 deadline. But a different kind of conflict is developing behind the scenes — one that could complicate the path to LIV 2.0 even if every player commitment falls into place.

Reports indicate that PIF, the Saudi Public Investment Fund that originally bankrolled LIV's launch and is exiting the bankruptcy with a $49.6 million debtor-in-possession loan, and BC Partners, the European private equity firm set to become LIV's new primary backer with a $300 million restructuring package, are at odds over the governance structure of the reorganized entity. The disagreement reportedly centers on who exercises control over key operational decisions in LIV 2.0 — team formation, event selection, and the commercial direction of the circuit.

Why This Matters for Players

The LIV 2.0 proposition that has been presented to players is premised on a specific structure: players hold majority ownership, BC Partners holds up to 45%, and PIF exits substantially from ongoing operations. The appeal of this model, for players weighing whether to commit, is the promise of greater autonomy and a circuit that responds to player interests rather than external sovereign priorities.

If PIF retains meaningful governance influence — whether through board representation, veto rights over major decisions, or continued involvement in the commercial side of the operation — the fundamental premise of LIV 2.0 as a player-controlled enterprise shifts. Players who are committing based on that premise may be committing to something materially different from what has been described.

The conflict is not necessarily fatal to the deal. Governance disputes between investors in a restructuring are common, and they often resolve before closing. But the timing — with the October 13 deadline eight days away — adds urgency to a resolution that may not be straightforward.

The Broader Context

LIV's bankruptcy was not simply a financial failure. It was the culmination of a model that placed sovereign investment and event spectacle over the structural foundations of a sustainable circuit. The promise of LIV 2.0 is that the mistakes of the original structure are corrected: real ownership for players, real commercial independence, real long-term viability.

If the conflict between PIF and BC Partners signals that those corrections are more contested than advertised, the deal's attractiveness to the players whose claim value matters most — Rahm, DeChambeau, Johnson — becomes less clear. These are players with leverage, and leverage requires knowing exactly what they are committing to.

What's Next

Eight days remain before the October 13 deadline. How publicly or privately this governance dispute is resolved will determine how players in the undecided column ultimately land. Watch for any formal statements from BC Partners or PIF representatives clarifying the governance structure — or any signals from top players that this conflict has affected their calculations. The next week may redefine what LIV 2.0 actually is.
