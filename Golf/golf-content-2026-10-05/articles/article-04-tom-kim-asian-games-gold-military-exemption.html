Tom Kim Wins Asian Games Gold — and Secures the Most Important Victory of His Career

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | October 5, 2026

Tom Kim has won tournaments on the PGA Tour. He has finished in the top five at major championships. He has established himself as one of the most compelling young players in professional golf. But when he closed out an 18-under par total at Kasugai Country Club with a final-round 6-under 64 and won the 2026 Asian Games gold medal by three strokes, he won something that will shape the rest of his career in ways that no PGA Tour title could match.

Under South Korean law, adult male citizens are required to complete approximately two years of mandatory military service. Athletes who win gold at the Asian Games are granted an exemption from that requirement — a provision that has had profound implications for several of the country's top athletes, and one that Kim has been playing his entire early professional career with in the background of every tournament.

The gold resolves that question entirely. Tom Kim can now play professional golf uninterrupted through the prime years of his career.

The Performance Itself

Kim arrived at Kasugai as a co-leader entering the final round and separated himself with a dominant close. A 6-under 64 — clean, precise, and authoritative — produced a three-stroke win over the field. At 18-under for the tournament, Kim did not simply avoid the pressure of the moment; he turned it into something that looked almost routine.

That composure under pressure has been a hallmark of Kim's game throughout the 2026 PGA Tour season. He won the Genesis Scottish Open earlier in the year and finished solo third at the U.S. Open — a season that placed him 14th in the FedExCup standings with nine top-25 finishes and four top-10s. He is 24 years old and already producing results that put him among the top tier of the game's active players.

The Withdrawal and What's Next

Kim withdrew from the Baycurrent Classic in the days following his Asian Games win. The decision was reasonable — a gold medal in a high-pressure event followed immediately by a return to Tour competition is a recipe for physical and mental fatigue, and Kim was right to give himself space before his next start.

When he returns to the PGA Tour, he does so as a player whose long-term availability is no longer a question mark. The golf world has seen South Korean players manage their careers around the military service question for a generation. Kim now joins the growing list of those who resolved it through gold.

What's Next

Kim will return to the PGA Tour schedule in the coming weeks as the fall season continues. With the FedExCup Fall providing opportunities to build toward 2027 exemption status, his focus returns to the regular competitive calendar. The military service question — which had shaped his strategic thinking about when and how to compete internationally — is now permanently resolved. His next chapter starts from a position of complete professional freedom.
