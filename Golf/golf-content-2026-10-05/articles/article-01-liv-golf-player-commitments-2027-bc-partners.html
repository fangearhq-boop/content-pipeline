LIV Golf Secures Enough Player Support to Proceed with BC Partners Restructuring

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | October 5, 2026

Eight days before the October 13 deadline, LIV Golf has reportedly secured enough player verbal commitments to clear the count threshold required for the BC Partners restructuring deal to move forward. The development represents the most significant positive news for LIV's future since the organization filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection on September 8.

The BC Partners deal — which would provide $300 million in total financing and reshape the organization as LIV 2.0 with greater player ownership and operational independence — requires two distinct commitment thresholds to be met. The first is a count threshold: 50% of players with claims must agree to participate. The second is a claim-value threshold: the collective value of the claims held by committing players must equal or exceed two-thirds of total player claims. Sources indicate LIV is "comfortably above" the count requirement as of early October.

What the Deal Offers Players

LIV 2.0 as structured would represent a substantially different proposition from the original league. Players would hold majority ownership of the new entity, with BC Partners holding up to 45%. The circuit would run ten events in 2027. Commercial rights and decision-making authority — areas where the original Saudi-backed model offered players little influence — would transfer significantly to the player-ownership group.

For players who joined LIV originally on the strength of guaranteed contracts, the appeal of LIV 2.0 is more complicated. Those contracts no longer exist in the same form — they became claims in the bankruptcy. Whether committing to the new structure preserves any of that original financial promise depends on how the bankruptcy court ultimately rules on player contract obligations and what BC Partners is willing to honor as part of the exit financing.

The Claim-Value Threshold Remains the Key Question

The count threshold, while an encouraging signal, is the easier of the two hurdles. The claim-value threshold — two-thirds of total claim value — is where the decisions of the highest-earning LIV players carry the most weight. Jon Rahm, as the largest individual unsecured creditor at $7.47 million, holds a position of unusual leverage in this calculation. Bryson DeChambeau and Dustin Johnson, also listed among the largest creditors, similarly affect whether the deal clears on claim value even if it clears on count.

The October 13 deadline is not a soft target. If the deal does not meet both thresholds by that date, the BC Partners financing — and with it, the entire LIV 2.0 framework — is at risk of collapsing. The next eight days may be the most consequential in professional golf's recent history.

What's Next

Players must deliver formal commitments — not just verbal indications — to meet the legal threshold by October 13. The bankruptcy court in New Jersey continues to oversee the process. The decisions of several undecided top players in the coming days will determine whether LIV Golf emerges from bankruptcy as a going concern or enters the final stages of dissolution. The count threshold being cleared is progress. The deal is not done yet.
