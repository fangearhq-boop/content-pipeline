Europe and USA Deadlocked at 8-8 — Sunday Singles Will Decide the 2026 Solheim Cup

By Ryan Calloway | September 13, 2026

After two days of foursomes and fourballs at Bernardus Golf in the Netherlands, the 2026 Solheim Cup is perfectly tied at 8-8 — with 12 singles matches on Sunday determining which team claims the trophy.

It came down to this: 8-8, 12 singles matches, and the Solheim Cup trophy sitting in a glass case waiting to be lifted by the team that gets to 14 points first.

The 2026 edition of women's golf's premier team event reached its deciding day Sunday at Bernardus Golf in Cromvoirt, Netherlands — and the scoreline could not be more balanced. Europe held a 7-5 lead heading into Saturday afternoon, then watched the United States storm back to erase the deficit with three consecutive fourball victories and tie the match at 8-8. Now it is Sunday, and 12 singles matches will determine the outcome.

How It Got to 8-8

Saturday told two contrasting stories. In the morning foursomes, Europe was dominant — winning the session 3-1 to extend a lead built on the first day's play. Maja Stark and Linn Grant beat Nelly Korda and Allisen Corpuz 3&2, ending Korda and Corpuz's five-match winning streak in foursomes competition. After Saturday morning, Europe led 7-5 and appeared to be pulling away.

What followed in the afternoon fourballs was a complete reversal. The United States won the final three matches of the session to close an 8-5 deficit and level the score. It was a remarkable recovery — one that required USA captain Angela Stanford's players to win three straight while Europe's players ran out of momentum. By the end of Saturday, Stanford's team had saved itself.

The Korda Question

One of the most significant decisions of the week was Stanford's choice to bench Nelly Korda — the World No. 1 ranked player in the LPGA — for Saturday afternoon's fourballs. Korda had been part of the foursomes loss to Stark and Grant that ended her partnership with Allisen Corpuz's unbeaten streak. Stanford rested her, along with Alison Lee, to manage fatigue going into Sunday.

The calculation is straightforward: Korda is too important for Sunday singles to be worn down by a third consecutive session. Europe captain Anna Nordqvist made similar decisions throughout the week. Both captains are managing the week as a three-day event, not a two-and-a-half-day one.

Korda is back for Sunday's singles. Her match is one of the 12 that will decide the trophy.

Sunday's Stakes

Fourteen points wins the Solheim Cup. With the score at 8-8, both Europe and the United States need 6.5 points from 12 singles matches — meaning a win in seven singles matches claims the trophy, assuming the other five are halved. Every match matters. A player who wins her singles contributes a full point; a halved match contributes half a point to each side.

Charley Hull of Europe leads off against Megan Khang of the United States. The order of play from there sets up a sequence of individual battles between players who have spent the week in team sessions — now facing each other one-on-one with the trophy on the line.

The Solheim Cup has not been decided on the first hole of singles or the last. It is decided across 12 matches, over the course of a Sunday afternoon that tends to produce moments nobody planned for.

What's Next

By Sunday evening at Bernardus Golf, the 2026 Solheim Cup will have a winner. Whether it is Europe — defending the match result from recent editions and playing on a continent with intense local support — or the United States — which showed the resilience in Saturday afternoon's comeback to suggest this team is not finished — depends entirely on what happens in those 12 singular matches. Captain Anna Nordqvist and Captain Angela Stanford have spent the week making decisions. Now their players make theirs.
