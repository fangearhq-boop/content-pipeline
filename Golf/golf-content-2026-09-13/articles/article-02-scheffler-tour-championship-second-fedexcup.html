Scheffler Fires 66 to Win Tour Championship — Joins Woods and McIlroy With Second FedExCup Title

By Jake Torres | September 13, 2026

Scottie Scheffler came from three shots back to close a final-round 66 at East Lake and win the 2026 Tour Championship by three shots — claiming the $10 million FedExCup bonus and cementing his status as the season's dominant player.

The list reads: Tiger Woods. Rory McIlroy. Scottie Scheffler.

Those are the only three players in the history of the FedExCup to win the season-long points competition more than once. Scheffler joined that group Sunday at East Lake Golf Club in Atlanta, shooting a final-round 66 to win the Tour Championship by three shots over Viktor Hovland and claim his second FedExCup title — adding the $10 million bonus to a season that was already the most dominant of his career.

The Final Round

Scheffler started Sunday three shots behind the leader — not the position a player wants at East Lake when the competition is as deep as it was in 2026. He responded by playing some of the cleanest golf of his season. His 66 covered the back nine particularly well, and by the time the field began to feel the pressure of the final holes, Scheffler had built a cushion that proved unassailable.

Viktor Hovland finished second at 13-under par — three shots behind Scheffler's winning total of 16-under. Ryan Gerard was third, four shots back. The margin at the top reflected the precision Scheffler brought to the final round: controlled, unhurried, exactly as much as was needed.

Historical Company

The FedExCup launched in 2007, and Tiger Woods won its inaugural edition, then repeated in 2009. Rory McIlroy became the second multiple winner — claiming the title in 2016, 2019, and 2022. McIlroy's three titles still stand as the FedExCup record.

Scheffler's first FedExCup came in 2024. Now, two years later, he has matched the milestone that only Woods had reached before McIlroy's run — and he did it in a season marked by disruption across professional golf. The PGA Tour has been navigating its relationship with LIV Golf's aftermath, the schedule has shifted, and the player environment in 2026 looks different from any previous FedExCup era. Scheffler won anyway.

McIlroy Calls It

Rory McIlroy, who won the Masters in 2026 to add to his own considerable résumé, was direct in his assessment of Scheffler's season. McIlroy publicly stated that Scheffler should be the PGA Tour's Player of the Year for 2026 — an endorsement that carries weight from a player who knows what elite seasons look like from the inside.

"He's been the best player this year," McIlroy said. That sentence carries the full weight of the season Scheffler has put together — multiple wins, consistent contention, and now the Tour Championship and FedExCup to close it out.

What's Next

The 2026 PGA Tour season is complete with the Tour Championship result. Scheffler's second FedExCup title closes a chapter that will be written into the record books alongside Woods and McIlroy — the only names that precede his on that short list. The fall schedule begins next, and the question heading into the offseason is whether Scheffler's dominance continues into 2027 or whether the field finds a way to close the gap. Based on 2026, there is no obvious answer to that question.
