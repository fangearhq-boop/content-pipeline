Jon Rahm Tees It Up at the Irish Open — The Question Now Is What Comes Next

By Jake Torres | September 13, 2026

One week after LIV Golf's Chapter 11 bankruptcy terminated all 48 player contracts, Jon Rahm — who led LIV's 2026 individual standings — is competing at the Amgen Irish Open alongside Rory McIlroy. The wave of player movement has begun.

Jon Rahm is back on the DP World Tour.

His appearance this week in the field at the Amgen Irish Open — a European Tour event that also features Rory McIlroy — is the first visible consequence of LIV Golf's Chapter 11 bankruptcy filing on September 8. All 48 LIV player contracts were terminated when the filing landed. Rahm, who led LIV Golf's 2026 individual standings across a season that ran February through August, found himself without a league contract for the first time in three years.

The Irish Open is where he started.

What Rahm Was Owed

Court documents filed in the LIV Golf bankruptcy proceedings identify Rahm as the No. 1 creditor among players. He is owed $7.5 million in unpaid prize money and contractual obligations — money that is now an unsecured claim in a bankruptcy proceeding that has $500 million in total debt and between $100 million and $500 million in assets.

Whether Rahm recovers that $7.5 million, and on what timeline, depends on how the bankruptcy resolves — through the "LIV Golf 2.0" restructuring plan backed by BC Partners, or through some other outcome. In the meantime, Rahm is not waiting. He is playing golf where he can play golf, which this week means the Amgen Irish Open.

The PGA Tour Question

Before the bankruptcy, PGA Tour commissioner Brian Rolapp offered Rahm, Bryson DeChambeau, and Cameron Smith reinstatement through the Returning Member Program — the same path Brooks Koepka used to return to the tour. The Koepka deal required a $5 million charitable contribution, no equity grants for five years, and no bonus pool participation in year one. All three declined the offer before the September 8 filing.

Now that all LIV contracts are terminated, the question of whether the PGA Tour will offer a new reinstatement path — and on what terms — is unresolved. Rolapp has not announced updated terms since the bankruptcy. The landscape has changed: LIV is no longer an active competitor in the same way it was when the offer was made. Whether the Tour uses that change to soften or harden its reinstatement position will define what happens next for Rahm, DeChambeau, Smith, and the other marquee names whose LIV contracts no longer exist.

The DP World Tour Option

Rahm never surrendered his DP World Tour membership when he joined LIV, and his Irish Open appearance confirms it remains active. The European circuit — rebranded to DP World Tour — has historically been more accommodating to LIV players than the PGA Tour, and with its own scheduling and playing opportunities, it represents a viable short-term home for players navigating the post-LIV transition.

Rory McIlroy's presence in the same Irish Open field adds symbolic weight to the moment. McIlroy has been one of the most consistent voices calling for professional golf's reunification — and his willingness to play the same event as Rahm, now that Rahm is no longer a contracted LIV player, suggests the temperature in the game may be changing.

What's Next

Rahm gave blunt public comments about LIV's future in interviews following the bankruptcy. Whether those comments harden his position against LIV 2.0, or whether the player-owned restructuring plan changes his calculus, is not yet known. What is known is that the 12 months following LIV Golf's bankruptcy will see more player movement than any comparable period in professional golf's recent history. Rahm teeing it up at the Irish Open is the first chapter of that story — not the last.
