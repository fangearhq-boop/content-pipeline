Rahm Out of LIV 2.0 — But His PGA Tour Return Won't Come Before 2027

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | October 20, 2026

Jon Rahm will not play in LIV Golf 2.0. His lawyer has confirmed that the proposed terms from the league's restructured format are "unacceptable," ending any speculation that the 2023 Masters champion would extend his association with the breakaway circuit into its next chapter. But the logical next step — a return to the PGA Tour — carries conditions that make a quick comeback impossible.

The Timeline Is Longer Than Reported

Under current PGA Tour regulations, Rahm remains ineligible for Tour events until late August 2027 at the earliest. That timeline would have been shortened had Rahm accepted a special reinstatement opportunity the PGA Tour offered him earlier in 2026. He declined. The reason he gave was not made public, but the decision extended his suspension by the full reinstatement period.

Earlier reports suggested Rahm was close to a return deal involving significant financial penalties and a public acknowledgment of the Tour's rules — a process that some in the golf media characterized as an "apology tour." Golf Digest reported those accounts as inaccurate, noting that Rahm remained under contract with LIV Golf at the time of those reports. With the legal separation now progressing, the path forward is clearer but slower than the speculation suggested.

The DP World Tour Path

There is one door that Rahm left open. In May 2026, he reached a conditional release with the DP World Tour that preserves his eligibility for the 2027 Ryder Cup at Adare Manor in Ireland. For a player who grew up in Spain and who counts the Ryder Cup as one of professional golf's most meaningful competitions, that path matters enormously.

The DP World Tour structure allows Rahm to maintain his eligibility for the Ryder Cup team without requiring a PGA Tour card, so long as he meets the Tour's exemption criteria through other eligible events. His former teammate Bryson DeChambeau also remains in the LIV ecosystem for now, having said in June 2026 that he was "not planning on going anywhere."

What It Means for the Tours

Rahm's exit from LIV 2.0 is significant for both sides. For the PGA Tour, it represents a psychological win — one of the sport's best players choosing not to extend his time in the rival format. For LIV 2.0, the loss of Rahm's global brand makes the league's task of maintaining relevance more difficult heading into a transition period.

For the major championships — Augusta, the US Open, The Open Championship, the PGA Championship — the question of Rahm's eligibility will depend on each event's specific rules regarding Tour membership. Augusta has historically been the most welcoming of major champions, regardless of Tour affiliation; other events may require active Tour membership.

What's Next

The November 5 legal proceedings date referenced in earlier reporting relates to the formal separation from LIV Golf, not a PGA Tour reinstatement. The next meaningful milestone for Rahm's Tour return is the late August 2027 eligibility window — at which point he could apply for reinstatement and begin working back through the Tour's exemption priority order. Until then, his competitive calendar will be shaped by whatever non-PGA-Tour events he can access through the DP World Tour arrangement and worldwide major exemptions.
