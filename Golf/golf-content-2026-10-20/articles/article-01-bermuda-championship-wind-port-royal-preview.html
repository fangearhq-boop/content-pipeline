Bermuda Championship Opens Thursday: Wind Will Decide the Winner at Port Royal

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | October 20, 2026

The 2026 Butterfield Bermuda Championship tees off Thursday, October 22 at Port Royal Golf Course in Southampton, Bermuda, and as always, the first thing players check when they land is not the leaderboard — it's the wind forecast. At Port Royal, where the Atlantic trades blow off the ocean with no apology, whoever manages the conditions usually manages the trophy as well.

What Makes Port Royal Different

Port Royal is a Government-owned course built on Bermuda's south shore, and its exposure to the Atlantic Ocean is both its charm and its challenge. On calm days it plays as a fair, scenic layout with several memorable ocean-view holes. On breezy days — which describes most October afternoons in Bermuda — it transforms into a course management examination that punishes anything less than precise execution.

The course's elevation changes and exposed coastal holes create wind tunnels that can add five or six clubs of adjustment to approach shots in the same round. Players who grew up in windy conditions — links-trained Europeans and Irish and Scottish tour regulars — tend to have a statistical edge here that doesn't show up at inland events.

Schenk Defends

Adam Schenk returns to Port Royal as the defending champion. His 2025 title at Bermuda was earned in conditions that filtered out most of the field, and he now knows every nuance of a course that rewards local knowledge. Winning back-to-back at any Tour event is difficult; doing it at a place as unpredictable as Port Royal makes Schenk one of the key names to watch through the first two rounds.

The $6 million purse draws a motivated field, and with the FedExCup Fall season points race in full swing, players fighting for their Tour cards are bringing maximum focus to every event remaining on the calendar.

The Fall Points Race Context

Bermuda is not just about the winner's check — it's one of the more valuable remaining points opportunities in the FedExCup Fall. Points flow to the top 65 finishers, and for players sitting outside the secured-status thresholds, the difference between finishing 30th and finishing 65th this week could determine their playing rights for the 2027 season.

That stakes context lifts the intensity of the entire field, not just the contenders at the top. Watch for aggressive play from the bubble names in the mid-range of the leaderboard through the back nine on Sunday.

What's Next

Tee times go out Thursday, October 22. Rounds 1 and 2 run Thursday and Friday; the cut comes Friday evening. The weekend rounds Saturday and Sunday play under whatever Atlantic conditions October delivers in Bermuda. Final round is Sunday, October 25. Watch the wind forecasts Thursday morning — they will tell you almost as much about the final leaderboard as the first-round scores.
