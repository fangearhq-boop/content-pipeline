FedExCup Fall Points at Bermuda: Who Needs What This Week

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | October 20, 2026

The Butterfield Bermuda Championship is one of the better-known events on the PGA Tour's fall schedule, and for good reason: it is beautiful, it is competitive, and the Atlantic wind makes it unpredictable enough to produce surprising leaderboards. But for a significant portion of the field teeing up Thursday at Port Royal, the stakes of the FedExCup Fall points race are more motivating than any scenic backdrop.

How the Fall Points Race Works

After the FedExCup Playoffs conclude in September, the Tour enters its Fall season — a stretch of events that runs through the RSM Classic in November. Scottie Scheffler won the 2025-26 FedExCup title, but the Fall season is not about competing for the overall Cup. It is about status: the FedExCup Fall standings determine next-season priority rankings for players who did not secure their cards through the regular-season or playoff routes.

Points are distributed to the top 65 finishers at each Fall event. Finishing in those 65 spots provides ranking points; the cumulative totals determine where a player sits in the priority order for Tour eligibility and event access in 2027. For players on the margin between keeping their cards and losing them, a top-40 finish at Bermuda could be a season-defining moment.

Why Bermuda Matters for Bubble Players

The Bermuda Championship's $6 million purse distributes meaningful prize money well down the leaderboard, and the corresponding FedExCup points follow the same curve. A player who finishes No. 60 in a 65-point payout still receives ranking points that can move the needle on their status calculations.

Port Royal's wind also creates a built-in scoring variance — the difference between a 67 and a 73 can depend as much on when a player's tee times fall in relation to the day's weather window as on raw skill. That means even players who are not at peak form can benefit from favorable conditions. The unpredictability of the course works in favor of the bubble players who are grinding for every point.

The Broader Picture

After Bermuda, the remaining Fall events include the VidantaWorld Mexico Open (October 29 – November 1), the World Wide Technology Championship (November 5-8), and the RSM Classic (November 22). Each one adds to the Fall standings before the season closes. Players with comfortable leads in the Fall points race can approach Bermuda freely; those in danger will be keeping one eye on the leaderboard and another on the standings tracker all weekend.

What's Next

First-round tee times begin Thursday, October 22. The cut falls after 36 holes on Friday. The final round is Sunday, October 25. For full Fall standings context, the PGA Tour's FedExCup Fall leaderboard is updated in real time throughout each event. Watch the Sunday back nine for the bubble players — they will be the most compelling storyline once the leaders separate from the pack.
