28 Years. 12 Consecutive Losses. Can Geoff Ogilvy's International Team Finally End It?

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | September 22, 2026

When Geoff Ogilvy walks into Medinah Country Club this week as International team captain, the weight he is carrying is measurable. Twenty-eight years. Twelve consecutive Presidents Cup losses. A record of 2-13-1 against a United States team that has built this biennial event into a victory lap. The last time the International team lifted the Presidents Cup, 1998, Tiger Woods had not yet won his first major. The 2006 US Open champion now serves as captain of a team that cannot win.

The Record in Full

The International team's last Presidents Cup victory came at Royal Melbourne in 1998. The result was dominant — a 20.5 to 11.5 win that represented the high-water mark of International Presidents Cup history. Since then: losses in 2000, 2003, 2005, 2007, 2009, 2011, 2013, 2015, 2017, 2019, 2022, and 2024. Twelve consecutive. The USA all-time record stands at 13 wins, 2 losses, and one draw.

The International record is 2-13-1. That is the mathematical summary of a structural disadvantage that has persisted across generations of players, multiple captains, different formats within the competition, and venues on three continents.

Ogilvy's Roster

What makes this year's International team different — what makes observers willing to say it might be the best-assembled squad in a decade — is the combination of proven winners and emerging talent that Ogilvy has put together.

Hideki Matsuyama is the centerpiece. The 2021 Masters champion has a major on his resume, experience competing and winning on American soil in championship conditions, and the kind of ball-striking game that translates to any format. In fourball, his birdie rate is a weapon. In foursomes, his consistency is a foundation. Ogilvy will build his pairings strategies around Matsuyama the way USA builds around Scottie Scheffler.

Tom Kim and Min Woo Lee represent the next generation. Both are World Golf Rankings top performers. Both have the shot-making range required to compete in alternate-shot foursomes. Adam Scott, who has played in more Presidents Cup matches than almost any active player, brings the tactical experience that young rosters need in team competition. Corey Conners and Sungjae Im round out a side that, on paper, should be capable of winning more than half its matches over the week.

The Question Behind the Talent

Talent has not been the International team's problem for years. The Presidents Cup field has included Matsuyama before. It has included Adam Scott, Louis Oosthuizen, and other proven major champions. The problem is not talent. The problem may be something harder to measure.

Twelve consecutive losses carry weight. Players who have been part of those losses know what it feels like to arrive at a Presidents Cup with justified optimism and leave having lost again. The psychological component of sustained defeat — the conditioning that produces doubt in moments where confidence is required — is not visible in a World Golf Ranking, but it accumulates. Whether Ogilvy's roster, several of whom are playing their first Presidents Cup, can operate without that inherited burden is the central psychological question of the week.

Medinah presents its own reminder: in 2012, Europe came back from 10-6 down to win the Ryder Cup 14.5 to 13.5 on Sunday. Dramatic results on the 18th green are not impossible here. Ogilvy was a player on that day. He knows the venue's capacity for drama.

What's Next

Pairings for Thursday's five fourball matches will be announced Wednesday. Ogilvy's first strategic decision — how to pair Matsuyama, and whether to load the early matches or save players for Sunday singles — will offer the first signal of what kind of captain he plans to be. For the International team, Medinah is simply the next chance. Twenty-eight years is a long time to wait for it.
