Nelly Korda and 17 Solheim Cup Players Return at Walmart NW Arkansas

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | September 22, 2026

The LPGA Tour resumes Thursday at Pinnacle Country Club in Rogers, Arkansas, for the 2026 Walmart NW Arkansas Championship. The 144-player field includes 17 players returning from the 2026 Solheim Cup, held earlier this month in the Netherlands, where Europe defeated the United States 15 to 13. For that group, Thursday is their first competitive round since representing their countries in team golf. For Nelly Korda, it is a chance to extend a 2026 season that has been exceptional on the individual level even as the team result stung.

Korda in 2026

Korda is the LPGA world No. 1 and has held that ranking through a season that includes victories at the 2026 Chevron Championship and the 2026 US Women's Open. Her individual résumé this year is as strong as anyone on Tour. At the Solheim Cup, she went unbeaten in singles — defeating rookie Mimi Rhodes 4 and 2 on Sunday to finish the week without a loss in her individual matches. The USA still lost the team competition 13 to 15, but Korda's personal performance during the event was not a factor in that result.

At Pinnacle Country Club, Korda returns to stroke play with two major wins behind her in 2026. This is the environment where her consistency and world ranking carry weight. The 54-hole, no-cut format at NW Arkansas rewards players who arrive in form and maintain it across three rounds. Korda qualifies on both counts.

The Solheim Cup Context

Seventeen players in the NW Arkansas field participated in the 2026 Solheim Cup. That is a significant portion of the 144-player field, and it makes this week's tournament the first major gathering of those players since the team competition ended. Whether the Solheim experience energizes players or leaves them mentally flat varies by individual — some players thrive coming off the adrenaline of team competition, while others need more time to reset before returning to stroke play.

The players who were on the European side at the Solheim arrive as winners. For the Americans who went 13-15, the return to individual competition is also a return to an environment where they control their own results. That shift in mental framing matters, and how quickly individual players make that transition will influence Thursday's scoring.

The Event

This is the 20th year of the Walmart NW Arkansas Championship at Pinnacle Country Club. The event has built a genuine tournament culture in Rogers over two decades — a golf community that supports the LPGA visit in a way that goes beyond a venue rental. The $3 million purse draws a full field of 144 players, and the 54-hole, no-cut format means every player in the field has competitive relevance through the final round Sunday.

The event runs concurrently with the Presidents Cup at Medinah, which means the golf weekend ahead — Thursday through Sunday — offers both the team competition in Illinois and the LPGA in Arkansas. For the sport as a whole, having both competitions in the same week creates a scheduling reality where casual fans may divide their attention. For the players at NW Arkansas, the job this week is simple: get through 54 holes at Pinnacle with the lowest score possible.

What's Next

The Walmart NW Arkansas Championship runs September 25-27 at Pinnacle Country Club. Thursday marks the first round. Nelly Korda, as world No. 1 and the LPGA's most prominent active player, will be the name that draws the most attention across the 144-player field. But the Solheim participants around her — winners and runners-up from the Netherlands — will collectively tell the story of how that team competition landed once the individual competition resumed.
