LIV Golf Has 21 Days to Find Out If Its Restructuring Plan Survives

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | September 22, 2026

LIV Golf filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on September 8, 2026. Since then, the clock has been running. The restructuring plan requires 50 percent of players by count and two-thirds of total dollar-value claims to commit by October 13 — a 35-day window from the filing date. As of September 22, 21 days remain.

Who Has Committed

The three biggest names attached to significant dollar claims have all publicly committed to the restructured entity. Cameron Smith was most direct: "I have made my bed." Jon Rahm and Bryson DeChambeau both declined the PGA Tour's one-time reinstatement window, which closed February 2, 2026, and both have confirmed their intention to stay with a restructured LIV.

Court filings indicate Rahm is owed more than $100 million from LIV Golf. DeChambeau is also listed as a creditor with a significant claim. Their presence in the restructured entity matters not just as marquee names but as the players who likely account for a disproportionate share of the two-thirds dollar-value threshold the plan requires to proceed.

Who Left

Brooks Koepka returned to the PGA Tour before the bankruptcy filing. He was one of the four players — alongside Rahm, DeChambeau, and Smith — who defined LIV Golf's early identity after signing the largest reported contracts. His departure removes a significant figure from the restructured roster. Whether other players with smaller individual claims follow Koepka back to the PGA Tour before October 13 will determine whether the 50 percent numerical threshold is reachable.

What the Restructured Entity Looks Like

The deal as currently structured is materially different from what players originally signed. BC Partners will provide $300 million in new financing. The Public Investment Fund of Saudi Arabia — LIV's original backer — will fully exit as a direct investor. The restructured tour would be majority player-owned. PIF would receive a 2 percent revenue share for seven years and a future expansion team right, in exchange for transitioning out of direct investment.

That shift — from sovereign wealth fund-backed to player-owned — is the most significant structural change in the plan. Whether it makes the restructured entity more sustainable, or simply less well-funded than it was at launch, is a question October 13 will not fully answer. The deadline answers whether the plan proceeds at all, not whether it succeeds once it does.

The Numerical Challenge

The 50 percent by count threshold is the one that carries the most uncertainty. Rahm, DeChambeau, and Smith represent enormous dollar value, and their commitments likely go a long way toward satisfying the two-thirds dollar-value requirement. But the 50 percent by count threshold requires that enough individual players — many of whom signed smaller contracts and whose individual claims are more modest — also commit to the plan.

Those mid-tier roster members have more options than their higher-profile counterparts. Players with smaller LIV contracts and shorter track records of superstar status on the PGA Tour are the ones the plan needs numerically but who have less financial exposure to the bankruptcy's outcome. Their calculation between committing to a restructured LIV or pursuing Tour reinstatement may ultimately determine whether October 13 produces a plan or a collapse.

What's Next

October 13 is the hard deadline. Between now and then, the restructuring advisors will be working through the roster to secure commitments. The Presidents Cup this week is not directly connected to the LIV timeline, but it represents a visible reminder of what life on the PGA Tour looks like — Koepka, who returned before the filing, will not be in the field, but the Tour's best players will be competing at Medinah. That context is not lost on anyone watching the LIV deadline approach.
