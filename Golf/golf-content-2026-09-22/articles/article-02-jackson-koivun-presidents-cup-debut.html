Jackson Koivun Won the 3M Open in His Third Start. Now He's at Medinah.

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | September 22, 2026

There are 12 players on the USA Presidents Cup roster, and none of them arrived at Medinah Country Club quite like Jackson Koivun. He is 21 years old. He turned professional after leaving Auburn University earlier this year. He had not competed in a single PGA Tour event before 2026. In his third professional start, he won the 3M Open, posted 25-under 259 across 72 holes, set the tournament's scoring record, and defeated world No. 1 Scottie Scheffler by three strokes.

By his fifth start on Tour, he had qualified for the FedExCup Playoffs. By September 1, he was a captain's pick for the Presidents Cup.

How Brandt Snedeker Explained It

USA captain Brandt Snedeker did not reach for statistics when asked to explain the Koivun selection. He went with instinct. "I just have a gut feeling," Snedeker said. The remark has circulated widely in golf coverage this week, partly because it is refreshingly honest and partly because the performance record attached to that gut feeling is difficult to argue with.

A 72-hole tournament scoring record at the 3M Open. A three-shot margin over the best player in the world. A FedExCup Playoffs berth in five starts. Snedeker's gut feeling, it turns out, had a fairly compelling evidentiary base.

The Historical Context

Koivun will be the youngest American at the Presidents Cup since Jordan Spieth was selected in 2015. That comparison is not as favorable to Koivun as it might appear — Spieth at that point was a defending Masters champion and US Open runner-up, already established as one of the best players in the world. Koivun has five PGA Tour starts to his name.

What makes the comparison work in Koivun's favor is the result he produced in those five starts. He is one of three players on the USA roster who were not born the last time the International team won the Presidents Cup, in 1998. The weight of that history does not appear to register on him the way it might for an older player. That may be exactly why Snedeker wanted him.

What Thursday Could Look Like

The Presidents Cup fourballs format — where both partners play their own ball and the better score counts — suits a player who hits a lot of birdies. Koivun shot 25-under at the 3M Open. His ball-striking statistics from his five Tour starts suggest a player who is already operating at a level most rookies do not reach until their second or third season. In fourball, a partner who is making birdies puts pressure on the opposition even when the partner is not otherwise contributing. Koivun could be that partner — or he could be the player winning the hole outright.

Snedeker has not announced pairings. Those come Wednesday. But whoever lines up alongside Koivun on Thursday morning at Medinah will have a partner who has not yet learned to be intimidated by the moment, simply because he has not been in enough of them to build that reflex.

What's Next

Pairings for Thursday's five fourball matches will be announced Wednesday. Koivun's Thursday debut at Medinah will be one of the most closely watched moments of the week — not just for what it means to USA's chances, but because he has already proven, in a limited sample, that he plays his best when the competition is at its highest level. Thursday at the Presidents Cup is the highest level he will have faced. The results from his first five starts suggest that might not slow him down at all.
