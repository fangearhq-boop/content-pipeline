The Presidents Cup Starts Thursday. Here Is Everything You Need to Know.

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | September 22, 2026

Practice rounds are underway at Medinah Country Club in Illinois, and the clock is ticking down to the first tee shot of the 2026 Presidents Cup. In 48 hours, five fourball matches will settle onto Course No. 3 at Medinah, beginning what might be the most competitive Presidents Cup in recent memory. USA needs to win for the 11th consecutive time. The International team has been waiting 28 years for their answer.

The Format

Thirty points are available across four days of competition. Thursday opens with five fourball matches, tee times beginning at 11:35 AM CDT. Friday brings five foursomes matches. Saturday returns to five fourball matches. Sunday delivers the twelve singles matches that will settle the result one way or another.

The math is simple: USA needs 15.5 points to retain the Presidents Cup. The International team needs 15.5 to win it outright for the first time since 1998. A draw would also see the Internationals claim the Cup, as the United States holds it going in. Pairings for Thursday's fourballs are expected to be announced Wednesday.

The USA Roster

Brandt Snedeker assembled a squad built around world No. 1 Scottie Scheffler. The automatic qualifiers earned their spots through the FedExCup standings: Scheffler, Cameron Young, Wyndham Clark, Sam Burns, Russell Henley, and Collin Morikawa. Snedeker's six captain's picks were Chris Gotterup, Xander Schauffele, Justin Thomas, Jacob Bridgeman, Patrick Cantlay, and 21-year-old Jackson Koivun.

Koivun is the name generating the most conversation. He won the 2026 3M Open in just his third PGA Tour start, posting 25-under 259 and defeating Scheffler by three strokes. He qualified for the FedExCup Playoffs in his fifth start. He is the youngest American in the Presidents Cup since Jordan Spieth was selected in 2015, and Snedeker was direct about the decision: "I just have a gut feeling." Given what Koivun has done in a matter of months, that feeling has supporting evidence.

The International Roster

Geoff Ogilvy, the 2006 US Open champion, leads an International team that many observers consider the strongest assembled in a decade. Hideki Matsuyama — the 2021 Masters champion — headlines the side. Tom Kim and Min Woo Lee bring youth and world ranking points. Adam Scott, who has played in more Presidents Cup matches than nearly anyone, provides experience Ogilvy will rely on in foursomes pairings. Corey Conners and Ryan Fox round out a squad capable of winning on any given day.

Sungjae Im is also in the field, giving the Internationals multiple proven tour winners who are accustomed to competing under pressure on American soil.

Why Medinah Matters

Medinah Country Club in Illinois is not neutral ground. It is American soil, American fans, and American conditions. On paper, that continues the long-running home-field advantage enjoyed by USA in every Presidents Cup since the event returned from Royal Melbourne in 1998.

But Medinah carries a different kind of history in team golf. This is the course where Europe mounted one of the greatest comebacks in Ryder Cup history, rallying from 10-6 down to win 14.5 to 13.5 in 2012 on a Sunday that changed how the sport thinks about team competition. Geoff Ogilvy was present at the 2012 Ryder Cup as a player. He knows what this course can produce in the right conditions. Whether that knowledge translates into something actionable for his International side remains to be seen.

What's Next

Pairings for Thursday's five fourball matches will be announced Wednesday. The first tee shot at Medinah is scheduled for 11:35 AM CDT Thursday. Television coverage runs on NBC and Golf Channel. For USA, this is an opportunity to extend a run of dominance that has become the defining narrative of Presidents Cup history. For the International team, Thursday morning is simply the next chance to end 28 years of waiting.
