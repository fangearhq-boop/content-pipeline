### STORY 1: Presidents Cup Preview

**FB Long-Form (8:00 AM ET)**
The 2026 Presidents Cup is 48 hours from its first tee shot. Practice rounds are underway at Medinah Country Club in Illinois, and the stage is set for what could be the most anticipated Presidents Cup in years.

Team USA enters with its customary advantages. Brandt Snedeker's squad features world No. 1 Scottie Scheffler, Xander Schauffele, Collin Morikawa, Wyndham Clark, Sam Burns, Cameron Young, Patrick Cantlay, Justin Thomas, Jacob Bridgeman, Chris Gotterup, Russell Henley, and the 21-year-old wildcard Jackson Koivun. But the format is designed for competitive golf. Thirty points are on the table across four sessions: five fourball matches on Thursday, five foursomes on Friday, five more fourballs Saturday, and twelve singles matches Sunday. USA needs 15.5 points to retain the cup. The Internationals need 15.5 to win it outright for the first time since 1998.

Pairings for Thursday's fourballs are expected to be announced Wednesday. The first shots fly at 11:35 AM CDT Thursday.

### STORY 2: Jackson Koivun

**FB Long-Form (10:30 AM ET)**
Jackson Koivun arrived at Medinah as the most improbable Presidents Cup player in recent memory — and possibly the most dangerous.

He is 21 years old. He turned professional just months ago after leaving Auburn University. He had never played a PGA Tour event before this year. In just his third professional start, he walked into the 2026 3M Open and defeated world No. 1 Scottie Scheffler by three strokes, shooting 25-under 259 and setting the tournament's 72-hole scoring record.

By his fifth start on the PGA Tour, he had qualified for the FedExCup Playoffs. By September 1, Brandt Snedeker had named him a captain's pick for the Presidents Cup.

"I just have a gut feeling," Snedeker said. That gut feeling has a 72-hole scoring record and a victory over the best player in the world attached to it.

Koivun will be the youngest American at the Presidents Cup since Jordan Spieth was selected in 2015, and he is one of three players on the USA team who were not even born the last time the Internationals won in 1998. His debut Thursday at Medinah will be one of the most-watched moments of the week.

### STORY 3: LIV Golf

**FB Long-Form (1:00 PM ET)**
There are 21 days left until October 13 — the deadline that will determine whether LIV Golf's restructuring plan survives or collapses.

LIV filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on September 8, 2026. The restructuring plan requires 50 percent of players by count and two-thirds of total dollar-value claims to commit by that October 13 deadline. If the numbers fall short, the plan does not proceed.

The three biggest names among those claims — Jon Rahm, Bryson DeChambeau, and Cameron Smith — have all publicly committed to remaining with a restructured LIV. Smith was most direct: "I have made my bed." Rahm and DeChambeau declined the PGA Tour's reinstatement window, which closed in February 2026.

The structure they are committing to is materially different from what any of them signed originally. BC Partners will provide $300 million in new financing. The Public Investment Fund of Saudi Arabia will fully exit as a backer. The restructured tour would be majority player-owned, with PIF taking a 2 percent revenue share for seven years and a future expansion team right, in exchange for transitioning out of direct investment.

The October 13 deadline is 21 days away. Whether enough players commit — particularly the mid-tier roster members whose individual claims are smaller but who make up the numerical majority — will determine whether LIV Golf re-emerges in 2027 or becomes the sport's most expensive failed experiment.

### STORY 4: International Team's Drought

**FB Long-Form (3:30 PM ET)**
When Geoff Ogilvy walks into Medinah Country Club this week as the International team captain, he carries the weight of 28 years of losing.

The International team has not won the Presidents Cup since 1998, at Royal Melbourne. That team beat the Americans 20.5 to 11.5 in one of the most dominant results in the event's history. Since then, nothing. Twelve consecutive Presidents Cup losses. A run of American dominance so complete that the format — designed as a biennial showcase for global golf talent — has started to feel like an extended victory lap for the United States.

The current USA all-time record is 13 wins, 2 losses, and one draw. The Internationals are 2-13-1.

Ogilvy's 2026 team may be the best-assembled International squad in recent memory. Hideki Matsuyama is in the field — the 2021 Masters champion, a proven winner on American soil in major championship conditions. Tom Kim and Min Woo Lee bring youth and form. Adam Scott, who has played more Presidents Cup matches than almost anyone, brings experience that Ogilvy needs to deploy carefully. Corey Conners and Ryan Fox represent a Canadian and a New Zealander who know how to compete on American courses.

The question is not whether the International team is talented enough to win. The question is whether twelve consecutive losses have accumulated into something psychological that the individual talent cannot overcome.

Medinah is in Illinois. It is American soil, American fans, American weather. And yet Medinah is also where the 2012 Ryder Cup produced one of the most dramatic comebacks in golf history — Europe's comeback from 10-6 down to win 14.5 to 13.5 on a Sunday that redefined the word "collapse." Ogilvy was there. He knows what Medinah can do.

### STORY 5: Walmart NW Arkansas

**FB Long-Form (6:00 PM ET)**
The LPGA season resumes Thursday at Pinnacle Country Club in Rogers, Arkansas, for the 2026 Walmart NW Arkansas Championship — and 17 players in the 144-player field are making their first competitive appearances since the Solheim Cup.

That context matters. The 2026 Solheim Cup took place in the Netherlands earlier this month, with Europe defeating the United States 15 to 13 in a result that sent American golf into reflection. The USA team featured some of the best players in the world — and still lost by two points.

Nelly Korda, the LPGA world No. 1 and winner of both the 2026 Chevron Championship and the 2026 US Women's Open, was one of the few bright spots in the Americans' Solheim campaign. She went unbeaten in singles, defeating rookie Mimi Rhodes 4 and 2 on Sunday. But individual performances do not change a 13-15 team result.

The $3 million purse at NW Arkansas draws a full field of 144 players across the 54-hole, no-cut format. This is the 20th year of the event at Pinnacle Country Club, and the golf community in Rogers has built a genuine tournament week culture around it. For the Solheim participants, it is also the first chance to see how their individual games have held up after two weeks of team golf, travel, and competition.

Korda is the name everyone will track, but the story heading into Thursday is the collective return of a group of players who spent the last two weeks representing their countries. Watch to see whether the Solheim experience energizes them or leaves them flat.
