Karl Vilips Fires Career-Low 62 to Lead VidantaWorld Mexico Open After Round 1

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap

October 30, 2026

Karl Vilips made the most of his Thursday at Vidanta Vallarta, posting a career-low 62 — 9-under par — to take a one-stroke lead after the first round of the VidantaWorld Mexico Open. The Australian closed his round with four consecutive birdies on holes 6 through 9, turning a very good round into a statement.

Kensei Hirata of Japan sits one stroke behind at 8-under. Max Homa carded a first-round 66 to reach 5-under, placing him inside the top 15. Defending champion Brian Campbell is in the field as Round 2 begins Friday.

The Closing Stretch

Vilips's four consecutive birdies on holes 6 through 9 separated the career-low round from the merely competitive ones. Coming home with four straight birdies requires execution under conditions when a round is already going well — when there is something to protect. Vilips did not protect it. He attacked.

The final hole represented the round's signature moment: needing to convert to preserve the four-birdie streak and reach -9, Vilips delivered. The result was a score that will sit at the top of the Mexico Open leaderboard through at least the second round's completion.

A career-low score on the PGA Tour carries meaning beyond any single tournament. It sets a new benchmark for what a player knows they are capable of. For Vilips, Friday's second round begins from a position of demonstrated quality — not just hope.

Hirata and the Leaderboard

Kensei Hirata's 8-under round places him one stroke off the pace — close enough that a low second round would put him in the lead, but far enough that the gap is real. Hirata has shown consistency on the PGA Tour's international circuit and is not the type of player who gives away strokes when contending.

Max Homa's 66 keeps him competitive without being an immediate threat to the leaders. At -5, Homa would need a low second round to position himself for the weekend in serious contention. Still, a player of his caliber at -5 through 18 holes is someone the leaderboard's top contenders are aware of.

Defending champion Brian Campbell adds an element of familiarity to the field. A player who has won at this venue before carries knowledge of the course's demands that first-time contenders do not.

Vidanta Vallarta and the Coastal Conditions

The Vidanta Vallarta course in Puerto Vallarta, Mexico, runs along Mexico's Pacific coast and has historically produced low scoring across multiple PGA Tour editions. The coastal environment creates aerodynamic conditions that differ from inland venues. Wind off the water is a variable; surface conditions respond differently to humidity than mountain courses.

For a player firing a 62, the conditions on Thursday clearly cooperated. Vilips's score suggests the course was accessible in Round 1. Whether Round 2 brings similar conditions or introduces complications is an open question that only the day's play will answer.

What's Next

Round 2 at the VidantaWorld Mexico Open is underway Friday. Vilips leads at -9. The cut will come at the conclusion of Round 2, reducing the field for Saturday and Sunday's final rounds.

Keep an eye on the leaderboard as the day develops. A 36-hole leader at this event entering the weekend in top position would set up a compelling final two days — particularly with Austin Smotherman's three-consecutive-wins storyline running in parallel through the same field.
