Billy Horschel Withdraws from VidantaWorld Mexico Open During Round 2 with Dizziness

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap

October 30, 2026

Billy Horschel withdrew from the VidantaWorld Mexico Open during Round 2 on Friday at Vidanta Vallarta, citing dizziness and lightheadedness. The former FedExCup champion stepped away from the course mid-round, removing a well-known name from the field as the tournament's second day unfolds in Puerto Vallarta.

The Withdrawal

Horschel's decision to withdraw mid-round reflects a health situation serious enough to prevent him from finishing his round. Dizziness and lightheadedness during a round of competitive golf — where walking significant distances across a course in outdoor conditions is required — can impair a player's ability to compete safely and effectively.

Professional golfers occasionally withdraw from tournaments, but mid-round withdrawals due to health concerns are relatively uncommon and tend to generate attention when they involve players of Horschel's profile.

Horschel on the PGA Tour

Billy Horschel has been a fixture on the PGA Tour for more than a decade, known for his competitive intensity and outspoken presence in the sport. He won the FedExCup in 2014 — one of professional golf's most demanding season-long achievements — and has collected multiple PGA Tour victories across his career.

His presence at the VidantaWorld Mexico Open made him a recognizable name in a field defined largely by the Smotherman three-peat storyline. His withdrawal from Round 2 narrows the field and changes the name count at the midpoint of the tournament.

The Remaining Field

Horschel's exit does not alter the leaderboard battle at the top of the Mexico Open, where Karl Vilips leads at 9-under after a career-low 62 in Round 1, with Kensei Hirata one stroke behind. Austin Smotherman remains in the field, continuing his pursuit of a third consecutive PGA Tour victory.

Vidanta Vallarta sits along Mexico's Pacific coast and is not a high-altitude venue, meaning the environmental conditions differ from mountain-location events. The cause of Horschel's dizziness and lightheadedness was not specified beyond the reported symptoms.

What's Next

Round 2 continues Friday at Vidanta Vallarta. The cut at the conclusion of Round 2 will set the field for the weekend. Horschel's withdrawal means one fewer name for the final 36 holes regardless of how his round was progressing.
