Roussin-Bouchard Carries Eight-Under Lead Into Maybank Championship Round 2

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap

October 30, 2026

Pauline Roussin-Bouchard carried a one-stroke lead from Thursday's opening round into Friday's second round of the LPGA Maybank Championship at the Kuala Lumpur Golf and Country Club. The LPGA star shot an 8-under 64 in Round 1, highlighted by three consecutive birdies on her closing holes, and enters Round 2 with the tournament's best mark at the KLGCC West Course in Malaysia.

Charley Hull sits at 6-under after Round 1, two strokes behind the leader. Lottie Woad is inside the field. Defending champion Miyu Yamashita is in contention. Round 2 is underway Friday in Kuala Lumpur.

Thursday's Statement Round

Roussin-Bouchard's opening 64 set the tone for the Maybank Championship in a particularly compelling way. The round was steady throughout, but its signature came at the end: three consecutive birdies to close the front nine and vault to the top of the leaderboard.

In professional golf, closing birdie streaks signal more than a hot finish. They demonstrate that a player can maintain focus and execution when the round's outcome is clear — when it would be easy to be conservative, protect what you have, and walk in satisfied. Roussin-Bouchard chose to keep attacking instead. The result was a round that put her one stroke clear of the field at a tournament where the caliber of play is among the LPGA Tour's highest of the season.

Hull's Position

Charley Hull's 6-under round places her in a realistic position to contend for the Maybank Championship title. Two strokes is a manageable gap across three remaining rounds — particularly for a player of Hull's experience in professional competition. Hull has been among the most consistent players on the LPGA Tour in recent seasons, and her ability to post low numbers when the conditions are right is well established.

Her Round 2 will be closely watched. A low start Friday closes the gap and repositions her as an equal contender. A difficult start widens the deficit and increases the pressure heading into the weekend.

The Defending Champion

Miyu Yamashita won the Maybank Championship in 2025 and arrives in Kuala Lumpur as the standard-bearer for what the event demands of its champion. The Japanese player's presence in the field adds a layer of historical weight to the competition — the knowledge that this venue has tested top players before and produced a worthy champion.

Yamashita's Round 2 position in the leaderboard will determine whether she enters the weekend as a threat or is chasing from behind. Either scenario tells the story of how well the defending champion has adjusted to the 2026 edition's conditions.

What's Next

Round 2 at the KLGCC West Course is underway Friday. The halfway cut will follow its conclusion, setting the field for Saturday and Sunday. Roussin-Bouchard's position at the top of the leaderboard makes her the player to watch — but the gap to Hull is small enough that two solid rounds from anyone near the top could reshape the tournament entirely.
