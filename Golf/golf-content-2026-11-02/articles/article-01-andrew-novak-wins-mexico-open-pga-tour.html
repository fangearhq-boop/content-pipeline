Andrew Novak Wins Mexico Open at Tournament-Record 25-Under, Claims First Solo PGA Title

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | November 2, 2026

Andrew Novak is a PGA Tour champion — and not just any champion. On Sunday at Vidanta Vallarta, the 31-year-old Wofford College graduate closed out the VidantaWorld Mexico Open at Vidanta by five shots, finishing at a tournament-record 25-under-par 259 to claim his first individual title on the PGA Tour.

The win was sweeping in its final margin, yet it required Novak to pass a genuine test in the back nine of the final round. A six-shot advantage was cut nearly in half as the field made its move — but Novak answered with three consecutive birdies at holes 12, 13, and 14 that effectively ended any suspense about the outcome. He crossed the finish line five shots clear of Steven Fisk and Kristoffer Ventura, who shared the runner-up position.

A Record Stands in Mexico

The 25-under 259 aggregate is the lowest score in the Mexico Open's history at Vidanta Vallarta. Novak set the tournament record while producing one of the more dominant wire-to-wire performances of the PGA Tour's fall swing. His final round, which came under pressure from challengers hoping to capitalize on any wobble, ultimately proved to be the performance of a champion who had been in this position before and knew how to close.

From the $6 million purse, Novak took home $1,080,000 — a career-high payday that reflects the significance of the result.

The Weight of "Solo"

Novak already had a Tour win on his résumé heading into Mexico. At the 2025 Zurich Classic, he and partner Ben Griffin won the pairs event that has become a fixture on the PGA Tour calendar. It was a legitimate victory and one Novak embraced — but a pairs format is different from stepping up alone, and Novak had been working toward that first individual title.

The road to it included a painful near-miss the week before the Zurich win, when Novak lost a playoff to Justin Thomas. He bounced back immediately, and that resilience is a thread that runs through his career. He is not a player who has been handed things, and Sunday's performance reflected everything he has put into his craft as a professional golfer.

Fisk and Ventura made Novak work, and the back nine briefly suggested there might be a late drama worth watching. But Novak's birdie sequence over holes 12 through 14 was the kind of response that separates champions from the field when the moment arrives.

What's Next

The PGA Tour continues its fall schedule with Novak now firmly among the season's most notable winners. For a player who spent years building toward a moment like this, the first solo title has arrived in a way that will be difficult to top — a record score, a five-shot win, and a championship that belongs to him alone.
