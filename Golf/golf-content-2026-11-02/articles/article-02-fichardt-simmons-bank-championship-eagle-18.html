Fichardt Makes Eagle on Final Hole to Win Simmons Bank Championship, Edge Zach Johnson by One

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | November 2, 2026

Golf decided to remind everyone why it is the sport it is on Sunday at the Simmons Bank Championship. Darren Fichardt stood on the 18th tee needing something extraordinary, and he delivered exactly that — an eagle on the final hole of the tournament to win the 2026 Simmons Bank Championship on the PGA Tour Champions circuit by one shot over Zach Johnson.

Fichardt finished at 22-under 194. Johnson, who had been in position to win until that final swing changed everything, had to settle for the runner-up position. Jerry Kelly placed third, finishing eight shots back of the winner.

The Moment That Won It

There are wins on the PGA Tour Champions that arrive with a comfortable cushion and methodical closing rounds, and there are wins like this one. Fichardt's eagle on the 18th was not just a birdie converted on a gettable hole — it was the kind of stroke that shifts an entire scoreboard in an instant. One shot behind or level, the eagle at 18 put Fichardt at the top and left Johnson without a response.

It is the kind of moment that defines a tournament and follows a player through the rest of their career. For Fichardt, it was the perfect punctuation on a round that required him to keep pace in demanding conditions.

Schwab Cup Implications

The Simmons Bank Championship result carries weight beyond the trophy. The PGA Tour Champions season feeds into the Charles Schwab Cup standings, where positioning determines year-end honors and eligibility for the season-closing championship event. Fichardt's victory improves his standing in that race in meaningful fashion.

Johnson, despite the runner-up finish, also added points to his Schwab Cup total. The battle for the season-long title among the over-50 circuit's elite remains active, and Sunday's result altered the landscape.

A Win Worth Watching

The PGA Tour Champions circuit has a way of producing these moments — veterans of the game, playing courses they know and in formats they have mastered, still capable of producing birdies and eagles under the most direct pressure. What Fichardt accomplished at No. 18 is a reminder that competition on the senior circuit is as genuine as anywhere in professional golf.

Kelly's third-place finish eight back did not reflect a poor tournament on his part so much as a statement of how sharp Fichardt and Johnson both were throughout the week. Two elite players, separated by a single eagle, deciding the outcome of a championship on the last hole of the last round. The sport earns its reputation in moments like these.
