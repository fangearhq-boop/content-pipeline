Who Is Andrew Novak? From Wofford College to Solo PGA Tour Champion

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | November 2, 2026

If you were not already familiar with Andrew Novak before Sunday, you are now. The 31-year-old won the VidantaWorld Mexico Open at Vidanta in record-setting fashion — 25-under par 259, five shots clear, his first solo PGA Tour title. But the more interesting question is not what he did this week. It is everything that came before it.

Wofford College Roots

Novak played collegiate golf at Wofford College in Spartanburg, South Carolina — a smaller program that does not appear regularly in conversations about PGA Tour pipeline production. It is a Division I program, but it is not the powerhouse recruiting ground that produces a steady stream of tour players.

That Novak built a professional career from there, worked his way onto the PGA Tour, and developed into a two-time winner is a genuine achievement. The Tour is unforgiving of talent that is not matched by consistency and preparation, and Novak has demonstrated both throughout his career.

The 100th Start — and the Playoff That Preceded It

The story of how Novak arrived at his first Tour win has a remarkable structure to it. The week before the 2025 Zurich Classic, Novak lost a playoff to Justin Thomas. Any professional golfer can tell you how a playoff loss lands — you were that close, and the margin between a win and a loss in that moment is almost nothing.

Novak responded the only way a champion responds: he came back the next week. At his 100th career PGA Tour start — the Zurich Classic — he and partner Ben Griffin won the pairs event. A milestone start, a tournament win, and the kind of story that confirms something about a player's character. That win was genuine, but it came with an asterisk in some conversations: it was a pairs format, not a solo title.

Mexico: The Solo Statement

Sunday's Mexico Open result removes any caveat from Novak's record. He won alone, at a tournament-record score, under pressure on the back nine, and with five shots to spare in the end. The 25-under 259 is the lowest aggregate score in the event's history at Vidanta Vallarta.

For a player who reached his first Tour win at his 100th start and spent years as a mid-level tour grinder, the arc of his career has now taken a genuinely compelling shape. Two wins. A record. A playoff loss turned into a Zurich title turned into a solo championship.

Not every champion arrives in a blaze of early hype. Some are built slowly, and the wins mean more for the patience they required. Novak's story belongs to that second category — and it is one of golf's better stories right now.
