LIV Golf 2026 Season Review: Rahm Wins Individual Title, Legion XIII Claims Team Crown

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | November 2, 2026

The 2026 LIV Golf season is over. When the circuit wrapped its schedule in August and September, it closed out what many observers considered the most competitive and high-profile campaign in the league's short history. Two champions emerged: Jon Rahm as the individual title winner, and Legion XIII as the team champion.

Rahm Delivers

Jon Rahm's decision to join LIV Golf generated enormous controversy and coverage, and 2026 was the season that validated the competitive side of his move. Winning the individual championship on the circuit he had committed his career to proved that Rahm remained one of the best players in the world regardless of which organization held his card.

Rahm is the captain of Legion XIII, the team whose name references the Spanish national soccer side's most prominent tournaments — a connection to his roots. Winning both the individual and team titles in the same season makes 2026 a defining year for him in this chapter of his career.

Legion XIII: Team Champions

The team format is one of LIV Golf's defining structural differences from traditional tour golf. Teams of four compete alongside individual play, and the team standings run separately from the individual race. Legion XIII's championship was not merely an extension of Rahm's performance — it required consistent contributions from across the roster across a full season of competition.

The team title adds a layer of achievement beyond what individual scoring reflects, and Legion XIII's win gives the franchise a claim as the circuit's dominant unit in 2026.

The Bigger Picture

LIV Golf's 2026 season played out against a backdrop of ongoing negotiations between the PGA Tour, the DP World Tour, and the Saudi backers who fund LIV. Those conversations have moved through multiple phases since the initial framework agreement announcement, and the ultimate shape of professional golf's landscape remains unsettled.

What the 2026 season demonstrated is that LIV has developed genuine competitive depth — Rahm had to earn his title against a field capable of winning. The marquee names the circuit attracted have stayed, the events have continued to generate coverage, and the team format has found an audience.

Whether 2027 looks like 2026 or whether some merger framework changes the picture depends on negotiations that neither side has yet concluded. For now, Rahm holds the individual title, Legion XIII holds the team trophy, and LIV Golf's most competitive season is complete.
