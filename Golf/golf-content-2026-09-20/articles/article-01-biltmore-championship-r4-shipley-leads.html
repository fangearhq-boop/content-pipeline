Final Round Today — Shipley (-15) Leads Kohles (-14) by One at the Biltmore Championship

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | September 20, 2026

PGA Tour / Biltmore Championship

Neal Shipley leads the inaugural Biltmore Championship by one shot entering Sunday's final round at -15. Ben Kohles sits at -14. Ricky Castillo and Eric Cole are tied at -11 (T3), four back of Shipley with 18 holes to play. The final twosome tees off at 9:55 AM ET at The Cliffs at Walnut Cove in Asheville, North Carolina, on a course playing with preferred lies for the fourth consecutive round.

The lead was built on a Friday that Shipley will not forget. He shot a career-low 62 in Round 2 — a round that took him from the pack to the front of a tight leaderboard and transformed a weekend storyline. Going into Saturday, Kohles made his own push and reached -14 to keep the Sunday chase competitive. Castillo and Cole represent a real threat from four back if either of the leaders stumbles.

Why This Tournament Is Different

The Biltmore Championship is the first PGA Tour event to be held in western North Carolina in 84 years. The region has been associated with golf through its mountain resort culture for over a century, but the PGA Tour had not returned to this corridor since 1942. The Cliffs at Walnut Cove — a private club course built in the Blue Ridge foothills — made its Tour debut this week in a tournament that carries both historical weight and a $5 million purse.

The winner takes $900,000. Beyond the check, the inaugural champion gets a piece of tournament history. Whoever hoists the trophy on Sunday afternoon will be the first PGA Tour winner in this market in modern professional golf memory.

The Leaderboard Entering Sunday

Shipley's one-shot lead is real but not comfortable. Kohles has been consistent throughout, posting the rounds necessary to stay within striking distance. Castillo entered the week with limited Tour headlines but has been positioned inside the top five since Friday. Cole, the veteran presence in the group, brings a different kind of psychological weight to Sunday's leaderboard.

Preferred lies have shaped this tournament from Round 1. The course conditions — heat, moisture, and the terrain characteristic of the Blue Ridge foothills — made the preferred lie decision early in the week one that affected how aggressively players could attack the fairways. All four rounds are played under the same condition, so Sunday's round will not play differently from a rules standpoint than any previous round this week.

Shipley's Position

Shipley came into the week ranked just outside the top 100 in the FedExCup Fall standings. The Biltmore Championship is part of the FedExCup Fall stretch, where points accumulated count toward the following season. A win here would change his status significantly — both in terms of the immediate payout and the season-long implications for his 2027 Tour card situation.

His 62 was not a course record, but it was the kind of round that moves a player from contender to co-favorite in a single afternoon. Whether he can hold it on Sunday against three players within four shots is the question that closes out the inaugural week at the Biltmore.

What's Next

Final round coverage runs on NBC (noon–12:30 PM ET) and Golf Channel (12:30–3 PM ET). Results will be available as the final groups complete their rounds Sunday afternoon. After the Biltmore Championship wraps, the PGA Tour's attention shifts to the Presidents Cup at Medinah Country Club beginning Thursday, September 24 — where two players from this week's Biltmore field, Jackson Koivun and Maverick Bridgeman, are both members of the USA side.
