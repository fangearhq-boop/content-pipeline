LIV Golf's 23-Day Countdown — BC Partners Needs 2/3 of Dollar Claims and Half the Players by Oct. 13

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | September 20, 2026

LIV Golf / Bankruptcy / Restructuring

October 13 is 23 days away. That date is the deadline set in LIV Golf's Chapter 11 restructuring agreement filed September 8 in New Jersey bankruptcy court — 35 days from the filing. For the BC Partners deal to close, players holding at least two-thirds of all outstanding claim dollars and at least half of all claimants by headcount must formally commit to LIV 2.0 by that date. If the threshold is not reached, the restructuring agreement fails.

The BC Partners deal, if it closes, will fundamentally reshape the circuit that launched in 2022 with Saudi sovereign wealth fund backing. Under the proposed structure, players would collectively own 52.5% of the reorganized LIV. BC Partners and affiliated investors would hold approximately 45%. A management incentive pool accounts for the remaining 2.5%. The Public Investment Fund — PIF, Saudi Arabia's sovereign wealth fund — exits completely. The $49.6 million PIF is providing through a DIP (debtor-in-possession) loan is the extent of its remaining financial involvement. Once the restructuring closes, PIF is out.

The Math That Decides Everything

The restructuring support agreement threshold is not simply a majority vote. It requires two tests to pass simultaneously. The dollar-threshold test — two-thirds of claim value — means that players with larger outstanding claims carry more weight per vote than players with smaller ones. The headcount test — half of all claimants — requires that enough individual players sign regardless of how the dollar values are distributed.

Three players account for a disproportionate share of the dollar-threshold math: Jon Rahm ($7.47 million), Bryson DeChambeau, and Dustin Johnson. The top three creditors together hold approximately $18.7 million in combined claims. Rahm's claim alone, at $7.47 million, represents the largest individual exposure of any single player in the filing. His signature carries more dollar weight in the threshold calculation than any other single player's. His signature alone does not decide the threshold, but his absence from the commitment pool creates a significant hole to fill.

Where Rahm Stands

As of September 15, Rahm has been publicly noncommittal about LIV 2.0. In interviews following his recent comments, he described seeing "opportunity" on the DP World Tour — language that signals active consideration of a return to European Tour golf rather than a default commitment to the restructured LIV circuit. He has since confirmed a formal agreement with the DP World Tour: outstanding fines paid, a commitment to play five DP World Tour events, and Ryder Cup eligibility restored for 2027 at Adare Manor, Ireland.

That agreement does not end his LIV affiliation before October 13. LIV has separately petitioned the bankruptcy court to reject all existing player contracts — a motion that, if granted, would formally sever all contractual relationships between LIV and its players. If the contracts are rejected by the court, Rahm would be free to leave without committing to LIV 2.0. The court has not yet ruled on that petition.

What BC Partners Brings

BC Partners is a European private equity firm with a portfolio history in media, sports, and entertainment businesses. The $300 million investment commitment represents a significant bet on the viability of a restructured professional golf circuit operating outside the PGA Tour ecosystem. The firm's involvement comes with the implicit understanding that LIV 2.0's commercial model — broadcast rights, sponsorships, venue deals, and player compensation — needs to be rebuilt from the ground up without PIF's subsidy cushion.

The player ownership percentage (52.5%) is designed to align incentives. If the tour succeeds commercially, players share in that upside. That structure is different from what LIV offered in its original form, where compensation was guaranteed regardless of competitive or commercial outcomes.

What's Next

The October 13 deadline applies to the restructuring support agreement threshold. A court hearing on the overall Chapter 11 proceedings continues in parallel with the commitment process. If the threshold is met by October 13, the case moves toward a reorganization plan approval — a separate court process that comes after the initial threshold is confirmed. LIV 2.0 is targeting a 2027 season start. Everything between now and October 13 determines whether that timeline is real.
