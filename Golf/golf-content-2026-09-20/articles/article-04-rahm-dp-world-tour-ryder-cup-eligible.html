Rahm's DP World Tour Agreement Is Done — Fines Paid, Ryder Cup Eligibility Restored for 2027

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | September 20, 2026

Jon Rahm / DP World Tour / Ryder Cup

Jon Rahm has reached a formal agreement with the DP World Tour. He has paid his outstanding fines, committed to play five DP World Tour events excluding major championships and co-sanctioned events, and has had his Ryder Cup eligibility restored. The 2027 Ryder Cup is at Adare Manor in Ireland — and Rahm is now eligible to be part of the European side.

The agreement is confirmed. The terms are straightforward: financial obligations cleared, event quota committed, membership standing restored. For Rahm, it means that the path back to the highest level of European team golf is no longer hypothetical. It exists. Whether he takes it depends on what he decides about LIV Golf's October 13 restructuring commitment.

Why This Matters Beyond the Fine

Rahm joined LIV Golf in late 2023, signing a deal reported at the time to be worth more than $300 million over several years. The move came with professional costs — he became ineligible for Ryder Cup selection because LIV players were not eligible for DP World Tour membership, and DP World Tour membership is the qualifying requirement for the European Ryder Cup team. He also owed outstanding fines to the DP World Tour for competing in LIV events before formally resigning membership.

The May 2026 agreement with the DP World Tour addressed both issues. The fines are paid. His membership is restored. The event quota — five DP World Tour events, excluding majors — is a commitment that keeps him in good standing going forward. He can now earn Ryder Cup points.

The 2027 Ryder Cup Context

Adare Manor is one of the most anticipated Ryder Cup venues in recent memory. The course in County Limerick, Ireland, was redesigned for Ryder Cup-level professional play under the direction of Tom Fazio and is widely regarded as one of the finest championship facilities built in the past two decades. It is a home game for European golf in the deepest sense — Ireland has produced some of the most passionate Ryder Cup galleries in the competition's history.

For Rahm, who is Spanish and whose career has been built on representing Europe in international team competition, the Ryder Cup is not just an appearance fee or a marquee event. His record is part of his legacy as one of the elite players of his generation. Being eligible to be part of that team again — if he is not playing LIV — is a meaningful professional consideration, not a secondary one.

What This Doesn't Decide

The DP World Tour agreement does not commit Rahm to walking away from LIV. It does not decide his October 13 restructuring commitment, one way or another. What it does is clarify the alternative. Before the agreement, Rahm's future outside LIV was undefined. Now it is defined: DP World Tour membership in good standing, a path to Ryder Cup selection for 2027, and five event commitments that would make him an active presence in European professional golf.

He is LIV Golf's largest individual creditor at $7.47 million. His claim carries more dollar weight in the BC Partners threshold calculation than any other single player. The question of whether he commits to LIV 2.0 by October 13 is not settled by the DP World Tour agreement — but the alternatives are no longer abstract.

What's Next

Rahm's October 13 LIV commitment decision remains pending. The bankruptcy court has also not yet ruled on LIV's petition to reject all existing player contracts — a ruling that, if granted, would formally end existing contractual obligations regardless of individual player decisions. The next substantive development on his situation will likely come from either a public statement ahead of October 13 or the court's ruling on the contract rejection petition. The DP World Tour agreement ensures that whichever way he goes, European golf has a place for him.
