Koivun Plays R4 Today — Then Straight to Medinah for His Presidents Cup Debut

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | September 20, 2026

Jackson Koivun / Biltmore Championship / Presidents Cup

Jackson Koivun made the cut at the inaugural Biltmore Championship. He sits at -7 through three rounds at The Cliffs at Walnut Cove in Asheville, NC — eight shots behind leader Neal Shipley, well outside the top-five conversation, but in the field for Sunday's final round. He plays his final 18 holes today. Then, within the next 48 hours, he heads to Medinah Country Club in Illinois for Presidents Cup practice rounds starting Tuesday, September 22.

At 21 years old, Koivun is the youngest player on either the USA or International team. USA captain Brandt Snedeker chose him as a captain's pick. This week at the Biltmore Championship is the last professional stroke play he will play before his first Presidents Cup competition begins Thursday, September 24.

The Biltmore Week

Koivun opened the Biltmore Championship with a 68 (-3) on Thursday and followed with a 67 (-4) on Friday to make the cut at -7. He was never in serious contention for the lead — Shipley's career-low 62 on Friday separated the leaders from the pack — but making the cut in a 72-hole PGA Tour event provides useful form data heading into a completely different format at Medinah.

He will spend Sunday playing a final round where the competitive stakes for him personally are modest: leaderboard improvement and the momentum of a clean finishing round matter, but the real week starts Tuesday. Snedeker, who selected him, will be watching to see how he handles the transition — from anonymous Sunday at a fall event to walking onto the first tee at Medinah with Team USA behind him.

Why Snedeker Picked Him

The captain's pick decision for Koivun was a bet on trajectory. He is not the most decorated player on the USA roster — Scheffler, Schauffele, Thomas, and Morikawa have combined for multiple major championships between them. But Snedeker's job as captain included building a team that functions across the full Presidents Cup schedule, and a 21-year-old with strong form and the temperament to compete under pressure is an asset in the four-ball and foursomes formats that open the week.

Maverick Bridgeman was also selected as a captain's pick and is also competing at the Biltmore this week, though with more modest results through the first three rounds. The pair's inclusion signals that Snedeker wanted youth on the roster — and at least some players who have been actively competing in stroke play conditions heading into the match play environment.

What the Format Shift Means

The transition from stroke play to match play is not simply a mental reset. Stroke play rewards avoiding mistakes over 72 holes; match play rewards winning individual holes, which changes risk management entirely. A player who would never attempt a low-percentage pin in stroke play — where a bogey costs one shot on the leaderboard — might attack that same pin in match play if being aggressive wins the hole and swings the match.

At 21, in his first Presidents Cup, Koivun will be paired by Snedeker for the team formats before taking on an International opponent in Sunday singles. His stroke play form is the warmup. His match play performance at Medinah is the test that matters this week.

What's Next

Koivun finishes his final round at the Biltmore Championship today, then travels to Medinah Country Club for practice rounds on Tuesday, September 22 and Wednesday, September 23. The Presidents Cup competition opens Thursday, September 24 with five four-ball matches beginning at 11:35 AM CDT. Koivun's pairing for Thursday will be announced ahead of the practice rounds. The full 30-match Presidents Cup schedule runs Thursday through Sunday at Medinah.
