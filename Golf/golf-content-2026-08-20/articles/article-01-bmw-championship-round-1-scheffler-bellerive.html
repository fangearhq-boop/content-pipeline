BMW Championship Round 1: Scheffler Leads FedExCup Field Into Bellerive

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | August 20, 2026

Round 1 of the BMW Championship tees off today at Bellerive Country Club in St. Louis, Missouri, and the stakes could not be clearer: the top 30 players at the conclusion of Sunday's final round will advance to the Tour Championship at East Lake in Atlanta, August 27-30. For everyone else, the 2026 PGA Tour season ends this week.

Scottie Scheffler arrives as the dominant force in the 50-man field. The world No. 1 leads the FedExCup standings with 4,873 points — a margin of nearly 1,400 points over second place — after his commanding 8-shot victory at the FedEx St. Jude Championship in Memphis last week. His East Lake berth is a formality. The question for Scheffler this week is whether he can add another FedExCup victory to cap a remarkable season.

The FedExCup Standings Entering Round 1

The field entering Bellerive represents the 50 best players in FedExCup points this season. Here's where the standings sit:

No. 1 Scottie Scheffler leads with 4,873 points, followed by Matt Fitzpatrick at No. 2, Cameron Young at No. 3, Si Woo Kim at No. 4, and Wyndham Clark at No. 5. Sam Burns, Chris Gotterup, Tommy Fleetwood, Collin Morikawa, and Ludvig Åberg round out the top 10.

Further down: Xander Schauffele sits 11th, Rory McIlroy is 13th, and Viktor Hovland is 22nd. All of these players are comfortably within the East Lake bubble.

One notable change to the field: Sepp Straka, who entered as No. 5 in FedExCup points, withdrew for a private family matter. The field was reduced to 49 players as a result, with no replacement. Straka is still expected to compete at East Lake based on his existing point total.

McIlroy's Mission at Bellerive

Rory McIlroy arrives at Bellerive in an unusual position for a player of his caliber — on the back foot after a difficult playoff opener. The two-time defending Masters champion (2025 career Grand Slam and 2026 defense over Scheffler in a playoff) posted a first-round that put him well back at the St. Jude Championship, eventually finishing 66th — far outside any meaningful result.

"Far away from where I need to be," McIlroy said after St. Jude. He spent time working with coach Michael Bannon following that event and arrives at Bellerive with something to prove. His No. 13 ranking means his East Lake spot is safe, but McIlroy is not a player who is satisfied just making it to the final event. He wants to win.

Bellerive is a par 70 at 7,448 yards — the course last hosted the BMW Championship in 2008, when Camilo Villegas won. The bunkers have been resodded with bentgrass. It's a demanding track that rewards ball striking and precise iron play — areas where McIlroy, when in form, can be as good as anyone in the world.

The East Lake Bubble

The FedExCup playoff format is clear: finish in the top 30 at Bellerive, advance to East Lake. A single strong performance this week can move a player from outside to inside the cutoff. A bad round at the wrong time can end a season that showed promise for months.

With Straka's withdrawal, the math around positions 28-31 shifted. Players ranked 31-40 entering Bellerive are in genuine jeopardy — they need results this week, not just participation. The Golf Channel bubble tracker has identified several names fighting for those final spots. One top-10 finish changes everything.

What's Next

Round 1 results will be available by Thursday evening. The leaderboard will take shape quickly — Bellerive's par 70 layout can produce low scores when conditions are favorable, and the field is the 50 best players in golf. Expect a competitive board from the first round.

The tournament concludes Sunday, August 23. East Lake begins the following Thursday. Everything this week has a one-week horizon and maximum stakes. That's what makes FedExCup playoffs compelling — and Bellerive is where the real pressure begins.
