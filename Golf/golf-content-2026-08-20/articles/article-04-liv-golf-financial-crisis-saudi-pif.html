LIV Golf's Financial Future in Jeopardy as Saudi PIF Ends Backing After 2026

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | August 20, 2026

New reporting confirms that Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund — which has invested approximately $5 billion in LIV Golf since the circuit launched in 2022 — will not continue its financial backing beyond 2026. The announcement is not unexpected given the months of financial strain that have been visible in the circuit's operations, but the confirmation changes the calculation for everyone involved: players, sponsors, broadcasters, and the PGA Tour, which has been in intermittent merger discussions with LIV Golf for years.

The most immediate visible evidence of the financial pressure is at this week's Indianapolis event, where the prize purse has been cut from $20 million to $10.1 million. The winner will receive $2.02 million instead of the $4 million that had been the standard for LIV events. The Michigan Team Championship, which had been on the 2026 schedule, was cancelled entirely. Across the season, approximately $60 million in promised prize money did not materialize.

The Scale of the Investment and What It Built

The $5 billion figure that has been invested by the Public Investment Fund over four-plus years purchased several things: marquee players, broadcast infrastructure, a team format, a global tournament schedule, and the public attention that came with the controversy of pulling major PGA Tour names away from the established tour.

LIV attracted Dustin Johnson, Phil Mickelson, Bryson DeChambeau, Jon Rahm, Cameron Smith, Brooks Koepka, and dozens of others through a combination of guaranteed contracts and enormous purses. The guaranteed money — paid regardless of performance — was the primary recruitment tool and the primary financial pressure point. Prize money can be cut; guaranteed contracts are obligations.

What the $5 billion did not purchase was financial self-sufficiency. LIV Golf has not generated the broadcast revenue, sponsorship income, or ticket revenue necessary to sustain its prize structure without external subsidy. The PIF was providing that subsidy. Without it, the current operating model is not viable.

The Lawsuit and Unpaid Obligations

The financial strain is not limited to prize money reductions. Fresh Tape Media filed suit on July 21 in New York Supreme Court, seeking $1.23 million or more in unpaid fees related to LIV Golf Week, a promotional event held January 12-14 in West Palm Beach, Florida. The lawsuit alleges that LIV Golf did not pay for services rendered in connection with that event.

Additionally, players are reportedly awaiting payments — a situation that, if accurate, represents an extraordinary breach of the guarantees that made LIV attractive to begin with. Players who signed multi-year guaranteed contracts based on financial promises made during the circuit's aggressive expansion phase are now facing uncertainty about whether those commitments will be honored.

LIV Golf has not publicly commented on the specific payment allegations. The circuit is still operational. Events are still taking place. But the gap between what was promised and what is being delivered has become visible in ways that are difficult to obscure.

What Comes Next

The most discussed scenario for LIV Golf's post-2026 existence involves some form of merger or integration with the PGA Tour. Those discussions have proceeded in fits and starts since the framework agreement announced in June 2023. The financial pressure on LIV could accelerate those talks — a circuit that cannot sustain its own operations is a weaker negotiating partner, but it may also be a more motivated one.

Alternatively, the circuit could restructure significantly: smaller purses, fewer events, a narrower field of players. The team format and some of the entertainment elements might survive in reduced form. What almost certainly cannot survive is the financial model that recruited players away from established tours by promising guaranteed wealth at levels the underlying business could not support.

For the players most exposed — those who signed the largest guaranteed deals and left significant PGA Tour standing behind — the uncertainty is real and the options are limited. Some have maintained eligibility pathways. Others are more thoroughly committed to LIV Golf as their professional home. Their situations will be different depending on the specific terms of their contracts and whatever transitional arrangements emerge.

What's Next

Indianapolis continues through Saturday. The circuit will not fold mid-event — these three rounds will be completed, the team championship will be contested, and the season will conclude on schedule. But the conversation happening around this week's golf is as significant as anything happening on the Chatham Hills course.

LIV Golf was built to disrupt professional golf's financial ecosystem. Four years later, its own financial ecosystem is the story. Whatever comes after 2026 will define whether the disruption produced lasting structural change or a well-funded experiment that ran its course.
