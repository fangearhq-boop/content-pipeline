CPKC Women's Open Round 1: Korda, Henderson, and Ko Lead Star-Studded Edmonton Field

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | August 20, 2026

Round 1 of the 52nd CPKC Women's Open is underway at Royal Mayfair Golf Club in Edmonton, Alberta, and the field that has assembled here is as formidable as women's golf has produced at a national championship in years. The event carries a $2.75 million purse, and organizers are projecting 75,000 to 80,000 spectators over the four days — figures that would set a new attendance record for the event.

This is not just a Canadian celebration, though the Canadian storylines are compelling on their own. The world's best women golfers are here, and the person most likely to lift the trophy Sunday is Nelly Korda.

Korda: The Overwhelming Favorite

Nelly Korda arrives in Edmonton as the world No. 1, the betting favorite at +440, and the most dominant player in women's golf this season. She has won four times in 2026, including three major championships: the Chevron Championship, the Riviera May Open, and the U.S. Women's Open. A victory this week would give her four majors in a single season — a feat with no modern precedent in the women's game.

Korda's ball striking has been at a different level from the rest of the field for the better part of two years. Royal Mayfair, a par 70 layout at 6,452 yards, rewards precision — the kind of iron play that Korda has made her signature. Wet conditions earlier this week softened the course slightly, which may allow for lower scoring, but Korda handles any setup. She has won on firm courses, on soft courses, in windless conditions and in the wind. The question at every major she enters is not whether she is capable of winning. It is whether anyone can match her.

Henderson: Defending at Home

Brooke Henderson won this event in 2025 at Mississaugua, closing with a 4-under 67 to edge Minjee Lee by one shot for her 14th career LPGA title. Now 28, she returns to defend in her home country — and this time in front of a crowd in Edmonton, where she has long been beloved.

Henderson is not a longshot here. She knows how to win this event. She knows how to handle the emotion of playing before Canadian fans who are rooting for her vocally and loudly from the first tee. In 2025, she channeled that energy into a closing round that held up under pressure. The question is whether she can do it again against a field that includes the best player in the world.

She arrives having played consistent LPGA golf through 2026, without the same peak results as Korda but with no sign that her game has regressed. Defending a major championship while carrying the expectations of a home crowd is its own distinct challenge — one Henderson has proven she can handle.

Ko: Chasing a Record

Lydia Ko first won this event in 2013 as a 15-year-old amateur — the youngest LPGA winner in history at the time. She has won the Canadian Women's Open three times in her career and returns this week with the chance to claim a record fourth title.

Ko is not currently among the top favorites in the betting markets, but her record at this specific event is exceptional. She knows Royal Mayfair, knows Canadian galleries, and has won here across different stages of her career. Three-time champions at single events become four-time champions precisely because they find a way to perform when history is available to them.

The Canadian Contingent

This year's field includes 15 Canadian players — the largest contingent in the event's 52-year history. That number reflects both the growth of Canadian women's golf and the organizational effort to elevate homegrown players on the national stage. For players ranked outside the top 100 globally, competing in a national major in front of a home crowd provides an opportunity they cannot replicate anywhere else on tour.

Six of the top 10 players in the world rankings are in the field: Korda, Henderson, HyoJoo Kim, Miyu Yamashita, Ruoning Yin, and Minjee Lee. Hannah Green is also in the field. Lee, who lost to Henderson by one shot in 2025 at Mississaugua, will be motivated to close the gap this week.

What's Next

Round 1 scoring will be available throughout the day, with the leaderboard taking shape by Thursday evening. At par 70, low rounds are possible — Korda has posted rounds of 63 and 64 at major championships this season. The field she is playing against is the best on the LPGA Tour, but rounds like that tend to build early leads that are difficult for the rest of the field to overcome.

The CPKC Women's Open concludes Sunday, August 23. Admission is free on Thursday and Friday for Canadian residents, which contributes to the projected attendance numbers. By the weekend, this field, these scores, and this setting should produce one of the best closing stretches in women's golf this season.
