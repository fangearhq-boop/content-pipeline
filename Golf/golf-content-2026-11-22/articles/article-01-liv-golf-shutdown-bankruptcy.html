LIV Golf Officially Winding Down After Five Years and Roughly $5 Billion in Saudi Investment

By Ryan Calloway | November 22, 2026 | Golf Fanrecap

LIV Golf is shutting down. The league's lawyers told a New Jersey bankruptcy court on November 5 that the organization would not proceed with its planned LIV 2.0 relaunch, formally confirming the end of the most disruptive force in professional golf's recent history. After five years and an investment from Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund estimated at roughly $5 billion, the 2026 season was the last.

LIV Golf filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on September 8. A bankruptcy plan filed November 10 targets an orderly January wind-down through liquidation proceedings. The next hearing in the case is scheduled for November 24.

How It Ended

The collapse of LIV Golf's 2.0 ambitions came after a series of funding failures. Saudi Arabia's PIF changed its investment strategy in April 2026 and informed the league it would not continue financing operations after the 2026 season concluded. A rescue plan from British private equity firm BC Partners — representing a commitment of upward of $300 million — subsequently fell apart when player participation did not materialize and BC Partners terminated the Restructuring Support Agreement.

Before filing for bankruptcy, LIV Golf laid off 289 employees and furloughed 22 more. The league's financial disclosures in the bankruptcy proceedings revealed the scale of the investment that had been poured into the venture: estimates in various filings range from $5 billion to $7 billion in PIF equity. Whatever the final number, it represents one of the most expensive failed sports ventures in history.

The Legal Aftermath

The bankruptcy proceedings have created their own drama. LIV Golf's creditors have subpoenaed the PGA Tour for documents, seeking materials relevant to their case against the defunct league. The PGA Tour called the request a "desperate fishing expedition" and indicated it intends to contest the subpoena. The dispute could ultimately go before Judge Michael Kaplan in the bankruptcy court.

Beyond the legal fight, the question of what happens to the more than 50 players who left the PGA Tour to join LIV Golf is now one of professional golf's most pressing issues. The Returning Member Program that allowed Brooks Koepka to return to the Tour is currently closed. PGA Tour CEO Brian Rolapp has indicated that any broader reinstatement decision will not come until at least January 2027.

What's Next

For professional golf, the post-LIV era begins immediately. The DP World Tour and other circuits will likely see an influx of players seeking competitive venues while the PGA Tour works through its reinstatement policy. The majors — the Masters, the PGA Championship, the U.S. Open, and The Open Championship — will each need to address eligibility for formerly suspended players. The answers will shape the competitive landscape of golf for years to come.
