PGA Tour Not Rushing LIV Reinstatement Decision, Program Closed Until at Least January

By Ryan Calloway | November 22, 2026 | Golf Fanrecap

The PGA Tour is in no hurry. That is the clearest message from CEO Brian Rolapp, who confirmed this week that the Returning Member Program that allowed Brooks Koepka to rejoin the Tour is now closed, and that any decision about the broader reinstatement of LIV Golf players has been pushed to at least January 2027.

When asked directly whether the Tour would reopen reinstatement discussions as LIV Golf's bankruptcy proceedings moved toward a resolution, Rolapp offered a simple answer: "There are no plans for that currently, no." For the more than 50 players who departed the PGA Tour for LIV, the message is straightforward — the offseason is going to be a waiting game.

Legal Complications

The situation is further complicated by the ongoing bankruptcy proceedings. LIV Golf's creditors have issued a subpoena to the PGA Tour, seeking documents relevant to their case against the defunct league. The Tour called the request a "desperate fishing expedition," suggesting it views the subpoena as an overreach rather than a legitimate legal inquiry.

The dispute could go before Judge Michael Kaplan, with the next bankruptcy hearing scheduled for November 24. How that proceeding unfolds may influence the timeline and tone of any subsequent conversations between the Tour and former LIV players.

What Players Are Waiting For

Jon Rahm has publicly stated his willingness to accept Koepka-style reinstatement terms. Bryson DeChambeau and Cameron Smith, two other high-profile LIV defectors, have been less forthcoming about their intentions. All three, along with dozens of other players, are effectively in competitive limbo — unable to access PGA Tour events while the Tour deliberates.

The DP World Tour will likely see an influx of LIV-era players seeking competitive play in 2027, with Rahm already confirming plans to play that circuit. Whether the European-based tour becomes a long-term home or a temporary waystation depends entirely on when — and how — the PGA Tour resolves its reinstatement framework.

What's Next

The November 24 bankruptcy hearing may provide further clarity on the legal aspects of LIV's wind-down. The PGA Tour's January timeline for a reinstatement decision is the most concrete date in the picture. Between now and then, the golf world will watch closely as the most significant restructuring of the professional game's competitive landscape in decades takes shape.
