Jon Rahm Open to Koepka-Style Deal for PGA Tour Return, But No Timeline in Sight

By Marcus Cole | November 22, 2026 | Golf Fanrecap

Jon Rahm says he would accept the same financial and competitive penalties that Brooks Koepka agreed to in order to return to the PGA Tour after his stint with LIV Golf. The statement from the two-time major champion acknowledges the reality of his situation: he wants back, he knows it will cost him, and the decision ultimately rests with the Tour.

Koepka's reinstatement terms were significant: a $5 million fine, no equity ownership in any PGA Tour-related entity for five years, and no direct access to the Tour's signature events or FedExCup bonus money. For a player of Rahm's caliber, those restrictions would materially alter his competitive and financial position for years.

The Obstacle: No Open Door

Rahm's willingness to accept the terms does not mean the terms are available. PGA Tour CEO Brian Rolapp confirmed that the Returning Member Program that enabled Koepka's return is now closed. When asked whether the program would reopen for other LIV players, Rolapp said clearly: "There are no plans for that currently, no."

Reports indicate that any ruling on LIV players rejoining the PGA Tour circuit has been pushed to January 2027. For now, Rahm has acknowledged he has "no idea" when he would be allowed back, and he is planning to compete on the DP World Tour in 2027 as a competitive outlet while awaiting a decision.

The Broader Picture

Rahm is one of roughly 50 or more players whose PGA Tour futures are now uncertain following LIV Golf's bankruptcy announcement. Bryson DeChambeau, Cameron Smith, and others are in similar positions. Each player's eligibility for major championships and world ranking points is now subject to whatever framework the governing bodies establish in the coming months.

With LIV Golf officially winding down, the pressure on the PGA Tour to establish a clear and fair reinstatement path will only increase. Players who left in good faith to compete in a legitimate league — however controversial — deserve a resolution that acknowledges the complexity of the situation they now find themselves in.

What's Next

Rahm will compete on the DP World Tour while monitoring developments from Ponte Vedra Beach. The PGA Tour's January timeline for a decision on LIV reinstatement will be one of golf's most watched storylines going into 2027. If the Tour opens a reinstatement path with terms similar to Koepka's deal, Rahm would appear ready to sign. The question is whether the Tour will move faster than its stated timeline once LIV Golf is fully dissolved.
