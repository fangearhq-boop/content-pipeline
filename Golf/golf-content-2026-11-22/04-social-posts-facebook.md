### STORY 1: LIV Golf Shutdown

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 10:30 AM ET

LIV Golf is done.

After five years, an estimated $5 billion in Saudi PIF investment, and a seismic impact on professional golf's landscape, the league is officially winding down. Its lawyers told a New Jersey bankruptcy court on November 5 that the league would not proceed with the LIV 2.0 relaunch that had been discussed as a potential path forward.

The story of LIV's end is as turbulent as its beginning. Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund changed its investment strategy in April 2026 and informed the league it would not continue funding after the 2026 season. A rescue deal from private equity firm BC Partners — worth upward of $300 million — subsequently collapsed when player participation didn't materialize. LIV laid off 289 employees and furloughed 22 more before filing for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on September 8.

A bankruptcy liquidation plan filed November 10 targets a January wind-down. The next hearing is November 24.

For the sport of golf, the questions now are significant: What happens to the players who left the PGA Tour? When does the Tour decide on reinstatement? Will major championship eligibility change? None of those answers are coming immediately — but the five-year experiment that divided professional golf is officially over.

### STORY 2: CME Group Tour Championship

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 10:30 AM ET

The 2026 LPGA season is coming down to its final round, and the storyline is as compelling as any in women's golf this year.

A Lim Kim holds an 18-under par lead at the CME Group Tour Championship in Naples, Florida, with Nelly Korda two strokes behind. The South Korean star has been in control since making a remarkable hole-in-one on the par-3 16th hole — a 7-iron from 168 yards — during the second round, and she enters the final 18 holes as the clear favorite to claim the $4 million top prize.

But the Korda angle is the one that makes this final round more than just another closing Sunday. That is an almost incomprehensible prize sitting just 18 holes away.

Jeeno Thitikul (Atthaya Thitikul), the defending champion, is also positioned in the final group, adding another layer to what promises to be a memorable finish to the season.

The $11 million CME Group Tour Championship is the LPGA's richest event. Someone will walk away with the sport's biggest individual prize by Sunday evening — the question is whether A Lim Kim can hold on, or whether Korda will make history.

### STORY 3: Jon Rahm / PGA Tour Return

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 10:30 AM ET

Jon Rahm wants back on the PGA Tour — and he's willing to pay for it.

The two-time major champion, who left for LIV Golf in December 2023, says he would accept the same terms that allowed Brooks Koepka to return to the PGA Tour. Koepka's deal included a $5 million fine, no equity ownership for five years, and no direct access to signature events or FedExCup bonus money.

It's a significant financial and competitive penalty, and Rahm is essentially saying he'll take it.

The challenge is that the door might not be open yet. PGA Tour CEO Brian Rolapp has made it clear that the Returning Member Program is closed, and any ruling on LIV players rejoining the American circuit has reportedly been pushed to January 2027. Rahm told reporters he has "no idea" when he would be allowed back on Tour. For now, he is planning to play the DP World Tour in 2027 as he waits.

With LIV Golf now officially shutting down, the question of reinstatement for more than 50 players who left the Tour will become one of the dominant stories of the golf offseason.

### STORY 4: PGA Tour / LIV Reinstatement Timeline

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 1:00 PM ET

The PGA Tour is not in a hurry.

That is the clearest takeaway from recent comments by PGA Tour CEO Brian Rolapp, who confirmed that the Returning Member Program that allowed Brooks Koepka to return to the Tour is now closed. Any decision about the future of LIV Golf players — including Jon Rahm, Bryson DeChambeau, and Cameron Smith — has reportedly been pushed to January 2027.

The legal backdrop makes the situation more complicated. LIV Golf's bankruptcy creditors have subpoenaed the PGA Tour for documents, seeking evidence relevant to their case against the defunct league. The Tour called the subpoena a "desperate fishing expedition" and has indicated it will contest the request. A bankruptcy court hearing is scheduled for November 24.

What this means for the roughly 50 or so LIV players currently in limbo: patience is the only option. The Tour's deliberate pace suggests it wants to manage reinstatement on its own terms, not in response to external pressure — financial, legal, or otherwise.

For players like Rahm who are eager to compete at the highest level again, the offseason is shaping up to be one of prolonged uncertainty.

### STORY 5: LPGA Season in Review

The 2026 LPGA Tour season wraps up today, and what a season it has been.

From the opening event on January 29 through today's CME Group Tour Championship finale on November 22, the Tour has played through $132 million in total prize money — a figure that reflects the remarkable growth of women's professional golf over the past decade. It is the richest season in LPGA history.

Nelly Korda has led the world rankings throughout the year, cementing her status as the face of the women's game. Jeeno Thitikul has been her most consistent challenger. A Lim Kim's emergence as a major contender — including her stunning hole-in-one this week in Naples — has been one of the season's most compelling stories.

The year closes with the CME Group Tour Championship final round unfolding right now, with $4 million on the line and a Hall of Fame entry potentially waiting for Korda if she can close a two-shot gap.

It's a fitting final round for a year that delivered at nearly every turn.
