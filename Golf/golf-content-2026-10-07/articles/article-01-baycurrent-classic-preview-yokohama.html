Baycurrent Classic Preview — Schauffele, Clark, and Matsuyama Head to Yokohama

By Ryan Calloway | October 7, 2026

The PGA Tour's annual stop in Japan has never looked quite like this. The 2026 Baycurrent Classic at Yokohama Country Club, running October 8-11, features one of the most compelling pre-tournament narratives of the FedExCup Fall: a defending champion trying to do something that has never been done in tournament history, the world's No. 5-ranked player fresh off a career-defining season, and Japan's most beloved golfer playing in his own backyard.

The $8 million purse and 500 FedExCup Fall points make this the third of eight Fall events — and one of the richest prizes left in the calendar year. With a no-cut format, all 72 players complete four rounds. Every leaderboard position on every day of the week matters.

Schauffele Seeks History

Xander Schauffele won the 2025 Baycurrent Classic with a closing 64, finishing 19-under to beat Max Greyserman by one shot. He returns as the defending champion and the betting favorite — and as the player attempting something no one has managed before: winning back-to-back titles at this event.

Schauffele has been steady in 2026 with seven top-10 finishes entering Yokohama, and his world ranking sits at No. 10. He understands this golf course better than almost anyone in the field. A familiar venue, a familiar target, and the chance to make history — those are the conditions Schauffele works best in.

Wyndham Clark — The Season's Standout Arrives

If Schauffele is the story of consistency, Wyndham Clark is the story of transformation. Clark entered 2026 ranked No. 75 in the world. He is now at No. 5 — the highest-ranked player in the Baycurrent field — after a season that included the CJ Cup Byron Nelson and a wire-to-wire victory at the U.S. Open at Shinnecock Hills.

Clark's style — aggressive off the tee, precise iron play, competitive nerve — suits the demands of a Japanese course where managing distances and undulating greens requires both power and touch. He arrives as one of two marquee names the gallery will follow from Thursday morning.

Matsuyama's Home Advantage

Hideki Matsuyama won the predecessor to this event — the Zozo Championship — in 2021, and his connection to Japanese golf crowds is unlike anything any other player in the field brings. Playing at home is a genuine variable in professional golf: the familiarity of culture and environment, the energy of a crowd that knows you, the absence of the disorientation that international travel can introduce.

Matsuyama has always been a threat when the tour plays in Asia. His game — precise, calculated, built on iron accuracy — suits the West Course at Yokohama Country Club. He will be the most watched player on the ground in Japan, and for good reason.

The Rest of the Field

The Baycurrent Classic's 72-player, no-cut format draws a field that includes most of the tour's significant names. Justin Thomas, Colin Morikawa, Rickie Fowler, Jordan Spieth, and Minwoo Lee are among those competing alongside Schauffele, Clark, and Matsuyama. Fourteen players from the Japan Golf Tour Organization also have entry, adding local flavor to a globally competitive week.

Tom Kim, who had been expected to play, withdrew after winning the Asian Games. His absence opens a spot in the conversation but does not diminish the quality of what remains.

What's Next

Round 1 tees off Thursday at Yokohama Country Club, with morning tee times in Japan running through Thursday evening in North America. The no-cut format means the leaderboard will take its final shape only on Sunday — but the first-round positions will tell a great deal about which player's setup work has translated to one of the most technically demanding courses on the fall schedule.
