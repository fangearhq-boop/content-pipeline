LIV Golf's October 25 Deadline — What the Equity Deal Means for the League's Future

By Jake Torres | October 7, 2026

The deadline that LIV Golf players had been tracking — October 13, the date by which they needed to commit to the restructured league — has been pushed back to October 25. The delay comes alongside confirmation that BC Partners, the London-based private-equity firm leading the investment, has secured an initial $300 million in financing for what is being called LIV 2.0.

For players, the two-week extension does not change the fundamental calculus of the deal. It only gives them more time to weigh a proposition that carries significant financial complexity: transform at least 50% of their unpaid contract value into a 52.5% equity stake in the reorganized entity, or walk away.

The Structure of the Deal

LIV Golf filed for bankruptcy protection in the United States after Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund (PIF) chose not to continue its funding beyond the 2026 season. PIF spent more than $5 billion over five years building a league that generated significant global attention but ultimately could not reach the financial sustainability it needed without continued sovereign wealth backing.

BC Partners stepped in with a plan that puts players at the center. For the deal to proceed, at least 50% of players with financial claims against LIV must agree to participate, and that group must represent at least two-thirds of the total dollar amount in claims outstanding. Reports suggest those thresholds have now been cleared.

Players who sign effectively become majority owners. They give up half or more of what they were owed in exchange for equity in a leaner, restructured organization. The appeal is that a successful LIV 2.0 could eventually be worth more than the original contracts. The risk is that it might not be.

Rahm, DeChambeau, and the Uncommitted

Jon Rahm and Bryson DeChambeau remain among the players whose futures are publicly uncertain. Both joined LIV on major contracts and both have been linked to potential returns to the PGA Tour if the new arrangement does not materialize or meet their expectations.

DeChambeau became a free agent following the 2026 season. His options are genuinely open. Rahm's situation is similar. For the PGA Tour, the potential availability of these two players represents one of the more consequential roster developments in years.

What LIV 2.0 Might Look Like

Court documents and reporting suggest that the restructured league is expected to shrink significantly. The original LIV model featured 14 team events with high prize purses and a format designed to challenge the PGA Tour's dominance. LIV 2.0 is likely to run approximately 10 principal events, with reduced prize money that reflects the reality of BC Partners' investment rather than PIF's effectively unlimited backing.

Whether that scaled-down version retains the same broadcast deals, the same team structures, and the same ability to attract top players remains to be seen. The October 25 deadline will tell a great deal about which players believe in the new model — and which do not.

What's Next

Players have until October 25 to formally commit. Any player who has not signed by that date will be deemed to have opted out of the reorganization process. The outcome of those commitments will determine whether LIV 2.0 launches for the 2027 season as planned or whether the bankruptcy proceeding leads to a different outcome entirely.
