Wyndham Clark's 2026 — From No. 75 to No. 5, and Now Yokohama

By Ryan Calloway | October 7, 2026

At the start of 2026, Wyndham Clark was No. 75 in the world. He missed the cut at the PGA Championship. Nothing on the early-season leaderboard suggested the year he was about to have. And now he arrives at Yokohama Country Club for the Baycurrent Classic as the highest-ranked player in the field — No. 5 globally — after one of the more remarkable single-season transformations in recent PGA Tour history.

The Turning Point

Clark's season turned at the CJ Cup Byron Nelson outside Dallas. After a difficult stretch that had pushed him deep into the world rankings, he played four rounds of clean, aggressive golf and walked away with his first win of 2026. The victory pushed him to No. 45. A third-place finish at the Memorial moved him to No. 37. He was building momentum.

Then came Shinnecock Hills and the U.S. Open.

Clark led wire-to-wire at the 2026 U.S. Open. He carried a six-shot advantage into the final round and held on to win his second U.S. Open title — completing a run that moved him from No. 37 to No. 8 in the world overnight. The week at Shinnecock was the kind of performance that defines careers: sustained precision under pressure at one of the most unforgiving venues in major championship golf.

What Clark Does Well

Clark's game is built on driving distance and iron accuracy — a combination that works at most PGA Tour venues. His mental framework under pressure has been one of the distinguishing features of his 2026 season: he did not shrink in the critical moments at the CJ Cup Byron Nelson or at Shinnecock Hills; he expanded. That is not something a player can manufacture. It has to develop through competition, and it appears Clark has found it.

At Yokohama Country Club, the West Course demands the kind of iron play that Clark does well. The undulating greens and strategic bunkering reward players who can control their approach angles — and Clark's record on courses that require shot-shaping precision suggests he will be comfortable there from the first round.

The Baycurrent Opportunity

Clark enters the Baycurrent Classic as one of two favorites alongside defending champion Xander Schauffele. The FedExCup Fall context gives the week added significance — 500 points for the winner, which could meaningfully reshape the fall standings with five events remaining after this one.

For Clark, a win at Yokohama would put a stamp on what is already one of the best seasons any player has had in 2026. He has won at a major and won on tour. Adding a Fall win at a venue this competitive — against a field that includes Matsuyama on home turf and a motivated defending champion — would be a legitimate capstone to a season that almost no one predicted.

What's Next

Clark tees off Thursday at Yokohama Country Club. Watch how he plays the opening holes at the West Course — his comfort level from the beginning of the week will tell a great deal about whether his form has carried intact from the U.S. Open stretch into Japan.
