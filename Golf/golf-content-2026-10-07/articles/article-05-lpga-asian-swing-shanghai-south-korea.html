LPGA Asian Swing — Three Countries, Three Weeks, One Player of the Year Race

By Jake Torres | October 7, 2026

The LOTTE Championship is over, and the LPGA Tour heads into the stretch of its schedule that most resembles a sprint. Three events. Three consecutive weeks. Three countries across Asia. By the time players finish the Maybank Championship in Kuala Lumpur at the start of November, the LPGA Player of the Year race may well be decided.

The Schedule Ahead

The Buick LPGA Shanghai at Sheshan Golf Club in Shanghai, China opens the swing October 15-18. Sheshan is a course with a distinguished history on the LPGA Tour — it has hosted this event for years and produces consistently competitive leaderboards. The $3.2 million purse makes it one of the richer stops on the fall calendar.

The BMW Ladies Championship follows October 22-25 at Pine Beach Golf Links in Haenam-gun, South Korea. At $2.35 million, it is slightly smaller in purse but no less competitive — the South Korean contingent in women's golf is substantial, and the home crowd creates one of the most energized atmospheres of the fall season.

The swing closes with the Maybank Championship at Kuala Lumpur Golf and Country Club's West Course in Malaysia, October 29 through November 1, with a $3 million purse. Three tournaments. Three weeks. Players who show up in form at Sheshan and carry it through South Korea and into Kuala Lumpur can shift the entire narrative of the season in a compressed window of time.

The Thitikul-Korda Dynamic

Jeeno Thitikul's LOTTE Championship win brought her to four wins in 2026 — matching Nelly Korda. Both players have built cases for LPGA Player of the Year, and neither has clearly separated from the other in the win-total column. The quality of wins, the consistency across varying conditions, and the next few weeks of results will determine how the award is ultimately decided.

Thitikul enters the Asian swing in form. She closed with a 68 at Hoakalei after a drama-filled back nine, which is the kind of mental resilience that translates across course types and continents. Korda's own record on the LPGA Tour in Asia over her career has been strong, and she will not concede the Player of the Year on a quiet Asian swing.

Why the Asian Swing Matters

For the sport globally, the LPGA's Asian swing is some of the best-attended and most-watched golf of the season. The fan bases in Japan, South Korea, China, and Southeast Asia for women's golf are among the most passionate anywhere in the world. The galleries at the BMW Ladies Championship in South Korea, in particular, have an energy that rivals the largest events on the calendar.

For players in the field, the compressed travel and the back-to-back-to-back schedule is demanding. Those who manage it well — who pace themselves physically, who adjust to the time zone and course changes efficiently — often make their biggest moves of the year in this three-week window.

What's Next

The LPGA Tour has a break this week before the Buick LPGA Shanghai begins October 15 at Sheshan Golf Club. Players will arrive in China having had a full week to recover from Hawaii's heat and prepare for the specific demands of Sheshan. The leaderboard there will be the first indicator of where the LPGA Player of the Year race stands heading into the season's final stretch.
