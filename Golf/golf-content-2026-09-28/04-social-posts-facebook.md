### STORY 1: USA Wins Presidents Cup 17-13

**Post:**
Team USA has won the 2026 Presidents Cup, defeating the International Team 17-13 at Medinah Country Club in Chicago -- completing the greatest Sunday comeback in the event's history.

The Internationals entered Sunday singles holding a 10.5-7.5 lead. It was the largest cushion the International Team had ever carried into singles in the Presidents Cup's 30-year history. They needed just 4.5 points from 12 singles matches to win.

USA needed 9 points. They got them.

Scottie Scheffler opened the scoring with a 2&1 win over Nico Echavarria. Collin Morikawa, who was trailing in his match, flipped it on the back nine. Patrick Cantlay drained a 57-foot putt on the 17th green to go 2-up against Adam Scott. Justin Thomas dismantled Corey Conners 5&3.

The clinching point came from the youngest player on the team. Jackson Koivun, 21 years old in his first Presidents Cup, defeated Si Woo Kim 2-up after winning four of five back-nine holes. When Koivun's match closed, the cup was decided.

Tom Kim was the bright spot for the Internationals, defeating Wyndham Clark 3&2 for one of the team's bright spots in an otherwise one-sided final session.

USA's all-time Presidents Cup record is now 14-1-1 across 16 events. This is their 11th consecutive title.

### STORY 2: Nishimura Wins NW Arkansas

**Post:**
Yuna Nishimura of Japan has won the Walmart NW Arkansas Championship for her first LPGA Tour title.

Nishimura shot a final-round 5-under 66 at Pinnacle Country Club in Rogers, Arkansas to finish at 21-under par, winning by five shots. She led wire-to-wire after opening rounds of 63-63.

It was not completely smooth on Sunday. Nishimura played the front nine in even par, and Aki Iwai began making an extraordinary charge -- going six birdies in seven back-nine holes to close to within range at one point. The tournament was still alive.

Then Nishimura hit her approach on the par-5 14th, a fairway wood over water, and made eagle. Two more birdies followed on the back nine. The gap stretched to five and did not move again.

Iwai finished with a bogey-free 62 but it was not enough. She tied for second at 16-under along with Chanettee Wannasaen (65) and In Gee Chun (67).

Nishimura takes home $450,000 from a $3 million total purse.

### STORY 3: LIV Golf Bankruptcy Deadline

**Post:**
LIV Golf's bankruptcy filing is three weeks old, and the most important deadline in the entire restructuring is now 15 days away.

By October 13, players holding LIV contracts must decide whether to commit to new, lower-cost deals. The math required for the restructuring to move forward is specific: at least two-thirds of eligible player claims by dollar amount AND at least half by number of players must agree to the new terms.

If they don't, the entire BC Partners rescue deal -- worth $300 million -- is at risk of collapsing. BC Partners, a London-based private equity firm, committed the rescue package that would see players collectively own 52.5% of the new LIV entity if the restructuring goes through.

The problem is that the original contracts were enormous. Jon Rahm and Bryson DeChambeau are among the players owed millions under the old deals. Players at the top of the contract hierarchy face the biggest relative reduction if they accept new terms.

A bankruptcy court hearing is scheduled for October 7 -- nine days away -- which will be the next public window into where the proceedings stand.

LIV's 2026 season ended early. The planned relaunch is early 2027, with a 10-event schedule, assuming the restructuring succeeds.

### STORY 4: Bank of Utah Championship Preview

**Post:**
The PGA Tour Fall schedule returns this week with one of the most visually striking venues in golf.

The Bank of Utah Championship runs October 1-4 at Black Desert Resort in Ivins, Utah -- a course that opened in 2023 set against the red-rock terrain near Zion National Park. When Round 1 tees off Thursday, players will be competing in one of the newest and most distinctive settings on the Tour calendar.

The Bank of Utah Championship is a full-field event offering FedExCup Fall points, which help players improve their standing heading into the following season. Most of the field is made up of players who did not qualify for the FedExCup Playoffs -- players using the fall swing to build status, earn cards, and position themselves for 2027.

Black Desert Resort made an immediate impression when it opened. The combination of the course design and the dramatic Utah landscape has drawn strong reviews, and the 2026 championship field should generate some compelling early-season storylines as the Tour heads into the fall.

### STORY 5: Koivun Clinches Presidents Cup

**Post:**
Jackson Koivun is 21 years old. On Sunday at Medinah, he delivered the point that won the Presidents Cup for Team USA.

Koivun was one of the youngest players captain Jim Furyk added to the American team -- a choice that raised questions at the time about whether a rookie could hold up under the pressure of match play at the Presidents Cup level. Furyk said he selected Koivun because of his composure.

The answer came on Sunday's back nine.

Koivun was in a tight match with Si Woo Kim, a four-time PGA Tour winner with serious experience at the highest level. Then Koivun went on a run, winning four of five holes on the back nine to build a 2-up lead. He closed the match. That point sealed the Presidents Cup for Team USA.

The comeback was complete: from 10.5-7.5 down, Team USA finished 17-13 in the greatest Sunday singles comeback in the event's history. The final winning point came from the youngest man on the team.
