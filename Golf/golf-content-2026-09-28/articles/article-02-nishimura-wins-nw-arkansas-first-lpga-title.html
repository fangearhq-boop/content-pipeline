Yuna Nishimura Wins the NW Arkansas Championship for Her First LPGA Tour Title

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | September 28, 2026

Yuna Nishimura of Japan won the Walmart NW Arkansas Championship at Pinnacle Country Club in Rogers, Arkansas on Saturday, shooting a final-round 5-under 66 to finish at 21-under par and claim her first LPGA Tour victory by five shots.

Nishimura led wire-to-wire after opening rounds of 63-63, but the win did not come without a Sunday scare.

A Close Call on the Back Nine

Nishimura played the front nine in even par, and Aki Iwai was making a run. The Japanese player went six birdies in seven back-nine holes, closing the gap to where the tournament was genuinely alive. It was the kind of back-nine charge that can unravel a wire-to-wire lead.

Then Nishimura stepped up on the par-5 14th. She hit a fairway wood over the water and made eagle. Two more birdies followed on the back nine. The gap extended to five shots, and Iwai's challenge was over.

Iwai finished with a remarkable bogey-free 62 -- a performance that would win most tournaments -- but it was not enough. She tied for second at 16-under alongside Chanettee Wannasaen (65) and In Gee Chun (67).

The Final Round Scorecard

Nishimura's 66 on Sunday broke into two very different halves. She was even par through nine, managing the pressure and staying patient while the field closed slightly. On the back nine she shot 30, anchored by the eagle at 14 that effectively ended Iwai's challenge.

The final total of 192 (63-63-66) at 21-under was the best score of the week by five shots. The win was Nishimura's first on the LPGA Tour.

The Prize

Nishimura takes home $450,000 of the $3 million total purse for the Walmart NW Arkansas Championship -- the winner's share of one of the more established events on the LPGA Tour calendar. Pinnacle Country Club in Rogers, Arkansas has hosted the event for several years and has a history of producing compelling final-round finishes.

What's Next

The LPGA Tour continues its fall schedule in the coming weeks. Nishimura's first victory opens the door to increased sponsorship opportunities and likely a stronger schedule for the remainder of the 2026 season and into 2027. Aki Iwai, who came the closest to Nishimura on Sunday, showed the kind of back-nine form that signals her own win is likely coming soon.
