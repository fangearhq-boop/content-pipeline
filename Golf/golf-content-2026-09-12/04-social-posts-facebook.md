### STORY 3: LIV Golf Restart Plans

**Post 3A — 2027 comeback angle**
Window: 12:00 PM ET

LIV Golf filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy four days ago. Its CEO says the plan is to come back in 2027.

Scott O'Neil confirmed the tour is targeting an "early 2027" restart. Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, which had pulled its general funding in April after investing a reported $5 billion, has now agreed to provide $49.6M in debtor-in-possession financing to keep the case moving. BC Partners Credit is expected to provide exit financing.

The numbers are significant. LIV owes over $45 million to players as unsecured creditors. Jon Rahm ($7.5M), Bryson DeChambeau ($5.7M), and Dustin Johnson ($5.5M) lead the list. All 48 player contracts were terminated when the filing was made.

Whether a 2027 LIV can actually assemble a competitive field after those player contracts expired and PGA Tour reinstatement windows have opened is a question that remains unanswered.

### STORY 4: LIV Players and PGA Tour

**Post 4A — Koepka path angle**
Window: 2:00 PM ET

Brooks Koepka showed how it's done. The question is whether Rahm, DeChambeau, and Smith will follow.

Koepka became the first LIV player reinstated by the PGA Tour through the Tour's "Returning Member Program." The terms were severe: a $5 million donation to charity, no equity grants for five years, no access to the bonus pool money in the year of his return.

PGA Tour CEO Brian Rolapp offered the same deal to Jon Rahm, Bryson DeChambeau, and Cameron Smith with a tight deadline. None of them accepted. Rolapp later said the Tour was "not inclined to discuss any paths back while players are under contract."

Now their LIV contracts are terminated. The Tour has not announced new terms or a new window. The three players are technically free agents without active contracts — and the only announced path back still carries the same penalty structure Koepka accepted.

### STORY 5: Biltmore Championship Preview

**Post 5A — Asheville debut angle**
Window: 6:00 PM ET

The PGA Tour is heading to the mountains.

Five days from now, the inaugural Biltmore Championship tees off at The Cliffs at Walnut Cove in Arden, North Carolina — just outside Asheville. It's the opening event of the FedExCup Fall and the first PGA Tour event in the Asheville area since 1942.

The course: designed by Jack Nicklaus, 7,167 yards, set in a valley bordering the Pisgah National Forest and the Blue Ridge Parkway. Elevation sits between 2,100 and 2,250 feet — higher than most PGA Tour venues and a factor players will need to account for across four rounds.

The stakes are real. This is the FedExCup Fall, where the results determine who keeps a PGA Tour card for 2027. Every shot counts differently when a playing card hangs in the balance.

Biltmore Estate and Explore Asheville are co-title sponsors. The event runs September 17-20 on Golf Channel and PGA Tour LIVE on ESPN+.
