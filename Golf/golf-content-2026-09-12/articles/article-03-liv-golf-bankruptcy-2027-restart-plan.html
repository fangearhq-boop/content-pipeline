LIV Golf CEO Targets 2027 Restart. The Tour Owes Players More Than $45 Million.

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | September 12, 2026

Four days after filing Chapter 11 bankruptcy, LIV Golf CEO Scott O'Neil has confirmed plans to restart the tour in early 2027. Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund is providing $49.6 million in debtor-in-possession financing. Player creditors include Jon Rahm, Bryson DeChambeau, and Dustin Johnson among the largest claimants.

LIV Golf filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection on September 8, 2026, in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court of New Jersey. Four days later, its CEO is talking about 2027.

Scott O'Neil confirmed in a press release that LIV Golf plans to restart in "early 2027," describing the bankruptcy filing as the beginning of a "new era" rather than the end of the organization. O'Neil framed Chapter 11 as a restructuring mechanism, not a shutdown. BC Partners Credit and other investors are expected to provide exit financing when the reorganized company completes the Chapter 11 process.

The financing keeping LIV Golf operating through the case comes from an unexpected source: Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, the same entity that pulled its general funding in April after investing a reported $5 billion total. PIF has agreed to provide $49.6 million in debtor-in-possession financing — a commitment that signals the kingdom still sees value in protecting its investment, even if the tour's original structure could not survive.

The Player Creditor List

LIV Golf's bankruptcy filing revealed the scope of what the tour owes to the players who left established careers to join it. Court records list the 30 largest unsecured claims. Among them:

Jon Rahm is owed $7.5 million. Bryson DeChambeau is owed $5.7 million. Dustin Johnson is owed $5.5 million. Cameron Smith is owed $4.8 million. Tyrrell Hatton is owed $3.4 million. Brooks Koepka is listed for $1.7 million — though Koepka has already returned to the PGA Tour.

Combined, LIV's outstanding player obligations in unsecured claims total more than $45 million. These are not guaranteed payments. In a Chapter 11 restructuring, unsecured creditors typically receive cents on the dollar. The players who turned down guaranteed PGA Tour money to join LIV may see a fraction of what they are owed, depending on how the case resolves.

What the Tour LIV Actually Had

The bankruptcy filing disclosed LIV Golf's asset and liability ranges: between $100 million and $500 million in assets, and between $500 million and $1 billion in liabilities. That means liabilities could exceed assets by as much as $500 million — an extraordinary gap for a sports league that never turned a profit and whose player contracts have all been terminated.

All 48 player contracts were voided at the time of filing. LIV's 2026 season has already concluded — the final event in Indianapolis saw Michael La Sasso win the individual championship at 22, becoming the youngest event winner in LIV history. Jon Rahm won the overall 2026 season title, his third straight individual crown. Legion XIII repeated as team champions.

Whether a 2027 LIV Golf can assemble a field remotely comparable to those three seasons is the central question the CEO's press release did not address.

What's Next

The Chapter 11 case moves through the U.S. Bankruptcy Court of New Jersey. The restructuring timeline, the exit financing terms from BC Partners, and any updated player outreach will determine whether O'Neil's 2027 target is achievable. Golf Fanrecap will track the case through its proceedings.
