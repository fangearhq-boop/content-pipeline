The Biltmore Championship Is Five Days Away. Here's What to Know About the PGA Tour's Asheville Debut.

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | September 12, 2026

The inaugural Biltmore Championship at The Cliffs at Walnut Cove in Arden, NC, tees off September 17-20 as the opening event of the FedExCup Fall. Designed by Jack Nicklaus, the course sits in the Blue Ridge Mountains at elevations between 2,100 and 2,250 feet — the first PGA Tour event in the Asheville area since 1942.

The PGA Tour has not held an event in the Asheville area since 1942. That changes in five days.

The Biltmore Championship tees off September 17 at The Cliffs at Walnut Cove, a private facility in Arden, North Carolina, roughly eight miles south of downtown Asheville. The tournament runs September 17-20, broadcast on Golf Channel with PGA Tour LIVE on ESPN+. It is the opening event of the FedExCup Fall — eight events that determine who holds a PGA Tour card for the 2027 season.

For the players in the field who are not comfortably inside the number that keeps a card, those four rounds are not incidental. Every shot in the FedExCup Fall carries consequences that extend beyond the tournament itself.

The Course

The Cliffs at Walnut Cove is a Jack Nicklaus design. At 7,167 yards, it fits the standard length of modern PGA Tour venues, but its setting is unlike most of the places Tour players compete. The course sits in a valley that borders the Pisgah National Forest and the Blue Ridge Parkway on roughly 1,000 acres of terrain. Elevation throughout the property sits between 2,100 and 2,250 feet above sea level.

That elevation differential from sea-level Tour venues affects ball flight in ways players need to account for. At altitude, the ball carries farther in the thinner air — club selection adjusts, and players who do not regularly play at elevation typically need to recalibrate during practice rounds. For the Biltmore Championship, that recalibration period is limited to the standard pro-am and practice window before Thursday's opening round.

The course itself, in photographs and course layout, features the Blue Ridge backdrop that makes western North Carolina's private clubs some of the most visually distinctive venues on any circuit. The Biltmore Estate and Explore Asheville committed to a four-year title sponsorship — enough of a runway to establish the event as a genuine FedExCup Fall fixture if the inaugural week delivers.

Why the FedExCup Fall Matters

The FedExCup Fall is eight tournaments following the conclusion of the regular season and playoffs. Players below the cut line for automatic PGA Tour card retention use this window to accumulate enough points to keep their status for 2027. A strong week in Asheville — especially for a player on the bubble — can be career-preserving. A poor week, or a missed cut, can be the difference between full Tour status and a return to qualifying school.

That structure gives tournaments like the Biltmore Championship a competitive edge that not all Tour events carry: the field is almost uniformly motivated. Players near the top of the standings are protecting and extending their lead. Players near the boundary are fighting for their professional livelihood. The tension that produces tends to be visible in how the leaderboard plays out.
