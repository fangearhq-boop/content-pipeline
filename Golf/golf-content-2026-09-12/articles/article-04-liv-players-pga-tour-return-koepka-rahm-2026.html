Brooks Koepka Is Already Back on the PGA Tour. Rahm, DeChambeau, and Smith Face a Much Less Clear Path.

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | September 12, 2026

Koepka accepted the PGA Tour's reinstatement terms — a $5 million charity payment, five years without equity grants, no bonus access in year one. The Tour offered the same deal to Rahm, DeChambeau, and Smith. None accepted. LIV is now bankrupt and all contracts are terminated. No new path has been announced.

The first LIV Golf player to find his way back to the PGA Tour did it the hard way.

Brooks Koepka accepted reinstatement through the PGA Tour's "Returning Member Program" before LIV Golf filed for bankruptcy. The terms: a $5 million donation to a charity designated by the Tour, no access to equity grants for five years, and no participation in the bonus pool during his return year. It is a significant financial penalty, calibrated to reflect the Tour's position that a player who left for a rival circuit had forfeited certain rights in the process of leaving.

Koepka agreed. He is playing PGA Tour events. The LIV chapter of his career is closed.

The Offer That Rahm, DeChambeau, and Smith Declined

PGA Tour CEO Brian Rolapp extended the same reinstatement offer to Jon Rahm, Bryson DeChambeau, and Cameron Smith — with a deadline. All three declined. At the time, LIV Golf was still operating and player contracts were still active. Rolapp said the Tour was "not inclined to discuss any paths back while players are under contract."

That position made sense in context. Players under active LIV contracts had agreed to be there. The Tour had no obligation to negotiate a return for competitors who had voluntarily left.

That context has now changed. LIV Golf filed Chapter 11 on September 8. All 48 player contracts were terminated at the time of filing. Rahm, DeChambeau, Smith, and their LIV colleagues are no longer under contract to a competing tour. They are, in a technical sense, free agents.

What the PGA Tour Has Said

Rolapp has not announced a new reinstatement path since the LIV bankruptcy filing. The Tour's public position — that it was not inclined to discuss returns while players were under contract — no longer applies in its original form, but no replacement framework has been offered publicly.

The original penalty structure that Koepka accepted remains on record as the known template. Whether the Tour would apply the same terms to a player of Rahm's stature — arguably the most decorated golfer who joined LIV, a two-time major champion and former No. 1 — or whether the altered circumstances create room for different terms, is an open question.

A LIV 2.0 restart in 2027, as CEO Scott O'Neil has described, would give players a reason to wait. But LIV's ability to actually assemble a field for 2027 depends on players staying out — and the more attractive the PGA Tour's return terms, the harder that becomes.

What's Next

The PGA Tour has not announced updated reinstatement terms for LIV players following the bankruptcy. Rahm, DeChambeau, Smith, and others remain free agents as of September 12. Golf Fanrecap will cover any Tour statement or player movement in the coming weeks as the bankruptcy case develops.
