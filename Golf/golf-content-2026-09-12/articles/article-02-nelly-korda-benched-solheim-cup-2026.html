Angela Stanford Benched Nelly Korda for Saturday Afternoon. It's the Biggest Call of the Solheim Cup.

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | September 12, 2026

After a morning foursomes loss with Allisen Corpuz, World No. 1 Nelly Korda was left off the USA lineup for Saturday afternoon fourballs. Captain Angela Stanford also rested Alison Lee despite Lee's perfect 3-for-3 record. Both are expected back for Sunday's decisive singles session.

When USA captain Angela Stanford named her Saturday afternoon fourball pairings at the 2026 Solheim Cup, two names were absent. Nelly Korda was not in the lineup. Neither was Alison Lee.

Korda, ranked No. 1 in the world, had just come off a 3&2 loss with Allisen Corpuz in the morning foursomes — a match that saw Maja Stark and Linn Grant handle them methodically. It was Korda and Corpuz's first-ever Solheim Cup foursomes defeat together, ending a five-match winning streak as a pairing in the format.

Lee, meanwhile, had been the most consistent American of the entire week. Three matches. Three points. Her partnership with Lauren Coughlin had produced wins in every session they were sent out. Stanford sat her anyway. Coughlin was also rested.

Why Stanford Did It

Stanford's logic is visible, if not stated directly. Korda had just played two sessions on Friday — morning foursomes and afternoon fourballs — plus the Saturday morning session. That's three rounds across roughly 36 hours. A foursomes loss like the one she just experienced carries mental weight alongside the physical demands of the week.

Resting her for four matches on Saturday afternoon preserves energy and — equally important — resets the narrative heading into Sunday. Rather than sending Korda out again on the same day she lost, Stanford puts her in a fresh position: Saturday evening rest, Sunday morning focus, a singles match that begins on a new day and a blank scorecard.

The calculus on Lee is simpler. A player in perfect form is valuable. She will be more valuable Sunday, when points are worth more relative to the match situation, than in Saturday afternoon fourballs where any individual point contributes incrementally. Stanford is banking Lee's form for the session where it matters most.

The Pressure on Sunday

USA goes into Sunday singles down 7-5 at Bernardus Golf. To win or retain the Solheim Cup, the Americans need 9 points from 12 singles matches. That means winning nine outright or some combination of wins and halves that produces 9 points. It is achievable — Solheim Cup singles comebacks have happened — but it requires the depth of the lineup to perform, not just the stars.

Korda is the headline name in the USA draw and one of the most formidable match-play competitors on the women's tour in any format. How Stanford deploys her — her position in the order, her opponent, the context of the overall match when she tees off — will be a significant strategic piece. Stanford's Saturday afternoon benching decision is an investment in getting that decision right Sunday.

Lee's order position will be watched closely as well. Her week has earned her a place at the top of any USA list of trustworthy players heading into the closing session. Stanford's choice to rest her tells you where Lee will be used when the stakes are highest.

What's Next

Sunday singles at Bernardus Golf begin the final day of the 20th Solheim Cup. USA captain lineup — including Korda and Lee's positions — will be released before Sunday morning's first tee time. Golf Fanrecap will have match-by-match coverage throughout the day.
