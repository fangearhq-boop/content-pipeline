Rory McIlroy Has Three FedExCup Titles. He's Coming to Memphis for a Fourth — and History.

By Marcus Cole | August 12, 2026 | Golf Fanrecap

Three times. Three times Rory McIlroy has lifted the FedExCup trophy. The years — 2016, 2019, 2022 — mark a decade of sustained excellence at the highest level of the PGA Tour's season-ending competition. No player in the event's history has won it more than three times.

Starting Thursday at TPC Southwind in Memphis, McIlroy begins his pursuit of a fourth. If he wins it, he will stand alone as the most decorated FedExCup champion in the history of the event.

A Season That Would Be the Best of Anyone Else's Career

Even before the playoffs begin, Rory McIlroy's 2026 campaign ranks among the finest single seasons a golfer has produced in the modern era. He won back-to-back Masters championships — in 2025 and 2026 — becoming only the second player in Masters history to defend the title. His six career major championships place him in rare company: tied with Nick Faldo for the European record.

McIlroy's 2022 season set what seemed like an unreachable bar — FedExCup title plus the Players Championship in the same year. His 2026 season is built around a different kind of achievement: consecutive Augusta victories that punctuate years of near-misses at the major that defined his legacy goals longest. By that measure, 2026 may already be his greatest season before a single playoff stroke has been struck.

A fourth FedExCup title on top of back-to-back Masters would make the argument definitive.

The 32nd-Ranked Entry That Isn't a Concern

McIlroy arrives at TPC Southwind ranked 32nd in FedEx Cup standings, a number that sounds low but understates his actual position. He ran a selective schedule this season, skipping the RBC Heritage, Cadillac Championship, and Travelers Championship — a deliberate choice that leaves him with fewer events played than the players ranked above him, rather than fewer points earned per event.

The playoff format's points reset compresses the field, and McIlroy's 32nd-place standing translates into a meaningful base entering St. Jude. The top seed entering a playoff reset does not win by virtue of their seeding — golf doesn't work that way. A strong week at TPC Southwind from McIlroy puts him in the same position as any other contender entering the BMW Championship. His credentials make that a realistic scenario.

His odds at St. Jude are listed at +1,300. That's a meaningful step behind Scheffler's +500, but reflects respect rather than dismissal. McIlroy at +1,300 is a golfer capable of winning any tournament he enters, not a longshot.

What He's Not Chasing This Week — But Will Be Soon

McIlroy's seventh major championship — which would break his tie with Faldo and set a new European record — will wait for the four majors of 2027. The FedExCup Playoffs carry no major championships. This week in Memphis, and through the three-event sprint to Atlanta, the record he's chasing is the one that matters most in this context: the most FedExCup titles in the history of a competition he has already owned longer than anyone else.

At 36, McIlroy is playing some of the best golf of his life. The back-to-back Masters are proof of that. The question entering the FedEx Playoffs isn't whether he can compete — it's whether this particular three-week window delivers the result that extends his FedExCup record to four.

What's Next

McIlroy tees off Thursday at TPC Southwind for round one of the FedEx St. Jude Championship. The tournament runs August 13-16. If he advances through St. Jude, the BMW Championship at Bellerive Country Club in St. Louis follows on August 20-23, with the Tour Championship at East Lake in Atlanta closing the playoffs August 27-30. The FedExCup champion takes home $18 million — and the record, if McIlroy is the one holding it on August 30.
