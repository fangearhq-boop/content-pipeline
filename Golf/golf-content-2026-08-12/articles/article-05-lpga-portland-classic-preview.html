The LPGA Portland Classic Begins Tomorrow — Powered by Shiho Kuwaki's AIG Women's Open Win

By Ryan Calloway | August 12, 2026 | Golf Fanrecap

While the men's FedEx Cup Playoffs open in Memphis on Thursday, the LPGA Tour tees it up in Portland — and the women's game arrives with momentum that deserves its own attention. The LPGA Portland Classic runs August 13-16 at Columbia Edgewater Country Club in Portland, Oregon. The $2 million purse attracts a strong international field, and the context entering this week is defined by one of the most dramatic finishes the women's game has produced all season.

Kuwaki's AIG Win: A Reminder of How Good This Field Has Gotten

Ten days ago, on August 2 at Royal Lytham and St Annes in England, Shiho Kuwaki won the AIG Women's Open in a two-hole playoff against Esther Henseleit. The finish was extraordinary.

Henseleit, the German star, appeared to have forced the playoff in remarkable fashion — holing a 40-foot birdie putt on the 72nd hole just to survive into extra holes. That is the kind of putt that defines championship golf, the kind that deserves to win. For a hole, it looked like it might. Then the playoff began, and the second sudden-death hole turned everything again: Henseleit's tee shot struck a marshal and deflected into a bush. Bogey. Kuwaki made par. The trophy went to Japan.

Kuwaki's winning score of -5 (72-66-68-73=279) came from a card that was anything but clean on Sunday — her final-round 73 left the door open — but the championship was hers. It was her first career LPGA major in just her seventh major start. She earned $1.5 million from a record $10 million AIG Women's Open prize fund.

Japan has now won two consecutive AIG Women's Opens.

Columbia Edgewater: A Course That Rewards Precision

Columbia Edgewater Country Club is a tight, tree-lined layout that puts a premium on accuracy off the tee and course management throughout. It is not a course that rewards power over precision. The Korean contingent on the LPGA Tour — always well represented in the tour's history and always formidable at Columbia Edgewater's demanding setup — enters the week with their usual depth of talent.

Portland is not always one of the LPGA's spotlight events. The purse, at $2 million, is on the smaller end for a tour that has seen prize fund increases across most of its schedule over the past three seasons. But the timing — right after the AIG Women's Open — gives it a different feel. The momentum from Kuwaki's win, and the conversations it's sparked about the Japanese players on tour, makes Columbia Edgewater a more compelling venue than the numbers alone would suggest.

Who to Watch: Korda's Return, the Korean Contingent, Lydia Ko

Nelly Korda enters Portland having finished T4 at the AIG Women's Open at -2 — close enough to contend, but not close enough to force the kind of playoff drama that unfolded between Kuwaki and Henseleit. Korda has been the face of American women's golf for several years, and Portland offers a chance to redirect the near-miss at Royal Lytham into a weekend where she controls the outcome.

Lydia Ko has shown renewed form this summer, returning to the kind of steady, efficient play that made her one of the sport's most decorated players in the prior decade. Ko at a course that rewards precision over power is a pairing that has produced results in the past.

The full Portland field is deep with international talent. The LPGA tour's global representation in 2026 has reached a point where American players are no longer the default headliners at any given event — the competition across nationalities is genuine and wide, which is part of what makes the tour compelling week to week.

What's Next

Round one of the LPGA Portland Classic begins Thursday, August 13 at Columbia Edgewater Country Club. The tournament runs through Sunday, August 16. The $2 million purse will be distributed across the field, with the winner taking the top share. Portland is one of the LPGA's late-summer events, and its finish will open a stretch run through the end of the LPGA's regular season before the tour's own playoff events take shape in the fall.
