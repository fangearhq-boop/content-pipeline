Scottie Scheffler Is the No. 1 Player in the World. The Player of the Year Debate Says Otherwise.

By Ryan Calloway | August 12, 2026 | Golf Fanrecap

Scottie Scheffler has been the best golfer in the world for 167 consecutive weeks. He has played in 2026 with a consistency that is nearly unmatched in the sport's modern era — five runner-up finishes, nine top-five results, not a single missed cut in a major. He is No. 1 in the world rankings by a margin that reflects not a hot stretch but a sustained standard of excellence.

None of that has translated into wins. One win in 2026, at the American Express in January. And because of it, the PGA Tour Player of the Year debate has taken a shape that would have seemed impossible at the start of the season: Chris Gotterup — with three tournament victories — leads the vote.

Tomorrow at TPC Southwind in Memphis, the FedEx Cup Playoffs begin. This is where Scheffler's 2026 story either turns or continues its frustrating pattern.

The Consistency Paradox

The numbers Scheffler has put up in 2026 would be the envy of almost any player in professional golf. One win. Five runner-up finishes. Nine top-five results in a season that isn't over yet. He is ranked No. 1 in the world — has been for over three years of consecutive weeks — and entered the summer as the consensus favorite for Player of the Year.

But the PGA Tour's Player of the Year award is voted on by the tour's player membership, not determined by a statistical formula. In that human calculus, wins carry weight that proximity-to-wins does not. A player who wins three tournaments tells a story that sticks. Five runner-up finishes tell a different story — an almost story. A story that almost doesn't count.

Scheffler acknowledged the frustration himself: "I've been really close to winning some tournaments, and that can be frustrating."

What Gotterup Has That Scheffler Doesn't

Chris Gotterup has three wins in 2026. Each one is an argument in the Player of the Year debate that doesn't require further explanation — a win is a win in a way that a second-place finish never is, regardless of how strong the field was or how close the margin.

Scheffler has no major championship wins in 2026 to anchor his case. Rory McIlroy won back-to-back Masters titles in 2025 and 2026, claiming the most consequential victories the sport offers. Scheffler's calendar includes no equivalent headline result. The narrative gravitational pull has shifted.

For the Player of the Year vote, which comes after the Tour Championship, recency matters. The final event — at East Lake — carries significant weight in voter memory. How a player performs in the playoffs is not incidental to the outcome; it is part of the evidence.

The Playoffs as Last Chance

Former PGA Tour winners and analysts who follow the Player of the Year race closely have projected that Scheffler likely needs at least two playoff victories to overtake Gotterup's three-win argument. One win might not be enough. The math is blunt: a single trophy, added to one already on the shelf, still puts him behind a player who collected three.

Scheffler enters the FedEx St. Jude Championship as the +500 favorite. He won this specific tournament in 2025, which establishes a track record at TPC Southwind. The Par 70 layout in Memphis heat — with 10-20 mph winds in the forecast — suits the kind of ball-striking precision that defines his game. If there is a venue in the playoffs where Scheffler should win, it is this one.

But must-win situations in golf don't guarantee outcomes. They just define what the outcome means.

What's Next

The FedEx St. Jude Championship begins Thursday, August 13 at TPC Southwind in Memphis. Scheffler is in the field and among the headliners of the first-round groupings. After St. Jude, the BMW Championship follows at Bellerive Country Club in St. Louis on August 20-23, and the Tour Championship at East Lake in Atlanta on August 27-30. Scheffler has three weeks and three tournaments to write a different conclusion to a season that has been remarkable in every measurable way except the one that matters most to the voters.
