The FedEx Cup Playoffs Begin Tomorrow in Memphis — and the Stakes Have Never Been Higher

By Marcus Cole | August 12, 2026 | Golf Fanrecap

The FedEx Cup Playoffs begin Thursday at TPC Southwind in Memphis, Tennessee — and if you need a single word to describe what's on the line, that word is everything. The FedEx St. Jude Championship runs August 13-16, kicking off the three-event sprint to the season's $18 million climax at East Lake Golf Club in Atlanta.

For the top 70 players in the FedEx Cup standings, this week is about survival and positioning. For a few players at the top of the leaderboard, it's about legacy. Memphis is where the 2026 season starts separating the contenders from the pretenders, and the field has the names to match the moment.

The Course: A Par 70 That Punishes Mistakes

TPC Southwind is not a course that lets you coast. At Par 70 and 7,233 yards, with no par-5 cushions to exploit, every stroke matters more than at a typical PGA Tour venue. Low scores are harder to come by — a 66 here carries the weight of a 64 at a Par 72 layout.

The conditions this week amplify the challenge. Temperatures in Memphis are projected to reach 93 degrees Fahrenheit, with winds forecast between 10 and 20 miles per hour across the week. Ball-strikers who can work the ball both ways will have an advantage. Players who rely on length over precision will be exposed.

The heat alone is a test of physical and mental endurance over 72 holes. Memphis in August is not a comfortable place to try to execute a tournament golf swing, and the compressed stakes of the playoff format remove any margin for a throwaway round.

The Field: Scheffler, McIlroy, and 68 Others With Something to Prove

Scottie Scheffler enters TPC Southwind as the betting favorite at +500. The world's No. 1 player has been the most consistent golfer in professional golf in 2026 — 167-plus consecutive weeks at the top ranking, one win at the American Express in January, five runner-up finishes, nine top-five results. He also won the FedEx St. Jude Championship in 2025, making him the defending champion at this venue.

But Scheffler arrives under a specific kind of pressure. He trails Chris Gotterup in the PGA Tour Player of the Year race — Gotterup has three wins to Scheffler's one — and analysts have suggested Scheffler likely needs at least two playoff victories to recapture the award. The next three weeks represent his clearest path to doing it.

Rory McIlroy (+1,300) confirmed his entry at St. Jude and brings his own remarkable storyline: a third FedExCup title would match his own record, and a fourth would be the most in the event's history. He comes in ranked 32nd in FedEx Cup standings entering the playoffs, having taken a selective schedule this season, but the points reset for the playoff format tightens the field enough to give him a legitimate base.

The Playoff Stakes: 500 Points, $18 Million, and Three Weeks

The FedEx Cup Playoffs operate on a three-event structure that progressively narrows the field. The 70 survivors of St. Jude advance to the BMW Championship at Bellerive Country Club in St. Louis on August 20-23. The top 50 from that event advance to the Tour Championship at East Lake in Atlanta on August 27-30, where the overall FedExCup champion is determined.

The winner at TPC Southwind receives 500 FedExCup points — roughly the equivalent of two mid-level regular season wins. In a playoff where points from all three events combine, a strong week at St. Jude can shift the entire landscape. It can also work in reverse: a missed cut here ends the season for any player outside the top 70 who fails to stay in position.

The FedExCup champion takes home $18 million. The format was designed to build drama week by week, and the first chapter always starts at TPC Southwind in August heat.

What's Next

Round one of the FedEx St. Jude Championship begins Thursday, August 13. Final tee times and groupings are set. The field will play 36 holes on Thursday and Friday, with the cut falling on Friday afternoon. The weekend rounds follow Saturday and Sunday, with a champion crowned August 16. Whoever stands on the 18th green at TPC Southwind on Sunday with the trophy in hand will have taken the first step toward Atlanta — and $18 million.
