LIV Golf CEO Meets With LPGA on Women's Expansion — What It Means for the Sport

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | November 19, 2026

LIV Golf CEO Scott O'Neil has held talks with LPGA Tour leadership about a potential women's golf expansion. The discussions, described as exploratory, are said to range from possible arrangements with current LPGA players to the more ambitious idea of a standalone women's LIV tour. No agreement has been announced, but the conversations themselves represent a development that few in golf anticipated this fall.

The backdrop makes the outreach striking. LIV Golf has been in Chapter 11 bankruptcy proceedings since September 2026 and is working toward an expected January 2027 liquidation target. Its men's tour collapsed following the withdrawal of Saudi Public Investment Fund backing in April 2026. And yet its CEO is at the table with women's golf leadership, apparently exploring what a rebuilt version of the LIV concept might look like on the women's side.

What O'Neil Is Reportedly Exploring

Reports characterize the LPGA discussions as genuinely open-ended. Options on the table are said to include signing arrangements with current LPGA Tour stars, potentially offering guaranteed contracts outside the traditional LPGA structure — the same approach that drew men's players to LIV in 2022. A more radical option, the launch of a parallel women's tour, would represent a much larger organizational commitment.

Whether the Saudi PIF would fund either of those options is unknown. The collapse of the men's tour was driven by PIF pulling its financial backing, and without similar resources, O'Neil's discussions with the LPGA would remain conversation rather than contract.

The LPGA's Position

The LPGA Tour has navigated this era of golf disruption carefully. Unlike the PGA Tour, which faced the direct financial competition that drove the LIV standoff, the LPGA has not been a formal target of LIV's expansion efforts — until now. LPGA leadership's willingness to meet with O'Neil suggests at least a curiosity about the potential, or a strategic calculation that it is better to engage than to dismiss.

The PGA Tour, by contrast, has been explicit that there is no reinstatement path for former LIV players until the bankruptcy process concludes. That hard line does not bind the LPGA, which operates independently.

What's Next

These are early-stage talks, and the gap between an exploratory meeting and a signed agreement is vast. Golf Fanrecap will continue monitoring developments as the golf offseason approaches. If the conversations between O'Neil and LPGA leadership produce anything concrete, it would reshape the structural picture of professional women's golf heading into 2027.
