RSM Classic Field: 35 Top-100 Players, Card Battles, and an Open Championship

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | November 19, 2026

When Scottie Scheffler skips a PGA Tour event, the narrative writes itself — the field is weaker, the champion less significant, the week less important. That reading underestimates what is at Sea Island this week. Thirty-five of the world's top-100 players are competing at the RSM Classic, and several of them have far more to gain here than they would in a typical fall event.

The RSM Classic is the final FedEx Cup Fall event of 2026. After Sunday, the PGA Tour goes dark. That means this week is the last opportunity for players on the edge of the Tour card threshold to do something about it.

The Card-Securing Stakes

PGA Tour exemptions are not guaranteed from year to year. Players outside the top finishers in FedEx Cup points must earn their way back through performance or through alternative pathways. For the group sitting just below the threshold, the RSM Classic is the final lever they can pull before the books close on the season.

That dynamic creates genuine desperation golf from a portion of the field — players who have nothing to protect and everything to gain. Those are often the most compelling competitive situations in golf, particularly when the leaderboard gets tight on the weekend.

Players to Watch

Ben James enters as the early favorite at +1950 on the futures market, with Michael Thorbjornsen (+2000) and Michael Brennan (+2050) close behind. All three bring form and course comfort that justifies their position near the top of the board.

Blades Brown deserves particular attention. His win at the World Wide Technology Championship this month demonstrated that his game is in a strong place. Players who have recently won tend to arrive at subsequent events with elevated confidence, and Brown's comfort on PGA Tour layouts of this type fits the RSM Classic format.

Sami Välimäki's presence as the defending champion is not trivial. He knows these courses, has won on them, and enters with a goal that motivates different preparation than a first-time visitor.

Course Dynamics

The RSM Classic uses two courses for the first two rounds. The Plantation Course and Seaside Course both host Thursday and Friday play, with the cut falling to the top 65 plus ties. The weekend moves exclusively to the Seaside Course, where precise iron play and course management on the coastal layout separate contenders from pretenders in the final rounds.

What's Next
