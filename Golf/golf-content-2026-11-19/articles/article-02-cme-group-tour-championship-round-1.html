CME Group Tour Championship Round 1: Thitikul Defends, Korda Chases, $4 Million Waits

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | November 19, 2026

The LPGA season's final chapter opened Thursday at Tiburón Golf Club in Naples, Florida. The CME Group Tour Championship, the richest event in women's golf, started Round 1 with 60 of the world's best players chasing an $11 million purse — including a $4 million check for whoever stands alone at the top after Sunday's final round.

No event on the LPGA calendar concentrates the sport's best talent into a single field quite like this one. The qualifier is simple: finish in the top 60 of LPGA season points. Everyone in this week's draw has earned their place, and the prize at the end is transformative by any standard in professional sports.

Thitikul's Title to Lose

Atthaya Thitikul won the CME Group Tour Championship in 2025 at -26 to par, a commanding performance that announced her status as one of the most dominant players in the women's game. She has not slowed down in 2026, adding a fourth LPGA title at the Lotte Championship. Coming back to Naples as a defending champion who has continued producing at that level is a position few players occupy.

The challenge of defending any title is that the target moves with you. Thitikul knows this course, knows this atmosphere, and knows what it takes to close. Those are significant advantages heading into the opening round.

Korda's Case for the Title

Nelly Korda's claim on the world No. 1 ranking has been consistent throughout 2026. Her season has featured the kind of steady excellence that demonstrates why she sits atop the world rankings — competitive in every event she enters, capable of separating herself in the final rounds. The CME Group Tour Championship represents the last opportunity of the year to validate that ranking with a title at the sport's highest-stakes event.

Korda has the game to handle Tiburón's demands. If she brings her best form into the weekend, she will be in contention.

The Supporting Cast

Haeran Ryu and Charley Hull represent two of the more intriguing non-favorites in the field. Ryu has shown consistent scoring ability throughout the season. Hull, the English player, brings a fan following and a game that fits well on Florida's warm, receptive turf. With 60 players in the field and no cut to thin it out, every round matters, and contenders can emerge from anywhere on the leaderboard through Friday.

What's Next

Round 1 unfolds Thursday, with Round 2 on Friday establishing the leaderboard that will drive the weekend. The 60-player field plays all four rounds at Tiburón — no cut means no one goes home early. Golf Fanrecap will track the CME Group Tour Championship through Sunday's final round.
