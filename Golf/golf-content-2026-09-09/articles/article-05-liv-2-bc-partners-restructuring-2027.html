LIV 2.0 Explained: BC Partners, 10 Events, and What Alternative Golf Looks Like in 2027

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | September 9, 2026

LIV Golf filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy. But the story of alternative professional golf does not end there.

BC Partners, a London-based private equity firm, has committed to funding a restructured LIV Golf beginning in 2027. The deal was described as "signed, close to completion" by league COO Scott O'Neil earlier in September, and the bankruptcy filing has only accelerated the timeline for understanding what LIV 2.0 actually looks like.

The Structure of LIV 2.0

The restructured league will look significantly different from the version PIF bankrolled. Ten events — down from LIV's larger format — with a global footprint anchored by four venues already announced: Saudi Arabia, Australia, South Africa, and Hong Kong.

The structural changes go beyond event count. LIV 2.0 is designed with player equity stakes — meaning the players themselves would hold ownership in the league. An independent board would govern operations, a meaningful departure from the PIF-controlled structure that led to this bankruptcy.

BC Partners brings institutional private equity discipline to an organization that operated for four years without sustainable revenue. The firm's track record includes investments across European sports and media, and its model requires a credible path to profitability — something LIV Golf never demonstrated.

What the Bankruptcy Means for LIV 2.0

The Chapter 11 process gives LIV 2.0 a complicated start. The bankruptcy filing means the new entity is building on an organization whose existing contracts are terminated, whose debts are being restructured in court, and whose credibility with players has been damaged by the outcome of the original structure.

Rahm, DeChambeau, Smith, and others who are owed millions in unsecured claims have no obligation to sign with BC Partners' version of LIV. The new organization is functionally a startup, even if it inherits LIV's brand, team structures, and some infrastructure.

Greg Norman's future role in LIV 2.0 remains uncertain. Norman was the public face of the original LIV — his relationship with BC Partners and with players who feel burned by the bankruptcy will be a significant factor in whether the restructured league retains its identity.

The Player Calculation

For LIV players deciding their futures, LIV 2.0 is a real option — but it requires trust. The original LIV promised guaranteed contracts and delivered a bankruptcy filing. BC Partners is a different financial backer, but the promise is similar: a competitive alternative to the PGA Tour with significant financial upside.

Players who choose LIV 2.0 give up any chance of PGA Tour reinstatement in the near term. The Tour has stated clearly that no easy path back exists. But if LIV 2.0 launches in 2027 with competitive prize money, player equity, and a sustainable model, that trade-off may be worth making for players who still believe in the alternative golf concept.

What's Next

The bankruptcy court process will determine the timeline for BC Partners assuming control and launching the restructured league. Player negotiations will begin in earnest as free agents assess their options. The four announced 2027 venues suggest the schedule is already in planning.

LIV Golf as it existed from 2022 to 2026 is over. Whether LIV 2.0 can succeed where $5 billion in Saudi money could not is the defining question for alternative professional golf — and it will take most of 2027 to get an answer.
