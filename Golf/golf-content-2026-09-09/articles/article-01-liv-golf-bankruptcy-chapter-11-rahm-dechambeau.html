LIV Golf Files Chapter 11 Bankruptcy: All Contracts Terminated, Rahm Leads Creditor List at $7.4M

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | September 9, 2026

LIV Golf has filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection, ending the Saudi-backed breakaway league's run as the world's most controversial golf circuit. The filing, made September 8, 2026, lists more than $500 million in debt and automatically terminates all existing player contracts.

Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, which ended its direct financial backing earlier in 2026 after spending an estimated $5 billion or more since the league's 2022 launch, has agreed to provide $49.6 million in debtor-in-possession financing. That funding will keep operations running during the bankruptcy process as the league attempts to restructure for a 2027 return.

The Players Left Holding the Bill

The bankruptcy has transformed LIV's biggest stars into unsecured creditors. Jon Rahm leads the player creditor list with approximately $7.4 million owed. Bryson DeChambeau is owed approximately $5.7 million, listed as a contingent claim. Dustin Johnson and Cameron Smith are also among the leading player creditors.

These are the players who walked away from PGA Tour cards and multimillion-dollar Tour earnings for what appeared to be ironclad guaranteed contracts. The bankruptcy filing reveals those guarantees were only as strong as LIV Golf itself — and LIV Golf has run out of money.

PGA Tour Commissioner Brian Rolapp has been explicit that there is "no current path back" for LIV players. He named Rahm, DeChambeau, and Smith specifically. The Tour reportedly offered a reinstatement window with a tight deadline before the bankruptcy was finalized; those players declined.

What Happened to $5 Billion

LIV Golf launched in 2022 with extraordinary ambition and PIF resources that seemed limitless. Signing bonuses — some reportedly in the tens of millions for top players — combined with operational costs for a global 54-hole tournament series with no established TV rights revenue created an unsustainable model.

The league never secured a major United States television deal. Without broadcast revenue to offset costs, PIF was funding the entire operation. When PIF withdrew direct support earlier in 2026, the league had no independent revenue stream capable of servicing its debt.

More than $500 million in total debt against limited assets led directly to the Chapter 11 filing.

LIV 2.0 Is the Plan

The bankruptcy does not mean alternative golf is finished. BC Partners, a London-based private equity firm, has committed to funding a restructured LIV Golf starting in 2027. The deal — reported at $250 to $350 million — was described as "signed, close to completion" by league COO Scott O'Neil earlier in September.

The restructured league will operate with 10 events, player equity stakes, an independent board, and a significantly smaller footprint than the original PIF-backed version. Four 2027 events have already been announced: Saudi Arabia, Australia, South Africa, and Hong Kong.

Greg Norman's future role in the restructured league remains unclear.

What's Next

The immediate future is the bankruptcy court process. LIV Golf aims to emerge from Chapter 11 in time for a 2027 season under BC Partners. Whether creditors — including Rahm, DeChambeau, and Smith — receive any of what they are owed will depend on the outcome of that process.

For the players themselves, the decision is urgent: pursue PGA Tour reinstatement through traditional qualifying paths, or bet on LIV 2.0 delivering on its promises. The PGA Tour is not making reinstatement easy. But LIV 2.0 requires trusting a new financial structure built on the wreckage of the old one.

The Saudi golf experiment isn't over. It's just starting a new chapter — a bankruptcy chapter, for now.
