LIV Players' Future: What Rahm, DeChambeau, and Smith Do Now

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | September 9, 2026

LIV Golf's Chapter 11 bankruptcy has automatically terminated every player contract in the league. The players are free — legally, at least. But free does not mean their paths forward are clear.

For Jon Rahm, Bryson DeChambeau, Cameron Smith, and dozens of other LIV players, September 9, 2026 is the beginning of the most consequential career decisions they have ever faced.

The PGA Tour Door: Open, but Narrow

The most obvious destination is the PGA Tour. But PGA Tour Commissioner Brian Rolapp has been direct: there is "no current path back" for LIV players. He named Rahm, DeChambeau, and Smith by name when making that statement.

Before the bankruptcy was finalized, the Tour reportedly offered a reinstatement window with a tight deadline. Rahm, DeChambeau, and Smith declined. The window closed. Rolapp's language since then has been unambiguous.

That said, the contract terminations create a new legal situation. Players who were technically still bound to LIV are now free agents. Whether that changes the Tour's calculus — or forces a legal challenge — remains to be seen.

Traditional qualifying paths still exist. Q-School and Monday qualifying are available to any player without a Tour card. For a former world No. 1 like Rahm, the idea of competing in qualifying school carries enormous logistical and reputational weight. But it is not impossible.

LIV 2.0: A Familiar Option, Different Terms

BC Partners plans to fund a restructured LIV Golf beginning in 2027. Players are under no obligation to sign with LIV 2.0 — their contracts are terminated, and the new entity is a different organization.

But LIV 2.0 offers something the PGA Tour currently does not: a place to play with competitive prize money and, reportedly, player equity stakes. If the BC Partners-funded league delivers on its structure — 10 events, independent board, four announced venues — it could be a credible alternative.

The question is trust. These players committed to LIV and ended up as unsecured creditors. Signing with the restructured version requires believing BC Partners can succeed where PIF ultimately did not.

International and Other Tours

The DP World Tour remains an option for European players like Rahm, who maintains European Tour membership from his amateur and early professional career. Other international circuits — the APIS Tour, Asian Tour, and others — offer competitive golf without the PGA Tour or LIV restrictions.

Some players may piece together a schedule across multiple tours while the legal and organizational landscape settles. Others may step away from professional golf entirely while they assess their options.

What's Next

The next several weeks will be decisive. The bankruptcy process will determine what, if anything, Rahm, DeChambeau, Smith, and other creditors recover from their terminated contracts. LIV 2.0 negotiations will begin in earnest as BC Partners moves from planning to recruiting.

The PGA Tour's position will be tested. If the Tour's top events — including the Presidents Cup, starting September 22 — draw strong fields without LIV players, Rolapp's hardline stance is easier to maintain. If the absences create a competitive vacuum, the pressure to negotiate grows.

Golf's biggest stars are free agents. Where they play in 2027 is now entirely up for grabs.
