Yamashita Six Clear at CPKC Women's Open, 16-Year-Old Deng Steals Saturday's Headlines

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | August 23, 2026

Miyu Yamashita holds a commanding position at the CPKC Women's Open heading into Sunday's final round at Royal Mayfair Golf Club in Edmonton. After a 5-under 65 on Saturday, the Japanese star stands at 19 under par — six shots clear of Gina Kim and Yuri Yoshida, both at 13 under. But the moment of the day in Edmonton belonged to someone else entirely.

Sixteen-year-old Canadian amateur Deng from Calgary fired a 7-under 63 on Saturday — the lowest round of the day — to reach 10 under par and climb inside the top ten. The Edmonton crowd, which turned out in large numbers for the home country's marquee women's golf event, responded with the kind of enthusiasm that transforms a tournament leaderboard story into something more memorable.

Yamashita's Dominance

The numbers tell one story and it is largely unambiguous. Yamashita has played Royal Mayfair's par-70 Stanley Thompson design at a level above the rest of the field all week. The $2.75 million purse is at stake, but so is Yamashita's chance to claim a major LPGA title on an international stage — exactly the kind of marquee result that defines a career.

Kim and Yoshida, both trailing by six, are capable players who could make a run. But a six-shot lead with 18 holes remaining at a par-70 course is a substantial cushion. Yamashita will need to be patient and consistent on Sunday rather than aggressive, and her performance through 54 holes suggests she has the game to close it out.

A Nation Behind Deng

The 63 Deng shot on Saturday gave the Canadian fans the kind of local storyline the CPKC Women's Open always hopes to generate. At 16 years old, she is playing an event where Canadian legends like Brooke Henderson — the 2025 CPKC champion — have built their legacies. Henderson is in the field and her presence added to an Edmonton atmosphere that genuinely loves this tournament.

The CPKC Women's Open regularly draws some of the most passionate gallery crowds on the LPGA Tour. Sunday will be no exception. Whatever the final leaderboard shows, the story of a teenager from Calgary firing a 63 at the national women's open is one the Canadian golf community will be talking about for years.

What's Next

The CPKC Women's Open final round takes place Sunday, August 23 at Royal Mayfair Golf Club in Edmonton, Alberta. After the Canadian event concludes, the LPGA Tour continues toward the season's remaining schedule. For Yamashita, a Sunday victory would represent her biggest professional title to date.
