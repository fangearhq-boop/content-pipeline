### STORY 2: LIV Golf October 13 Deadline

**Long-Form Post**
Twelve days.

That's how long is left before the October 13 deadline for players to decide on LIV Golf's merger framework. The deal, as structured, offers participating players a 52.5% equity stake in the combined entity — a figure that would make them among the most financially empowered athletes in professional sports.

And yet several high-profile names haven't committed.

Bryson DeChambeau — arguably the most recognizable face in LIV Golf — has not publicly announced his position. Sources indicate at least six marquee players remain undecided as the deadline approaches.

The October 13 date isn't soft. It was set publicly, accepted by the relevant parties, and carries the weight of a process that has already been running for months. What happens after it passes — whether extensions are possible or whether non-signers fall outside the framework — isn't entirely clear.

We're 12 days out. The next 12 matter.

### STORY 3: LOTTE Championship — Hawaii

**Long-Form Post**
The LPGA Tour heads to Hawaii this week for the LOTTE Championship at Hoakalei Country Club in Ewa Beach — and the field assembled at one of golf's most scenic venues is as strong as the fall schedule gets.

Nelly Korda arrives as the headliner after winning three of her last six starts on tour. A victory at the LOTTE would extend a stretch of dominance that's been the defining story of the LPGA's second half of 2026.

The competition isn't short of quality. Patty Tavatanakit, Atthaya "Jeeno" Thitikul, and Lydia Ko are all in the field. Thitikul, who reached world No. 1 earlier this season, has been knocking on the door in recent weeks.

The $2.1 million purse is part of the LPGA's premier fall stretch. Hoakalei's oceanfront layout adds a visual dimension that few venues on any tour can match.

Round 1 is underway today. Full coverage all week.

### STORY 5: Presidents Cup International Losing Streak

**Long-Form Post**
The International team in the Presidents Cup hasn't won since 1998.

That was Royal Melbourne. The International side beat the United States 20.5-11.5, a result so dominant it felt like a turning point for the event. Instead, it became the last chapter of a competitive era that has not returned.

Eleven consecutive losses followed. The most recent, 17-13 at Medinah just days ago, extended the streak into a territory most team sports competitions never reach. USA has won every Presidents Cup of the 21st century.

Trevor Immelman will lead the International team into the 2028 edition. The challenges are structural: the International side draws from players outside Europe and the United States, meaning its pool lacks the organizational depth of Ryder Cup-calibrated European players. The selection process, the preparation timeline, the captains' pick philosophy — all have been questioned after every defeat.

What changes now? Golf Fanrecap takes a look at what would actually need to be different in 2028 for the outcome to have any chance of being different too.
