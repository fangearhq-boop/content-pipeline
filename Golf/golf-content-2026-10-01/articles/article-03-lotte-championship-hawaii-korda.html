The LOTTE Championship Opens in Hawaii — Korda Is Chasing Back-to-Back Wins

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | October 1, 2026

The 2026 LOTTE Championship gets underway this week at Hoakalei Country Club in Ewa Beach, Hawaii, and the LPGA Tour's premier fall stretch opens with one of the more compelling individual storylines of the season: Nelly Korda is here, she is the No. 1 ranked player in the world, and she is chasing back-to-back victories.

The tournament runs October 1-4 with a $2.1 million purse. The field is strong. The conditions will be unlike anything players see on the continental schedule. And Korda enters as the clear favorite.

Korda's 2026 Form

Nelly Korda has won three of her last six LPGA starts. That is a rate of production that defines a dominant stretch rather than a single hot week. A win this week would give her back-to-back victories and extend what is already the most sustained run of her 2026 season into the fall calendar.

Korda has held the No. 1 world ranking through this stretch. Her ball-striking has been consistent, and her short game has been particularly sharp at events where the greens allow for creative approach play. Hoakalei Country Club, with its oceanfront layout and coastal conditions, will test a different set of skills — and Korda has shown this year that she can adapt.

The Rest of the Field

The LOTTE Championship field is not a soft draw. Patty Tavatanakit, a past champion at this event, brings form and familiarity with the conditions. Lydia Ko, one of the most decorated players of her generation, is in the field and has shown competitive sharpness in recent weeks.

Atthaya Thitikul — known as Jeeno on tour — may be the most dangerous player in the field behind Korda. Thitikul held the No. 1 world ranking earlier in 2026 and has come close to a win in several recent events without closing it out. Hawaii could be where that changes. She has the ball speed and competitive composure to win any event she enters, and this week's field gives her one of the more realistic opportunities of the fall stretch.

Hoakalei Country Club and Hawaii Conditions

Hoakalei Country Club sits in the Ko Olina area of Ewa Beach on Oahu's western shore. The course plays as a par 71 at approximately 6,600 yards, shorter than many LPGA layouts but demanding in ways that raw yardage does not capture. The oceanfront setting brings trade winds that shift throughout the round, coastal humidity that affects ball flight differently than the dry air of inland venues, and firm conditions that punish imprecise approaches.

The wind, in particular, is the variable that separates this event from anything players face on the continental schedule. Shots that would hold a line in calm conditions are pushed or pulled at Hoakalei. Club selection becomes a test of judgment rather than just distance control. Players who grew up playing in wind — or who have developed shot shapes that work in crosswinds — tend to perform better here than their statistics might suggest.

The Hawaii setting also gives the LOTTE Championship a distinct identity within the LPGA calendar. It is not a standard resort course staged to be spectator-friendly. It is a genuine test in a genuinely unusual environment, and the players who win here tend to earn it.

The Fall Stretch Begins

The LOTTE Championship is the opening event of the LPGA's fall stretch — a run of tournaments that carries real stakes for year-end standings and Player of the Year positioning. For Korda, a win this week would add to an already compelling case for Player of the Year honors.

For Thitikul, a win would signal her return to the top of the world rankings. For Tavatanakit and Ko, this is familiar territory — both players have proven they can win in Hawaii and in conditions that challenge the rest of the field.

Round 1 is underway. The leaderboard will take shape over the opening 18 holes, and by Friday the contenders will be clear. Korda is the story to watch, but this field is deep enough that the week ahead is unlikely to follow the expected script.
