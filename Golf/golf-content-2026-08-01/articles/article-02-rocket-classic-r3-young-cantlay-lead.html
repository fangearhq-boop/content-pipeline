Cameron Young and Patrick Cantlay Share Lead at Final-Ever Rocket Classic

By Ryan Calloway | August 1, 2026

DETROIT, MICHIGAN — The last-ever Rocket Classic is heading into Sunday with a leaderboard that could not have been scripted better. Cameron Young and Patrick Cantlay share the 54-hole lead at 10-under par at Detroit Golf Club, setting up a final-round duel with 500 FedExCup points and a piece of history on the line.

Rocket Companies is ending its title sponsorship of the PGA Tour event after 2026. Whatever happens Sunday will close the book on a tournament that gave Detroit professional golf for years and bows out with one of the most dramatic setups in its history.

Young's Career Round

Cameron Young arrived at this week's second round ranked well down the field. He left it having fired a 61 — a number that vaulted him 68 spots up the leaderboard in a single afternoon. The round included six consecutive birdies, a stretch of golf that turned the Rocket Classic leaderboard upside down.

Young has been knocking on the door of a major breakthrough for two seasons. A win Sunday on a renovated, toughened Detroit Golf Club layout — now a par 70 restored to Donald Ross's 1914 design — would be one of the more significant victories of the year on Tour.

Cantlay's Steady Climb

Patrick Cantlay matched Young's pace the way Cantlay tends to do things: efficiently, without theater. He sits at the same 10-under mark heading into Sunday through a methodical approach that has put him in the final group at majors and big events with regularity over his career.

Cantlay is a former FedExCup champion and has the experience of playing under pressure on a Sunday with everything on the line. His game suits the renovated Detroit Golf Club layout, which rewards precise ball-striking over length.

The Chasing Pack

Rickie Fowler and Matt Wallace are one back at 9-under, giving Sunday a four-player cluster at the top that could collapse into each other quickly on a par-70 course where birdies are possible but mistakes are punished. The small margin separating the field means the final round will have scoreboard swings all day.

FedExCup Stakes

The Rocket Classic winner earns 500 FedExCup points. With only the Wyndham Championship remaining on the regular season schedule after this event, Sunday's champion will have an enormous say in who enters the postseason with momentum — and who outside the top 70 gets a final lifeline at the Wyndham.

The FedExCup Playoffs begin August 13 at the FedEx St. Jude Championship in Memphis. Players outside the playoff cutline after the Wyndham are finished for the season.

The Final Goodbye

Detroit Golf Club underwent a substantial renovation before this season, returning the Donald Ross design to its original 1914 specifications and trimming the layout from a par 72 to a par 70. The result is a tougher, more demanding test that has revealed itself over the first three rounds as a championship-caliber course.

When the winner lifts the Rocket Classic trophy Sunday afternoon, it will be for the last time. The tournament ends here.

What's Next

The final round of the 2026 Rocket Classic tees off Sunday, August 2, at Detroit Golf Club. Coverage is available on Golf Channel and CBS. After the Rocket Classic, the PGA Tour's regular season concludes with the Wyndham Championship before the FedExCup Playoffs begin August 13 in Memphis.
