FedExCup Bubble: Koivun at No. 70, Koepka and Finau Fighting for Playoff Lives

By Marcus Cole | August 1, 2026

Jackson Koivun, 21 years old and one week removed from the best result of his professional career, sits at exactly No. 70 in the FedExCup standings. That is the last spot that advances to the FedExCup Playoffs. He holds it by a margin that could evaporate this weekend.

Outside the line: Brooks Koepka, Tony Finau, and Keegan Bradley — three established veterans and former major champions trying to hold off a hard cutoff with only two tournaments left on the regular-season schedule.

Koivun's Slim Cushion

Koivun won the 3M Open last week, earning 500 FedExCup points and launching himself into the playoff picture. The win was the first of his young PGA Tour career, and it came at exactly the right time — or almost exactly the right time. He lands at No. 70, not 69, not 65. The last spot. One week after winning a PGA Tour event, he is already in danger of falling out of it.

The FedExCup standings shift with every tournament result. The Rocket Classic, currently in its final round Sunday at Detroit Golf Club with 500 points for the winner, will push and pull positions across the standings. Koivun is not playing this week, meaning he watches his cushion from home.

The Veterans Outside the Line

Brooks Koepka won the 2023 PGA Championship and has been one of the more decorated major champions of his generation. He is currently outside the top 70. Tony Finau, a consistent PGA Tour presence with multiple tour wins, is also on the wrong side of the line. Keegan Bradley, the 2011 PGA Championship winner and a former Ryder Cup captain, is outside as well.

None of these players are in danger of losing their PGA Tour cards. But missing the FedExCup Playoffs represents a significant financial and competitive shortfall for established veterans who expect to be competing in Memphis in August.

The Path to Memphis

The FedEx St. Jude Championship in Memphis begins August 13. Only the top 70 players in FedExCup points after the Wyndham Championship — the final regular-season event — advance to that first playoff event.

The Rocket Classic finishes Sunday with 500 points available. The Wyndham Championship follows as the last chance. Players outside the bubble need strong finishes at one or both events, and they need players above them to falter.

The math is tight. For Koepka and Finau in particular, a deep run at the Wyndham may be the only realistic path to Memphis.

Why This Matters

The FedExCup Playoffs are where the season's biggest prize money is concentrated. The champion of the FedEx St. Jude leads into the BMW Championship and then the Tour Championship at East Lake, where the top players compete for the largest single-event payout on Tour.

Missing the playoffs is not just a matter of prestige. For players outside the top 70 who needed a strong FedExCup season to offset a slower year, the Wyndham Championship becomes a must-play-well situation. The pressure that creates — veterans trying to manufacture results in the final week of the regular season — tends to produce drama.

What's Next

The Rocket Classic finishes Sunday, August 2, and will adjust the FedExCup standings significantly. The Wyndham Championship follows as the final regular-season event. After the Wyndham, the top 70 in FedExCup points advance to the FedEx St. Jude Championship in Memphis beginning August 13. Players currently outside the line have limited runway left.
