Haeran Ryu Leads AIG Women's Open R3, History Within Reach at Royal Lytham

By Marcus Cole | August 1, 2026

LANCASHIRE, ENGLAND — Haeran Ryu of South Korea stands one win away from something women's golf has not seen in thirteen years. Entering Round 3 of the AIG Women's Open at Royal Lytham & St Annes Golf Club, Ryu leads at 7-under par, one shot clear of Japan's Shiho Kuwaki, with the rest of a stacked leaderboard scrambling to close the gap.

If Ryu wins on Sunday, she will have captured three major championships in a single calendar year. The last woman to do that was Inbee Park in 2013.

The Leaderboard After Round 2

Ryu has been the model of consistency all week at Royal Lytham, a par-71 course with a history of producing dramatic final rounds. Kuwaki at 6-under sits alone in second. Yealimi Noh and Jeeno Thitikul are tied at 5-under in third.

Nelly Korda is approximately five shots back, but she carries her own piece of history into Round 3. Korda has already claimed two majors in 2026 — the ANA Chevron Championship and the US Women's Open. A Korda victory Sunday would also give her three majors this season and tie the same Park record.

England's Charley Hull and Lottie Woad, both roughly five back, are chasing a home major in front of a strongly partisan crowd.

Ryu's 2026 Major Season

Before arriving at Lytham, Ryu had already clinched the KPMG Women's PGA Championship and the Evian Championship in 2026. That two-major haul was already one of the most accomplished seasons in recent women's golf. A third title would move her into a tier shared only by Inbee Park in the modern era.

Ryu is 27 years old and has shown no sign of the nerves that typically accompany a run at a record. She has hit fairways, avoided the deep rough that has punished challengers, and given herself birdie looks on the holes that matter.

Korda's Parallel Run

The mathematics of the leaderboard means this is not a two-horse race from a scoring standpoint — but it is, historically speaking, exactly that. Ryu or Korda winning would produce the same outcome: a 2026 three-major season, something the sport has not witnessed since Inbee Park swept the ANA Inspiration, KPMG Women's PGA, and US Women's Open in 2013.

Korda has won two of the four women's majors already contested in 2026. She is five shots back with 36 holes to play at Lytham — a deficit that is entirely closable, particularly if Ryu stumbles on a golf course that has seen final-round leaderboards shuffled dramatically in the past.

What's Next

Round 3 is underway on Saturday at Royal Lytham. The final round is scheduled for Sunday, August 2. The AIG Women's Open carries a $10 million purse, the largest in women's major history. Coverage is available on Golf Channel and through LPGA.com. If Ryu can hold her lead through the weekend, she will claim the final women's major of 2026 and write her name into golf history.
