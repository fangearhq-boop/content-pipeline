Scottie Scheffler's Strange 2026: Five Runner-Ups, One Win, Still No. 1

By Ryan Calloway | August 1, 2026

Scottie Scheffler is entering the FedExCup Playoffs as the No. 1 seed. He is the World No. 1. He has 10 top-10 finishes on the year. And he has one tournament win in 2026 — the American Express in January.

It is one of the strangest dominant seasons professional golf has produced in recent memory.

The Numbers Don't Add Up

By almost every metric, Scheffler has been the best golfer in the world this season. But the wins haven't come. He has five runner-up finishes in 2026, a number that is historically remarkable on its own. According to PGA Tour records, Scheffler and Cameron Young — who had five second-place finishes in 2022 — are the only players in the past 40 years to accumulate five or more runner-up results in a single season on Tour.

Runner-ups mean you played well enough to win. They also mean you didn't.

The Major Question

The sharpest edge of the 2026 narrative cuts at the majors. Scheffler went 0-for-4 at the four major championships this season. Rory McIlroy won the Masters. The US Open at Shinnecock produced a different champion. Scheffler finished T4 with Tommy Fleetwood at The Open Championship at Royal Birkdale. The PGA Championship at Aronimink also slipped by.

For a player who won the Masters in 2022 and again in 2024 and has been the consensus best golfer in the world for three years, going major-less through all four in a single season is a gap his results otherwise can't explain.

He also missed the cut at the Scottish Open — his first missed cut in more than four years. It was a brief aberration, but it stood out on a résumé that has otherwise been built on relentless presence near the top of every leaderboard he enters.

What Scheffler Says

Scheffler's own read on the season was measured and self-aware. "I think I've been really close to winning some tournaments, and that can be frustrating," he said in July. That's it. No panic. No crisis mode. Just an acknowledgment that the margin between No. 1 in the world and actually winning is sometimes smaller than the box score makes it look, and sometimes wider.

The Playoff Picture

None of this changes the fact that Scheffler arrives at the FedExCup Playoffs in the best position of any player in the field. He is the No. 1 seed entering the FedEx St. Jude Championship in Memphis on August 13. He has accumulated his points the hard way — through a volume of top-10s rather than a wave of victories — which is itself a kind of consistent excellence.

The three playoff events (St. Jude, BMW Championship, Tour Championship) run through August and into September. Scheffler has won the Tour Championship before. His game is suited for the pressurized formats of the postseason. The question that will follow him into Memphis is whether the playoff stage is where 2026's win total finally starts reflecting his ranking.

What's Next

Scottie Scheffler skipped the Rocket Classic and will enter the FedExCup Playoffs as the No. 1 seed. The FedEx St. Jude Championship in Memphis begins August 13. Scheffler will be one of the top draws throughout the three-event playoff stretch. Whether he can convert his consistent excellence into a postseason run — and perhaps his second major or additional wins to close out 2026 — is the defining story as the PGA Tour's regular season comes to an end.
