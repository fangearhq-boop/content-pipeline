Roussin-Bouchard Bogey-Free Through 36 Holes at Maybank Championship

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap

October 31, 2026

Pauline Roussin-Bouchard has played 36 holes at the Maybank Championship in Kuala Lumpur. She has not made a single bogey. That stat, sitting alongside a T2 position at -11 on the leaderboard, tells the story of a player who has not just survived the halfway mark — she has done it without losing a shot to par.

Bogey-free golf through the halfway point of an LPGA Tour event is rare. It requires the kind of consistent decision-making, iron control, and putting certainty that professional golfers aim for across every round but rarely achieve across multiple. Roussin-Bouchard has done it across two, and she arrives at Saturday's Round 3 with a mindset and a momentum that is genuinely difficult to manufacture.

The Statistical Significance

To be clear about what bogey-free golf through 36 holes means: it is not just about the scoreline. A player who avoids bogeys across two rounds has managed risk well, recovered from difficult positions without giving shots back, and navigated pressure moments — the tough par-4, the bunker that catches everyone — without conceding to the course.

Roussin-Bouchard at -11 is 2 shots behind defending champion Miyu Yamashita at -13. The gap is manageable, and it has been accumulated entirely through birdies, not through scrambling from bogeys. There is no weight of dropped shots to carry into Round 3. She arrives fresh, clean, and positioned.

The Maybank Championship Context

Kuala Lumpur Golf and Country Club is a course that rewards the kind of bogey-avoidance Roussin-Bouchard has demonstrated. The layout presents enough challenge to punish loose approaches and careless putts, but it rewards players who can commit to their lines and execute. Through two rounds, Roussin-Bouchard has executed.

The LPGA Tour field at the Maybank Championship is strong. Behind Roussin-Bouchard and López Ramírez at -11 sit A Lim Kim at -10 and a group at -9 that includes Amy Yang, Charley Hull, Maja Stark, and Sophia Schubert — all capable of a run if Saturday produces a low number from them and a difficult one from the leaders.

Yamashita's 2-shot advantage is real but not large. At an LPGA Tour event with a legitimate field, two shots is a conversation-starter on Saturday morning and not necessarily a conclusion by Saturday evening.

Roussin-Bouchard's Path to Contention

A player who has gone bogey-free for 36 holes is, by definition, keeping the door open for herself. She does not need to manufacture a dramatic swing on Saturday — she needs to keep doing what she has been doing. Birdie-birdie-par golf is more sustainable than birdie-bogey-birdie-bogey golf, and Roussin-Bouchard has shown she is playing the former.

If she continues playing bogey-free golf in Round 3 and adds a handful of birdies, she will either be tied with Yamashita or ahead of her entering Sunday. That is not a prediction — it is the math. And the math currently runs in Roussin-Bouchard's favor.

What's Next

Round 3 of the Maybank Championship tees off Saturday at Kuala Lumpur Golf and Country Club. Roussin-Bouchard will play in one of the final groups, 2 behind Yamashita. Whether she extends her bogey-free streak through a third round — and whether that streak becomes the story of the tournament's back nine on Sunday — is the subplot to watch heading into the weekend.
