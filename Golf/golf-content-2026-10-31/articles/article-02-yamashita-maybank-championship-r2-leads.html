Yamashita Leads Maybank Championship at -13, Defending Champion on Track

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap

October 31, 2026

Miyu Yamashita came to Kuala Lumpur as the defending champion at the Maybank Championship. After 36 holes, she leads it. The Japanese LPGA star carded a second-round 66 to reach -13 overall — 2 shots ahead of Julia López Ramírez and Pauline Roussin-Bouchard, both at -11.

The defending champion storyline in women's golf carries its own particular weight. To return to a title venue, navigate two rounds under a different kind of expectation, and still sit atop the leaderboard heading into the weekend takes more than form. It takes composure. Yamashita has shown both through 36 holes.

Yamashita's Round 2: What a 66 Means Here

A 66 at Kuala Lumpur Golf and Country Club on the LPGA Tour is not a gift. The course rewards precision and punishes lapses. Six under par in the second round is the kind of day that consolidates a lead, closes out competition from the middle of the field, and positions a player for a genuine Sunday run.

Yamashita's move to -13 through the halfway point puts her in the commanding position a defending champion needs. She has the experience of winning here. She has the game to protect a lead or extend one. The 2-shot margin is comfortable but not comfortable enough to coast — and she will know that.

The Challengers: López Ramírez and Roussin-Bouchard

The two players at -11 both present different kinds of threat heading into Round 3.

Julia López Ramírez brings an aggressive ball-striking game that can produce low numbers quickly. She has not won at this level yet, but her R2 positioning shows she belongs in the conversation.

Pauline Roussin-Bouchard has not made a single bogey through 36 holes. That kind of discipline — not chasing birdies at the cost of pars, not letting one hole bleed into the next — tends to be sustainable. A player who arrives at Saturday's third round bogey-free and 2 back of the leader is a player who is managing the tournament rather than reacting to it.

A Lim Kim at -10 adds another former LPGA champion to the mix, 3 shots back but present enough to make a move on a good Saturday. Amy Yang, Charley Hull, Maja Stark, and Sophia Schubert all sit at -9 — still within range if the leaders stumble.

Defending at the Maybank

Yamashita's win last year was built on similar qualities — consistent ball-striking, course management suited to Kuala Lumpur's demands, and putting that didn't leak strokes on days when others were pressing. If she replicates that approach across R3 and R4, she will be difficult to catch.

The LPGA field at the Maybank Championship is legitimate. The names behind Yamashita on the leaderboard are not decoration — they are players with the capacity to shoot 65 or lower when everything works. But the defending champion leads, and she earned this position by playing smart, clean golf across two rounds.

What's Next

Round 3 of the Maybank Championship takes place Saturday at Kuala Lumpur Golf and Country Club. Yamashita will play in one of the final groups alongside López Ramírez and Roussin-Bouchard. The lead is hers to hold or lose. How she responds to the pressure of defending a title she won 12 months ago will define the weekend's story. If she plays the way she has for two rounds, she will be standing in the same position Sunday afternoon.
