Andrew Novak Leads Mexico Open at -14 After Course-Record-Tying 61

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap

October 31, 2026

Andrew Novak made himself impossible to ignore at the VidantaWorld Mexico Open on Friday. His second-round 61 tied the course record at VidantaWorld in Puerto Vallarta and pushed him to -14 overall — 3 shots ahead of five players at -11 heading into Round 3 on Saturday.

The number that matters is 61. Novak went through the card with the kind of round that leaves leaderboards reshuffled and conversations started: long, clean off the tee, sharp with his irons, and a putter that didn't give him a reason to complain. A course-record-tying performance doesn't happen by accident. It happens when everything aligns.

The Leaderboard After 36 Holes

Novak's -14 is the kind of lead that looks comfortable on paper. In practice, a 3-shot cushion over five players at -11 is anything but a guarantee. That group — Ben Griffin, Kihei Akina, Matthieu Pavon, Adrien Dumont de Chassart, and Kensei Hirata — covers a range of playing styles and experience, and any of them is capable of closing 3 shots on a Saturday afternoon in Mexico.

Griffin is a measured ball-striker who rarely beats himself. Akina has shown course-management instincts that play well under pressure. Pavon brings European Tour pedigree and a game that travels well internationally. Dumont de Chassart has a swing built for consistent iron play. Hirata adds a fifth voice to a genuinely contested leaderboard.

All five are 3 back. All five will be watching Novak's Saturday scorecard in real time.

Novak's Position Heading Into Round 3

54-hole leaders do not always close. But Novak's margin, combined with the form he showed in Round 2, suggests Saturday is his to lose rather than the field's to take. A course-record-tying round earns credibility — it suggests the game is in a place where par feels like a mistake rather than an achievement.

The question for Saturday is not whether Novak can stay in front. It is whether he can play the kind of conservative, mistake-minimizing golf that preserves a lead while the field presses. The tendency for leaders to tighten up on moving day is real. Whether Novak's R2 confidence carries into Saturday will tell us more about this week than any single hole.

VidantaWorld and the Mexico Open Context

The VidantaWorld Mexico Open is a PGA Tour event in Puerto Vallarta, and the course at VidantaWorld rewards the kind of game that combines distance with accuracy. Novak's R2 showed both. The 61 that ties the course record is the kind of round that becomes part of a tournament's local conversation regardless of what happens next.

For Novak, the Mexico Open represents a chance at a PGA Tour win — always a significant professional milestone — in a field that includes legitimate contenders within striking distance. He goes into Saturday as the leader, the owner of the day's signature round, and the player every other name on the leaderboard needs to beat.

What's Next

Round 3 of the VidantaWorld Mexico Open tees off Saturday. Novak will play in one of the final groups alongside members of the -11 cluster. The 54-hole leader at a PGA Tour event is, all else being equal, the favorite to win — but five players at -11 give Saturday's round a contested, compelling shape.
