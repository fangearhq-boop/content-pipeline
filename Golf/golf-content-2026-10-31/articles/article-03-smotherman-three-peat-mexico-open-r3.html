Smotherman's Three-Win Chase: Back-to-Back PGA Tour Champion Arrives at Mexico Open

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap

October 31, 2026

Austin Smotherman came to Mexico with momentum that most golfers spend a career trying to find. He has won two consecutive PGA Tour events in October 2026, and at the VidantaWorld Mexico Open, he has an opportunity to win a third in a row — a run that would rank among the most remarkable stretches of the season.

Three consecutive PGA Tour wins is a rare achievement at any point in a season. To do it in October, across different venues and fields, requires consistency of execution that separates a peak run from everything else a player produces in a year. Smotherman has done two. Round 3 Saturday gives him the setting to start chasing the third.

The October Streak in Context

Back-to-back PGA Tour wins in the same month is not something that appears on the schedule — it happens when a player's game peaks at the right time and doesn't let up. Smotherman's run in October 2026 has produced consecutive titles, and arriving in Puerto Vallarta for the Mexico Open is the next test of whether that peak continues or whether the field finally catches up.

The historical significance of three consecutive PGA Tour wins would put Smotherman's name in a category shared by very few active players. It is the kind of stretch that elevates a career narrative — from a player who can win to a player who can dominate a window of the season.

The Current Situation: Novak Leads at -14

The honest framing of Smotherman's Saturday is that he needs to contend from wherever he sits entering Round 3. Andrew Novak currently leads the tournament at -14 after his course-record-tying 61 in Round 2 — a dominant performance that puts Novak firmly in control. Five players are tied at -11, three shots back.

For Smotherman to factor into Sunday's title conversation, he needs a significant R3 to move into that cluster or ahead of it. Golf's history is filled with 54-hole leaders who did not close — and with players who used Saturday to vault into Sunday contention. But the gap matters, and Novak's Round 2 was exactly the kind of round that builds and sustains leads.

The three-consecutive-win chase is a real storyline. The odds require Smotherman to play the round of his week on Saturday and trust that the leaderboard ahead of him cooperates.

What's Next

Round 3 of the VidantaWorld Mexico Open tees off Saturday at VidantaWorld in Puerto Vallarta. Novak holds the lead at -14. Where Smotherman stands relative to that number — and whether he can use Saturday to position himself for a Sunday title run — will determine how much life the consecutive-win storyline has heading into the final round. History is not made on Friday leaderboards. It is made on Sunday afternoons with everything on the line.
