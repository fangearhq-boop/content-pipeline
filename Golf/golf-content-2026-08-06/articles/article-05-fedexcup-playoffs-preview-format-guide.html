The FedExCup Playoffs Start Next Week — Your Complete Guide to Golf's $25 Million Postseason

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | August 6, 2026

PGA Tour / FedExCup Playoffs / 2026 Season

Next Thursday, August 12, the PGA Tour's FedExCup Playoffs begin in Memphis — and for the first time this season, the stakes will be felt in a single moment rather than across a long weekly accumulation. One event. One cut. One step closer to a $25 million prize.

If you haven't been following the FedExCup standings all season, here is everything you need to know about what happens next.

The Three-Event Format

The FedExCup Playoffs consist of three events played in three consecutive weeks, each narrowing the field and building toward the Tour Championship at East Lake Golf Club in Atlanta.

FedEx St. Jude Championship — August 12-16 | TPC Southwind, Memphis, TN

The field: the top 70 players in the FedExCup standings after the Wyndham Championship. This is the first cut of the Playoffs — roughly a third of the original qualifying field. TPC Southwind is a West Tennessee course that rewards accuracy, consistent iron play, and clutch putting. The tournament has been known for competitive finishes under pressure. After Sunday, the top 50 advance.

BMW Championship — August 20-23 | Bellerive Country Club, St. Louis, MO

The field: the top 50 from St. Jude. Bellerive Country Club is one of the most storied venues in American golf — host to the 1992 and 2018 PGA Championships and the 2018 Ryder Cup. It is a demanding, classic parkland course where the best players tend to separate themselves from the pack. After Sunday, the top 30 advance to the final event.

Tour Championship — August 27-30 | East Lake Golf Club, Atlanta, GA

The final 30 players compete in a format unique to professional golf: staggered starting scores based on FedExCup points standing. Scottie Scheffler, as the No. 1 seed, starts at the most favorable score. Other players start progressively further back depending on their ranking. Whoever accumulates the best total score wins the FedExCup and the $25 million championship prize.

The Contenders

Scottie Scheffler enters as the odds-on favorite — 4,123 points, world No. 1, and the structural advantage of the staggered format at East Lake. He is the clear leader.

Rory McIlroy is the most dangerous challenger. The Northern Irishman has won the FedExCup four times and has demonstrated repeatedly that he knows how to peak in August. His Masters win this year established his 2026 credentials. If anyone can run down Scheffler over three weeks, it is McIlroy.

Xander Schauffele has been one of the most consistent ball-strikers on the Tour for several seasons. Patrick Cantlay has shown the ability to make deep Playoffs runs. Viktor Hovland's game suits the demanding courses the Playoffs tend to visit. Collin Morikawa's iron play is historically precise under pressure.

What's Next

The Wyndham Championship wraps up Sunday, setting the final Playoffs field. Then Memphis. Then St. Louis. Then Atlanta. The road to the FedExCup ends on August 30 at East Lake — where a three-week postseason of extraordinary golf will produce one name on the champion's list.

The best golf of the year is played in August. It starts in six days.
