Shiho Kuwaki Wins the AIG Women's Open After Henseleit's 40-Foot Birdie Forces a Playoff at Royal Lytham

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | August 6, 2026

LPGA / AIG Women's Open / Major Championship

The 2026 AIG Women's Open produced a finish worthy of the tournament's 50th staging — a drama-filled afternoon at Royal Lytham and St Annes that ended with a new major champion and one of the most stunning 72nd-hole putts in recent memory.

Shiho Kuwaki of Japan is the 2026 AIG Women's Open champion after winning a two-hole sudden-death playoff against Germany's Esther Henseleit on August 2. Both players finished regulation tied at 5-under par, a score of 279, with Kuwaki carding a closing 70. The playoff win earned Kuwaki $1,500,000 from a record $10 million total purse — and her first major championship title.

The Shot That Changed Everything

The 72nd hole at Royal Lytham was supposed to be Kuwaki's closing moment. Instead, Henseleit turned it into the most dramatic sequence of the week.

Facing a 40-foot birdie putt on the final hole of regulation — with Kuwaki already in the clubhouse and seemingly holding the trophy — Henseleit struck one of the most important putts of her career. The ball rolled across the contours of the Royal Lytham green and found the bottom of the cup. Sudden-death playoff.

The gallery's reaction told the story: collective shock followed by an eruption of appreciation for a player who had refused to surrender. In a sport where the mental weight of trailing in a major championship often collapses games, Henseleit's composure on that final green was extraordinary.

Kuwaki's Response

What separates champions from contenders is often how they respond when an apparent victory is suddenly in jeopardy. Kuwaki responded with clarity. In the two-hole playoff at Royal Lytham, she made her pars where she needed to, giving Henseleit no path to an opening. Henseleit, perhaps drained by the emotional energy of the 40-foot birdie, could not replicate the magic in overtime.

Kuwaki's victory was the 50th staging of the AIG Women's Open — a milestone anniversary for one of women's golf's four major championships. The record $10 million purse, with Henseleit receiving $994,669 as runner-up, reflected the ongoing investment in women's professional golf at the elite level.

The Rest of the Leaderboard

Ayaka Noh had held the lead heading into the final round but shot 74 on Sunday, finishing T3 as the pressure of major championship Sunday caught up. World No. 1 Nelly Korda and world No. 2 Atthaya Thitikul — entering the week as the two most decorated players in the field — both tied for fourth, continuing a pattern of near-misses in major championships that has begun to follow both players.

What's Next

For Kuwaki, this victory changes her career trajectory immediately. A first LPGA Tour win at a major championship is a launching pad — one that typically brings sponsorship, invitation access, and the kind of competitive confidence that only a major title can provide. The LPGA Tour continues with its remaining fall events, and Kuwaki will arrive at each with the credibility of a Women's Open champion.

For Henseleit, the 40-foot birdie will live in golf history. Runner-up at a major is not where careers end — it is where they are sometimes defined.
