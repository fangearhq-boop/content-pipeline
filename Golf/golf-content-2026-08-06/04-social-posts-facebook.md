With winning scores on this par-70 typically reaching 20-under, the leaderboard compresses quickly and players need to go low early.

The Wyndham has a reputation for dramatic finishes — not because of the trophy, but because of what's at stake beyond it. This year will be no different.

His Legion XIII captain leads with 938.44 points — a margin of 219.04 points over second-placed Bryson DeChambeau (719.40 points). Lucas Herbert sits third with 531.23 points after winning two of the last four LIV events.

### STORY 3: Shiho Kuwaki Wins the AIG Women's Open

**Long-Form Post**
🏆 The 2026 AIG Women's British Open ended with one of the most dramatic finishes in major championship history — and a new champion to match.

Playing at Royal Lytham & St Annes in England, the 50th staging of the AIG Women's Open came down to a two-hole playoff between Shiho Kuwaki of Japan and Germany's Esther Henseleit. Both players finished 72 holes tied at 5-under par (279), setting up the overtime drama.

How they got there was remarkable in itself. Henseleit — who had been trailing heading into the final holes — drained a 40-foot birdie putt on the 72nd hole to force the tie. It was a shock that silenced the Royal Lytham gallery and sent Kuwaki, who had carded a final-round 70, into a playoff she hadn't been anticipating just minutes earlier.

But Kuwaki held her nerve. She won the two-hole sudden-death playoff to claim the title, the trophy, and $1,500,000 from a record $10 million total purse. Henseleit received $994,669 as runner-up.

The rest of the leaderboard told its own story. Ayaka Noh had led entering the final round but shot 74, finishing T3. World No. 1 Nelly Korda and world No. 2 Atthaya Thitikul both finished tied for fourth — a result that will fuel questions about who can challenge them in the major championship setting.

For Kuwaki, this was a life-changing moment — her first LPGA Tour victory was on the sport's grandest stage. For the AIG Women's Open in its 50th year, it was a fitting celebration of everything that makes golf's major championships unpredictable.

The world No. 1 and PGA Tour's most prolific winner of recent years has accumulated 4,123 FedExCup points across 17 events in 2026, placing him far ahead of the field in the race for golf's most lucrative prize: a $25 million FedExCup championship payout.

Scheffler's season has been excellent but also oddly quiet on the winner's side. His last victory was the American Express in January — his 20th career PGA Tour win, completed at 27-under par by four strokes. Since then, he's added five runner-up finishes, including a memorable T2 at the 3M Open behind Min Woo Lee's historic 59, and a narrow defeat to Rory McIlroy at Augusta.

The question the Playoffs will answer: can Scheffler close out a season's worth of consistent excellence with a title run, or will someone — McIlroy, Schauffele, Morikawa — find the hot streak at exactly the right time?

### STORY 5: FedExCup Playoffs Preview

**Long-Form Post**
📅 The FedExCup Playoffs start next week — and if you're not familiar with how they work, here's your complete guide to what's coming.

The FedExCup Playoffs represent golf's version of a postseason: a three-event gauntlet that narrows the PGA Tour field from 70 players down to 30, with a $25 million prize waiting at the end for whoever emerges from the Tour Championship victorious.

**FedEx St. Jude Championship — August 12-16 | TPC Southwind, Memphis, TN**
The opening event. The top 70 players in the FedExCup standings compete. Anyone outside the top 70 after the Wyndham Championship this week misses the Playoffs entirely. TPC Southwind is a demanding West-Tennessee course that rewards accuracy and putting consistency.

**BMW Championship — August 20-23 | Bellerive Country Club, St. Louis, MO**
The field narrows to the top 50 from St. Jude. Bellerive, the historic 1992 and 2018 major venue, is the traditional proving ground of Playoffs contenders. This is where the real sorting happens.

**Tour Championship — August 27-30 | East Lake Golf Club, Atlanta, GA**
The final 30. East Lake uses a staggered starting-score format to reflect the points standings — so Scheffler, as No. 1, begins at a significant advantage. The player who accumulates the best overall score wins the $25 million.

The contenders beyond Scheffler: Rory McIlroy (four-time FedExCup champion), Xander Schauffele, Patrick Cantlay, Viktor Hovland, and Collin Morikawa are all considered capable of the kind of hot streak that can overturn a points lead at the Tour Championship.

The best golf of the year happens in August. It starts next week.
