### STORY 1: LIV Golf Bankruptcy

**FB Long-Form**
Window: 9:00 AM ET

The moment that could redefine the landscape of professional golf may be arriving as soon as this week.

According to reporting from the Financial Times, LIV Golf could file for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection during the week of September 7 — a stunning conclusion for a league that the Saudi Public Investment Fund backed with more than $5 billion since its launch in 2022. PIF announced in April 2026 that it would end its funding commitment at the close of the 2026 season, and without that lifeline, LIV's financial position has become increasingly untenable.

The implications are staggering. LIV has already sent settlement offers to current players who hold contracts with guaranteed money beyond 2026, and those offers are being described as worth only "cents on the dollar" of the original guaranteed amounts. Players who signed multi-year, guaranteed deals to leave the PGA Tour — believing they were securing financial security in exchange for competitive risk — may now receive a fraction of what was promised.

A proposed restructuring, informally dubbed "LIV 2.0," has been circulated to players. The plan involves a pre-packaged bankruptcy that would convert outstanding player claims into equity stakes in the reorganised entity. Private capital firm BC Partners is reportedly in discussions about a $300 million investment for LIV's assets. PIF is expected to contribute a bankruptcy loan under $100 million as part of the process — a far cry from the billions that sustained the league through its first four years.

What does a LIV bankruptcy actually mean for professional golf? In the best-case scenario, a restructured LIV 2.0 continues operating in some form — leaner, with player ownership, and without PIF's direct control. In a more dramatic scenario, some portion of the player pool finds its way back to PGA Tour eligibility, potentially returning marquee names to the circuit they left under contentious circumstances.

The next few days could be among the most consequential in golf in a generation. Watch this space.

### STORY 2: Jon Rahm — $100M LIV Deal

**FB Long-Form**
Window: 11:00 AM ET

When Jon Rahm left the PGA Tour for LIV Golf ahead of the 2024 season, it was the move that defined the breakaway league's ambitions. Here was a two-time major champion — 2021 US Open, 2023 Masters — one of the sport's most dominant and marketable players, choosing the Saudi-backed circuit over the established order.

The deal was reported in various outlets at different figures: some said $150 million, others reported the total guarantee closer to $300 million. What is clear — and what multiple outlets confirmed this week — is that Jon Rahm is still owed more than $100 million from that agreement.

And LIV Golf may file for bankruptcy as soon as this week.

The financial exposure is extraordinary. LIV's settlement offers to players have been described as worth "cents on the dollar" of guaranteed amounts, which means Rahm's nine-figure expectation could be reduced dramatically depending on how any bankruptcy proceedings unfold. The proposed "LIV 2.0" restructuring would give players equity stakes in a reorganised entity — meaningful if the restructured league succeeds, worth little if it does not.

Rahm has been one of LIV's genuinely compelling players — still elite, still winning, still the kind of name that drives attention. His presence gave the league credibility at a moment when it desperately needed it. But credibility and financial security are different things, and the contractual promises made during LIV's spending peak may prove very difficult to honour in a bankruptcy context.

Whatever the outcome of the filing, Rahm's situation illustrates the fundamental risk that every LIV signee accepted when they left the PGA Tour: the financial guarantees were only as good as PIF's continued willingness to fund them.

### STORY 3: Solheim Cup Preview

**FB Long-Form**
Window: 1:00 PM ET

Women's golf's most anticipated team event of the year is just four days away.

The 2026 Solheim Cup takes place September 11-13 at Bernardus Golf in Den Bosch, Netherlands — and if today's practice rounds are any indication, both teams are ready for competition. The USA and Europe squads are on the ground, working the course, preparing their strategies for one of the most unique competitive formats in golf.

The Solheim Cup operates on the Ryder Cup model, pitting the best American women against the best European women across three days of match play. Day one (September 11) and day two (September 12) feature foursomes and fourball matches, where each result contributes to the overall team point total. Day three (September 13) delivers the drama of singles matches — 12 head-to-head contests that traditionally produce some of the most electric moments in women's golf.

Bernardus Golf is a world-class venue — a private club in the Dutch countryside that has been preparing for this moment for years. The course setup, the local gallery support, and the pressure of team competition combine to create a tournament atmosphere unlike anything the individual LPGA Tour schedule produces.

For American golf fans, this is an opportunity to see the USA's best compete with the weight of national pride behind them. For European fans — and for the passionate Dutch golf community — it is a home event with genuine potential for an upset.

Competition begins Friday. Mark it on your calendar.

### STORY 4: Presidents Cup 2026

**FB Long-Form**
Window: 3:00 PM ET

In exactly 17 days, Medinah Country Club will host one of professional golf's great team events.

The 2026 Presidents Cup runs September 24-27 at Medinah's legendary Course No. 3 — a venue that has hosted the 2012 Ryder Cup, multiple US Opens, and PGA Championships, and was most recently redesigned by OCM in 2024 with larger greens, wider fairways, and expansive fescue rough that promises to reward precision and punish the wayward.

This is the 16th edition of the Presidents Cup, which pits the United States team against an International squad representing the rest of the world outside Europe. Brandt Snedeker will captain the American side. Geoff Ogilvy, the former US Open champion from Australia, leads the International team.

The Presidents Cup does not carry the cultural weight of the Ryder Cup, but it has produced its share of drama, and the Medinah setting gives this edition a gravitas that few venues can match. Snedeker's USA team will be heavy favourites — the American side has dominated the Presidents Cup for most of its history — but the International squad, typically built around strong Asian and Australasian players, will be motivated to make a statement on American soil.

Player picks are still being finalised. The team selections and final roster announcements in the lead-up to September 24 will be worth following closely.

Seventeen days. Medinah awaits.

Window: 5:00 PM ET

No PGA Tour tournament this week — but the fall stretch is coming, and it arrives quickly.

After the conclusion of the FedEx Cup playoffs and the Tour Championship, the PGA Tour enters its fall schedule — a series of tournaments that sit outside the official FedEx Cup points race but carry their own significance for player exemptions, sponsorship commitments, and title prestige.

The next event on the PGA Tour calendar is the Biltmore Championship, scheduled for September 17-20. The field will be composed largely of players pursuing the exemption opportunities the fall events provide, with some established players using the events as competitive tune-ups heading into the 2027 season.

From there, the schedule leads immediately into the Presidents Cup at Medinah (September 24-27), which will pause all other PGA Tour activity as the sport's spotlight turns to team competition.

For the world's top-ranked players, this week represents a recovery window after the intensity of the FedEx Cup playoffs and an opportunity to assess their games before the fall commitments begin. Practice rounds, fitness work, equipment testing — the invisible preparation work that fans rarely see.

The Biltmore Championship starts in 10 days. The Presidents Cup in 17. Golf is far from quiet.
