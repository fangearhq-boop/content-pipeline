PGA Tour Fall Swing Preview: Biltmore Championship Next as Teams and Players Reset Before Presidents Cup

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | September 7, 2026

No PGA Tour event this week as the field recovers from the FedEx Cup playoffs. The Biltmore Championship arrives September 17-20, immediately followed by the Presidents Cup at Medinah (September 24-27). Here's the upcoming schedule and what it means for the top players.

The PGA Tour calendar has a rare quiet week.

Following the intensity of the FedEx Cup playoffs and Tour Championship, the world's top professional golfers have a week without competitive obligations — a window to rest, reset, and prepare for what is an eventful stretch of golf ahead. There are no PGA Tour events scheduled for the week of September 7. The next tee time on the Tour calendar arrives in 10 days.

For most players, this week means returning to their practice facilities, reviewing where their games stand, and beginning the mental and physical preparation for the commitments that follow. For some — those in contention for Presidents Cup captain's picks — it may also mean conversations with team staff about selection status.

The Biltmore Championship: September 17-20

The next event on the PGA Tour schedule is the Biltmore Championship, a fall swing tournament running September 17-20. Like many fall swing events, the Biltmore Championship falls outside the official FedEx Cup points season that concluded with the Tour Championship, meaning it does not directly affect the season-long standings that determine exemption categories and tour card status for the 2027 season.

That does not make the fall events meaningless. For players who need to build momentum, practice competitive rounds in tournament conditions, or fulfil sponsor commitments that carry into the offseason calendar, the fall swing provides a structured competitive environment with meaningful prize money on offer.

The field for the Biltmore Championship will be confirmed closer to the event. Expect a mix of established Tour veterans using the week as a tune-up and emerging players chasing any remaining opportunities the fall schedule offers. Top-50 world-ranked players committed to Presidents Cup duty may skip to focus their preparation on Medinah.

The Presidents Cup: September 24-27 at Medinah

The Biltmore Championship and the Presidents Cup are back-to-back on the schedule — separated by only a few days — which means the week of September 17 serves as a final competitive tune-up for the players who will represent the United States and the International team at Medinah.

The Presidents Cup runs September 24-27, and the team selection process is in its final stages. Brandt Snedeker (USA) and Geoff Ogilvy (International) will announce their remaining captain's picks in the days ahead. For players on the bubble of both squads, the next few weeks carry significant stakes — a pick to Medinah represents a prestigious team experience and an opportunity to represent their country or region on one of golf's most celebrated courses.

Medinah's Course No. 3 was redesigned in 2024 and features new characteristics — larger greens, wider fairways, fescue rough — that will reward different skill sets than the Medinah that hosted the 2012 Ryder Cup. Understanding those changes will be a priority for both teams' preparation staffs heading into the event week.

The Bigger Picture

For the PGA Tour as an institution, the fall schedule represents a transition period between the FedEx Cup season and the 2027 schedule that begins in earnest after Presidents Cup. Teams of players and caddies use these weeks to evaluate equipment changes, work on swing adjustments with coaches, and set competitive goals for the year ahead.

The competitive calendar then continues through the rest of September and into a full October-through-April 2027 schedule that will include the Tour's major championships and marquee events. The quiet week now is a necessary pause before a long stretch of sustained competition.

In the meantime, the LIV Golf situation — which could see a bankruptcy filing this very week — hangs over the broader professional golf landscape as a potential reshaper of the competitive environment. If LIV players seek PGA Tour reinstatement through the bankruptcy process, the field compositions of events like the Biltmore Championship could look different by 2027 than they do today.
