Solheim Cup Practice Week: USA and Europe Prepare for Battle at Bernardus Golf

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | September 7, 2026

The 2026 Solheim Cup opens for competition September 11-13 at Bernardus Golf in Den Bosch, Netherlands. Today's practice rounds set the stage for one of women's golf's premier team events — here's what to know before the match play begins.

The flags are flying at Bernardus Golf. The practice rounds are underway. In four days, the formal competition begins.

The 2026 Solheim Cup — women's golf's answer to the Ryder Cup — runs September 11-13 at Bernardus Golf in Den Bosch, Netherlands, pitting the United States against Europe in a three-day match play contest that has produced some of the most memorable moments in women's professional golf. Today's schedule is about preparation: team meetings, course walkthroughs, partnership pairing discussions, and the quiet work of competition calibration that the cameras rarely capture.

Come Friday, the scoring starts.

The Format

The Solheim Cup uses the same format as the Ryder Cup, adapted for the women's game. Day one (Friday, September 11) and day two (Saturday, September 12) feature foursomes and fourball matches — team formats where each point won contributes to the overall tally. Day three (Sunday, September 13) brings the 12 singles matches that have historically delivered the tournament's most electric finishes.

In foursomes, partners share a ball and take alternate shots. In fourball, each player plays their own ball and the team score on each hole is the better of the two partners' scores. Both formats require different skills and communication styles, and how the captains pair their players across those sessions is one of the great strategic chess games in team golf.

Each session contributes points toward the 28-point target (14 points available over the first two days, 12 in singles). A team needs 14.5 points to win the Cup. Europe, competing as the host-adjacent side in the Netherlands, will carry the weight of local support and the home advantage that team environments can amplify.

The Bernardus Golf Setting

Bernardus Golf is a premier private club set in the Dutch countryside outside Den Bosch. The course has been preparing for this moment for years — an international stage that demands meticulous course setup, spectator infrastructure, and competition-quality conditions. The Netherlands is not the traditional heartland of elite golf, but the Solheim Cup's presence here reflects the growing European passion for the women's game and the LPGA Tour's appetite for staging premium events in new markets.

The course's characteristics will favour different players and pairing strategies. Understanding the setup — the rough depth, green speeds, and strategic risk points on the layout — has been the primary focus of today's practice sessions for both teams.

The Stakes

The Solheim Cup has been one of women's golf's most unpredictable events, with momentum shifting dramatically across the three days of competition. European teams have won at home before, and the combination of local support and home-course familiarity (assuming the European team practices extensively at Bernardus) gives the visiting American side something to overcome beyond the golf itself.

For the players, the Solheim Cup represents a different kind of pressure than anything on the individual LPGA Tour calendar. The team context — playing for a collective outcome, supporting and being supported by teammates — creates emotional investments that simply do not exist in stroke play competition. Past Solheim Cups have produced moments of great joy and heartbreak in equal measure.

The 2026 edition promises to be no different.
