LIV Golf Bankruptcy Filing Expected This Week — Players Offered Cents on the Dollar as PIF Pulls Out

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | September 7, 2026

The Saudi-backed breakaway league could seek Chapter 11 protection as soon as this week, per Financial Times reporting. PIF has poured $5B+ into LIV since 2022 but ended funding at the close of the 2026 season. Players with guaranteed contracts are receiving settlement offers worth a fraction of what was promised.

The moment that could redefine professional golf's competitive landscape may be just days away.

LIV Golf — the Saudi-backed breakaway league that launched in 2022 with billions from the Public Investment Fund and a promise to revolutionise the sport — is preparing to file for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection as soon as the week of September 7, according to reporting from the Financial Times that has been widely confirmed by multiple outlets covering the situation.

The filing would represent a stunning reversal of fortune for a league that spent freely to recruit world-class players from the PGA Tour, promising financial security, team-based competition, and a fresh vision for the game. That security now appears considerably less certain than the original agreements suggested.

How LIV Got Here

PIF, the sovereign wealth fund of Saudi Arabia, poured more than $5 billion into LIV Golf from its 2022 launch through the close of the 2026 season. The investment was at once a sporting venture and a geopolitical statement — an attempt by Saudi Arabia to use professional golf as a platform for international reputational investment, commonly referred to as "sportswashing" by critics.

In April 2026, PIF made a significant announcement: it would end its funding commitment to LIV at the conclusion of the 2026 season. Without PIF's direct financial support, LIV found itself unable to sustain the contractual obligations — including multi-year guaranteed player deals worth hundreds of millions of dollars in aggregate — that it had used to recruit its talent pool.

The league has spent the months since April 2026 attempting to secure alternative financing. Private capital firm BC Partners is reportedly in discussion about investing approximately $300 million in LIV's assets as part of a restructuring. PIF is expected to contribute a bankruptcy loan of less than $100 million — enough to facilitate a structured process, but without any commitment to ongoing operational support.

What Players Are Facing

The settlement offers already sent to LIV players are stark. Described by multiple outlets as worth "cents on the dollar" of original guaranteed contract values, the offers represent a dramatic reduction from what players were promised when they signed. For a player who agreed to a $50 million guaranteed deal, a settlement offer at, say, 10 cents on the dollar would mean receiving $5 million. The gap between expectation and reality is enormous.

A proposed restructuring plan — informally called "LIV 2.0" — would convert outstanding player claims into equity stakes in the reorganised entity. In theory, this gives players financial participation in whatever LIV becomes next. In practice, equity in an organisation emerging from bankruptcy with a smaller funding base and an uncertain competitive identity is a far more speculative asset than a guaranteed cash payment.

The bankruptcy filing, if it proceeds as reported, will trigger a court-supervised process during which creditors — including players — negotiate with the debtor (LIV) and any acquiring entities. The outcome of that process will determine how much players ultimately recover.

What Comes Next for Professional Golf

The implications of a LIV bankruptcy extend well beyond the financial drama. The PGA Tour, which suspended and eventually banned players who joined LIV without permission, has navigated the Saudi-backed challenge for four years. A LIV bankruptcy reopens questions about player eligibility — could some LIV players seek reinstatement to the PGA Tour as their league's structure dissolves?

The PGA Tour has not made any public statements about reinstatement pathways as of September 7, but the organisation will need to address the question as the legal proceedings unfold.

For the broader sport, LIV's financial collapse would be a data point in an ongoing debate about whether large-scale, guaranteed-contract breakaway circuits can build sustainable business models in professional golf. The answer, at least in this case, appears to be coming soon — and it may not be the one LIV's architects intended.

What's Next

If the bankruptcy filing proceeds this week, it will mark the beginning of a court-supervised process that could take months to resolve. Watch for official announcements from LIV Golf and PIF, player statements about the restructuring offers, and PGA Tour responses to reinstatement questions as the situation develops.
