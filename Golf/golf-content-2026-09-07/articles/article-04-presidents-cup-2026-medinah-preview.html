Presidents Cup 2026 Preview: Medinah Awaits as Snedeker and Ogilvy Finalize Their Teams

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | September 7, 2026

The 16th Presidents Cup runs September 24-27 at Medinah Country Club in Illinois — just 17 days away. Brandt Snedeker captains USA; Geoff Ogilvy leads the International side. The freshly redesigned course is ready for its biggest moment.

In 17 days, one of professional golf's great venues will host one of the sport's great team events.

The 2026 Presidents Cup takes place September 24-27 at Medinah Country Club in Medinah, Illinois — the storied Course No. 3 that hosted the 2012 Ryder Cup (and that remarkable European comeback from 10-6 down that European fans still celebrate), two PGA Championships, three US Opens, and a host of other elite professional tournaments. It is, in other words, exactly the kind of venue that makes a Presidents Cup feel consequential.

Brandt Snedeker captains the United States team. Geoff Ogilvy — the former US Open champion from Australia — leads the International side. Twelve players per team will compete across four days of foursomes, fourball, and singles matches that will determine which side lifts the Presidents Cup trophy at Medinah.

The Medinah Setting

Medinah Country Club has been preparing for this moment since the Presidents Cup was announced, and the course has undergone a significant transformation in the process. OCM completed a redesign of Course No. 3 in 2024 that introduced larger greens, wider fairways, bigger-scale bunkers, and expansive areas of fescue rough that will reward precision off the tee and punish the loose shot.

The result is a course that will play differently to the Medinah that professionals have competed on in previous decades. The scale changes — larger greens, broader fairways — suit the modern game while the fescue rough introduces a strategic premium on the tee that has been described as reminiscent of links-style thinking in an inland parkland setting.

For the captains, the Medinah setup will influence pairings strategy significantly. Longer hitters who can carry the fescue and hit firm greens from distance will be at a premium. The team that manages its tee-ball accuracy through the fescue corridors most effectively will create the most birdie opportunities.

The Captains

Brandt Snedeker brings extensive experience as a Ryder Cup and Presidents Cup participant to his first captaincy. The former FedEx Cup winner is known as one of the most popular figures in the American locker room — players respect his career achievements and appreciate his character within team environments. His USA side will be the favourite entering the week.

Geoff Ogilvy is one of the International team's most credentialed captains in years — a former US Open champion who won at Winged Foot in 2006 and has a career that spans the full modern era of the sport. The Australian brings both credibility with players from his own era and a reputation as a deep thinker about the game. He will need every bit of that acumen to compete on American soil, where the International side has historically struggled against well-resourced US squads.

Team selections and captain's picks are in their final stages. The composition of each team will be one of the major storylines heading into the week of September 24.

The Competition

The Presidents Cup format mirrors the Ryder Cup with some differences: 12 players per team, four sessions of foursome and fourball matches over the first three days, and a concluding singles session. The team that reaches 15.5 points wins the Cup.

The United States has dominated the Presidents Cup historically — the International side's wins have been exceptional rather than the rule. But the team golf format introduces variables that individual stroke play averages cannot capture: chemistry, crowd energy, partner dynamics, and the specific pressure of playing for a team rather than oneself.

Medinah's 2012 Ryder Cup is the template that International fans invoke when asked about comebacks on US soil. History does not guarantee anything, but it provides a reference point for what is possible when a team finds its rhythm.
