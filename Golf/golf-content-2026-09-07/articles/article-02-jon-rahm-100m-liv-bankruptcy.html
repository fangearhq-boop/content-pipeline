Jon Rahm Owed Over $100 Million as LIV Golf Prepares Bankruptcy Filing

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | September 7, 2026

The two-time major champion left the PGA Tour with one of the sport's largest-ever guaranteed contracts. Now, with LIV Golf preparing to file for bankruptcy as soon as this week, those guarantees face significant legal and financial uncertainty.

When Jon Rahm left the PGA Tour for LIV Golf ahead of the 2024 season, it was the most consequential signing in the breakaway league's short history. A two-time major champion — winner of the 2021 US Open and the 2023 Masters — trading the sport's most established circuit for a Saudi-backed rival was a statement of intent that neither side could ignore.

The financial terms matched the magnitude of the move. Rahm's deal was reported by various outlets at different figures — some said $150 million, others suggested the total guarantee was closer to $300 million across the contract's length. What is now clear, according to multiple reports confirmed this week, is that Rahm is still owed more than $100 million from that agreement. And LIV Golf may file for bankruptcy as soon as the week of September 7.

The Deal That Defined LIV's Ambition

Rahm's signing was LIV Golf's most audacious recruitment — a statement that the league could attract not just the ageing or fringe Tour players it had initially targeted, but the absolute elite of the game at the peak of their powers. He had won at Augusta in April 2023, cementing his status as one of the sport's most complete and dominant players. By the end of that year, he was wearing LIV colours.

The guarantees were the mechanism that made the move financially rational for Rahm. Unlike the PGA Tour's performance-based prize money model, LIV offered multi-year guaranteed payments — amounts that would be paid regardless of competitive results. For a player like Rahm, whose elite status was already established, the arrangement offered financial certainty on a scale that no PGA Tour-based earning trajectory could easily match.

The assumption, of course, was that the guarantor — LIV Golf, funded by PIF — would remain solvent and operational for the duration of the contract. That assumption is now under significant scrutiny.

What Rahm Is Facing

The LIV settlement offers sent to players in the lead-up to the potential bankruptcy filing have been described uniformly as worth "cents on the dollar" of original guaranteed amounts. The implications for Rahm's more-than-$100 million outstanding balance are significant: even at 10 cents on the dollar — which may be at the optimistic end of the settlement range — the player would recover $10 million or less from a nine-figure expectation.

The proposed "LIV 2.0" restructuring complicates the picture further. Under a pre-packaged bankruptcy arrangement, Rahm and other players would be offered equity stakes in the reorganised entity in lieu of cash settlements. Whether that equity is worth anything meaningful depends entirely on whether a restructured LIV can build a sustainable business — a proposition that has not been demonstrated in four years of PIF-funded operations.

BC Partners' reported $300 million investment discussion and PIF's potential bankruptcy loan of under $100 million provide some floor under the restructuring. But for a player owed nine figures, those numbers are not reassuring.

What Options Does Rahm Have?

Rahm's legal team will have options, but none of them are simple. Bankruptcy creditor proceedings can be lengthy and complex, and the outcome is determined by the court rather than the individual creditor's preferences. As an unsecured creditor — which player contracts in this context likely represent — Rahm's claims would be subordinate to secured lenders in a typical bankruptcy hierarchy.

There is also the question of PGA Tour reinstatement. Rahm left the Tour under circumstances that resulted in his suspension; whether the Tour would welcome him back — and on what terms — is unknown. The organisation has not publicly addressed the question as of September 7. But if LIV's structure dissolves through bankruptcy, the competitive landscape for Rahm could change significantly.

For now, Rahm remains a LIV golfer playing an uncertain season while one of the largest contractual disputes in sports history plays out in real time.

What's Next

The LIV Golf bankruptcy filing — if it proceeds this week — will begin a legal process that will take months to fully resolve. Rahm's situation, as one of the league's highest-profile and most financially exposed players, will be closely watched. Official filings will be public record, and the terms of any restructuring agreement will clarify how much players can expect to recover.
