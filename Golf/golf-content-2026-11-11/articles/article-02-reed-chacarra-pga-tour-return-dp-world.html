Reed and Chacarra Show the Way Back: PGA Tour Cards Earned via DP World Tour

By Jake Torres | November 11, 2026

As LIV Golf shuts down, Patrick Reed and Eugenio Chacarra have provided the clearest blueprint for a return to the PGA Tour. Both players earned their Tour membership through performance on the DP World Tour — a path that required no special programs or negotiations, only results on the golf course.

No Shortcuts — Just Scoring

For the players caught in the LIV Golf collapse, the most pressing question is simple: what happens next? Patrick Reed and Eugenio Chacarra answered that question by playing their way back into the PGA Tour through the DP World Tour — golf's other major global circuit and the established pathway to PGA Tour membership for players outside the established system.

Both players competed on the DP World Tour and finished in positions that earned them PGA Tour cards for the upcoming season. The AP reported their status on November 9, 2026, confirming that their Tour membership for the following year is secured. It is a straightforward return, earned the same way any professional golfer earns their place on the premier circuit: by shooting low numbers and finishing where it matters.

The DP World Tour Path

The DP World Tour's integration with the PGA Tour — strengthened significantly in recent years partly in response to LIV's competitive pressure — created a formal pathway for players to move between the two circuits. That relationship now becomes the most viable route for LIV players seeking PGA Tour membership without waiting for a reinstatement program that the Tour has shown no interest in creating.

For Reed and Chacarra, competing on the DP World Tour meant accepting that their LIV experience, however lucrative while it lasted, did not give them a free pass back to the top of professional golf's hierarchy. They had to perform. They had to grind through the European calendar, earn their points, and qualify through merit. The experience likely humbled them — and also demonstrated their quality when the pressure was real rather than contractually guaranteed.

What This Means for Others

The Reed and Chacarra precedent matters for the broader LIV diaspora. It demonstrates that the DP World Tour route is achievable for players with PGA Tour-caliber talent — but that it requires willingness to compete in a circuit where the purses are smaller, the schedule is less glamorous, and the pressure to perform against peers hungry for the same spots is intense.

Not every LIV player will choose this path, and not every one who tries it will succeed. Some of the sport's biggest names — Rahm, DeChambeau, and others — face circumstances that make even the DP World Tour route complicated by legal history, suspended status, or lack of a clear performance record on that circuit. For them, the path remains uncertain.

What's Next

Reed and Chacarra will join the PGA Tour for the upcoming season, competing alongside peers who never left and those who returned through the now-closed Returning Member Program. Their presence will be a living reminder that the LIV era, whatever its legacy, did not end careers — for players willing to put in the work to come back.

As more LIV players assess their options in the wake of the league's collapse, the DP World Tour pathway will likely see increased applications and competition. For professional golfers who want to get back to the sport's main stage, the blueprint now exists. The only question is who has the game to execute it.
