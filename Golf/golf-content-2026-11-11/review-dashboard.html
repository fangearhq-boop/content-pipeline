LIV's Legacy — Five Years, $5 Billion, and What It Changed About Golf Forever
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Posting window: 2:00 PM ET LIV Golf is finished. But the sport it leaves behind is fundamentally different from the one it arrived in. Five years ago, the established professional golf structure — PGA Tour, DP World Tour, their affiliates — was largely unchallenged. Players competed within the system, negotiated their schedules within it, and built their careers through it. LIV changed everything. Guaranteed contracts, team formats, elevated appearance fees, and the injection of Saudi sovereign wealth into the game forced every tour to respond. The PGA Tour raised its top event purses dramatically. It introduced the PIP bonus program. It explored merger conversations it would never have considered before. The European Tour transformed itself into the DP World Tour and built closer ties with the PGA Tour partly in response to LIV's competitive threat. The irony is that LIV's greatest impact may be the changes it forced on tours it couldn't ultimately beat. Players who stayed on the PGA Tour are earning significantly more than they were five years ago — in large part because LIV made the alternative real. Whether you think LIV was a force for good in golf, a threat to the sport's integrity, or simply a failed experiment — its impact on how professional golf is structured, compensated, and contested will be felt for a generation. --- ### Image Post Caption Posting window: 3:00 PM ET LIV Golf is gone — but it changed professional golf forever.
