LIV Golf CEO Confirms BC Partners as Lead Investor: Players Become Majority Owners in Restructured League

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | August 10, 2026

LIV Golf / Golf Business / BC Partners / LIV 2.0

LIV Golf CEO Scott O'Neil confirmed at the Bedminster event this weekend that the league has secured BC Partners' credit arm as its new lead investor, in a deal valued at approximately $250 to $300 million. The deal has been signed by the investor and approved by the league's board.

The announcement gives shape to what had been reported in Bloomberg and the Financial Times earlier this month: LIV Golf is transitioning from Saudi government funding to a player-equity model backed by institutional investment. That transition has a name — LIV 2.0 — and now it has a confirmed lead financial partner.

The Structure of LIV 2.0

Under the restructured model, players will become majority equity holders in LIV Golf. Tournament purses will decrease — the compensation structure shifts from cash-heavy event payouts to equity stakes in the league itself. Players who hold contracts will effectively become investors in the enterprise they compete in.

Ten events are planned for the 2027 LIV Golf season. The September 2026 expected close date means the new structure will be in place before the current season concludes, giving player-investors clarity on the entity they'll be holding stakes in before any decisions about contract renewals or defections need to be made.

More than 12 additional minority investor parties are reportedly in discussions alongside BC Partners, suggesting the capital base for LIV 2.0 extends beyond the headline investment.

The Saudi Exit

Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund announced earlier this year that it would stop funding LIV Golf at the end of 2026. Since the league's 2022 launch, PIF has invested approximately $5 billion — a figure that made LIV Golf's entry into professional golf possible and that paid for the prize money, player signing fees, and operational costs that defined its first four years.

BC Partners' involvement represents the answer to the question LIV Golf had to answer: what comes after Saudi funding ends? The BC Partners deal provides that answer. The league survives, restructured, with players as majority owners and institutional money replacing government investment.

What This Means for Players

The equity model creates a fundamentally different financial proposition for LIV players than the one they signed up for. Those who committed to LIV for guaranteed appearance fees and large purses will now be holding equity in a league whose value depends on how the enterprise performs, how media rights are valued, and whether the 2027 season attracts the sponsorship and broadcast attention needed to make the equity meaningful.

Whether players view that as an upgrade or a downgrade depends on how they evaluate LIV Golf's future prospects. O'Neil's Bedminster confirmation suggests the leadership believes the equity story is worth telling — and that players should too.

What's Next

The BC Partners deal is expected to close in September 2026. At that point, the legal and financial structure of LIV 2.0 will be formalized, and players will know the terms of the equity stake they hold. Saudi Arabia's PIF funding ends at year-end. From January 2027 forward, LIV Golf operates under the new model — 10 events, player-majority ownership, and BC Partners as the headline institutional backer.
