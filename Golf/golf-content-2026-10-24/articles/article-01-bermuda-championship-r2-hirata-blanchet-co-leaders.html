Two Rookies Share the Bermuda Lead: Hirata and Blanchet at 13-Under After 36 Holes

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | October 24, 2026

Two players who have never won a PGA Tour event share the 36-hole lead at the 2026 Butterfield Bermuda Championship — and they've given the field every reason to take them seriously.

Kensei Hirata and Chandler Blanchet are tied at 13-under 129 at Port Royal Golf Course in Bermuda following Friday's second round. Blanchet, who opened with a remarkable 62 in Round 1, added a steady 67 on Friday. Hirata answered with a 64 of his own, matching his co-leader at the top of a tight leaderboard.

Both players are considered rookies at this event — neither has previous appearances at the Bermuda Championship to draw on — and yet both have looked entirely comfortable at Port Royal through two rounds.

The Chase Pack

Neither co-leader can afford to ease up. A five-man group sits at 12-under and just one shot back, bringing considerably more experience to the pursuit.

Seamus Power, the Irish veteran, is well-suited to the seaside conditions Port Royal demands. Corey Conners, one of the most precise iron players on Tour, is the kind of player who rarely beats himself. Eric Cole, Ben Silverman, and Danny Walker round out the group — any of whom could make a Saturday move.

Greyson Sigg also had one of the best rounds of the day, firing a 64 in Round 2 to reach 11-under and sit in solo eighth place.

Schenk Out Early

One notable absence from the weekend field: defending champion Adam Schenk. The reigning Bermuda title-holder missed the cut at 5-under 137, just inside what proved to be an exacting line. Defending a title is never easy, and Schenk will have to wait another year for another shot at Port Royal.

What's Next

Round 3 goes Saturday, with the final round set for Sunday. The 2026 Butterfield Bermuda Championship concludes October 25. Hirata and Blanchet will be grouped together at the top — with five experienced chasers ready to test their nerve. Port Royal's uphill finishing hole has a history of making Saturday leaders sweat. The weekend is shaping up to be one of the more compelling finishes of the fall PGA Tour calendar.
