### STORY 4: PGA Tour "No Merger" with LIV Golf

**Facebook Long-Form** | Window: 6:00 PM ET

After three years of framework agreements, negotiations, congressional hearings, and months of breathless reporting, PGA Tour CEO Brian Rolapp has drawn a clear line.

"There's no merger, no conversations," Rolapp told Bloomberg earlier this month. "We've been really concentrated on the PGA Tour."

It's the clearest statement yet that the long-speculated union between the PGA Tour and Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund — the backers of LIV Golf — is not happening. The framework agreement that sent shockwaves through the golf world when it was announced in June 2023 has now gone more than 26 months without producing anything official. Rolapp's language suggests that even the conversation is no longer active on the Tour's side.

The underlying numbers help explain why the Tour isn't rushing back to the table. LIV Golf's TV audience remains roughly 17 times smaller than the PGA Tour's. LIV still has no Official World Golf Rankings points in 2026 — a critical structural disadvantage for attracting the sport's top players. The OWGR application submitted by LIV was denied in October 2023, and LIV's CEO has not submitted a new one.

The two tours will coexist — as separately as possible, it now seems. LIV has announced plans for what some are calling "LIV 2.0" with new investor involvement, but the path to OWGR points, the one thing that would make LIV truly competitive for elite players, remains blocked. For now, the PGA Tour is running its own race — and winning.
