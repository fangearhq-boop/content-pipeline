PGA Tour CEO Says It Out Loud: "There's No Merger, No Conversations" with LIV Golf

By Marcus Cole | August 14, 2026 | Golf Fanrecap

After three years of framework agreements, congressional hearings, executive negotiations, and months of breathless reporting on whether a merger between the PGA Tour and LIV Golf was coming, PGA Tour CEO Brian Rolapp has made the clearest statement yet: it is not.

"There's no merger, no conversations," Rolapp told Bloomberg earlier this month. "We've been really concentrated on the PGA Tour."

That quote, published August 7, 2026, effectively closes the chapter that opened in June 2023 when the PGA Tour and Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund — the backers of LIV Golf — announced a framework agreement that sent shockwaves through professional golf. The framework, which was supposed to lead to a negotiated deal, has now gone more than 26 months without producing one. Rolapp's language makes clear that the Tour is no longer waiting.

Why the Deal Fell Apart

The underlying numbers were never favorable for LIV Golf, and they have not improved. LIV's television audience remains roughly 17 times smaller than the PGA Tour's — a gap that matters enormously when considering the value of any combined commercial enterprise. Broadcast rights, sponsorship dollars, and fan engagement are all downstream of audience size, and on that metric, LIV has not narrowed the gap.

More structurally damaging is the absence of Official World Golf Rankings points. LIV Golf still has no OWGR points in 2026. The application LIV submitted was denied in October 2023, and LIV's CEO has not submitted a new one. Without OWGR points, the tour cannot attract the sport's elite players on a merit basis — top-ranked players protect their world ranking, and a tour that doesn't count toward it is a tour they cannot prioritize competitively.

Two Tours, Permanently Separate

LIV has announced plans for what some are calling "LIV 2.0," with new investor involvement aimed at reviving the enterprise after the PIF-led original structure struggled to reach sustainability. But the fundamental challenge — OWGR points — remains blocked. And without that, the cycle continues: no points, fewer elite players, smaller audiences, less commercial value, no path to legitimacy.

The PGA Tour, meanwhile, is running its own playoffs this week with a field that includes Scottie Scheffler, Tommy Fleetwood, Jordan Spieth, and Rory McIlroy. The three-week FedEx Cup postseason carries a $25 million bonus for the overall champion and a combined purse structure that LIV cannot match in terms of competitive prestige. For players at the top of the world rankings, the incentive structure of the PGA Tour remains unambiguously superior.

What Rolapp's quote confirms is that the Tour is no longer treating the LIV question as an open item. The period of uncertainty — which stretched from June 2023 through early 2026 — appears to be over. The tours will coexist, but they will do so separately.

What's Next

The LIV 2.0 announcement, with new investor involvement, is the next development to watch. Whether the restructured entity can secure OWGR recognition under a different ownership structure remains the central question — and the one outcome that would meaningfully change the competitive landscape. Without OWGR points, LIV's market position remains static. The PGA Tour, for its part, appears to have moved on: the 2026 FedEx Cup Playoffs are underway, the fan base is engaged, and the CEO's words suggest the energy is entirely inward-facing. Whether LIV finds a path to relevance is now largely LIV's problem to solve.
