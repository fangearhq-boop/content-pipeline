Scheffler Banked $10 Million Before the Playoffs Started. Now He Has to Win Them.

By Jake Torres | August 14, 2026 | Golf Fanrecap

Scottie Scheffler entered the 2026 FedEx Cup Playoffs with $10 million already in his pocket. That is the bonus for finishing No. 1 in FedExCup regular-season standings, and Scheffler claimed it for the fourth time in five years. He arrives in Memphis with 4,123 points, a 794-point advantage over No. 2 Matt Fitzpatrick — numbers that describe a regular season of almost unprecedented dominance.

But through two rounds of the FedEx St. Jude Championship at TPC Southwind, Scheffler is six shots behind Tommy Fleetwood. The $10 million is banked. The FedEx Cup itself is not.

Four Times in Five Years

The context around the four regular-season titles matters. This is not a stretch of near-misses where Scheffler happened to come out on top. This is sustained, season-long dominance — the kind that produces a 794-point gap over the No. 2 player entering the playoffs. One win, five runner-up finishes, and five other top-10s defined a 2026 regular season that puts Scheffler in conversations with the all-time great seasons in PGA Tour history.

The tour's top 10 entering the playoffs — Scheffler, Fitzpatrick, Cameron Young, Wyndham Clark, Chris Gotterup, Collin Morikawa, Si Woo Kim, Sam Burns, Tommy Fleetwood, and Ludvig Åberg — reads like a who's who of the current game. Scheffler outpaced all of them over nine months of regular season golf. The $10 million bonus is what that achievement is worth in real terms.

The Playoff Logic Is Different

The FedEx Cup Playoffs do not reward regular-season points. They reward performance across three specific weeks: the St. Jude Championship in Memphis, the BMW Championship, and the Tour Championship at East Lake in Atlanta. At East Lake, the No. 1 seed starts at 10-under par — a meaningful advantage, but not an insurmountable one if someone is making eagles while the leader is making pars.

Scheffler's Round 2 at TPC Southwind included four bogeys, three of them on the back nine. Fleetwood went the other direction, posting a 64 to match his opening 63. The gap that was six shots after Round 1 remains six shots after Round 2. That is not the trajectory a player chasing down a hot leaderboard wants to see.

The $20 million St. Jude purse is still fully available. Scheffler is capable of closing a six-shot gap over two rounds — he has the firepower and the talent to produce a weekend that reshapes the leaderboard. But the first step is halting any further drift. Two bogey-free rounds would not be enough to catch Fleetwood at his current pace; the math requires Scheffler to produce something near his own ceiling.

What's Next

Rounds 3 and 4 are Saturday and Sunday at TPC Southwind. Scheffler will need to close the gap on Fleetwood while keeping his own game clean — a two-part task that the four bogeys in Round 2 suggest is not yet fully resolved. If he wins the St. Jude, the regular-season dominance and the playoff performance align into one complete picture. If Fleetwood holds on, Scheffler's advantage heading into the BMW will still be substantial — but the FedEx Cup title he has never won will remain out of reach for another week's worth of chasing.
