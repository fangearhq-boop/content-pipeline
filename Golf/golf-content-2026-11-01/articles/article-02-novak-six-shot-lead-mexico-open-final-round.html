Andrew Novak Enters Mexico Open Final Round with Historic Six-Shot Lead

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | November 1, 2026

Andrew Novak has never won an individual PGA Tour event. Entering Sunday's final round of the VidantaWorld Mexico Open at Vidanta Vallarta, he has played the last two days of his professional career as if he always knew this was coming.

Novak carries a six-shot lead into Sunday at 23-under par — the largest 54-hole advantage in the history of the Mexico Open — after posting back-to-back rounds of historic proportions. On Friday, he tied the Vidanta Vallarta course record with an 11-under 61. On Saturday, he followed it with a bogey-free 9-under 62. Those two rounds alone represent 123 strokes of golf excellence that have left the rest of the field wondering whether the tournament is already over.

The Weekend That Turned Heads

Novak's Friday 61 announced his presence at the top of the leaderboard in emphatic fashion. Tying the course record in the second round, with a full day of golf still ahead of him, was the kind of performance that earns immediate attention. He backed it up Saturday with perhaps an even more impressive round: 62, no bogeys, no wasted shots, a methodical dismantling of Vidanta Vallarta while the rest of the field clawed for birdies that Novak was producing in clusters.

Combined across both rounds: 123 total shots. For context, par for two rounds at Vidanta Vallarta is 144. Novak played those two days 21 under par. It is the kind of scoring that stands out in any era of professional golf.

His putter has been the defining weapon. Novak has made birdie after birdie from distances that most players only dream about on good days, and his ability to convert the 10-to-15 foot range consistently is the statistical signature of a player in a zone that does not come around often.

What a First Win Means

Novak has won on the PGA Tour before — but as part of a team event. The 2025 Zurich Classic of New Orleans, which he won alongside partner Ben Griffin, does not carry the same weight as an individual title. Every professional golfer understands the distinction. An individual win changes a career, a legacy, and a professional identity in ways that team victories simply cannot replicate.

For Novak, who has been grinding on the PGA Tour and has established himself as a consistent presence without the breakthrough result that defines elite careers, this week in Mexico could be that defining moment. Six shots with 18 holes to play is not an insurmountable lead — history has seen larger advantages evaporate — but in the context of the scoring Novak has produced, the task facing Matthieu Pavon and Doug Ghim borders on the impossible.

The Chasers

Matthieu Pavon enters Sunday in second place at 17-under, six shots behind the leader. The French professional is a proven PGA Tour winner with the technical skills and mental fortitude to go low on any given day. Six shots, however, requires something extraordinary: Pavon would need to shoot something in the range of 63 or better while Novak plays to par or worse.

Doug Ghim sits third at 16-under, seven back. Ben Griffin, Max Greyserman, and Pierceson Coody are tied in fourth at 15-under, eight behind. None of the chasers have shown in rounds one through three that they can match Novak's current level of play.

What's Next

Sunday's final round at Vidanta Vallarta began with Novak, Pavon, and Ghim in the final group. The course is playing receptively, the weather conditions are ideal, and Novak has shown every indication that his game is locked in at the highest level. If he maintains anything close to the pace of play he has established through 54 holes, the 2026 VidantaWorld Mexico Open will belong to him — and Andrew Novak will walk off the 18th green with his name on a PGA Tour individual trophy for the first time.
