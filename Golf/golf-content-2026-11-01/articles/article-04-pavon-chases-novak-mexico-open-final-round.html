Pavon's Six-Shot Mountain: France's Best Chasing Novak at Vidanta Vallarta

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | November 1, 2026

Matthieu Pavon is one of the best players in European golf. A proven PGA Tour winner. A ball-striker who can go as low as almost anyone in the field on a good day.

On Sunday at Vidanta Vallarta, he will need the best round of his life just to make the Mexico Open interesting.

Pavon enters the final round of the 2026 VidantaWorld Mexico Open at 17-under par — six strokes behind Andrew Novak, who has turned the last two days of this tournament into a personal gallery of low-round records. Six shots, while not strictly insurmountable, requires a combination of factors that rarely aligns: the leader making multiple mistakes while the chaser shoots something exceptional.

The Case for Pavon

Pavon has earned his reputation. He has won at this level and knows how to compete when the tournament matters. Vidanta Vallarta is a scoring course, and the conditions have been receptive all week — there are birdies available for any player willing to attack aggressively from the start of the final round.

A player of Pavon's caliber can certainly shoot 63 or better on a course like this. The question is not whether Pavon can play at that level. The question is whether Novak — who has shot 61 and 62 in his last two outings and shown zero signs of stress — will provide the opening Pavon needs by playing ordinary golf or worse.

The Mathematics of a Comeback

For Pavon to overtake Novak on Sunday, he would need to net at least seven shots against the leader across 18 holes. Playing in the same final group — Novak, Pavon, Ghim tee off last — Pavon will see every move Novak makes in real time. That proximity can be an advantage (if Novak stumbles early, Pavon knows immediately) or a psychological burden (watching a player shoot 62 in the group ahead is demoralizing under any circumstances).

The Mexico Open's final round will have a clear storyline by the fifth or sixth hole. If Novak is making birdies and Pavon cannot manufacture the gap, it will become obvious quickly that the Frenchman faces a mathematical reality he cannot escape. If Novak stalls and Pavon catches early momentum, the tournament gets interesting.

What's Next

Whether Pavon wins or finishes a distant runner-up, his performance at the 2026 Mexico Open has been strong. He will leave Vallarta with a high finish on his season record and the experience of competing in a final group with a player putting on a historic week. If this is his only result this week, it is still a quality showing. But Pavon, as a competitor, will be chasing history from the opening tee shot on Sunday — because that is what professionals do when they are six shots back with one round to play.
