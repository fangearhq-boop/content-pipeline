Yamashita Makes History: Defends Maybank Championship for First-Ever Back-to-Back Title

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | November 1, 2026

Miyu Yamashita did something on Sunday in Kuala Lumpur that no golfer had ever done before at the LPGA Tour's Maybank Championship — she won it twice in a row.

With a composed final-round 68 at Kuala Lumpur Golf and Country Club, Yamashita held off China's Yan Liu by two strokes to successfully defend her title, finishing at 22-under par (266) for the tournament. The Japanese star is now the first back-to-back champion in the event's history, a distinction that cements her status as one of the premier players in women's professional golf.

Commanding from the Start

Yamashita seized control of the Maybank Championship in Friday's second round and never relinquished the lead. She led through three rounds heading into Sunday with a two-stroke cushion, but the margin felt larger than the scoreboard suggested given the consistency with which she had played throughout the week.

Her final round of 68 was steady rather than spectacular — five birdies and a single bogey at the par-4 ninth — but it was exactly the kind of ball-striking that wins championships on a Sunday afternoon when the pressure is highest. Yamashita made the key birdie putts when she needed them and avoided the kind of stretches of dropped shots that could have invited Liu back into serious contention.

Liu Pushed Hard

Yan Liu gave Yamashita genuine competition throughout the final round. The Chinese player had surged up the leaderboard with a stunning 63 in round three, turning a comfortable deficit into a two-stroke gap and bringing real drama to the final 18 holes. But Yamashita, who had been in this position before — as defending champion, as title leader entering Sunday — handled the pressure with the experience of someone who knows how to close.

Liu finished at 20-under par, two strokes behind the champion. Her runner-up finish represents significant achievement for Chinese women's golf and marks Liu as a rising force on the LPGA Tour heading into the end of the 2026 season.

The Best in the World

This victory is Yamashita's third of the 2026 LPGA season and the fifth of her career. Earlier in 2026, she claimed the Meijer LPGA Classic and CPKC Women's Open. Three wins in a single season is a mark that puts her in the company of the best players in women's golf history, and her back-to-back Maybank titles have now made her the most decorated champion this event has ever seen.

The consistency, the ability to perform in Asia where the conditions and pressure of local tournaments add extra weight, and the mental fortitude to defend a title successfully — all of it points to a player who has arrived at the pinnacle of the LPGA Tour and intends to stay there.

What's Next

Yamashita returns to the LPGA Tour schedule for the closing weeks of the 2026 season, now with three wins and the clearest possible claim to the LPGA Player of the Year award. She has made the Maybank Championship her personal possession, and the question heading into future seasons is no longer whether she can win here — it is how many times she will add her name to this trophy. With the 2026 season still winding down, she may not be done yet.
