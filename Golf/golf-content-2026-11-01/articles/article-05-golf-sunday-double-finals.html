Golf's Sunday Double: Yamashita Wins Maybank, Novak Leads Mexico Open into the Final Round

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | November 1, 2026

Sunday delivered two compelling storylines across two continents for professional golf fans. In Kuala Lumpur, Miyu Yamashita completed a historic defense of the Maybank Championship, becoming the first back-to-back champion in that tournament's history. Simultaneously, on the Pacific coast of Mexico, Andrew Novak entered the final round of the VidantaWorld Mexico Open with a six-shot lead and a very real chance to claim the individual PGA Tour title he has never won.

It was the kind of Sunday the sport occasionally produces: multiple tournaments reaching their climax at once, each with its own stakes, its own drama, and its own narrative to be decided before the day was done.

Yamashita Writes History in Kuala Lumpur

The Maybank Championship story was resolved first. Miyu Yamashita shot a final-round 68 at Kuala Lumpur Golf and Country Club to finish at 22-under par, holding off a determined challenge from China's Yan Liu by two strokes. The Japanese star had led since the second round and never let the lead slip — even when Liu fired a stunning 63 in round three to close to within two shots and set up what appeared to be a genuine final-round battle.

Yamashita's response was exactly what champions produce under pressure: a clean, steady round when a clean steady round was what the moment required. Five birdies, one bogey, a final margin of two. No drama in the closing stages. No wobble under pressure. The kind of controlled performance that defines the best players in the world.

The back-to-back distinction is more than a historical footnote. In a field-strength era where LPGA Tour events draw the world's best, defending a title successfully is genuinely rare. Yamashita now owns the Maybank Championship in a way no previous champion has, and her three wins in 2026 — this result, the Meijer LPGA Classic, and the CPKC Women's Open — make a compelling case for LPGA Player of the Year.

Liu's runner-up performance deserves its own recognition. A 63 in round three to surge back into contention, followed by a respectable Sunday that came up two short, is a result that marks her as one of the more dangerous players in women's golf entering the final stretch of the 2026 season.

Novak's Historic Lead on the Pacific Coast

While Yamashita was closing out her title in Malaysia, Andrew Novak was teeing off in the final group at Vidanta Vallarta, carrying the largest 54-hole lead in Mexico Open history.

The setup: Novak at 23-under par after rounds of 61-62 in the second and third rounds, a six-shot advantage over France's Matthieu Pavon entering Sunday. The two previous rounds amount to one of the most dominant 36-hole stretches any player has produced in recent PGA Tour memory — 123 total shots across 36 holes, 21-under par, with the putter performing at a level that puts him in the statistical conversation for best putting performance of the 2026 season.

Novak has won on the PGA Tour before, but only as part of a team — the 2025 Zurich Classic alongside partner Ben Griffin. An individual title is the next frontier, and Sunday in Mexico represents the most favorable conditions imaginable for him to cross it. Six shots with 18 holes to play, against a field that has shown through three rounds it cannot approach his current level of play.

Pavon is a proven winner and a dangerous adversary for any final group, but even the best players in the world rarely manufacture six-shot swings in a single round when the leader is actively playing well. The mathematical reality for Pavon is steep. The reality for Novak is straightforward: play recognizable golf, and the Mexico Open title is his.

A Sunday Worth Watching

The dual-event Sunday was a reminder of why the early November portion of the golf calendar, often overlooked in favor of the majors-heavy spring and summer, can produce the sport's most compelling viewing. Yamashita's defense played out across four rounds of sustained excellence. Novak's run through Vidanta Vallarta played out like a highlight reel of everything that can go right when a player finds an elite level and holds it for an extended stretch.

Both tournaments were worth following. Both stories, as of Sunday, demanded attention from any golf fan paying attention to the 2026 season. Whether Novak closes out his first individual title later in the day is the final question. The rest of the Sunday story is already written — and Yamashita, for the second straight year, is its champion.
