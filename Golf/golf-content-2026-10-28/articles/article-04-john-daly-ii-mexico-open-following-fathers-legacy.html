John Daly II Tees Up at the Mexico Open, Carrying a Famous Name Into a New Chapter

By Ryan Calloway

October 28, 2026

John Daly II | PGA Tour | VidantaWorld Mexico Open

When the pairings sheet for the VidantaWorld Mexico Open went out Thursday morning, one name caught the eye of fans immediately: John Daly II. He tees off at 10:44 AM ET at Vidanta Vallarta — and while the surname alone would turn heads at any professional golf event, it is the story of what Daly II is building on his own terms that makes this week worth watching.

The son of legendary PGA Tour champion John Daly has been developing his professional career on golf's lower circuits, working through the developmental pathway that brings young tour hopefuls to events like the Mexico Open. His presence in the field this week represents both an opportunity and a moment in a golf story that was never going to be simple.

A Famous Name and All That Comes With It

John Daly Sr. is one of the most beloved and colorful figures in the history of professional golf. His two major championships — the 1991 PGA Championship and the 1995 Open Championship — were won with a combination of prodigious power and go-for-broke audacity that earned him a following that has never fully faded. His story is one of the sport's most human: extraordinary talent, enormous popularity, and a career defined as much by its vulnerabilities as its victories.

Carrying that name into a PGA Tour event is not a burden in the traditional sense — no professional would trade visibility for anonymity at this stage of their career — but it comes with a particular kind of scrutiny. Every shot John Daly II hits will be measured against a legend, at least informally. That is simply the reality of the name on his bag.

Building His Own Story

What Daly II has demonstrated through his development path is a commitment to building his game through the proper channels — competing in events, accumulating experience, and earning his opportunities rather than leaning on a surname to open doors. His presence at Vidanta Vallarta this week is a reflection of that work: he earned his way into the Mexico Open field through performance, not inheritance.

Whether he contends for the title or battles to make the cut, the experience of competing in a full PGA Tour field at a $6 million event is the kind of competition that shapes careers. The players who will finish near the top of this leaderboard Sunday — Clark, Smotherman, the established names — are exactly the opponents that a developing young professional needs to see up close to understand what the standard requires.

What's Next

John Daly II tees off Thursday at 10:44 AM ET at Vidanta Vallarta. Follow his early rounds for the first real indicator of where his game is right now relative to the week's competitive field. Whether this week becomes a memorable milestone in a long professional career, or simply a chapter in a continuing development story, it is one of the more human narratives the Mexico Open has to offer this year.
