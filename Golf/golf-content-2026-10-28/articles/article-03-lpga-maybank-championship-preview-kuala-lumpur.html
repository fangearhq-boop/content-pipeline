LPGA Maybank Championship Preview: Elite Field Descends on Kuala Lumpur for Asia Swing Finale

By Marcus Cole

October 28, 2026

LPGA | Maybank Championship | Kuala Lumpur

The 2026 LPGA Maybank Championship begins Thursday at Kuala Lumpur Golf & Country Club's West Course in Malaysia, bringing one of the strongest international fields of the tour's Asian swing to a venue with a history of producing compelling champions. The event runs October 29 through November 1, closing out a remarkable stretch of LPGA golf in the Asia-Pacific region.

The field includes five players ranked in the top eight of the Rolex Rankings: No. 3 Haeran Ryu, No. 4 Miyu Yamashita, No. 5 Ruoning Yin, No. 7 Lottie Woad, and No. 8 Charley Hull. With that level of talent concentrated at one venue, the Maybank Championship has the makings of one of the most competitive events of the fall season in women's golf.

Yamashita Defends, Yin Holds the Record

Miyu Yamashita arrives as the defending champion after her 2025 victory in a playoff at 18 under par. The Japanese player has been one of the most consistent performers on the LPGA Tour over the past two seasons, and returning to a venue where she claimed a title adds a familiar edge to her preparation this week.

Ruoning Yin brings the most intimidating course history of anyone in the field. The Chinese star won the 2024 Maybank Championship at 23 under par — a tournament record — setting a scoring benchmark that has yet to be matched. If conditions at Kuala Lumpur Golf & Country Club allow for low scoring this week, Yin's intimacy with what those conditions feel like could be a significant advantage.

Haeran Ryu, ranked No. 3 in the world, comes in as one of the most consistent players on tour this season. Her game travels well across different course setups, and the West Course at KLGCC rewards her particular strengths: controlled ball-striking and a reliable short game.

Rising Stars and Storylines

Lottie Woad, ranked seventh in the world, has been one of the tour's compelling ascent stories of 2026. The British player has accumulated results that have pushed her into the top 10 in a relatively short time, and this week in Malaysia gives her another opportunity to perform on a stage where the world's best are watching.

Charley Hull, ranked No. 8, is among the tour's most popular players and has been notably strong in Asian swing events throughout her career. Her game — all-court versatility and an aggressive approach to scoring opportunities — suits the West Course layout well.

What's Next

Round 1 tees off Thursday at Kuala Lumpur Golf & Country Club. Given the time zone difference, scores from Thursday's round will begin filtering through late Thursday evening and early Friday morning ET. The leaderboard should take shape by the end of the second round Friday, with the weekend rounds Saturday and Sunday completing the LPGA Tour's Asian swing on a high note.
