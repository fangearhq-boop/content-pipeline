Brandon Smotherman Arrives in Mexico With a Chance to Win Three Consecutive PGA Tour Events

By Jake Torres

October 28, 2026

Brandon Smotherman | PGA Tour | VidantaWorld Mexico Open

Brandon Smotherman has not lost since early October. After winning back-to-back PGA Tour events — including a playoff victory at the Bermuda Championship on Monday — he heads to Vidanta Vallarta this week with the chance to do something very few golfers in the modern era have accomplished: win three consecutive PGA Tour tournaments.

The Bermuda win was dramatic. Smotherman held his nerve through a playoff on Monday to claim the trophy, becoming the first player since the FedExCup era began to hold consecutive PGA Tour victories in the month of October. He boarded the plane to Mexico not on a rest, but on momentum — the kind of momentum that changes careers.

What Three Straight Would Mean

Three consecutive PGA Tour wins is one of the most exclusive achievements in professional golf. The modern tour schedule, with its rotating fields and varying course setups, makes sustaining excellence across three consecutive events extraordinarily difficult. Different conditions, different competitors, different mental demands week to week — each new event erases the privileges earned in the last one and forces a player to earn their position again from scratch.

Smotherman's back-to-back wins already mark him as one of the most compelling stories of the 2026 season. A third straight would move his name into conversations normally reserved for players who have defined eras of the sport. That is not hyperbole — it is the mathematics of how rare the achievement is.

The Mental Challenge Ahead

The hardest part of a winning streak in professional golf is not execution. It is expectation management. Smotherman will walk to the first tee Thursday morning as the hottest player in the field and the most-watched name on every leaderboard. His competitors will know exactly where he is on the course. Every bogey will generate questions; every birdie will be treated as confirmation of something foregone.

He will need to play his own game. The players who have strung together multiple consecutive wins in the modern era — and there are very few — have typically done so by treating each shot as independent of the streak's narrative, focusing on process over history. That is easier to say than to execute when you know the entire golf world is watching.

Smotherman has handled pressure before. The playoff at Bermuda, which ran into Monday due to weather delays, was exactly the kind of moment that separates streaks from near-misses. He converted it. That track record of clutch performance will serve him well this week.

What's Next

Smotherman tees off in Round 1 Thursday at Vidanta Vallarta. His place in the morning or afternoon wave will determine when eyes turn to his scorecard first. The three-peat storyline will run all week regardless of his position on the leaderboard — but the golf he plays in the first 36 holes will set the terms of that conversation.
