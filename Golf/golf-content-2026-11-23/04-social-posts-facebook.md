### STORY 2: LIV Golf Bankruptcy Hearing

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 9:00 AM ET
The formal process of winding down LIV Golf moves through a New Jersey court on Tuesday, November 24. A bankruptcy disclosure hearing will consider conditionally approving LIV's disclosure statement — the step that officially begins the liquidation of the $5 billion league that Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund built from scratch in 2022.

Meanwhile, the PGA Tour is in a legal standoff with LIV's creditors. The official creditors' committee subpoenaed the Tour demanding 18 categories of financial documents — revenue, sponsorship agreements, media rights contracts and player compensation data. The Tour called the demand a "desperate fishing expedition." Judge Michael Kaplan could rule on the subpoena dispute around the same time as Tuesday's disclosure hearing.

Whatever happens Tuesday, the message is clear: LIV Golf, as it existed, is finished.

### STORY 3: LIV Players Migrate to DP World Tour

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 10:00 AM ET
As LIV Golf winds down, its players are finding new homes — and the DP World Tour is becoming the primary destination. Bryson DeChambeau leads the wave, joining as a Category 1 full member via the exemption he earned at the 2024 U.S. Open. Jon Rahm and Louis Oosthuizen are back on the European circuit through career exemptions.

Also returning: Tyrrell Hatton. And Dustin Johnson, Cameron Smith and Bubba Watson have signed agreements to play in specific DP World Tour events. Some 19 former LIV players in total have found routes into the European circuit's ecosystem.

DeChambeau, however, faces a different challenge with the PGA Tour. Reports suggest he could be ineligible to return to the American circuit for at least 12 months from his last LIV appearance. His path to Augusta and the other majors will likely run through the DP World Tour's world rankings qualification in the near term.

### STORY 4: Blades Brown's Historic PGA Tour Win

**Long-Form Post** | Window: 11:00 AM ET
On November 8, 2026, a 19-year-old named Blades Brown walked off the 18th green at El Cardonal having become the youngest PGA Tour winner in 95 years. His story — seven shots back entering the final round, a 9-under 63, a birdie on the second playoff hole over Ben James — reads like something from a movie.

Brown turned professional as a teenager and earned his PGA Tour card for 2027 by finishing in the top 20 on the Korn Ferry Tour points list. The win at the World Wide Technology Championship came before most people outside golf circles had heard his name. Now he has an invitation to The Players Championship and the PGA Championship next year.

The last player to win on Tour younger was Charles Kocsis at the 1931 Michigan Open. That was 95 years ago.
