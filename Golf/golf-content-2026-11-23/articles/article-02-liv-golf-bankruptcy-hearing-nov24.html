LIV Golf Bankruptcy Disclosure Hearing Tuesday: Wind-Down Begins, PGA Tour Fights Creditor Subpoena

By Jake Torres | November 23, 2026 | Golf Fanrecap

Tuesday's hearing in a New Jersey bankruptcy court represents the clearest single moment yet in the unraveling of LIV Golf. Judge Michael Kaplan is set to consider conditionally approving LIV's disclosure statement — the step that formally initiates the wind-down of a league that Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund built into a $5 billion enterprise before stopping its funding in April 2026.

The hearing is scheduled for November 24. If the disclosure statement is approved, it begins the process of distributing LIV's remaining assets to creditors and moves the organization toward its January 2027 liquidation target. For a league that spent three years disrupting professional golf at every level, this week marks the most significant legal milestone in its collapse.

How LIV Got Here

LIV Golf filed Chapter 11 bankruptcy on September 8, 2026, following the collapse of its restructuring deal with BC Partners Credit, which had pledged $300 million for a potential LIV 2.0 league. When that deal fell through — unable to secure enough support for a continuing operation — Saudi Arabia's PIF, which had been LIV's primary backer since its 2022 launch, declined to continue funding. The result was a league with significant remaining obligations and no path to continuation.

LIV's bankruptcy plan, filed in November, sets a January liquidation target. Some 289 employees were laid off in the wind-down, with 22 more furloughed. The league's contracts with players — many of whom received significant guaranteed payments to leave the PGA Tour — are among the creditors' primary concerns as the proceeding advances.

The PGA Tour Subpoena Fight

Alongside Tuesday's disclosure hearing, the PGA Tour is engaged in a separate dispute in the same bankruptcy proceeding. LIV's official creditors' committee issued a sweeping subpoena demanding the Tour provide documents in 18 categories — including its revenue figures, sponsorship agreements, media rights contracts and player compensation data.

The Tour called the request a "desperate fishing expedition," and indicated it would resist. Creditors set a deadline of December 4 for Tour document production. Judge Kaplan could address the subpoena dispute as part of this week's hearing or in a subsequent proceeding.

The creditors' interest in Tour financials stems from the question of whether the PGA Tour's competitive advantages over LIV — including access to major championships and world ranking points — contributed to LIV's inability to sustain its business model. The Tour has consistently rejected that framing.

What's Next

Tuesday's hearing will determine whether the wind-down proceeds on its current timeline. PGA Tour CEO Brian Rolapp has said the Tour has no current plans for a formal LIV player reinstatement program, and that any decisions on returning players will not come before January 2027. As the bankruptcy proceeding advances, the remaining questions for professional golf — where the displaced players land, how the DP World Tour absorbs LIV's roster, and whether the PGA Tour ultimately reinstates any former LIV members — will begin to find answers in the coming months.
