Thitikul Wins CME Three-Peat, Passes Sorenstam Career Earnings Record With Historic Tiburón Victory

By Ryan Calloway | November 23, 2026 | Golf Fanrecap

Atthaya "Jeeno" Thitikul shot a final-round 7-under 65 to win the 2026 CME Group Tour Championship at Tiburón Golf Club in Naples, Florida, completing a three-consecutive-year run at the LPGA's season finale that has never been accomplished by any player in the event's history. The two-shot victory over Aki Iwai was Thitikul's fifth LPGA title of 2026 and lifted her career earnings to $24,292,858 — surpassing Annika Sorenstam's record on the LPGA all-time money list.

The first-place prize was $4 million, the largest in women's golf, collected by Thitikul for the third year running. Nelly Korda, who entered the final round with a chance to earn automatic LPGA Hall of Fame entry with a win, finished third — four shots behind. For the second consecutive year, Thitikul denied Korda the season-ending victory that Korda's HOF case needed.

What Three in a Row Means

No player had won the CME Group Tour Championship in three consecutive seasons before this week. The event, the LPGA's most lucrative, is contested by the tour's best players — the field is a qualifying snapshot of the season's top performers. To dominate it three straight years at this level of competition represents a form of sustained excellence that connects Thitikul to a very short list of LPGA greatness.

The comparison to Inbee Park's three consecutive Women's PGA Championships (2013-2015) has emerged as the closest parallel, though even that achievement came in a designated major rather than a season-ending invitational. Thitikul's three-peat stands alone in this specific context, and it arrives having beaten the best field in women's golf each time.

Sorenstam's Record Falls

Annika Sorenstam's career earnings record on the LPGA Tour represented the financial peak of the greatest women's golfer of her generation — 10 major championships, 72 LPGA victories, and a career that defined the standard for excellence in women's golf. Thitikul's win at Tiburón pushed her past that figure with career earnings of $24,292,858.

She is 23 years old.

The speed at which Thitikul has accumulated career earnings reflects both her talent and the expansion of LPGA purses in recent seasons. The tour's total prize money reached $132 million in 2026 — a figure that makes the financial comparisons to Sorenstam's era complex. But the record itself belongs to Thitikul now, in whatever context it is viewed.

Five Titles, A Season to Remember

The 2026 CME win was Thitikul's fifth LPGA title of the year, cementing a season that ranks among the most productive in recent LPGA history. Five wins in a single season is an achievement that connects her to the best campaigns the sport has seen. Through the year, Thitikul maintained her world No. 1 ranking and delivered when the biggest events arrived.

What's Next

The 2026 LPGA Tour season is now complete. Thitikul enters the offseason as the tour's dominant figure, the holder of its all-time earnings record and a three-peat champion at the sport's richest event. The 2027 season schedule will be announced in coming weeks, and the conversation about where Thitikul fits among the LPGA's all-time greats — and whether she can eventually approach Sorenstam's victory and major championship totals — will only intensify.
