### STORY 2: LIV Golf Shutdown

**Long-Form Post — 8:00 AM ET**

After five years, billions of dollars, and a rivalry that fundamentally changed the conversation about professional golf, LIV Golf is done.

The Saudi-backed league announced its dissolution on November 5, 2026, after the Public Investment Fund of Saudi Arabia withdrew its financial support following the conclusion of the 2026 season. The league ran from 2022 to 2026, staging 54 events on a shotgun-format calendar that attracted dozens of the world's top players with unprecedented appearance fees and prize money.

The PGA Tour has yet to issue an official statement on how — or whether — former LIV players can return to the Tour's ranks. Some players may look to the DP World Tour, whose top 10 points finishers earn PGA Tour cards at year's end, as a re-entry route. Others may opt for PGA Tour Q-School.

For five years, LIV Golf was the most contentious topic in golf. Now it's history.

### STORY 5: Post-LIV Landscape

**Long-Form Post — 8:30 PM ET**

LIV Golf is folding. Five years after it launched with the promise of revolutionizing professional golf, the Saudi-backed league is shutting down — and its roster of players is left figuring out what comes next.

The options are limited. PGA Tour Q-School offers a traditional path, though many LIV players haven't competed in the Q-School format for years. The DP World Tour presents another route: its top 10 points finishers at the end of the European season receive PGA Tour cards, giving LIV players who can earn their way onto that circuit a viable back door.

The PGA Tour has yet to announce any formal reinstatement program, and players like Erik van Rooyen have made clear that not all PGA Tour pros are eager to roll out the welcome mat.

The post-LIV era of professional golf is beginning. The 2027 season's starting grid will look very different from anything we've seen in recent years. How it shakes out will define the sport's landscape for the next decade.
