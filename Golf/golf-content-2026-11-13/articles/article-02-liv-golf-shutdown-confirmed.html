LIV Golf Is Done: Five-Year Experiment Ends as Saudi PIF Withdraws Funding

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | November 13, 2026

LIV Golf announced its dissolution on November 5, 2026, after Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund pulled funding following the conclusion of the 2026 season, ending a five-year rivalry that transformed professional golf's landscape.

The most disruptive force in professional golf over the past five years has come to an end.

LIV Golf announced its dissolution on November 5, 2026, confirming what many in the industry had anticipated for months. The Saudi Arabia Public Investment Fund, which had bankrolled the breakaway circuit from its launch in 2022, withdrew its financial support after the conclusion of the 2026 season, and the league is now moving toward full liquidation.

Five Years That Changed Golf

Whatever one's view of LIV Golf's merits as a sporting product, its impact on professional golf is undeniable. The circuit launched in 2022 with unprecedented financial firepower, luring dozens of the world's top players — including multiple major champions — away from the PGA Tour and DP World Tour with appearance fees and prize money that those established circuits could not match.

The result was a five-year period of intense division in professional golf, with players, fans, and organizations forced to take sides in a debate that went well beyond sports and into questions of sportswashing, athlete rights, and the economics of elite competition.

LIV's 2026 season, which ran from February to August, was the league's last. After 54 events across its entire run, the circuit is now history.

The Return Question

The PGA Tour has yet to issue a formal statement on how — or whether — former LIV players will be allowed to return. The absence of an official policy has created uncertainty for dozens of players who now find themselves without a home circuit and facing limited options.

Some may pursue the DP World Tour route, as the European circuit's top 10 points finishers at year's end receive PGA Tour cards, providing a viable if challenging re-entry pathway. Others may look to the PGA Tour's own qualifying school structure, which remains the traditional mechanism for earning or restoring Tour status.

What's Next

Professional golf now enters a genuinely uncharted period. The LIV era is over. Its players, its infrastructure, and its ambitions have dissolved with it. What comes next — how the PGA Tour responds, how the DP World Tour absorbs new players, and how the sport's hierarchy reshapes itself — will be the defining story of the next twelve months in golf.

The five-year experiment is over. The reckoning is just beginning.
