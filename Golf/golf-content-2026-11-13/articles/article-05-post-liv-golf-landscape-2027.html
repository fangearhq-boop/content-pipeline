Life After LIV: Where Do Golf's Exiled Players Go in 2027?

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | November 13, 2026

With LIV Golf dissolved and the PGA Tour offering no immediate reinstatement path, the sport's former LIV roster faces a complicated road back to mainstream competition — through Q-School, the DP World Tour, or somewhere in between.

The circuit is gone. The paychecks have stopped. The question now, for dozens of professional golfers, is what comes next.

LIV Golf's dissolution on November 5, 2026 did not come with a reinstatement plan attached. The Saudi Public Investment Fund's withdrawal of funding ended the league abruptly, and the PGA Tour — which spent five years fighting the upstart circuit — has yet to issue any official policy on how or whether former LIV players can return to its ranks.

That leaves the golfers in limbo. Here is a look at the paths available to them.

Path One: PGA Tour Q-School

The traditional route back to PGA Tour status is qualifying school — a grueling multi-stage process that determines Tour cards at the end of each calendar year. For many LIV players, it's been years since they've competed in a Q-School format, and the fitness for that process — mentally, physically, and logistically — may vary significantly from player to player.

Some PGA Tour members, including Erik van Rooyen, have been vocal that this is exactly the route former LIV players should take. It is the path every other golfer without a Tour card must navigate, and the argument for consistent application of Tour rules has significant support within the player community.

Path Two: The DP World Tour

The European circuit's DP World Tour offers a potential back door. At the end of its season, the Tour's top 10 players in the rankings standings earn PGA Tour cards for the following year. For LIV players with enough eligibility to compete on the DP World Tour — and who can navigate that circuit at a high level — this provides a route back to the PGA Tour that bypasses Q-School entirely.

The challenge, of course, is that the DP World Tour's top 10 finishers are typically players who have been competing consistently at a high level within that circuit all season. Breaking into the top 10 from a standing start would require sustained excellence.

The Bigger Picture

Whatever individual paths are taken, the net result of LIV Golf's dissolution is a wholesale reshuffling of professional golf's landscape heading into 2027. Players who have been absent from PGA Tour events for two to five years are now seeking re-entry. How the Tour handles this influx — and how equitably it manages the return process — will shape the sport's culture and competitive dynamics for years to come.

What's Next

The PGA Tour's reinstatement policy, when it comes, will be the most consequential decision the Tour has made since LIV's launch. For now, golf's post-LIV era has begun — and the first chapter is being written in uncertainty.
