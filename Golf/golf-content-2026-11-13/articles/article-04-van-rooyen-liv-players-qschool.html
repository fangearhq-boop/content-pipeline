Van Rooyen Doubles Down: LIV Players Should Earn PGA Tour Status Back Through Q-School

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | November 13, 2026

PGA Tour player Erik van Rooyen reiterated this week that former LIV Golf players who left the Tour should be required to regain status through qualifying school, saying "shouldn't we just follow the rules?" as the post-LIV reinstatement debate intensifies.

Erik van Rooyen isn't sugarcoating his position.

With LIV Golf confirmed to be dissolving and its players facing an uncertain path back to mainstream competitive golf, the South African PGA Tour player has doubled down on his stance that there should be no shortcuts. Former LIV players who chose to leave the PGA Tour, in van Rooyen's view, should be required to earn their way back through qualifying school — just like any other player who loses their Tour card.

"Shouldn't we just follow the rules?" van Rooyen said this week, pushing back on any suggestion of special treatment or expedited reinstatement for his former competitors.

The Argument for Q-School

Van Rooyen's position reflects a sentiment held by a segment of the PGA Tour's player base. The argument centers on competitive equity: players who maintained their Tour cards, ground through the Q-School process, or fought their way through the Korn Ferry Tour earned their spots under the same rules that have always governed the Tour. Granting LIV defectors preferential reinstatement, the argument goes, would be unfair to those who didn't take the financial path.

There is also the question of precedent. The PGA Tour's membership rules exist for a reason, and departing for a rival circuit was a deliberate choice made by LIV's players — many of whom were well-compensated for that decision. Using those same rules consistently going forward would, in van Rooyen's framing, simply mean holding everyone to the same standard.

The Counter-Argument

Others in golf circles have argued that blanket Q-School requirements for established major champions or former world No. 1 players would be unnecessarily punitive — a position that could also damage the Tour's reputation for welcoming elite competition. The PGA Tour, as of this writing, has not issued an official reinstatement policy.

What's Next

The debate over how the PGA Tour handles its post-LIV era will likely intensify through the winter as players seek clarity on their options. Van Rooyen, meanwhile, is backing his words with performance: he co-leads the Austin Championship at 8-under after Round 1, keeping him at the center of this week's golf conversation in every sense.

The PGA Tour's official stance on former LIV players — when it comes — will be one of the most consequential policy decisions in professional golf in years.
