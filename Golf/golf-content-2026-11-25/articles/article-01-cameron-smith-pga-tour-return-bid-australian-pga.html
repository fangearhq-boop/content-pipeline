Cameron Smith Declares PGA Tour Return His Goal as BMW Australian PGA Begins

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | November 25, 2026

Cameron Smith has never been one to hide what he wants. And ahead of the BMW Australian PGA Championship at The Lakes in Sydney, Australia's most prominent golfer of recent years delivered his clearest statement yet on where his career is headed next.

"It's where I want to be now, without a doubt," Smith said when asked about returning to the PGA Tour, in pre-tournament interviews conducted Monday and Tuesday.

The comment was unambiguous — and it sets the stage for what could be one of the most compelling storylines of the Australian golf summer.

A Familiar Course, a New Mission

Smith tees off Thursday at The Lakes alongside Nicolai Højgaard and Corey Conners, playing under a past-champion exemption. He is a three-time BMW Australian PGA winner, having claimed the title in 2017, 2018, and 2022, and his history at this course makes him one of the most dangerous players in the field regardless of his current tour status.

But this week is about more than a trophy. It is about re-establishing Smith in the mainstream global game — and in that context, every round matters.

The Road Back

Smith left the PGA Tour in 2022 to join LIV Golf, a decision made just weeks after his stunning victory at The Open Championship at St. Andrews. His departure — like those of many marquee players before and after — suspended his PGA Tour membership.

With LIV Golf now having effectively ceased operations following the broader reconciliation between the PGA Tour and DP World Tour, Smith's options for returning to the highest level of professional golf are becoming clearer.

Two primary pathways exist: win a co-sanctioned PGA Tour/DP World Tour event, which would restore tour eligibility directly, or finish inside the top 10 of the DP World Tour's season-long points race, which would secure a full tour card for the following year.

The BMW Australian PGA Championship, co-sanctioned by both tours, qualifies as one of those opportunities. It is not lost on anyone that Smith is stepping onto a stage specifically designed for this kind of comeback narrative.

A Genuine Contender

To view Smith purely through the lens of his tour status would be to miss the golfer standing in front of you. At 33, he remains one of the most skilled players in the world. His short game and putting rank among the best of his generation, and his comfort at The Lakes — where he has three trophies — gives him an edge that few players in the field can match.

The 2026 Australian golf season offers two major events: the BMW Australian PGA this week, and the Australian Open at Kingston Heath in Melbourne (December 3-6), where McIlroy, Smith, and Adam Scott are all expected to compete.

For Smith, the goal is now stated plainly. The work begins Thursday.

What's Next

First-round play at The BMW Australian PGA Championship gets underway Thursday at The Lakes in Sydney. Smith tees off alongside Nicolai Højgaard and Corey Conners. The Australian Open follows December 3-6 at Kingston Heath in Melbourne, where Smith is expected to continue his bid for a PGA Tour return exemption.
