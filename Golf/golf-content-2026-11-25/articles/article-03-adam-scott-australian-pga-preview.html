Adam Scott Eyes Historic Third BMW Australian PGA Title at The Lakes Sydney

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | November 25, 2026

Some players age gracefully. Adam Scott ages defiantly.

At 46 years old, the Queensland-born golfer is in the field at this week's BMW Australian PGA Championship at The Lakes in Sydney — the venue where he has already claimed two titles — and his form suggests he is arriving with more than sentimental purpose.

The History

Scott won the BMW Australian PGA Championship in 2013 and again in 2019, six years apart. Those victories, bookended around a Masters title in 2013, speak to a player who has remained competitive at the highest level for an extraordinary stretch of time.

A third title would put Scott in a category of his own at this event. It would also be a milestone moment for Australian golf — a home hero completing a historic trefoil at a beloved national venue.

"He's shown he still belongs," said one tour observer ahead of the week. "And this is the kind of course where experience matters."

Arriving in Form

The 2026 season has not been Scott's most prolific in terms of victories, but his results speak to a player who has maintained elite competence well into his mid-40s. He finished tied for ninth at The Open Championship and tied for 12th at the FedEx St. Jude Championship — results that reflect consistent ball-striking and sharp short game play.

At last year's edition of the BMW Australian PGA, Scott finished tied for ninth. That result, at a course where he has previously won, suggests he knows how to navigate The Lakes and continues to do so at a high level.

Beyond The Lakes

Scott is also confirmed in the field for the Australian Open at Kingston Heath in Melbourne, running December 3-6. The Australian Open is the one significant title that has eluded him throughout a career that includes a Masters, multiple PGA Tour victories, and a former No. 1 world ranking.

For fans who have watched Scott's career closely, that gap remains one of the more curious chapters in his record. He has been competitive in the Australian Open on multiple occasions without breaking through. At 46, with his career entering its final competitive chapters, the Australian Open carries particular resonance.

What to Watch

At The Lakes this week, Scott will be one of the most watched players in the field — not just because of his history there, but because of the broader narrative around this Australian golf double-header. With Cameron Smith pursuing a PGA Tour return bid and Rory McIlroy expected at Kingston Heath, the Australian golf calendar has rarely been more star-studded.

Scott does not need the extra storylines. For him, the focus is what it has always been: a golf course he knows well, a trophy he has lifted before, and the belief that there is more to give.

What's Next

First-round play at the BMW Australian PGA Championship begins Thursday at The Lakes in Sydney. Scott will then travel to Melbourne for the Australian Open at Kingston Heath, December 3-6, 2026.
