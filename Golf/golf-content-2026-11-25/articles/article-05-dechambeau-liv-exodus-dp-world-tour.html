DeChambeau and the LIV Exodus — 10+ Former Players Reset at DP World Tour 2027

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | November 25, 2026

The golf civil war is ending. It is not ending with a bang, but with a series of quiet confirmations, membership announcements, and carefully worded press releases — and at the center of it is Bryson DeChambeau, the most prominent face of LIV Golf's commercial era who is now leading the route out.

The Wave

DeChambeau is confirmed as a DP World Tour member for the 2027 season, joining a group of at least nine other former LIV Golf players who have secured the same status. Among those confirmed: Jon Rahm, Dustin Johnson, Bubba Watson, Patrick Reed, and Louis Oosthuizen.

The list represents a cross-section of LIV's most recognizable names — multiple major champions, former world No. 1 players, and players who left the PGA Tour at the peak of their earning power for the financial security of the Saudi-backed breakaway circuit.

LIV Golf has now effectively ceased operations following the broader reconciliation framework agreed between the PGA Tour and the DP World Tour. The specifics of that framework remain complex, but the practical result is becoming clear: the independent circuit that disrupted professional golf for three years is winding down, and its players are returning.

The DP World Tour as Re-Entry Point

The DP World Tour's role in this transition is deliberate and significant. The European-based tour has become the primary re-entry pathway for former LIV players before they can regain PGA Tour eligibility. By joining the DP World Tour for 2027, players like DeChambeau and Rahm can rebuild their world ranking points, play in world-ranking-eligible events, and demonstrate to the PGA Tour that they are genuinely reintegrating into the mainstream structure.

The DP World Tour has shown itself willing to facilitate this process — this week at the BMW Australian PGA Championship, three former LIV players received special invitations despite not holding current cards, and eight former LIV players are in the full field. The pattern is clear.

The PGA Tour Ban Remains — For Now

What the DP World Tour cannot fix immediately is the PGA Tour's standing ban on returning LIV players. The Tour's policy mandates a one-year suspension from the date of a player's last LIV Golf appearance before they can apply for PGA Tour membership reinstatement.

PGA Tour CEO Brian Rolapp has indicated that an update to this policy may come in early 2027. The nature of that update — whether it accelerates the timeline, waives bans entirely, or simply provides more clarity — remains to be seen.

Until it arrives, the most important players in the world will be competing on a tour that — while prestigious — is a rung below where they once stood.

What It Means for the Game

The return of DeChambeau, Rahm, Johnson, and others matters for professional golf's commercial health. These are players who drive television ratings, sell tickets, and define the narrative of the sport. Their return to world-ranking events restores a level of talent concentration to global tournament golf that the LIV era had diluted.

It is not a clean ending. The financial wounds, trust deficits, and policy questions from the LIV era will take years to fully resolve. But the direction is now unmistakable, and DeChambeau's presence at the front of the returning pack gives it a face that even casual fans recognize.

What's Next

Bryson DeChambeau and other confirmed DP World Tour 2027 members will begin their European Tour campaigns when the new season opens in January 2027. PGA Tour CEO Brian Rolapp is expected to provide an update on the ban policy in early 2027.
