Rahm Wins LIV Indianapolis, Clinches Third Consecutive Individual Season Title

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | August 21, 2026

Jon Rahm capped a dominant 2026 LIV Golf season by winning the Indianapolis event at The Club at Chatham Hills in Westfield, Indiana — adding an individual tournament title to the individual season championship he had already locked up weeks earlier. It was the kind of closing statement that underscores just how completely Rahm has owned the LIV circuit across three consecutive seasons.

Rahm had clinched his third straight LIV Golf Individual Season Championship before arriving in Indianapolis, securing the title at LIV Golf New York with 944.54 season points. The Indianapolis finale was, in one sense, a coronation lap. In another, it was proof that Rahm competes the same way whether the trophy is decided or not.

Three in a Row

Three consecutive LIV individual season championships is a record that puts Rahm's circuit dominance in stark historical context. The LIV Golf circuit, now in its fourth full season, has never had another player approach that level of sustained individual excellence. Rahm's combination of elite ball-striking, competitive intelligence, and mental toughness has made him the face of the tour in a way that transcends the team structure that defines most of LIV's weekly competition.

The Indianapolis purse stood at $10.1 million — reduced from the original $20 million following the Saudi PIF's announced decision to end its direct financial backing of LIV Golf after 2026. Even at the reduced figure, the winner's share of $2.02 million represents meaningful prize money. Rahm collected both the event title and the season crown in the same week.

Pete Dye's Final Design

The Club at Chatham Hills holds a particular distinction in golf architecture: it was the final course designed by Pete Dye before his death in January 2020. Dye's fingerprints are visible throughout the layout — the railroad tie bunker faces, the island green aesthetics that made TPC Sawgrass famous, the deceptive par-3s that reward accurate club selection over raw distance.

Hosting the final LIV Golf event of the 2026 season gave Chatham Hills a moment of visibility befitting the course's architectural pedigree. For Rahm, winning at a Pete Dye design carries its own resonance — he has won on Dye layouts before, and his game translates well to the precision demands that Dye courses tend to impose.

Team Title on the Line

While Rahm's individual story dominated the individual headlines, the team championship carried its own weight entering Indianapolis. 4Aces GC — the team of Dustin Johnson, Thomas Pieters, Thomas Detry, and Anthony Kim — held a 2.25-point lead over Ripper GC (Cameron Smith, Marc Leishman, Lucas Herbert, and Smylie Kaufman) heading into the finale. The Indianapolis points would determine which team claimed the 2026 LIV team title.

The team competition adds a layer to LIV events that separates the circuit from conventional stroke play — every shot carries implications not just for the individual, but for three teammates whose fortunes are tied to the same scoreboard. The finale format, where team standings are on the line simultaneously with individual results, creates a pressure environment that tests different competitive instincts.

What LIV Looks Like After 2026

The Saudi PIF's decision to end direct backing after 2026 makes this season's Indianapolis finale something of a pivot point for the circuit. LIV Golf has operated under that model since launching in 2022, and the structural changes coming after this season will reshape what the tour looks like in 2027 and beyond.

Rahm has been the constant through that uncertainty — winning individual season championships regardless of the external conversation about LIV's future. Whatever comes next for the circuit, his three-title run stands as the defining individual narrative of the LIV era to this point.
