McIlroy Shoots 64 to Co-Lead BMW Championship — One Week After 66th at St. Jude

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | August 21, 2026

Seven days ago, Rory McIlroy shot 8-over par at the St. Jude Championship and finished 66th. He said he was "far away from where I need to be." He went home, worked on his game, and came to Bellerive Country Club.

Then he shot a 64.

McIlroy's first round at the BMW Championship produced a 6-under 64 that tied him for the lead alongside Gary Woodland, Wyndham Clark, J.J. Spaun, and Chris Gotterup — five players sharing the top of the leaderboard after Thursday's opening round at the Robert Trent Jones Sr.-designed par-70 layout in St. Louis. While defending FedExCup points leader Scottie Scheffler opened at +2 in a tie for 44th, McIlroy arrived at Bellerive and played like the back-to-back Masters champion he is.

The Reversal

The narrative contrast is striking enough that Golf Channel led with it: "Scheffler struggles, McIlroy surges" as the defining story of Thursday at Bellerive. It's a reversal of the roles these two have played for much of 2026. Scheffler has been dominant — the FedExCup points leader by roughly 1,400 points, the St. Jude winner by 8 shots. McIlroy has been formidable in the majors — back-to-back Masters champion — but inconsistent during the summer's FedExCup stretch, including that dismal St. Jude finish.

A round of 64 at Bellerive is not an accident. The course asks for precise driving and sharp iron play into relatively small greens on a layout that plays over 7,400 yards. McIlroy gave Bellerive both, constructing a round that looked like a reminder to the rest of the field of what he's capable of when everything clicks.

The Co-Leaders

The five-way tie at -6 includes a notable subplot: Gary Woodland's inclusion in the lead group carries FedExCup implications that extend beyond the BMW title race. Woodland entered the week in 31st place in FedExCup standings — just outside the top 30 that advance to the Tour Championship at East Lake. His 64 included a hole-out eagle, five birdies, and one bogey, a round that puts him inside the bubble picture in a significant way as Round 2 develops Friday.

Clark, Spaun, and Gotterup round out the five-way tie, each with realistic paths to the weekend as leaders at a tournament where the FedExCup pressure adds an extra layer of stakes to every shot.

Scheffler's Position

A +2 opening round for Scottie Scheffler at the BMW Championship is not a crisis. His FedExCup position is iron-clad — the gap between Scheffler and the field in the points standings is such that he could miss the cut at Bellerive and still advance to East Lake comfortably. The BMW title is a different matter. Eight shots back after Round 1 on a par-70 course where low scoring is required demands an extraordinary weekend to factor in the top group.

Scheffler has made habit of not staying down. His record in 2026 includes rounds where he's come from behind to win. Whether Bellerive is one of those occasions is the storyline that runs through Rounds 2, 3, and 4.

What's Next

Round 2 is underway Friday at Bellerive Country Club. McIlroy was paired with Xander Schauffele and teed off at 10:47 AM ET. The BMW Championship is a 72-hole strokeplay event; the top 30 players after the conclusion of the tournament advance to the Tour Championship at East Lake Golf Club in Atlanta, August 27-30. Scheffler, despite his Round 1 struggles, remains safely inside that number. The BMW title is another question entirely.
