Yamashita Leads CPKC Women's Open After Round 1 — Korda 3 Back at Royal Mayfair

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | August 21, 2026

Japan's Miyu Yamashita opened the 2026 CPKC Women's Open with a brilliant first round at Royal Mayfair Golf Club in Edmonton, taking the lead at 8-under par and establishing herself as the early name to beat at one of Canadian golf's most celebrated annual events.

Compatriot Nasa Hataoka is one shot back at -7, and American Kiara Romero sits at -6. The most watched player on the leaderboard, though, is Nelly Korda — the World No. 1, winner of four LPGA events in 2026 including three majors — who opened at -3 with a 67. Korda is three shots back of Yamashita, well within striking range entering Round 2 on Friday.

Yamashita's Strong Opening

Yamashita's round was among the best of the day at Royal Mayfair, a par-70 layout designed by Stanley Thompson that stretches to 6,452 yards. The course rewards precision over power, and Yamashita's ball-striking gave her multiple birdie opportunities that she converted at a consistent rate throughout the round. Going -8 on a par-70 layout means shooting 62 — a number that underscores the quality of the opening performance.

Hataoka, at -7, gives the Japanese contingent a strong pair at the top of the leaderboard. Both players have experience competing in North American conditions, and the pairing of two Japanese players leading a major Canadian event adds an interesting international dimension to the weekend storylines.

Korda In Pursuit

Nelly Korda's 2026 season has been one of the great individual performances in recent LPGA history — four wins including the Chevron Championship, the Riviera May Open, and the U.S. Women's Open have established her as the year's dominant player. She came to Edmonton as the heavy pre-tournament favorite.

A -3 opening round puts Korda three back of Yamashita, a deficit that is perfectly manageable over the remaining three rounds. The pattern of Korda entering weekend contention from off the pace and using her mental and physical strength to close is well-documented from her 2026 campaign. Royal Mayfair may be one more example of the same pattern if she can bring her best golf on Friday and Saturday.

Henderson, Ko, and the Canadian Thread

Brooke Henderson, the defending champion who won the 2025 edition by a single shot over Minjee Lee for her 14th LPGA title, is the emotional center of the week for Canadian fans. Playing in front of a home crowd that has followed her career from near-misses to major victories, Henderson represents the kind of storyline that elevates a tournament beyond sport into something personal for the fans in attendance.

Lydia Ko also looms over the proceedings in a way unique to this event. Ko is a three-time CPKC Women's Open champion — and her first title at Royal Mayfair came in 2013 when she was 15 years old, an amateur, and the youngest LPGA winner in history at the time. A fourth title at this course would be one of golf's more romantic narratives. Ko is in the field and chasing.

The Canadian field contingent this year is the largest in tournament history — 15 Canadian players competing on home soil, a record that reflects both the growth of the sport in Canada and the event's stature as a destination for the country's best talent.

What's Next

Round 2 of the CPKC Women's Open continues Friday at Royal Mayfair Golf Club in Edmonton. Coverage is available on Golf Channel. The tournament runs through Sunday, August 23, with the winner claiming the largest share of a $2.75 million purse. Going into the weekend, the leaderboard picture will become clearer as the field completes Round 2 on Friday.
