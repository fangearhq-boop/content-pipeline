Scheffler Opens BMW Championship at +2 — Eight Shots Back, East Lake Not in Doubt

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | August 21, 2026

Scottie Scheffler entered Bellerive Country Club this week as the FedExCup points leader by approximately 1,400 points, the St. Jude Championship winner by 8 shots, and the player everyone expected to contend for the BMW title. After Thursday's first round, he sits at 2-over par in a tie for 44th, eight shots behind the five co-leaders.

Scheffler's trip to the Tour Championship at East Lake is not in doubt — his FedExCup lead is large enough to survive a missed cut, let alone a difficult opening round. His BMW Championship title prospects, however, require an extraordinary charge beginning Friday if he intends to factor into the weekend conversation at Bellerive.

A Course That Demands Precision

Bellerive Country Club's Robert Trent Jones Sr. design plays at 7,448 yards this week, a par-70 layout that asks for precision from the tee and sharp iron play into relatively small greens. Scheffler is one of the best ball-strikers in professional golf, but on Thursday at Bellerive, a few misses compounded in a way that pushed his scorecard to 2-over rather than the 4-under-or-better number that contention typically requires at this level.

The scoring conditions at Bellerive on Thursday were clearly favorable — five players reached -6, and the top of the leaderboard was clustered with players who scored well. When the course is gettable and a player of Scheffler's caliber opens at +2, the gap feels larger than the raw numbers suggest. Eight shots in professional golf at a 72-hole event is not insurmountable, but it is a significant deficit to overcome in three rounds.

The McIlroy Reversal

The defining narrative from Thursday at the BMW Championship is the contrast between Scheffler and Rory McIlroy. One week ago at the St. Jude Championship, McIlroy finished 66th at 8-over. He said he was "far away from where I need to be." Scheffler won St. Jude by 8 shots, further cementing what had already been an exceptional 2026 campaign.

At Bellerive on Thursday, those roles reversed entirely. McIlroy shot a 64 to co-lead the BMW Championship. Scheffler shot a 72 and sits eight shots back. The swing in their relative positions over the span of seven days — from a 14-shot gap in Scheffler's favor at St. Jude to a 8-shot gap in McIlroy's favor at the BMW — is the kind of fluctuation that golf produces with regularity, and which makes predicting outcomes over any short stretch nearly impossible.

Scheffler's Record of Recovery

The reason Scheffler at +2 in a tie for 44th is not yet a crisis is his documented capacity for recovery. His 2026 season includes rounds and stretches where he has fallen back from early deficits and reasserted himself. The question at Bellerive is whether the deficit is too large, too early, on a course where low scoring is available but cannot be manufactured without everything working together.

Scheffler holds 4,873 FedExCup points entering the BMW Championship, a total that secures his East Lake berth regardless of what happens this week. The BMW title is a separate ambition — and one that would require a near-perfect three-round sequence from a player who has demonstrated that kind of sequence is within his range when his game is sharp.

What's Next

Round 2 continues Friday at Bellerive. Scheffler's position heading into the weekend will clarify whether a realistic comeback into contention is possible, or whether this becomes a week focused on maintaining form for East Lake rather than competing for the BMW title. The co-leaders at -6 — McIlroy, Gary Woodland, Wyndham Clark, J.J. Spaun, and Chris Gotterup — set a target that demands low scoring from every remaining round to reach. Scheffler is capable of that scoring. Whether he delivers it at Bellerive is what Round 2 will begin to answer.
