LIV Golf Bankruptcy — Oct. 13 Deadline Looms as Rahm Hints at DP World Tour Return
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Window: 10:30 AM ET LIV Golf filed Chapter 11 on September 8. Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, the circuit's original backer, ended its financial commitment after investing more than $5 billion since the league launched in 2022. PIF then turned around and provided $49.6 million in debtor-in-possession financing — essentially keeping the lights on while a restructured organization takes shape. October 13 is the critical court deadline. Players are reportedly in "advanced discussions" to take a controlling stake in LIV 2.0. And then there's Jon Rahm. The two-time major champion is owed approximately $150 million from his original LIV contract — and he holds an unsecured claim of $5 million-plus in the bankruptcy. On September 15, Rahm told media he was "noncommittal on LIV 2.0" and saw the DP World Tour as an "opportunity." He added that full clarity won't come until "the current season is done." That's not a commitment to leave. But it's the clearest signal yet that the path back to European and world tour golf is something Rahm is seriously considering. #Golf #LIVGolf | Follow Golf Fanrecap for updates as the restructuring unfolds. ---

Jon Rahm "Noncommittal on LIV 2.0" — What It Means for Golf's Future
NOT VERIFIEDFACEBOOK
### Long-Form Post Window: 1:00 PM ET Jon Rahm is dropping the clearest hints yet about his future. The two-time major champion — who left the PGA Tour for LIV Golf in late 2023 as part of one of the largest contracts in golf history — told media on September 15 that he is "noncommittal" about LIV 2.0, the restructured organization being built out of the Chapter 11 bankruptcy filing. He also specifically mentioned the DP World Tour as an "opportunity," a phrase that carries significant weight. Rahm is owed approximately $150 million from his original LIV contract. With the bankruptcy reorganization ongoing and the October 13 court deadline approaching, the question of whether that obligation transfers to LIV 2.0, gets restructured, or gets challenged entirely is unresolved. His ability to return to the DP World Tour — or potentially the PGA Tour — depends on those legal outcomes. Rahm said he cannot provide full clarity until "the current season is done," which is consistent with honoring his existing obligations while remaining open to what comes next. But in the increasingly fragmented landscape of professional golf, Rahm's next move matters enormously. #Golf | Follow Golf Fanrecap for the latest on LIV Golf and the tours. ---

### Long-Form Post Window: 8:30 PM ET Scottie Scheffler won the 2026 FedEx Cup. And now the fall season begins. Scheffler came from behind at East Lake to win the Tour Championship by three strokes over Viktor Hovland at 16-under par. The victory gave him his second FedEx Cup title in three years — joining Tiger Woods (2007, 2009) and Rory McIlroy (2016, 2019, 2022) as the only players in history to win the FedExCup multiple times. He also passed Woods on the PGA Tour's all-time career earnings list, surpassing $130 million. His 2026 season: three wins, including the Tour Championship, with only two finishes outside the top 15 in 20 starts. No major in 2026, but the consistency argument for Player of the Year is compelling. The FedExCup Fall launches Thursday with the Biltmore Championship in Asheville, and while Scheffler is taking a short break before the Presidents Cup, the field in North Carolina will be laying down markers for the 2026-27 season.
