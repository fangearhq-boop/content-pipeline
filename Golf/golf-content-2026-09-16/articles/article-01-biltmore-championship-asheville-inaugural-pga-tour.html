Asheville Hosts Its First PGA Tour Event Since 1942 — The Inaugural Biltmore Championship Tees Off Thursday

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | September 16, 2026

For the first time in more than 80 years, professional golf's top circuit is coming to Asheville, North Carolina. The inaugural Biltmore Championship at The Cliffs at Walnut Cove opens Thursday, September 17-20, marking the first PGA Tour event in the region since 1942. It also launches the FedExCup Fall, the stretch of the season that sets the table for where players stand heading into the 2026-27 campaign.

The 132-player field carries more weight than a typical fall event. Eight of the 24 players selected for the 2026 Presidents Cup — which begins at Medinah Country Club the following weekend — are scheduled to compete in Asheville. For those players, the Biltmore Championship is part preparation, part final tune-up before one of golf's marquee team events.

What's at Stake

The winner receives 500 FedExCup Fall points and 25 Official World Golf Ranking points — meaningful totals in a fall season that feeds directly into priority status for the 2026-27 tour schedule. Players who perform early in the fall build security for the full-season schedule, and OWGR points accumulate toward world ranking positions that affect major championship exemptions.

The headliners include Justin Thomas, Jackson Koivun — the youngest member of Brandt Snedeker's Presidents Cup team — JT Poston, Tony Finau, and Billy Horschel. For Thomas in particular, the fall represents an opportunity to build on a season that saw him selected as a Presidents Cup pick, a signal from Snedeker that Thomas remains one of the team's cornerstones.

Asheville's Return to Tour Golf

The Biltmore Estate and the surrounding Asheville region have a history with championship golf, but the PGA Tour's presence in the area had been absent since the early 1940s. The Cliffs at Walnut Cove, located in Arden just south of Asheville, provides a mountain-setting layout that will be new to essentially every player in the field.

New venues always create unpredictable first-time results — players who prepare best for courses they've never played have historically overperformed at inaugural events. That narrative is worth watching across all four rounds.

What's Next

The Biltmore Championship runs September 17-20. Watch for leaderboard updates from the opening round Thursday, and track which Presidents Cup players use the event most effectively as a warm-up. The following weekend's Presidents Cup at Medinah — September 24-27 — will be the immediate next chapter for eight of the players teeing it up in Asheville this week.
