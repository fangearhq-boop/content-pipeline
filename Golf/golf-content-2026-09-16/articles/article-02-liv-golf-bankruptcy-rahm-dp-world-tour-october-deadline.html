LIV Golf's October 13 Deadline Approaches — Rahm Says He's "Noncommittal" on LIV 2.0 and Sees DP World Tour "Opportunity"

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | September 16, 2026

Jon Rahm issued his clearest signal yet on September 15 that his future beyond LIV Golf's Chapter 11 restructuring is genuinely open. Speaking to media, the two-time major champion described himself as "noncommittal" on participating in LIV 2.0 — the restructured organization being assembled through the bankruptcy process — and said he "sees opportunity" in the DP World Tour. He added that full clarity won't come until "the current season is done."

The backdrop is LIV Golf's Chapter 11 bankruptcy filing on September 8, 2026, in a New Jersey court. Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, which had invested more than $5 billion in LIV since the circuit launched in 2022, ended its primary financing commitment. PIF then pivoted to provide $49.6 million in debtor-in-possession financing — bridge funding to keep operations running during the restructuring. The Restructuring Support Agreement was signed September 8, making October 13 the next critical court deadline.

The Money at Stake

Rahm's situation within the bankruptcy is complicated. His original contract with LIV is reportedly valued at approximately $150 million. He also holds an unsecured creditor claim of more than $5 million in the bankruptcy proceedings. The central legal question is what happens to those obligations in a restructured LIV 2.0 — whether they carry over, get renegotiated, or get challenged as part of the reorganization.

Players are reportedly in "advanced discussions" to take a controlling ownership stake in the restructured entity, with BC Partners Credit expected to provide exit financing when LIV 2.0 formally emerges from Chapter 11. What that structure actually looks like — and whether it's attractive enough to retain a player of Rahm's caliber — remains unresolved.

What "Sees Opportunity" Means

Rahm's choice of language matters. He said he "sees opportunity" in the DP World Tour rather than saying he is pursuing a return. That phrasing keeps his options open while signaling genuine interest in reconnecting with European professional golf. If LIV 2.0's structure doesn't honor the original contract terms, Rahm would have considerable legal and practical basis to exit.

A return to the DP World Tour would not automatically restore PGA Tour membership, which operates under separate rules. But given the ongoing normalization between the PGA Tour and DP World Tour, a Rahm return to European golf could still give him access to major championship exemptions and some of the world's premier events.

What's Next

October 13 is the date to watch in the bankruptcy proceedings. Rahm has framed the end of the current LIV season as when he'll provide clearer answers. Follow developments in U.S. Bankruptcy Court as the restructuring timeline moves forward — any indication of how player contracts will be treated in LIV 2.0 will be a significant signal of whether Rahm stays or goes.
