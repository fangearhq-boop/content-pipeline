What Jon Rahm Returning to the DP World Tour Would Actually Mean for Golf

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | September 16, 2026

Jon Rahm's September 15 comments — "noncommittal" on LIV 2.0, "sees opportunity" in the DP World Tour — landed with impact because of what they imply, not just what they say. Rahm is the most prominent player in the LIV circuit by virtually any measure. Two major titles. Former world No. 1. If he walks, the argument that LIV 2.0 is a legitimate successor to the original league becomes significantly harder to make.

The question worth examining is what a Rahm return to the DP World Tour actually looks like in practical terms.

The Legal Path

Rahm's existing contract with LIV is valued at roughly $150 million. Leaving it — or having it rendered unenforceable — requires either that LIV 2.0 fails to honor the terms, that the bankruptcy court effectively eliminates the obligation, or that a mutual release is negotiated. None of those outcomes is certain before October 13, the next major deadline in the Chapter 11 proceedings.

If the contract is restructured or not honored in full, Rahm would likely have grounds to consider himself released from his LIV obligation. At that point, the DP World Tour's doors would be open. His reinstatement would require applying to the DP World Tour's membership governance structure, but given his stature in the game, that process is not expected to be a barrier.

What It Means for Major Exemptions

DP World Tour membership provides access to the Race to Dubai, the Rolex Series events, and importantly, several routes to major championship exemptions — the Open Championship in particular. A Rahm return to the DP World Tour would keep him competing at the highest level of global golf, even without PGA Tour membership.

The broader convergence between the PGA Tour and DP World Tour under the ongoing joint framework negotiations makes the path between the two systems more fluid than it was when Rahm originally left. A return to European tour golf isn't a consolation prize in 2026 the way it might have felt in 2023.

What's Next

Rahm has set "the end of the current season" as when he'll provide clearer answers. Watch October 13 and the subsequent bankruptcy proceedings for any indication of how player contracts are being treated. If LIV 2.0's terms are unfavorable, Rahm's "opportunity" in Europe could become a decision faster than the timeline suggests.
