Nelly Korda Enters ANNIKA Final Round Behind Leaders, World No. 1 Set Up for Sunday Chase

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | November 15, 2026

Nelly Korda enters the final round of The ANNIKA driven by Gainbridge at Pelican several shots behind leader Lindy Duncan. The world's No. 1 player came to Belleair described as "chasing history" by the LPGA. Sunday will show what that means in real time.

The World's Best Player Is Behind

BELLEAIR, Fla. — When the LPGA described Nelly Korda's entry into The ANNIKA driven by Gainbridge at Pelican as "chasing history," it was a promotional statement. On Sunday, it describes a sporting reality. Korda enters the final round behind Lindy Duncan's 54-hole lead at -13 and faces the mathematics of the classic Sunday chase.

The world's No. 1 player needs to go low. The leader needs to give shots back. Neither is guaranteed. Both are possible. And Korda is exactly the kind of player who makes both more likely by her mere presence on the leaderboard.

Duncan leads at -13. Hye-Jin Choi is at -12, one back. Yuri Yoshida is at -11. Korda is tracking further back — but the gap to the lead is not insurmountable for a player of her caliber in a final round with $3.25 million and 500 CME Global points on the line.

What Korda Brings to Any Sunday

Korda's 2026 season has been defined by the kind of steady, elite performance that has made her the most reliable closer in women's golf. She is the world's No. 1 player not because of a single tournament or a single season, but because she competes at the highest level, week in and week out, and she wins when the moment calls for it.

That track record is precisely what makes her dangerous entering the final round of any event. Players behind a lead often fade when the pressure builds. Korda typically moves in the other direction — toward the moment rather than away from it. The final-round stage at Pelican Golf Club in Belleair is the type of setting she competes best on.

For Lindy Duncan, the presence of Korda on the leaderboard adds a layer of psychological complexity that most competitors cannot match. Duncan has earned this lead through 54 holes of excellent golf. Maintaining it against a player with Korda's closing credentials requires the same composure that produced Saturday's bogey-free 69 — and then some.

The $3.25 million purse and 500 CME points give the contest weight. The storyline gives it theater. Korda chasing Duncan — world No. 1 hunting a 12-year veteran seeking her first win on her 222nd try — is exactly the kind of Sunday that makes golf worth watching.

What's Next

Final round coverage of The ANNIKA driven by Gainbridge at Pelican begins at 1:30 PM ET on Golf Channel and CNBC. The gap between Korda and the lead will determine whether the narrative today is a first-time winner holding off the best player in the world, or the best player in the world denying a 12-year wait its ending.
