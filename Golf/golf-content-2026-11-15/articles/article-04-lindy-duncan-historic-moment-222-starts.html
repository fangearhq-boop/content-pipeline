Lindy Duncan's 222-Start Journey: The Road to The ANNIKA Final Round Is One She's Earned

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | November 15, 2026

Lindy Duncan has played 222 LPGA Tour events in a 12-year professional career without winning. That number is not a failure — it is the evidence of a career built on longevity, persistence, and competitive integrity. Sunday at The ANNIKA is her day to close the gap.

What 222 Starts Actually Means

There is a version of Lindy Duncan's story that gets told as a tragedy: 222 starts, zero wins, still searching. That version misses the point.

To play 222 times on the LPGA Tour, you have to be good enough to earn a card, hold a card, and compete at the highest level of women's professional golf year after year. Players come and go. Tours are won and lost. The grind weeds out everyone except the ones who belong. Duncan has belonged for 12 years.

She turned professional in 2013 and has built a career of consistent, competitive golf that the win column has not reflected. That is not unusual in professional sports, where the margin between winners and everyone else can come down to a putt at the right moment, a tee shot that finds rough instead of fairway, a bounce that sends a ball into the gallery instead of past the pin. Duncan has been competitive. She has not had the moment.

Sunday at Pelican Golf Club in Belleair, Florida, may be that moment.

How She Got Here

Duncan's week at The ANNIKA driven by Gainbridge at Pelican has been a demonstration of everything that has made her career worth following. She opened with a 62 — a round that said clearly this is not someone hanging on, this is someone competing for a title. She followed it with a 66, holding serve in the middle of the tournament. Then came Saturday's cold front, the kind of weather that separates the mentally prepared from the mechanically sound.

Duncan shot a bogey-free 69 on Saturday. Clean golf in difficult conditions with the first LPGA Tour win of her career suddenly close enough to see. That 69 — especially the absence of bogeys — suggested a player who understood exactly what was required and delivered it without drama.

She enters Sunday's final round at -13, one shot ahead of Hye-Jin Choi. The event carries a $3.25 million purse and 500 CME Global points. The number that matters most to Duncan, though, is simpler: 1. One win. That is all that separates her from a career that has had everything except the defining moment she deserves.

What's Next

The ANNIKA final round begins at 1:30 PM ET on Golf Channel and CNBC. Duncan enters as the 54-hole leader in her 222nd LPGA Tour start. Win or not, this week is already one of the defining chapters of a career worth celebrating. If she wins, it becomes one of the most satisfying stories the LPGA has produced in years.
