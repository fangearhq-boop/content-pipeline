LIV Golf Bankruptcy: $40M+ Owed to Players, Rahm and DeChambeau Among Largest Claimants

By Marcus Cole | Golf Fanrecap | November 15, 2026

LIV Golf's Chapter 11 bankruptcy case in New Jersey continues to develop, with court filings revealing more than $40 million owed to players. Jon Rahm is listed at approximately $7.5 million, Bryson DeChambeau at approximately $5.7 million. A January 2027 emergence target depends on enough players signing under a "LIV 2.0" structure.

The Numbers Are Stark

The documentation emerging from LIV Golf's Chapter 11 bankruptcy case in New Jersey tells a story of an organization that spent billions to build a league — and cannot pay the players who gave up their PGA Tour memberships to play in it.

Court filings reveal that the total amount owed to players exceeds $40 million. Jon Rahm, the Spanish major champion who left the PGA Tour in one of the league's highest-profile signings, is listed at approximately $7.5 million in unpaid amounts. Bryson DeChambeau is owed approximately $5.7 million. When all player claims are counted, the total picture is one of significant, widespread financial obligations that LIV cannot currently meet.

The court has approved $14 million in debtor-in-possession financing to keep operations running during the restructuring process. That approval, granted earlier this fall, allows LIV to continue conducting business while it works through the bankruptcy proceedings. Without it, the organization would have faced the prospect of an immediate operational shutdown.

LIV 2.0 and the BC Partners Path

LIV Golf is targeting a January 2027 emergence from bankruptcy under a restructured entity referred to internally as "LIV 2.0," built around a deal with BC Partners. That deal carries conditions: a sufficient number of players must sign new agreements within a defined window for the transaction to proceed. The timeline is tight, and the November 24 court hearing will be a key milestone in determining the path forward.

Complicating the picture further are reported motions to reject certain executory contracts — the agreements with players including DeChambeau, Dustin Johnson, and Cameron Smith may be targeted in this process. A rejected contract, in bankruptcy terms, means the player would be treated as an unsecured creditor for the value of that agreement, potentially reducing what they can recover while also freeing them from any remaining obligations.

The PIF — Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, which bankrolled LIV Golf's launch — has reportedly exited at significant loss. Court documents referenced in reporting from Front Office Sports indicate the scale of the PIF's financial exposure from its investment in the league.

What's Next

The November 24 hearing is the next significant moment in the bankruptcy proceedings. It will likely address the DIP financing structure, the status of player contracts, and the timeline for the BC Partners reorganization. For the players owed money, the central question is whether "LIV 2.0" actually emerges in January 2027 — and whether the agreements signed under a restructured entity bear any resemblance to the ones that brought them to the league in the first place.
