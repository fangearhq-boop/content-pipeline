### STORY 3: LIV Golf Bankruptcy

**FB Long-Form (12:00 PM ET)**
The numbers inside LIV Golf's Chapter 11 bankruptcy filing paint a stark picture of an organization that burned billions in pursuit of a vision that ultimately couldn't sustain itself financially.

LIV Golf filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in New Jersey earlier this fall, and the documentation that has emerged since makes clear the scale of what players are owed. Jon Rahm is listed at approximately $7.5 million in unpaid amounts. Bryson DeChambeau is owed approximately $5.7 million. When all player claims are counted, the total exceeds $40 million owed to athletes who gave up PGA Tour memberships to play for an organization that is now asking a court for protection.

The court has approved $14 million in debtor-in-possession financing to keep operations running during the restructuring process. LIV is targeting a January 2027 emergence under a "LIV 2.0" structure via a BC Partners deal — but that deal depends on a sufficient number of players signing new agreements within a defined window.

A hearing is set for November 24. The question isn't just whether LIV survives. It's whether the players who bet their careers on it see anything close to what they were owed.

Whatever happens today, this week is already one Lindy Duncan will not forget. If she wins, it becomes one of the most satisfying stories of the LPGA season.
