Scottie Scheffler's 2026 Season Was One of the Greatest in PGA Tour History

By Jake Torres | Golf Fanrecap | November 17, 2026

The 2026 PGA Tour season is wrapping up at the RSM Classic in Sea Island, Georgia this week. When it closes, the defining narrative will not be LIV Golf's collapse or what happens next in reinstatement negotiations. It will be the year Scottie Scheffler posted one of the most statistically dominant individual seasons in professional golf history.

The Numbers

Scheffler's 2026 campaign by the numbers: a scoring average of 67.86, No. 1 rankings in more than 20 PGA Tour statistical categories, three victories — The American Express, the 3M Open, and the FedEx St. Jude — and 13 top-five finishes in 20 starts. He won his second FedEx Cup title at the Tour Championship, adding to the one he claimed in 2024.

Then came the milestone that placed the 2026 season in a different category entirely: Scheffler surpassed Tiger Woods on the PGA Tour's all-time career earnings list. The number is not just symbolic — it reflects a sustained run of dominance across multiple seasons that has no modern comparison except the man whose record he broke.

What 67.86 Means

A scoring average of 67.86 is extraordinary. The PGA Tour tracks adjusted scoring averages against the field, which accounts for course difficulty and conditions. Scheffler's raw figure, before any adjustments, represents playing courses at more than two shots below par on average across a full season schedule — a standard of consistency that only a handful of players in the sport's history have approached.

Tiger Woods posted a 68.17 scoring average in 2000, a year widely regarded as the greatest single season in modern professional golf. Scheffler's 2026 figure compares favorably on that metric alone, even without the four major titles Woods claimed that year. Context matters, but the statistical foundation is there for a serious historical comparison.

Three Wins, All of Them Earned

Scheffler's three victories in 2026 did not come at soft fields or low-profile events. The American Express is an early-season desert swing event with strong participation. The 3M Open in Minnesota drew a competitive summer field. The FedEx St. Jude in Memphis is a FedEx Cup Playoffs event, with the season's best players competing for points when they matter most.

Beyond the wins, the 13 top-five finishes across 20 starts speak to consistency that wins and losses alone cannot capture. Scheffler was a contender nearly every week he played. On the weeks he was not victorious, he was almost always in the conversation.

The FedEx Cup

Scheffler's second FedEx Cup title confirms what his weekly results suggested all season: he was the best player on the PGA Tour by a wide margin. The FedEx Cup Playoffs format, which includes the best 70 players in a three-event stretch culminating in the Tour Championship at East Lake, rewards exactly the kind of consistent excellence that defined Scheffler's year. He arrived at East Lake with the points lead and left with the trophy.

What's Next

Scheffler is not in the RSM Classic field this week — the season-ending event at Sea Island draws those chasing final card-securing finishes and first-time winners, not the FedEx Cup champion who has nothing left to prove in 2026. When the PGA Tour season resumes in January, Scheffler will be the defending FedEx Cup champion, the tour's all-time career earnings leader, and the overwhelming favorite to repeat everything he accomplished this year. That is not an expectation that would have felt reasonable for any golfer in any era. With Scheffler, it simply feels like a baseline.
