PGA Tour CEO: No Plans to Welcome Back LIV Golf Players as League Heads to Liquidation

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | November 17, 2026

The door to the PGA Tour is not open for LIV Golf's displaced players — at least not right now.

PGA Tour CEO Brian Rolapp confirmed on Monday that the organization has no plans to reinstate former members who left for LIV Golf, ruling out a new Returning Member Programme as the bankrupt Saudi-backed league moves toward liquidation. Speaking after board discussions, Rolapp said there had been "thoughtful discussions" but that there is currently "no urgency" to decide the matter.

"We have a membership organization, we have rules," Rolapp said. "Rules matter and they will be enforced."

The LIV Era Ends in Bankruptcy

The timeline of LIV Golf's collapse is striking in its speed. The Saudi Public Investment Fund, having invested an estimated $5 billion into the venture, announced in April 2026 that it would end its funding at the close of the 2026 LIV season. LIV Golf filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in September. On November 5, 2026, the organization announced it was folding entirely, with a target liquidation date of January 2027.

The bankruptcy proceedings have revealed debts exceeding $45 million owed to players, with reports indicating Jon Rahm alone was owed in excess of $100 million from his contract. These figures underscore the scale of the financial commitments LIV made to attract players — and the magnitude of the fallout for those who left the PGA Tour expecting guaranteed income.

Brooks Koepka's Return as Template and Exception

The only player to formally return to the PGA Tour through the tour's reinstatement process is Brooks Koepka, who agreed to significant terms: a $5 million charitable contribution, loss of eligibility for the FedEx Cup bonus program, and forfeiture of Player Equity Program grants for five years. The conditions were steep but reflected the PGA Tour's approach: returnees would face meaningful consequences, not an open door.

Rolapp's statement Monday suggests that even Koepka's model — which Rolapp called the Returning Member Programme — will not be extended to others at this time. Patrick Reed took a different route, leaving LIV without formal reinstatement after his original contract expired and he declined a new agreement.

What Happens to Rahm, DeChambeau, and Others

Jon Rahm, who maintains European Tour membership and has publicly expressed a desire to return to the PGA Tour, now waits in a holding pattern. DeChambeau's contract expired at the close of the 2026 LIV season, leaving his next move unresolved. Dustin Johnson and other prominent LIV players face similar uncertainty.

Reports suggest big-name players may ultimately have more leverage in reinstatement negotiations than lesser-known LIV participants, given their commercial value and global profile. But the PGA Tour's current posture suggests those negotiations will not be happening on a rushed timeline.

What's Next

As the PGA Tour heads into its 2026 season finale at the RSM Classic this week, the LIV reinstatement question remains unresolved. The tour's leverage is at its peak — LIV is gone, the European Tour pathway is limited, and the best players on the planet need competitive venues. How and when the PGA Tour chooses to act on that leverage will define professional golf's next chapter. For now, the message from Rolapp is clear: the rules have consequences, and the tour is not in a hurry.
