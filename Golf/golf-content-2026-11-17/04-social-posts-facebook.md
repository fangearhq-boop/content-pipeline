The door is closed.

PGA Tour CEO Brian Rolapp confirmed on Monday that the tour has no plans to reinstate LIV Golf players, ruling out a new Returning Member Programme in the wake of LIV's bankruptcy. Speaking after a board meeting, Rolapp said: "We have a membership organization, we have rules... Rules matter and they will be enforced."

The statement comes as LIV Golf spirals toward liquidation. The Saudi PIF pulled funding in April 2026. LIV filed for Chapter 11 in September. The organization announced it was folding on November 5. The Saudi-backed league that disrupted professional golf for four years has collapsed with debts of more than $45 million owed to players — with Jon Rahm reportedly owed over $100 million.

Now Rahm, Bryson DeChambeau, Dustin Johnson, and dozens of other players who left the PGA Tour for guaranteed LIV contracts face uncertain futures. The only player to return so far through the PGA Tour's formal process is Brooks Koepka, who agreed to significant financial penalties.

Four years. $5 billion. Chapter 11 bankruptcy.

LIV Golf is officially collapsing, and professional golf is reckoning with what the Saudi-backed experiment ultimately meant for the sport.

LIV launched in 2022 with guaranteed contracts that pulled some of the biggest names out of the PGA Tour. Brooks Koepka, Jon Rahm, Bryson DeChambeau, Dustin Johnson — the list of players who left was long and star-studded. The PGA Tour was shaken.
