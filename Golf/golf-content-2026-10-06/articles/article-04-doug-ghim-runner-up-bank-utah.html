Doug Ghim's Runner-Up at Bank of Utah Championship Signals a Breakout Fall

By Ryan Calloway | Golf Fanrecap | October 6, 2026

The story of the Bank of Utah Championship was Austin Smotherman — 100th start, first win, legacy moment. But the player who pushed him every step of the way deserves his own accounting.

Doug Ghim finished second at Black Desert Resort, closing Sunday with a 69 to reach 24-under par for the tournament. He lost by two shots. It was, by most measures, one of the best finishes of his PGA Tour career — and it came at precisely the right time of the FedExCup Fall.

What Ghim Did This Week

Ghim's 24-under total at a course that challenged the entire field was not an accident. He played four consistent rounds at Black Desert Resort, gave himself birdie opportunities throughout the week, and entered Sunday with a legitimate chance to win.

His final-round 69 — three under par — was solid without being spectacular. On a day when Smotherman made one significant error (the double bogey at 13) and recovered without conceding momentum, Ghim needed a couple of things to break differently. They didn't. But the performance itself was real.

A Player Finding His Level

Ghim, 29, has spent his Tour career as a player who seemed consistently capable of more. His ball-striking metrics have long ranked among the better numbers on Tour; converting those metrics into contending performances has been the question.

The Bank of Utah runner-up is evidence that the conversion is happening. This isn't a one-tournament fluke — Ghim has been building form throughout the FedExCup Fall, and a solo second at a $6 million event says something about where his game is right now.

The result earns him significant FedExCup Fall points as the Tour heads to Japan, Bermuda, and Mexico. Three events remain, and Ghim arrives at each of them with momentum.

The Runner-Up That Matters

Finishing second rarely gets celebrated. Second place in golf means the winning putt didn't drop, or someone else was just a little bit better. But runner-up finishes at PGA Tour events aren't cheap, and this one — behind a feel-good story that had the whole gallery rooting for the winner — says something about Ghim's competitive mentality.

He didn't win. But he played his way into the final-day conversation, stayed there through 72 holes, and made Smotherman earn every bit of his two-shot margin.

What's Next

Ghim heads to the Baycurrent Classic in Yokohama, Japan (Oct. 8-11), then Bermuda (Oct. 22) and Mexico Open (Oct. 29). With the FedExCup Fall standings still being written, his Sunday performance at Black Desert Resort is now part of his story for the season.

If Sunday was a preview, the next three events will say whether he can finish the job.
